## Abstract
Polystyrene nanoplastics (PS-NPs) exposure can induce placental toxicity, but the molecular mechanism of PS-NP-induced placental toxicity is poorly understood. We selected PS-NPs (80 nm) as a model of plastic particles and treated the human trophoblast cell line JEG3 (6.25, 12.5, 25, 50, and 100 mg/L) and pregnant mice with PS-NPs (15, 30 and 60 mg/kg·bw). These results found that PS-NPs regulated key ferroptosis-associated proteins (GPX4, ACSL4, and FTH1), indicating that PS-NPs induced ferroptosis. Here, we show for the first time that PS-NPs trigger placental ferroptosis by disrupting the p62-Keap1 interaction, thereby blocking Nrf2 degradation. Although m6A modification of the p62-Keap1-Nrf2 axis has been described in other models, its involvement in nanoplastic-induced placental injury remains unexplored. Here, we showed that PS-NPs induce ferroptosis by promoting METTL3 expression in JEG cells and placental tissues. Furthermore, knockdown of METTL3 inhibited PS-NP-induced ferroptosis by regulating Keap1 m6A, which inhibited the p62-Keap1-Nrf2 pathway in vitro. PS-NP-induced ferroptosis was attenuated by METTL3 knockdown via the inhibition of Keap1 m6A, which regulates the p62-Keap1-Nrf2 pathway. Collectively, these findings indicate thatMETTL3-mediated m6A methylation induced by PS-NPs may represent a previously unrecognized molecular basis for placental injury.

## Introduction
Nanoplastics are common contaminants in living environments and are used worldwide in various applications, including manufacturing, packaging materials (Yang et al. ; Qian et al. ), personal products, and medical devices (Zhang et al. ; Wu et al. ). Nanoparticles are defined as particulates that are less than 100 nm in size (Wen et al. ). Polystyrene, one of the most extensively used plastics, is an elementary component of plastic waste in the environment (Shi et al. ). They have garnered growing attention due to their potential to infiltrate the human body via the respiratory tract, skin, and digestive system(Wan et al. ). Recent studies also have reported that polystyrene nanoplastics (PS-NPs) were ingested and inhaled across cell barriers to placental and fetal tissues (Wan et al. ; Schmidt ; Enyoh et al. ). Few studies on placental cell models and placental tissues have demonstrated the transplacental transport of PS-NPs.
Ferroptosis is morphologically and functionally distinct from necrosis, apoptosis and autophagy (Zhong et al. ), all of which contribute to PS-NP-induced injury and offer a separate mechanistic route for toxicity (Shi et al. ; Li et al. ). Moreover, multiple reviews showed that ferroptosis occurs in placental cells (Tang et al. ). GPX4 plays a central role in placental development and functional maintenance. As the key enzyme suppressing ferroptosis, its down-regulation or functional loss directly triggers lipid peroxidation and trophoblast cell death, a phenomenon validated in multiple models. In human early-onset pre-eclamptic (E-PE) placentas, GPX4 expression is markedly reduced, accompanied by elevated levels of the lipid-peroxidation product malondialdehyde (MDA) and mitochondrial structural damage, indicating that ferroptosis is an important contributor to placental pathology(Park et al. ). Although previous researches has linked nanoparticles to iron-metabolism disruption and ferroptosis signalling in mammalian cells (Shi et al. ; Tang et al. ), their involvement in PS-NP-driven placental injury remains largely unexplored.
Epigenetic modifications may be involved in placental toxicity and play a vital role in the rapid response to environmental stimuli (Qu et al. ). The N6-methyladenosine (m6A) modification, the most abundant form of messenger RNA(mRNA) (Zheng et al. ; Li et al. ), which altered reprogram mRNA stability and translation efficiency, thereby promoting ferroptosis in models of PS-NP-induced reproductive and cardiac toxicity(Qu et al. ). Methyltransferase-like 3 (METTL3) can either stabilize or destabilize target mRNAs depending on cellular context and transcript structure by upregulating m6A levels, which can affect cell death, proliferation, differentiation, and the function of immune inflammatory cells (Xia et al. ). METTL3 directly methylates SLC7A11 mRNA, whereas METTL3 knock-down reduces GPX4 protein, elevates Fe2⁺, ROS and MDA, and triggers classic mitochondrial shrinkage-phenotypes reversed by the ferroptosis inhibitor Fer-1(Xia et al. ; Chen et al. ; Zhang et al. ). Besides, METTL3 over-expression boosts ROS levels by dampening the Keap1-Nrf2 antioxidant axis in mouse renal tubular epithelial cells (mRTECs)(Xia et al. ). However, the biological significance and underlying mechanisms of METTL3-mediated m6A modifications in PS-NP-induced placental toxicity have not yet been explored.
To fill the gaps in existing studies, our study aimed to verify the biological processes on PS-NPs-induced placental toxicity and investigate the effect of m6A methylation on PS-NPs-induced ferroptosis in vitro and in vivo. The present study provided new sights to clarify the mechanisms of PS-NPs-induced ferroptosis, thereby contributing to understand potential effects on placental toxicity.

## Materials and methods

## Cell culture and MTT assay
JEG3 cells (American Type Culture Collection) were maintained at 37 °C in DMEM containing 10% FBS and 1% penicillin/streptomycin under 5% CO2 (Thermo Fisher). Cell viability was assessed by MTT assay: 5 × 103 cells per well in 96-well plates were treated for 24 h, incubated with MTT, dissolved in DMSO, and absorbance read at 450 nm to calculate survival.

## Transfection and METTL3 knockdown
For siRNA-mediated knock-down, 2 × 105 JEG3 cells were seeded in 6-well plates 24 h before transfection so that they reached 60–70% confluence. Cells were transfected with 50 nM siRNA duplexes targeting METTL3 or a non-targeting control (siRNA-NC) using Lipofectamine RNAiMAX (Invitrogen, 13778–150) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. After 6 h the complexes were replaced with complete DMEM. Cells were harvested 48 h post-transfection for Western blot to confirm knock-down efficiency. All functional assays were performed 48 h after transfection unless otherwise stated.

## Characterization of PS-NPs
PS-NPs(smaller than 80 nm) obtained from Tianjin Bestra Chromatography Technology Development Center, and the morphology of PS-NPs was verified morphology by scanning electron microscopy (SEM). The PS-NPs used in this study were sourced from the same supplier and batch as those employed in previous work, in which dynamic light scattering (DLS) and zeta potential measurements had already been characterized(Jing et al. ). Comprehensive details regarding the physicochemical properties and preparation of the PS-NPs are provided.

## Animals and treatment
Pregnant female C57BL/6 J mice were used in the experiment and treated once daily from the first day of pregnancy. To establish a robust and clinically relevant model of PS-NPs (80 nm) exposure, we administered the particles via gastric gavage, a route that faithfully mimics oral ingestion the primary portal through which humans encounter NPs in food, water, and beverages. Mice were treated with various concentrations of PS-NPs (15, 30 and 60 mg/kg·bw), which were respectively low-, medium- and high-dose group. PS-NPs exposed to pregnant mice once daily for 19 days, which affects fetal development.

## ELISA assay
On gestational day 19, 0.8–1.0 mL maternal blood was drawn from the submandibular vein. Estradiol (E2), prolactin (PRL), progesterone (P), follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), luteinizing hormone (LH), and testosterone (T) in serum were quantified with Cusabio mouse ELISA kits. 20 µL serum was incubated with HRP-conjugated antibody (1 h, 37 °C) and absorbance read at 450 nm on a SpectraMax i3x reader.

## Histology
Placental tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 24 h, cut into 5-μm sections, and stained with haematoxylin and eosin (H&E) before microscopy.

## Immunofluorescence (IF) staining
Placental tissues and cells (JEG3 cell lines) were cryosectioned and immunolabeled as previously described(Yujiao et al. ). The primary antibody used were GPX4, p62, METTL3, Nrf2 and Keap1, which were all used at a 1:300 dilution and were purchased from Proteintech..

## Immunohistochemistry (IHC) Analysis
Placental tissue sections were dewaxed with xylene (washed twice for 10 min each) and dehydrated in graded ethanol solutions for immunohistochemical examination. Endogenous peroxidase activity was consistent by treating quenched antigen extract with a concentration of 0.01 mol/L trisodium citrate buffer (pH 6.0). The sections were incubated with the following primary antibodies: METTL3, GPX4, FTH1, Nrf2, Keap1, ACSL4 and HO-1, which were all used at a 1:300 dilution and were purchased from Proteintech. Tetrachloride (Sigma) with 3, 30-diaminobenzidine incubated with the avidin–biotin complex kit.

## Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) assays
Cells were spun at 800 rpm for 5 min, fixed with 4% glutaraldehyde (4 °C, 2 h), post-fixed with 1% osmium tetroxide (4 °C, 1 h), dehydrated in graded ethanol and acetone, and embedded. Ultrathin sections were cut and stained with uranyl acetate and lead citrate.

## Real-time quantitative PCR analysis
RNAout reagent kit was used to extract total RNA. cDNA was synthesized using the PrimeScript RT reagent kit, and mRNA expression levels were assessed with Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix on an ABI 7500 platform. β-actin served as the internal control for normalization purposes. The mRNA expression was determined using the ΔΔCt method.

## Western blotting
RIPA buffer (P0013B; Beyotime) was used to extract the protein of JEG3 cells and placental tissues. After blocking in 5% skim milk for 1 h, the membranes were incubated with primary antibodies, including METTL3, GPX4, FTH1, Nrf2, Keap1, p62, ACSL4, HO-1, NQO1 and β-actin, which were all used at a 1:1000 dilution and were purchased from Proteintech. TBST was used to wash the membranes, which then incubate with HRP-conjugated goat anti-rabbit/anti-mouse IgG.

## MeRIP-qPCR
Cell and tissue lysates were prepared following PS-NPs treatment. Samples were treated with m6A antibody (positive), IgG antibody (negative), and Keap1 antibody. Protein A/G beads were pre-blocked with BSA and then incubated with the respective antibodies. Subsequently, RNA was isolated for RT-qPCR assay. The primers of Keap1 are as following: Keap1-Forward: CTGGAGGATCATACCAAGCAGG, Keap1-Reverse: GGATACCCTCAATGGACACCAC.

## Statistical analysis
Data are presented as mean ± SD and analysed in SPSS 23.0. Before conducting parametric tests, we assessed homogeneity of variances using Levene’s test. Variances were checked with Levene’s test: if P > 0.05, one-way ANOVA plus Tukey was used, and if P ≤ 0.05, Welch’s ANOVA with Games-Howell was applied.

## Results

## Characterization of PS-NPs
The characterization of the PS-NPs was shown in Fig.  . The scanning electron microscopy images revealed that all particles had an almost spherical shape and exhibited good dispersion (Fig.  A). The mean primary diameter of the PS-NPs was approximately 80 nm (Fig.  B). The intensity of PS-NPs with red fluorescence was significantly higher than that without red fluorescence (Fig.  C).
 

## PS-NPs induced placental toxicity in vivo
We constructed a mouse model that was exposed to various concentrations of PS-NPs (15, 30 and 60 mg/kg·bw) for 19 days (Fig.  A). PS-NPs increased the body weight of pregnant mice and decreased their placental weight (Fig.  B and Fig.  C). Previous study demonstrated that inflammation-driven maternal oedema, fat deposition and hyperphagia outweighed fetal weight loss, yielding a net increase in maternal body weight(Ye et al. ). PS-NPs exposure induced significant placental tissue destruction, which affected fetal growth and development. Compared to controls, the level of P was significantly downregulated and E2 was upregulated in the high-dose PS-NPs group (Fig.  D). H&E staining in PS-NPs exposure groups showed changes in placental morphology in a dose-dependent manner (Fig.  E), including the increase of placental villi number and the decrease of decidua area. The results revealed that PS-NPs induced placental toxicity by altering P and E2 levels in a dose-dependent manner.
 

## METTL3 mediated PS-NPs-induced ferroptosis through regulating p62 Keap1-Nrf2 signaling pathway in vivo
To determine whether the p62-Keap1-Nrf2 pathway mediated PS-NP-induced ferroptosis in vivo, coimmunoprecipitation of Keap1, p62, and Nrf2 was used to investigate the function of the p62-Keap1-Nrf2 pathway in ferroptosis triggered by PS-NP. Our in vivo results demonstrate that METTL3 up-regulation coincides with elevated p62, decreased Keap1 and increased Nrf2 activity in PS-NP-exposed placenta. A significant increase in the interaction between Keap1 and p62 and a decreased interaction between Keap1 and Nrf2 in the high-dose PS-NPs group (Fig.  A and Fig.  B). Because of the PS-NP-induced decrease in interaction between Keap1 and Nrf2, Nrf2 degradation was inhibited. The METTL3, p62 and Nrf2 protein levels increased, and the protein levels of Keap1 decreased in the high-dose PS-NPs groups (Fig.  C and Fig.  D). Thus, the ferroptosis-related proteins FTH1 and GPX4 decreased, while NQO1, HO-1, and ACSL4 increased in the high-dose PS-NPs group. IHC staining also showed that PS-NPs exposure increased METTL3, Nrf2, and HO-1, and reduced Keap1, FTH1, and GPX4 (Fig.  E). These results indicated that METTL3 induces ferroptosis through the p62-Keap1-Nrf2 axis in vivo.
 

## PS-NPs induced cell cytotoxicity
Cell viability was calculated after treating with various concentrations of PS-NPs (6.25, 12.5, 25, 50 and 100 mg/L) for 24 h (Fig.  A). We examined the uptake of red fluorescence-labelled PS-NPs by JEG3 cells. Fluorescence microscopy images showed that red fluorescence-labeled PS-NPs aggregated in the cells (Fig.  B). To further confirm this, the internalization of PS-NPs and specific morphological changes associated with ferroptosis in the JEG3 cells of the PS-NPs-treated group were observed using TEM (Fig.  C), including mitochondrial membrane rupture, decreased or vanishing mitochondrial cristae density, and mitochondrial swelling. These observations demonstrate that PS-NPs accumulated in JEG3 cells induced ferroptosis. As shown in Fig.  D, cell viability decreased at concentrations of PS-NPs, which gradually decreased. These results revealed that PS-NPs exposure resulted in cytotoxicity.
 

## METTL3 mediated PS-NPs-induced ferroptosis through regulating p62-Keap1-Nrf2 pathway in vitro
Western blotting and IF revealed elevated METTL3 after PS-NP treatment (Fig.  A-D), indicating that METTL3 drives PS-NP-triggered ferroptosis. To illustrate the effect of the p62-Keap1-Nrf2 pathway on PS-NP-induced ferroptosis, protein expression analysis revealed that p62 and Nrf2 expression in the PS-NPs group was strongly elevated. Keap1 protein in the PS-NPs group was lower than that in the controls. The fluorescence of p62, Nrf2, and Keap1 was consistent with their protein expression levels (Fig.  E). Ferroptosis induced by PS-NPs cloud depend on the p62-Keap1-Nrf2 pathway.
 We measured GPX4 and ACSL4 expression, which mediated PS-NPs-induced ferroptosis. PS-NPs treatment downregulated GPX4 and FTH1, when ACSL4 protein elevated in Fig.  A and Fig.  B. In addition, the green fluorescence of GPX4 was higher in the PS-NPs group (Fig.  E).

## METTL3 regulated Keap1 m6A through activating ferroptosis triggered by PS-NPs
To investigate whether upregulated METTL3 is involved in PS-NP-induced ferroptosis, we first measured cell viability after treatment with siRNA-METTL3, which downregulated METTL3 expression. The MTT assay showed that METTL3 knockdown significantly impaired PS-NP-induced growth inhibition in JEG3 cells (Fig.  A and Fig.  C). METTL3 knockdown decreased PS-NP-induced upregulation of METTL3. Importantly, METTL3 knockdown increased the ferroptosis-associated GPX4 and FTH1, and decreased the expression of the autophagy-associated protein p62. In addition, we determined whether METTL3 knockdown regulates the expression of p62, Keap1, and Nrf2. The expression of Keap1 and Nrf2 in the si-METTL3#3 + PS-NPs group was lower in the group, and p62 expression increased in the si-METTL3#3 + PS-NPs group (Fig.  D).
 Keap1 m6A was determined using MeRIP-qPCR, and Fig.  B showed that PS-NPs increased Keap1 m6A. Furthermore, si-METTL3#3 reversed the increase of Keap1 m6A induced by PS-NPs. Collectively, these results suggest that METTL3 mediates PS-NP-induced ferroptosis by increasing Keap1 m6A, which activates the interaction between p62 and Keap1 and is responsible for Nrf2 expression in ferroptosis.

## Discussion
The adverse effects of plastic pollution on humans have attracted increasing attention (Enyoh et al. ; Zambrano-Jerez et al. ). It is the first study to report that microplastic pieces were discovered within the human placenta(Ragusa et al. ). In addition, PS-NPs induce abnormalities in placental development, which is the key tissue that mediates the association of fetal with maternal environments (Wan et al. ; Enyoh et al. ). The mechanisms of PS-NPs were explored using 80 nm PS-NPs by exposing to mice for 19 days, which exposed to various concentrations (15, 30 and 60 mg/kg·bw). The selection of these doses is also informed by previous studies on the toxicity of PS-NPs and other nanoparticles, which exhibit different biological effects at various concentrations(Zhong et al. ; Gu et al. ). Further, these selected concentrations are also reflective of potential real-world exposure scenarios, which are within a range that is considered relevant for risk assessment(Tang et al. ; Shan et al. ). Besides, previous studies have already confirmed the biodistribution and uptake of PS-NPs in placenta(Jing et al. ). Therefore, we focused on demonstrating intracellular uptake and localization using JEG3 trophoblast cells in this study. The JEG3 cell line, a relevant model derived from a choriocarcinoma, has been widely used to investigate placental toxicity(Zhuang et al. ; Mihalic et al. ).We also found that PS-NPs induced significant placental tissue destruction in vivo and injury to JEG3 cells after treating various concentrations of PS-NPs. In line with previous studies (Yang et al. ), the increased E2 level and downregulated P level might be correlated with PS-NPs treatment, which indicated that PS-NPs resulted in imbalances in endogenous hormone concentrations in mice during pregnancy. Previous studies also reported that Elevated E2 engages ERα-dependent PI3K/Akt signalling, which up-regulates CYP450-dependent lipid-desaturases, enriching poly-unsaturated phospholipids, and increases TFRC expression, thereby expanding the labile Fe2+ pool-two prerequisites for lipid-ROS amplification(Yu et al. ). Concurrent progesterone withdrawal relieves its tonic inhibition on Nrf2, leading to decreased GPX4 abundance and diminished antioxidant capacity(Yu et al. ). Thus, E2↑/P↓ primes trophoblast membranes for peroxidation while simultaneously crippling their lipid-repair machinery, aligning with the “hormonal sensitizer” concept described for vascular cells(Yu et al. ). However, there is limited information regarding the molecular mechanisms underlying PS-NP-induced placental toxicity.
Ferroptosis is a nonapoptotic cell death process that involves sex-specific small intestinal toxicity in the offspring after the exposure of PS-NPs to pregnant mice(Tang et al. ). However, studies on PS-NPs-induced placental toxicity through ferroptosis remain unclear. Herein, we confirmed the significant decrease of GPX4 and FTH1 expression in PS-NP-treated placental tissues and JEG3 cells compared to the control groups, which are unique and specific activators of ferroptosis. GPX4, an important regulator of ferroptosis, effectively reduces lipid peroxidation (Chen et al. ; Wang et al. ). Exogenous free Fe3+ is internalized by the cell via transferrin and TfR1 on the cell surface, then acidified to Fe2+, which subsequently binds to intracellular ferritin, encompassing both the ferritin light chain and FTH1(Mi et al. ). Building on our previous work showing that pro-inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL-6 and TNF-α) and nuclear translocation of transcription factors such as NF-κB also contribute to PS-NP-induced toxicity, the present study focused specifically on the role of ferroptosis in placental injury(Das et al. ). The inflammatory component will be systematically dissected in follow-up studies. Our present study suggests that PS-NPs induce placental toxicity by activating ferroptosis in vivo and in vitro.
Nrf2-Keap1-p62 antioxidative signaling mediates PS-NP-induced ferroptosis(Wei et al. ; Guo et al. ). P62 is recognized as a key regulator of autophagy, which modulates Nrf2 degradation by directly interacting with Keap1 (Luo et al. ; Cai et al. ). Multiple independent groups have demonstrated that Genetic deletion or pharmacological inhibition of Keap1 stabilizes Nrf2, which in turn transcriptionally up-regulates P62/SQSTM1 and downstream antioxidant enzymes, thereby suppressing lipid peroxidation and ferroptotic cell death(Dixon et al. ; Okazaki et al. ). P62 knockout models (both cellular CRISPR-Cas9 and whole-body P62−/− mice) exhibit increased sensitivity to erastin- or RSL3-induced ferroptosis, accompanied by elevated ROS and malondialdehyde levels, confirming the pathway specificity(Zhang et al. ). Besides, conditional Keap1-knockout mice show marked resistance to ischemia–reperfusion injury in liver and kidney in vivo, a phenotype that is largely reversed by additional Nrf2 deletion, underscoring the linear Nrf2-Keap1-p62 axis(Baird and Yamamoto ). Collectively, these prior loss-of-function studies provide strong and specific evidence that the Nrf2-Keap1-p62 module is both necessary and sufficient for ferroptosis control. Emerging evidence indicates that Nrf2 activation not only fails to antagonize ferroptosis but actually exacerbates cell death by blocking GPX4 translation. Sustained or excessive Nrf2 activity promotes eIF2α phosphorylation and stress-granule assembly, thereby suppressing cap-dependent translation of GPX4 mRNA and diminishing de-novo GPX4 synthesis(Alanazi et al. ; Ji et al. ). Concomitantly, Nrf2-driven overexpression of HO-1 liberates free Fe2+, fueling Fenton chemistry and amplifying lipid-ROS bursts(Alanazi et al. ; Ji et al. ). Importantly, genetic ablation (global or conditional Nfe2l2 knockout) or pharmacologic inhibition of Nrf2 with ML385 or brusatol relieves translational repression of GPX4(Yan et al. ), restores its protein levels, curtails lipid ROS, and markedly reverses the ferroptotic phenotype, improving cell viability. Collectively, these findings establish that prolonged or hyperactivated Nrf2 can promote ferroptosis through translational control and iron-metabolic reprogramming, providing a theoretical framework for precision interventions targeting the Nrf2-GPX4 axis. We also found that PS-NPs suppressed Keap1 expression and increased p62 and Nrf2 expressions. Further, the association of p62 with Keap1 increased, and the relationship between Nrf2 and Keap1 decreased after exposing PS-NPs. Weakened Nrf2-Keap1 binding drives Nrf2 accumulation during PS-NP-triggered ferroptosis, with the p62-Keap1 interface preventing Nrf2 degradation(Sun et al. ). Early work showed that Nrf2 ferroptotic activity relies on induction of NQO1 and HO-1(Sun et al. ). The decline in progesterone relieves its brake on Nrf2 transcriptional activity, leading to decreased GPX4 expression and thereby facilitating ferroptotic cell death. In addition, we observed that PS-NPs suppressed FTH1 expression and promoted NQO1 and HO1 expression, indicating that PS-NPs induced ferroptosis via the Nrf2-Keap1-p62 signaling pathway. However, the mechanisms underlying the Nrf2-Keap1-p62 pathway on PS-NPs induced ferroptosis remain unclear.
The N6-methyladenosine (m6A) modification has been reported to participate in placental toxicity induced by environmental toxins (Qu et al. ), and m6A methyltransferase METTL3 mediated ferroptosis (Zheng et al. ; Xia et al. ). In this study, PS-NPs increased METTL3 expression in placental tissues and JEG3 cells. METTL3 promotes mRNA degradation by regulating m6A levels. In line with previous studies (Wang et al. ), our study also found that METTL3 regulated Keap1 expression through m6A modification, thus affecting the interaction among Keap1, p62, and Nrf2. Interestingly, METTL3 promoted Keap1 m6A, which enhanced the interaction between p62 and Keap1 and decreased the interaction between Keap1 and Nrf2 after PS-NPs exposure, thus inhibiting Nrf2 degradation.

## Conclusion
Our study suggested that PS-NPs induce placental toxicity by promoting ferroptosis. Mechanistically, the Nrf2-Keap1-p62 antioxidative signaling pathway mediates PS-NP-induced ferroptosis in vivo and in vitro. Moreover, METTL3 promoted ferroptosis by increasing the levels of Keap1 m6A. Keap1 m6A enhances the interaction between p62 and Keap1 and suppresses the interaction between Nrf2 and Keap1, resulting in PS-NP-induced ferroptosis via Nrf2 activation. Our results provide new insights into the mechanisms of PS-NP-induced ferroptosis involving METTL3-mediated m6a modification.
