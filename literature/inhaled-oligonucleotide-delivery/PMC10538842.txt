During the final (third) trimester outside of the womb, the retina develops significantly and is vulnerable to problems. Similar to how the cerebral cortex does, the cerebellum also grows quickly during this time and is susceptible to upsetting environmental influences.[ ] The brain—and specifically the cerebellum—develops quickly, making it susceptible to environmental changes that could take place during this important time. IGF-1 and vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), in particular, control the appearance of retinal vessels in the fourth month of pregnancy, when they are typically mature by 36–40 weeks’ gestation.[ ] Vertical development would be negatively correlated with retinopathy of prematurity (ROP) as a result of these changes, which include a sharp decline in IGF-1 following preterm birth and persistent IGF-1 in low concentrations in the weeks that follow. Because the cortex grows quickly during the third trimester of pregnancy and is potentially vulnerable to severe ROP, a decrease is expected in IGF-1 levels as well as other unfavorable environmental and epigenetic factors.[ ] Using magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) at a term-equivalent age, researchers examined the relationship between severe ROP and morphological brain development in this case–control study (TEA). Severe ROP in preterm infants and controls with matched gestational age (GA), sex, and birth weight (BW) were compared for brain volumes of major brain structures and measures of cortical morphology.[ ] It was predicted that preterm infants with ROP would have less complex cortical gyrification and smaller brain volumes than preterm control infants. Relationship between neurodevelopmental outcomes assessed at ages of 15 months and 2 years corrected and severe ROP (CA). Infants with severe ROP were thought to perform worse on cognitive and motor tasks. Smaller brain volumes and poor motor neurodevelopmental outcomes were linked to severe ROP.[ ]

## Retinopathy of prematurity and motor development

## Retinopathy without any intervention
At 2 months post-term:
All retinopathy-affected infants displayed a distinct delay in head control as well as exaggerated and aggravated “fidgety” movements. Further, a protracted period of ataxic features defined postural control. This is a result of the proprioceptive and vestibular systems not receiving the normal calibration from vision. After a few weeks, early signs of visuomotor coordination such as head orientation and coordinated eye–head scanning vanished.[ ]
At 18 months:
The main centers for the primary motor and visual pathways are optic radiations, the posterior limb of the internal capsule, posterior white matter, and the external capsule. Severe ROP is associated with maturational delay in these areas and is linked to worse motor and cognitive outcomes at 18 months of corrected age.[ ]
At 24 months:
Without any treatment, strabismus was more prevalent in children with ROP stage 3.[ ]

## Retinopathy with intervention
Anti-VEGF medications have advantages over laser treatment in that they are simpler to administer, require less anesthesia, may improve peripheral vision, and cause less refractive error. Therefore, using anti-VEGF medications to treat ROP is becoming more and more common.
The theoretical worry that intraocular anti-VEGF could have negative effects on other organ systems, including the brain, is raised by the fact that VEGF levels in the blood are reduced after anti-VEGF agents are administered intravitreally. As a result, neurodevelopmental results in infants receiving anti-VEGF medication have drawn more attention recently.[ ]

## Relationship between ROP and brain volumes
The third trimester is when the cerebral cortex, cerebellum, and retina develop. These areas are vulnerable to environmental influences if their development is interrupted. Throughout childhood and adolescence, neurodevelopmental defects have been linked to impaired cortical development and smaller brain volumes. Reduced cerebellar volumes have been linked to an increased risk of autism spectrum disorder, lower motor performance, impaired executive functioning, and poorer cognitive outcomes. Cognitive functioning has been shown to be significantly influenced by the cerebellum, which performs this function via cerebellar pathways. Early interference with cerebellar growth may affect the developing cerebral cortex and, as a result, movement, cognition, and affective regulation because the cerebellum serves as a safeguard center for higher-order regions of the cortex.[ ]
MRI tests were conducted as a part of the evaluation. Using an automated segmentation method, cortical gray matter (CoGM), cerebellum, unmyelinated white matter (UWM), brainstem, and extracerebral and ventricular cerebrospinal fluid volumes (mL) were intuitively T2-weighted images on coronal segments. When compared with controls, preterm infants with severe ROP had significantly smaller absolute (P = 0.04) and relative (P = 0.048) cerebellar volumes. Additionally, both the absolute and relative brainstem volumes in ROP infants were noticeably smaller. The transcerebellar diameter was also noticeably smaller in infants with severe ROP (P = 0.04). Between ROP infants and controls, no discernible differences were observed in the CoGM, UWM, or CSF volumes. Measures of cortical development such as the gyrification index and cortical surface area also showed no significant differences.[ ]
Impairment of cerebellar growth has been connected to supratentorial brain injury. As the highest number of glucocorticoid receptors are found in the neonatal cerebellum, the aggregate intake of steroids demonstrated a relationship with ROP, lower cerebellar and UWMV, and lower mental developmental index. The production of cerebellar granule neuron precursors is regulated by high glucocorticoids, particularly in studies using mice. A lower cerebellar volume was associated with an excessive total steroid intake (P = 0.001). In preterm infants, postnatal corticosteroid exposure has been linked to increased ROP risk, altered optic radiation structure, and compromised cerebellar growth. In conclusion, developmentally impaired outcomes and progression of ROP at any stage in very preterm infants were linked to smaller brain volumes. Therefore, preventing ROP at any stage has positive effects on the brain’s development.

## Risk factor of retinopathy of prematurity
To create predictive models and gain insight into the pathophysiology of retinal vascular diseases and diseases of prematurity, it is imperative to comprehend ROP risk factors. The list below includes two different categories of risk factors.

Modifiable:

Maternal factors, perinatal factors, prenatal eclampsia, and pre-eclampsia.
Neonatal risk factors (prematurity-related comorbidities, days spent on oxygen, blood transfusion demographics, medical interventions, nutrition, blood transfusion, and genetic factors).[ ]

Non-modifiable:

Low gestational age (major <25 wks GA)
Birth weight <1500 g
Multiple gestations.

The only factors that show promise for lowering the incidence and ROP severity among high-risk infants are prematurity prevention, preeclampsia control, and prudent use of oxygen therapy and ventilation.[ ] Although there were a number of risk factors that could affect the outcome, including, birth weight, gestational age, test-tube babies, the number of fetuses, complications during pregnancy, Apgar scores in the first 10 min, and delivery mode and percentage of weight gain at 1–6 weeks after birth, history of oxygen inhalation, postnatal feeding mode, anemia, and blood transfusion, only gestational age is the main factor in both developing and developed nations.[ ]

## ROP and Autism
Complete blindness in children brought on by ROP is more frequently linked to autism and cerebral damage.[ ]

## Treatment of retinopathy of prematurity
Ranibizumab and bevacizumab are anti-vascular endothelial growth factor medications. A mitogen, VEGF is crucial for angiogenesis during fetal life. Researchers are treating ROP, a vasoproliferative disorder with anti-VEGF medications such as bevacizumab/ranibizumab intravitreally. Clinical trials have particularly demonstrated the anti-neovascularization activity of intravitreal bevacizumab in neonates with acute stage 3 ROP in zone I or posterior zone II with plus disease, as measured by neovascular vessel regression of ROP. Pegaptinib is an intravitreal oligonucleotide that has been pegylated and specifically binds VEGF to prevent neovascularization.[ ]

## Conventional laser therapy
The complete avascular retina, as well as the peripheral retina, should be treated during retinal ablation with laser photocoagulation (using a transpupillary diode, 11 argon, and three FD-YAG) or cryoablation as failing to do so promotes disease progression and results in unfavorable anatomical and refractive outcomes. Avascular retina can be treated quickly and effectively to preserve central macular vision.
