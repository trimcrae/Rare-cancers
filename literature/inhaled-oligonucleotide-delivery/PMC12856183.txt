## Abstract
RNA therapeutics have come of age as clinically validated modalities including mRNA, siRNA, antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs), and in vivo genome editing, with lipid nanoparticles (LNPs) as the main non-viral delivery system. This review defines programmable LNPs as systems whose composition and interfacial chemistry are tuned to control organ tropism, cell specificity, intracellular trafficking, and immune interactions. We summarize design rules across four core components (ionizable lipid, phospholipid, cholesterol, PEG-lipid) and highlight levers like apparent pKa optimization (∼6–7 for hepatic delivery), biodegradable linkers, PEG-anchor-dependent shedding, ligands (e.g., GalNAc), and selective organ-targeting (SORT) lipids that redirect biodistribution beyond the liver. We survey advances in data-guided formulation, including DNA-barcoded in vivo libraries, machine learning, and physics-based prediction, plus scalable manufacturing (microfluidics, confined impinging-jet mixing, tangential-flow filtration) and Quality-by-Design with process-analytical technologies. A comprehensive characterization toolkit (size/ζ-potential, cryo-EM/SAXS, RNA encapsulation and integrity, apparent pKa, in vivo barcoding) maps to critical quality attributes. Applications span vaccines, protein replacement, siRNA/ASO delivery, and CRISPR platforms, with clinical examples like patisiran, COVID-19 and RSV mRNA vaccines, in-human transthyretin (TTR) editing, and individualized melanoma vaccination. We analyze translational constraints like endosomal escape, reactogenicity and anti-PEG immunity, complement activation, and lot-to-lot control, plus success factors: corona-aware design, dose-efficient potency at low lipid burden, redosing strategies, and fit-for-purpose biomarkers. Together, programmable LNPs offer a generalizable path to extrahepatic, cell-aware RNA medicine when coupled to rigorous analytics and platform manufacturing.
Keywords: Lipid nanoparticles, RNA therapeutics, Programmable delivery systems, Ionizable lipids, Clinical translation, mRNA vaccines, Gene editing

## Introduction
RNA therapeutics now span diverse modalities, including messenger RNA (mRNA; A complete list of abbreviations is provided at the end of the article.), small interfering RNA (siRNA), and antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs), with newer platforms such as RNA editing and circular RNA in preclinical and translational stages. Collectively, these enable programmable control of protein expression or gene regulation at the RNA level, supporting rapid design-build-test cycles and modular manufacturing. Clinically, the field extends beyond COVID-19 vaccines: several RNA drugs have gained approval, with a broad pipeline across rare and common diseases, highlighting the modality's scope and growing regulatory track record [ , ]. summarizes RNA modalities, key indications, and delivery bottlenecks. Strategic analyses emphasize platform thinking, layering chemistry, delivery, and analytics to boost applicability, efficacy, and safety across indications [ ]. Yet major hurdles persist, including extracellular and endosomal degradation, innate immune activation, and the need for cell-type-specific delivery. Overcoming these bottlenecks has made delivery science, especially lipid nanoparticles (LNPs), central to RNA therapeutics. These features position RNA as an adaptable tool for precision medicine [ ].
 The trajectory of delivery systems runs from early liposomes and cationic lipoplexes to ionizable LNPs that condense RNA at acidic pH yet stay near neutral in blood, balancing potency and tolerability while promoting endosomal escape. Authoritative reviews have codified the four-component architecture, ionizable lipid, helper phospholipid, cholesterol, and polyethylene glycol (PEG) lipid, plus its structure-function rules for mRNA delivery [ , ]. As shown in , over the past decade, lipid nanoparticles (LNPs) have moved from an enabling concept to a clinical workhorse. LNPs have provided the packaging and intracellular delivery needed for human efficacy [ , ]. A clinical milestone was patisiran (Onpattro) in 2018, the first siRNA therapeutic, whose LNP enabled liver-directed silencing of transthyretin (TTR) and improved neuropathy in a phase 3 trial [ ]. These advances, combined with mRNA vaccine lessons, established LNPs as the leading non-viral vehicle for systemic RNA delivery and paved the way for reconfigurable designs [ ].
 We define programmable LNPs as carriers whose molecular composition and interfacial chemistry are tuned to control organ tropism, cell specificity, intracellular trafficking, and immune interactions. A key example is selective organ targeting (SORT): adding a fifth SORT lipid to standard LNPs reprograms biodistribution among liver, spleen, and lung after systemic dosing, enabling extrahepatic mRNA delivery and in vivo gene editing [ ]. Mechanistic work shows how charge distribution and component ratios drive SORT behavior and tissue specificity, treating composition as a tunable variable rather than a fixed limit [ ]. Beyond combinatorial chemistry libraries, computational approaches train models on thousands of LNP activity data points to suggest new ionizable-lipid structures for targeted RNA delivery, guiding programmable LNPs from hypothesis to candidate [ ].
At the materials level, most successful systems share a four-component architecture (an ionizable lipid, helper phospholipid, cholesterol, and a PEG-lipid) whose structures and ratios jointly control particle assembly, serum–corona formation, biodistribution, and endosomal escape [ , ]. Meanwhile, formulation physics, especially rapid mixing via staggered-herringbone, confined-impinging-jet, or multi-inlet vortex mixers, has become a first-order determinant of critical quality attributes (size, PDI, encapsulation, apparent pKa), linking process parameters to in vivo performance [ , ]. On the discovery side, route- and organ-aware frameworks, including SORT chemistries and DNA/mRNA-barcoded in vivo screens, now connect composition to tissue tropism at library scale.
Programmable LNPs could extend RNA therapies from hepatocytes to immune, pulmonary, and neuromuscular targets, but translation demands aligning design levers with clinical needs like high potency at low doses, predictable biodistribution, acceptable reactogenicity, and scalable chemistry-manufacturing-controls (CMC) [ , ]. Clinical precedents highlight opportunities and limits. Onpattro set standards for systemic RNA delivery and LNP product quality, serving as a template for later efforts [ ]. Separately, first-in-human in vivo CRISPR editing with LNP-encapsulated Cas9/sgRNA against TTR (NTLA-2001) achieved deep serum TTR reductions after one infusion, underscoring optimized hepatic delivery's potential and the benchmarks in dose-response, durability, and safety that future programmable systems must meet or exceed [ ].
Prior reviews have excellently covered LNP fundamentals, barriers, and routes of administration, but typically treat composition, assembly process, and translational endpoints in relative isolation. Here we offer an integrated design-to-translation perspective: (i) we formalize programmability as a set of actionable levers each mapped to expected effects and trade-offs; (ii) we link those levers to formulation physics (mixing time/solvent exchange) and critical quality attributes (size/PDI, encapsulation, ζ-potential, apparent pKa) with assay best practices; and (iii) we connect both to application-level objectives (organ tropism, redosing feasibility, reactogenicity) and regulatory expectations under quality-by-design (QbD) and process-analytical technology (PAT). We also incorporate newer discovery paradigms (SORT chemistries, barcoded in-vivo libraries, and early AI-guided lipid design) to show how data-driven selection can shift dose–response and biodistribution curves in target tissues. Collectively, this synthesis aims to serve as a practical playbook for choosing and validating LNP designs across indications, while highlighting translational inflection points, such as anti-PEG immunity and complement-mediated reactions that influence redosing, safety, and lot-to-lot reliability.

2.

## Fundamental components and design principles
The modern LNP platform is best understood as a constrained design space in which a small set of tunable materials determines macroscopic pharmacology (biodistribution, transfection kinetics, and tolerability) under clinically validated boundary conditions [ , ]. Clinical precedents such as patisiran established that appropriately engineered lipids can repeatedly achieve therapeutic exposure in humans, anchoring safety and efficacy assumptions for subsequent designs [ , ]. The COVID-19 mRNA vaccines then demonstrated global scalability of LNP-mRNA formulations and the robustness of their performance across diverse populations, moving the field from proof-of-concept to mass deployment [ , ]. Against that backdrop, contemporary reviews formalized the component-level levers and biological barriers that govern LNP behavior, providing the mechanistic vocabulary used throughout this section [ ].
Because programmability must ultimately translate to manufacturable, reviewable products, the section also frames composition and interfacial chemistry as critical material attributes (CMAs) within a QbD paradigm. ICH Q8(R2) explicitly links material choices and process parameters to critical quality attributes (CQAs) via a defined design space, an approach we adopt when discussing how specific lipid identities and ratios map to performance envelopes [ , ]. For vaccine use cases, global guidance further clarifies expectations for defining starting materials, characterization, specifications, and control strategies for mRNA/LNP products, ensuring that programmable options remain compatible with good manufacturing practice (GMP) and regulatory review [ ].
2.1.

## Core LNP components
State-of-the-art RNA-LNPs have a standard four-component structure which includes ionizable lipids, helper phospholipids, cholesterol, and PEG-lipids. This simple setup allows efficient nucleic-acid encapsulation, colloidal stability, and in vivo delivery while keeping systemic toxicity low. Typical formulations use narrow molar ranges, about 40–50 % ionizable lipid, 38–45 % cholesterol, 10–12 % phospholipid, and 1–2 % PEG-lipid, with many systems starting from the 50:38:10:1.5 ratio. These proportions balance electrostatic complexation, structural integrity, and circulation time, matching mechanistic reviews and experiments in clinically relevant LNPs [ ]. shows a schematic illustration of the structure of an LNP encapsulating RNA molecules.
 The ionizable lipid is the key element for potency and safety. It stays mostly neutral at physiological pH to limit nonspecific interactions but gains positive charge in the acidifying endosome, condensing RNA and aiding endosomal escape [ ]. The global or apparent pKa measured on the assembled LNP (commonly by TNS fluorescence) is a practical surrogate for the net protonation state across the endosomal pH trajectory and remains one of the most informative formulation-level predictors of mRNA delivery [ ]. Across different chemotypes, functional LNPs show an apparent lipid pKa around 6–7, now measured directly in formulations and optimized in silico and experimentally [ ]. Importantly, this range reflects a hepatic optimum associated with ApoE-mediated LDLR uptake. For extrahepatic targets (e.g., lung, spleen), effective formulations often deviate from this window and/or incorporate SORT lipids, alternative helper lipids/sterols, or different routes (e.g., inhaled) to shift corona formation, charge distribution, and uptake pathways. Thus, pKa should be optimized in an organ-aware manner rather than treated as a universal value [ , , , , ]. Recent views highlight that apparent pKa, not solution pKa, controls activity, and targeted changes like ester linkages for hydrolytic lability can fine-tune endosomal interactions and speed biodegradation after delivery, boosting tolerability without losing efficacy [ , ]. In intramuscular vaccine optimization and, iterative ionizable-lipid design identified ethanolamine-core lipids whose LNPs performed best when the apparent pKa was ∼6.6–6.9, aligning with this broader rule [ ]. Recent structure–activity studies further show how tail unsaturation and headgroup polarity shift the apparent pKa and thereby alter function, underscoring that pKa is an emergent LNP property rather than an intrinsic small-molecule constant [ , ]. A comparison of approved and investigational ionizable lipids, including motifs, synthesis routes, and trade-offs, appears in .
 Helper phospholipids and accessory components manage structure and trafficking. Saturated zwitterionic phospholipids like DSPC strengthen the LNP interfacial monolayer, minimize leakage, and appear in approved products; fusogenic options like DOPE can replace them to adjust stability and membrane fusion [ , ]. Substituting unsaturated DOPE (or DOPC) for saturated DSPC often increases transfection, likely via increased membrane fluidity and hemifusion propensity. Quantitative surface-sensing work showed DOPE-containing LNPs adsorb more apolipoprotein E (ApoE) than DSPC analogues, cohering with higher hepatic accumulation mediated by the ApoE/LDLR axis [ ]. Comparative formulation studies likewise report higher expression with DOPE/DOPC relative to DSPC in several mRNA contexts, while noting that optimal choices may differ for siRNA [ ].
Cholesterol or sterol analogues plasticize the membrane, affect raft-like organization, and can promote fusion or hinder transit if over-stabilizing. Cholesterol analogues (e.g., β-sitosterol and other C-24 phytosterols) reshape LNP morphology and promote endosomal perturbation, increasing functional mRNA delivery. Mechanistic imaging revealed ∼10-fold more endosomal disruption events with β-sitosterol LNPs than with cholesterol controls, and structure–activity mapping implicated C-24 side-chain features as critical [ ]. A broader analysis confirmed that phytosterol-containing LNPs adopt polymorphic shapes and enhance transfection across cell types, pointing to sterol chemistry as a programmable handle for endosomal escape [ ]. Finally, PEG-lipids form a hydrophilic corona that curbs aggregation and opsonization during manufacture and circulation; their acyl-chain length controls shedding kinetics, with shorter tails (e.g., DMG-PEG2000, C14) shedding faster than longer ones (e.g., DSG-PEG2000, C18), trading extended stealth for better cellular uptake [ ]. These support lipids serve as adjustable controls for size, stability, biodistribution, and endosomal escape when paired with the ionizable lipid [ , ].

2.2.

## Programmable design strategies
LNP programmability arises from deliberate control of surface chemistry and lipid composition to guide organ, cell, and intracellular targeting. One approach tunes PEG-lipid shedding to time protein corona formation and receptor binding: shorter anchors (e.g., DMG-PEG2000, C14) shed quickly in serum, enabling ApoE adsorption and LDLR-mediated hepatic uptake, while longer anchors extend stealth for tissue targeting and repeat dosing [ , ]. Another uses ligands like GalNAc to bind asialoglycoprotein receptor (ASGPR) on hepatocytes or alternative ligands for other cells; GalNAc-LNPs have supported liver gene editing in preclinical models and are advancing with helical peptide scaffolds for better specificity [ ]. A third adds SORT lipids to adjust surface charge and phase behavior, redirecting delivery to lung or spleen for extrahepatic options [ ].
For intravenously dosed LNPs, adsorption of ApoE is a dominant determinant of liver tropism, ApoE bridges LNPs to LDL receptors on hepatocytes and related cell types, driving rapid hepatic uptake [ ]. Elegant small-angle scattering and contrast-variation studies showed that ApoE binding triggers structural rearrangements within mRNA-LNPs, with redistribution of shell and core lipids that could facilitate escape [ ]. These mechanistic insights explain why seemingly subtle formulation changes (helper-lipid unsaturation or sterol substitution) can reroute protein adsorption and thereby organ targeting. Moreover, in vivo barcoding has clarified that while ApoE-mediated LDLR uptake is conserved across species for liver delivery, alternative, ApoE-independent pathways likely govern extrahepatic targeting for specific LNP chemistries [ , ].
A major milestone programmable tropism is the SORT strategy, where adding a small fraction of a permanently charged lipid rebalances surface charge/pKa and plasma protein interactions to redirect delivery to lung or spleen without sacrificing potency. Foundational studies demonstrated that cationic SORT lipids (e.g., DOTAP) bias toward lung, whereas anionic SORT molecules bias toward spleen, establishing a recipe-like approach to extrahepatic delivery [ ]. Mechanistic follow-up showed these changes alter corona composition and receptor engagement, consistent with tissue-specific uptake pathways [ ].
Route of administration is another programmable axis. After intramuscular dosing, LNPs traffic to draining lymph nodes and transfect local immune cells, a feature leveraged in vaccines [ ]. Conversely, pulmonary delivery is emerging for lung diseases: combinatorial lipid discovery plus nebulization-stable formulations markedly improved lung mRNA expression in rodents [ ]. Overviews from 2025 summarize formulation and aerosolization constraints unique to inhaled LNPs (e.g., shear and osmolarity), reinforcing that route-specific rules complement composition rules [ , ].
Chemical programmability also directs intracellular trafficking and tolerability. Biodegradable linkers (e.g., esters, disulfides) in ionizable lipids speed post-delivery clearance without reducing potency, now a core feature in lipid designs [ ]. Potency ties closely to the formulated LNP's apparent pKa; studies point to an optimal range (6.2–6.8) that ensures neutrality at physiological pH and protonation in endosomes for escape [ ]. Endosomal escape improves through lipids and helper mixes that trigger pH-dependent phase shifts and interact with raft-like domains, traits now studied at molecular scale to boost cytosolic delivery [ , ]. Additionally, in vivo performance reflects not only bulk composition but also the protein corona that forms within minutes after administration, which shapes opsonization, biodistribution, and cellular uptake. PEGylation moderates corona formation and retards aggregation, yet anti-PEG antibodies (pre-existing or vaccine-induced) can accelerate clearance, dampen expression, and in some settings heighten reactogenicity [ , ]. Emerging PEG substitutes aim to retain stealth while minimizing immunogenicity and improving redosing [ , , ]. summarizes different approaches to program LNPs.
 
2.3.

## Computational and rational design
Rational design has evolved from trial-and-error to data-guided cycles combining high-throughput in vivo screens with machine learning. DNA-barcoded nanoparticle libraries profile hundreds of LNP variants in one animal, uncovering structure-activity links and tissue preferences; recent studies used this to find degradable cationic ionizable lipids that enhance pulmonary mRNA delivery and clarify headgroup-linker roles [ ]. Early and recent platforms, including peptide-encoded mRNA barcodes and reverse-transcription barcode readouts, quantify functional delivery across tissues and immune cell types, accelerating identification of extrahepatic hits [ ]. This methodology has uncovered lung- and tumor-tropic formulations and clarified mechanistic distinctions between ApoE-dependent hepatic delivery and alternative pathways [ ].
AI models trained on chemistries and outcomes suggest ionizable lipids meeting biophysical criteria like packing and protonation, hitting the pKa sweet spot and speeding lead selection over manual methods [ ]. Reviews of this shift show how blending informatics with variables like lipid ratios, PEG content, and N/P ratios is transforming workflows from discovery to scale-up [ ]. Physics-based computation supports data methods by revealing hard-to-measure mechanisms. Atomistic and coarse-grained simulations track pH-dependent lipid shape and packing changes that drive RNA condensation, particle remodeling, and fusion [ ]. Tools now predict apparent pKa of ionizable lipids in LNPs, offering an early filter to prioritize candidates before synthesis [ ]. At formulation scale, optimization using organ-specific metrics (e.g., altering canonical components for extrahepatic bias) proves rational tweaks alone can alter tropism, often combined with corona-focused strategies considering protein adsorption and PEG shedding in biofluids [ ].

3.

## Formulation, synthesis, and characterization
Translating programmable designs into products hinges on an end-to-end control strategy that links materials, unit operations, and analytics to CQAs [ , ]. In practice, upstream assembly must be harmonized with downstream concentration, buffer exchange, and sterilizing filtration, where membrane selection, fouling mechanisms, and filter capacity materially affect recovery and reproducibility at scale [ , ]. Regulators are also converging on mRNA-specific expectations (e.g., platform considerations and change management for strain updates), which this section aligns with process choices [ , ].
Stability and presentation are integral to formulation rather than afterthoughts of characterization. Lyophilization is rapidly maturing, from carbohydrate cryo/lyoprotectants to dual-function trehalose strategies that preserve both colloidal integrity and mRNA chemistry, expanding storage from frozen to refrigerated while minimizing potency loss after reconstitution [ , ]. These choices couple back to synthesis (e.g., residual solvent, N/P, and particle morphology) and to downstream operations (e.g., TFF diafiltration windows), so the manufacturing section that follows emphasizes how to co-optimize assembly, purification, and presentation under a single, testable control strategy [ , ].
3.1.

## Methods for LNP preparation
Rapid solvent exchange between ethanolic lipid and aqueous RNA phases remains the standard route to assemble LNPs. Controlling local mixing times, Reynolds number, and flow-rate ratios (FRR) is key to tuning particle size and polydispersity. Microfluidic platforms like hydrodynamic flow focusing, herringbone or chaotic mixers, and combined advective-diffusive designs enable sub-millisecond mixing, reproducible size control, and library-scale screening for structure-activity mapping. Recent reviews highlight how device topology (e.g., staggered baffles versus serpentine channels) and FRR influence nucleation overgrowth, setting encapsulation and potency ranges [ , , ]. Parallelization has matured microfluidic production, running dozens of channels together with consistent CQAs across them. These advances support high-throughput composition exploration, including programmable fifth components, linked to in vitro or in vivo data, and fit with GMP unit operations [ , ]. shows an overview of bulk-mixing and microfluidic assembly routes.
 For industrial scale, confined impinging-jet and multi-inlet vortex mixers create turbulent micro-mixing that retains bench-scale physicochemical traits while achieving liters-per-hour output. Protocols describe two-jet confined impinging-jet (CIJ) and four-jet micro multi-inlet vortex mixer (μMIVM) geometries, plus residence time, jet velocity, and solvent quench or dilution to manage desolvation kinetics [ ]. After assembly, downstream steps include in-line dilution, ethanol removal and buffer exchange via TFF, sterile filtration, and aseptic fill-finish. Bioprocess studies note membrane selection, fouling control, and filter capacity as early scaling needs [ ]. Regulators favor continuous manufacturing with PAT for real-time checks of particle size, lipid concentration, and encapsulation, aligning with QbD for robust LNP processes [ ].

3.2.

## Formulation strategies and post-assembly modifications
Programmable LNP performance depends on composition and interfacial chemistry. Ionizable lipid structure (headgroup pKa, linker, tails) governs endosomal behavior; studies link apparent LNP pKa values to efficient mRNA release and tolerability, serving as a selection metric for new lipids [ ]. Helper lipids and sterols affect membrane phase and rigidity, while PEG-lipid mol% and anchor length influence stability, in vivo exchange, biodistribution, and blood protein corona [ ]. Programmability also stems from formulation stoichiometries like amine-to-phosphate (N/P) ratio and total lipid concentration, which impact encapsulation efficiency, particle growth, and potency; optimization ties these to CQAs in design-of-experiments (DoE) frameworks [ , ].
Beyond assembly, post-formation edits expand options. Ligand post-insertion is established: ligands (peptides, antibodies, glycans) conjugate to reactive PEG-lipids (e.g., maleimide-DSPE-PEG) and intercalate into preformed LNPs for multi-valent surface displays without core disruption [ ]. Composition can redirect organ tropism via SORT lipids as a fifth component, adjusting surface charge and serum interactions for extrahepatic RNA delivery after systemic dosing [ ]. Emerging chemistries add conditional features, such as acid-labile or degradable ionizable lipids that disassemble in endosomes for faster cargo release and reduced tissue persistence, complementing ligand approaches with stimulus-responsive layers [ ].

3.3.

## Comprehensive characterization approaches
A rigorous characterization toolkit translates programmability into predictable outcomes. Orthogonal assays measure size and polydispersity (dynamic light scattering, nanoparticle tracking analysis), surface potential (ζ-potential), morphology (cryo-TEM), encapsulation efficiency, residual solvents, and lipid composition or impurities. Reviews recommend panelized CQAs and methods, with recent work on assay best practices and pitfalls [ , ]. For RNA, RiboGreen dye methods estimate encapsulated versus free cargo and suit automation; refinements improve surfactant choice and full-length versus degraded RNA discrimination for better accuracy and comparability [ ]. Complementing bulk metrics, apparent LNP pKa via TNS or potentiometric titration acts as a surrogate for endosomal protonation, often paired with in silico predictions in lipid selection [ , ]. summarizes CQAs and related assays for LNPs.
 Programmable traits often emerge from mesoscale organization and bio-nano interactions, so higher-order tools matter. Cryo-EM or cryo-TEM and small-angle scattering (SAXS/SANS) reveal internal phases, blebs, and RNA placement, linked via automated mixing to efficacy across LNP libraries [ ]. In vivo barcoding enables multiplexed delivery quantification across tissues and species, screening programmed tropism before candidate advancement [ ]. Aligning characterization with ICH Q8(R2) Quality-by-Design (defining design space, PAT, risk-based controls) ensures robust scale-up and regulatory fit, turning programmable levers into controlled clinical attributes [ ]. summarizes the process of formation, synthesis, and characterization of programmable LNPs.
 While administration parameters are tightly controlled, pre-measurement handling of LNPs is far less standardized and can bias the very CQAs we report. Small changes in buffer identity, molarity, pH, and dilution prior to sizing or pKa assays can shift hydrodynamic size/PDI and even the apparent pKa readout (e.g., citrate strength, assay dye, and ionic strength effects), complicating cross-study comparisons [ ]. In addition, PEG-lipid desorption kinetics and particle concentration at the time of measurement can change surface presentation and potency proxies, yet these are rarely recorded alongside results [ , ]. Finally, storage and freeze–thaw history (cryoprotectant choice, cooling/thawing rates, time at 4–25 °C) can induce fusion, leakage, or aggregation before characterization—factors that are tightly controlled for dosing but often under-reported for analytics [ , ]. Key under-studied areas include: (i) pre-incubation with biological fluids (serum concentration, time, and source) immediately before analytics, which reshapes the protein corona and alters measured charge/size; (ii) method-dependent readouts (DLS/MALS/NTA) whose limits and artifacts under different concentrations are seldom benchmarked in parallel; and (iii) shear/filtration histories (needle gauge, filter type) that may perturb particle integrity. Systematic studies quantifying how these variables shift CQAs would enable comparability across labs and better translation of in-vitro metrics to in-vivo performance [ , ].

4.

## Applications for RNA therapeutics
LNPs provide a modular chassis that can encapsulate and protect diverse nucleic-acid cargos (mRNA/circRNA, siRNA/miRNA, ASO, pDNA, and CRISPR guides and mRNAs). As shown in , after systemic dosing, LNPs encounter a cascade of extracellular barriers: adsorption of serum proteins that shape biodistribution and opsonization; strong hepatic tropism driven in part by apolipoprotein-E binding and LDLR-mediated uptake; nuclease-rich fluids; and vascular extravasation constraints. Once internalized predominantly by endocytosis, most particles undergo endocytic recycling or lysosomal trafficking. Productive delivery hinges on rare endosomal escape events that release cargo to the cytosol which is a recognized efficiency bottleneck. Cytosolic cargos then act via translation (mRNA/circRNA) or RISC (siRNA/miRNA/ASO), whereas others (pDNA, some CRISPR templates) require nuclear import to access transcriptional or genome-editing machinery [ ].
 4.1.

## mRNA delivery applications
Preclinical mRNA-LNP platforms span systemic protein replacement, in situ immune engineering, and organ-selective transfection. High-throughput DNA-barcoded libraries allow dozens to hundreds of LNP variants to be ranked in a single animal, revealing structure-activity rules that guide potency and tropism, an approach that has uncovered chemotypes and compositions with superior in vivo mRNA expression over conventional trial-and-error screens [ ]. Concurrently, selective-organ-targeting strategies modulating formulation charge/phase behavior redirect mRNA expression from liver to spleen or lung without covalent ligands, establishing a design language for extrahepatic applications in vaccination and cell programming [ ]. These advances draw from a growing mRNA-LNP literature detailing uses from prophylactic and therapeutic vaccines to enzyme or hormone supplementation, defining the modality as a versatile, dose-programmable tool in vivo [ ].
In oncology and infectious disease, programmable mRNA-LNPs are tuned for cell-type engagement and exposure kinetics. mRNA cancer vaccines priming dendritic cells and driving cytotoxic T cells in tumor models show how formulation and route (i.m., i.v., intratumoral) can optimize antigen presentation and trafficking, while next-generation vaccines extend similar principles to influenza, RSV, and personalized neoantigens [ ]. Emerging targeting paradigms, including bispecific antibodies that transiently tether unmodified LNPs to disease markers in vivo, offer modular ways to concentrate mRNA expression in chosen tissues without permanent surface conjugation, aiding translation and manufacturing simplicity [ ]. In parallel, quantitative systems models capturing LNP recycling, re-uptake, and expression dynamics optimize dose and schedule for non-secreted mRNA therapies before wet-lab work, speeding preclinical-to-clinical decisions [ ].

4.2.

## siRNA and ASO delivery
For siRNA, LNPs remain the workhorse for hepatocyte delivery, yet preclinical efforts focus on overcoming liver tropism to reach immune, pulmonary, and endothelial targets. Reviews highlight that ApoE-LDLR interactions dominate passive liver uptake, motivating composition and charge re-tuning (including SORT-like additives) to shift distribution and enable extrahepatic knockdown in vivo [ ]. Mechanistic studies show that adding cationic helper lipids or adjusting helper-lipid chemistry can redirect LNPs toward lung endothelium and alter local transcriptional responses. These insights now guide design of siRNA LNPs that silence targets in the respiratory tract and beyond in murine models [ ]. Together, these data argue that extrahepatic siRNA delivery is a formulation-addressable problem rather than a payload limitation, with evidence that precise helper-lipid and ionizable-lipid choices can bias organ engagement [ ].
LNPs are also explored for ASOs, where encapsulation can reshape distribution and pharmacology in desirable ways. In the central nervous system, head-to-head preclinical comparisons demonstrate that LNP-encapsulated ASOs exhibit distinct tissue partitioning and immune effects relative to free ASOs, underscoring the need to co-optimize chemistry, dose, and route for each indication [ ]. Broader surveys of locked-nucleic-acid ASO (LNA-ASO) therapeutics emphasize that LNPs offer a complementary path to conjugate strategies (e.g., GalNAc) for tissues where receptor-mediated uptake is weak or absent, provided endosomal escape and tolerability are maintained [ ]. To that end, bioreducible/biodegradable ionizable lipids have enabled efficient ASO delivery in cells and animals while improving clearance [ ].

4.3.

## Gene editing
LNPs have enabled systemic, in-human genome editing by packaging CRISPR components as RNA so that target cells transiently express the nuclease and guide and execute edits in situ. The clearest proof is NTLA-2001, an LNP that co-delivers Cas9 mRNA with an sgRNA against TTR: a single intravenous dose produced rapid, deep, and durable reductions in circulating transthyretin in patients with hereditary transthyretin (hATTR), establishing clinical feasibility for liver-tropic LNP delivery of CRISPR and a therapeutic window for transient dosing [ ]. A complementary paradigm is base editing for cardiometabolic disease: VERVE-101 uses an LNP to carry mRNA encoding an adenine base editor and a guide that inactivates PCSK9 in hepatocytes, with early clinical and extensive non-human primate (NHP) data showing sustained LDL-C lowering after one infusion, which shows how programmable chemistry and payload selection can translate into durable pharmacology from a single LNP dose [ ]. Beyond the liver, lung-directed SORT LNPs have achieved in vivo genome editing in murine models, highlighting that organ-level targeting can be dialed in formulation space without covalent ligands and that extrahepatic CRISPR delivery is increasingly tractable with rational LNP design [ ].
Furthermore, base editing has emerged as a leading approach for single-nucleotide correction because deaminase-Cas9 nickase fusions affect predictable transition or transversion chemistries without introducing double-strand breaks, thereby reducing indel burden and DSB-associated toxicity relative to nuclease approaches [ ]. Translating base editors in vivo requires delivery systems that combine high encapsulation efficiency, tunable biodistribution and transient intracellular activity; lipid nanoparticles (LNPs) have enabled several recent breakthroughs by enabling mRNA- or RNP-based delivery to target tissues. For example, ionizable LNP libraries screened for perinatal brain tropism achieved efficient adenine-base-editor mRNA delivery and biochemical rescue in neonatal disease models, demonstrating feasibility of correcting CNS mutations via minimally immunogenic, non-viral LNP platforms [ ]. Optimization of ionizable lipid chemistry, PEG-lipid content and microfluidic formulation has further improved endosomal escape and reduced the lipid dose required for therapeutic editing, while RNP-encapsulation strategies reduce exposure time and off-target activity compared with sustained expression modalities [ , ]. Despite these advances, bystander deamination, transcriptomic off-targets and the challenge of robust extra-hepatic delivery remain key obstacles; resolving these will require paired progress in editor engineering and LNP design to achieve clinical-grade potency and safety [ , ].
Prime editing extends the therapeutic repertoire by enabling precise substitutions, short insertions and deletions through a Cas9-nickase–reverse-transcriptase fusion guided by a pegRNA, obviating the need for donor DNA or DSBs and thus increasing applicability in non-dividing cells [ ]. Crucially, prime editing's complexity (large protein payload plus structured pegRNA) places stringent demands on delivery chemistry. Co-formulation of chemically modified pegRNAs with prime-editor mRNA into LNPs achieved the first demonstrations of reproducible in vivo prime editing in mouse liver and revealed critical variables, pegRNA chemical modification, dosing interval and ionizable-lipid identity that determine efficacy and tolerability in repeated administrations [ ]. Parallel work with RNP-LNPs shows that stabilizing editors as protein–RNA complexes and tuning LNP composition can markedly enhance delivery efficiency while limiting duration of activity and off-target risk, suggesting a pathway toward safer clinical translation of prime editors [ ]. Nevertheless, prime editing efficiency is often lower and more context-dependent than base editing, and pegRNA design, intracellular stability and immune responses to repeated dosing remain open problems that must be addressed through iterative pegRNA chemistry, editor evolution and LNP optimization [ , ].

4.4.

## Emerging applications and critical analysis
Programmability is extending the editing toolbox and the reachable cell types while sharpening the engineering constraints that determine translational success. First, prime editing is progressing toward LNP delivery: formulation/analytical workflows have identified LNPs that encapsulate prime-editor mRNA together with appropriate guide RNAs and support intracellular prime-editing activity, suggesting a fully transient, non-viral route as LNP composition and dose are optimized [ ]. Second, targeted LNPs are opening hematology: CD117-directed particles deliver mRNA to hematopoietic stem cells in vivo, enabling gene-editing strategies for blood disorders without ex vivo manipulation or myeloablation, a step change in scope that underscores how receptor-aware design can reprogram tropism beyond hepatocytes [ ]. Against these opportunities, endosomal escape remains a principal bottleneck: quantitative and mechanistic analyses indicate that only a small fraction of internalized LNPs release cargo into the cytosol, motivating design rules that couple apparent pKa, helper-lipid selection, and membrane phase behavior to maximize productive escape while maintaining tolerability in repeat-dose settings [ ]. summarizes applications of LNPs for targeted RNA delivery.
 

5.

## Clinical translation
NP-delivered RNA therapeutics have advanced from isolated proofs of concept to a clinically established modality spanning disease prevention, protein knockdown, and in certain contexts one-time in vivo editing. A key milestone was patisiran, an intravenous LNP-siRNA for hATTR amyloidosis; in the phase 3 APOLLO trial, patisiran showed marked improvements in neuropathy scores and quality-of-life over placebo, proving that repeated systemic LNP dosing can provide durable benefit in chronic disease [ ]. The platform's scalability and reproducibility faced population-level testing with COVID-19 mRNA vaccines. Large randomized trials confirmed high efficacy and acceptable safety for two LNP-formulated vaccines, mRNA-1273 and BNT162b2, validating manufacturing, stability, and pharmacovigilance for LNP products globally [ ]. More recently, LNP-mRNA entered respiratory markets: in older adults, the RSV prefusion F mRNA vaccine (mRNA-1345) met phase 3 efficacy and safety goals, showing LNP-mRNA competitiveness beyond pandemics and supporting LNPs as a reusable backbone over bespoke chemistry [ ]. Together, these precedents create a mature landscape where programmable tweaks to LNP composition and surface chemistry pair with indication-specific pharmacology and trial design. provides a non-exhaustive overview of ongoing clinical trials that use programmable or targeted LNPs.
 Despite quick progress, broad clinical adoption of programmable LNPs relies on handling immunologic risks, applying QbD principles, and linking biomarkers to mechanisms. Immunologically, LNPs can activate innate pathways; when PEG-lipids are used, they may interact with pre-existing or induced anti-PEG antibodies. Recent overviews explain how these responses affect reactogenicity and possibly pharmacokinetics, guiding approaches like PEG-free designs, other stealth polymers, or spaced repeat dosing [ , ]. Complement activation-related pseudoallergy (CARPA) remains a rare issue for liposomal and LNP products; studies clarify roles of complement pathways and surface charge/PEGylation in acute reactions, backing monitored premedication in at-risk cases [ ]. Beyond this, CMC rigor is vital: reviews highlight QbD development that defines a design space tying critical attributes (ionizable lipid, ratios, PEG content) and processes (mixing, dilution, filtration) to quality standards, with analytical tools ensuring scalable production. Programs incorporating pharmacodynamic biomarkers, like serum TTR for targeted agents or immunogenicity measures for vaccines, plus dose-response modeling in early trials, better convert programmable features such as tropism, escape, and ligand display into efficient efficacy and tolerability, aligning design with outcomes [ ].
LNP-encapsulated mRNA vaccines and traditional antigen-based (protein/subunit or inactivated) vaccines occupy complementary translatability landscapes: LNP-mRNA enables ultra-rapid antigen design, strong in situ antigen expression and germinal-center induction that can yield high neutralizing: binding antibody ratios, and the LNP itself contributes adjuvant activity—features that together lowered the development bar for COVID-19 vaccines and offer clear advantages for rapid outbreak response [ , ]. Protein/antigen vaccines retain operational strengths—established manufacturing, long regulatory pedigrees, and generally lower reactogenicity per dose—and can achieve comparable immunogenicity when paired with modern adjuvants or LNP-based adjuvant strategies [ , ]. Translationally, LNP platforms must therefore clear a minimal bar defined not only by non-inferior protective efficacy but also by acceptable reactogenicity, thermostability/manufacturability, and dose-sparing (lipid burden) that justify their logistical costs [ , ]. Analogously, small-molecule inhibitors offer oral bioavailability, broad tissue exposure and matured CMC pathways, whereas antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) provide sequence specificity for “undruggable” targets but rely on effective delivery (often requiring LNPs or conjugates), repeat dosing, and clear biomarker-to-clinical benefit translation to compete with small molecules [ , ]. Framing these modality-specific minimal bars in the Clinical Translation section will help readers evaluate when LNP innovations truly add translational value [ , , , ].

## Future perspectives and limitations
Programmable LNPs are shifting beyond liver bias to true tissue and cell specificity, driven by composition control and data-rich screening. Selective organ targeting links lipid structure, corona dynamics, and charge distribution to biodistribution rules, offering ways to adjust tropism without custom ligands [ ]. High-throughput in vivo barcoding measures localization and functional expression in one test, accelerating hypothesis to lead and enabling multi-parameter optimization [ ]. Peptide-encoded reporters with pooled LNP libraries expose route-dependent immune tropism and composition-activity maps hidden in single studies [ ]. As screens expand and pair with machine learning, programmable LNPs can evolve toward extrahepatic, cell-restricted delivery consistent across species and doses.
Manufacturing and analytics will decide if programmability scales up. Continuous or hybrid workflows focus on high-shear mixing with in-line dilution, solvent removal, and real-time PAT, supporting bench-to-plant transfer without attribute drift [ ]. Scalable microfluidic and impinging-jet platforms maintain size, encapsulation, and potency while increasing productivity markedly [ ]. Standards are aligning, with reviews detailing core assays for mRNA vaccines and therapeutics to enable consistent lot release across sites [ , , ]. These trends foster stable platforms for indication-specific compositions without reworking processes or controls.
Immunologic and long-term safety are key as programs advance to redosing, extrahepatic targets, and combinations. Anti-PEG antibodies appear in some individuals, shortening circulation, changing biodistribution, or causing infusion reactions. In vaccine settings, antigen-specific responses may see little impact, but non-vaccine cargos could be more affected [ ]. PEG alternatives like poly(2-oxazoline) polymers and zwitterionic or biomimetic headgroups reduce immunogenicity while keeping transfection and tunable corona formation [ , , ]. Future adaptive formulations that change stealth chemistries or ligand densities between doses, linked to patient immunomonitoring, may stabilize exposure and safety in chronic or multi-agent therapy. Tackling these systematically matches the importance of better endosomal escape or organ tropism for translation.
LNP platforms face interlinked biological challenges that must be solved before broad clinical deployment of programmable RNA therapeutics. First, inherent and formulation-dependent activation of innate immunity (TLR and complement pathways) can reduce potency, skew pharmacodynamics, and drive reactogenicity, effects that differ between vaccine and therapeutic dosing regimens and between lipid chemistries [ , ]. PEGylation, while critical for colloidal stability and circulation time, provokes anti-PEG antibodies in a subset of subjects and can alter biodistribution, accelerate clearance, or produce hypersensitivity on repeat dosing, an especially important barrier for chronic or multi-dose gene-editing approaches [ , , ]. Also, the strong liver tropism of canonical ionizable LNPs and the formation of a dynamic protein corona constrain efficient extra-hepatic access (e.g., lung, CNS, muscle, hematopoietic compartments) required for many genetic diseases; achieving predictable, tissue-selective targeting without increasing off-target accumulation or toxicity remains a central unmet need [ , ]. Resolving these biological problems will require coordinated advances in lipid chemistry, stealthing strategies beyond PEG, and rational targeting informed by in vivo protein-corona and immune-profiling [ , ].
On the other hand, manufacturing, stability, and quality-control hurdles are equally critical impediments to translation. LNPs are sensitive to process variables (mixing, headspace, shear and air-liquid interfaces) that can alter size, polydispersity, encapsulation efficiency and ultimately in vivo potency; these sensitivities complicate scale-up and lot-to-lot reproducibility for clinical manufacture [ , ]. Cold-chain dependence and susceptibility to oxidation or mechanical stress (freeze–thaw, light, agitation) impose strict handling constraints and limit global access unless robust lyophilization or thermally stable formulations are developed [ ]. Moreover, regulatory readiness demands standardized analytical methods that correlate orthogonal physicochemical metrics with biological potency (encapsulation, integrity, protein expression, immunogenic impurities), and process platforms that multiplex throughput without sacrificing product fidelity, microfluidic parallelization and SCALAR-type chips illustrate scalable paths but require integrated downstream purification and in-line QC to meet GMP [ , ]. Addressing these engineering and regulatory gaps (analytics, stability engineering, continuous manufacturing) is essential to move programmable LNP-enabled editors and RNA therapeutics from promising preclinical demonstrations to safe, equitable clinical use [ ].

7.

## Conclusion
Programmable LNPs have moved RNA delivery beyond a liver default to an engineerable system. By tuning composition and surface chemistry, developers can achieve tissue selectivity, intracellular efficacy, and acceptable tolerability. Actionable rules include targeting a formulated pKa, using degradable linkers to speed clearance, adjusting helper lipid and sterol to favor pH-responsive phases, modulating PEG-anchor length and content to balance stability and cell engagement, and adding ligands or SORT lipids when rerouting biodistribution. Data-rich discovery that combines barcoded in vivo screens, machine learning, and mechanistic simulation can replace trial and error. Manufacturing should apply QbD and PAT to tie materials and process to clinical CQAs. Clinically, these levers should be paired with indication-specific pharmacology, prospective biomarkers, and redosing strategies that consider PEG alternatives. The main gaps are endosomal escape and immune liability. Treated as a platform, LNPs can extend RNA medicines beyond the liver to enable one-time editing, repeat modulation, and personalized vaccination at scale.
