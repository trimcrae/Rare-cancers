## Summary

## Background
Unexplained miscarriage (UM) highly occurs and largely limits human reproduction. Cellular senescence is a ubiquitous process that is associated with many human diseases. Increasing global usage and wear of tyres result in worldwide pollution of 6PPD (an antioxidant in tyres) in various environments and even in pregnant women's bodies. Importantly, whether 6PPD exposure might cause senescence to induce unexplained miscarriage is largely unknown and should be urgently explored.

## Methods
We combined case–control study using a UM case–control group and in vitro functional assays using mouse model and human trophoblast HTR-8/SVneo cells. The associations between environmental exposure to 6PPD and cell senescence were explored. Trophoblast cell senescence and the BAZ1B-mediated ubiquitination degradation of P21 were also investigated.

## Findings
Higher urinary 6PPD levels are closely associated with villous tissue senescence and miscarriage (n = 50). 6PPD-exposed mouse model further confirms that exposure to high doses of 6PPD causes placental senescence (≥12 mg/kg/d) to induce mouse miscarriage (≥36 mg/kg/d). In cellular mechanisms, 6PPD exposure down-regulates BAZ1B expression levels, suppresses BAZ1B-mediated ubiquitination degradation of P21, and thus up-regulates P21 protein levels. Subsequently, the up-regulated P21 results in cell senescence, which further induces miscarriage. The mechanisms in 6PPD-exposed trophoblast cells are consistent with those in 6PPD-exposed mouse placental tissues and in UM vs HC women villous tissues. Therapeutic down-regulation of p21, supplement with murine Baz1b, or direct suppression of senescence could effectively reduce placental senescence and suppress mouse miscarriage in the 6PPD-exposed mouse models.

## Interpretation
This study not only discovers new health risks of 6PPD exposure on miscarriage, explores potential pathogenesis and biological mechanisms in 6PPD-induced miscarriage, but also provides potential biological targets for treatment against miscarriage, providing a new example about the crosstalk among environmental 6PPD exposure, cellular senescence, and female reproductive health.

## Research in context

## Evidence before this study
Unexplained miscarriage (UM) highly occurs and largely limits human reproduction. Cellular senescence is a ubiquitous process that is associated with many human diseases. Increasing global usage and wear of tyres result in worldwide pollution of 6PPD (an antioxidant in tyres) in various environments and even in pregnant women's bodies. Importantly, whether 6PPD exposure might cause senescence to induce unexplained miscarriage is largely unknown and should be urgently explored.

## Added value of this study
We conduct a UM case–control group, 6PPD-exposed mouse model, and cell-based assays, and find that 6PPD exposure increases UM risk. 6PPD-exposed mouse model further confirms that 6PPD exposure causes placental senescence to induce mouse miscarriage. Mechanistically, 6PPD down-regulates BAZ1B levels, suppresses BAZ1B-mediated ubiquitination degradation of P21, and thus up-regulates P21 levels.

## Implications of all the available evidence
This study provides insights into 6PPD exposure as a miscarriage risk by characterising its pathogenesis, identifying therapeutic targets, and mapping the crosstalk between environmental 6PPD and reproductive health through biological mechanisms.

## Introduction

## Unexplained miscarriage
Approximately 15%–25% pregnant women end with miscarriage and 1%–5% suffer from recurrent miscarriage in the world, which greatly limit global reproduction. Moreover, 41.2% patients with recurrent miscarriage have long-term anxiety, 8.6% have severe depression, and 1.4% even threaten life safety. The causes of miscarriage are very complex, including genetic factors, chromosomal abnormalities, infections, prothrombotic states, abnormal uterine anatomy, endocrine, metabolic and reproductive immune diseases. However, there are still 50% miscarriage with unknown causes, which are generally termed as unexplained miscarriage (UM). , As the key component of placenta, trophoblast cells play vital roles in embryo implantation and healthy pregnancy. , The dysfunctions of trophoblast cells always induce miscarriage. To explore what and how might cause trophoblast cell dysfunctions to induce miscarriage is essential for understanding the pathogenesis of unexplained miscarriage.

## 6PPD exposure and miscarriage
Increasing studies have demonstrated that many environmental pollutants might induce miscarriages, such as polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon, , , , , , , , nanoplastics, , copper exposure, lithium exposure, , bisphenol F , bisphenol B , hypoxia, ciprofloxacin, or disinfection by-products of tap water, by causing trophoblast cell senescence, ferroptosis, pyroptosis, cuproptosis, or apoptosis, or by supressing migration/invasion or proliferation. Nonetheless, other novel pollutants that might also induce miscarriages should still be urgently explored. Currently, vehicle tyre-related pollution has attracted significant global attention. Globally, approximately 2.3 billion tyres are produced and used annually, and run trillions of miles annually, leading to widespread tyre wear particle pollution. In tyres, N-(1,3-dimethylbutyl)-N′-phenyl-p-phenylenediamine (6PPD) is a synthetic antioxidant to prevent rubber cracking and oxidative degradation, , with a mass ratio as high as 0.4%–2% in tyres. As a result, 6PPD in tyre wear particles can spread to different ecosystems worldwide through surface runoff and atmospheric transport. , Globally, 6PPD has been found in soil, road dust, urban runoff water, and snow. The average content of 6PPD has been detected as 0.21–2.71 μg/L in urban runoff water, 0.82–6.3 pg/m3 in air particles, 31.4–831 ng/g in roadside soil, 4.1–429 ng/g in road dust and underground parking lots, and 0.75 μg/g in lettuce. Unavoidably, 6PPD enter human body through multiple routes such as ingestion, inhalation, and dermal contact. , , The median level of 6PPD has been detected as 0.057–0.13 ng/mL in human serum samples, , 0.068 ng/mL in pregnant woman urinary samples, and 0.018 ng/mL in ordinary adult urinary samples. Emerging studies have shown that 6PPD exposure can cause embryonic developmental toxicity, neurotoxicity, , eye developmental disorders, , hormone alteration, and cardiac damage in zebrafish. A mouse model has confirmed that 6PPD can transport cross placenta and suppress mouse embryo development. These preliminary studies have raised concerns about the potential effects of 6PPD exposure on adverse pregnant outcomes. Herein, whether 6PPD exposure might cause placental dysfunctions to induce miscarriage is completely unknown and should be urgently explored.

## Cellular senescence
Cellular senescence is a ubiquitous process in which cell cycle is permanently arrested. Cellular senescence is associated with many human diseases, such as brain diseases (e.g. Alzheimer’s disease, cerebral haemorrhage, Parkinson’s disease), tumours, and cardiovascular diseases. , , Senescence is characterised as higher SA-β-gal (senescence-associated β-galactosidase) activity, higher protein levels of cell cycle inhibitors P21, and higher levels of SASPs (pro-inflammatory senescence-associated secretory phenotypes). , , It has been reported that the levels of senescence are significantly higher in placental or decidual tissues of patients with miscarriage relative to those in healthy controls. , In our recent study, ciprofloxacin exposure induces miscarriage by causing placental cell senescence through activating TRIM21-mediated MFF degradation. However, whether 6PPD exposure might cause senescence to induce unexplained miscarriage is completely unknown and should be fully explored.
Therefore, as far as we known, the association, causation, and underlying mechanisms among 6PPD exposure, senescence, and unexplained miscarriage are still largely uncovered. For this aim, we collect urine and villous tissue samples from patients with unexplained miscarriage (UM) and healthy control (HC) group (n = 50), construct 6PPD-exposed mouse models, and use 6PPD-exposed human trophoblast HTR-8/SVneo cell models. We find that exposure to high levels of 6PPD causes senescence to induce miscarriage by suppressing BAZ1B-mediated ubiquitination degradation of P21. Collectively, this study provides new insights in pathological and biological mechanisms of 6PPD exposure-induced trophoblast cell senescence and miscarriage, which deserves attention in the field of environment and female reproductive health fields.

## Collection of women villous tissues and urine samples
Placental villous tissue samples and urine samples were collected from 50 patients with unexplained miscarriage (UM group) and 50 women who had personally elective miscarriage to terminate their unwanted pregnancies (healthy control (HC) group) between 25 and 30 years old, as the similar methods described previously. , , Any woman in both UM and HC group with one of the following features was excluded, including uterine abnormalities, abnormal karyotype, autoimmune abnormality, antiphospholipid antibody syndrome, endocrine or metabolic diseases, polycystic ovarian syndrome, pre-eclampsia or eclampsia, as the method described previously. , , The criteria of pregnancy was considered by the professional doctors according to factors such as the missed menstruation, β-hCG levels, early pregnancy factors, and so on. Miscarriage is defined as the loss of an embryo or foetus weight <500 g or before 20 weeks of gestation, including spontaneous loss or personally elective miscarriage. HC group had previous successful pregnancies. HC and UM women did not received any treatment. The characteristic of these HC and UM women were listed in , including age, gestational day, BMI, education, household income, smoking, and drinking in the period of three month before miscarriage operation. All the information was obtained from medical records. A piece of villous tissue with dimensions of approximate 2 × 0.5 × 0.5 cm3 was manually dissected from the foetal side of placenta and was cleared of maternal decidua. After washing with sterile saline, villous tissue samples were immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 °C before protein extraction. A single-spot of midstream urine sample was collected from these women using glass containers on the same day before surgical miscarriage. The experiment protocols have been authorised by the Eighth Affiliated Hospital, Sun Yat-sen University. Written informed consent has been signed before the study.

## Sample preparation and instrumental analysis
Urine samples were prepared by salting-out assisted liquid–liquid extraction. , Briefly, 1 mL thawed urine sample containing 0.33 g/mL NaCl was transferred to a 10 mL glass centrifuge tube and spiked with 20 ng surrogate standard of 13C6-6PPD-Q. After equilibration for 30 min, 0.2 mL of 1 mM glutathione solution was added as it could suppress potential oxidative transformations of 6PPD during sample preparation. Then, the sample was extracted with 3 mL acetonitrile by vortexing for 10 min and ultrasonication for 10 min. Following centrifugation at 4500 rpm (2000 × g) for 5 min, the supernatant was transferred to another precleaned glass tube. The extraction operations were repeated twice. Then, the combined supernatants were concentrated to 2 mL under gentle nitrogen and filtered with a 0.22 μm filter member for instrumental analysis. The quantitative analysis of 6PPD was conducted on a ExionLC AC ultrafast liquid chromatography interfaced with an AB Sciex Triple Quad 5500 mass spectrometer (LC-MS/MS, Applied Biosystems, Framingham, MA). In every batch of 20 samples, one procedural blank was analysed by passage of ultrapure water (in lieu of urine) through the entire analytical procedure. 6PPD was not found in the procedural blanks. The sampling process also did not introduce contamination, as no target 6PPD was detected in the field blanks. Urine samples were all analysed within 2 weeks of collection to avoid potential oxidative transformation of 6PPD during sample storage, and glutathione solution was added prior to sample extraction to suppress potential oxidative transformation during sample analysis. The average recoveries of 6PPD in spiked urine (n = 5) were in the range of 70–75%, while the average matrix effects were in the range of 72–81%. The method quantification limit (MQL), defined as ten times the signal-to-noise (S/N) ratio, was 0.012 ng/mL for 6PPD. 13C6-6PPD-Q had similar recoveries (average 71–76%) and matrix effects (average 74–84%) relative to 6PPD and was used for the quantification of 6PPD. Data were acquired using Analyst 1.6.3 (Applied Biosystems). Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 23.0 (IBM Corporation).

## Mouse models and experimental design
6PPD-exposed mouse models were constructed as the similar methods described previously. , , Pregnant C57BL/6 mice (Charles River Company, Beijing, China) were randomly divided into different groups (n = 6 per group) and the age distribution was consistent in all experimental and control groups. The experiments were blinded to data analysts. Mice were free access to excessive food and water. Females and males were caged together at a ratio of 4:1. The appearance of a vaginal copulation plug was considered as day 1 (D1) of pregnancy, which was further confirmed by weighting mouse body every day. The exposure doses of 6PPD were selected based on the results in literature. To explore the effects of the real environmental exposure dose (REED) of 6PPD on miscarriage, we calculated the REED of 6PPD in mouse models. A study showed that the urine concentrations of 6PPDQ (the oxidant of 6PPD) in 4 mg/kg/d 6PPDQ-exposed mice by oral gavage were similar to those detected in human; whereas the urine concentrations of 6PPD in 4 mg/kg/d 6PPD-exposed mice were two orders of magnitude higher than those in human. Therefore, the exposure dose of 4 mg/kg/day 6PPD was considered within environmentally relevant concentrations, which was considered as 1-fold REED in this study. To reduce the dose, we also use 0.1-fold REED (0.4 mg/kg/day) of 6PPD to explore the low-dose effects. In another study, C57BL/6 mice that were exposed to 100 mg/kg/d 6PPD by oral gavage for 6 consecutive weeks caused hepatotoxicity in mice. Considering the doses used in literature, short lifespan of mice, and the feasibility of mouse model assays, we chose 0, 0.4, 4, 12, 36, or 108 mg/kg/d 6PPD in corn oil in mouse models, with an equal volume of corn oil as control group, corresponding to 0 (control), 0.1-, 1-, 3-, 9- or 27-fold REED of 6PPD ( ).
Model 1, 6PPD-exposed pregnant mouse model. Pregnant mice were orally administered with 0, 0.4, 4, 12, 36, or 108 mg/kg/day 6PPD in corn oil from D1 to D13. Model 2, miscarriage intervention model with anti-senescence. Pregnant mice were orally administered with 108 mg/kg/day 6PPD in corn oil and/or orally administered with 5 mg/kg/3d dasatinib (D) and 50 mg/kg/3d quercetin (Q) in 10% polyethylene glycol-400 (PEG-400) or intraperitoneally injected with 10 mg/kg/3d AS-p21 (knockdown of mouse p21, ) with AS–NC (siRNA control) as control in saline from D1 to D13. ① control group, ② 6PPD group, ③ 6PPD and PEG-400 group, ④ 6PPD and D + Q group, ⑤ 6PPD and AS–NC group, and ⑥ 6PPD and AS-p21 group. Model 3, miscarriage intervention model with treatment with pcDNA3.1-Baz1b (Baz1b overexpression). Pregnant mice were orally administered with 108 mg/kg/day 6PPD in corn oil and also intraperitoneally injected with 10 mg/kg/3d pcDNA3.1-BAZ1B (plasmid for overexpression of mouse Baz1b) with pcDNA3.1 (empty vector control) as control in saline from D1 to D13. ① 6PPD and pcDNA3.1 group, ② 6PPD and pcDNA3.1-BAZ1B group.
On D14, all mice in three models were euthanised by intraperitoneal injection of pentobarbital sodium (100 mg/kg). The operation and humane endpoints complied with The ARRIVE guidelines 2.0. Embryo resorption was identified by smaller size or darker colour compared to viable and pink healthy embryos. The miscarriage rate for each mouse was calculated as (the number of embryo adsorption)/(total number of normal embryos + the number of embryo adsorption). The average miscarriage rate for each group was also determined. Proteins were extracted from a random placenta of each mouse for Western blot analysis. This animal experiment was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Eighth Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University.

## Cell culture
Human trophoblast HTR-8/SVneo cells (immortalised by SVneo virus, purchased from Hunan Fenghui Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Cat. No. CL0164; RRID: CVCL_7162) were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium containing 10% foetal bovine serum (FBS) (Gibco, Invitrogen), 100 units/mL penicillin, and 100 ng/mL streptomycin at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2. To explore the effects of real environmental exposure dose (REED) of 6PPD on trophoblast cell functions, we calculated the actual exposure dose of 6PPD in human body and also considered the doses used in other cell models. 6PPD levels were detected as 0.068 ng/mL in urine samples of pregnant women under real environmental exposure. Exposure to 50 nM 6PPD resulted in endometrial cell dysfunctions. Exposure to 10 μM 6PPD activated DNA damage response and suppressed cardiac contraction genes in human heart organoids. In another study using endometrial cells, exposure to 0.5 μM 6PPD activated ERα transcriptional activity. Based on these literature results and our pre-experiments, in this study, human trophoblast cells were treated with 0, 0.01, 0.5, 1, or 2 μM 6PPD at 37 °C for 48 h.

## Proteomic analysis
Two pairs of random UM vs HC women villous tissues (0.1 g), 108 vs 0 mg/kg/d 6PPD-exposed mouse placental tissues (0.1 g), and 2 vs 0 μM 6PPD-exposed human trophoblast HTR-8/SVneo cells (5 × 106 cells) were used for proteomic sequencing (Novogene, China). Proteomic sequencing was performed on Orbitrap Q Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Briefly, proteins were extracted, and their concentrations were determined by Bradford protein assay (Bio-Rad, USA). After proteins were digested with Trypsin Gold (Promega), shot-gun proteomics analysis was performed using an EASY-nLC 1200 UHPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific) coupled with an Orbitrap Q Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) in the data-dependent acquisition mode. Protein differential analysis was performed based on protein quantification results. Proteins with significantly altered abundance between comparison groups were screened and subjected to Student's t-test to obtain P-values. To reduce the false positive rate, multiple hypothesis test correction was applied to the P-values, and the Benjamini and Hochberg (BH) method was used to calculate the false discovery rate (FDR). The differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) with differences >1.2- or 1.5-fold and p < 0.05 were generated from read counts using the online bioinformatic platform provided by Novogene (https://magic.novogene.com/customer/main#/loginNew). The DEPs were searched in the UniProt Mus musculus (Mus_musculus_uniprot_2022_1_27.fasta, 86,492 sequences) database and Homo sapiens (homo_sapiens_uniprot_2022_1_27.fasta, 203,711 sequences) database to determine their genome loci. These DEPs were used for gene ontology (GO) and KEGG analysis to generate GO and KEGG plots, respectively. , 

## IP-MS analysis of P21-bound proteins
P21-bound proteins were analysed by EASY-nLC 1200 UHPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific) coupled with Q Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) by Novogene Genetics company (Beijing, China), as the methods previously described. In brief, the proteins bound to P21 were separated and digested with trypsin (10 ng/μL) overnight at 37 °C. The samples were desalted, lyophilised, and re-dissolved in loading buffer A (0.1% formic acid, wt/vol). Subsequently, the samples were eluted and separated on a reverse-phase column with loading buffer B (0.1% FA in 80% acetonitrile, wt/vol), and then injected into the mass spectrometer at a constant flow rate of 600 nL/min. The eluted peptides were analysed by mass spectrometer by scanning m/z from 300 to 1500 in a positive polarity mode with spray voltage of 2.3 kV. Primary mass spectrometry resolution was set to 60,000 (at 200 m/z). The 40 most abundant precursor ions from full MS scan were selected for analysis at a resolution of 15,000 (at 200 m/z). Raw files of mass spectrometry were identified by MaxQuant (www.maxquant.org). Proteins were identified by searching based on UniProt homo_sapiens database (www.uniprot.org). 

## Cell transfection
Empty vector pcDNA3.1 (Catalogue No. V790-20) was purchased from Thermo Fisher Scientific Company. cDNAs that were used for construction of overexpression plasmid of P21 (pcDNA3.1-P21) and BAZ1B (pcDNA3.1-BAZ1B) were synthesised and constructed by Addgene. The corresponding RNA sequences were obtained from National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) database (Gene Bank, Homo sapiens, GRCh38.p14; sequences in ). Empty vector pcDNA3.1 was used as negative control. Si-P21, si-BAZ1B, and si-NC (negative control) were customised by Thermo Fisher (sequences in ). HTR-8/SVneo cells (1 × 106 cells/well) were seeded in 6-well plates and cultured to 80% confluence. Trophoblast cells were transfected with 1 μg plasmids or 50 nM siRNAs in turbofect transfection reagent (R0531, Thermo Scientific) for 24 h according to the manufacturer's protocols. The transfection efficiencies were validated by Western blotting.

## Cell viability
Cell viability was evaluated using a Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK8, ab228554, Abcam, Cambridge, UK). Cells (5 × 103 cells per well, three replicates per group) were seeded in 96-well plates and treated with 0, 0.5, 1, or 2 μM 6PPD for 0, 12, 24, 36, or 48 h. In addition, HTR-8/SVneo cells (5 × 103 cells per well, three replicates per group) were seeded in 96-well plates, firstly transfected with 1 μg plasmid or 50 nM siRNA, and then co-treated with 0 or 2 μM of 6PPD for 0, 12, 24, 36, or 48 h. Subsequently, 10 μL incubation reagent and 90 μL DMEM/F12 medium were added according to the manufacturer's protocol. The 96-well plates were completely covered with aluminium foil to avoid light. The plates were then incubated at 37 °C for 1 h. Absorbance was detected at 450 nm using a microplate reader (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA) with cell culture medium as the background. Cell proliferation was expressed as changes in absorbance at 450 nm. All experiments were repeated three times.

## β-galactosidase activity assay
β-galactosidase activity was determined using senescence-associated β-galactosidase (SA-β-gal) staining kit (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China). Women villous tissues, mouse placental tissues, and HTR-8/SVneo cells were used for β-galactosidase activity assays. The frozen sections of women villous tissues or mouse placental tissues and cells were fixed in 1.0 mL fixation buffer at room temperature for 15 min. The frozen sections or cells were incubated with the staining mixture overnight at 37 °C. On the next day, the sections or cells were washed with PBS, and the staining sections or cells were observed under a microscope. SA-β-Gal-positive cells (bluish-green colour) were counted in 15 random fields and expressed as % of total positive intensity for tissues or % of total cells.

## Quantitative reverse transcription PCR (RT-qPCR)
Total RNAs were extracted from various HTR-8/SVneo cells using Trizol (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, USA). , RNA quality and quantity were assessed using a NanoDrop 2000 UV spectrophotometer (Thermo Fischer Scientific, Waltham, USA); and RNA integrity number (RIN) were assessed on an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies, Palo Alto, CA, USA). The concentration of RNA was in 500–1500 ng/μL and the RIN ≥9. The RNA purity was high as the A260/280 values were in 1.8–2.2. The isolated RNAs (800 ng) were converted into cDNAs using the first-strand cDNA synthesis kit (Invitrogen). Then, cDNAs were amplified using a 20-μL SYBR Green Supermix (Takara, Kyoto, Japan). The RT-qPCR program was described as follows: pre-denaturation at 95 °C for 30 s, cycling reaction at 95 °C for 10 s for 40 cycles, dissolution at 95 °C for 15 s, 60 °C for 60 s, and then 95 °C for 15 s. The sequences of the specific primers were shown in . The mRNA levels of GAPDH were not changed in our experiments and were used as normalisation internal standard for all mRNA detection. All experiments were replicated thrice. The levels of mRNAs were expressed as 2−ΔΔCt, where Ct was cycle threshold, ΔCt = testing gene (Ct) - average GAPDH (Ct), and ΔΔCt = sample group Δ(Ct) - average control group Δ(Ct).

## Western blot analysis
Total proteins were extracted from human trophoblast HTR-8/SVneo cells, women villous tissues, and mouse placental tissues using RIPA lysis buffer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and quantified using Pierce BCA Protein Assay Kit (Pierce). Proteins (10–30 μg/well, equal amounts within group but different amounts among groups for better comparison) were separated on 8% or 12% SDS-PAGE gel and transferred on an equilibrated polyvinylidene difluoride membrane (PVDF, Amersham Biosciences, Buckinghamshire, UK). After blocking with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA, Sigma-Aldrich) in TBST (10 mM Tris-HCl, 150 mM NaCl, and 0.1% Tween 20) for 1 h at room temperature, the membrane was incubated with primary antibody overnight at 4 °C. Primary antibodies included anti-P21 (82669-2-RR, Proteintech, 1:800 dilution, RRID: AB_3086498), anti-BAZ1B (68361-1-Ig, Proteintech, 1:5000 dilution, RRID: AB_3085083), anti-GLB1 (66586-1-Ig, proteintech, dilution 1:1000, RRID: AB_2881946), anti-p16 (ab189034, abcam, dilution 1:1000, RRID: AB_2737282), anti-p38 (80821-2-RR, proteintech, dilution 1:2000, RRID: AB_3670489), anti-p53 (10442-1-AP, proteintech, dilution 1:5000, RRID: AB_2206609) and anti-β-Tubulin (2146, CST, 1:1000 dilution, RRID: AB_2210545). The PVDF membrane was washed thrice with TBST and incubated with secondary antibody for 1 h at room temperature. Secondary antibodies included goat anti-rabbit IgG (ab205718, Abcam, 1:10,000 dilution, RRID: AB_2819160) and goat anti-mouse IgG (ab6789, Abcam, 1:10,000 dilution, RRID: AB_955439). Afterwards, PVDF membrane was washed thrice, and proteins were detected by enhanced chemiluminescence (Amersham Corporation, Arlington Heights, IL, USA). The intensity of each band was quantified by Image J. All experiments were replicated thrice. The value of each band density in experimental and control groups was normalised to that of its corresponding β-Tubulin band (loading control, ratio to β-Tubulin%).

## Protein stability assay
The stability of P21 protein was detected in cells treated with the protein synthesis inhibitor cycloheximide (CHX, Abcam, ab120093) to evaluate protein degradation. For 6PPD-exposed cells that were co-treated with CHX, cells were first treated with 2 μM 6PPD for 48 h, and then incubated with 10 μmol CHX for 0, 2, 4, 6, or 8 h. Cells were then harvested, and proteins in the supernatant of cell lysates were analysed by Western blotting. Protein band intensity was quantified using ImageJ software. These experiments were performed independently thrice.

## RNA stability analysis
HTR-8/SVneo cells (1 × 106 cells/well) treated with 6PPD were seeded in a 6-well plate for 24 h. Then, the cells were treated with 5 μg/mL actinomycin D (Sigma-Aldrich) to block mRNA transcription. After 0, 2, 4, 6, or 8 h, RNAs were extracted from cells, and the BAZ1B mRNA was analysed by RT-qPCR assays. GAPDH mRNA was used as the normalisation internal standard.

## Effect of 6PPD on protein degradation approach
Protein degradation assays were performed as the methods described previously. , Cells were treated with 10 μM MG132 (proteasome inhibitors) or 50 μM CQ (lysosomal blockers) for 6 h to suppress protein degradation. For 6PPD -exposed trophoblast cells with co-treatment with CHX and MG132 or CQ, cells were firstly treated with 2 μM 6PPD for 48 h and then incubated in a fresh medium. Then, cells were incubated with 10 μM CHX for 4 h, and subsequently incubated with 10 μM MG132 or 50 μM CQ for another 6 h. Afterwards, the total proteins were extracted and the remained proteins in cell lysates were analysed by Western blotting. These experiments were independently performed thrice.

## Co-Immunoprecipitation assay
Trophoblast HTR-8/SVneo cells (1 × 107) was lysed with IP lysis buffer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) on ice. The villous or placental tissues (approximately 0.2 g) were mixed with IP lysis buffer and milled in a mill. Proteins in cell lysates were quantified using Pierce BCA Protein Assay Kit. The lysates were incubated with P21 antibody (82669-2-RR, Proteintech, dilution 1:800, RRID: AB_3086498) or BAZ1B antibody (68361-1-Ig, Proteintech, 1:5000 dilution, RRID: AB_3085083) at 4 °C overnight, with equal weight of IgG antibody (ab172730, Abcam, dilution 1:200, RRID: AB_2687931) as negative control, and then incubated with Protein A/G magnetic beads (HY-K0202, MedChemExpress) for another 6 h to form bead-immunoprecipitate complex. Subsequently, beads were washed five times with IP washing buffer and the immunoprecipitated proteins were harvested by boiling in 1 × SDS loading buffer at 98 °C for 10 min for subsequent Western blot analysis.

## Ubiquitination analysis
Cells and tissue lysates (1 × 107 cells in 0.1 mL IP lysis buffer or 0.2 g tissues in 1 mL IP lysis buffer) were incubated with anti-P21 (82669-2-RR, Proteintech, dilution 1:800, RRID: AB_3086498) at 4 °C overnight with gentle rotation, with equal weight of IgG antibody (ab172730, Abcam, dilution 1:200, RRID: AB_2687931) as negative control, and then incubated with 40 μL Protein A/G Magnetic Beads (B23201, MCE, USA) at 4 °C for 4 h with gentle rotation. Afterwards, the beads were separated and thorough washed with IP lysis buffer, and then mixed with SDS loading buffer and boiled at 95 °C for 10 min. Subsequently, the immunoprecipitated proteins were separated by 10% SDS-PAGE and the ubiquitin-modified P21 was detected by Western blotting using anti-Ubiquitin (dilution 1:5000, ab179434, Abcam).

## Statistical analysis
All experiments were replicated thrice independently with similar results. The measured or calculated data (including control group) were presented as mean ± SD (standard deviation, n = 3). SPSS 26.0 or R software was used to analyse the data, and GraphPad Prism 8.0 as well as R software was used for figure generation. In cellular assays, the number n = 3 indicated that all experiments were replicated thrice independently. Data in the test groups were normalised against that in the control group. In animal model, the number n = 6 indicated 6 mice in each group. In villous tissue assays, n = 50 indicated 50 pair of HC and UM women tissue or urine samples. Levene's test was used to evaluate the homogeneity of variance of the data. Student's t-test was used to compare differences between two groups. ANOVA and Fisher's least significant difference (LSD) test were used to compare differences among multiple groups. The qualitative data were compared using the Chi-square test. The correlation analysis of the relative expression levels was performed using Pearson analysis. The odds ratio (OR) of each variable and the risk of unexplained miscarriage and its 95% confidence interval (95% CI) before adjusting potential confounders were analysed by univariate logistic regression analysis. The OR and 95% CI after adjusting potential confounders were analysed by multivariate logistic regression analysis. The data about the association among key molecules, cell phenotypes, and human diseases were obtained from the Comparative Toxicogenomics Database (CTD; http://ctdbase.org). The alluvial plot was prepared using the online OmicShare tools (https://www.omicshare.com/tools). When p < 0.05, the difference was considered to be statistically significant.

## Results

## Part I. 6PPD exposure induced miscarriage

## Epidemiological analysis showed that 6PPD exposure was associated with women unexplained miscarriage
To investigate the potential association between 6PPD internal exposure levels and unexplained miscarriage (UM), we conducted a case–control study containing 50 patients with UM and 50 healthy controls (HC) who had personally elective miscarriage to terminate their unwanted pregnancies ( A). The known clinical causes of miscarriage, such as chromosomal abnormalities, cervical insufficiency, endocrine or metabolic diseases, viral or bacterial infections, have been excluded , To consider the effects of potential confounders, some variables were collected from medical records based on prior studies and their potential associations with miscarriage, , such as baseline characteristics, clinical information, and lifestyle ( ). These variables did not show statistically significant differences between these two groups ( ).
 The levels of 6PPD were detected in urine samples collected from HC and UM groups. The median level of 6PPD in urine samples was 58 ng/L (51–64.25 ng/L) in HC group and 78.5 ng/L (66–89.75 ng/L) in UM group. 6PPD levels in urine samples were statistically significantly higher in UM vs HC groups ( B). Univariate logistic regression analysis showed that higher 6PPD levels in urine samples (odds ratio (OR) = 1.085, 95% confidence interval (CI) = 1.047–1.124) were positively associated with unexplained miscarriage ( C). Based on directed acyclic graph (DAG) analysis and previous literature, , education level, age, body mass index (BMI), household income, smoking, and drinking were considered as potential confounders ( D). To consider their effects, multivariate logistic regression analysis by adjusting for all these confounders showed that 6PPD levels in urine samples were still positively associated with miscarriage (the adjusted OR = 1.092 with 95% CI = 1.051–1.134, C). To further examine whether the estimated association differed among subpopulations, stratification analysis showed that the stratifying factors did not significantly alter the association between 6PPD levels and miscarriage (all P for interaction >0.05, E), confirming the robustness of these results. Collectively, all the statistical analysis confirmed that 6PPD internal exposure levels in urine samples were positively associated with unexplained miscarriage.

## Mouse model assays confirmed that 6PPD exposure induced mouse miscarriage
Subsequently, we explored the causation whether 6PPD exposure might induce miscarriage. For this aim, we constructed a 6PPD-exposed pregnant mouse model by orally gavage of 0, 0.4, 4, 12, 36, or 108 mg/kg/day 6PPD ( F). We found that exposure to ≥12 mg/kg/day 6PPD reduced body weight gain and body weight on Day 14 ( G and H), and exposure to ≥36 mg/kg/day 6PPD increased embryo resorption ( I) and elevated miscarriage rates ( J). Therefore, these results confirmed that exposure to high levels of 6PPD could induce mouse miscarriage.

## Part II. 6PPD exposure induced miscarriage by promoting P21-mediated senescence

## Proteomic analysis indicated the association among 6PPD exposure, P21-mediated senescence, and miscarriage
In order to explore which kind of phenotype might be involved in 6PPD exposure-induced miscarriage, we performed proteomics to screen the top altered cell phenotype. In the proteomic analysis using UM vs HC villous tissues, we set a relatively low threshold of 1.2-fold change to capture as many genes as possible that might be associated with UM. In cellular and animal models, we adopted a more stringent 1.5-fold change threshold to identify the genes that are more significantly regulated by 6PPD exposure. Firstly, two pairs of random HC and UM villous tissues were used for proteomic analysis, giving 2810 up-regulated and 2140 down-regulated proteins with change >1.2-fold and p < 0.05 ( A). GO and KEGG analysis of the differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) revealed that cellular senescence-related GO items (such as ageing, cell ageing and cellular senescence) and KEGG pathways (cellular senescence) were significantly enriched ( B and C). Secondly, 108 vs 0 mg/kg/d 6PPD-exposed mouse placental tissues were also used for proteomic analysis, giving 592 up-regulated and 1120 down-regulated proteins with change >1.5-fold and p < 0.05 ( D). GO and KEGG analysis of these DEPs revealed that cellular senescence-related GO items (ageing) and KEGG pathways (cellular senescence) were significantly enriched ( E–F). Thirdly, human trophoblast cells in placental villous tissues play crucial roles in woman healthy pregnancy; and HTR-8/SVneo cells have been widely used as trophoblast cell model in various miscarriage studies. HTR-8/SVneo cells treated with 2 vs 0 μM 6PPD were used for proteomic analysis, giving 454 up-regulated and 274 down-regulated proteins with change >1.5-fold and p < 0.05 ( G). GO and KEGG analysis of these DEPs revealed that 6PPD exposure altered senescence-related GO items (ageing) and KEGG pathways (cellular senescence) ( H and I). Therefore, all the proteomic analysis indicated that senescence might be involved in 6PPD exposure-induced miscarriage.
 Subsequently, we further explored which protein might play essential roles in 6PPD exposure-caused senescence. In the intersection of three proteomic datasets shown in A, D and G, we identified that P21 protein was top highly expressed ( J). P21 has been known as an important senescence protein to induce senescence in various cell models. , , Collectively, the proteomic data indicated that 6PPD exposure, P21-mediated senescence, and miscarriage might be positively associated.

## Cell, tissue, and mouse model assays confirmed that 6PPD exposure caused senescence to induce miscarriage
To experimentally validate these findings, we measured several senescence biomarkers in 6PPD-exposed trophoblast cells, UM vs HC villus tissues, and 6PPD-exposed mouse placental tissues. Firstly, 6PPD exposure reduced HTR-8/SVneo cell viability in a dose-dependent manner ( A), which could be efficiently rescued by treatment with D + Q, a canonical senescence inhibitor , ( B). Secondly, 6PPD exposure increased the levels of SA-β-Galactose positive cells and the secretion of senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP, such as the mRNA levels of IL-6, MCP1, MMP1, and MMP24) in a dose-dependent manner ( C–E). Thirdly, 6PPD exposure increased the protein levels of P21 in 6PPD-exposed HTR-8/SVneo cells in a dose-dependent manner ( F and G). However, 6PPD exposure did not alter P21 mRNA levels ( H). Collectively, all these results confirmed that 6PPD exposure induced HTR-8/SVneo cell senescence.
 To explore the roles of P21 in trophoblast cell senescence, P21 was overexpressed or silenced in trophoblast cells ( ). Overexpression of P21 suppressed cell viability; whereas knockdown of P21 increased cell viability ( ). Meanwhile, overexpression of P21 increased, whereas knockdown of P21 reduced, the levels of SA-β-Galactose positive cells ( ). These results showed that 6PPD up-regulated P21 protein levels and P21 itself could cause trophoblast cell senescence. Furthermore, co-knockdown of P21 in 6PPD-exposed trophoblast cells ( A and B) restored (i.e. increased) cell viability ( C) and (i.e. decreased) the levels of SA-β-Galactose positive cells ( D and E), showing that co-knockdown of P21 could reduce 6PPD exposure-caused trophoblast cell senescence. Collectively, these results supported that 6PPD exposure up-regulated P21 protein levels and thus caused trophoblast cell senescence. In other words, 6PPD exposure caused trophoblast cell senescence by up-regulating P21 protein levels.
 Secondly, we also measured the levels of senescence biomarkers in UM vs HC villus tissues. The levels of SA-β-Galactose positive intensity ( F and G) and the protein levels of P21 and β-gal were higher in UM vs HC villous tissues ( H–J), indicating higher levels of senescence in UM vs HC villous tissues. Moreover, Pearson correlation analysis showed that 6PPD levels in urine samples were positive correlated with the protein levels of P21 or β-gal in UM villous tissues ( K and L). The datapoints in UM and HC groups were obviously separated. Collectively, these results indicated that 6PPD exposure, villous tissue senescence, and unexplained miscarriage were positively associated.
Thirdly, we also detected the levels of senescence biomarkers in mouse placental tissues. The mRNA and amino acid sequences of P21 and β-gal were conserved in human, mouse, monkey, pig, and bovin ( ). The levels of SA-β-Galactose positive intensity ( ) and the protein levels of murine p21 and β-gal ( A–C) were higher in 6PPD-exposed mouse placental tissues, confirming that 6PPD exposure caused mouse placental senescence and also induced mouse miscarriage. To further validate the roles of p21-mediated senescence in 6PPD exposure-induced miscarriage, we constructed a mouse miscarriage intervention model in which 108 mg/kg/d 6PPD-exposed pregnant mice were orally administered with 5 mg/kg/3d D and 50 mg/kg/3d Q (D + Q), with PEG-400 solvent as control, or intraperitoneally injected with 10 mg/kg AS-p21 (antisense oligonucleatides of p21), with AS–NC as control ( D). D + Q was a typical senescence suppressor, , which has been widely used in various cell and animal assays. , AS-p21 could efficiently down-regulate the expression levels of p21 by targeting and degrading p21 mRNA. As validation, D + Q treatment and p21 knockdown reduced p21 protein levels in mouse trophoblast cells ( ). D + Q treatment or knockdown of p21 recovered (i.e. increased) the body weight gain of pregnant mice on D14 ( ), reduced embryo resorption ( E), and decreased miscarriage rates ( ) in 6PPD-exposed mice. These treatments also reduced the levels of SA-β-Galactose positive intensity ( ) and the protein levels of murine p21 and β-gal ( F–H) in mouse placental tissues. Taken together, these results confirmed that the suppression of mouse placental senescence or direct knockdown of p21 could efficiently reduce mouse miscarriage in this 6PPD-exposed mouse model. In other words, 6PPD exposure induced mouse miscarriage by causing placental senescence through up-regulating p21.
 Collectively, based on the assays using 6PPD-exposed trophoblast cells, HC and UM villous tissues, and 6PPD-exposed mouse model, we confirmed that 6PPD exposure caused P21-mediated senescence to induce miscarriage.

## Part III. 6PPD suppressed BAZ1B-mediated P21 ubiquitination degradation

## 6PPD suppressed BAZ1B-mediated P21 ubiquitination degradation

## 6PPD exposure inhibited P21 ubiquitination degradation
Since 6PPD exposure up-regulated the protein but not mRNA levels of P21, subsequently, we explored its underlying mechanism. Protein stability assays revealed that 6PPD exposure suppressed the degradation of P21 protein ( A and B). In general, proteins are degraded through ubiquitination-proteasome pathway or autophagy-lysosome pathway, which could be specifically blocked by proteasome inhibitor MG132 or lysosomal inhibitor CQ, respectively. , In 6PPD-exposed trophoblast cells, treatment with MG132, but not with CQ, significantly increased the accumulation of P21 protein ( C and D). Exposure to 2 μM 6PPD, but not 0.01 μM 6PPD, reduced the protein levels of the ubiquitinated P21 (P21-Ub), an intermediate for ubiquitination degradation of P21 ( E and F). Therefore, these results indicated that 6PPD exposure suppressed the ubiquitination degradation of P21 and thus up-regulated P21 protein levels.
 

## BAZ1B was a new E3 ubiquitin ligase of P21
Ubiquitination degradation requires E3 ubiquitin ligases. However, whether 6PPD might alte the E3 ubiquitin ligase of p21 is still unknown and should be identified. The existing and potential E3 ligases of P21 were predicted by the UbiBrowser 2.0 database ( G). Meanwhile, P21 IP-MS assays also identified the proteins that were interected with P21 ( H). In the intersection of three datasets from G, G and H, BAZ1B protein was top enriched ( I). BAZ1B gene is missing in canonical Williams syndrome and loss of Baz1b causes early neonatal death in mice. However, it is still unclear whether BAZ1B might act as E3 ubiquitin ligase of P21. Therefore, we selected BAZ1B and explored its ligase functions in the following studies. IP analysis confirmed that P21 proteins bound with BAZ1B ( J). Overexpression of BAZ1B reduced P21 protein levels; whereas knockdown of BAZ1B increased P21 protein levels ( K–M). IP assays using identical but excessive P21 antibody further showed that overexpression of BAZ1B up-regulated the protein levels of BAZ1B and P21-Ub enriched by P21 antibody and reduced P21 protein levels in cell lysates ( ); whereas knockdown of BAZ1B gave the opposite results ( ). Therefore, these results confirmed that BAZ1B was an E3 ubiquitin ligase of P21 and promoted P21 ubiquitination degradation.

## 6PPD inhibited BAZ1B-mediated P21 ubiquitination
Next, we investigated the joint roles of 6PPD and BAZ1B in P21 ubiquitination degradation. Firstly, we explored whether 6PPD might regulate BAZ1B expression levels. We found that 6PPD exposure down-regulated the mRNA levels of BAZ1B ( ) but did not affect its mRNA stability ( ). We also found that 6PPD exposure down-regulated the protein levels of BAZ1B in a dose-dependent manner ( A and B). Moreover, 6PPD exposure also reduced the protein stability of BAZ1B ( ). Furthermore, IP assays using using identical but excessive BAZ1B antibody showed that more P21 protein was pulled down by BAZ1B in 6PPD-exposed HTR-8/SVneo cells ( C–E). In contrast, IP assays using identical but excessive P21 antibody showed that 6PPD reduced the protein levels of BAZ1B and P21-Ub enriched by P21 antibody and increased P21 protein levels in cell lysates ( F–H). Moreover, co-transfection assays showed that co-overexpression of BAZ1B restored (i.e. decreased) P21 protein levels in 6PPD-exposed trophoblast cells ( I–K). IP assays using identical but excessive P21 antibody also showed that co-overexpression of BAZ1B restored (i.e. increased) the protein levels of P21-Ub enriched by p21 antibody and (i.e. decreased) the protein levels of P21 in cell lysates ( L–P) in 6PPD-exposed trophoblast cells. Collectively, these results supported that 6PPD exposure down-regulated BAZ1B protein levels, suppressed BAZ1B-mediated ubiquitination degradation of P21, and eventually up-regulated p21 protein levels in 6PPD-exposed trophoblast cells.
 Subsequently, we further explored whether BAZ1B might regulate 6PPD-exposed trophoblast cell senescence. Overexpression of BAZ1B promoted, whereas knockdown of BAZ1B suppressed, trophoblast cell viability ( ). Overexpression of BAZ1B reduced, whereas knockdown of BAZ1B increased, the levels of SA-β-Galactose positive cells ( ) and the protein levels of β-gal ( ) in trophoblast cells. Co-transfection assays also showed that co-overexpression of BAZ1B restored (i.e. increased) cell viability ( ) and (i.e. reduced) the levels of SA-β-Galactose positive cells ( ) and (i.e. decreased) the protein levels of β-gal ( ) in 6PPD-exposed trophoblast cells. Therefore, these results showed that 6PPD exposure down-regulated BAZ1B protein levels and thus up-regulated P21 protein levels, which further caused senescence in 6PPD-exposed trophoblast cells.

## Part IV. Verification of BAZ1B/P21 axis in women villous tissues and mouse placental tissues

## Verification of BAZ1B/P21 axis in women villous tissues
Then, we studied BAZ1B-mediated ubiquitination degradation of P21 in HC and UM villous tissues. The protein levels of BAZ1B were lower in UM vs HC villous tissues ( A and B). IP assays using identical but excessive P21 antibody showed that the protein levels of BAZ1B and P21-Ub that were pulled down by P21 antibody were lower, whereas the protein levels of P21 in tissue lysates were higher, in UM vs HC villous tissues ( C–E). Moreover, Pearson correlation analysis showed that the levels of 6PPD in urine samples were negatively associated with the protein levels of BAZ1B or P21-Ub in UM villous tissues ( F and G); the protein levels of BAZ1B were positively associated with P21-Ub in UM group ( H); and the protein levels of BAZ1B were negatively associated with the protein levels of P21 or β-gal in UM group ( I and J). The datapoints in UM and HC groups were relatively separated. Taken together, these results confirmed that 6PPD exposure, the protein levels of BAZ1B and P21-Ub in villous tissues, senescence, and unexplained miscarriage were closely associated.
 

## Verification of murin e Baz1b/p21 axis in 6PPD-exposed mouse model
Subsequently, we also validated Baz1b/p21 axis in this 6PPD-exposed mouse model. The amino acid sequence of BAZ1B was conserved in human, mouse, monkey, pig, and bovin ( ). The protein levels of murine Baz1b were lower in 6PPD-exposed mouse placental tissues in a dose-dependent manner ( A, ). IP assays using identical but excessive p21 antibody showed that the protein levels of Baz1b and p21-Ub that were pulled down by p21 antibody were lower, whereas the protein levels of p21 in cell lysates were higher, in 6PPD-exposed mouse placental tissues ( B, ). To further validate the roles of Baz1b in 6PPD-induced miscarriage, we constructed another miscarriage intervention model in which 6PPD-exposed pregnant mice were intraperitoneally injected with Baz1b overexpression plasmid, with empty vector as control ( C). As validation, overexpression of Baz1b up-regulated the protein levels of Baz1b in mouse trophoblast cells ( ). Overexpression of Baz1b reduced embryo resorption and decreased mouse miscarriage rates in this 6PPD-exposed mouse model ( D and E). IP assays using identical but excessive p21 antibody showed that overexpression of Baz1b restored (i.e. increased) the protein levels of Baz1b and p21-Ub that were enriched by p21 antibody, and (increased) the protein levels of Baz1b and (decreased) the protein levels of p21 in tissue lysates ( F, ). Co-overexpression of Baz1b also restored (i.e. decreased) the levels of SA-β-Galactose positive intensity ( G and H) and the protein levels of p21 and β-gal ( I–K) in 6PPD-exposed mouse placental tissues. Collectively, these results indicated that 6PPD exposure down-regulated Baz1b protein levels, suppressed Baz1b-mediated ubiquitination degradation of p21, up-regulated p21 protein levels, caused placental senescence, and thus induced mouse miscarriage in this 6PPD-exposed mouse model.
 

## Discussion

## 6PPD exposure causes placental senescence to induce miscarriage
With increasing global usage and wear of tyres, environmental 6PPD pollution and its healthy problems have been becoming a new important concern worldwide in the environment and health field. 6PPD has been detected to be significantly higher in urine samples of pregnant women than those in general adults or children in southern China. A recent study of 148 paired maternal–foetal samples demonstrates that 6PPD is the dominant PPD analogue in maternal plasma, cord plasma, and placenta, with a detection frequency of 63%–81%. 6PPD could cross through placental barrier via both passive diffusion and active transport, which has been validated in animal models, supporting that 6PPD might produce toxicity in placenta. However, whether and how 6PPD exposure during pregnancy might induce unexplained miscarriage remain completely unknown.
In this study, we find that urine 6PPD levels are significantly higher in UM vs HC women; and higher 6PPD levels in urine sample are positively associated with senescence in villous tissues and unexplained miscarriage. Furthermore, in mouse model, we confirm that 6PPD exposure induces mouse miscarriage by promoting placental senescence. Moreover, cellular assays confirm that 6PPD exposure induces trophoblast cell senescence. P21 is known as a key biomarker that causes senescence. In this study, we find that 6PPD exposure causes trophoblast cell senescence by suppressing BAZ1B-mediated ubiquitination degradation of P21 protein. The regulatory mechanisms are proposed ( A). 6PPD exposure down-regulates the expression levels of BAZ1B by down-regulating its mRNA levels, protein levels, and protein stability. BAZ1B is identified as a new E3 ligase of P21 and promotes the ubiquitination degradation of P21. Thus, 6PPD exposure suppresses BAZ1B-mediated ubiquitination degradation of P21 and thus up-regulated P21 protein levels. Subsequently, the up-regulated P21 causes trophoblast cell senescence by elevating the levels of SA-β-Galactose positive cells and SASPs. Subsequently, trophoblast senescence induces miscarriage. The cellular mechanisms are consistent with those in UM vs HC women villous tissues and in 6PPD-exposed mouse placental tissues. Supplement with murine Baz1b, knockdown of murine p21, or reduction in placental senescence by treatment with D + Q (senescence suppressor) could effectively reduce mouse placental senescence and reduced mouse miscarriage in the 6PPD-exposed mouse models. Therefore, based on population cohort, mouse model, and cellular mechanistic studies, these data demonstrate that 6PPD exposure causes senescence to induce unexplained miscarriage by suppressing BAZ1B-mediated ubiquitination degradation of P21.
 In our previous work, we have demonstrated that many environmental pollutants might induce miscarriages, such as polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon, , , , , , , , nanoplastics, , copper exposure, lithium, , bisphenol F , bisphenol B , hypoxia, ciprofloxacin, or disinfection by-products of tap water, by causing trophoblast cell senescence, ferroptosis, pyroptosis, cuproptosis, or apoptosis, or by supressing migration/invasion or proliferation. Among them, ciprofloxacin exposure induces miscarriage by causing placental senescence through activating TRIM21-mediated MFF degradation. In this study, we identify that 6PPD exposure induces miscarriage by causing placental senescence by suppressing BAZ1B-mediated ubiquitination degradation of P21. All these studies enrich the database of the pathogenesis and biological mechanisms of various environment toxicants-induced unexplained miscarriage.

## Miscarriage intervention and treatment
Recently, increasing studies have uncovered the molecular mechanisms of unexplained miscarriage. , , , , , , , Fructose-1,6-diphosphate has been reported to prevent miscarriage by inducing COX-2 macrophage differentiation in the decidua. Also, increasing succinate levels intravenously during the first trimester reduces the risk of recurrent spontaneous miscarriage. In our recent studies, we have found that supplement with GPX4 could suppress ferroptosis, recover angiogenesis, and alleviate miscarriage in BaP-induced mouse model. Knockdown of either murine lnc-hz06 or Ncoa4 efficiently suppresses ferroptosis and alleviates miscarriage in hypoxic mouse model ; knockdown of murine Ahr efficiently recovers homologous recombination repair in placental tissues and alleviates miscarriage in a BaP-exposed mouse miscarriage model ; knockdown of Nlrp3 efficiently reduces placenta pyroptosis and alleviates BaP-induced mouse miscarriage ; supplementing with murine SOX2 or ROCK1 could efficiently rescue migration/invasion and migrasome formation and alleviate polystyrene nanoplastics (PS-NPs)-induced miscarriage ; supplement with Bcl-2 efficiently suppresses apoptosis in PS-NPs-exposed trophoblast cells and reduces apoptosis and alleviates miscarriage in PS-NPs-exposed pregnant mouse model ; treatment with TTM (a cuproptosis inhibitor) suppresses placental cuproptosis and alleviates miscarriage in CuCl2-exposed mouse mode ; the therapeutic down-regulation of FOXO1 or STEAP4 suppresses placental cuproptosis to alleviate miscarriage in LiCl-exposed mouse mode ; therapeutic down-regulation of Trim21 could efficiently recover mitochondrial functions and suppress placental senescence, and thus reduce mouse miscarriage ; and supplement with murine Pkca or Tspan4 can efficiently improve migration/invasion and treat against BPB-induced miscarriage. In this study, we find that supplement with D + Q, knockdown of p21, or supplement with Baz1b could inhibit placental senescence to alleviate mouse miscarriage in the 6PPD-exposed mouse models, providing new targets for treatment against unexplained miscarriage. Collectively, all these studies enrich the biotargets and approaches for treatment against specific environment toxicant-induced unexplained miscarriage.

## Limitations and prospects
The UM case–control study has several limitations, such as missing other potential impact factors (including psychosocial stress, subclinical placental differences, lifestyle, socioeconomic factors, or health status) and the possibility of misclassifications. A prospective cohort with more population is still needed to further explore this association. In this study, we used a single-spot of urine samples, which might have potential bias if the 6PPD levels vary greatly over time due to changed external exposures, daily activities, or physiological status. A long-term cumulative exposure should be recorded to more accurately reflect the internal exposure of 6PPD. In addition to P21, the protein levels of other indicators of senescence, such as β-gal, P53, P38, and P16, , were also up-regulated in 6PPD-exposed HTR-8/SVneo cells in a dose-dependent manner ( ). In addition to senescence, proteomic analysis also shows that other cell phenotypes might also be altered, such as cell cycle and apoptosis ( B and C, E and F, H and I). Therefore, 6PPD exposure might induce miscarriage also possibly through other cell phenotypes. Moreover, the alluvial plot shows that 6PPD, BAZ1B, and P21 are associated with other cell phenotypes, such as cell proliferation, migration, apoptosis, pyroptosis, protein transport, and are also associated with many other human diseases, such as chemical and drug induced liver injury, necrosis, weight loss, kidney diseases, inflammation, nerve degeneration and seizures ( B). , The association and causation among chemical-gene-phenotype-disease should be further investigated.
Although we use high doses of 6PPD in mouse and cell assays, frankly, there is the possibility that women might exposure to excessive 6PPD in areas with high 6PPD pollution, with 6PPD occupational exposure or occupational poisoning, or with possible long-term cumulative exposure. To some extent, the short-term and high doses of 6PPD-exposed mouse and cell models might reflect or predict the long-term and low doses of 6PPD exposure on miscarriage in human cohort. It has been reported that the transformation ratio from 6PPD to 6PPD-Q is less than 2% in human liver microsomes. In another study, 6PPD-Q is only detected in five urine samples from ten 6PPD-exposed mouse samples. The detected concentrations of 6PPD-Q are all less than the limit of quantitation, suggesting that 6PPD-Q might not be the major biotransformation product of 6PPD in mice. Therefore, human are exposed to 6PPD-Q mainly through direct environmental exposure rather than through 6PPD metabolism. 6PPD-Q has exhibited toxicities in various cells and animal models. , , , However, till now, the direct comparison of the toxicity originated from 6PPD or 6PPD-Q in human is still very limited. Future studies should distinguish the difference in transportation, location, and toxicity of 6PPD and 6PPD-Q. Moreover, to detect 6PPD levels in urine might be concerned in annual routine physical examination. To reduce environmental 6PPD contamination is an important topic in the environmental-health field and should be addressed jointly by global governments and enterprises.
In general, environmental toxicants might have their corresponding receptors. For example, polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, bisphenol analogues, and dioxins , might activate their common receptor AhR. AhR is typical transcription factor and activates the transcription of a group of target genes to perform down-stream signalling and functions. To identify the common receptors in various toxicant-induced miscarriage is also an important topic. Finally, the intervention and treatment of unexplained miscarriage should be further explored for better clinical application.
