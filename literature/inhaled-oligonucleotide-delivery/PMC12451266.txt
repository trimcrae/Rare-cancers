This editorial provides an overview of formulations for antibody-based therapeutics and highlights a few hot topics and emerging trends, including high concentration formulations, fixed-dose combinations, novel excipients and delivery technologies, and AI integration. ## Introduction
Drug product formulation transforms active pharmaceutical ingredients into drug products, ensuring stability, manufacturability, efficacy, and patient safety. Driven by biotech advancements and patient needs, new antibody-based therapies and combinations are expanding, significantly evolving the formulation landscape. This special collection of Antibody Therapeutics journal highlights recent advancements in lyophilization [ ] and reconstitution [ ], excipients [ ], high concentration formulations [ ], antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) formulations [ ], and placebo development [ ].

## Overview of formulations for antibody-based therapeutics
The majority of approved antibody-based therapies encompass monoclonal antibodies (mAbs), bispecific antibodies, and ADCs [ ]. Other variations exist, such as Fab and single-chain variable fragments, mAb-peptide conjugates, and mAb-protein fusions. These biologics require innovative formulation techniques to address challenges such as protein aggregation, degradation, and particulate formation. Manning et al. [ ] reviewed protein degradation mechanisms and instability issues, providing comprehensive insights into formulation fundamentals.
Advancement in lyophilization, buffer optimization, and excipient selection are essential for maintaining molecular integrity and biological activity throughout product shelf life. Mieczkowski et al. [ ] analyzed formulations of 165 monoclonal antibody products approved from 1986 to January 2023, noting a decrease in average formulation pH from 6.3 to ~5.8. Common excipients include histidine, sucrose, metal chelators, arginine, and methionine. Wen et al. [ ] reviewed formulations for 16 ADC products, highlighting buffers, sugars, and surfactants as key components, with the majority of these products being lyophilized.

## Current topics and emerging trends in antibody-based formulations

## High concentration formulations
High concentration drug products enhance efficacy and reduce dosing frequency by increasing the drug dose without raising the dose volume. Transitioning from IV to high concentration SC formulation boosts patient adherence and comfort. Most high concentration formulations are designed for SC administration [ , ]. These formulations are also suitable for other routes of administration, such as intramuscular (IM) and intravitreal (IVT) injections, which require delivering drugs in small volumes with concentrated formulations.
The main challenge of developing high concentration formulations is to reduce viscosity while ensuring solution stability and osmolality. Strategies include adjusting pH, using excipients, and employing viscosity-reducing agents such as arginine hydrochloride. Ren [ ] critically reviewed arginine’s role in protein formulation in this special collection. Additional development efforts focus on advancing safe and effective viscosity reducers such as proline analogs [ ], caffeine [ ], and chaotropic reagents [ ].

## Fixed-dose combinations
Fixed-dose combinations (FDC) may improve efficacy, reduce dose frequency, simplify administration, enhance patient adherence, and potentially lower costs [ ]. Current commercial FDCs include Inmazeb®, Opdualag™, Phesgo®, among others undergoing clinical trials in the field of infectious disease and oncology. In recent years, FDCs have evolved to incorporate not only antibody–antibody combinations, but also antibody co-formulations with siRNA [ ], and various other innovative modalities.

## Novel surfactant development
Surfactants are crucial excipients in protein formulations, ensuring the stability, efficacy, and safety of protein-based pharmaceuticals. Surfactants effectively prevent protein from unfolding and aggregating owing to surface tension and shear forces. Additionally, they inhibit proteins from being adsorbed to surfaces during manufacturing and administration processes. Polysorbate 20 and 80 are the most commonly used surfactants in protein formulations. However, polysorbates are susceptible to oxidation and enzymatic hydrolysis. The degradation of polysorbates in protein formulations may result in reduced protection to proteins, causing changes in quality attributes such as aggregation and particle formation. Furthermore, hypersensitivity to polysorbates, including systemic and injection- or infusion-site adverse events, has been reported. Therefore, the development of alternative surfactants that effectively stabilize proteins with improved safety profiles compared to polysorbates is highly desirable [ ]. Among the development of alternative surfactant, poloxamer 188 has been used a few biological formulations and has been recently reviewed [ ].

## New formulation and delivery technology development
New technologies are being developed to advance antibody formulations. These include long-acting protein formulations [ ] via biodegradable polymers to reduce dosing frequency, inhaled [ ] and transdermal [ ] antibody formulations, and protein crystalline formulations.

## Artificial intelligence in formulation development
Computational models and machine learning [ ] are used to predict mAb stability, aggregation, viscosity [ ], and optimal excipient combinations. By training these models on extensive datasets, protein formulation stability and efficacy under various conditions can be forecasted, thereby reducing development time and costs. Artificial intelligence (AI) analyzes complex multidimensional data to enhance formulation processes, resulting in more stable and effective protein drug products.

## Conclusions
Antibodies have significantly influenced therapeutic approaches for various diseases, such as cancer, autoimmune disorders, and infectious diseases. The formulation of antibody-based therapeutics has evolved significantly, and the development of these therapeutics is ongoing, driven by the need for improved stability, patient convenience, and therapeutic efficacy. Current trends include high concentration formulations, fixed-dose combinations, novel excipients and delivery technologies, and AI integration in mAb development. As the field progresses, innovative technologies and sustainable practices will be important in addressing the increasing demand for these therapies.
