The activation and proliferation of myocardial fibroblasts serve as crucial factors in the process of the development of fibrosis in the myocardium. Our research delved into the role that Rap2a plays in cardiac function as well as myocardial fibrosis, while its effects on cardial fibroblasts (CFs) proliferation, migration, and phenotypic transformation were also explored. Examination of the GEO database showed a notable increase in the expression of Rap2a within myocardial tissue from mice with MI compared to normal mice. Rap2a deficiency relieves MI in mice and restrains the phenotypic transition, proliferation, and migration of CFs. The absence of Rap2a mitigates MI in mice. Besides, it curbs the growth of CFs, restricts their movement, and prevents them from undergoing phenotypic conversion. Rap2a can bind to TNIK in myocardial fibroblasts and enhance TNIK expression; Merlin/YAP signaling pathway was assessed as a downstream target of TNIK to further elucidate the regulatory mechanism through which Rap2a influences cardiomyocytes. In conclusion, this study provides evidence that Rap2a promotes myocardial fibrosis through mediating the myofibroblast transformation, proliferation, and migration of CFs via the TNIK/Merlin/YAP pathway, thereby exacerbating symptoms of myocardial infarction.

## Introduction
As the most prevalent fatal cardiovascular disease, myocardial infarction (MI) exhibits an escalating trend in both morbidity and mortality rates over time. Approximately 18 million individuals succumbed to cardiovascular diseases in 2019, accounting for 32% of global deaths and establishing it as the primary cause of mortality across all ailments (Shi et al. ; Roth et al. ). Recent data from the American Heart Association in 2022 revealed that myocardial infarction contributed to around 46% of fatalities associated with cardiovascular diseases (Tsao et al. ). Furthermore, the outbreak of novel coronavirus in 2019 has further exacerbated the incidence of myocardial infarction among patients (Case et al. ; Kayikcioglu et al. ). Extensive study has demonstrated that prompt revascularization following myocardial infarction through interventions such as percutaneous coronary intervention, thrombolytic therapy, and bypass surgery, is of vital importance in improving cardiac function and preventing pathophysiological remodeling after myocardial infarction (Reed et al. ). However, these treatments may not be universally applicable due to potential risks including severe complications like bleeding and reperfusion injury (Marín-Juez et al. ; Rentrop and Feit ). The utilization of medications (antiplatelet drugs, antiarrhythmic drugs, and angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors) aimed at limiting infarct size often proves ineffective while also predisposing patients towards heart failure as well as cardiac hypertrophy (Reed et al. ; Pan et al. ). Therefore, delving deeper into understanding the pathogenesis underlying myocardial infarction holds immense significance for clinical prevention and treatment strategies targeting this condition.
Cardiac remodeling refers to the self-repair and adaptive changes of the heart following injuries such as myocardial infarction, hypertension, heart valvular disease, and cardiomyopathy (Matta et al. ; Dhalla et al. ). Myocardial fibrosis is a crucial pathophysiological factor that affects ventricular remodeling after MI. It is typified by the arousal of fibroblasts, their erratic proliferation, the over-accumulation, and the irregular distribution of extracellular matrix proteins (Yin et al. ; Travers et al. ; Song et al. ). Cardiac fibroblasts (CFs) are crucial target cells during the development of myocardial fibrosis. They have a major impact on cardiac fibrosis. CFs transform into myofibroblasts and simultaneously produce and release large quantities of extracellular matrix proteins, which is key to the process of cardiac fibrosis (Lin et al. ; Chu et al. ). The activation and proliferation of CFs represent a critical link in myocardial fibrosis (Wang et al. ). Therefore, inhibiting or reversing myocardial fibrosis constitutes the primary research direction for determining therapeutic targets for myocardial infarction.
The Ras superfamily serves as molecular switches within signal transduction pathways, exerting their effects on various downstream receptor proteins and mediating changes in cytoplasmic or nuclear biological characteristics induced by extracellular signals, thereby regulating diverse cellular processes (Zhang et al. ; Sadeghi Shaker et al. ; El-Tanani et al. ). The RAP protein family, a group of small GTPases, encompasses members such as RAP1 A, RAP1B, RAP2 A, RAP2B, and RAP2 C. These proteins serve pivotal regulatory functions within the cell, engaging in a spectrum of biological processes. Such processes include cell signaling transduction, regulation of the cytoskeleton, cellular proliferation, differentiation, vesicular transport, and cell adhesion (Lian et al. ). Nevertheless, the function of RAP proteins in the context of myocardial infarction remains incompletely characterized, with specific pathways requiring systematic delineation.
In this study, we have undertaken an analysis of RAP proteins expression within the myocardial tissue of mice post-myocardial infarction, with the objective of pinpointing genes encoding RAP proteins that exhibit the most significant differential expression compared to normal mouse myocardial tissue. We have explored their impact on the symptoms and their roles in the growth, movement, and phenotypic change of CFs. Furthermore, our research focus extends to elucidating the RAP proteins that regulate myocardial fibrosis following myocardial infarction, with the aim of uncovering their underlying pathogenic mechanisms. The objective of this research is to provide novel diagnostic and therapeutic targets for addressing MI.

## Methods

## Bioinformatics analysis
The GSE775 dataset (GEO accession number: GSE775; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE775) containing transcriptomic profiles of myocardial tissues from 18 myocardial infarction (MI) mice and 23 sham-operated mice was retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. Raw data were preprocessed using the GEOquery R package with background correction and quantile normalization. Probes were annotated to gene symbols using the latest platform annotation file (GPL81). Batch effects were assessed via principal component analysis (PCA) and corrected using ComBat when necessary. Quality control included removal of samples with > 20% missing values and genes expressed in < 50% samples. In the GSE775 dataset, GEO2R was applied to detect the differential expression of Rap2a between the myocardial tissue of mice with MI and normal myocardial tissue.

## Induction of MI
Male C57BL/6 mice, at eight weeks of age, were sourced from Beijing Vitong Lihua Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. During the experiment, the LAD of some experimental animals was permanently ligated, while others received a sham operation. The procedure involved administering 2% isoflurane inhalation anesthesia and making a small skin incision over the left chest. A 6–0 silk suture was employed to ligate the LAD), threading it about 2–3 mm beneath the tip of the left auricle. Mice that died within 24 h after the surgery were not included in the analysis. The sham group had the same surgical process, except for the ligation of the coronary artery. At designated time intervals, the mice were euthanized using a combination of CO₂ inhalation and cervical dislocation, and then tissues were collected for further analysis. The ethical committee of Fuwai Central Cardiovascular Hospital approved this study, and the approval number is ChiCTR2400083850.

## qRT-PCR
Nucleic acid isolation was conducted with the RNeasy Mini System (Qiagen, Germany). Following RNA purification, reverse transcription was executed using the PrimeScript RT Platform (Takara, Japan). Amplification analysis was performed on the Applied Biosystems 7500 Real-Time PCR platform with SYBR Green chemistry (Thermo Fisher Scientific), employing Qiagen-designed primer pairs (GAPDH: F-ACCACAGTCCATGCCATCAC, R-TCCACCACCCTGTTGCTGTA; Rap2a: F-GAAGCTTATGCGCGAGTACAAAG, R-GGAATTCCTATTGTATGTTACATG). The quantification cycle (Cq) values were normalized against the endogenous control GAPDH for relative expression determination.

## Western blot analysis
Lysis was conducted in myocardial tissue or CFs using RIPA buffer (Thermo Scientific, USA) to extract total protein. Following SDS—PAGE gel electrophoresis, the samples were transferred onto PVDF membranes and then blocked with skim milk. The membranes underwent an overnight incubation with primary antibodies that were specifically selected for this study. These primary antibodies were diluted to a concentration of 1:2000 prior to use. After the overnight incubation, an HRP—conjugated secondary antibody was introduced and allowed to incubate for 1 h. Once the incubation with the secondary antibody was complete, the membranes were treated with an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) substrate. This ECL substrate facilitated the visualization of protein bands on the membranes. All antibodies utilized in this research, including anti-Rap2a, anti-α-SMA, anti-collagen I, anti-collagen III, and anti-GAPDH, were sourced from Cell Signaling Technology, which is located in Danvers, MA, USA.

## Echocardiography
First, anesthesia was induced in the mice by administering a combination of 1.5% isoflurane and oxygen flowing at a rate of 1 L/min. Following anesthesia, cardiac function in the mice was assessed by using a Vevo 770 imaging system (VisualSonics, Toronto, Canada) with a 30 MHz transducer. The short—axis view derived from M-mode recordings was used to measure parameters such as LVEF (%), LVFS (%), LVESd, and LVEDd.

## Collection of tissues
Following anesthetic induction with 3% isoflurane, humane termination was achieved via intraperitoneal delivery of sodium pentobarbital (Nembutal, 50 mg/kg; CAS 57–33-0) in accordance with AVMA guidelines. A thoracotomy was performed to excise the cardiac organ, followed by the resection of atrial chambers, principal vasculature, and ungulate structures; subsequently, targeted specimens were harvested and immersion-fixed in appropriate solutions.

## TTC staining
Cardiac specimens from multigroup murine cohorts underwent rapid cryofixation at −80 °C (8-min protocol). Subsequently, the frozen hearts were sectioned into five thick circular slices, ranging in thickness from 0.1 to 0.3 cm. These sections were then immersed in a prepared solution containing TTC and incubated at 37 °C for thirty minutes under light-protected conditions. Following this, the sections were placed in a solution consisting of 10% formaldehyde and allowed to incubate for one week. Finally, the sections were arranged in ascending order based on their size and photographs capturing myocardial infarction size were captured using a Sony camera.

## H&E staining
After fixation in a 4% solution of paraformaldehyde, cardiac specimens underwent perfusion-rinsing prior to paraffin-encapsulation. Microtome sectioning at 5 µm thickness was followed by solvent-mediated lipid clearance and gradient dehydration. H&E histochemical protocol was then applied to achieve chromatin-cytoplasmic differentiation, with eosinophilic cytoplasmic delineation and basophilic nuclear contrast. Microscopic imaging was performed on these stained sections using an Olympus microscope (Tokyo, Japan).

## Masson’s trichrome staining
Formaldehyde-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue blocks underwent microtome sectioning at 5 µm thickness for Masson's trichrome histochemical analysis. The polychromatic staining protocol differentially demonstrated extracellular matrix components: collagenous matrices exhibited azure coloration, cytoplasmic elements displayed eosinophilic affinity, and nuclear chromatin manifested basophilic characteristics.

## Immunohistochemistry (IHC)
The sections underwent antigen retrieval by boiling in Na-citrate buffer, followed by overnight incubation with anti-collagen I, anti-α-SMA, anti-collagen III. Subsequently, the slices were counterstained with hematoxylin and then coverslipped after dehydration. Microscopic images were captured at 200 × magnification.

## Primary CFs isolation and culture
Primary cardiac fibroblasts were isolated from neonatal C57BL/6 mice (postnatal day 1–3). Briefly, ventricular tissues were aseptically dissected into 1 mm3 fragments using micro-dissection scissors (Fine Science Tools, 15,000–03) and subjected to sequential digestion with 0.1% collagenase II (Worthington, LS004176) and 0.25% trypsin–EDTA (Gibco, 25,200,072) in HBSS buffer (37 °C, 5% CO₂) with gentle agitation. After 8-min digestion cycles, supernatants were collected through 100 μm cell strainers and neutralized with DMEM/F12 medium (Corning, 10–092-CV) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gibco, 10,437,028). The cell suspension was differentially plated for 1.5 h in T25 flasks (Corning, 430,639) to facilitate fibroblast adhesion. Non-adherent cells (predominantly cardiomyocytes) were removed through medium replacement. Adherent fibroblasts were maintained in complete growth medium (DMEM/F12 with 10% FBS, 1% penicillin–streptomycin) at 37 °C in a humidified 5% CO₂ incubator. Cells between passages 2–4 were used for experiments after verifying fibroblast purity (> 95% vimentin-positive, < 1% α-actinin-positive) through immunofluorescence staining (Anti-Vimentin, Abcam ab92547; Anti-α-Actinin, Sigma A7811). For serum starvation synchronization, confluent cultures were washed twice with PBS and incubated in serum-free DMEM/F12 for 24 h prior to experimental treatments. All procedures were conducted under strict aseptic conditions with regular mycoplasma testing (LookOut Mycoplasma PCR Detection Kit, Sigma MP0035).

## Cell transfection
The Rap2a siRNA, Merlin siRNA, YAP overexpression, YAP siRNA, and their corresponding negative controls (NC) were synthesized by GeneSeed (China). Cells (3 × 105) were inoculated onto a 6-well plate at a confluency of 40% to 60% and transfected with Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, USA), or infected with lentivirus. In preliminary experiments, we also determined the optimal infection multiplicity (MOI) by titrating lentiviruses labeled with different doses of GFP. For subsequent experiments, the infected cells were replenished with fresh medium.

## Proliferation detection
The EdU DNA Cell Proliferation Kit (RiboBio, China) was used to detect cell proliferation. Initially, cells were incubated for 2 h in the presence of 50 mM EdU. Next, the proliferating cells were stained with Apollo Dye Solution and then mounted in a mounting medium containing Hoechst 33,342. After that, an Olympus FSX100 microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan) was utilized to take images and count the number of EdU-positive cells in five randomly selected fields.

## Transwell migration assay
Cell migration was evaluated using 8 μm pore-size Transwell chambers (Corning, USA). Briefly, 1 × 105 CFs suspended in serum-free DMEM were seeded into the upper chamber. The lower chamber was filled with DMEM containing 20% FBS as a chemoattractant. After 24 h of incubation at 37 °C with 5% CO₂, non-migrated cells on the upper membrane surface were gently removed with a cotton swab. Migrated cells on the lower surface were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min and stained with 0.1% crystal violet for 20 min. Five random fields per chamber were imaged under an inverted microscope (Olympus, Japan) at 200 × magnification. Quantification was performed using ImageJ software (NIH, USA) by counting migrated cells.

## ELISA
After a 24-h period of coculturing cells, the levels of collagen III and collagen I in the medium were assessed using ELISA kits (R&D, USA). The detection process strictly followed the guidelines provided by the manufacturer.

## Immunofluorescence staining
For visualization purposes, nuclei were stained with 4',6 – diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI; Sigma Aldrich, St. Louis, MO), and fluorescence microscopy (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan) was applied to photograph 10 randomly selected regions of each sample. Antibodies against α—SMA, collagen I, and collagen III were obtained from Abcam (Cambridge, MA), while fluorescent secondary antibodies were procured from Servicebio (Wuhan, China).

## Dual-luciferase reporter assays
For reporter gene analysis, reverse-transfected HEK293 T cells (2 × 104/well) in 96-well plates received 50 nM miR-138-5p or scrambled control mimic (RiboBio, Guangzhou). Post-24 h liposomal delivery, cultures underwent co-transfection with either wild-type or mutant 3'-UTR constructs of CDKN1B/CDKN1 C (2 µg/mL). Following 48 h culture (37 °C/5% CO₂), firefly/Renilla luciferase bioluminescence was quantified using the DLR Assay (Promega, Madison, WI; Cat# E1910) with luminescent signal normalization.

## Immunoprecipitation
For the immunoprecipitation procedure, cells were lysed by making use of a lysis buffer that consisted of 1% Triton X—100 (Cell Signaling Technology, #9803) and was fortified with protease inhibitors. The lysates, with a concentration of 1 mg/ml, were placed on a rocking platform at 4 °C and incubated overnight with the corresponding antibody. After this incubation, Protein G magnetic beads from Thermo Scientific (catalog number 10004D) were added to the mixture and incubated further. To ensure thorough purification, the immunoprecipitates underwent five washes using the lysis buffer. Subsequently, they underwent denaturation in 2 × Laemmli buffer at 100 °C for a duration of 15 min and then were subjected to analysis alongside equalized lysates through immunoblotting techniques. The intensity of band signals was quantified using ImageJ software. It is important to note that all samples loaded onto their respective gels originated from the same experiment and were processed simultaneously to maintain consistency throughout analysis.

## Reverse Phase Protein Array (RPPA) analysis
The CFs was utilized for RPPA, which involved the utilization of 487 validated antibodies. Particularly, the cells were fragmented and made ready for analysis following the protocol formulated by the MD Anderson Proteomics Core Facility.

## Statistics
Public data quality was ensured through: (1) Exclusion of outlier samples (defined as > 3 SD from group mean); (2) Cross-validation of platform annotation consistency; (3) Independent replication of key findings in our experimental cohort. Quantitative variables are reported as arithmetic mean ± standard error of measurement. Comparative analyses were conducted using parametric approaches (two-group comparisons: Student's t-test; multi-group comparisons: one-way ANOVA with Tukey's post hoc correction) implemented in GraphPad Prism 8.0 (San Diego, CA). Time-to-event endpoints were evaluated via Kaplan–Meier estimator with Mantel-Cox log-rank testing. Statistical significance threshold was defined as α = 0.05 for all inferential analyses.

## Results

## Rap2a expression increases after MI in mice
To study the function of RAP proteins in MI, we primarily queried the GEO database for high-throughput sequencing data sets. We identified GSE775, which comprises samples from the cardiac regions between the apex and the left coronary artery in 18 mice with myocardial infarction and samples from the left ventricular region above the left artery in 23 sham-operated mice. Through analysis with a threshold of logFC > 0 and P < 0.05, it was revealed that there were 2,813 upregulated genes and 3,054 downregulated genes in the myocardial tissue of mice suffering from MI compared to the sham group (Fig.  A). Subsequent hierarchical clustering analysis of RAP proteins gene expression and the construction of a heatmap (Fig.  B) indicated that among the RAP proteins, only Rap2a exhibited a differential expression in the myocardial tissue of MI mice, with an upregulation in expression compared to the sham-operated group (Fig.  C). Subsequently, we set up a mouse model of MI, indicating a substantial upregulation of Rap2a in the myocardium of infarcted mice in contrast to control mice (Fig.  D-E). 

## Rap2a deficiency relieves MI
For determining the role of Rap2a in MI, we conducted a comparative analysis of myocardial fibrosis levels and cardiac function changes between wild-type mice (WT) and Rap2a−/− mice following LAD surgery. The levels of Rap2a in the myocardial tissue of Rap2a−/− MI mice were significantly lower compared to those in WT MI mice (Fig.  A-B). Fourteen days post-MI, echocardiographic analysis demonstrated that WT MI mice exhibited significantly reduced LVEF and LVFS, along with increased LVESd and LVEDd, indicating a significant decline in cardiac function. However, the cardiac function of Rap2a−/− MI mice exhibited significant improvement compared to the WT MI group (Fig.  C). The TTC staining assay was employed to evaluate the area of myocardial ischemia, revealing no signs of myocardial infarction in sham group mice, while WT MI mice developed MI following the ligation of the left anterior descending coronary artery. which can be attenuated upon Rap2a knockout (Fig.  D). HE staining demonstrated intact cell structures with regular myofibril arrangement and absence of inflammatory cell infiltration as well as smaller cross-sectional areas for myocardial cells within the sham group. In contrast, the MI group exhibited disordered myocardial fibers, enlarged myocardial cells, extensive infiltration by inflammatory cells, with significantly reduced degree of myocardial injury observed in Rap2a−/− MI mice (Fig.  E). The Masson staining technique was employed to assess fibrosis in mice with MI. In comparison to MI-WT mice, Rap2a−/− MI mice exhibited a significant reduction in myocardial fibrosis (Fig.  E). To validate the findings of Masson's staining, we conducted Western blot analysis as well as immunohistochemistry separately to detect alterations in the expression of pro-fibrotic proteins 14 days post-MI. As α-SMA is a marker for conversion of CFs, which secrete extracellular matrix primarily composed of collagen I and III, we evaluated fibrosis by analyzing levels of these three pro-fibrotic proteins. Our results depicted in Fig.  F, G demonstrate that compared to Sham group, the WT MI group exhibited increased expression of pro-fibrotic proteins; furthermore, the Rap2a−/− MI group showed significantly reduced levels of collagen I, α-SMA, and collagen III than WT MI group. Collectively, our data suggest that Rap2a knockout relieves symptoms associated with MI. 

## Rap2a Deficiency inhibits the proliferation, migration, and phenotypic transition of CFs
During the process of myocardial fibrosis, CFs undergo a series of pathological events, including proliferation, phenotypic transition, migration, and collagen secretion. These changes are considered as the fundamental pathological alterations in the heart following MI (Yin et al. ). The down-regulation of Rap2a expression in CFs was achieved using siRNA and confirmed through Western blot analysis (Fig.  A). EdU experiments validated that knockdown of Rap2a reduced the proliferation capacity of CFs (Fig.  B). To evaluate the effect of Rap2a on CFs migration, Transwell and scratch assays were performed. The results demonstrated, compared to the control group, the number of migrated CF cells was significantly reduced and the migration rate was decreased after Rap2a knockout (Fig.  C and Fig ), indicating inhibition of CFs migration. Furthermore, ELISA and immunofluorescence were employed to detect α-SMA expression along with Collagen I and Collagen III levels-proteins associated with phenotypic changes. It was observed that Rap2a knockdown inhibited phenotypic transition of CFs (Fig.  D-E). Collectively, these findings suggest that inhibiting Rap2a can hinder functional modifications in CFs. 

## Overexpression of TNIK reverses the effect of Rap2a Deficiency on CFs
Considering that TNIK is targeted by Rap2a [28, 29], our analysis found that knocking out Rap2a reduced the mRNA level and protein content of TNIK in CFs (Fig.  A-B). This indicates that TNIK may be potentially involved in regulating the Rap2a-mediated myocardial fibrosis. To investigate this hypothesis further, we performed plasmid transfection to achieve overexpression of TNIK while simultaneously knocking down Rap2a (Fig.  C), followed by assessing various cellular functions in CFs. The proliferation and migration of CFs in the si-Rap2a + pc-TNIK group were significantly enhanced than the si-Rap2a + pc-NC group (Fig.  D-E and Fig.  ). Additionally, ELISA and immunofluorescence analyses revealed an enhanced expression of Collagen I, α-SMA, Collagen III, and other proteins associated with phenotypic transformation following overexpression of TNIK with Rap2a knockdown (Fig.  F-G). In conclusion, our results indicate that overexpressing TNIK can counteract the effects caused by Rap2a deficiency on CFs'proliferation, migration, and phenotypic transition. 

## TNIK inhibition effectively alleviates the symptoms of MI
Next, we examined the effect of NCB-0846 (a TNIK inhibitor) on the phenotype and degree of myocardial fibrosis in mice with MI by giving them NCB-0846. Compared to the Vehicle group, echocardiographic analysis showed a marked increase in LVEF % and LVES% in the NCB-0846 group 14 days post-MI. Additionally, LVESD and LVEDD were decreased, indicating a notable enhancement in cardiac function. Notably, NCB-0846 significantly improved the impaired cardiac function of mice with MI (Fig.  A). TTC staining demonstrated a reduced size of MI following treatment with NCB-0846 in mice with MI (Fig.  B). HE staining and Masson staining showed that NCB-0846 reduced the inflammatory cell infiltration and inhibited myocardial fibrosis in MI mice (Fig.  C). Compared to the Vehicle group, Collagen I, α-SMA, and Collagen III-related protein were reduced within heart tissues from the NCB-0846 group (Fig.  D-E). In summary, TNIK inhibition effectively alleviates the symptoms of MI. 

## Merlin is TNIK's downstream target
Previous studies have demonstrated the potential of Merlin as a novel substrate for TNIK (Torres-Ayuso et al. ). With the purpose of confirming the regulatory role of TNIK on CFs function through Merlin, we initially examined the association between Merlin and TNIK. Immunoprecipitation was performed to isolate overexpressed Merlin from CFs co-expressing either TNIK or a kinase dead mutant of TNIK (K54R), followed by detection of phosphorylation levels using panthenia/threonine phosphorylated antibodies. The interaction between Merlin and TNIK was further analyzed via western blotting. Indeed, the co-expression of TNIK and Merlin led to an increase in phosphorylation of Merlin, which was found to be responsive to the addition of NCB-0846, an inhibitor of TNIK (Fig.  A). Furthermore, we observed that Merlin and TNIK were able to co-immunoprecipitate (Fig.  A). Interestingly, although a kinase-inactive TNIK mutant (K54R) interacted with Merlin, it did not trigger its phosphorylation (Fig.  A). The Western blot analysis indicates that TNIK inhibition reduces Merlin's phosphorylation level (Fig.  B). In conclusion, Merlin serves as a downstream target for TNIK. 

## Merlin mediates proliferation, migration and phenotypic transition of CFs
Next, we determined whether Merlin plays a role in regulating the function of CFs. The proliferation of Merlin CFs was assessed using EdU following lentivirus infection (Fig.  A), in contrast to the si-NC group, the CFs in the si-Merlin group displayed a remarkably decreased proliferative capacity (Fig.  B). Knockdown of Merlin can hinder the migration of CFs (Fig.  C and Fig S3). After Merlin knockdown, the expressions of Collagen I, α-SMA, and Collagen III in CFs in the si-Merlin group were significantly reduced (Fig.  D). 

## TNIK increases YAP by phosphorylating merlin
To investigate the interaction between TNIK and Merlin/YAP-mediated Rap2a regulation in cardiomyocytes, we employed reverse phase protein microarray to assess the impact of TNIK knockdown on YAP levels. Additionally, immunoprecipitation was utilized to evaluate the activation status of YAP following TNIK knockdown or Merlin phosphorylation, as depicted in Fig.  A-B. Notably, downregulation of YAP levels was observed upon TNIK knockdown. Western blot analysis further demonstrated that TNIK modulates the level of YAP depending on Merlin (Fig.  C). Moreover, we investigated whether the introduction of a phosphomimetic mutant (4D) could rescue the effect of TNIK knockdown on YAP activation levels. Intriguingly, our results revealed that TNIK enhances YAP/TAZ expression in a manner dependent on Merlin phosphorylation (Fig.  D). 

## TNIK/Merlin/YAP axis mediates Rap2a's effects on CFs.
To elucidate the involvement of YAP in regulating CF function, lentiviral transfection was used to construct CFs with knockdown or overexpression of YAP, followed by cell function experiments (Fig.  A-B). Overexpression of YAP enhanced CF proliferation while knockdown significantly reduced it (Fig.  C). Overexpression of YAP promoted the migration of CFs, whereas knockdown had the opposite effect (Fig.  D and Fig S4). Western blot analysis showed that the overexpression of YAP increased the levels of α-SMA, Collagen I, and Collagen III. These are associated with phenotypic conversion; conversely, knockdown of YAP downregulated their expression levels (Fig.  E). Further experiments elucidated that Rap2a mediates the regulation of CFs function through the TNIK/Merlin/YAP axis (Fig.  F). 

## Discussion
Rap2a has an essential role in cell proliferation, migration, differentiation, and apoptosis (Carvalho et al. ; Minato ; Wu et al. ). Wu et al. demonstrated that increased phosphorylation (p)-AKT levels mediated by RAP2 A promote the invasion and migration of renal cancer cells (Wu et al. ). Moreover, Rap2a is implicated in modulating the metastasis and growth of cells across various malignancies such as glioma (Wang et al. ) and gastric cancer (Ti et al. ). However, the involvement of Rap2a in cardiac remodeling after MI remains incompletely investigated; furthermore its impact on the biological function of CFs remains unknown. During our research, we have found Rap2a to be a prospective therapeutic target for MI based on bioinformatics analysis and experimental validation by making use of a mouse model of MI. In the myocardial tissue of mice suffering from myocardial infarction, Rap2a is found to be upregulated, suggesting its potential involvement in the pathogenesis of this condition. Notably, overexpression of Rap2a exacerbates the symptoms of myocardial infarction in our mouse model, highlighting its detrimental effects. Furthermore, our data strongly suggest that increased expression of Rap2a promotes proliferation, migration, and phenotypic transformation of CFs.
The Traf2- and Nck-interacting kinase (TNIK) co-localized with Rap2a, establishing TNIK as the target of Rap2a. Both proteins functioned as regulators of cytoskeleton dynamics and cellular morphology. TNIK's interaction with Rap2 was mediated by its C-terminal CNH domain, while Rap2a facilitated TNIK phosphorylation to enhance its activity (Taira et al. ; Kawabe et al. ). Notably, TNIK has been recognized as a potential therapeutic target for the treatment of fibrosis (Ren et al. ). A recent study conducted by Samuel C Buchl et al. in 2022 disclosed that TNIK is a serine/threonine kinase, and its enzymatic activity is crucial for procollagen secretion. Furthermore, the small molecule compound NCB-0846 exhibited inhibitory effects on liver fibrosis in mice through the inhibition of TNIK (Buchl et al. ). Similarly, our findings demonstrate that NCB-0846 alleviated myocardial infarction symptoms in mice and inhibited myocardial fibrosis. In the past several decades, a great many studies have emphasized the importance of targeting TNIK in diverse cancer types like thyroid cancer, ovarian cancer, and colorectal cancer. This is because TNIK is implicated in tumor cell invasion, migration, and proliferation (Hu et al. ; Wu et al. ; Yi et al. ). The inhibition of TNIK inhibitor was observed to suppress the TGF-β-induced process of EMT, migration, and invasion by targeting the non-Smad and Smad signaling pathways alike. This includes the Wnt signaling pathway involving TCF4/β-catenin (Park and Kim ). In CFs, overexpression of Rap2a upregulates TNIK expression, while inhibition of TNIK attenuates the proliferative, migratory, and phenotypic transformation effects induced by Rap2a.
Merlin, a phosphorylation-regulated scaffold protein, acts as a regulator of downstream effectors (Torres-Ayuso et al. ). Within the scope of this research, we pinpointed the interaction between Merlin and TNIK, establishing Merlin as a downstream target of TNIK. Pedro's investigation revealed potential phosphorylation sites for TNIK on Merlin at T576 as well as several other sites including S13, T23, and T272, which align with the common sequence motif of TNIK. These findings suggest that Merlin could serve as a novel substrate for TNIK. Subsequent experiments confirmed the utilization of Merlin as a new substrate for TNIK in lung tissue. Co-expression analysis demonstrated an association between increased phosphorylation of Merlin and the presence of co-expressed TNIK (Torres-Ayuso et al. ).
The Hippo signaling pathway is the key molecular mechanism through which Merlin functions, and it mediates signal transduction via the MST1/2-LATS1/2-YAP classical signaling cascade (Hu et al. ). Merlin can deactivate YAP phosphorylation and reduce its nuclear localization (Wu et al. ). In our study, TNIK was able to maintain YAP levels in a manner dependent on phosphorylation of Merlin. Numerous prior investigations have shown that the Hippo/YAP signaling pathway is of vital importance in governing cell differentiation, survival, migration, and proliferation. It also contributes to the pathophysiological processes of fibrotic diseases affecting various organs such as lung, liver, heart, skin and kidney. Inhibition of YAP has been shown to suppress fibroblast activation and attenuate organ fibrosis (Mia and Singh ). Masum M Mia et al. discovered that following MI, YAP/TAZ is activated in CFs leading to enhanced cardiac fibrosis. Therefore, YAP/TAZ is of great significance in regulating MI—induced myocardial fibrosis. It does so by modulating the activation of CFs, fibroblast proliferation, and by triggering a fibroinflammatory response (Mia et al. ). Our results suggest that the knockdown of YAP suppresses the phenotypic transformation, proliferation, and migration of CFs; conversely overexpression of YAP exerts an opposing effect.
In conclusion, this study provides evidence that Rap2a promotes myocardial fibrosis by mediating the proliferation, migration, and myofibroblast transformation of cardiac fibroblasts through the TNIK/Merlin/YAP pathway, thereby exacerbating symptoms of myocardial infarction (Fig.  ). As far as we are aware, this represents the initial research to clarify the function of Rap2a in MI and its underlying mechanism. Targeting Rap2a may serve as a possible therapeutic tactic for the management of myocardial infarction and the development of new drugs.
