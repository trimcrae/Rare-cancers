To view a copy of this licence, visit http://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-nd/4.0/.Persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn (PPHN) is a syndrome of acute respiratory failure characterized by systemic hypoxemia and elevated pulmonary arterial pressure, which leads to pathological changes in pulmonary vascular remodeling and endothelial cell function. Long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) H19 has been shown to be involved in the regulation of arterial endothelial cell function, but its regulatory role in PPHN is not fully understood. In the present study, mouse pulmonary artery endothelial cells (MPAECs) were cultured in a hypoxic conditions. Subsequently, the regulatory function of lncRNA H19 on MPAECs was explored by constructing adenoviruses knocking down and overexpressing lncRNA H19. The results revealed that the hypoxic conditions could induce the proliferation and migration of MPAECs, as well as the high expression of lncRNA H19 in MPAECs. Knockdown of lncRNA H19 expression in MPAECs reversed hypoxic environment-induced functional changes in endothelial cells, whereas overexpression of lncRNA H19 further enhanced the proliferation and migration of MPAECs. In addition, lncRNA H19 upregulated the hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α)/vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) pathway through sponge of miNA-20a-5p, which in turn promoted changes in endothelial cell function. LncRNA H19 may interfere with vascular remodeling in hypoxia-induced pulmonary hypertension by upregulating the expression of HIF-1α and VEGF in vascular endothelial cells.

## Introduction
Persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn (PPHN) is a condition characterized by the failure of pulmonary vascular resistance to decrease at birth, leading to reduced pulmonary blood flow and impaired gas exchange (Fortas et al. ). Abnormal pulmonary vascular reactivity is the main clinical feature of PPHN (Singh and Lakshminrusimha ). The primary clinical feature of PPHN is abnormal pulmonary vascular reactivity, which often originates during fetal life and disrupts the normal adaptation of pulmonary circulation at birth. As a result, newborn with PPHN experience significant hypoxemia, increasing the risk of mortality and long-term complications. (Sankaran and Lakshminrusimha ; Babooa et al. ).
Research suggests that adverse conditions, such as chronic hypoxia during pregnancy, can alter the structure and reactivity of the pulmonary vasculature, preventing the normal dilation of pulmonary vessels at birth (Nair and Lakshminrusimha ; Wedgwood et al. ). Hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α), a protein with a basic helix-loop-helix PAS domain, is a key regulator of cellular and developmental responses to low oxygen levels (Rashid et al. ). HIF-1α influences cellular adaptation to hypoxia by upregulating vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), a crucial transcription factor (Song et al. ; Lu et al. ). VEGF is a specific endothelial cell mitogen that regulates endothelial cell differentiation, angiogenesis, and vascular maintenance (Shifren et al. ). It is essential for early vascular development and serves as a critical regulator of pulmonary vascular growth, development, and maintenance throughout the embryonic, fetal, and postnatal stages (Wiszniak and Schwarz ). In the human fetal lung, VEGF is primarily localized to alveolar epithelial cells and myocytes (Shifren et al. ). Studies have shown that VEGF stimulation increases the expression of endothelial nitric oxide synthase, which enhances blood flow in vivo (Kroll and Waltenberger ; Bauters et al. ). Chronic intrauterine pulmonary hypertension inhibits vessel growth, potentially due to disruptions in VEGF-NO signaling (Grover et al. ). Indeed, VEGF expression in the lungs is significantly reduced in chronic intrauterine pulmonary hypertension, suggesting that impaired VEGF signaling could adversely affect vascular function, as well as the growth and structural development of the lung, contributing to the pathogenesis of PPHN (Grover et al. ). However, increased pulmonary VEGF mRNA expression and its receptor have been observed in patients with severe pulmonary hypertension and in animal models of hypoxia-induced pulmonary hypertension (Voelkel and Gomez-Arroyo ; Nadeau et al. ; Tuder et al. ), indicating that the HIF-1α/VEGF axis may play a role in regulating the development of hypoxia-induced PPHN. Despite these findings, the exact regulatory mechanisms remain poorly understood.
Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs), a group of non-coding transcripts ≥ 200 nucleotides in length, are transcribed by RNA polymerase II. LncRNAs have little or no potential to encode proteins (Jathar et al. ; Zhang et al. ). With the development of high-throughput sequencing technologies, cumulative evidence suggests that lncRNAs can regulate the expression of protein-coding genes epigenetically, transcriptionally, and post-transcriptionally, thereby affecting a range of biological processes (Ashrafizadeh et al. ; Bridges et al. ). LncRNA H19, located on human chromosome 11p15.5, is a well-characterized imprinted gene implicated in multiple diseases, including cancer (Müller et al. ), diabetes (Li et al. ), and cardiovascular disorders (Zhang et al. ). Numerous conditions, such as lung cancer, pulmonary hypertension, and asthma, are increasingly recognized as systemic diseases with significant pulmonary manifestations. Notably, the progression of these diseases has been closely associated with the regulation of lncRNA H19 (Wang, et al. ; Zhang et al. ; Chen et al. ). Specifically, lncRNA H19 promotes lung fibrosis in human lung fibroblasts by inhibiting microRNA (miRNA or miR)−140 expression, resulting in enhanced extracellular matrix deposition (Wang, et al. ).
Furthermore, lncRNA H19 exerts regulatory effects on airway smooth muscle cells, adult myocytes, cardiomyocytes, and both small cell and non-small cell lung cancer cell lines (Sun et al. ; Tan et al. ; Xie et al. ). Recent studies have highlighted the role of lncRNAs in pulmonary vascular remodeling. In particular, lncRNA H19 has been implicated in the remodeling of small pulmonary arteries and the pathogenesis of pulmonary hypertension in adults (Wang et al. ; Hoffmann et al. ). Overproliferation of pulmonary artery smooth muscle cells and dysfunction of arterial endothelial cells contribute to vascular remodeling (Kim ). Studies demonstrate that lncRNA H19 can bind to let-7b as a competitive endogenous RNA, downregulating its expression, upregulating cyclin D1, and promoting vascular smooth muscle proliferation and remodeling (Sun et al. ). Additionally, by binding and isolating let-7b, lncRNA H19 upregulates angiotensin I receptor-1 (AT1R) expression in platelet-derived growth factor-BB-stimulated rat pulmonary artery smooth muscle cells (Su et al. ).
Nevertheless, the role of lncRNA H19 in the pathogenesis of persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn (PPHN) remains uncertain. This study aims to develop an in vitro model of pulmonary artery endothelial cell dysfunction to investigate PPHN pathophysiology through hypoxia induction. Following this, the effects of lncRNA H19 knockdown and overexpression will be examined via cell transfection.

## Materials and Methods

## Cell Culture
Mouse pulmonary artery endothelial cells (MPAECs) were purchased from Shanghai Fuheng Biotechnology Co (FH-Y055). Cells were cultured in DMEM (Gibco) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Gibco) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin in a 5% CO2 incubator at 37 °C. When the cells reach 80–90% fusion, digested with 0.25% trypsin and passage them. The culture medium was changed every two days.
The hypoxic intervention cells were placed in a hypoxic cell culture incubator (3% O2), and the normoxic cells were cultured in normal cell culture incubator (21% O2).

## Cell Infection and Transfection
Adenovirus infection with shRNA-lncRNA H19 was used to inhibit the expression of lncRNA H19 in MPAECs, the insert sequence for shRNA-lncRNA H19 group is 5′-GGTCCTGGGTCCATCAATAAA-3′; the insert sequence for the shRNA-NC group is 5′-CGCTGAGTACTTCGAAATGTC-3′. pDC316-mCMV-ZsGreen was used as a vector to construct adenovirus overexpressing lncRNA H19(NR_130973.1). The overexpression and negative control (NC) adenovirus were added directly into the complete medium according to MOI = 100 in hypoxic cell culture incubator. The adenovirus was designed and synthesized by Shenzhen TOP Biotechnology Co., Ltd.
For microRNA transfection, MPAECs were seeded in 6-well plates and allowed to adhere for 24 h. According to the manufacturer’s instructions, the cells were transfected at a final concentration of 30 nM siRNA/mimic using Lipofectamine RNAiMAX Reagent (Thermo Fisher, USA). miR-20a-5p mimic was purchased from Guangzhou RiboBio Co., Ltd. (miR10000529-1–5). The siRNA was synthesized by OBiO Technology (Shanghai) Corp.,Ltd and siRNA-miR-20a-5p sense sequence is CAUCUACUGCAUUACGAGCACUUAA, antisense sequence is UUAAGUGCUCGUAAUGCAGUAGAUG, the siRNA-control sense sequence is CAUUCAACGAUUAGCACGUCCUUAA, and antisense sequence is UUAAGGACGUGCUAAUCGUUGAAUG.
After 24 h of infection/transfection, the medium was changed to normal complete medium for another 24 h culture. And all the experiments were done in a hypoxic culture incubator.

## Cell Migration Assay
Transwell chambers (8 μm pores, Corning Inc., Corning NY) were pre-coated with matrigel (1.25 mg/mL) and incubated at 37 ℃ for 30 min. Add 200 μl (4 × 104cells) serum-free medium of cell suspension to the upper chamber of the transwell, while 700 μl of complete medium containing 20% FBS was added to the lower chamber of the transwell. The cells were incubated in the incubator for 48 h. The upper layer of liquid was discarded from the transwell and soaked in 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min. The chambers were washed three times with PBS (each time with slow shaking for 5 min). After staining with 0.1% crystalline violet for 15 min at room temperature, the chambers were washed three times with PBS. Cotton swabs were used to dab off the upper layer of unpenetrated cells from the chambers. Finally, the cells were observed and photographed under a high magnification microscope.

## Flow Cytometry Analysis
In the cell transfection group, adenovirus was added directly to complete medium at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 100. Flow cytometry was performed 48 h after infection to detect apoptosis or cell cycle.
For apoptosis analysis, the apoptosis rate was detected by annexin V and PI double staining (cat. no. 640932; BioLegend, Inc.) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, cells were collected and washed once with PBS. Next, 100 µl Annexin V Binding Buffer was added to each tube to resuspend the cells. In total, 5 µl Annexin V-APC and 10 µl PI were added to stain the cells at room temperature in the dark for 15 min. Finally, the samples were transferred to flow-through sampling tubes after adding 400 µl buffer.
For cell cycle assay, a DNA content assay kit to evaluate the cell cycle (cat. no. CA1510; Beijing Solarbio Science & Technology Co., Ltd.) was used after collection of each group of cells, following the instructions of the kit’s manufacturer. CytoFLEX S flow cytometer (Beckman Coulter, Inc.) was used to perform the assay.

## Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) Assay
5 × 103 cells per well were seeded in 96-well plates and incubated overnight for adherence. After hypoxic intervention or transfection, CCK-8 assay was carried out at 0, 6, 24, 48, and 72 h. A total of 10 μl CCK-8 solution was added to each well, and the plates were incubated in an incubator for 0.5 h and 450 nm zymography was used to determine the OD value.

## qPCR Assay
Total RNA from MPAECs was extracted according to the RNAiso plus manufacturer’s instructions (Invitrogen). Total RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA according to the instructions of the reverse transcription kit (cat. no. RR037Q; Takara Biomedical Technology Co., Ltd.) and stored at −20 ℃. The sequence of the primers is as follows: GAPDH, which was used as the internal reference gene (upstream sequence 5′–3′: GGCACAGTCAAGGCTGAGAATG, downstream sequence 5′–3′: ATGGTGGGTGAAGACGCCAGTA); target gene lncRNA H19 gene (upstream sequence 5′–3′: TCGCTCCACTGACCTTCTAAAC, downstream sequence 5′–3′: CCTGCCTTTCTATGTGCCATTC); HIF-1α gene (upstream sequence 5′–3′: CCACCACAACTGCCACCACTG, downstream sequence 5′–3′: TGCCACTGTATGCTGATGCCTTAG). For microRNA, Mir-X miRNA First-Strand Synthesis Kit (Takara, Code No.:638315) was used for reverse transcription. miR-20a-5p gene (upstream sequence 5′–3′: CGGCGCTAAAGTGCTTATAGTGC, downstream sequence was obtained form the Mir-X miRNA First-Strand Synthesis Kit); U6 as the internal reference gene (upstream sequence 5′–3′: CTCGCTTCGGCAGCACA, downstream sequence 5′–3′: AACGCTTCACGAATTTGCGT). qPCR detection was performed using SYBR Green Mixture (cat. no. RR820Q; Takara Biomedical Technology Co., Ltd.). The reaction system and reaction conditions were prepared according to the instructions. There are three samples in each group, and each sample is set with two repetitions holes. The relative expression quantity (RQ) of the target genes was calculated using the ΔΔCq method (23): RQ = 2 −ΔΔCq.

## Western Blotting
RIPA lysis buffer was used to lyse MPAECs at the end of the treatment period and sonication was used to fully lyse the cells. The cells were then centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 ℃. Next, the supernatant was separated and analyzed with BCA Protein Quantification Kit (cat. no. P0010S; Shanghai Biyuntian Biotechnology Co., Ltd) to adjust the total cellular protein concentration. Samples were then incubated at 100 ℃ for 5 min after adding 5X SDS-PAGE sample loading buffer. Proteins were separated by SDS-PAGE and then transferred to PVDF membranes, which were then blocked with skimmed milk. After three washes with TBS-Tween 20 (TBST) buffer, the primary antibody was added and incubated overnight on a shaker at 4 ℃, anti-HIF-1α (1:1000; cat. no. PA3-16,521; Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc.), anti-VEGF (1:1000; cat. no. MA5-13,182; Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc.), and anti-β-actin (1:1000; cat. no. AC026; ABclonal Technology Co.Ltd.). After three washes with TBST buffer, the secondary antibody was added and incubated at room temperature for 1–1.5 h. Subsequently, the proteins were visualized using ECL system (cat. no.WBULP; Merck Pty. Ltd.). Finally, ImageJ software (version 2022) was used for semiquantitative analysis of protein expression. β-actin was used as internal reference.

## Statistical Analysis
The experimental data were statistically and analyzed using SPSS 23. 0 software (IBM Corp.). Mean ± standard deviation (Mean ± SD) was used to express the data results. Graphpad software (Version 9.0) was used to graph the statistics. For comparisons between the two groups, independent samples t tests were was performed. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results

## Hypoxia Induces Dysfunction and Promotes the Expression of lncRNA H19 and Angiogenesis-Related Genes in MPAECs
The regulation of endothelial cell migration is fundamental to angiogenesis. Therefore, in this study, MPAECs were cultured in an atmospheric low oxygen workstation (3% O2, 5% CO2, 94% N2, 37 °C) or an atmospheric normoxic incubator (21% O2, 5% CO2, 74% N2, 37 °C) for 48 h, respectively. Transwell results showed that compared with the normoxic culture group, low oxygen treatment with 3% O2 significantly promoted the migration of MPAECs (Fig.  A). As shown in Fig.  B, C, hypoxia significantly inhibited the apoptosis of MPAECs. MPAECs proliferation was enhanced under hypoxic conditions (Fig.  D). The above results suggest the successful establishment of a hypoxia-induced MPAECs dysfunction mimicking pulmonary hypertension model, which provides a basis for further investigation of the mechanism of vascular remodeling in PPHN. 
HIF-1α and VEGF are key factors of endothelial cell angiogenesis. In our study, qPCR results showed that hypoxia induction significantly promoted the expression level of lncRNA H19 in MPAECs (Fig.  E). In addition, the expression of HIF-1α mRNA was also significantly increased (Fig.  F). Further, hypoxia induction significantly upregulated HIF-1α and VEGF protein expression in MPAECs. This suggests that lncRNA H19 may be involved in mediating the potential role of angiogenesis in MPAECs.

## LncRNA H19 Promotes Protein Expression of HIF-1α and VEGF in MPAECs Under Hypoxic Conditions
LncRNA H19 knockdown and overexpression cell models were constructed to further clarify the regulatory relationship between lncRNA H19 and HIF-1α/VEGF. The results showed that knockdown of lncRNA H19 significantly reduced the mRNA and protein levels of HIF-1α, as well as the protein expression levels of VEGF, under hypoxic conditions (Fig.  A–E). Conversely, the overexpression of lncRNA H19 significantly promote the mRNA and protein expression of HIF-1α, as well as the protein expression levels of VEGF (Fig.  F–J). This suggests that lncRNA H19 may play a critical role in hypoxia-induced pulmonary hypertension by regulating the HIF-1α/VEGF signaling pathway. 

## LncRNA H19 Affects Migration and Proliferation of MPAECs Under Hypoxic Conditions
The migration and proliferation of MPAECs were detected by transwell and CCK8 assay in vitro. Transwell results showed that knockdown of lncRNA H19 significantly reduced the migration of MPAECs, while overexpression of lncRNA H19 significantly increased the migration of MPAECs (Fig.  A, B). In addition, CCK8 results showed that knocking down lncRNA H19 significantly inhibits proliferation of MPAECs, while overexpression of lncRNA H19 promotes proliferation of MPAECs (Fig.  C, D). 

## LncRNA H19 Affects Apoptosis and Cell Cycle of MPAECs Under Hypoxic Conditions
Flow cytometry was used to further observe the effects of knockdown/overexpression of lncRNA H19 in a hypoxic environment on apoptosis in MPAECs. The results showed that the apoptosis of MPAECs was significantly increased after knock down the expression of lncRNA H19 (Fig.  A, B). Conversely, after overexpression of lncRNA H19, the anti-apoptotic ability of MPAECs was significantly enhanced (Fig.  C, D). 
The cell cycle includes the G1, S, G2, and M phases. Subsequently, the DNA content of the cells was detected using a flow cytometric sorter, which allowed for the determination of the temporal phase of the cell cycle that the cells were in and the proportion of cells in each phase. We found that the (G2 + S)/(G1 + G2 + S) ratio of endothelial cells was significantly reduced after inhibiting the expression of lncRNA H19 using shRNA, which suggests that the cell proliferation activity was significantly inhibited (Fig.  E, F). However, after overexpression of lncRNA H19, the (G2 + S)/(G1 + G2 + S) ratio of MPAECs was significantly increased, which indicated that the cell proliferative activity was increased (Fig.  G, H).

## miR-20a-5p Alleviates Dysfunction of MPAECs by Regulating the HIF-1α/VEGF Signaling Pathway
Previous studies have shown that lncRNA H19 participated in the regulation of myofibroblast fibrosis by directly targeting miR-20a-5p (Lin et al. ). Our results confirmed that lncRNA H19 negatively regulates the expression of miR-20a-5p (Fig.  A, B). However, the effect of miR-20a-5p on MPAECs is unclear. Therefore, siRNA was used to knock down miR-20a-5p in MPAECs to further validate the targeted regulatory role of lncRNA H19. As shown in Fig.  C–E, after miR-20a-5p was knocked down in MPAECs, the mRNA and protein expression of HIF-1α were significantly increased. Furthermore, knockdown of miR-20a-5p significantly increased the migration (Fig. H) and decreased the apoptosis (Fig. I, J) of MPAECs. 

## lncRNA H19 Influences MPAECs Migration and Apoptosis by Regulating miR-20a-5p
Using transwell and flow cytometry to examine the effects of knocking down or adding miR-20a-5p mimics on endothelial cell migration and apoptosis in the context of lncRNA H19 knockdown or overexpression. The results showed that in the case of lncRNA H19 inhibition using shRNA-LncRNA H19, adding siRNA-miR-20a-5p promoted endothelial cell migration and inhibited the apoptosis of MPAECs (Fig. A, C, D). Conversely, in the case of lncRNA H19 overexpression, adding miR-20a-5p mimics inhibited endothelial cell migration and promoted endothelial cell apoptosis (Fig. B, E, F). These findings indicate that lncRNA H19 affects endothelial cell function by regulating the expression of miR-20a-5p. 

## Discussion
PPHN is a clinical neonatal critical illness with a relatively high incidence, which may occur in 1–2 per 1,000 newborns (Rubin ; Kipfmueller, et al. ). It has become a disease associated with high morbidity, mortality, and disability (Ruoss et al. ; Chetan et al. ). Impaired relaxation of the pulmonary vasculature in the early postnatal period leads to pulmonary circulatory resistance exceeding body circulatory resistance, resulting in severe hypoxemia and respiratory distress, and ultimately PPHN (Sankaran and Lakshminrusimha ). Currently, the clinical treatment strategy for PPHN is focused on mechanical ventilation to maintain systemic circulatory pressure and reduce pulmonary hypertension (Pels et al. ). Neonatal care, including the use of inhaled NO and other vasodilators, improves the prognosis of infants with PPHN (Ball et al. ). However, clinical statistics show that ≤ 30% of newborns with PPHN do not respond to this treatment and require invasive life support measures (Nair and Lakshminrusimha ). Therefore, it is crucial to further explore effective novel treatment strategies for PPHN.
The pathophysiology of PPHN is characterized by increased pulmonary vascular resistance, resulting in reduced pulmonary blood flow and, therefore, reduced oxygenated blood volume returning to the left side of the heart, leading to hypoxia, reduced end-organ perfusion, acidosis, and cyanosis. Therefore, hypoxic culture is common used to simulate the pathological state of PPHN in mechanistic studies (Dakshinamurti ). In the present study, hypoxic treatment with 3% O2 was used to induce lung endothelial cell dysfunction to establish a PPHN cell model. The results showed that the proliferation and migration of endothelial cells were significantly promoted, while apoptosis was significantly decreased under hypoxic conditions. Previous study has also found that low oxygen environment promotes the vascular remodeling of endothelial cells (Chelladurai, et al. ), which is consistent with the present findings.
LncRNAs are a kind of ncRNA with a length > 200 bp, which is widely involved in the occurrence and development of a variety of diseases, such as cancer and respiratory diseases (Paulin and Michelakis ). Previous studies have demonstrated that lncRNA H19 regulates endothelial cell function and angiogenesis in a variety of diseases, including glioma (Jia et al. ), hypoxic-ischemic brain damage (Fang et al. ), and arteriosclerosis obliterans (Li et al. ). In the current study, the hypoxic environment effectively promote the expression level of lncRNA H19 in MPAECs, which was accompanied by high expression of HIF-1α and VEGF. HIF-1α is an important transcription factor that regulates the effects of hypoxia. HIF-1α has a broad distribution and role, which can enhance the tolerance of vascular endothelial cell to hypoxia by regulating downstream target genes (Grover et al. ). It has been shown that increased HIF-1α signaling contributes to pulmonary vascular remodeling in adult pulmonary hypertension (Makker et al. ). VEGF is an important target gene for HIF-1α, as well as one of the most potent and critical regulators of pulmonary vascular growth, development, and maintenance throughout embryonic, fetal, and postnatal life (Jiang et al. ; Johns et al. ). VEGF is strongly expressed in the developing respiratory epithelium from the embryonic stage, and promotes angiogenesis by regulating endothelial cell proliferation, migration, and differentiation (Pugliese et al. ; Maron et al. ; Thompson and Lawrie ). Disruption of VEGF gene expression leads to severe defects in blood vessel formation. Loss of a single VEGF allele results in a notable reduction in endothelial cells, which in turn causes early embryonic mortality (Krishnan and Rosenzweig ). Previous studies have reported elevated lung VEGF mRNA in chronic hypoxia-induced pulmonary hypertension (Tuder et al. ; Christou et al. ). In addition, IL-33 expression is elevated in human pulmonary artery epithelial cells under hypoxic conditions, which may be due to the increased downstream expression of HIF-1α and VEGF. This, in turn, triggers vascular remodeling and leading to hypoxic pulmonary hypertension (Liu et al. ), which is consistent with the present results. A recent clinical study has also demonstrated  that newborns with PPHN exhibit lower levels of VEGF in their blood (Lassus et al. ). In addition, another study demonstrated a reduction in pulmonary VEGF due to monocrotaline-mediated pulmonary hypertension (Partovian et al. ). These studies suggest the involvement of non-hypoxia-dependent mechanisms that may regulate pulmonary VEGF expression, such as altered hemodynamic stress. Therefore, the expression of VEGF and its intrinsic regulatory mechanisms in PPHN models mediated by different mechanisms need to be further explored.
The current study focused on lncRNA H19, which is the upstream of the HIF-1α and VEGF genes. It was found that hypoxia induced elevated expression of lncRNA H19 in pulmonary artery epithelial cells of mice, suggesting that lncRNA H19 is involved in mediating angiogenesis in MPAECs. Numerous studies have demonstrated that lncRNAs play powerful regulatory functions in biological processes at the epigenetic, transcriptional, and post-transcriptional levels (Qin, et al. ; Qin, et al. ; Boucherat et al. ). Previous research has revealed that vascular remodeling is the result of pulmonary artery smooth muscle cell over proliferation and arterial endothelial cell dysfunction (Kim ). LncRNA H19 plays a central role in vascular smooth muscle cell proliferation and myocardial extracellular matrix alteration, suggesting that it may be a potential drug target in pulmonary arterial hypertension. In addition, it has been shown that patients with end-stage pulmonary arterial hypertension have significantly increased levels of lncRNA H19 in their blood, which is positively correlated with the degree of right ventricular hypertrophy (Su et al. ). The lncRNA H19/let-7b axis was reported to be involved in PDGF-BB-stimulated pulmonary artery vascular remodeling (Sun et al. ). Similarly, Wang et al. (Wang et al. ) reported an upregulation of lncRNA H19 in a melatonin-mediated rat model of pulmonary hypertension. Targeted modulation of the lncRNA H19/miR-200a/PDCD4 axis exerts therapeutic effects in pulmonary arterial hypertension in rats. The above results suggest that lncRNA H19 may play a critical role in the pathological progression of PPHN. In the present study, the anti-apoptotic ability of MPAECs was impaired after lncRNA H19 was knocked down. Conversely, overexpression of lncRNA H19 significantly enhanced the anti-apoptotic ability of MPAECs, which is consistent with previous findings.
It has been reported that miR-20a-5p is a target of lncRNA H19 (Lin et al. ), and the current results also found that lncRNA H19 could negatively regulate the expression of miR-20a-5p. However, it is not yet known whether lncRNA H19 affects endothelial cell function through regulating miR-20a-5p under hypoxic conditions. Therefore, miR-20a-5p siRNA or mimics were added while knocking down or overexpressing lncRNA H19. The results showed that this effectively reversed the effects of lncRNA H19 on endothelial cell migration and apoptosis, which indicates that lncRNA H19 may be involved in regulating the HIF-1α/VEGF signaling pathway by targeting miR-20a-5p to promote the improvement of pulmonary artery endothelial cell function. Thus, the present results suggest that the lncRNA H19/miR-20a-5p/HIF-1α/VEGF regulatory axis may play an important role in the pathological regulatory process of PPHN. Notably, the current PPHN cell model exposure factor was a hypoxic environment. Combined with the results of previous studies, it was found that different environmental and temporal exposure factors may lead to different results. Therefore, further studies are still needed to investigate and validate the PPHN regulatory mechanisms in different models.
Although our results revealed that lncRNA H19 may interfere with vascular remodeling in hypoxia-induced pulmonary hypertension by upregulating the expression of HIF-1α and VEGF in MPAECs, and the lncRNA H19/miR-20a-5p/HIF-1α/VEGF regulatory axis may play a key regulatory role in the PPHN process, there are still several limitations in the present study. Firstly, our study focuses on cell experiments and did not investigate the function of lncRNA H19 in animal model. Therefore, whether lncRNA H19 can be used as a therapeutic target for PPHN still needs further investigation. Secondly, the target of lncRNA H19 is based on others’ research, and new targets will be sought through high-throughput sequencing in subsequent studies.
