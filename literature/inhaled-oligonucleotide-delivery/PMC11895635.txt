## Abstract
Airway mucous cell metaplasia and mucous hypersecretion is one of the key characteristic pathophysiological status of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). micro(mi)RNAs are acknowledged as non‐encoding RNA molecules playing important roles in gene expression regulation. In this study, we searched the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database for the differentially expressed miRNAs between COPD and non‐COPD controls with bioinformatics analysis. Finally, we focused on miR‐513a‐5p and investigated the potential mechanism by which miR‐513a‐5p regulates airway mucous hypersecretion and goblet cell metaplasia. A dual‐luciferase reporter assay was then showing that miR‐513a‐5p targeted the 3′‐UTR of TFR1 and inhibited its expression in vitro. In vivo transfection demonstrated that TFR1 downregulation partially blocked MUC5AC hypersecretion and goblet cell hyperplasia in COPD model rats. In vitro study, CSE increased the intracellular expression and secretion of MUC5AC by BEAS‐2B branchial epithelial cells in the BEAS‐2B cell and THP‐1 cell coculture system. Coculture with either miR‐513a‐5p mimic‐pretreated or TFR1‐deficient THP‐1 cells attenuated intracellular MUC5AC expression in BEAS‐2B cells exposed to CSE. ELISA demonstrated that transfection of TFR1 siRNA or pretreatment with miR‐513a‐5p mimic reduced the secretion of inflammatory factors that are responsible for airway goblet cell hyperplasia, such as IL‐1β, IL‐13, and IL‐17, by THP‐1 cells after CSE stimulation. Our findings supported that miR‐513a‐5p/TFR1 signaling axis might activate macrophages as well as promote airway inflammation and airway mucous cell hyperplasia in COPD.

## 
miR‐513a‐5p targets TFR1 and negatively regulates TFR1 expression in vitro
TFR1 was predicted to be a downstream target gene of miR‐513a‐5p via the TargetScan database (https://www.targetscan.org/vert_80/). As indicated in Figure  , TFR1 was selected as a possible miR‐513a‐5p target gene. To verify the target relationship between miR‐513a‐5p and TFR1, a dual‐luciferase reporter assay was further performed. Briefly, a psiCHECK‐2 plasma carrying the wild‐type (WT)‐TFR1 3′‐UTR or a mutant (MT)‐TFR1 3′‐UTR (Figure  ) was constructed. MiR‐513a‐5p mimics, miR‐513a‐5p inhibitor, NC miRNA, or NC miRNA inhibitor were transfected into the A549 cell line together with the WT‐TFR1 3′‐UTR or MT‐TFR1 3′‐UTR. The results indicated that miR‐513a‐5p mimics considerably attenuated the activity of luciferase in the WT‐TFR1 3′‐UTR. Moreover, the miR‐513a‐5p inhibitor significantly enhanced the luciferase activity of the WT‐TFR1 3′‐UTR. However, these changes in luciferase activity were not observed with the MT‐TFR1 3′‐UTR (Figure  ). These results indicated that miR‐513a‐5p could target the 3′‐UTR and inhibit the expression of TFR1 in vitro. Real‐time qPCR was performed to verify the correlation of miR‐513a‐5p with TFR1 in human THP‐1‐derived macrophages. The results showed significant suppression of TFR1 by miR‐513a‐5p mimic transfection. In addition, transfection of the miR‐513a‐5p inhibitor markedly enhanced the expression of TFR1 in human THP‐1‐derived macrophages (Figure  ). Similar results of synthesized cellular TFR1 were verified by western blotting, as shown in Figure  .

## 
TFR1 promotes airway goblet cell hyperplasia and airway mucus hypersecretion in COPD model rats
Goblet cell hyperplasia and mucus hypersecretion are representative pathological changes that occur during the remodeling of airways in COPD. Considering that airway goblet cells secrete a large amount of acidic mucin, which is their main characteristic, AB‐PAS staining was used to measure the degree of airway goblet cell hyperplasia (Figure  ). In the control (CTL) rats, AB‐PAS‐positive epithelial cells were rarely observed in the airway tracks. However, AB‐PAS‐positive epithelial cells were easily observed in the airway epithelium in COPD model rats (Figure  ). To evaluate TFR1 in the formation of airway goblet cell hyperplasia, rats were transfected with TFR1‐siRNA by liposome complex inhalation through the nasal cavity. The transfection efficacy was determined by western blotting of harvested rat lung tissue (Figure  ). Pretreatment with TFR1‐siRNA intra‐airway transfection significantly reduced goblet cell hyperplasia in rats with CS‐induced COPD (Figure  ).
 Based on the evidence that MUC5AC constitutes the majority of airway gel‐forming mucus secretion, MUC5AC levels in rat BALF were assessed by ELISA. Intra‐airway transfection with TFR1‐siRNA significantly attenuated MUC5AC secretion in COPD model rats (Figure  ). Immunochemistry was used to visualize intracellular MUC5AC in bronchial epithelial cells from model rats. Intracellular MUC5AC levels in the bronchial epithelium are expressed as integrated optical density/area (IOD/area) (Figure  ). Compared with CTL rats, the proportion of bronchial epithelial cells that were positively stained with the MUC5AC antibody was markedly increased in COPD model rats. TFR1‐siRNA pretreatment partially reduced the proportion of bronchial epithelial cells that were positively stained for MUC5AC (Figure  ). Similar results were confirmed in MUC5AC secreted into bronchoalveolar lavage fluid of rats (BALF; Figure  ).

## The miR‐513a‐5p/TFR1 signaling axis activates macrophages and promotes airway inflammation and mucus hypersecretion in vitro
Before in vitro experiments, a time gradient test was designed to determine the optimal CSE exposure time. Cellular viability was assessed using a conventional MTT reduction assay. The results revealed no significant decreases in the viability of BEAS‐2B, 16HBE14o‐, or THP‐1‐derived macrophages after exposure to 5% CSE for up to 12 h (Figure  ). Based on this evidence, 5% CSE for up to 12 h of exposure was selected as the appropriate CSE stimulation in the following experiments.
Previous studies have indicated that the TFR1‐positive cells that are enriched around the airway are macrophages. We designed two ex vivo coculture systems that included human THP‐1‐derived macrophages and the human bronchial cell line BEAS‐2B, THP‐1‐derived macrophages and another human bronchial cell line 16HBE14o‐, respectively. The levels of IL‐1β, IL‐13, and IL‐17, which are the major inflammatory factors that promote goblet cell metaplasia in COPD, in the culture supernatants were measured by ELISA. , These inflammatory factors were detected at significantly lower concentrations in superstates from cocultures treated with the miR‐513a‐5p mimic or cocultures with TFR1‐defective macrophages after CSE stimulation (Figure  ).
 Considering that intracellular MUC5AC is one of the major biomarkers of goblet cell metaplasia in the airway epithelium, intracellular MUC5AC levels in BEAS‐2B or 16HBE14o‐ cells were visualized by immunochemistry and laser confocal microscopy. Intracellular MUC5AC protein levels were normalized to those of the negative controls, respectively. The results indicated a significant increase in intracellular MUC5AC protein expression in the BEAS‐2B cell line after CS stimulation. Coculture with either miR‐513a‐5p‐pretreated or TFR1‐deficient macrophages attenuated intracellular MUC5AC in BEAS‐2B cell lines after CS stimulation. Similar findings were proven in the 16HBE14o‐ and THP‐1‐derived macrophages co‐culture systems (Figure  ).

## DISCUSSION
Airway mucus hypersecretion and goblet cell metaplasia are the typical pathophysiological features of COPD. Airway goblet cell metaplasia and mucus hypersecretion not only lead to airflow limitation in small airways but also increase the incidence of lower respiratory tract infection. In recently updated GOLD2023 guidelines, the correlation between chronic airway mucus hypersecretion and the concurrent decrease in FEV1 was first proposed. Among airway mucins, sputum MUC5AC, which is primarily secreted by proliferative goblet cells, has been more specifically associated with acute exacerbation of COPD and greater decrease in lung function. 

Several studies have revealed the differential expression of several miRNAs in various clinical samples, such as the peripheral blood, lung tissue, and sputum, of COPD patients. , , These differentially expressed miRNAs might play different pathophysiological roles in the occurrence and progression of COPD. However, the vast underlying mechanisms of these miRNAs remain unclear. In our research, the GSE38974 dataset was selected, and ultimately, we focused on miR‐513a‐5p due to its decreased expression in both the lung tissue and peripheral blood of COPD patients. Online database prediction revealed the possible binding of miR‐513a‐5p and the TFR1 3′‐UTR. To define the relationship between miR‐513a‐5p and TFR1, a luciferase reporter assay was carried out. In the luciferase reporter assay, the activity of the TFR1 3′‐UTR was reduced by the miR‐513a‐5p mimic, and the miR‐513a‐5p inhibitor promoted its activity. TFR1 protein expression is greatly reduced by miR‐513a‐5p mimic treatment. However, the expression of TFR1 was increased due to the miR‐513a‐5p inhibitor. Our study obtained sufficient evidence to confirm that miR‐513a‐5p targets TFR1 and regulates its expression in patients with COPD.
Upregulated TFR1 has already been reported in several chronic inflammatory diseases, such as inflammatory bowel disease and pulmonary fibrosis. , More recently, a study revealed that ferroptosis caused by CS is involved in the pathogenesis of COPD. Upregulated TFR1 expression after CSE acts as an important phenomenon that facilitates iron overload in tissues and is accompanied by increased ROS levels. Considering the potential risks of BALF sampling for COPD patients with poor lung function in this study as well as evidence that suggests that induced sputum sampling is a safe and noninvasive method to analyze airway inflammation and the inflammatory microenvironment in COPD patients, inducing sputum was ultimately selected to measure miRNA levels. Our results indicated that sputum miR‐513a‐5p expression was significantly lower in the COPD group and correlated with poor lung function.
Disrupted iron homeostasis has been reported in several inflammatory diseases. Increased iron deposition in lung tissues has been recognized as one of the key pathophysiological characteristics of COPD patients who smoke. , This increased intracellular iron is more common in alveolar macrophages, probably due to the phagocytosis of AM secondary to cellular apoptosis in COPD patients. These iron deposits in the lungs and iron overload in AM interfere with the normal physiological function of AM, leading to the predisposition of infection in COPD patients. Thus, researchers proposed that intracellular iron chelators might offer potential therapeutic benefits for COPD patients. , However, several studies have described the risk of iron‐deficiency anemia in COPD patients. , Therefore, the risk of impaired systemic iron metabolism associated with the application of iron chelators in COPD patients needs to be fully evaluated.
According to our animal studies, CSE increases the expression of TFR1 in rat lungs. Moreover, pulmonary TFR1 levels were positively correlated with the metaplasia of rat epithelial goblet cells and airway MUC5AC secretion. A previous study suggested that macrophages are the predominant source of increased TFR1‐positive cell numbers in the lung in a house dust‐induced asthma model. Therefore, we designed an in vitro coculture system with THP‐1‐derived macrophages and the bronchial epithelial cell line BEAS‐2B to verify the relationship between TFR1 expression and airway mucus hypersecretion and goblet cell metaplasia in COPD models. Our study design is similar to some other experiments that selected bilateral cell compartments to establish a macrophage and epithelial cell coculture system. , The major advantage of this coculture system that includes bronchial epithelial cells and macrophages is to better match the cellular crosstalk between these cells that is induced by external stimulation in vitro. This coculture system markedly facilitated the better understanding of the changes in the morphology and function of bronchial epithelial cells in vitro. In our research, bronchial epithelial cells cocultured with TFR 1‐knockout macrophages showed less sensitivity to CSE stimulation. Lower intracellular MUC5AC expression was observed in bronchial epithelial cells cocultured with TFR‐1‐knockout macrophages exposed to CSE than in those cocultured with wild‐type macrophages, indicating that TFR‐1 might play an essential role in CSE stimulation‐induced bronchial epithelial goblet cell metaplasia. Several in vivo and in vitro studies have demonstrated that IL‐1β, IL‐13, and IL‐17 are the major inflammatory factors that lead to airway goblet cell metaplasia. , , In our experiment, we observed lower levels of IL‐1β, IL‐13, and IL‐17 in the coculture supernatants of TFR1‐deficient macrophages after CSE stimulation than in that of wild‐type macrophages. This evidence suggests that the overexpression of TFR1 in CSE‐treated macrophages is one of the important prerequisites for the secretion of inflammatory cytokines that are essential for inducing airway goblet cell metaplasia.
In conclusion, our study first revealed the important role of reduced miR‐513a‐5p expression in the pathogenesis of COPD and decreased lung function in COPD patients. TFR1, which is targeted by miR‐513a‐5p, is highly expressed in lung tissues in COPD model rats. In vitro, upon CSE stimulation, macrophages overexpress TFR1 and induce airway goblet cell metaplasia through the hypersecretion of IL‐1β, IL‐13, and IL‐17. These effects can be partially suppressed in vitro by additional miR‐513a‐5p supplementation or TFR1 knockdown in macrophages.
