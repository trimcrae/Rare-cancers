## Abstract
Melanoma, recognized as the most aggressive type of skin cancer, has experienced a notable increase in cases, especially within populations with fair skin. This highly aggressive cancer is largely driven by UV radiation exposure, resulting in the uncontrolled growth and malignant transformation of melanocytes. The cGAS-STING pathway, an immune signaling mechanism responsible for detecting double-stranded DNA in the cytoplasm, is essential for mediating the immune response against melanoma. This pathway serves a dual purpose: it enhances antitumor immunity by activating immune cells, but it can also promote tumor growth when chronically activated by creating an immunosuppressive environment. This review comprehensively examines the multifaceted implication of the cGAS-STING pathway in melanoma pathogenesis and treatment. We explore its molecular mechanisms, including epigenetic regulation, interaction with signaling pathways such as AR signaling, and modulation by various cellular effectors like TG2 and activin-A. The therapeutic potential of modulating the cGAS-STING pathway is highlighted, with promising results from STING agonists, combination therapies with immune checkpoint inhibitors, and novel drug delivery systems, including nanoparticles and synthetic drugs. Our findings underscore the importance of the cGAS-STING pathway in melanoma, presenting it as a critical target for enhancing anti-tumor immunity. By leveraging this pathway, future therapeutic strategies can potentially convert ‘cold’ tumors into ‘hot’ tumors, making them more susceptible to immune responses.
Keywords: Skin cancer, Immunotherapy, Nanomedicine, Oncolytic viruses, Nano vaccines, cGAS, STING

## Introduction
Melanoma, the deadliest form of skin cancer, has experienced a significant increase in incidence, particularly among fair-skinned populations in regions like Northern Europe, North America, Australia, and New Zealand. This highly aggressive cancer originates from the uncontrolled growth and malignant transformation of melanocytes, largely triggered by UV radiation exposure from sunlight and tanning beds, which are responsible for 60–70% of all cases [ ]. Early detection is vital, as survival rates drop significantly with advanced stages; this is achieved through visual inspection and dermoscopic evaluation [ ]. Treatment options currently encompass surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, immunotherapy, and targeted therapies, which are often combined to address challenges like resistance and adverse side effects [ ]. Signaling pathways are crucial for melanoma development and progression as they regulate key cellular functions, including growth, survival, migration, and metabolism. The MAPK pathway, often triggered by BRAF and NRAS mutations, enhances cancer cell proliferation and apoptosis resistance. The PI3K pathway, altered through PTEN loss, similarly fosters cell growth and survival. The Wnt pathway affects melanocyte development and migration, aiding tumor invasion. Metabolic reprogramming in melanoma, driven by BRAFV600E and other mutations, supports rapid cell proliferation and adaptation to low-oxygen environments. Insights into these pathways have been critical for creating targeted therapies, improving treatment outcomes, and exploring new strategies for prevention and early detection [ – ]. The cGAS/STING pathway is an immune signaling mechanism where the enzyme cyclic GMP-AMP synthase (cGAS) detects cytoplasmic double-stranded DNA (dsDNA), leading to the production of cyclic GMP-AMP (cGAMP). This molecule then binds to the stimulator of interferon genes (STING) protein, triggering a cascade that activates type I interferons (IFNs) and other inflammatory cytokines. In cancer, the cGAS/STING pathway plays a dual role. It can enhance antitumor immunity by promoting the maturation of dendritic cells (DCs), recruiting T cells, and inducing cell senescence and apoptosis. Conversely, prolonged activation of the pathway can facilitate tumor growth and metastasis by fostering an inflammatory environment, aiding in immune evasion, and enhancing the invasiveness of cancer cells. This dual nature makes it a critical target for cancer therapies, where both agonists and inhibitors of the pathway are being explored for therapeutic benefits [ , ]. Previous reviews on the role of cGAS-STING signaling in melanoma have been relatively superficial and have not thoroughly examined its comprehensive impact on melanoma pathogenesis and treatment. These reviews often provide a general overview without delving into the intricate mechanisms by which the cGAS-STING pathway influences tumor immunity, immune evasion, and therapeutic resistance. They frequently overlook the detailed molecular interactions, the specific epigenetic modifications leading to STING suppression, and the potential therapeutic benefits of reactivating this pathway. Moreover, earlier analyses have not fully explored the diverse therapeutic strategies targeting cGAS-STING, including novel agonists, delivery systems, and combination therapies with immune checkpoint inhibitors. A more exhaustive review is needed to systematically assess the current research, highlight critical findings, and identify gaps in the understanding of cGAS-STING signaling in melanoma, ultimately paving the way for more effective therapeutic approaches [ , ].

## cGAS-STING pathway overview
The cGAS-STING pathway is activated when cGAS detects dsDNA in the cytoplasm, indicating potential pathogenic infection or cellular damage. cGAS, an enzyme in the cytoplasm, binds to this cytosolic DNA, undergoing a structural change that allows it to convert ATP and GTP into cGAMP. This reaction produces 2’3’-cyclic GMP-AMP (2’3’-cGAMP), a secondary messenger in the signaling cascade. Once synthesized, cGAMP binds to STING, a transmembrane protein in the endoplasmic reticulum (ER). This binding causes STING to change shape and relocate from the ER to the Golgi apparatus, a crucial step for further signaling. In the Golgi, STING activates TANK-binding kinase 1 (TBK1) and IκB kinase (IKK). TBK1 phosphorylates interferon regulatory factor 3 (IRF3), while IKK phosphorylates nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB). The phosphorylated IRF3 and NF-κB then move to the nucleus to act as transcription factors. Within the nucleus, IRF3 mainly induces the production of type I IFNs and other inflammation-related genes, while NF-κB promotes the expression of various genes, including proinflammatory cytokines like interleukin-6 (IL-6) and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α)(Fig.  ). The cGAS-STING pathway is crucial in immune responses but has a dual role. It is vital for antiviral defense and maintaining genomic stability, yet its malfunction can lead to autoimmune diseases and chronic inflammation. In antiviral defense, this pathway detects viral DNA, leading to the production of interferons and cytokines that create an antiviral state. In autoimmune diseases, improper activation of cGAS-STING by self-DNA in the cytosol can cause autoimmunity, contributing to conditions like Aicardi-Goutières syndrome and systemic lupus erythematosus. In cancer, the pathway can enhance anti-tumor immunity by promoting type I IFNs and other cytokines that stimulate immune cell activity. However, chronic activation can also foster an immunosuppressive environment, complicating treatment strategies. Due to its key role in immune regulation, the cGAS-STING pathway offers various therapeutic opportunities. Activating the pathway with cGAS or STING agonists can boost immune responses against viral infections and improve cancer immunotherapies. Conversely, inhibiting cGAS or STING can be beneficial for treating autoimmune diseases where the pathway is overly active. Researchers are developing small molecule inhibitors and other methods to modulate this pathway effectively. Combining cGAS-STING modulators with other treatments, such as immune checkpoint inhibitors, may produce synergistic effects in the treatment of cancers and chronic infections [ , – ].
 

## Molecular mechanisms of cGAS-STING pathway in melanoma
STING signaling in human melanoma cells significantly influences antitumor immune responses. Researchers have observed variability in STING and cGAS expression among melanoma cell lines, with many exhibiting non-functional STING signaling. In cell lines where STING signaling is intact, activation with the agonist 2’3’-cGAMP led to increased expression of major histocompatibility complex (MHC) class I, making these cells more recognizable and susceptible to lysis by tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs). This enhancement is mediated through the production of IFNβ and CXCL10, which are crucial components of the immune response. Conversely, cell lines with defective STING signaling did not exhibit these enhancements, suggesting that impaired STING signaling could be a mechanism for tumor immune evasion. These findings underscore the potential of targeting STING signaling to restore MHC class I expression and improve the efficacy of TIL-based and checkpoint blockade immunotherapies in melanoma, offering a promising strategy to enhance antitumor immunity [ ]. In many melanoma cells, the genes encoding STING and cGAS are frequently epigenetically silenced through promoter hypermethylation, which reduces their expression and impairs STING signaling. This silencing hinders the immune system’s ability to recognize and attack tumor cells. Treatment with the DNA methylation inhibitor 5-aza-2’-deoxycytidine (5AZADC) reactivated STING signaling in melanoma cell lines by demethylating the promoters of the STING and cGAS genes, thereby restoring their expression. This reactivation led to the production of type I IFNs and other immune-stimulatory molecules. The reconstitution of STING signaling significantly improved the antigenicity of melanoma cells by upregulating MHC class I molecules on their surface. This upregulation enhanced the ability of cytotoxic T cells to recognize and kill the melanoma cells. The study found that melanoma cells treated with 5AZADC showed increased susceptibility to lysis by TILs, demonstrating that epigenetic reprogramming can be a potent strategy to enhance the efficacy of T cell-based immunotherapies in melanoma [ ]. In melanoma, cGAS expression has also been observed to impact tumor immune infiltration and patient outcomes. Specifically, elevated cGAS expression in melanoma cells has been linked to improved progression-free survival (PFS) in patients treated with targeted therapies like BRAF inhibitors. This positive correlation suggests that cGAS may enhance the antitumor immune response by promoting CD8 + T cell infiltration into the tumor microenvironment (TME), potentially increasing treatment effectiveness. This chapter reviews the molecular interactions of the cGAS-STING pathway in melanoma (Fig.  ).
 

## Upstream triggering of cGAS-STING in melanoma

## Androgen receptor (AR) signaling
The persistent activation of the androgen receptor (AR) pathway markedly enhances cell division and tumor development, with elevated AR activity facilitating melanoma progression by averting cellular aging and preserving genomic integrity in melanoma cells. On the other hand, the inhibition or genetic disruption of AR via pharmaceuticals diminishes melanoma cell proliferation, triggers senescence, and causes DNA damage and cytoplasmic leakage, thereby activating the STING-dependent inflammatory pathway. This inhibition results in decreased tumor growth and a heightened presence of immune cells, such as macrophages and cytotoxic T cells, within TME. Consequently, AR signaling plays a crucial role in melanoma tumorigenesis, and targeting AR represents a promising therapeutic approach for melanoma treatment, regardless of the patient’s sex [ ].

## Transglutaminase type 2
In melanoma cells, transglutaminase type 2 (TG2) is crucial in controlling IRF3 activity and influencing the cGAS/STING pathway, thus affecting type I IFN production. When treated with the chemotherapeutic agent doxorubicin, which induces DNA damage, TG2 moves from the cytosol to the nucleus of apoptotic melanoma cells. In the nucleus, TG2 facilitates the formation of covalent cross-linked IRF3 dimers. This dimerization is significant because it inhibits IRF3 from effectively promoting IFN I gene transcription. Typically, IRF3, activated via the cGAS/STING pathway, dimerizes and moves to the nucleus to enhance IFN I production, essential for initiating immune responses against tumor cells. TG2 knockdown or inhibition, using methods such as RNA interference or inhibitors like Z-DON, disrupts this dimerization, increasing IFN I mRNA levels. Thus, TG2 acts as a negative regulator of the IRF3/IFN I pathway and the cGAS/STING axis in melanoma cells, aiding immune evasion and contributing to tumor survival and progression by modulating the immune system’s capacity to recognize and attack cancer cells [ ].

## Activin-A
Activin-A, a member of the TGF-β family, significantly promotes melanoma progression through its reliance on the STING pathway. Overexpressed in various cancers and linked to poor prognosis, Activin-A secreted by melanoma cells hampers CD8 + T cell-mediated anti-tumor immunity by fostering an immunosuppressive TME. This environment is marked by an increase in monocytes/macrophages and a decrease in neutrophils and pericytes. While Activin-A stimulates proinflammatory interferon signaling across various cell types within the TME, it does not improve T cell recruitment or activation, thereby enabling immune evasion. The tumor-promoting effects of Activin-A in melanoma are heavily dependent on the STING pathway within melanoma cells. Reducing STING expression diminishes the tumor growth advantage provided by Activin-A, indicating that STING activation helps create an inflammatory yet immunosuppressive environment that supports tumor progression. These insights suggest that targeting the STING pathway could counteract the tumor-promoting effects of Activin-A and enhance the effectiveness of melanoma immunotherapies [ ].

## Ubiquitination

## USP18
USP18, ubiquitin-specific peptidase 18, is an enzyme that removes ubiquitin from target proteins, thereby regulating their stability and function. In melanoma, USP18 stabilizes the cGAS protein through deubiquitination, preventing its degradation and thus regulating cGAS expression. In vemurafenib-resistant BRAF V600E mutant melanoma cells, USP18 expression is significantly elevated, leading to higher cGAS protein levels. This stabilization of cGAS enhances autophagy, a process contributing to drug resistance. USP18 removes ubiquitin chains from cGAS, ensuring its persistence within the cell and inducing protective autophagy. This autophagy helps melanoma cells survive and proliferate despite vemurafenib treatment. Consequently, USP18-mediated stabilization of cGAS promotes resistance to vemurafenib, reducing the drug’s effectiveness. This mechanism underscores the critical role of the USP18/cGAS axis in drug resistance in BRAF V600E mutant melanoma, suggesting that targeting USP18 or the autophagy pathway could potentially overcome resistance and improve therapeutic outcomes [ ].

## TRIM10
TRIM10, a ubiquitin E3 ligase, plays a critical role in promoting STING aggregation and activation within the Golgi apparatus in melanoma. Upon dsDNA stimulation, TRIM10 mediates K27 and K29-linked polyubiquitination of STING, enhancing its function. In TRIM10-deficient mice, melanoma growth was accelerated, accompanied by reduced infiltration of CD8 + T cells, highlighting the importance of TRIM10 in the anti-tumor immune response [ ].

## OTUD5
OTUD5, also known as ovarian tumor domain-containing deubiquitinase 5, is an enzyme that removes ubiquitin molecules from proteins. It has a crucial function in enhancing the body’s natural antiviral and anticancer immune responses by interacting with and stabilizing the STING protein. OTUD5 has a specialized role of cutting K48-linked polyubiquitin chains off STING. This action stops STING from being broken down by the proteasome, which in turn increases the stability and effectiveness of STING. The stability of this process results in a continuous activation of type I IFNs signaling and the synthesis of proinflammatory cytokines, which play a crucial role in promoting efficient antiviral and anticancer immune responses. The experimental data obtained from knockout mouse models clearly indicate that the lack of OTUD5 leads to heightened vulnerability to viral infections and accelerated melanoma progression, highlighting the crucial function of OTUD5 in STING-mediated immunity [ ].

## MB21D2
MB21D2, belonging to the cGAS/DncV-like nucleotidyltransferase (CD-NTase) family, plays a vital role in boosting cGAS-mediated antiviral and antitumor immune responses. It engages with the N-terminal of cGAS, facilitating its transformation into a liquid state and enhancing its ability to bind to DNA. These processes are crucial for the production of cGAMP. This connection greatly enhances the synthesis of cGAMP, which then triggers the activation of the STING pathway, resulting in the phosphorylation of TBK1 and IRF3 and the consequent generation of type I IFNs. THP-1 cells and macrophages from mice lacking the MB21D2 gene exhibit a significant decrease in the generation of IFN-β when stimulated with DNA. Mice lacking the MB21D2 gene exhibit increased vulnerability to DNA virus infections and faster melanoma progression. MB21D2 boosts the signaling pathway of IFN-β via mechanistically boosting the phosphorylation of STING, TBK1, and IRF3, and allowing the movement of IRF3 into the nucleus. MB21D2-deficient animals display increased death rates and higher viral loads in vivo during HSV-1 infection. In addition, MB21D2 enhances the presence and activation of CD8 + T cells within tumors, hence promoting antitumor immunity. Tumors in mice lacking MB21D2 exhibit accelerated growth and a decreased presence of infiltrating CD8 + T cells, resulting in lower production of IFN-γ, granzyme B, and perforin. In addition, the development of DCs and their capacity to display antigens are hindered when MB21D2 is not present. In summary, MB21D2 plays a crucial role in the efficient activation of cGAS, thereby improving both the body’s ability to fight against viruses and its ability to combat tumors, especially in cases of melanoma [ ].

## AKT1
AKT1, a constituent of the AKT kinase family, has a crucial function in controlling several cellular activities, such as proliferation, survival, and metabolism. In the context of cancer, AKT1 frequently exhibits hyperactivity and plays a role in the advancement of tumors. This study provides evidence that AKT1 plays a crucial role in the full senescence response triggered by the CDK4/6 inhibitor Palbociclib in human melanoma cells driven by the BRAF-V600E mutation. AKT1 plays a vital role in activating the NF-κB pathway, which is essential for the production of senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) components. Moreover, AKT1 has a substantial influence on the cGAS-STING pathway, which is a crucial element of the cellular immunological response to cytosolic DNA. AKT1 deletion cells displayed diminished amounts of cGAS protein and poor functionality of the cGAS-STING pathway, as indicated by reduced phosphorylation of IRF3 and decreased generation of type I IFNs (IFNα and IFNβ) following dsDNA transfection. These findings indicate that AKT1 promotes the production and activity of cGAS, which in turn enhances a strong immunological response mediated by STING. Nevertheless, whereas AKT1 plays a part in the cGAS-STING pathway, its main impact on Palbociclib-induced senescence seems to stem from its control over NF-κB signaling, rather than directly altering cGAS-STING activity [ ]. The investigation of STING treatment and its augmentation through AKT inhibition has been attempted. The administration of the STING agonist cGAMP directly into the tumor causes a quick and programmed cell death of the endothelial cells within the tumor. This effect is mostly observed in tumor models that have been artificially implanted, and it reveals that TNFα plays a crucial role in mediating this process. Nevertheless, spontaneous tumors do not exhibit this apoptotic response because they are resistant to it. The resistance can be overcome by effectively inducing EC apoptosis and increasing the anti-tumor immune response by the combination of cGAMP and an AKT 1/2 inhibitor (AKTi). This combined treatment demonstrates substantial inhibition of tumor growth in both spontaneous breast cancer and melanoma models. The results indicate that inhibiting AKT improves the activation of the STING pathway, resulting in better treatment outcomes for cancers that are resistant to STING agonist monotherapy. This technique has the potential to be a promising tool for treating primary human cancers that are resistant to single-agent STING treatment [ ].

## RPL15
RPL15, a ribosomal protein found in the 60 S subunit, has been discovered as a new target of topotecan (TPT), a substance that inhibits topoisomerase I. RPL15 plays a crucial role in regulating the immune response against tumors mediated by STING in melanoma. TPT binding to RPL15 hinders its interaction with RPL4, resulting in ribosomal stress through the inhibition of preribosomal subunit synthesis. The ribosomal stress induces the release of damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) from melanoma cells. The DAMPs stimulate the STING pathway, which is essential for launching immune responses against malignancies. Activation of STING by these DAMPs leads to an augmented synthesis of type I IFNs and other cytokines, which amplifies the recruitment and activation of cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs) while decreasing the number of immunosuppressive regulatory T cells (Tregs) in the TME. This alteration in the immunological environment enhances the susceptibility of melanoma tumors to anti–PD-1 therapy, hence increasing their receptiveness to immune checkpoint blockage. Hence, by specifically targeting RPL15, not only can we impair the functioning of ribosomes, but we can also enhance the immunogenicity of the tumor environment by activating STING. This presents a hopeful approach to enhance the effectiveness of cancer immunotherapy [ ].

## Downstream effectors

## Tumor endothelial cell autophagy
Autophagy in tumor endothelial cells (apop) plays a pivotal role in maintaining an immunosuppressive TME in melanoma by regulating processes that inhibit effective antitumor immune responses. This process involves the degradation of intracellular components, which sustains cellular homeostasis and suppresses inflammatory responses. Specifically, autophagy in TECs has been shown to inhibit the activation of key immune pathways, including the NF-κB and STING signaling pathways, both of which are crucial for initiating immune responses. When autophagy is active, TECs exhibit reduced expression of adhesion molecules like VCAM1 and ICAM1, as well as decreased secretion of immune-attracting chemokines, thereby limiting the recruitment and infiltration of cytotoxic CD8 + T cells into the tumor. Furthermore, autophagy contributes to abnormal tumor vasculature, resulting in poor blood flow and physical barriers that hinder T-cell access to tumor cells. Consequently, autophagy in TECs fosters a TME that promotes tumor growth and facilitates immune evasion, particularly in the context of resistance to immune checkpoint blockade therapies. By promoting an immune-excluded phenotype, autophagy in TECs diminishes the body’s antitumor immune surveillance, supporting melanoma progression and immune escape [ ]. Intratumoral injection of cGAMP significantly enhances STING pathway activation in tumor endothelial cells, leading to improved antitumor immunity. Endothelial cells in the TME are the primary producers of type I IFNs, especially IFN-β, following STING activation, rather than dendritic cells. This highlights the critical role of tumor blood vessels in initiating CD8 T-cell-mediated immune responses. The antitumor effects of cGAMP are further amplified when combined with PD1 and CTLA4 checkpoint inhibitors, suggesting a promising therapeutic strategy for metastatic melanoma and potentially other cancers [ ].
While TECs are a critical component of this immune suppression, emerging evidence also points to a potential role of autophagy in melanoma cells themselves in modulating the cGAS/STING pathway. A recent study suggests that autophagy in BRAF V600E mutant melanoma cells may contribute to therapeutic resistance by stabilizing cGAS protein through USP18-mediated deubiquitination. This stabilization of cGAS promotes protective autophagy, which enhances resistance to the BRAF inhibitor vemurafenib in these melanoma cells. In this context, autophagy acts as a survival mechanism, enabling melanoma cells to withstand therapeutic pressure by modulating the cGAS/STING axis [ ].
However, it is important to note that while these findings are promising, the evidence linking autophagy and the STING pathway in melanoma cells remains limited. Most of the research has focused on specific melanoma models, such as BRAF V600E mutant cells, and the broader implications of autophagy’s regulation of the STING pathway in other melanoma subtypes or in immune responses outside the context of drug resistance are not yet fully understood. Further studies are needed to explore the interplay between autophagy and the STING pathway in melanoma cells and to determine how these mechanisms may influence both tumor progression and responses to various therapeutic strategies.

## TREX1
TREX1, also known as three prime repair exonuclease 1, is an essential DNA repair enzyme responsible for maintaining genome stability by degrading cytosolic DNA, thus preventing aberrant immune activation. In melanoma, TREX1 is often suppressed, which can lead to unregulated cell growth and tumor progression. When TREX1 is upregulated in melanoma cells, it enhances the breakdown of cytosolic DNA, thereby contributing to the activation of the cGAS-STING pathway. Although TREX1’s primary function is to degrade cytosolic DNA, this degradation can paradoxically result in the activation of immune responses. Specifically, TREX1 overexpression can generate DNA fragments or influence immune signaling, which subsequently triggers the cGAS pathway. Activation of this pathway leads to the phosphorylation of TBK1 and IRF3, promoting the production of type I interferons (IFNs) and other immune signals that initiate apoptosis and suppress melanoma cell proliferation. Furthermore, studies show that chemotherapy agents such as carboplatin can induce TREX1 expression, leading to enhanced activation of the cGAS-STING pathway. This immune activation improves the body’s anti-tumor response, leading to reduced tumor size and improved therapeutic outcomes in melanoma patients [ ].

## NF2
NF2, also known as Neurofibromin 2, encodes merlin, a tumor suppressor protein that regulates cell proliferation and communication. Mutations in NF2 lead to the production of defective merlin, which disrupts its regulatory functions. In melanoma, mutant NF2 induces phase separation, creating distinct cellular compartments that trap and sequester the cGAS-STING machinery. This sequestration prevents the cGAS-STING pathway from initiating its typical immunological response, including the production of type I IFNs. As a result, immune detection and elimination of melanoma cells are impaired, allowing tumor growth to proceed unchecked. This phenomenon highlights the role of mutant NF2-induced phase separation in suppressing antitumor immunity, offering insights into potential therapeutic strategies to enhance immune responses in melanoma treatment [ ].

## AIM2
Absent in melanoma 2 (AIM2) is a protein that binds to dsDNA in the cytoplasm. It creates a complex called an inflammasome, which activates caspase-1. This activation leads to the release of inflammatory cytokines IL-1β and IL-18 and the initiation of pyroptosis. In the field of tumor immunology, specifically in melanoma, AIM2 has a suppressive impact on the immune system. Studies have demonstrated that it exerts a negative regulatory effect on the STING pathway, which plays a vital role in the immune response to tumors. The activation of the STING pathway occurs when cytosolic DNA is present, resulting in the generation of type I IFNs and other chemicals that stimulate the immune system. This stimulation facilitates the infiltration of CD8 + T cells into tumors, thereby boosting the body’s ability to fight against tumor cells. AIM2 suppresses this pathway by diminishing the activation of crucial signaling molecules like TBK1 and IRF3, thus limiting the synthesis of type I IFNs and their subsequent impacts. Therefore, by specifically focusing on AIM2, either by genetically eliminating it or reducing its expression via siRNA, the activity of the STING pathway is increased. This results in enhanced immune responses against tumors and improved effectiveness of immunotherapies such as adoptive T cell treatment and anti-PD-1 therapy [ ].

## HMGB1
HMGB1 is a strongly preserved protein that binds to DNA and is not a histone. It has important functions in maintaining the balance of the nucleus and triggering inflammatory reactions. HMGB1 in melanoma inhibits TRIM30α, an E3 ligase that downregulates STING, hence influencing STING-mediated senescence. The induction of senescence in melanoma cells by doxorubicin is dependent on HMGB1, which increases the expression of STING by binding to the TRIM30α promoter and suppressing its transcription. By inhibiting TRIM30α, the breakdown of STING is halted, resulting in the promotion of senescence through the STING-STAT6-p21 pathway. The findings demonstrated that suppressing HMGB1 resulted in a decrease in STING and p21 levels. Conversely, inhibiting TRIM30α led to an increase in STING expression and indicators of senescence, thereby proving the crucial role of HMGB1 in regulating TRIM30α and influencing STING-mediated senescence [ ].

## IL-15
The activation of the STING pathway in melanoma leads to a substantial increase in the synthesis of soluble IL-15/IL-15Rα complexes inside the TME. This procedure entails the intratumoral administration of STING agonists, such as c-di-GMP or 2′3′-cGAMP, which stimulates an inflammatory reaction leading to an upregulation of IL-15 expression by myeloid cells and, in certain instances, by tumor cells themselves. Elevated IL-15 levels stimulate the growth and stimulation of CD8 T cells and natural killer (NK) cells, resulting in strong immune response against tumors. Consequently, the activation of the STING pathway can cause an increase in IL-15, which leads to the regression of tumors and the stimulation of antitumor responses throughout the body. This can also result in the regression of secondary cancers located far from the primary tumor, thereby enhancing the overall efficacy of cancer immunotherapy [ ].

## DNA regulatory mechanism

## EZH2
EZH2, also known as enhancer of zeste homolog 2, is an enzyme that performs the function of histone methyltransferase. It specifically catalyzes the process of trimethylation of histone H3 on lysine 27 (H3K27me3). This enzymatic activity results in the inhibition of transcription of target genes that are implicated in tumor suppression and immunological responses. Melanoma is characterized by elevated levels of H3K27me3, which is linked to the inhibition of the STING pathway. The STING pathway plays a crucial role in the innate immune response to combat malignancies. EZH2 inhibitors, like GSK126, decrease the levels of H3K27me3 and enhance the acetylation of H3K27ac at the STING promoter, resulting in the upregulation of STING expression. When diABZI, a STING agonist that activates the STING pathway, is used in combination with EZH2 inhibitors, it leads to an increased activation of the downstream TBK1-IRF3 signaling cascade. This synergistic impact results in the enhanced production of IFN-β, the upregulation of MHC I molecules, and the secretion of chemokines that recruit CD8 + T lymphocytes to the tumor site. As a result, this combined treatment transforms cancers that have little immune cell presence (known as ‘cold’ tumors) into tumors that have strong immune responses against the tumor (known as ‘hot’ tumors). This leads to a considerable reduction in tumor development and improved survival rates in models of melanoma [ ].

## DNA methylation
STING signaling plays a pivotal role in facilitating antitumor immune responses in melanoma, but its functionality is frequently compromised in these tumors. This dysfunction is mainly attributed to epigenetic changes, specifically promoter hypermethylation, which results in the transcriptional silencing of the STING gene. Research has demonstrated that this silencing can be reversed with DNA methylation inhibitors such as 5-aza-2’-deoxycytidine (5AZADC), which restore both STING expression and activity. Upon reactivation of STING signaling in melanoma cells, there is an elevation in type-I IFNs production and an increase in MHC class I molecules, thereby enhancing tumor cell antigenicity and improving T cell recognition and recruitment. This reactivation also amplifies the effectiveness of STING agonist therapies, promoting tumor regression through a mechanism dependent on CD8 + T cells. Additionally, the depletion of DNA (cytosine-5)-methyltransferase 3 A (DNMT3A) and DNMT3B, but not DNMT1, has been shown to restore STING expression, underscoring the specific role of these DNA methyltransferases in regulating STING in melanoma. Therefore, the regulation of STING in melanoma is closely linked to its epigenetic status, which can be targeted therapeutically to boost antitumor immunity [ ].

## PRMT5
PRMT5, also known as protein arginine methyltransferase 5, is an epigenetic enzyme responsible for adding methyl groups to arginine residues on both histones and non-histone proteins. This enzymatic activity has a significant impact on a range of cellular processes, including the development of cancer. PRMT5 inhibits the immune response against melanoma by altering important elements of the cGAS/STING pathway. PRMT5 specifically adds methyl groups to IFI16 (or its murine equivalent IFI204), a DNA sensor in the cGAS/STING pathway. This process reduces the activation of TBK1 and IRF3, which are essential for the generation of IFN and chemokines, in response to cytosolic DNA. This alteration leads to decreased production of IFNB1, CCL5, and CXCL10, impeding the immune response against melanoma cells. The experimental findings showed that reducing the activity of PRMT5 in B16 melanoma cells resulted in an increase in the production of IFNs and chemokines. This also led to an enhancement in the dimerization and polymerization of STING, which in turn strengthened the immune response against tumors. The findings indicate that PRMT5 functions as a suppressor of the cGAS/STING system, hence aiding immune evasion in melanoma [ ].

## HASPIN
HASPIN, also known as Histone H3-associated protein kinase, is a serine/threonine kinase that has a vital function in the process of mitosis. More precisely, HASPIN adds a phosphate group to histone H3 at threonine 3 (H3pT3), which is a necessary alteration for the accurate arrangement and separation of chromosomes during cell division. The small chemical CX-6258 has been demonstrated to effectively inhibit HASPIN, resulting in a major influence on the cGAS-STING pathway in melanoma cells and leading to noteworthy anticancer effects. CX-6258 specifically inhibits HASPIN kinase, a crucial controller of cell division, leading to abnormalities in the process of mitosis and the development of micronuclei in melanoma cells. The micronuclei have a tendency to break, causing the DNA to be released into the cytosol. This DNA is then detected by the cytosolic DNA sensor known as cGAS. The activation of cGAS results in the synthesis of cGAMP, which then triggers the activation of the STING pathway. STING activation triggers a IFN-1 response, which is characterized by the synthesis of IFNα and IFNβ. This reaction not only suppresses the growth of tumor cells but also influences the immunological microenvironment. More precisely, it enhances the abundance of CD8 + T cells that produce IFNγ and reduces the occurrence of Tregs, which usually have an immunosuppressive effect. These modifications establish a more advantageous immunological setting for the activity against tumors and amplify the efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors such as anti-PD-1 treatment. In summary, the inhibition of HASPIN with CX-6258 offers a hopeful approach to address medication resistance in melanoma and potentially other types of cancer. This is achieved by utilizing the cGAS-STING pathway to enhance the body’s immune response against tumors [ ].

## PD-L1
The cGAS-STING pathway is integral to the immune response against cancer by detecting cytoplasmic double-stranded DNA, typically from mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) leakage. This detection initiates the production of type I IFNs and other inflammatory cytokines, which can lead to increased immune cell infiltration and enhanced anti-tumor immunity. However, this pathway also interacts complexly with programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1), a protein that assists tumors in evading immune detection [ ]. STING activation has been shown to upregulate PD-L1 expression on tumor cells, contributing to an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME) that impairs T cell function and promotes tumor survival and progression. This dual role of STING—boosting immune responses while also enhancing PD-L1 expression—creates a paradox where the pathway’s activation can both promote anti-tumor immunity and enable tumor evasion. Recent research highlights that STING plays a critical role in modulating PD-L1 expression through its impact on cellular senescence. Specifically, STING can facilitate nuclear localization of PD-L1, thereby influencing its transcriptional regulation. In melanoma cells, suppression of PD-L1 results in increased STING expression at the gene transcription level, enhancing its own promoter activity. This upregulation of STING is associated with increased senescence markers such as p16, p21, and H3K9 trimethylation, which contribute to the senescent phenotype of melanoma cells, including reduced proliferation and positive staining for senescence-associated β-galactosidase (SA-β-Gal). Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) and DNA pull-down assays have demonstrated a direct interaction between nuclear PD-L1 and the STING promoter, revealing that PD-L1 inhibits STING transcription by binding to its promoter region [ ]. Furthermore, the effects of STING on PD-L1 expression are amplified when PD-L1 suppression is combined with the chemotherapeutic agent doxorubicin (Dox), leading to enhanced tumor growth inhibition. This suggests that targeting the nuclear PD-L1-STING interaction could be a strategic approach to disrupt the immunosuppressive effects of PD-L1 and strengthen anti-tumor responses. In addition to these findings, research in mouse models has shown that cGAS plays a pivotal role in the efficacy of PD-L1 antibody treatments. Mice deficient in cGAS (cGas−/−) exhibit impaired tumor regression and survival benefits from PD-L1 antibody treatment compared to mice with functional cGAS. This underscores the importance of cGAS-STING pathway activation in enhancing PD-L1 antibody efficacy and suggests that combining cGAS-STING pathway activation with PD-L1 checkpoint inhibitors could improve cancer immunotherapy outcomes [ ].

## Lon/STING/PD-L1 axis
Mitochondrial Lon functions as a chaperone and DNA-binding protein that has a role in maintaining protein quality, responding to stress, and regulating mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) metabolism. Lon overexpression in melanoma cells results in elevated generation of reactive oxygen species (ROS), which causes damage to mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) and subsequent release of mtDNA into the cytoplasm. The mtDNA present in the cytosol initiates the cGAS-STING pathway, leading to the phosphorylation of TBK1 and IRF3. This, in turn, causes the production of IFNs and the increase in levels of immunosuppressive proteins such PD-L1 and IDO-1. These proteins hinder the activation of T-cells, which helps the immune system avoid detection and supports the advancement of tumors. In addition, extracellular vesicles (EVs) generated by Lon, which include mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) and PD-L1, contribute to an increased level of immunosuppression within the TME [ ].

## SMARCAL1
SMARCAL1 governs the activity of PD-L1 and the cGAS-STING pathway in melanoma by upholding the integrity of the genome and managing the accessibility of chromatin. It inhibits the signaling of the body’s natural immune response by reducing the amount of DNA damage. This, in turn, stops the activation of the cGAS-STING pathway, which is an important factor in the body’s immune response to DNA in the cytoplasm. Simultaneously, SMARCAL1, in partnership with the AP-1 family member JUN, maintains the accessibility of chromatin at a specific regulatory region of the PD-L1 gene, thus enhancing the expression of PD-L1. SMARCAL1-deficient cells exhibit elevated levels of IRF3 phosphorylation on serine 386, indicating increased IRF3 activation. This suggests that IRF3 directly influences the expression and function of SMARCAL1. The loss of SMARCAL1 leads to decreased PD-L1 levels and heightened IRF3 activation, which underscores the interplay between these two factors in modulating immune responses and PD-L1 expression in melanoma.These combined effects enhance the body’s immune response to tumors and render melanoma tumors more susceptible to immune checkpoint blockade therapy [ ].

## BANF1
BANF1, a protein that binds to DNA, has a deleterious effect on the cGAS-STING pathway. This pathway is crucial for the body’s natural immunological responses and the infiltration of CD8 + T cells in the TME. Upregulation of BANF1 in melanoma is associated with a negative prognosis and decreased infiltration of immune cells. The knockout of BANF1 (BANF1 KO) triggers the activation of the cGAS-STING pathway, which in turn boosts the expression of interferon-stimulated genes and inflammatory chemokines. This ultimately results in an increase in the infiltration of CD8 + T cells and the enhancement of antitumor immunity. In studies using mouse models of melanoma, the knockout (KO) of BANF1 gene has been found to greatly inhibit the growth of tumors. This impact is reliant on the presence of a functioning immune system. The combination of BANF1 knockout (KO) with PD-1 blocking therapy enhances therapeutic results, indicating that targeting BANF1 can enhance the effectiveness of PD-1 immunotherapy in melanoma [ ].

## MTH1
MTH1, also known as MutT Homolog 1, is a protein that plays a crucial role in preserving the accuracy of genetic material by breaking down oxidized nucleotides. This process prevents these damaged molecules from being included in DNA and RNA. This activity provides cellular protection against oxidative damage and mutations. By using substances such as TH1579 to block MTH1, it is possible to cause oxidative DNA damage in cancer cells exclusively. This damage ultimately results in the death of the cancer cells through a process known as mitotic catastrophe and apoptosis. The specific targeting of MTH1 makes it an attractive candidate for cancer therapy. TH1579 is a compound that inhibits a process called mitosis, which is involved in cell division. It specifically targets cancer cells and causes their death by interfering with mitosis and introducing oxidative DNA damage. Research has shown that it greatly improves the effectiveness of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) by increasing the expression of PD-L1 and activating an inflammatory response via the cGAS-STING pathway. TH1579 therapy in melanoma leads to an elevation in PD-L1 levels at both the protein and mRNA levels. This rise enhances the sensitivity of tumor cells to anti-PD-L1 therapies such as atezolizumab. The chemical triggers the activation of the cGAS-STING pathway, resulting in the phosphorylation of TBK1 and the subsequent synthesis of type I IFNs (such as IFN-β) and chemokines (such as CCL5 and CXCL10). These chemicals have essential functions in attracting and stimulating T-cells in the TME, hence enhancing an immune response against the tumor. TH1579, when combined with atezolizumab, showed significant efficacy in reducing tumor growth in syngeneic mouse models of melanoma. This suggests that TH1579 has the ability to transform “cold” tumors into “hot” tumors, thereby enhancing the overall effectiveness of ICI therapy [ ].

## Immune cells related functions of cGAS-STING in melanoma

## T helpers
STING activation in CD4 T cells has a critical role in promoting anti-tumor immunity, specifically by driving the differentiation of CD4 T cells into TH9 cells. This process is characterized by increased IL-9 production, which enhances the anticancer activity of TH9 cells in melanoma. Interestingly, this differentiation occurs independently of the traditional type I IFN signaling (via IFNAR) and the IRF3 pathway, relying instead on the mTOR pathway. The importance of this mechanism was demonstrated in experimental models, where the transfer of STING-activated TH9 cells into melanoma-bearing mice resulted in reduced tumor growth and improved survival. These findings suggest that targeting STING in CD4 T cells could enhance the efficacy of TH9 cell-mediated immunotherapy for melanoma (Fig.  ) [ ].
 

## Regulatory T cells (Tregs)
While direct evidence for STING’s effects on Tregs in melanoma is limited, it is known that cGAS-STING activation in the TME modulates immune responses by enhancing CTL activity and reducing Treg-mediated suppression. This indirect impact on Tregs occurs primarily through type I IFN production, which is stimulated by STING activation in dendritic cells (DCs) and macrophages. The overall effect is a reduction in Treg numbers and suppressive functions, thereby facilitating a more robust anti-tumor immune response [ ].

## Natural killers (NK)
STING activation plays an essential role in the recruitment and activation of NK cells in melanoma. Upon detecting cytosolic DNA from tumor cells, the cGAS-STING pathway triggers the release of type I IFNs and other pro-inflammatory cytokines, creating an environment conducive to NK cell activation [ ]. This process is crucial for efficient NK cell-mediated cytotoxicity against melanoma cells. Both host immune cells and tumor cells contribute to NK cell recruitment by producing chemokines such as CCL5 and IP-10, which promote NK infiltration into the TME [ ].

## CD8 + T cells
STING activation also enhances the anti-tumor activity of CD8 + T cells. By promoting the production of type I IFNs and pro-inflammatory cytokines, the cGAS-STING pathway improves CD8 + T cell recruitment and activation within the TME. Additionally, STING activation enhances the presentation of tumor antigens, increasing the cytotoxic potential of CD8 + T cells against melanoma cells. This mechanism provides a promising avenue for augmenting the efficacy of existing immunotherapies [ , ].

## Macrophages
The cGAS-STING pathway plays a critical role in the immune response to melanoma tumors by activating macrophages within the TME. When cytosolic DNA is detected, cGAS activates, producing cGAMP, which binds to and activates STING. This activation leads to phosphorylation of TBK1 and the production of type I interferons, especially IFN-β. In melanoma, suppression of the lysosomal protein PPT1 activates this pathway, triggering IFN-β secretion from macrophages. This enhances CD8 + T cell activity, shifts macrophage polarization from pro-tumor (M2) to anti-tumor (M1), and reduces myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs), improving the efficacy of treatments like anti-PD-1 antibodies [ ]​. Additionally, intratumoral delivery of cGAMP activates the STING pathway, resulting in the recruitment of a specific macrophage population and increased production of TNFα and chemokines like Cxcl10 and Cxcl11, which attract T cells. These macrophages also express genes associated with anti-tumor responses, strengthening both innate and adaptive immunity [ ].

## DCs
The cGAS-STING pathway also significantly influences the function of DCs, which are key players in initiating adaptive immune responses. Upon cGAMP production, DCs exhibit enhanced expression of IFN-β and the co-stimulatory molecule CD86, both critical for T cell activation. Additionally, cGAMP-activated DCs improve the presentation of tumor antigens to CD8 + T cells, reinforcing the cytotoxic immune response against melanoma [ ].

## Gut microbiome effect
The gut microbiota impacts melanoma by activating the immune system through the cGAS-STING pathway. Microbiota-derived c-di-AMP stimulates this pathway in tumor-infiltrating monocytes, leading to the production of type I interferons (IFN-I), which enhances immune responses and boosts the effectiveness of immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) therapies. Additionally, fiber-rich diets promote beneficial bacteria like Akkermansia muciniphila, which also activates the cGAS-STING pathway, further supporting the body’s immune response against melanoma [ ].

## cGAS-STING in treatment of melanomas
The cGAS/STING pathway is an attractive therapeutic target in malignancies because of its crucial involvement in the immune response to cytoplasmic DNA. This pathway has the ability to identify aberrant DNA, which triggers the creation of type I IFNs and other immune mediators that strengthen the body’s ability to fight against tumors. The cGAS/STING pathway can impede tumor growth by stimulating immune cells, facilitating apoptosis, and triggering cell senescence. Nevertheless, the continuous stimulation of this process might potentially play a role in the development of tumors by promoting an inflammatory and immunosuppressive milieu within the tumor. From a therapeutic perspective, the modulation of this pathway using STING agonists can enhance the effectiveness of current treatments such as radiotherapy, chemotherapy, immune checkpoint inhibitors, tumor vaccines, CAR T cell therapy, and oncolytic viruses. This enhancement can lead to improved antitumor efficacy and potentially minimize adverse effects. On the other hand, STING inhibitors may be advantageous in cancers where the overactive inflammation and immune suppression caused by this system contribute to the advancement of tumors. Hence, directing efforts towards the cGAS/STING pathway provides a two-fold strategy in cancer treatment: augmenting the body’s ability to fight against tumors and reducing the harmful inflammation that promotes tumor growt [ , ]. STING agonists have demonstrated substantial preclinical efficacy by augmenting these immune responses and inducing a “hot” TME, rendering tumors more vulnerable to immunological assaults. In addition, the combination of STING agonists with other immunotherapies, such as immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) and cancer vaccines, has shown synergistic effects. This provides a holistic strategy to overcome cancer resistance and enhance therapeutic outcomes. Although there are significant obstacles and additional investigation is required, the cGAS-STING pathway’s capacity to utilize and enhance the body’s innate immune responses renders it a very promising approach for cancer therapy [ ]. The impact of a low-dose STING agonist, ADU S-100, on the proliferation of melanoma and the TME. Injecting ADU S-100 directly into the tumor in mice with pre-existing B16.F10 melanomas resulted in a notable decrease in tumor growth rate and an extension of the animals’s lifespan. The observed therapeutic effect was linked to an improvement in the normalization of tumor blood vessels and the development of tertiary lymphoid structures within the TME. The main discoveries consisted of heightened synthesis of substances that inhibit blood vessel growth, decreased oxygen deficiency, and enhanced blood flow and structural integrity of blood vessels, as evidenced by improved blood vessel perfusion and higher coverage by pericytes. The treatment additionally promoted the maturation of DCs and increased the expression of proteins that induce the formation of tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS), leading to the creation of an inflammatory environment that supports the development of TLS. We observed an increased infiltration of CD8 + T cells and CD11c + DCs, as well as the establishment of a highly oligoclonal T cell repertoire that contained clonotypes peculiar to the TME. The therapeutic effects relied on the expression of host STING, highlighting the crucial significance of immune cell-mediated responses. The results indicate that therapies based on STING agonists have the ability to modify the TME in order to facilitate immune infiltration and the creation of tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS). This presents a potential approach for enhancing the effectiveness of immunotherapy in treating melanoma (Table  ) [ ].
 

## Dendritic cell-mediated cancer immunotherapy
Utilizing STING agonists in DC-mediated cancer immunotherapy shows promise as a method for treating melanomas by harnessing the body’s innate and adaptive immune responses. This approach utilizes a polymeric prodrug platform called polySTING to improve the delivery and effectiveness of STING agonists. PolySTING includes mannose targeting ligands that enable the specific absorption by CD206+/mannose receptor + APCs within the TME. After being absorbed into the body, the STING agonist is released into the cells through a breakable connection, which then selectively triggers the activation of the STING pathway. This stimulus triggers a strong immune response driven by type-I IFN, which enhances the development and ability of CD8 + DCs to transmit antigens to other immune cells. The outcome is a heightened infiltration of cytotoxic CD8 + T cells into the TME, hence augmenting the immune response against the tumor. Systemic delivery of polySTING in melanoma models effectively suppresses tumor development, extends longevity, and triggers a robust immune response while causing minimal damage. This highlights the potential of polySTING as a promising therapy approach for melanomas [ ].

## CAR-T cells
Chimeric antigen receptor T (CAR-T) cells are a type of immunotherapy in which T cells taken from the patient are genetically altered to produce receptors that precisely recognize tumor antigens. These modified receptors enable T cells to efficiently attach to and eliminate cancer cells. Nevertheless, CAR-T cell therapy encounters difficulties when dealing with solid tumors such as melanoma, mostly due to the presence of an immunosuppressive TME and tumor heterogeneity. These factors contribute to the emergence of immune escape variants that manage to evade identification by CAR-T cells. In order to surmount these obstacles, scientists have devised an innovative strategy that entails delivering CAR-T cells and STING agonists simultaneously through biopolymer implants. The biopolymers function as a delivery mechanism, precisely positioning the CAR-T cells at the tumor location and maintaining their effectiveness. STING agonists are chemicals that stimulate the innate immune system, leading to enhanced antitumor activity. This dual strategy not only enables the targeted and continuous assault on the tumor by CAR-T cells, but also amplifies the stimulation and development of DCs and other antigen-presenting cells in the TME. Consequently, this integrated approach triggers a potent and all-encompassing immune response that can eradicate both CAR-T cell-targeted melanoma cells and antigen-negative melanoma cells. Additionally, it also generates systemic immunity, which can prevent the spread of cancer and the reappearance of tumors [ ].

## Adoptive cell therapy (ACT)
Adoptive cell therapy (ACT) is an immunotherapy technique where immune cells are taken from a patient, altered or improved outside the body to increase their cancer-fighting capabilities, and then reintroduced into the patient. This strategy is designed to augment the body’s innate immune response in order to more efficiently target and eradicate cancer cells. To combat melanomas, a form of skin cancer, ACT can be employed by isolating particular immune cells such as T cells or macrophages. Subsequently, these cells are modified or stimulated to enhance their capacity to destroy tumors. For example, macrophages can be treated with certain agonists to simultaneously activate TLR and STING signaling pathways, so augmenting their inflammatory and anticancer characteristics. After being reintroduced into the patient’s body, these modified cells are able to retain their anticancer characteristics even in the presence of immunosuppressive conditions inside the TME, resulting in strong prevention of tumor development. This procedure enhances the abundance of TILs, namely CD8 + T cells, which play a vital role in targeting melanoma cells. ACT, or adoptive cell therapy, presents a promising therapeutic approach for the treatment of melanomas by addressing the limitations of conventional treatments and countering the tumor’s immune evasion mechanisms [ ].

## Synthetic drugs for inducing cGAS-STING in Melanoma

## Platinium based drugs
Carboplatin is a second-generation chemotherapeutic treatment that belongs to the platinum-based class of drugs. It is derived from cisplatin and is specifically developed to treat different types of malignancies, including melanoma. It operates by creating connections between DNA strands, both within and between them, which disturb the structure of DNA and stop the process of DNA synthesis, resulting in the death of cells. This work provides evidence that carboplatin efficiently inhibits the formation of melanoma by increasing the expression of TREX1 (three-prime repair exonuclease 1), a gene that is frequently downregulated in melanoma cells. The overexpression of TREX1 triggers apoptosis and inhibits cell proliferation by activating the cGAS-STING pathway, which is a vital mechanism involved in the immunological response against cytosolic DNA. Carboplatin was found to activate TREX1 in both laboratory and living organism models, which in turn stimulates the cGAS-STING pathway. This leads to an increase in programmed cell death and a decrease in the formation of tumors. The results indicate that carboplatin has the potential to effectively treat human melanoma by specifically targeting the TREX1/cGAS-STING pathway. This discovery presents a promising approach for tackling this aggressive form of cancer [ ]. MSA-2-Pt is a modified form of the non-nucleotide STING agonist MSA-2, where platinum is added to enhance its chemotherapeutic capabilities and activate the STING pathway for immunotherapy. MSA-2-Pt demonstrated a considerable ability to cause cell death in MC38 colon cancer cells in a dose-dependent manner when tested in a laboratory setting. This impact was observed at a concentration of 75 µM and above, but MSA-2 alone did not show any effectiveness at this dosage. In addition, MSA-2-Pt stimulated the STING pathway in RAW264.7 murine macrophage cells, resulting in an enhanced release of IFN-β and the phosphorylation of crucial proteins involved in the STING system. MSA-2-Pt exhibited improved anticancer effects in both MC38 colon carcinoma and B16F10 melanoma models in vivo. In the B16F10 melanoma model, which has low immunogenicity and high aggressiveness, the administration of MSA-2-Pt resulted in a decrease in tumor growth and an improvement in survival rates. Specifically, 2 out of 10 mice treated with MSA-2-Pt did not develop tumors, whereas treatment with MSA-2 alone or cisplatin did not yield the same results. In addition, the administration of MSA-2-Pt resulted in an augmented influx of CD4 + and CD8 + T cells into the TME, indicating a strong immunological response. The results suggest that MSA-2-Pt has great promise as a treatment for melanomas, as it can both directly kill cancer cells and stimulate the immune system to fight against tumors [ ].

## TTK inhibitors
OSU13 is a tiny chemical specifically developed to inhibit TTK, a kinase enzyme that plays a crucial role in ensuring accurate separation of chromosomes during cell division. OSU13 has significant anti-tumor efficacy in melanoma models by triggering G2 cell cycle arrest, senescence, and death in cancer cells. OSU13 treatment induces DNA damage and the generation of micronuclei, which trigger the activation of the cytosolic DNA-sensing cGAS-STING pathway. The activation is essential for the recruitment and functioning of T lymphocytes in the TME. OSU13-treated melanoma cells exhibit enhanced release of chemokines CCL5 and CXCL10, which stimulate the recruitment of immune cells towards the tumor. In addition, when administered to animal models, OSU13 therapy results in an increased ratio of T and NK cells that penetrate the tumors and triggers the activation of the PD-1/PD-L1 immune checkpoint. When used in conjunction with anti-PD1 therapy, OSU13 effectively suppresses tumor development and enhances survival, indicating its ability to augment the effectiveness of immunotherapies in malignancies with functional STING [ ].

## 5-Fluorouracil
5-Fluorouracil (5-FU) is a commonly employed chemotherapeutic medication that demonstrates efficacy against a range of cancer types, such as colorectal, pancreatic, gastric, biliary, head and neck, and breast cancers. Within the context of melanoma cells, the tumor burden is decreased by 5-FU through the activation of the cancer-cell-intrinsic STING pathway. This activation results in the synthesis of type I IFNs, which are essential for triggering an immune response against tumors. The study found that 5-FU does not cause resistance in melanoma cells without STING in a laboratory setting. However, its effectiveness in living organisms relies significantly on the existence of STING within the cancer cells themselves. The administration of 5-FU treatment leads to an increase in T cells within the tumor. Conversely, the removal of T cells greatly reduces the efficiency of 5-FU, emphasizing the crucial role of T cells in the anti-tumor action of 5-FU. Furthermore, without the presence of STING, significantly larger amounts of 5-FU are necessary to achieve comparable tumor shrinkage. This suggests that successful 5-FU treatment relies on the cancer cells’ inherent STING pathway to activate strong anti-tumor immune responses [ ].

## Zebularine
Zebularine is a compound that inhibits the activity of DNA methyltransferase, an enzyme involved in adding methyl groups to DNA. This inhibition has demonstrated potential in cancer treatment as it can influence the regulation of gene expression through epigenetic mechanisms. Zebularine has its influence on tumor cells in melanoma by demethylating the promoter region of the STING gene, resulting in enhanced production of STING. The overexpression of STING amplifies the activation of the cGAS-STING pathway, which is an essential element of the innate immune response to cytosolic DNA. Treatment with Zebularine induces the buildup of cytoplasmic DNA in cancer cells, without causing any DNA harm. This triggers the activation of cGAS, resulting in the synthesis of cGAMP. Generated cGAMP triggers the activation of STING, leading to the production of type I IFNs and interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs). The immunological responses attract CD8 + T cells and NK cells to the TME, thereby strengthening the body’s ability to fight against the tumor. Zebularine injection in mouse models of melanoma leads to a decrease in tumor size and an increase in survival time. These effects are reliant on the presence of functional cGAS and STING in both the tumor cells and the cells of the host. Zebularine’s therapeutic effectiveness is enhanced when used in conjunction with cGAMP and immune checkpoint inhibitors, underscoring its potential as a key element in combination cancer immunotherapy [ ].

## diABZI
DiABZI, also known as dimeric amidobenzimidazole, is a newly developed small chemical that functions as a STING agonist. Its purpose is to stimulate the innate immune response in cells. DiABZI has demonstrated promise in addressing resistance to BRAF inhibitors, a frequently employed treatment for melanoma, a highly aggressive form of skin cancer. Melanoma cells frequently acquire resistance to BRAF inhibitors by activating the MAPK pathway and undergoing metabolic changes mediated by the NRF2 pathway, which aids in cellular response to oxidative stress. diABZI activates the STING pathway, resulting in enhanced phosphorylation of STING, TBK1, and IRF3. These phosphorylation events are essential for triggering immunological responses. This activation leads to the decrease in NRF2 activity, hence diminishing its defensive antioxidative response. Therefore, when diABZI is combined with BRAF inhibitors such as Vemurafenib or Dabrafenib, it increases the susceptibility of melanoma cells to these treatments. This leads to a notable reduction in cell growth, movement, and the initiation of cell death. The use of a combination treatment approach, particularly when paired with Vemurafenib, offers a hopeful method for tackling medication resistance in the treatment of melanoma [ ].

## IACS-8803 and IACS-8779
IACS-8803 and IACS-8779 are highly effective cyclic dinucleotide (CDN) STING agonists specifically developed to boost the immune system’s ability to fight against malignancies. These molecules were engineered with precise alterations to the nucleobase and ribose components in order to enhance their effectiveness and durability. Experiments conducted in a laboratory setting using human THP-1 and mouse J774 cells showed that both IACS-8803 and IACS-8779 strongly stimulate the STING pathway. This stimulation leads to the activation of the IRF3 and NF-kB pathways, which in turn causes an increase in the production of interferon beta (IFN-β) and other cytokines that promote inflammation. Experiments conducted on live subjects using the B16 murine melanoma model shown that these STING agonists not only caused substantial reduction in tumors at the injection site, but also promoted overall anti-tumor responses throughout the body, resulting in the regression of untreated cancers located elsewhere. This indicates a strong and comprehensive activation of the immune system, resulting in greater percentages of full tumor regression as compared to the clinical standard ADU-S100. The remarkable anti-tumor effectiveness of IACS-8803 and IACS-8779 underscores their promise as strong therapeutic agents in the treatment of melanoma by harnessing the innate and adaptive responses of the immune system [ ].

## ZelNate
ZelNate is a therapeutic agent for bovine respiratory disease (BRD) that consists of plasmid DNA-based immunomodulator enclosed in cationic liposomes. It operates by triggering IRF pathway via STING protein and cGAS, bypassing the conventional TLR9 pathway. ZelNate triggers a robust activation of the IRF pathway in B16 murine melanoma cells, resulting in the synthesis of secreted embryonic alkaline phosphatase (SEAP). The reaction to ZelNate is contingent upon the presence of STING, as cells lacking this gene do not exhibit any activation. The outcome is a powerful type I IFNs reaction, which can aid in an immune response against tumors, potentially impeding the advancement of melanoma by augmenting the immune system’s ability to identify and eliminate cancer cells [ ].

## Zendusortide
Sudocetaxel Zendusortide (TH1902) is an innovative compound that combines a peptide and a medication. It specifically targets the sortilin receptor (SORT1) found in cancer cells, which improves its ability to enter the cells and enhances its effectiveness. TH1902 triggers the cGAS/STING pathway, a crucial component of the body’s natural defense system, resulting in the production of type I IFNs and pro-inflammatory cytokines including TNFα. These substances cause an increase in the expression of PD-L1 and MHC-I on cancer cells. This function enhances the detectability of tumors by the immune system, facilitating the entry of immune cells, such as cytotoxic T-cells, NK cells, and macrophages, into the TME. TH1902 shown more efficacy in suppressing tumor growth compared to docetaxel in a mouse model of B16-F10 melanoma. It also resulted in enhanced infiltration of white blood cells and higher levels of cytotoxic markers including perforin, granzyme B, and caspase-3. When used alongside anti-PD-L1 therapy, TH1902 not only increased tumor regression but also improved survival. This shows that TH1902 has the ability to boost the effectiveness of immune checkpoint inhibitors by transforming “cold” cancers into “hot” tumors, thus bypassing mechanisms of immune resistance [ ].

## Nanoparticle-based therapeutics targeting cGAS-STING in melanoma
Nanomedicine use nanoparticles’ unique characteristics to precisely administer medicinal chemicals, thereby improving efficacy and reducing adverse effects. Nanoparticles provide numerous advantages, including precise delivery to certain tissues or cells, regulated release of medications, enhanced solubility and stability of medicinal substances, and multifunctionality. Nanomedicines can greatly enhance the delivery and efficacy of treatments for a range of disorders, such as cancer, by exploiting their specific attributes. The cGAS-STING pathway plays a crucial role in the immune system’s reaction to cytosolic DNA, triggering the synthesis of type I IFNs and other inflammatory cytokines that assist in fighting against malignancies. Nanoparticles can be engineered to stimulate this system, augmenting the body’s immunological response against cancer cells. This technique can be classified as monotherapy and combination therapies, each with distinct mechanisms and advantages. Nanoparticles are synthesized for the purpose of delivering STING agonists exclusively to tumor cells, hence inducing activation of the cGAS-STING pathway. Liposomal nanoparticles containing cGAMP stimulate the polarization of M1-like macrophages and increase T-cell activity, hence augmenting the immune response against malignancies. In addition, nanoparticles have the ability to improve the transportation of traditional chemotherapy drugs and stimulate the cGAS-STING pathway. For instance, when PLGA nanoparticles are used to simultaneously deliver metabolic blockers and STING agonists, they stimulate TBK1 phosphorylation and STING dimerization, which ultimately boosts the body’s immune response against tumors. Combination therapy involve a wider variety of methods. Immunotherapy can be greatly enhanced by synergistically combining nanoparticles with immune checkpoint inhibitors or cancer vaccines. Nanovaccines that activate STING, such as those containing manganese and risedronate, efficiently suppress tumor growth by boosting both humoral and cellular immunity. Another method that can be used is chemo-dynamic treatment (CDT), in which nanoparticles produce ROS inside the TME. This causes the death of cells and boosts immune activation. Manganese nanoparticles exhibit high efficacy in producing ROS and triggering the cGAS-STING pathway, thereby eliciting a potent immune response against tumors. Furthermore, nanoparticles have the ability to enhance the effectiveness of radiotherapy (RT) by activating the cGAS-STING pathway and augmenting immune responses. Biomineralized manganese oxide nanoparticles enhance the DNA damage caused by radiation and stimulate the immune system, resulting in improved tumor management. Chemotherapy utilizes nanoparticles to transport substances that induce DNA damage, therefore triggering the cGAS-STING pathway. An illustration of this approach is the utilization of polymeric nanoparticles that combine IDO inhibitors and platinum prodrugs. These nanoparticles cause DNA damage and enhance the immune system’s reaction to combat osteosarcoma. Moreover, the synergistic utilization of photothermal therapy (PTT) or photodynamic therapy (PDT) in conjunction with cGAS-STING activation might significantly augment the body’s immune response against tumors. Nanoparticles such as Prussian blue doped with manganese induce the production of ROS, trigger the cGAS-STING pathway, and enable the thermal destruction of tumor cells. This provides a holistic strategy for treating cancer. Overall, nanomedicine-based therapeutics offer a hopeful approach to enhance the effectiveness of cancer treatment by specifically activating the cGAS-STING pathway, boosting immune responses, minimizing adverse effects, and attaining better clinical results (Reviewed by [ ]) .Herein, we provide a brief classification of nanomedicine therapeutics against melanoma.

## Polymer-based nanoparticles
Poly(β-amino ester) (pBAE) nanoparticles are a specific kind of polymeric nanoparticles that are capable of degrading naturally and are compatible with living organisms. These nanoparticles are specifically engineered to transport medicinal substances, with a particular focus on their use in cancer treatment. Nanoparticles improve the stability and effectiveness of STING agonists, which are potent chemicals that activate the body’s natural immune response to combat cancer. In order to tackle the problems of low absorption by cells and quick elimination, STING agonists such CDNs are chemically attached to pBAE nanoparticles using a cathepsin-sensitive linker. This linker enables the deliberate and regulated release of the STING agonist inside the cell by means of lysosomal proteolysis, hence facilitating its activation. The resulting conjugated nanoparticles (CDN-NPs) have a diameter of around 41 nm and a positive surface charge of + 22 mV, which guarantees improved durability and stability in biological settings. The elimination of the main tumor in melanoma mice models is mostly caused by the activation of STING in immune cells of the host, rather than in the cancer cells themselves. The conjugation technique enables the CDN-NPs to function as a reservoir within cancer cells, gradually releasing the agonist to stimulate immune cells in the TME. This sustained release formulation improves the recruitment and activation of DCs, natural killer cells, and T cells, leading to an enhanced immune response against tumors. It also helps create immunological memory, which helps prevent tumor recurrence [ ]​. Chimaeric polymersomes (CPs) are sophisticated nanocarriers made up of a poly(ethylene glycol)-b-poly(dithiolane trimethylene carbonate-co-trimethylene carbonate)-b-polyethyleneimine (PEG-P(TMC-DTC)-PEI) copolymer. These nanocarriers are specifically designed to encapsulate and transport CDN STING agonists, such as ADU-S100, to improve the effectiveness of tumor immunotherapy. The colloidal particles (CPs) have a hydrodynamic size of around 47 nm, with a high encapsulation effectiveness of 86% and exceptional stability. This ensures that the CPs can stay in the tumor for a long time and protect the CDNs from being quickly cleared or degraded. When CPs are injected directly into a tumor, they help CDNs enter DCs and escape from endosomes, allowing them to enter the cytosol. This process effectively activates the STING pathway. This stimulation results in the synthesis of type I IFNs (IFN-β) and pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α), which enhance the development of DCs and the subsequent presentation of tumor antigens to T cells. As a result, a robust innate and adaptive immune response against melanoma is triggered. When CPs-CDN is combined with low-dose fractionated radiation therapy, it increases the immune response to tumors by causing immunogenic cell death (ICD) and increasing STING-mediated signaling. This leads to better suppression of tumors, longer survival rates, and minimum damage throughout the body. The combination of CPs-CDN showcases the ability to overcome obstacles in delivering cancer immunotherapy and enhance its long-lasting effects. This offers a promising treatment option for malignant melanoma [ ]. Endosomolytic polymersomes are nanoparticles that are specifically designed to efficiently carry therapeutic substances into cells. These substances are made up of amphiphilic diblock copolymers that have features that can destabilize membranes and respond to changes in pH. This allows them to break apart and release their contents into the cytosol when they come into contact with the acidic conditions found in endosomal compartments. When combined with CDN STING agonists like cGAMP, endosomolytic polymersomes greatly improve the effectiveness and strength of the agonists by overcoming obstacles in their delivery, such as inadequate cellular targeting and quick elimination. Within the realm of melanoma treatment, these polymersomes serve as protective carriers for cGAMP, safeguarding it until it is transported into cancerous cells. Once inside, they facilitate the release of cGAMP from endosomes and trigger the activation of the STING pathway. This activation initiates a strong immune response driven by type I IFNs, which transforms TME that suppress the immune system into ones that promote immune activity. This leads to increased infiltration of CD8 + T cells and natural killer cells into the tumor, improves the effectiveness of immune checkpoint blockade, and generates long-lasting immune memory to protect against future tumor challenges. This novel method showcases substantial therapeutic effectiveness whether administered either directly into the tumor or through intravenous injection, providing a hopeful technique to enhance clinical results in melanoma immunotherapy [ ]. Flash nanoprecipitation (FNP) is a method that utilizes PEG-bl-DEAEMA-co-BMA polymers to produce consistent nanocarriers that are responsive to changes in pH. These nanocarriers are specifically intended for delivering drugs. FNP involves the fast blending of polymer and drug solutions, resulting in the creation of nanoparticles with a variety of shapes, including micelles, vesicles, and multi-compartment vesicles. Through the manipulation of the polymer composition, these nanocarriers can effectively enclose therapeutic compounds and improve their transportation to the cytoplasm of certain cells. Within the realm of melanoma, these nanocarriers have the capability to be filled with immune-stimulating drugs like as cGAMP. Upon administration, they enhance endosomal escape and trigger the STING pathway in tumor cells, resulting in a robust anti-tumor immune response. The focused delivery system effectively hinders the growth of tumors and prolongs survival, demonstrating its promise as a viable treatment for melanoma [ ].

## Lipid-based nanoparticles
STING agonist-loaded lipid nanoparticles (STING-LNPs) have the ability to activate NK cells and overcome resistance to anti-PD-1 treatment in melanoma lung metastasis. In the B16-F10 mouse melanoma lung metastasis model, where resistance to anti-PD-1 monotherapy is observed, the intravenous injection of STING-LNPs greatly improved the effectiveness of anticancer treatment when paired with anti-PD-1 therapy. The process entails liver macrophages internalizing the STING-LNPs, resulting in the synthesis of type I IFNs. These IFNs stimulate NK cells throughout the body, which express PD-1 and secrete interferon-gamma (IFN-γ). IFN-γ enhances the production of PD-L1 on cancer cells, resulting in a feedback loop that increases the tumor’s vulnerability to PD-1 inhibition. The concurrent administration of these therapies reinvigorates NK cells, augmenting their ability to destroy tumor cells and successfully diminishing resistance to anti-PD-1 therapy. The findings suggest that by targeting the STING pathway to stimulate NK cells, it is possible to transform an immunologically inactive tumor into an active tumor, leading to enhanced therapeutic effects of checkpoint inhibitors in cancer types that are resistant to treatment [ ]. The utilization of STING agonist-loaded lipid nanoparticles (STING-LNPs) in combination with CpG-ODNs is an innovative immunotherapeutic strategy aimed at augmenting the immune system’s capacity to specifically eliminate melanoma cells. STING-LNPs are lipid nanoparticles that include a STING agonist, which stimulates the STING pathway, resulting in the strong production of type I IFNs. This process stimulates and triggers the involvement of NK cells and other components of the immune system. CpG-ODNs, which stands for cytosine-phosphate-guanine oligodeoxynucleotides, are man-made DNA sequences that imitate bacterial DNA. They stimulate Toll-like receptor 9 (TLR9) and trigger the synthesis of Th1 cytokines, specifically interleukin-12 (IL-12) and interleukin-18 (IL-18). The combo therapy greatly increases the production of these cytokines, resulting in the development and activation of memory-like NK cells that have a CD11bhighCD27low phenotype. These NK cells with memory-like properties demonstrate increased ability to kill target cells and remain active for a longer period of time, resulting in a continuous and effective fight against tumors. The synergistic combination of STING-LNPs and CpG-ODNs leads to robust immune system activation, specifically targeting and triggering apoptosis in melanoma cells, thus inhibiting lung metastasis and providing enduring preventive advantages [ ]. Similarly, liposomes containing the STING pathway ligand, cyclic di-GMP (c-di-GMP), efficiently stimulate NK cells and provide substantial anticancer effects in a lung metastasis animal model with B16-F10 melanoma, a tumor form that does not express MHC-I. Administering c-di-GMP/YSK05-liposomes resulted in elevated cytokine production (IFN-β, TNF-α, IL-6, IFN-γ), heightened activation of NK cells, and a significant decrease in tumor development. NK cells were recognized as the principal agents responsible for this antitumor reaction, as the elimination of NK cells nullified the therapeutic benefits. The results indicate that c-di-GMP/YSK05-liposomes have potential as a novel adjuvant for cancer immunotherapy, capable of inducing both MHC-I restricted and non-restricted immune responses against tumors [ ]. R-DOTAP, also known as R-1,2-dioleoyl-3-trimethyl-ammonium-propane, is a specific type of cationic lipid nanoparticle that is used for cancer immunotherapy. It effectively triggers strong immune responses from CD8 T cells by using a new method that involves the production of type I IFNs through the MyD88 pathway and the activation of endosomal TLR7 and TLR9 receptors. R-DOTAP, when coupled with melanoma-associated antigens such as Trp2 and anti-PD1 checkpoint drugs, effectively suppresses tumor growth in melanoma treatment. This therapy combines different treatments to increase the presence and effectiveness of CD8 T lymphocytes that specifically target tumors in the TME. This leads to strong anti-tumor effects and longer survival in models of melanoma [ ]. Similarly, SAProsome-3 is a nano-liposomal vesicle that is chemically designed to activate STING. It contains a pro-drug derivative of the non-nucleotide STING agonist MSA-2, which can be activated by an esterase. This formulation improves the pharmacokinetic characteristics and immune stimulatory capacity in comparison to the unbound medication. SAProsome-3 enhances the transportation effectiveness to tumors and lymphoid compartments, resulting in strong activation of the STING pathway, heightened release of inflammatory cytokines, and a cancer-killing immune environment. SAProsome-3 shown exceptional therapeutic effectiveness in the B16F10 melanoma model, leading to substantial reduction in tumor size, long-lasting absence of the disease, and extended lifespan. The intervention led to a notable rise in the number of CD8 + cytotoxic T cells, a higher ratio of CD8 + to CD4 + T cells, and a decrease in M2-polarized macrophages within the tumors. Furthermore, SAProsome-3 augmented the import of NK cells, enhanced the expression of DC costimulatory markers, and decreased the presence of MDSCs. The combination of SAProsome-3 with anti-PD-L1 checkpoint blockage resulted in even greater effects, leading to total elimination of the tumor and a synergistic impact in stimulating a systemic immune response against the tumor [ ].

## Hybrid nanoparticles
Hybrid nanovaccines, like the MPO nanovaccines, are advanced medicinal substances made up of biodegradable nanohybrids that combine manganese oxide nanoparticles (MnOx) with cationic polymers. These nanovaccines are specifically created to improve the entire process of vaccination, which involves loading the antigen, transporting it to the lymph nodes, being taken up by DCs, the maturation of DCs, and the cross-presentation of the antigen. MnOx nanoparticles, due to their uneven surface and ideal dimensions, enhance the flow towards lymph nodes and absorption by DCs, hence stimulating their maturation via the cGAS-STING pathway. Cationic polymers facilitate the process of enclosing antigens and enabling their escape from lysosomes through the proton sponge effect, hence improving the transport of antigens into the cytosol. These pathways together lead to strong T-cell immunological responses. In addition, MPO nanovaccines trigger ICD, resulting in the release of tumor-associated antigens (TAAs) at the site of action, hence enhancing the activation of antitumor immune response. The nanovaccines also alter TME by transforming macrophages from an immunosuppressive M2 state to an anticancer M1 state, and decreasing the presence of immunosuppressive cells such as myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) and Tregs. Hybrid nanovaccines have the potential to be highly effective in tailored cancer immunotherapy. In melanoma models, these vaccines have been shown to significantly decrease tumor development, lengthen survival, and enhance both preventive and therapeutic antitumor immunity [ ]. Hybrid cellular membrane nanovesicles (hNVs) are a novel therapeutic strategy that utilizes a combination of nanovesicles formed from multiple types of cells to bolster immune responses against cancer. The hNVs are created by combining nanovesicles derived from platelets, M1 macrophages, and engineered cancer cells that have been modified to produce high-affinity SIRPα variants. hNVs are designed to specifically accumulate at surgical wound sites, where they can interact with circulating tumor cells (CTCs) and change the polarization of tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) from a tumor-promoting M2 phenotype to an anticancer M1 phenotype. Through the inhibition of the CD47-SIRPα connection, which normally hinders macrophages from engulfing cancer cells, hNVs effectively stimulate macrophage-mediated phagocytosis and boost the immune response of T cells against tumors. In addition, these hNVs can be infused with a STING agonist to enhance the immune response, especially in tumors that have a weak ability to provoke an immunological response. In studies involving melanoma models, the administration of hNVs through intravenous infusion has shown notable effectiveness in preventing both the reappearance of cancer in the same area and the spread of cancer to other parts of the body after surgery, resulting in extended survival. This versatile platform demonstrates the capabilities of hNVs in augmenting immune responses and presents a promising approach for tailored cancer immunotherapy [ ]. Furthermore, self-degradable poly(β-amino ester)s are polymers that are compatible with living organisms and break down at particular pH values. This process results in the release of their cargo, such as antigens and STING agonists, into the cytosol of specific cells. These polymers are utilized in the field of melanoma treatment to produce a nanovaccine that amplifies immune responses against malignancies. The degradation products of the polymer enhance the release of these therapeutic compounds from the endosome into the cytosol, resulting in strong activation of CD8 + T cells and the generation of pro-inflammatory cytokines. This mechanism converts TME from being immunologically inactive to being highly active, which encourages the infiltration of immune cells and boosts the body’s ability to combat melanoma. As a result, tumor growth is suppressed and survival rates in treated animals are improved [ ].

## STING agonist co-loaded nanoparticles
Polymersomes containing the STING agonist 2′3′-cGAMP have anti-melanoma activity by employing multiple molecular pathways. When these polymersomes are injected into the bloodstream, they increase the amount of time the STING agonist stays in the body and how it is distributed, making sure it builds up in tumors and secondary lymphoid organs. Upon entering TME, the STING pathway becomes active, resulting in the generation of type I IFNs and pro-inflammatory cytokines. This activation alters the tumor immunological milieu by facilitating the entry of CD4 + and CD8 + T cells, which are crucial for the body’s defense against tumors. In addition, it upregulates the production of chemokines such as CXCL9 and CXCL10, hence enhancing the recruitment of immune cells to the tumor location. STING-NPs have been found to effectively suppress tumor growth and enhance survival rates in melanoma models. This effect is particularly pronounced when STING-NPs are used in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors like αPD-L1. These findings highlight the potential of STING-NPs to transform “cold” tumors into “hot” ones, thereby increasing their sensitivity to immunotherapy [ ]. UniSTING, a type of universal STING mimic, is specifically engineered to boost the body’s immune response against tumors. It achieves this by selectively activating signaling pathways that limit tumor growth, while avoiding the activation of pro-inflammatory pathways that can actually help tumors grow. UniSTING is formed by combining a tetramerization motif with the cytoplasmic domain of STING. This allows UniSTING to activate STING signaling without relying on the presence of endogenous STING. UniSTING-mRNA, when enclosed in lipid nanoparticles (LNPs), exhibits strong anticancer properties in many animal and human tumor types, including melanoma. It achieves this by enhancing the IRF3/IFN-I pathway and bypassing the NF-κB pathway. As a consequence, there is an improvement in the development of DCs, an increase in CD8 + T-cell reactions, and a suppression of tumor growth. In addition, extracellular vesicles derived from tumor cells treated with uniSTING enhance the sensitivity of DCs by the transfer of exosomal miRNAs, specifically targeting the immunosuppressive Wnt2b pathway. The combination of LNP-uniSTING-mRNA and anti-Wnt2b antibodies has a synergistic effect, enhancing the effectiveness of tumor treatment and extending the lifespan. This approach shows promise in the treatment of malignancies that lack the STING protein [ ].
The utilization of nanoparticles as a co-delivery strategy for doxorubicin (DOX) and cGAMP exhibits encouraging outcomes in combating melanoma. This novel strategy utilizes the ICD characteristics of DOX to induce the release of DAMPs and tumor-associated antigens. This, in turn, stimulates the maturation of DCs and the infiltration of CTLs. Simultaneously, cGAMP triggers the STING pathway, amplifying the immune response by stimulating the release of type I IFNs and pro-inflammatory cytokines. Hence, the combined therapy not only effectively eliminates cancer cells but also triggers a strong immune reaction against the tumor, converting “cold” tumors into “hot” tumors that are more vulnerable to immune assault. Furthermore, the nanoparticles facilitate precise administration, enhance medication stability, and maintain a controlled release, hence decreasing systemic toxicity and improving the overall therapeutic effectiveness. This dual approach not only efficiently hinders the growth of tumors but also alters TME by reducing the presence of immunosuppressive cells, such as Tregs, MDSCs, and M2 macrophages. Therefore, the co-delivery system enhances the body’s ability to fight against tumors and shows great promise as a treatment approach for melanoma [ ]. Lipo/TK-CDN/TPP/Y6 is a versatile nanoparticle system specifically created for precise treatment of melanoma. It integrates photothermal therapy (PTT), photodynamic therapy (PDT), and immunotherapy to maximize therapeutic efficacy. This system comprises of thioketone-linked cyclic dinucleotide (TK-CDN), a prodrug that responds to ROS, and chlorin e6 (Y6), a photosensitizer. These components are enclosed within liposomes that have been modified with triphenylphosphonium (TPP) to specifically target mitochondria. The nanoparticles demonstrate a strong photothermal effect, effectively raising the temperature when exposed to 808 nm laser light. This leads to improved absorption by cells and increased production of ROS within B16F10 melanoma cells. Lipo/TK-CDN/TPP/Y6, when exposed to laser irradiation, triggers a significant generation of ROS, which leads to the programmed death of tumor cells and enhances the release of CDN. This, in turn, activates the STING pathway. In vivo experiments have shown that this nanoparticle formulation effectively suppresses the growth of tumors in mice with melanoma by promoting the activation of STING and enhancing the infiltration of immune cells that fight against tumors, such as CD8 + T cells and CD103 + DCs. As a result, it induces a robust immune response throughout the body to combat melanoma [ ]​. CpG oligodeoxynucleotides (CpG ODN) are brief artificial DNA strands that consist of unmethylated cytosine-phosphate-guanine (CpG) dinucleotide patterns. These patterns imitate bacterial DNA and function as substances that stimulate the immune system by activating Toll-like receptor 9 (TLR9) found in specific immune cells, such as DCs and B cells. Stimulation of TLR9 by CpG ODN initiates a series of immunological reactions, such as the generation of pro-inflammatory cytokines and the augmentation of both innate and adaptive immunity. CpG ODNs, known for their capacity to enhance immunological responses, are commonly employed as adjuvants in vaccines and as immunotherapeutic agents in the treatment of cancer and infectious disorders. The combination of CpG ODN and cGAMP inside pH-sensitive liposomes greatly improves the effectiveness of treatment for melanoma by enhancing the stability and cellular uptake of these adjuvants. The co-encapsulation results in a significant augmentation in the synthesis of type I and II interferons and Th1-biased cytokines, such as IL-6 and IL-12, which play a vital role in promoting efficient immune responses. The liposomal delivery technique guarantees the simultaneous administration of both adjuvants to the same cells, thereby amplifying their synergistic effects. This leads to the alteration of the TME, decreasing the occurrence of immunosuppressive M2 macrophages and encouraging a more inflammatory, anti-tumor M1 macrophage phenotype. As a result, this method not only decelerates the growth of tumors but also improves the immune response of CD8 + T cells that target specific antigens, resulting in substantial reduction of the tumor [ ]. Khalifa et al. conducted a study to determine the importance of the interval, sequence of administration, and number of cycles in maximizing the combination anticancer effects of STING agonist loaded lipid nanoparticles (STING-LNP) and PD-1 antibody against melanoma lung metastasis. The study revealed that a single round of the combined treatment did not have a noteworthy impact on tumor growth. However, two and three rounds of treatment resulted in a considerable reduction in tumor size, with two rounds being the most effective. The timing of doses was critical; a two-day interval resulted in notable antitumor effects, whereas a four-day interval did not yield the same outcome. The sequence of administration (whether STING-LNP or PD-1 antibody was administered first) did not affect the antitumor response in a protocol with overlapping treatments. The best combination impact found with the two-cycle treatment can be explained by the following mechanisms: increased activation markers in NK cells, heightened levels of IFN-γ, PD-1, and PD-L1 in TME, and greater activation of NK cells in the lung. These findings emphasize the significance of doing multiple cycles with a brief interval in order to attain adequate activation of NK cells and a good immune response for successful tumor suppression [ ].

## Peptide nanotubes
Peptide nanotubes (PNTs) are nanostructures that are created by the self-assembly of peptides. In this research, KL-7 peptides, which are produced from Aβ amyloid fibrils, were particularly tailored for this purpose. The nanotubes serve as carriers for medicinal medicines because they have the ability to encapsulate and transport different compounds. By loading these nanotubes with the STING agonist (c-di-GMP), the therapeutic effectiveness of c-di-GMP is significantly improved. This is particularly beneficial because c-di-GMP often faces difficulties such as limited capacity to pass through cell membranes and low availability in the body. The PNTs facilitate the efficient transportation of c-di-GMP into tumor cells, so successfully initiating the STING pathway. Activation of this pathway triggers a robust immunological response, characterized by the release of cytokines such as IL-6 and INF-β and elevated levels of phospho-STING, leading to enhanced activity of the immune system. Administering c-di-GMP-loaded PNTs (c-di-GMP-PNT) in melanoma models increases the expression of important inflammatory cytokines (INF-β, TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β) and improves the function of CD4 and CD8 T cells. As a result, this causes a substantial inhibition of tumor growth, demonstrating the promise of c-di-GMP-PNT as an effective nanomaterial for cancer immunotherapy. Furthermore, this medication demonstrates no notable damage to vital organs or negative impact on the biochemical parameters in the mice that received it, highlighting its safety and effectiveness [ ].

## Metallo-nanomedicines
Metallo-nanomedicines are medicinal substances that utilize metal ions within nanostructures to take advantage of the distinct characteristics of metals for medical purposes, specifically in augmenting immune responses and focusing on diseases such as cancer. Manganese@Albumin Nanocomplexes (Mn@BSA) are metallo-nanomedicine entities in which manganese ions are chemically bonded to bovine serum albumin (BSA) to create nanocomplexes. The Mn@BSA nanocomplexes can activate pro-inflammatory reactions in macrophages via TLR4-dependent signaling pathways, such as NF-κB and IRF7. This activation results in the release of cytokines and chemokines that strengthen the immune response. When these nanocomplexes are combined to form nanowires, they enhance and extend the immune response. Mn@BSA nanocomplexes and nanowires have been found to successfully stimulate peritoneal macrophages and alter tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) in melanoma models, resulting in the suppression of tumor growth. These nanocomplexes and nanowires exhibit notable anticancer properties and are safer than conventional TLR4 agonists such as lipopolysaccharides (LPS). The use of Mn@BSA demonstrates its potential as an effective tool in cancer immunotherapy [ ]. Furthermore, the combination of photoirradiation and manganese-coordinated nanomedicine (PMC NPs) efficiently triggers pyroptosis (PhotoPyro) and activates the cGAS-STING pathway in melanoma cells. PMC NPs were found to produce ROS when exposed to light in laboratory trials conducted outside of a living organism. This resulted in a notable level of cell death and a specific type of programmed cell death called pyroptosis, which was confirmed by observing distinct changes in cell shape and the release of markers associated with inflammation. In an in vivo setting, the therapy effectively suppressed the growth of both primary and distant tumors in a mouse model of melanoma by promoting the maturation of DCs and activation of T cells, resulting in a strong systemic immune response against the tumor. The data indicate that photoirradiation of PMC NPs presents a promising noninvasive method to enhance melanoma immunotherapy [ ]. Iron oxide nanoparticles (IONPs) are magnetic contrast agents used in clinical settings to improve cancer treatment, namely for melanoma, at the cellular and molecular levels. IONPs enhance the production of IFN-I by the STING agonist MSA-2 by creating ROS within cells through the Fenton reaction. This, in turn, activates nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB). As a result, this activation increases the intensity of STING activation, so strengthening the body’s natural immunological responses. When included into an iron nanoadjuvant called PEIM, which is created by combining PEPA ionizable copolymer with IONPs and MSA-2, IONPs enhance the transportation through the lymphatic system and the presentation of antigens to CD169 + APCs in the nearby lymph nodes. This procedure enhances the activation of CD8 + cytotoxic T-lymphocytes, resulting in strong antigen-specific T-cell immunity. The PEIM@OVA nanovaccine, consisting of the combination of PEIM and the model antigen ovalbumin (OVA), effectively inhibits tumor growth and eliminates pre-existing melanoma tumors, resulting in robust antitumor immunity and the inhibition of lung metastasis [ ].

## Silica nanoparticles
Cationic silica nanoparticles (CSiNPs) are silica-based nanostructures with a positive charge. They possess distinct physicochemical features that make them well-suited for a range of therapeutic uses, such as cancer therapy. The nanoparticles have an average diameter of approximately 30 nm and contain surface amine groups that give them a positive charge. The presence of a positive surface charge increases their affinity for negatively charged cellular membranes and extracellular matrix components, facilitating their absorption and accumulation in specific tissues. CSiNPs have a dual role in melanoma therapy. They function as both cytotoxic agents and carriers for immunostimulatory molecules. When CSiNPs are injected directly into the tumor, they cause rapid death of melanoma cells by damaging the outer layer of the cells and producing ROS, which results in oxidative stress. Tumor cell death results in the release of tumor-associated antigens into TME. These antigens can then be collected by antigen-presenting cells, which in turn trigger or enhance an immune response. In addition, CSiNPs can form complexes with cyclic dinucleotides such as c-di-GMP, which is an agonist of the STING pathway, in order to further increase immunological activation. The concurrent occurrence of necrosis generated by CSiNP and immunostimulation mediated by STING results in a robust immunological response to tumors, which is characterized by heightened infiltration and activation of CD8 + T cells. This method not only hinders the growth of the main tumor but also triggers the development of immunological memory, providing defense against the reappearance of the tumor. Therefore, CSiNPs offer a hopeful platform for in situ cancer vaccination by utilizing their inherent ability to kill cells and deliver immunostimulatory substances directly to the tumor location [ ].

## Nanovaccine formulations
Nanovaccines are sophisticated therapeutic medicines that utilize nanoparticles to effectively transport antigens and activate immune responses that specifically target tumors. They boost the body’s ability to fight against tumors by optimizing every stage of the vaccination process and triggering a type of cell death that stimulates the immune system (known as immunogenic cell death or ICD). The work examines the utilization of biodegradable nanohybrids consisting of manganese oxide nanoparticles and cationic polymers to create MPO nanovaccines. The nanovaccines are specifically engineered to utilize the inherent characteristics of nanoparticles, including their dimensions, shape, surface charge, and immunoregulatory capabilities. The nanohybrids enhance the process of antigen encapsulation, promote effective drainage to lymph nodes, and facilitate internalization by DCs. In addition, they stimulate the maturation of DCs through the cGAS-STING pathway and improve the ability of antigens to escape from lysosomes and be presented to immune cells through the “proton sponge effect.” In addition, the nanohybrids stimulate ICD, resulting in the liberation of tumor-associated antigens (TAAs) and subsequent vigorous T-cell immunological responses. The extensive improvements lead to a substantial reduction in tumor growth, a reversal of immune system suppression, and a longer lifespan in models with tumors. This demonstrates the potential of nanovaccines for customized cancer immunotherapy [ , ]. PC7A is a synthetic nanoparticle made of polymers that stimulates the STING pathway, which in turn improves the delivery of antigens and the process of cross-presentation. This leads to an increased immune response by T cells against malignancies. The PC7A nanovaccine has been successfully administered directly into the tumor, resulting in a greater concentration of the vaccine within TME. This has led to an enhanced production of CXCL9 in myeloid cells, effectively combating melanoma. This chemokine has the ability to draw cytotoxic CD8 + T cells to the location of the tumor. These T cells then release IFNγ, which in turn increases the expression of CXCL9. This creates a positive feedback loop that improves the recruitment and infiltration of T cells. This approach not only enhances the immune response against tumors in the local area, but also triggers a systemic immunological memory, which offers long-lasting defense against melanoma. Intratumoral administration overcomes the constraints of subcutaneous delivery by assuring strong infiltration of antigen-specific T cells into the tumor. This promotes efficient elimination of the tumor and has the potential to enhance clinical results in melanoma therapy [ ]. Biomimetic nanovaccines are novel vaccines that aim to increase cancer immunotherapy by increasing the targeting and internalization of DCs. The vaccines are composed of a core made of nanoparticles that respond to reactive oxygen species, and a shell made of cancer cell membranes. The shell is fused with a peptide that induces macropinocytosis, which is a process that allows cells to take in large amounts of fluid. This peptide specifically targets the CXCR4 receptor on DCs. This design enables the direct transport of cancer membrane-associated antigens and the STING agonist cGAMP into the cytosol, which enhances the maturation of DCs and the activation of T cells. The molecular mechanism is the utilization of the CXCR4-mediated macropinocytosis pathway, which enables the efficient internalization of the nanovaccine by DCs. This is followed by the production of cGAMP in response to ROS, which activates the STING pathway. Consequently, this triggers an inflammatory response and facilitates successful antigen presentation. At the cellular level, this process improves the development of DCs and the stimulation of tumor-specific cytotoxic T lymphocytes, leading to substantial tumor elimination and inhibition of melanoma growth. This effect is particularly pronounced when paired with immune checkpoint inhibitors such as anti-PD-1 [ ].
STING-activating DNA nanovaccines (STING-NVs) are a novel method of delivering cancer immunotherapy, specifically for melanoma. The nanovaccines consist of PLA-b-PEG nanoparticles as the core, which are covered with cytosine-rich i-motif DNA on the surface. This design enables the efficient loading of CDNs, such as cyclic dimeric guanosine monophosphate (CDG), at a pH level that is typical for living organisms, by utilizing hydrogen bonding through G: C base pairing. When the i-motif DNA comes into contact with the acidic environment of endosomes in immune cells, it undergoes a change in shape, generating C-quadruplexes that release the CDG, ensuring its successful delivery. STING-NVs improve cancer immunotherapy by safeguarding CDG from enzymatic breakdown, facilitating its internal transportation, and enabling its release from endosomes into the cytosol, where it can activate the STING pathway. STING activation results in strong antitumor immune responses, characterized by the increased expression of proinflammatory cytokines and chemokines, which in turn enhance both innate and adaptive immune responses. In laboratory tests, it has been demonstrated that STING-NVs have the ability to convert immunosuppressive M2-like macrophages into anticancer M1-like macrophages. Additionally, they enhance the production of cytokines such as IL-6, IL-12p40, and IFN-α, and stimulate DCs by increasing the expression of MHC-II, CD80, and CD86. STING-NVs alter the tumor immune microenvironment in mouse melanoma models by reducing the expression of M2-associated gene transcripts (Ym1, Arg1, Mrc1) and boosting the expression of M1-associated (Tnf, IL6, Nos2) and antitumor gene transcripts (Ifnb1, Cxcl9, Cxcl10). This modulation decreases the repression of the immune system and increases the activation and recruitment of T cells, resulting in a significant decrease in tumor growth and a longer lifespan in the mice that were treated. When compared to free CDG and liposomal CDG (Lipo-CDG), STING-NVs demonstrate higher therapeutic effectiveness and survival advantages without notable toxicity. This makes them a promising tool for enhancing the results of cancer immunotherapy [ ].
The Mn-cGAMP Nanovaccine (Mn-cGAMP NVs) is specifically engineered to augment the body’s immune responses against melanoma, by addressing the challenges associated with delivering cGAMP. The nanovaccine utilizes thiolated and Mn2 + coordinated cGAMP to enhance its stability and facilitate direct delivery into the cytosol, avoiding degradation in the endo-/lysosomes. This design amplifies the activation of the STING pathway, resulting in elevated cytokine production and the recruitment of CD8 + T cells. Mn-cGAMP NVs exhibited substantial inhibition of both main and distant tumor growth in a B16F10 melanoma model, leading to extended survival by enhancing the immune response mediated by T-cells. In addition, the combination of Mn-cGAMP NVs with an immune checkpoint inhibitor, αPD-L1, resulted in a synergistic enhancement of the anticancer effects. This combination effectively prevented tumor immune escape and ultimately led to improved therapeutic outcomes [ ].
The use of a polyethyleneimine (PEI)-based vaccine platform effectively produces strong CD8 + T cell responses and promotes their penetration into melanoma tumors, resulting in personalized combination nano-immunotherapy. This platform utilizes nanoparticles (NP) that contain neoantigen peptides and CpG adjuvants. These nanoparticles are delivered subcutaneously to stimulate the activation and antigen cross-presentation of DCs. Although there is a substantial presence of neoantigen-specific CD8 + T cells in the bloodstream, the initial immune response has limited penetration into the tumor. In order to tackle this issue, the delivery of a STING agonist directly into the tumor site stimulates the release of chemokines and cytokines that attract T cells. This greatly improves the recruitment of CD8 + T cells that have been primed by the vaccination into TME. The combo treatment exhibits strong anti-tumor effectiveness, leading to tumor shrinkage and the development of lasting immune memory against melanoma. This has been demonstrated in laboratory mice with B16F10 melanoma [ ].

## Nanoparticle-based immune modulation

## Reprogramming myeloid cells
Biodegradable nanoparticles like ONP-302, composed of poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA), are engineered to naturally decompose within the body. ONP-302 selectively targets myeloid cells, such as monocytes and macrophages, activating the cGAS/STING pathway and triggering the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines, including TNF-α, MIP-1α, MIP-1β, and IL-15. This activation enhances the immune response by boosting the activity of NK cells and CD8 + T cells. In melanoma models, ONP-302 significantly reduced tumor growth, increased tumor necrosis, and improved survival rates. Additionally, it enhanced the effectiveness of anti-PD-1 therapy in previously resistant tumors, underscoring its potential to improve immunotherapy outcomes [ ].

## Targeting macrophage polarization
R-M@CS-PAA NPs, composed of chitosan-poly(acrylic acid), contain the TLR7/8 agonist R848 and manganese chloride (MnCl2), designed to release their payload in the acidic TME for enhanced treatment efficacy. These nanoparticles exhibit strong anti-tumor effects in melanoma by promoting the infiltration of pro-inflammatory M1 macrophages and CD8 + T cells, while reducing suppressive immune cells. They also stimulate DC development through the STING and NF-кB pathways, boosting antigen presentation and CD8 + T cell-mediated tumor destruction. This immune modulation effectively suppresses melanoma growth, making R-M@CS-PAA NPs a promising cancer immunotherapy strategy [ ]. 

## Targeting innate immunity
A novel modular adjuvant platform has been developed using nanostructured capsules that incorporate antigens and multiple toll-like receptor agonists (TLRas). Targeting endolysosomes, these capsules enable precise control over signal transduction across various TLR pathways, eliciting diverse antigen-specific immune responses. They effectively remodel lymph node tissue, fostering an environment conducive to T cell proliferation. Capsules with multiple TLRas enhance T cell performance and antitumor effects compared to those with a single TLRa. This platform significantly increases IFNγ production without elevating IL-6 or IL-10, demonstrating its ability to tailor immune responses. The iPEM vaccine alters lymph node activation states, boosting dendritic cell (DC) numbers and promoting T and B cell interaction, indicative of a robust pro-immune environment. Adoptive immunity experiments show that iPEM vaccination expands antigen-specific T cells and enhances their proliferation in associated lymph nodes. In anticancer studies, immune-potentiated exosomes (iPEMs) containing multiple TLRas provide substantial tumor protection and improved survival rates, outperforming single TLRa and soluble formulations. The platform’s flexibility in combining various antigens and TLRas, including those targeting human melanoma, underscores its potential for rapid integration and precise immune regulation, advancing vaccine and immunotherapy development [ ].

## Photodynamic sensetive nanoparticles
Photodynamic therapy (PDT) is a two-phase cancer treatment involving the administration of a photosensitizer (PS) followed by exposure to specific wavelengths of light, activating the PS to produce reactive oxygen species (ROS). This leads to localized oxidative stress and cell death, triggering a strong immune response against melanomas by inducing oxidative ER stress and apoptosis. Consequently, type 1 interferons (IFN-α/β) are upregulated, activating the IFN-1 pathway, which enhances IRF-3 and STAT1 phosphorylation and promotes interferon-stimulated gene (ISG) expression. This pathway is vital for dendritic cell (DC) activation, evidenced by increased expression of co-stimulatory signals (CD80, MHC-II) and improved DC migration to tumors, thereby initiating adaptive immune responses and bolstering the overall anti-tumor effect of PDT [ ]. Photodynamic nanoparticles, like NPPDT-56MESS, merge PDT with additional therapeutic capabilities, specifically targeting cancer. NPPDT-56MESS combines a photodynamic polymer (P1) with the cationic platinum (II) agent 56MESS. When stimulated by an 808 nm laser, ROS are generated, causing the rapid degradation of the nanoparticle and releasing 56MESS. This platinum-based drug damages DNA by inserting itself into the nuclear DNA and perforating mitochondria, releasing mtDNA into the cytoplasm. The accumulation of ROS and 56MESS increases cytoplasmic double-stranded DNA (dsDNA), activating the cGAS pathway, which produces cGAMP that binds to STING. This activation triggers a signaling cascade involving TBK1 and IRF3 phosphorylation, leading to the production of type I IFNs and pro-inflammatory cytokines, promoting DC development and enhancing the infiltration of immune cells like NK cells and cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs) into the tumor microenvironment. Overall, this approach effectively destroys tumor cells while stimulating the body’s immune response, offering a comprehensive treatment for uveal melanoma (UM) with reduced systemic toxicity [ ].

## cGAS-STING in vaccine therapy of melanomas
The cGAS-STING pathway agonists are becoming increasingly recognized as potent vaccine adjuvants because of their crucial role in coordinating both innate and adaptive immune responses. This makes them particularly efficient in boosting the immunogenicity of vaccines. These agonists activate the cGAS-STING signaling pathway, which is essential for the generation of type I IFNs and other inflammatory cytokines that enhance the body’s immunological defense mechanisms. When employed as adjuvants, cGAS-STING pathway agonists augment the body’s immune response to vaccines by promoting more robust and enduring immunological reactions, hence heightening the efficacy of the vaccines in thwarting infectious illnesses and maybe malignancies. Their capacity to stimulate a strong immune response makes them especially important in the development of contemporary subunit and nucleic acid vaccines, which often have lower immunogenicity when used alone. Furthermore, these agonists have demonstrated promise in preclinical investigations for diverse ailments, such as COVID-19, underscoring their adaptability and extensive range of usefulness in vaccine creation. Integrating cGAS-STING agonists into vaccination platforms is a progressive strategy for developing more powerful vaccines, particularly in response to the difficulties presented by rapidly evolving infectious illnesses and cancer immunotherapy [ ]. The TheraVac Vaccine (TheraVac) is a cutting-edge immunotherapeutic treatment that aims to fight cancer by stimulating and strengthening the body’s immune system. The composition comprises three primary constituents: HMGN1 (N1), which acts as an agonist for TLR4; R848, which acts as an agonist for TLR7/8; and an immunological checkpoint inhibitor known as anti-PDL-1. These components work together to stimulate the activation of DCs, enhance Th1 immune responses, and decrease the immunosuppression associated with tumors. Nevertheless, TheraVac alone was inadequate in eradicating melanoma in mice. By adding cGAMP, an agonist of the STING pathway, to TheraVac, the therapy was able to improve the development and activation of DCs that had infiltrated the tumor, as well as boost the production of cytokines that stimulate the immune system, such as IL-12p70 and TNF-α. The administration of this combined treatment resulted in a notable enhancement in the infiltration of immune cells, specifically CD8 + cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs) and memory T cells, into melanoma tumors. Consequently, this led to strong and effective antitumor effects. The mice that received treatment exhibited a significant increase in tumor elimination and acquired durable, tumor-specific immunity, indicating that TheraVac has the potential to be an effective therapeutic approach for melanoma [ ]. The co-administration of STING and TLR 7/8 agonists, namely DMXAA and 522, has demonstrated substantial potential as vaccine adjuvants for melanoma immunotherapy owing to their capacity to synergistically stimulate both innate and adaptive immune responses. This combination augments the stimulation of DCs generated from bone marrow, resulting in heightened production of co-stimulatory molecules and the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-12. These cytokines play a vital role in the efficient activation of T-cells. Studies conducted in live melanoma models showed that the combination treatment led to strong and specific immune responses from CD8 + T cells and moderate activation of NK cells. This resulted in a considerable reduction in tumor growth and increased rates of survival compared to using each treatment individually. In addition, the combination therapy decreased the number of pro-tumorigenic M2 macrophages while increasing the number of tumoricidal M1 macrophages, thereby further improving the immune environment against tumors. The results were supported by the presence of increased amounts of IFN-γ and decreased levels of IL-10 in the body, suggesting a shift towards a Th1-skewed immune response. The results highlight the potential of DMXAA and 522 as powerful adjuvants in melanoma vaccines, capable of coordinating a strong and all-encompassing immune response against tumor cells [ ].
mRNA vaccines offer a hopeful approach to preventing and treating cancer. These vaccines contain the entire antigens and allow for temporary expression, which helps avoid the difficulties of insertional mutagenesis that are often associated with DNA vaccines. Heterocyclic lipids can promote the transport of mRNA vaccines, leading to improved intracellular delivery and immune activation. Miao et al. generated a comprehensive collection of more than 1,000 lipid formulations in order to discover the most effective carriers for delivering mRNA. The most effective formulations, which have an unsaturated lipid tail, a dihydroimidazole linker, and cyclic amine head groups, produced strong immune responses that greatly reduced tumor development and increased survival in melanoma models.The findings demonstrated that cyclic lipids promote the transfer of mRNA and stimulate antigen-presenting cells (APCs) through the STING pathway, rather than the Toll-like receptors. This leads to a restricted release of cytokines throughout the body and an improved effectiveness in combating tumors. The primary contender, A18, exhibited notable mRNA protein expression, robust immunological response, and extended life in several tumor types, such as melanoma, with more than 60% of treated mice surviving beyond 40 days. The cyclic lipids induced the maturation of APCs, causing an increase in the expression of markers such as CD40 and MHCII. Additionally, they enhanced the expression of IFN-stimulated genes, indicating the activation of the STING pathway. In summary, the work demonstrates that cyclic lipid-mRNA vaccines can effectively stimulate a strong immune response against tumors by specifically activating the STING pathway [ ]. The L17-F05 mRNA vaccine shows substantial promise in fighting B16F10 melanoma in mice through many pathways. The unique LNP formulation efficiently transports mRNAs that encode tumor antigens Gp100 and Trp2 to antigen-presenting cells (APCs), specifically conventional DCs and macrophages, located in the draining lymph nodes (dLNs). The vaccination amplifies the expression of co-stimulatory molecules on cDCs, thereby designating them as the principal antigen-presenting cells (APCs). At the same time, macrophages start strong natural immune responses focused on producing IFN-I through the STING pathway. This process helps the maturation and activation of antigen-presenting cells (APCs). Administering L17-F05 subcutaneously leads to substantial suppression of tumor growth and extended longevity of mice, principally via stimulating robust CD8 + T cell responses specific to OVA. The vaccine’s ability to activate the STING-IFN-I pathway and enhance immune responses is essential for effectively suppressing the progression of melanoma and increasing its anti-tumor effectiveness [ ].
Protein-based cancer vaccines are created with the intention of activating the immune system to identify and combat cancerous cells by introducing particular tumor antigens to immune cells. Vaccines usually contain protein constructions that include epitopes recognized by T cells, which then trigger a specific immune response. The KISIMA™ platform exemplifies a vaccine that incorporates a cell-penetrating peptide, a multiantigenic domain, and a self-adjuvanting component to augment immunogenicity. The immune response is greatly intensified when a STING agonist, which activates the STING pathway, is used in combination. STING agonists elicit a strong inflammatory reaction, marked by elevated levels of type I IFNs and other pro-inflammatory cytokines. This fosters a more conducive environment for immunological activation and the infiltration of T cells into tumors. When B16 melanoma cells are treated with both the KISIMA vaccination and a STING agonist, there is a significant increase in the number and effectiveness of CD8 T lymphocytes that specifically target antigens. This increase is observed both in the peripheral blood and within the tumor itself. The combination also augments the polarization of CD4 T cells towards a Th1 phenotype, diminishes the presence of immune-suppressive cells, and fosters a more inflammatory TME. These modifications collectively lead to enhanced regulation of tumor growth and a heightened anti-tumor immune response [ ]. Similarly, a study examined how the effectiveness of a peptide vaccine against melanoma in mice could be improved by using the STING activator c-di-GMP. The findings indicated that c-di-GMP greatly enhanced the ability of the peptide vaccination to stimulate an immune response and inhibit tumor growth. The co-administration of c-di-GMP with the vaccine led to a significant augmentation in CD8 T cell responses specific to the antigen, hence decelerating tumor growth. To get a stronger therapeutic outcome, it was necessary to simultaneously provide the adjuvant poly-IC and the costimulatory anti-CD40 antibody. The study emphasized the significance of IFN-I in facilitating these combined effects, but c-di-GMP also demonstrated potential in enhancing T cell activity regardless of IFN-I. The results emphasize the possibility of enhancing the immune response against tumors in mice with melanoma by combining STING activation and peptide vaccinations [ ].

## cGAS-STING in enhancing radiotherapy response of melanomas
By influencing the immunological environment of TME, the cGAS-STING pathway improves the efficacy of radiation therapy (RT) in cancer. Cytoplasmic dsDNA activates the cGAS, which synthesizes cGAMP, in response to RT-induced DNA damage. The STING on the endoplasmic reticulum membrane is bound and activated by this second messenger. When STING is activated, it sets off a chain reaction that produces pro-inflammatory cytokines such type I IFNs. Importantly, these type I IFNs activate DCs and other APCs, which improves the presentation of tumor antigens to T cells and triggers a strong immune response against tumors. Furthermore, by activating STING, the recruitment of MDSCs to the TME can be reduced, and the immunosuppressive function can be diminished, both of which contribute to an immune-permissive environment. Local tumor treatment leads to systemic anti-tumor effects, boosting overall tumor control and patient outcomes. This is achieved through a dual action of enhancing antigen presentation and lowering immunosuppression, which amplifies the abscopal effect of radiation therapy [ ]. After radiotherapy, the non-canonical NF-κB signaling in DCs is activated through the STING sensor-dependent DNA-sensing pathway. This activation occurs when tumor cells that have been irradiated trigger the STING pathway. As a result, TBK1 is activated, leading to the phosphorylation of p100. This phosphorylation causes the formation of p52-RelB heterodimers, which then move into the nucleus. This activation exerts a negative regulatory effect on the synthesis of type I IFNs by impeding the binding of RelA to the Ifnb promoter. In studies using melanoma models, blocking this pathway led to a boost in the body’s ability to fight against tumors by enhancing the maturation and activation of DCs. This, in turn, increased the recruitment and effectiveness of CD8 + T lymphocytes, resulting in better treatment outcomes after radiotherapy [ ]. The therapeutic effectiveness of cyclic dinucleotide (CDN) STING agonists in nonimmunogenic cancers, including melanoma, relies on the combined actions of TNFα and radioresistant stromal cells. These two factors play a vital role in promoting immunological responses and inducing tumor necrosis. TNFα, a pro-inflammatory cytokine largely synthesized by cells originating from the bone marrow, is crucial for initiating acute tumor necrosis after delivery of CDN. This procedure entails substantial inflammation and infiltration of immune cells, which are essential for targeting the tumor cells. Additionally, radioresistant stromal cells present in TME have a crucial function in reacting to STING activation. This reaction leads to an increased immune response and improved rejection of the tumor. The interaction between immune cells that produce TNFα and stromal cells that respond to STING signaling generates a strong antitumor environment that is crucial for effectively eliminating these otherwise resistant nonimmunogenic tumors. This discovery is especially intriguing because it emphasizes the significance of TME, not only limited to immune cells, in the effectiveness of immunotherapies. It indicates new possibilities for improving cancer treatment by focusing on both immune and stromal components within the TME [ ]. Carbon ion radiotherapy (CIRT) is highly effective against melanoma, a kind of skin cancer that is usually unresponsive to conventional radiation therapies. CIRT not only induces substantial DNA damage that triggers the cGAS-STING pathway, but also brings about considerable changes in the cellular and molecular composition of TME. It promotes the entry of important immune cells into melanoma tissues, including as NK cells, CD4+, and CD8 + T cells. This is essential because these cells have critical functions in facilitating anti-tumor responses. CIRT also enhances the expression of many immunological checkpoint proteins, such as PD-1, LAG-3, and TIM-3, which play a crucial role in regulating the activity of immune cells. Significantly, CIRT enhances the surface presentation of PD-L1 on cancerous cells more efficiently than X-ray therapies. Targeting the overexpression of PD-L1, a protein employed by tumor cells to avoid immune destruction, can be done combined with PD-L1 inhibitors. When PD-L1 inhibition is paired with CIRT, it enhances the number of effector T cells and NK cells in the tumor, hence strengthening the immune response and significantly slowing down the progression of melanoma compared to other treatments. The combination of directly targeting the tumor and boosting immune response offers a promising approach for treating melanoma [ ].
The inhalable nanoparticle (NP)-cGAMP system shows great potential in improving the effectiveness of radiotherapy for treating lung metastases of melanomas. It works by utilizing molecular and cellular mechanisms to overcome the immunosuppressive TME. When inhaled, the NP-cGAMP, made up of liposomes coated with phosphatidylserine and containing the STING agonist (cGAMP), specifically targets pulmonary APCs like alveolar macrophages and DCs. The presence of phosphatidylserine on the surface of the nanoparticle aids in the effective recognition and phagocytosis by these APCs via their specific receptors for phosphatidylserine (PS). After being taken in by the cell, the acidic conditions inside the endosomes cause the release of cGAMP into the cytosol, avoiding degradation in the lysosomes. Within the cytosol, cGAMP triggers the activation of the STING pathway through its interaction with the STING protein situated on the ER membrane. This process results in the phosphorylation and activation of TBK1 and IRF3, ultimately leading to the generation of type I IFNs and various pro-inflammatory cytokines. The cytokines facilitate the development of a TME that promotes inflammation, leading to the growth and activation of antigen-presenting cells (APCs). This is marked by an upregulation of co-stimulatory molecules (CD86 and MHC-II) and an enhanced ability to present tumor antigens. This process triggers a strong adaptive immune response, resulting in a notable rise in tumor-specific CD8 + CTLs. When radiotherapy is combined with NP-cGAMP inhalation, it has an even greater impact. It triggers ICD in the tumor cells that have been irradiated, causing the release of tumor-associated antigens (TAs). These antigens are then captured and displayed by APCs. The interaction between NP-cGAMP and radiotherapy not only enhances the immune response in the area being irradiated, but also stimulates a broader immune response that can effectively attack metastases in unaffected lungs. Through this combined treatment, there is a significant rise in the infiltration of activated CTLs into both irradiated and non-irradiated tumor sites. This ultimately leads to enhanced tumor control and extended survival. In addition, the remarkable capacity of NP-cGAMP to modify TME and boost the body’s immune response against tumors, all while minimizing any harmful effects, highlights its potential as a valuable complement to radiotherapy in the treatment of metastatic melanomas [ ].
The abscopal effect refers to the phenomenon where localized RT not only targets the irradiated tumor but also triggers systemic immune responses that cause the regression of distant, non-irradiated tumors. This effect is significantly enhanced when combined with immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) therapies like αPD-1, which inhibit the PD-1 protein on T cells, thereby enhancing the immune system’s capacity to combat cancer cells. Incorporating a single low dose of liposomal doxorubicin (Doxil) substantially boosts this abscopal effect. This enhancement is heavily reliant on the presence of mtDNA in tumor cells and the activation of the cGAS/STING pathway. Doxorubicin induces the release of mtDNA into the cytosol of tumor cells, which then activates the cGAS. Activated cGAS produces cGAMP, which binds to the STING protein, initiating a cascade that results in the production of IFN-I. These interferons are crucial for DCs to cross-present tumor antigens and for recruiting and priming CD8 + T cells. Consequently, the tumor-specific CD8 + T cells proliferate and infiltrate both the irradiated and non-irradiated tumors, mediating systemic antitumor immunity. The presence of mtDNA and functional cGAS/STING signaling in tumor cells is essential for this process, as it drives ICD and the associated release of DAMPs, facilitating robust immune responses. Therefore, Doxil enhances the RT/αPD-1-induced abscopal effect by leveraging the mtDNA/cGAS/STING axis to amplify immune-mediated tumor destruction, particularly in melanomas [ ].
ATM, short for Ataxia-Telangiectasia Mutated, is a crucial enzyme that plays a vital role in the cellular response to DNA damage, namely in repairing DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs). The DNA damage response (DDR) pathway relies on this mechanism as a fundamental component and becomes active when double-strand breaks (DSBs) occur. When activated, ATM enzyme adds a phosphate group to several proteins that play a role in DNA repair, cell cycle control, and programmed cell death. These proteins include important ones like p53, H2AX, CHK2, and BRCA1. Phosphorylated proteins have crucial functions in starting cell cycle arrest, aiding DNA repair activities, and activating programmed cell death if repair is not possible. Genetic alterations in the ATM gene result in ataxia-telangiectasia, an uncommon inherited condition characterized by neurodegeneration, weakened immune system, and an increased susceptibility to malignancy. ATM inhibitors are employed in cancer therapy to impede the DNA repair systems of tumor cells, consequently heightening their vulnerability to therapies such as radiation therapy that provoke DNA damage [ ]. ATM inhibition enhances the accumulation of cytosolic dsDNA in radiated B78 melanoma cells, which leads to the activation of STING-dependent type I IFNs expression. This accumulation of dsDNA is a result of DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs) caused by radiation. Typically, the ATM kinase triggers DNA repair mechanisms. However, when it is blocked by AZD0156, these repair processes are disrupted, resulting in a buildup of double-stranded DNA. The cytosolic dsDNA (dsDNA) initiates the activation of the cGAS-STING pathway, which is a crucial detector of cytosolic DNA. This activation subsequently leads to the generation of type I IFNs, namely IFN-β. The generation of IFN-β increases immunological responses, which includes increasing the expression of MHC-I and PD-L1 on tumor cells. This enhances the infiltration and activity of CD8 + T cells inside TME. Therefore, the suppression of ATM enhances the immunogenic effects of radiation by intensifying the STING-mediated type I IFN response, so stimulating a strong antitumor immune response [ ].

## cGAS-STING in virotherapy of melanomas
The cGAS-STING pathway is highly regarded as a potent tool for oncolytic viruses due to its pivotal role in the immune response to viral infections and its capacity to stimulate both intrinsic and adaptive immunity. When cancer cells are infected by oncolytic viruses, the existence of cytosolic DNA triggers the STING pathway, resulting in the generation of type I IFNs and other inflammatory cytokines. This activation not only amplifies the direct viral oncolytic effect by facilitating the destruction of tumor cells, but also triggers a strong anti-tumor immune response, recruiting and activating immune cells such as T cells and NK cells. Moreover, numerous types of cancer display impairments in the STING pathway, rendering them more vulnerable to viral infection and oncolysis. By utilizing STING’s ability to both identify viruses and activate the immune system, oncolytic virotherapy can be enhanced to effectively eliminate tumors and induce durable anti-tumor immune responses, thereby enhancing the efficacy of cancer treatment [ ]. The cGAS-STING pathway is regarded as a vulnerable point of melanoma for oncolytic virus therapies due to its inhibition in melanoma cells, which creates an exploitable weakness for these therapies. The cGAS-STING pathway is essential in the innate immune response since it detects cytosolic DNA and initiates the production of type I IFNs and other cytokines. These molecules aid in the immune system’s ability to identify and eliminate cancer cells. Epigenetic silencing of either the cGAS enzyme or the STING adaptor protein commonly occurs in melanoma, resulting in a diminished immune response. Consequently, melanoma cells that have impaired cGAS-STING signaling are unable to effectively protect themselves against viral infections, rendering them extremely vulnerable to oncolytic viruses such as herpes simplex virus type 1 (HSV-1). Oncolytic viruses have the ability to infect and destroy melanoma cells more effectively, while also stimulating a broader immune response against tumors across the body. The vulnerability of the cGAS-STING pathway presents an opportunity to significantly improve the effectiveness of oncolytic virus therapy. Therefore, evaluating and perhaps repairing this route could lead to better treatment outcomes for melanoma [ ]. Talimogene laherparepvec (T-VEC) is a type of cancer treatment that uses a virus called HSV-1, which has been altered through genetic modification. Its purpose is to specifically invade and eradicate cancerous cells while simultaneously triggering an immunological response against the tumor. T-VEC triggers ICD in melanoma cells that have low levels of STING expression by utilizing its genetically modified HSV-1 structure, which carries the gene for granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF). When T-VEC infects melanoma cells, it stimulates the release of DAMPs, including high mobility group box 1 (HMGB1), adenosine triphosphate (ATP), and ecto-calreticulin. These substances are important markers of ICD. This production of DAMP molecules triggers the immunological response of the host, resulting in the attraction and activation of CD8 + T lymphocytes in TME. Although STING plays a part in facilitating antiviral reactions, its limited presence in specific melanoma cells unexpectedly increases their susceptibility to T-VEC. This is because these cells have weakened antiviral defenses, which enables more efficient viral reproduction and dissemination. The enhanced destruction of cancer cells and the accompanying release of antigens boost the body’s immune response against tumors. This is supported by the presence of CD8 + T cells that specifically target viral and tumor antigens, as well as an increased expression of genes associated with inflammation. Notably, this immune response is observed even in tumors that are resistant to PD-1 inhibition. Therefore, T-VEC efficiently transforms cancers with low STING expression, which are considered “cold,” into tumors that are considered “hot,” exhibiting strong immune activation and regression of the tumor [ ]. Recombinant Herpes Simplex Viruses (HSVs) that produce STING or cGAS effectively fight against melanomas by restoring the STING signaling pathway, which is frequently impaired in cancer cells. These genetically modified viruses initiate the STING pathway, resulting in the generation of interferons and other chemicals that stimulate the immune system. This ultimately improves the immune system’s capacity to identify and eliminate cancer cells. Although these HSVs have decreased replication in cancer cells as a result of the activation of antiviral defenses, they nevertheless possess substantial anti-tumor efficacy in vivo. The primary mechanism by which this treatment inhibits tumor growth is through the stimulation of STING signaling in APCs, rather than by directly destroying cancer cells. The recombinant HSV enhance the process of presenting tumor antigens by APCs, resulting in a strong cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) response against melanoma cells [ ].
Murine cytomegalovirus (MCMV) greatly augments the immune responses against melanoma by predominantly activating the STING pathway within the TME. MCMV infection of the tumor triggers the activation of STING signaling in tumor-associated macrophages by acting as a STING agonist. This stimulus initiates the synthesis of type I IFNs, specifically IFN-α and IFN-β. Type I IFNs are essential in triggering a series of immunological responses by causing the production of proinflammatory cytokines (such as IL-6 and TNF-α) and chemokines (such as CXCL9 and CXCL10) in both infected macrophages and B16-F0 melanoma cells. The chemokines CXCL9 and CXCL10 play a crucial role in attracting CD8 + T lymphocytes to TME. STING-deficient mice exhibit a notable decrease in the synthesis of inflammatory mediators and chemokines due to the absence of STING signaling. This leads to a deficiency in the recruitment of CD8 + T lymphocytes, resulting in the inability to effectively slow down tumor growth. Additionally, the study demonstrates that MCMV infection boosts the ability of macrophages to engulf foreign particles, but this effect is not dependent on the STING pathway. However, the generation of proinflammatory cytokines does rely on the STING pathway. The restoration of tumor development delay in STING-deficient animals injected with MCMV-infected wild-type macrophages demonstrates that the activation of STING inside these macrophages is enough to launch and maintain the immune response against tumors. The anticancer action of MCMV is primarily achieved through a STING-dependent mechanism that alters TME, making it more immunogenic. This alteration enables the efficient clearance of tumors by CD8 + T cells [ ].
iMVA is a variant of the modified vaccinia virus Ankara (MVA) that has been rendered non-replicative through the process of heat or ultraviolet (UV) inactivation. MVA is an attenuated variant of the vaccinia virus first created as a more secure substitute for the smallpox vaccine. The administration of iMVA directly into the tumor activates the body’s immune response against the tumor, leading to a systemic antitumor immune response. This effect is achieved through many ways. When administered directly into the tumor, iMVA is absorbed by cDCs that are present in TME. DCs play a crucial role in activating the cGAS-STING cytosolic DNA-sensing pathway. This pathway is necessary for identifying viral DNA inside cells and triggering the production of IFNs and other proinflammatory cytokines. iMVA elicits markedly elevated amounts of IFNs and proinflammatory chemicals in comparison to live MVA, hence augmenting immune activation. This strong immune response involves the development of DCs and the increase of MHC I molecules on tumor cells, which helps in the display of tumor antigens to T cells. The process of presenting antigens is essential for triggering adaptive immune responses against the tumor. In addition, tumor cells infected with iMVA create elevated quantities of type I IFNs, which promote the recruitment and activation of immune cells within the tumor. The efficacy of iMVA in inducing widespread antitumor immune response relies heavily on the presence of functional STING and Batf3-dependent DCs. STING functions as a crucial adaptor protein in the cytosolic DNA-sensing pathway, which results in the activation of interferon regulatory factors and the generation of type I IFNs. The absence of STING in mice leads to a notable decrease in the effectiveness of iMVA in fighting tumors, underscoring the crucial role of this pathway in facilitating the immune response. DCs that rely on the Batf3 gene, namely those expressing the CD103 and CD8α markers, play a crucial role in delivering tumor antigens to activate CD8 + T cells. These specialized DCs play a crucial role in producing efficient cytotoxic T cell responses against malignancies. The therapeutic advantages of iMVA are greatly reduced in animals lacking Batf3, highlighting the essential role of these DCs in coordinating the immune response against tumors. Intratumoral administration of iMVA not only elicits immune responses against the injected tumor, but also stimulates a systemic anticancer immune response. This systemic effect is evidenced by the elimination of both locally administered and remote, non-administered tumors in animal models. In addition, mice treated with iMVA exhibit long-lasting immunity, demonstrated by their capacity to reject reimplanted tumors at various locations, even several months after the first therapy. The prolonged immunity is ascribed to the development of memory T cells that stay vigilant against the reappearance of tumors. An other noteworthy discovery is the increased effectiveness observed when intratumoral iMVA is combined with systemic immune checkpoint inhibitors, such as anti–CTLA-4, anti–PD-1, or anti–PD-L1 antibodies. These inhibitors hinder the signals that reduce T cell activation, enabling a more potent immune response against tumor cells. When used in conjunction with iMVA, these checkpoint inhibitors produce heightened T cell activation and proliferation, resulting in more robust and enduring antitumor reactions. This combined therapeutic approach has demonstrated enhanced therapeutic results in both tumors that have been injected with medication and tumors that have not been injected, presenting a hopeful method for treating advanced and resistant forms of melanoma [ ].

## Other therapeutics targeting cGAS-STING in melanoma

## Microparticles
PLGA microparticles, composed of poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid), are extensively employed in drug delivery applications owing to their biodegradable and biocompatible nature. These microparticles possess the capability to encapsulate diverse therapeutic agents and regulate their release kinetics. PLGA microparticles engineered for sustained, intermittent release of STING agonists provide a new strategy in melanoma immunotherapy. The microparticles are created using a meticulous microencapsulation process. This process includes dissolving the STING agonist in a PLGA polymer solution, then emulsifying and evaporating the solvent. The microparticles obtained have a spherical shape and have a homogenous size distribution, which guarantees a constant release of the medication. The designed microparticles function by undergoing slow disintegration in the body, which enables the controlled and prolonged release of the enclosed STING agonist. PLGA undergoes degradation, resulting in the formation of lactic and glycolic acid. These acids are naturally metabolized by the body, which reduces any potential worries regarding toxicity. The pulsatile release is accomplished by engineering the microparticles to intermittently release the STING agonist at specific time intervals, thereby sustaining therapeutic drug levels for prolonged durations without the necessity of frequent dosing. The STING pathway is crucial in melanoma immunotherapy as it triggers the innate immune response and boosts the production of type I interferons, which are vital for anti-tumor immunity. These PLGA microparticles deliver the STING agonist in a controlled manner, resulting in a continuous stimulation of the immune system to identify and combat melanoma cells. The release pattern of the microparticles imitates the natural variations in immune signaling, which could potentially improve the effectiveness of the treatment and decrease the likelihood of immune tolerance [ ]. Acetalated dextran (Ace-DEX) microparticles (MPs) greatly improve the transportation and effectiveness of cGAMP as a cancer immunotherapy that targets STING. Among the many types of MPs loaded with PAMP, cGAMP MPs exhibited exceptional anti-tumor effects by substantially decreasing tumor growth and enhancing survival in mouse models. These effects were achieved at dosages that were 100–1000 times lower than the doses required for soluble cGAMP. The intratumoral route of delivery demonstrated the highest efficacy, while other routes also yielded notable advantages. The therapy resulted in an augmentation of tumor-infiltrating NK cells and CD8 + T cells. In a melanoma model, NK cells played a vital role in initiating early anti-tumor responses, while in a triple-negative breast cancer model, both NK cells and CD8 + T cells were essential. These findings indicate that Ace-DEX MPs enhance the transport of cGAMP within cells, resulting in a strong and long-lasting immune response against tumors, while decreasing the risk of damage [ ].

## Microneedles
Dissolvable hyaluronic acid microneedles (HMNs) are an innovative medication delivery method specifically developed to improve the targeted treatment of diseases like melanoma. The microneedles are predominantly made of hyaluronic acid, a biocompatible and biodegradable substance that is widely present in human connective tissues. This characteristic ensures their safety for use in medical applications. The HMNs are created by integrating medicinal chemicals, specifically the chemotherapeutic medicine temozolomide (TMZ) and manganese chloride (MnCl2), into a hyaluronic acid solution. This solution is then poured into a microneedle mold, resulting in the formation of a collection of small, biodegradable needles. When these microneedles are put to the skin, they pierce the outer layer without causing discomfort. This creates tiny channels that allow the encapsulated medications to be released directly and locally into the underlying tissues. HMNs provide substantial benefits compared to conventional systemic chemotherapy in the treatment of melanoma. By utilizing HMNs, the targeted delivery of TMZ guarantees elevated drug concentrations specifically at the tumor location, while simultaneously minimizing systemic exposure and the resulting adverse effects. TMZ causes DNA damage in melanoma cells, resulting in their death and also stimulating an immunological response by facilitating the maturation of DCs through ICD. Mn2 + boosts this therapeutic impact by stimulating the cGAS-STING pathway, a crucial immunological pathway that triggers the production of type I interferons and other immune-stimulatory molecules, thus intensifying the anti-tumor immune response. The concurrent occurrence of these effects not only hinders the expansion of the main tumor but also aids in the prevention of metastasis, offering a holistic approach to treating melanoma. The utilization of dissolvable HMNs in this manner shows great potential in improving the effectiveness of melanoma treatments while reducing negative side effects [ ].

## Injectable hydrogels
BG-Mngel is a hydrogel that is injectable and bioactive. It is created by chemically bonding sodium alginate (SA) with bioactive glass that has been doped with manganese (BG-Mn). This hydrogel is developed to treat malignant melanoma by combining anti-tumor immunotherapy with wound healing after surgery. The presence of manganese ions (Mn2+) in the bioactive glass triggers the activation of the cGAS-STING immunological pathway, resulting in a potent immune response that improves the body’s capacity to identify and eradicate cancer cells. In addition, the presence of Mn2 + in the hydrogel gives it outstanding photothermal characteristics, enabling it to produce gentle hyperthermia when exposed to near-infrared (NIR) light. This, in turn, enhances immune activation and reverses the tumor’s immuno-suppressive microenvironment. When combined with α-PD-1 antibody, BG-Mngel not only enhances tumor growth suppression but also facilitates the development of durable immunological memory, hence decreasing the probability of cancer recurrence. In addition to its ability to combat cancer, BG-Mngel greatly enhances the speed of wound healing by increasing the activity of genes associated with the development of blood vessels and facilitating the regeneration of skin tissue. This makes it a highly effective therapy option for melanoma and aids in the recovery process after surgery [ ].

## Optogenetic engineering
An optogenetic device is a biological instrument that use light to manipulate cells in living tissue, usually neurons, which have been genetically altered to produce light-sensitive ion channels or other light-responsive proteins. This method integrates the fields of genetics and optics to precisely regulate and observe the behavior of individual cells within living organisms. The optogenetic device, LiSmore, utilizes the light-sensitive characteristics of the Arabidopsis protein cryptochrome 2 (CRY2) to accurately regulate the cGAS/STING signaling pathway in melanoma. LiSmore is created by combining CRY2 with the C-terminal tail (CTT) of STING, which has the necessary domains for activating downstream signaling. When exposed to blue light, CRY2 forms oligomers, causing the STING-CTT domains to cluster together. This process imitates the activation of STING that occurs when natural ligands bind to it. This process involves the recruitment of TBK1 and IRF3, resulting in the phosphorylation of these proteins. Phosphorylated IRF3 moves to the nucleus and activates the production of type I interferons and other proinflammatory cytokines. In melanoma, this mechanism improves the maturation of DCs and the presentation of antigens, leading to an increase in the activation and proliferation of CTLs that are specific to tumor antigens. As a result, this mechanism promotes a strong immune response against the tumor. The integration of LiSmore with immune checkpoint blockade therapy leads to a substantial enhancement in the effectiveness of anticancer treatment. This combination demonstrates a precise and controlled activation of the innate immune system, followed by better adaptive immunological responses [ ].

## Bacterial materials
ATP-responsive manganese-based bacterial materials are biohybrid constructions that have been created to boost tumor immunotherapy by activating the cGAS-STING pathway. The materials are composed of E. coli or other bacterial strains that are covered with a manganese-based compound called PDMC (polymer-doped Mn-TCPP), and concealed with a layer of polyethylene glycol (PEG). In the presence of high levels of ATP found in tumors, the PDMC complex undergoes degradation, resulting in the release of divalent manganese ions (Mn2+) and the exposure of bacteria. The process of breakdown causes the bacteria to release extracellular DNA (eDNA). When combined with Mn2+, this eDNA synergistically activates the cGAS-STING pathway. Manganese ions (Mn2+) amplify the responsiveness of cGAS to extracellular DNA (eDNA), resulting in elevated synthesis of the signaling molecule cGAMP. This, in turn, triggers the activation of STING. This stimulus initiates the synthesis of IFN-I and other pro-inflammatory cytokines, which promotes the development of DCs and the activation of effector T cells. The in vivo investigations showed that these biohybrid materials efficiently suppress the growth of subcutaneous melanoma in mice by enhancing the tumor immune microenvironment, demonstrating a viable approach for melanoma immunotherapy [ ]. Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG (LGG) is an extensively studied beneficial bacteria that is frequently employed to enhance gastrointestinal well-being and bolster immune system activity. Within the realm of melanoma treatment, it has been demonstrated that LGG greatly improves the effectiveness of ICB therapies, including anti-PD-1 immunotherapy. The method entails the alteration of the gut microbiota through the influence of LGG, resulting in the creation of a more advantageous immunological milieu. LGG specifically facilitates the growth of advantageous microbial species, such as Lactobacillus murinus and Bacteroides uniformis. These species contribute to the activation of DCs and the recruitment of T cells to the tumor site. LGG further induces the generation of type I interferons in DCs through the cGAS/STING pathway. This process is essential for the activation and cross-priming of CD8 + T lymphocytes, ultimately enhancing their ability to destroy tumor cells. The use of probiotics to boost immune responses has been shown to result in more efficient tumor suppression and increased survival rates in melanoma models when used in combination with ICB treatments [ ].

## Natural products
Resveratrol, a naturally occurring molecule renowned for its anti-inflammatory and antioxidant characteristics, has demonstrated a substantial ability to augment MHC I antigen presentation in melanoma cells. The study showed that resveratrol enhances the expression of MHC-I on the surface of melanoma cell lines (B16-F10 in mice and A375, MeWo in humans) by the analysis of proteins and gene expression. The stimulation of the STING pathway, which is vital in the innate immune response, facilitates this increase. Resveratrol promotes MHC-I expression through both interferon-dependent and interferon-independent pathways, which is quite intriguing. Resveratrol enhances the expression of ISG15, a protein similar to ubiquitin, through STING-dependent pathways that are not reliant on interferon. This, in turn, boosts antigen presentation. Functional experiments verified that melanoma cells treated with resveratrol exhibited enhanced antigen presentation, resulting in heightened T-cell mediated cytotoxicity. The activation of both pathways expands the possible therapeutic uses of resveratrol, especially when used alongside immune checkpoint blockade medicines for melanoma. This offers a promising approach to overcome resistance and enhance patient outcomes [ ]. Icaritin (ICT) is a flavonoid chemical obtained from the traditional Chinese medicinal plant Epimedium brevicornu Maxim. It has significant anti-inflammatory and anti-tumor characteristics. Within the realm of melanoma, ICT improves the inflammatory environment that promotes the spread of cancer cells (pre-metastatic niche or PMN) caused by small particles released by tumor cells (extracellular microparticles or MPs) by influencing the cGAS-STING signaling pathway. Tumor-derived microparticles (MPs) transport DNA and proteins that trigger the cGAS-STING pathway in lung cells of the recipient, leading to inflammation and facilitating the growth of metastatic cancer cells. ICT hinders this process by impeding the addition of phosphate groups and the formation of pairs of STING, as well as its downstream signaling molecules, TBK1 and IRF3. As a result, the production of pro-inflammatory substances like MCP-1, SAA, and S100A8/A9 is decreased. In addition, ICT lowers the levels of PMN indicators such as fibronectin (FN) and LOX, hence inhibiting the formation of a metastatic environment and decreasing the occurrence of lung metastasis in melanoma models. The understanding of this mechanism highlights the promise of ICT as a therapeutic drug that specifically targets TME to impede the progression of cancer [ ].

## Exosome
Exosomes are tiny extracellular vesicles, usually about 40–150 nm in diameter, that are naturally released by different cell types into the surrounding extracellular space. They are created by the inward protrusion of cellular membranes and consist of a wide range of bioactive substances, such as proteins, lipids, and nucleic acids, which represent the type of cell they come from. Exosomes are being investigated as medication carriers in cancer treatment, namely for melanoma, due to their innate capacity for intercellular communication and their compatibility with biological systems. Exosomes can be modified to transport therapeutic substances, such as the STING agonist cGAMP, in order to boost immune reactions against melanoma. The modified exosomes, known as iExoSTINGa, have demonstrated enhanced pharmacokinetic characteristics, resulting in higher absorption by DCs and heightened activation of the STING pathway. This results in a more robust immune response to tumors, which is characterized by a higher presence of CD8 + T cells that are capable of killing cancer cells. This immune response leads to a decrease in tumor growth and may also have impacts on other parts of the body affected by metastasis. Exosomes provide a promising and unique method for delivering specific immunotherapies to treat cancer (Fig.  ) [ ].
 

## Combination of STING agonists with anti-vascular agents
The combination of STING pathway agonists with anti-vascular drugs can be highly effective in treating melanoma. This approach harnesses both the innate and adaptive immune responses, while also directly attacking the tumor vasculature. STING agonists, like cGAMP, stimulate the cGAS-STING pathway, resulting in the synthesis of type I interferons and pro-inflammatory cytokines. This activation amplifies antigen presentation and increases the capabilities of DCs and T lymphocytes, ultimately converting TME from an inactive state to an active and responsive state. In melanoma, there is a higher presence of cytotoxic CD8 + T lymphocytes, which are immune cells that can kill cancer cells. However, many of these T cells show signs of exhaustion, as shown by the presence of the marker PD-1. This suggests that combining immune checkpoint inhibitors with other treatments could potentially lead to better therapeutic outcomes. Simultaneously, the anti-vascular RGD-(KLAKLAK)2 peptide specifically binds to αvβ3 integrin receptors, which are abundantly present on both melanoma cells and tumor endothelial cells. This targeting mechanism enables the peptide to be taken up by cells and then rupture the membranes of mitochondria, resulting in the programmed cell death of endothelial cells and subsequent death of the tumor. The concurrent administration of these two drugs not only eradicates the blood arteries that sustain the tumor, effectively depriving it of vital nutrients, but also amplifies the enlistment and stimulation of natural immune cells, specifically NK cells, which are pivotal in facilitating anti-tumor reactions. The implementation of this multifaceted strategy leads to a synergistic impact that effectively hinders the growth of tumors and enhances the overall effectiveness of therapy against melanoma [ ].

## Clinical aspects of targeting cGAS-STING in Melanoma
MIW815 (ADU-S100) is a man-made cyclic dinucleotide and STING agonist that is specifically created to stimulate the natural immune response by triggering the creation of pro-inflammatory cytokines and type I interferons. This, in turn, boosts the adaptive immune response against malignancies. Spartalizumab (PDR001) is a type of antibody called a humanized IgG4 monoclonal antibody. It works by blocking the PD-1 receptor, which is a protein found on T cells. When this receptor is activated, it reduces the immune response against malignancies. A phase Ib clinical trial was conducted to examine the efficacy of combining MIW815 and spartalizumab in patients with advanced/metastatic solid malignancies, specifically melanoma. Although the combination was well-tolerated and showed a reasonable safety profile, with common side effects like fever, discomfort at the injection site, and diarrhea, its clinical effectiveness was restricted. The trial had an overall response rate of 10.4%, and the median progression-free survival was around 1.9 months. The response rate in melanoma patients who had previously been unresponsive to anti-PD-1 therapy was 7.8%. This indicates that although there were signs of immune activation and tumor regression in the injected lesions, the combined treatment did not lead to significant improvements in outcomes for this group of patients who had received extensive prior treatment. The low effectiveness of intratumoral therapies emphasizes the difficulties in achieving a strong overall antitumor response and underscores the necessity for further refinement of therapeutic approaches [ ].
Manganese (Mn) is a necessary trace element that plays a vital role in several physiological functions, such as immunological function. The activation of the cGAS-STING pathway is crucial for the recognition and response to cytosolic dsDNA in the immune system. Manganese (Mn) amplifies the responsiveness of the cGAS sensor to dsDNA and augments the strength of the interaction between cGAMP and STING. This, in turn, stimulates the generation of type I interferons and cytokines that are crucial for the activation of antitumor immune responses. Studies have demonstrated that Mn enhances the maturation of DCs and macrophages in melanoma models. This improvement in maturation leads to better antigen presentation and activation of CD8 + T cells and NK cells. These results result in a higher influx of immune cells into tumors, increased secretion of IFNγ and TNFα by CD8 + T cells, and a larger population of memory T cells. Through experimental means, the administration of Mn resulted in a reduction of tumor loads and metastasis in several mice models, leading to a significant extension of survival. In addition, the combination of Mn with immune checkpoint inhibitors, such as anti-PD-1 antibodies, showed a synergistic impact. This resulted in a decrease in the necessary amount of antibodies and an enhancement in the overall effectiveness against tumors. A phase 1 clinical trial shown that the administration of Mn priming in conjunction with anti-PD-1 therapy resulted in favorable clinical results in patients with advanced metastatic solid tumors. This was particularly observed in patients who had not shown positive responses to earlier treatments. The medication achieved these outcomes by stimulating the production of type I IFN and causing a controlled release of cytokines. Therefore, Mn plays a crucial role in promoting anticancer immune responses through the cGAS-STING pathway and greatly improves the effectiveness of immunotherapies in melanoma [ ].

## Conclusion
The cGAS-STING pathway has emerged as a crucial component in the immune response against melanoma, significantly influencing tumor progression and therapeutic outcomes. This pathway’s ability to detect cytosolic DNA triggers a cascade of immunological events, leading to the production of type I interferons and pro-inflammatory cytokines that are vital for promoting anti-tumor immunity. While acute activation of cGAS-STING enhances immune cell recruitment and activation, chronic or dysregulated activation can foster an immunosuppressive TME, which may facilitate tumor growth and metastasis.
Our review has highlighted several key mechanisms through which the cGAS-STING pathway impacts melanoma, including its role in modulating immune cell infiltration, epigenetic regulation, and interactions with other cellular pathways. Notably, epigenetic silencing of cGAS and STING genes, due to promoter hypermethylation, impairs immune responses—a condition that can be reversed with DNA methylation inhibitors like 5-aza-2’-deoxycytidine. Additionally, targeting critical upstream regulators such as androgen receptor signaling, and downstream effectors like TG2 and activin-A, has shown promise in reactivating STING signaling and enhancing tumor antigenicityWhile the cGAS-STING pathway is recognized for its critical role in enhancing antitumor immunity, its dual nature presents some controversy within the field. On one hand, STING activation promotes type I interferon production and recruits immune cells to the tumor microenvironment, enhancing the effectiveness of immunotherapies. However, chronic activation of this pathway can foster a pro-inflammatory and immunosuppressive environment, potentially aiding tumor progression and immune evasion. Additionally, there are concerns that certain melanoma cells can epigenetically silence the STING pathway to evade immune detection, complicating the effectiveness of STING-targeted therapies. These opposing viewpoints highlight the need for more research to fully understand the conditions under which STING activation promotes either tumor suppression or progression, which could significantly influence the development of future therapeutic strategies.
The integration of cGAS-STING pathway modulation with existing and novel therapies presents promising avenues for advancing melanoma treatment. STING agonists, particularly in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy, have demonstrated the potential to convert immunologically ‘cold’ tumors into ‘hot’ tumors, improving their susceptibility to immune attacks and enhancing therapeutic efficacy. Emerging strategies, including nanoparticle-based delivery systems and synthetic drugs like MSA-2-Pt and zebularine, further highlight the potential for targeted cGAS-STING activation to amplify systemic anti-tumor responses .
Looking forward, several key areas warrant further investigation to fully capitalize on the therapeutic potential of the cGAS-STING pathway in melanoma. First, comprehensive studies are needed to elucidate the complex regulatory mechanisms governing cGAS-STING pathway activation. A deeper understanding of how molecular and epigenetic factors influence pathway function will be essential for developing targeted therapies.
Second, research should focus on the impact of cGAS-STING modulation on the TME. Exploring how pathway activation affects various immune cell subsets, stromal components, and metabolic factors within the TME will provide insights into optimizing combination therapies that can reprogram the TME from an immunosuppressive to an immunostimulatory state.
Third, advancing the development and clinical translation of novel STING agonists and delivery systems is critical. Innovative formulations and delivery methods that ensure sustained activation of the pathway, while minimizing off-target effects, will be crucial for maximizing therapeutic efficacy. Additionally, exploring combinations with emerging therapies such as CAR-T cells, personalized vaccines, and other immune modulators could further enhance the effectiveness of STING-based treatments EVEN MORE Compared to existing therapies, STING agonists offer potential synergy with immune checkpoint inhibitors and radiation therapy, but their long-term efficacy remains a subject of ongoing research. While checkpoint inhibitors have revolutionized melanoma treatment by blocking inhibitory signals in T cells, STING-based therapies aim to amplify immune activation from the innate immune system. Thus, balancing the timing and intensity of cGAS-STING signaling is crucial for maximizing its therapeutic potential and mitigating adverse effects.
Finally, clinical trials should focus on optimizing dosing regimens, identifying patient populations most likely to benefit from STING-based therapies, and assessing both efficacy and safety over the long term. Translational research efforts should also aim to identify reliable biomarkers that predict response to STING activation and guide personalized treatment strategies.
In summary, the cGAS-STING pathway represents a pivotal nexus in melanoma’s immune landscape, offering multiple therapeutic targets for enhancing anti-tumor immunity. By addressing these future research directions, we can harness the full potential of cGAS-STING signaling to develop more effective therapies, strategically enhance immune responses, and improve patient outcomes in melanoma management.
