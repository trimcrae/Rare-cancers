## Abstract
Cardiac fibrosis remains an unresolved clinical issue in patients with heart diseases. CircRNAs have emerged as potential targets for treatment of heart diseases. Exploring the functional circRNAs in fibroblast activation is one of the ways to develop innovative drugs for the treatment of cardiac fibrosis. This study aimed to screen for fibroblast-related circRNAs in cardiac fibrosis and elucidate their roles and underlying mechanisms. By screening for fibrosis-responsible circular RNAs (circRNAs), we identified a highly conserved circRNA, circular RNA Sterile alpha motif domain containing 4 (circSamd4), that drives cardiac fibrosis. circSamd4 is prominently expressed in cardiac fibroblasts (CFs) and is upregulated in the fibrotic hearts of humans and mice. Fibroblast-specific silencing of circSamd4 reduced cardiac fibroblast activation and alleviates cardiac fibrosis. Conversely, overexpression of circSamd4 in fibroblasts exacerbates cardiac fibrosis and rescues cardiac function. Bioinformatics and functional analyses revealed that circSamd4 regulates the plasminogen activation. Plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 (PAI-1, encoded by Serpine1) is a key effector of plasminogen activation and redox homeostasis and contributes to fibrotic diseases. Here, PAI-1 serves as a leading functional downstream factor of circSamd4 because PAI-1 is highly expressed in cardiac fibroblasts and contributes to circSamd4 functions in regulating fibroblast activation and cardiac fibrosis. Mechanistically, circSamd4 functions as a sponge for miR-1894-3p to trigger Serpine1 expression and subsequent fibroblast activation, and cardiac fibrosis. Therefore, we identified a fibroblast-specific circSamd4-miR-1894-3p-Serpine1 axis driving fibroblast activation and cardiac fibrosis. Adeno-associated virus (AAV)-mediated knockdown of circSamd4 or Serpine1 alleviated cardiac fibrosis and cardiac dysfunction. These findings suggest that circSamd4 and Serpine1 are promising therapeutic targets for inhibiting cardiac fibrosis.
Keywords: Cardiac fibrosis, Fibroblast, CircSamd4, miR-1894-3p, Serpine1

1.

## Introduction
Cardiac fibrosis is a pathological consequence that frequently accompanies various cardiac diseases and is a leading cause of heart failure. However, it remains a formidable challenge in cardiovascular medicine owing to the lack of effective pharmacotherapies [ , , ]. Cardiac fibroblasts (CFs) are central actors, and their excessive activation is the most direct cause of cardiac fibrosis. Multi-omics sequencing data and human heart single-cell sequencing have revealed that cardiac fibroblasts are activated (marked by the expression of Postn, Acta2, Ccl2, and other profibrotic genes) after acute cardiac injury or chronic pressure overload [ , , ]. These active CFs exhibit pronounced upregulation in the production of extracellular matrix (ECM) components (e.g., collagen, fibronectin, and ECM enzymes, such as matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), which initially aid in stabilizing the heart. However, as CFs continue to be abnormally activated, excessive accumulation of cardiac ECM may increase cardiac stiffness and impair heart function; persistent fibrosis ultimately leads to heart failure [ ]. The importance of CFs in cardiac fibrosis makes them promising therapeutic targets for heart failure [ , ]. Thus, elucidating the molecular mechanisms underlying CF activation and fibrosis is essential for developing targeted therapies to prevent cardiac fibrosis and heart failure.
Genomic and transcriptomic analyses have revealed that over 90 % of the human genome is actively transcribed; however, only 1–2 % of the transcripts encode proteins, whereas several transcripts are categorized as noncoding RNAs (ncRNAs) [ , , ]. Circular RNAs (circRNAs) constitute a unique category of endogenous ncRNAs generated by back-splicing from the 3′ to the 5’ end during the RNA back-splicing process. Compared to linear RNA, their covalently closed structure makes them more stable [ , ]. Moreover, circRNAs display tissue- and disease-specific features and can be packaged into exosomes for intercellular transport [ , ]. Therefore, circRNAs are considered ideal biomarkers and promising targets for diagnostic and therapeutic techniques for cardiovascular diseases [ , , ].
CircRNAs are often dysregulated in response to stress conditions, and increasing evidence has demonstrated that circRNAs can modulate critical biological processes in the cardiovascular system, including cell proliferation, differentiation, and death [ , , ]. Several studies have reported the role of circRNAs in cardiac injury and repair and in vascular remodeling [ , , , , ]. However, the knowledge of circRNAs in cardiac fibrosis is limited; thus, the functions of circRNAs in fibroblast activation and cardiac fibrosis remains unclear.
Therefore, the present study was designed to screen for fibroblast-related circRNAs in cardiac fibrosis and to elucidate their roles and underlying mechanisms. We found that circSamd4 was significantly upregulated in human and mouse fibrotic hearts and in activated CFs. We utilized multiple strategies and demonstrated that circSamd4 aggravated fibroblast activation and cardiac fibrosis by activating the coagulation factor Serpine1 in an miR-1894-3p-dependent manner. Thus, we identified a previously unrecognized circSamd4-miR-1894-3p-Serpine1 axis that drives fibroblast activation and cardiac fibrosis and may serve as a therapeutic target.

2.

## Results
2.1.

## Identification of the circRNAs in response to cardiac fibrosis in humans and mice
To elucidate the changes in the expression of circRNAs in cardiac fibrosis, we used the Arraystar Mouse circRNA Array to examine circRNA expression in mouse hearts subjected to sham surgery or thoracic aortic constriction (TAC), followed by bioinformatics analysis ( A and B). Four weeks after TAC, the mice exhibited cardiac hypertrophy, cardiac fibrosis, and the upregulation of fibrosis markers ( A). Notably, in pressure overload-induced cardiac fibrosis, mmu_circSlc8a1, mmu_circMyom2, and mmu_circSamd4 were significantly upregulated ( C). We further analyzed the expression of these three circRNAs in human heart failure, revealing a significant upregulation of hsa_circSAMD4 compared to that in normal hearts ( D). Furthermore, we validated the upregulation of hsa_circSAMD4 in 14 failing hearts of humans ( E). To identify the circRNAs that are highly expressed in CFs under stress conditions, we isolated mouse CFs and cardiomyocytes. We examined the expression levels of mmu_circSlc8a1, mmu_circMyom2, and mmu_circSamd4 in mouse CFs and cardiomyocytes. Using Tcf21 and Fsp1 as marker genes for CFs, and Myh7 for cardiomyocytes [ ], the results revealed that mmu_circSamd4 was primarily expressed in fibroblasts, whereas mmu_circSlc8a1 and mmu_circMyom2 were mainly distributed in Myh7-expressing cardiomyocytes ( F). In addition, FISH results also confirmed that circSamd4 is enriched in cardiac fibroblasts, particularly in activated cardiac fibroblasts ( ).
 To validate the upregulation of circSamd4 in fibroblasts of fibrotic hearts, we isolated CFs from sham and TAC mice and observed that circSamd4 was significantly upregulated in CFs after TAC ( G). In addition, we isolated mouse CFs and activated the fibroblasts with TGF-β1. After treating CFs with TGF-β1 for 24 h, we observed a significant increase in circSamd4 expression, which was coupled with the overexpression of fibrotic markers α-SMA, Postn, and Ccn2 ( H and I). Given that CF activation is a crucial factor in cardiac fibrosis, our study focused on circSamd4, which is highly expressed in CFs and has increased expression in activated CFs.
CircSamd4 is generated by the back-splicing of exon 3 of the Samd4 gene and is 519 nucleotides in length. CircSamd4 has a highly conserved sequence between humans and mice, with a conservation of approximately 93 % conservation ( ). We validated the head-to-tail junction sequence of circSamd4 in CFs via PCR amplification and Sanger sequencing, which was consistent with sequences in the CircBase database (http://www.circbase.org/) ( J). Divergent primers were then designed to amplify circSamd4, and convergent primers were designed to amplify Samd4 mRNA. circSamd4 was detectable only in complementary DNA (cDNA), but not in genomic DNA (gDNA) from CFs, which also supported its circular structure ( K). Furthermore, nuclear-cytoplasmic fractionation assays revealed that circSamd4 is predominantly localized in the cytoplasm of CFs, accounting for approximately 97 % of its distribution ( L).
Collectively, we identified circSamd4 as a conserved and fibroblast-enriched circRNA that is upregulated in human and mouse fibrotic hearts, suggesting that circSamd4 may participate in cardiac fibrosis.

2.2.

## Fibroblast-expressing circSamd4 exacerbates cardiac fibrosis in mice
After documenting the characteristics and expression patterns of circSamd4, we investigated its role in cardiac fibrosis by using an adeno-associated virus 9 (AAV9)-based strategy. This strategy was designed to specifically knock down the expression of circSamd4 in fibroblasts under the control of the Postn promoter [ , , ]. One week post-TAC, we administered AAV9-Postn-sh-Ctrl or AAV9-Postn-sh-circSamd4 via tail vein injection to sham and TAC mice, respectively. Four weeks after TAC, cardiac function was assessed via echocardiography, followed by pathological analysis of the hearts ( A). To investigate the specificity of sh-circSamd4 in CFs, we sorted CFs from mice in each group using flow cytometry and identified the expression of circSamd4 in CFs (CD45−CD31−mEF-SK4+ cells). The RT-qPCR results indicated that shRNA effectively silenced circSamd4 in CFs without altering the expression of circSamd4 in other non-fibroblast cells in the heart. ( B; ; ); Moreover, it did not affect Samd4 expression ( ). We observed that knockdown of circSamd4 partially alleviated TAC-induced cardiac dysfunction with increased ejection fraction (EF) and fractional shortening (FS) ( C and D). Gross morphological examination showed that the hearts in the knockdown of circSamd4 group were significantly smaller, and the heart weight-to-body weight (HW/BW) and heart weight-to-tibia length (HW/TL) ratios were substantially lower in mice treated with AAV9-Postn-sh-circSamd4 than in those treated with AAV9-Postn-sh-Ctrl ( E and F). Additionally, the pathological staining results showed that compared with the AAV9-Postn-sh-Ctrl group, the cross-section of the heart in the AAV9-Postn-sh-circSamd4 group was significantly reduced ( G), and the fibrosis area was reduced ( H). Moreover, the fibroblast-specific knockdown of the circSamd4 in CFs reduced the mRNA expression of the fibrosis markers Ccn2, Postn, and Col1a1 ( I).
 We also investigated the effect of overexpressing circSamd4 on cardiac fibrosis. One week post-TAC, AAV9-Postn-Ctrl or AAV9-Postn-circSamd4 was administered via tail vein injection to sham and TAC mice. Four weeks post-TAC, cardiac function was assessed using echocardiography, followed by pathological analysis of the hearts ( A). The significant upregulation of circSamd4 expression ( B, ), we observed that overexpression of circSamd4 further promoted TAC-induced cardiac hypertrophy and fibrosis. Specifically, mice treated with AAV9-Postn-circSamd4 exhibited further reduced EF and FS ( C and D), increased heart size ( E), and increased HW/BW and HW/TL ratios ( F). Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E), wheat germ agglutinin (WGA), and Masson staining revealed that the fibroblast-specific overexpression of circSamd4 increased the cross-section of the heart and the degree of collagen deposition, with a significant increase in the fibrotic area in the TAC + AAV9-Postn-circSamd4 treatment group ( G and H). Additionally, we observed that overexpression of circSamd4 significantly upregulated the mRNA expression of the fibrotic markers Postn and Col1a1 in fibrotic hearts ( I). Thus, fibroblast-enriched circSamd4 can drive cardiac fibrosis.
 
2.3.

## CircSamd4 promotes the proliferation and activation of cardiac fibroblasts
Fibroblast activation is a key driver of cardiac fibrosis. To explore whether circSamd4 participates in the progression of cardiac fibrosis by affecting the proliferation and activation of CFs, we modulated the expression of circSamd4 in CFs and analyzed its effects on CF proliferation, migration, and activation marker expression.
First, we silenced circSamd4 via small interfering RNA (siRNA) and confirmed the efficiency of its downregulation using RT-qPCR. The results indicated that siRNA effectively silenced circSamd4; however, it did not affect Samd4 expression ( A, ). Effective silencing of circSamd4 mitigated the mRNA expression of the activation markers Ccn2, Postn, and Acta2 in TGF-β1-treated CFs ( B). Immunofluorescence results revealed that the knockdown of circSamd4 decreased the fluorescence intensity of α-SMA ( C). Furthermore, the results of the 5-ethynyl-2′-deoxyuridine (EdU) assay, collagen contraction test, and cell scratch assay revealed that silencing circSamd4 significantly reduced the proliferation, collagen contraction ability, and migration capacity of CFs treated with TGF-β1 ( D‒F).
 To explore whether circSamd4 promotes fibroblast activation, an adenovirus was used to overexpress circSamd4 in CFs. After 24h of infection at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 100, approximately 90 % of CFs displayed GFP staining via fluorescence microscopy, indicating high infection efficiency and significant upregulation of circSamd4 expression in CFs ( G). Overexpression of circSamd4 did not affect the effect on Samd4 expression ( G, ). And the overexpression of circSamd4 further promoted the activation of CFs induced by TGF-β1, manifested as increased mRNA expression of the activation markers Ccn2, Postn, and Acta2 ( H) and significant upregulation of FN1, CTGF, COL1A2, COL3A1, and α-SMA protein levels ( I–J, ).

2.4.

## Serpine1 is a circSamd4 downstream effector activating cardiac fibroblasts
To understand the global impact of circSamd4 on the CFs transcriptome and its target genes during CF activation, we performed RNA-sequencing (RNA-seq) to CFs with/without circSamd4-knockdown. Cluster analysis revealed tight clustering of samples under each experimental condition ( A). We then analyzed the expression trends of these genes under three experimental conditions (si-NC, si-NC + TGF-β1, and si-circSamd4+TGF-β1) and identified nine distinct expression clusters ( B).
 Since circSamd4 is predominantly located in the cytoplasm of CFs, it likely functions via a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) mechanism [ , ]. In this regulatory mode, when circSamd4 expression is inhibited by siRNA, its target mRNA expression is also reduced [ ]. Therefore, among the nine expression clusters shown in B, we focused on genes whose expression trends were consistent with those of circSamd4, specifically clusters #3 and #4. In these clusters, critical markers of CF activation, such as Fn1, Ccn2, and Acta2, showed significantly increased expression following TGF-β1 treatment and were decreased by silencing circSamd4 ( C), reaffirming that silencing circSamd4 significantly in downregulated the gene expression of CF activation markers. We subsequently performed Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment of the differentially expressed genes in clusters #3 and #4. The results revealed that genes regulated by circSamd4 are enriched in key fibrotic hallmarks, such as cell redox homeostasis, cell-substrate adhesion, and cell migration ( D). Notably, the leading pathway regulated by circSamd4 knockdown was the plasminogen activation ( D), which has attracted the attention of the research community owing to its role in organ fibrosis [ , , , , ].
Additionally, we used the miRanda, miRDB, and miRWalk databases to predict the circSamd4-regulated mRNAs and their underlying ceRNA mechanistic axes. Bioinformatic analysis suggested 276 target genes that may be regulated by circSamd4 through a ceRNA mechanism ( E). We then conducted a Venn diagram analysis of these predicted target genes with the 228 genes from RNA-seq clusters #3 and #4, identifying six intersecting genes ( E; ). We hypothesized that circSamd4 might regulate these six target genes through a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) network. Among these, the leading altered gene Serpine1 (encoding plasminogen activator inhibitor 1 (PAI-1)) was particularly interesting ( F) because it is closely related to fibrotic diseases [ , ]. Moreover, Serpine1 presented the most significant differences in expression and fold change. Most importantly, single-cell RNA sequencing revealed the enriched expression of SERPINE1 in fibroblasts of healthy and failed human hearts ( G; ). The role of Serpine1 in fibrosis of various organs has previously been reported [ , , , ], but its roles in cardiac fibrosis remains unclear. First, we analyzed the correlation between SERPINE1 level and fibrotic marker genes in human fibrotic hearts. Spearman's correlation analysis revealed that SERPINE1 expression levels were positively correlated with the mRNA levels of fibrotic markers, including POSTN, CTGF, and FIBRONECTIN ( H).
Furthermore, we verified the relationship between Serpine1 and circSamd4 in the cardiac tissues and CFs. The results revealed that the expression of Serpine1 was increased in fibrotic hearts and reduced by circSamd4 knockdown ( I). In CFs, TGF-β1 stimulation significantly upregulated the mRNA and protein expression of Serpine1. Notably, when circSamd4 was knocked down, the mRNA and protein expression of Serpine1 was downregulated in activated CFs ( J and K). Therefore, we speculate that Serpine1 may be a key effector of circSamd4 in regulating CF activation and cardiac fibrosis.

2.5.

## CircSamd4 regulates Serpine1 by sponging miR-1894-3p in cardiac fibroblasts
According to our analysis above, circSamd4 acts as a molecular sponge for miR-1894-3p to regulate the expression of Serpine1 ( E and F). Bioinformatic predictions revealed 10 binding sites between circSamd4 and miR-1894-3p, indicating a strong sponge-like interaction. However, the role of miR-1894-3p in CF activation and cardiac fibrosis has not been reported. Thus, we explored their roles in CF activation. First, we observed that miR-1894-3p expression was significantly decreased in fibrotic hearts and in activated CFs ( A and B). Next, we investigated the effects of miR-1894-3p on CF activation by transfecting with miR-1894-3p mimics. miR-1894-3p overexpression significantly decreased the expression of fibrotic markers (FN1, POSTN, and CTGF) in TGF-β1-treated CFs ( C and D).
 We further explored whether circSamd4 regulates miR-1894-3p expression. The results showed that the knockdown of circSamd4 increased the expression of miR-1894-3p in CFs ( E). We analyzed the binding sites between circSamd4 and miR-1894-3p based on bioinformatic predictions. Using the predicted binding sites of miR-1894-3p and circSamd4, we constructed wild-type and mutant luciferase reporter gene vectors for circSamd4 with potential binding sites for miR-1894-3p (PCK-circSamd4-WT and PCK-circSamd4-MUT) and transfected them into HEK293T cells ( F). Dual-luciferase reporter assays revealed that overexpression of miR-1894-3p significantly reduced the luciferase activity of PCK-circSamd4-WT, but not that of PCK-circSamd4-MUT ( G). These data suggested that circSamd4 acts as a sponge that negatively regulates miR-1894-3p expression in CFs.
Next, we explored whether miR-1894-3p regulates Serpine1 expression. First, we tested the expression of Serpine1 in CFs transfected with mimics-1894-3p. The results showed that overexpression of miR-1894-3p significantly decreased the mRNA and protein expression levels of Serpine1 in CFs ( H and I). Then, we analyzed the binding sites between miR-1894-3p and the 3′-UTR of Serpine1 based on bioinformatics predictions ( J). Using the psiCHECK-2 vector, we constructed wild-type (WT) and mutant (MUT) luciferase reporter gene vectors for the Serpine1 3′-UTR with potential binding sites for miR-1894-3p (PCK-Serpine1-WT and PCK-Serpine1-MUT). PCK-Serpine1-WT and miR-1894-3p mimics were transfected into HEK293T cells along with PCK-Serpine1-MUT and miR-1894-3p mimics. Dual-luciferase reporter assays indicated that the overexpression of miR-1894-3p significantly reduced the luciferase activity of PCK-Serpine1-WT but not that of PCK-Serpine1-MUT ( K). Moreover, the circRNA pull-down assay demonstrated that circSamd4 directly interacted with miR-1894-3p but with Serpine1 mRNA ( ). Finally, we tested whether circSamd4 regulates Serpine1 expression in an miR-1894-3p-dependent manner in CFs. The results showed that circSamd4 knockdown reduced Serpine1 expression, whereas miR-1894-3p knockdown increased Serpine1 levels. Notably, miR-1894-3p knockdown blocked circSamd4-mediated regulation of Serpine1 expression in CFs ( L). Collectively, these results indicate that circSamd4 promotes Serpine1 expression by acting as a sponge to repress miR-1894-3p expression in CFs ( M).
Furthermore, we validated the conclusion above in vivo. One week post-TAC, we administered AAV9-Postn-Ctrl/miR-1894-3p sponge and AAV9-Postn-sh-circSamd4 to the TAC mice ( A). The expression of miR-1894-3p was significantly inhibited by the AAV9-mediated delivery of its sponge ( B). Compared to the AAV9-Postn-sh-Ctrl group, the Serpine1 and PAI-1 levels were reduced in the AAV9-Postn + sh-circSamd4 group. At the same time, the mRNA and protein levels of Serpine1 (PAI-1) were increased in the AAV9-Postn-miR-1894-3p group, but there was no difference between the TAC + AAV9-Postn-sh-Ctrl + miR-1894-3p sponge group and the TAC + AAV9-Postn-sh-circSamd4+miR-1894-3p sponge group ( C), further suggesting the involvement of miR-1894-3p in circSamd4-mediated upregulation of Serpine1. Consistent with the previous results, we found that knockdown of circSamd4 partially alleviated the TAC-induced cardiac functional damage and cardiac fibrosis, primarily reflected in EF, FS, HW/BW, HW/TL, the cross-sectional area of the heart, the fibrotic area, and the expression of fibrotic genes (Ccn2, Postn, and Col1a1;
 D–J). However, these cardiac function indicators and cardiac fibrosis further deteriorated in the TAC + AAV9-Postn-sh-Ctrl + miR-1894-3p sponge group, and there was no difference between the TAC + AAV9-Postn-miR-1894-3p sponge group and the TAC + AAV9-Postn-sh-circSamd4+miR-1894-3p sponge group ( D–J), suggesting that circSamd4 regulates cardiac fibrosis in an miR-1894-3p-dependent manner. These results demonstrated that circSamd4 regulates Serpine1 and cardiac fibrosis by inhibiting miR-1894-3p.
 
2.6.

## circSamd4-miR-1894-3p-Serpine1 axis promotes fibroblast activation and cardiac fibrosis
Next, a series of phenotypic experiments was performed to validate the role of the circSamd4-miR-1894-3p-Serpine1 pathway in CF activation. The siRNA-mediated knockdown of Serpine1 effectively reduced its expression in CFs ( A and B). Knockdown of Serpine1 significantly decreased the fibrotic marker COL3A1, COL1A2, CTGF and α-SMA protein expression in TGF-β1-activated CFs ( C and D). Notably, either overexpression of miR-1894-3p or knockdown of Serpine1 effectively reduced the CF activation phenotype induced by circSamd4 overexpression, as evidenced by COL3A1, COL1A2, CTGF and α-SMA expression ( E and F), CF migration ( G), and collagen contraction ability ( H) in TGF-β1-activated CFs. Thus, miR-1894-3p and Serpine1 are involved in circSamd4 functions in CFs activation.
 Finally, the role of the circSamd4-Serpine1 pathway in cardiac fibrosis was also validated using a mouse model. One week after TAC surgery, the mice were divided into four treatment groups and received the corresponding injections of AAVs (AAV9-Postn-Ctrl, AAV-Postn-circSamd4, AAV-Postn-sh-Ctrl, and AAV-Postn-sh-Serpine1), followed by echocardiographic examination and pathological analysis 4 weeks after TAC surgery ( A). The efficacy of the AAV9-mediated downregulation of Serpine1 was validated by RT-qPCR ( B). Western blotting data showed that AAV-mediated sh-Serpine1 significantly downregulated PAI-1 expression, whereas circSamd4 upregulated PAI-1 expression. There was no difference in PAI-1 levels between the TAC + AAV9-Postn-Ctrl + sh-Serpine1 group and the TAC + AAV9-Postn-circSamd4+sh-Serpine1 groups ( C).
 As mentioned above, circSamd4 overexpression in fibroblasts aggravated cardiac dysfunction and fibrotic remodeling. Notably, when we knocked down Serpine1, cardiac dysfunction and fibrosis induced by circSamd4 overexpression were also alleviated. Specifically, the echocardiographic parameters EF and FS were improved by fibroblast-specific Serpine1 knockdown ( D and E). Gross morphological examination of the hearts revealed that knockdown of Serpine1 reduced TAC-induced hypertrophic growth ( F), and that the HW/BW ratio was significantly decreased by fibroblast Serpine1 knockdown ( G). H&E and WGA staining showed that knockdown of Serpine1 decreased cardiomyocyte size ( H). Masson staining revealed reduced interstitial collagen deposition in the heart tissue of the Serpine1 knockdown ( I), which was coupled with reduced mRNA expression of the fibrosis markers Ccn2, Postn, and Fibronectin ( J). Notably, when fibroblast Serpine1 was silenced, circSamd4 overexpression did not promote a decline in EF and FS, hypertrophic growth, fibrotic remodeling, and overexpression of fibrotic marker genes in fibrotic hearts ( D–J). In summary, Serpine1 in fibroblasts contributes to circSamd4 functions in cardiac fibrosis.

3.

## Discussion
Cardiac fibrosis remains a notable risk factor for mortality in patients with heart disease. Thus, the molecular mechanisms underlying cardiac fibrosis must be investigated urgently to identify new and promising targets for prevention and treatment. In this study, we identified a highly conserved circRNA, circSamd4, which is prominently expressed in CFs and is upregulated in the fibrotic hearts of humans and mice. Further, loss-of-function and gain-of-function experiments demonstrated that fibroblast-enriched circSamd4 aggravated CF proliferation, activation, and cardiac fibrosis. Mechanistically, circSamd4 acts as a molecular sponge for miR-1894-3p to promote Serpine1 expression and thus activate CFs. Finally, we provided evidence that the fibroblast-enriched circSamd4-Serpine1 axis promotes cardiac fibrosis in mice. These results highlight the role of the novel circSamd4-Serpine1 axis in fibroblast activation and cardiac fibrosis and support circSamd4 as a potential therapeutic target ( ).
 CircRNAs have emerged as essential players in gene regulation and have several applications. In particular, the development of strategies for investigating circRNAs and RNA-based gene therapeutics makes circRNA a hot topic in disease pathology and treatment [ , , ]. CircRNAs also play a key role in cardiovascular diseases by regulating key biological processes in the cardiovascular system, such as cell proliferation, apoptosis, mitochondrial damage, and oxidative stress [ , , , ]. Several studies have reported that abnormal circRNA expression is involved in the occurrence of heart failure [ , , ]. However, previous studies have focused on the roles of circRNAs in regulating cardiomyocytes and immune cells, and few studies have attempted to understand their roles in cardiac fibroblasts.
Through a series of screening processes, we identified the upregulation of the fibroblast-enriched circSamd4 in human and mouse fibrotic hearts. circSamd4 is conserved in humans, rats, and mice (>90 %), suggesting that it plays a critical role in cardiac homeostasis and diseases. Indeed, using AAV9-based loss-of-function and gain-of-function strategies, we demonstrated that fibroblast circSamd4 promotes the development of cardiac fibrosis in preclinical animal models, and targeted inhibition of circSamd4 in fibroblasts could reduce fibrosis and reverse cardiac function in fibrotic hearts. We also provided evidence that circSamd4 directly promotes the activation of CFs. Adenovirus-mediated overexpression of circSamd4 promotes the activation of cardiac fibroblasts, while siRNA-mediated knockdown of circSamd4 alleviated CF activation. Therefore, these results provide new information in the field of cardiac fibrosis by identifying the role of circSamd4 in CFs.
Transcriptome analysis and GO enrichment revealed that circSamd4 mainly regulates the plasminogen activation gene set and identified Serpine1 as a key downstream effector of circSamd4. Serpine1 is a member of the serine protease inhibitor superfamily. The protein, PAI-1, encoded by Serpine1, controls the activity of urokinase/tissue-type plasminogen activator(uPA/tPA)/plasmin/MMP to regulate the proteolytic degradation of ECM proteins, Thereby maintaining tissue homeostasis [ , ]. PAI-1 is a key effector of ROS and is involved in the pathological processes of various fibrotic diseases by regulating fibrinolysis. In our previous research, we found that (H2S)-AMP-activated protein kinase is a sensor of fibrotic stress and serves as a key mechanism upregulating PAI-1 in endothelial cells to participate in lung fibrosis [ ]. Indeed, LPS or oxidative stress caused an enhanced level of PAI-1 [ ], Zhang et al. discovered that Nnattokinase markedly inhibited serum PAI-1 levels induced by LPS, thereby blocking the deposition of fibrin in the glomeruli of endotoxin-treated animals [ ]. Besides, PAI-1 mediates fibroblast-mast cell interactions to drive the development of skin fibrosis [ ]. However, the roles of PAI-1 in fibroblasts and cardiac fibrosis remain elusive. Previous data from human and mouse studies have shown that genetic deficiency of SERPINE1 (PAI-1) is associated with the development of cardiac fibrosis [ , , , ]. However, some studies in mice have shown that the inhibition of PAI-1 alleviates myocardial and vascular fibrosis [ , ]. We speculate that PAI-1 in different types of cardiac cells may have different functions during myocardial fibrosis. Additionally, systemic deficiency and conditional knockout of Serpine1 (PAI-1), as well as knockdown/knockout of Serpine1 (PAI-1) in different cell types, may have different effects on cardiac fibrosis. In the heart, targeting different cells to intervene with the same gene can result in different effects. For example, in cardiomyocytes, overexpression of YAP mediates the inhibition of cardiac fibroblast activation through a non-canonical Wnt pathway, promoting cardiac regeneration after myocardial infarction [ ]. In CFs, sustained activation of YAP leads to cardiac fibroblast activation and heart failure [ ], and fibroblast-specific knockout of YAP improves cardiac remodeling after myocardial infarction [ ]. Our study provided evidence that PAI-1 was predominantly expressed in fibroblasts of the heart tissues and was upregulated in fibrotic hearts. Notably, PAI-1 expression was positively correlated with human fibrotic gene expression in human fibrotic hearts. Our in vitro and in vivo results suggested that PAI-1 promotes fibroblast activation in CFs and cardiac fibrosis in preclinical animal models. We also observed that Serpine1 mRNA levels were regulated by circSamd4 and contributed to the role of circSamd4 in regulating fibroblast activation and cardiac fibrosis. Thus, these findings provided the first line of evidence that PAI-1 promotes fibroblast activation and cardiac fibrosis and explain the mechanisms underlying circSamd4 function in cardiac fibrosis.
In general, the subcellular localization of circRNAs is closely related to their functions. Based on our analysis, circSamd4 is mainly distributed in the cytoplasm, which was also confirmed by previous studies [ , , ]. The subcellular location of circSamd4 makes its possible for binding microRNAs and regulating genes via a ceRNA regulatory mechanism in CFs [ ]. Through RNA-sequencing and bioinformatic analysis, we speculated that the target gene of circSamd4 is Serpine1 and predicted that its ceRNA axis is circSamd4-miR-1894-3p-Serpine1. We discovered that circSamd4 contains binding sites for miR-1894-3p. We mutated the binding site and performed a dual-luciferase reporter assay to confirm the binding of circSamd4 to miR-1894-3p. By mutating the binding site of miR-1894-3p in Serpine1 and performing a dual-luciferase reporter assay, we also confirmed the binding of miR-1894-3p to circSamd4. The gene expression results also showed an expression-regulatory relationship between the three genes. Functional recovery experiments revealed that CF activation exacerbated by overexpression of circSamd4 was eliminated by miR-1894-3p overexpression or Serpine1 knockdown. Finally, the role of the circSamd4-Serpine1 pathway in cardiac fibrosis was validated using a mouse model. In addition to miRNA sponging, circRNAs may act through diverse mechanisms, including regulating host genes and sequestering proteins [ ]. In this study, we focused only on the ceRNA mechanism of circSamd4. Determining other mechanisms that contribute to the functions of circSamd4 in cardiac fibrosis is of interest. The role of miR-1894-3p in the repression of cardiac fibrosis in vivo is important for future studies.
In all in vivo experiments in our study, changes in the fibrotic phenotype always occurred concurrently with or preceded the hypertrophic phenotype. Our data demonstrate that circSamd4 does not affect hypertrophy directly ( ). Thus, we speculate that this may be due to paracrine communication between fibroblasts and cardiomyocytes. Under pathological stimulation, fibroblasts undergo a phenotypic switching into myofibroblasts that secrete a wide range of cytokines, chemokines, and growth factors that directly influence cardiomyocyte physiology. TGF-β, interleukin-6 (IL-6), and fibroblast growth factor-2 (FGF-2) have been shown to exert direct effects on cardiomyocyte hypertrophy and cardiac hypertrophy [ , , ]. Notably,​ our data show that knockdown or overexpression of circSamd4 in vivo significantly modulate the Tgfb1 mRNA levels in cardiac fibroblasts of fibrotic hearts ( ). Therefore, the observed phenotypes lead us to speculate that circSamd4 may promote fibroblast activation and upregulated Tgfb1 that promote cardiac hypertrophy, thereby influencing cardiac fibrosis while accompanying hypertrophic remodeling. Future research can further focus on whether circSamd4 affects communication among various cell types in the heart and participates in myocardial pathological processes.
In summary, our findings have identified the therapeutic value of circSamd4 for the treatment of cardiac fibrosis in humans and preclinical animals. Fibroblast-enriched circSamd4 aggravates cardiac fibrosis by regulating Serpine1 expression in CFs. And AAV-mediated circSamd4 or Serpine1 knockdown alleviated cardiac fibrosis and cardiac dysfunction, offering an alternative clinical strategy for inhibiting cardiac fibrosis. These findings suggest that circSamd4 and Serpine1 are promising therapeutic targets for inhibiting cardiac fibrosis.

## Materials and methods
4.1.

## Animal experiment
All animal procedures conformed to the National Institutes of Health Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals. And all animal experiments were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of the West China Hospital, Sichuan University (ethics number: 20230625005). C57BL/6 mice, aged 6–8 weeks, were acquired from GemPharmatech Co., Ltd. (Nanjing, China). The mice were housed under specific pathogen-free conditions and maintained at constant temperature and humidity with a 12:12-h light/dark circadian cycle. Standard chow and water were freely accessible to the animals at the Animal Experiment Center of the West China Hospital. The cardiac fibrosis model was established through TAC surgery, as described previously [ , ]. Briefly, the mice were anesthetized via isoflurane inhalation and positioned supine on a warming pad for surgery. For isoflurane inhalation, the mice were connected to a ventilator and 4–5 % was used for induction and 2–3 % was used for maintenance of general anesthesia throughout the TAC surgery. After intubation, a small animal ventilator was used for mechanical ventilation to maintain the airway. A transverse incision in the left chest allowed access to the thoracic aorta, located in the second intercostal space. After thoracotomy, the aortic arch was constricted using a 5–0 silk suture looped around a blunt 27-gauge needle, which was subsequently removed prior to chest closure. A parallel procedure was performed in the sham-operated group without aortic ligation.

4.2.

## Echocardiography
Cardiac function was assessed using transthoracic echocardiography, utilizing a Vevo®3100 high-resolution imaging system with a 40 MHz transducer (VisualSonics, Toronto, Canada). To ensure a heart rate (400–600 beats/min) throughout the echocardiography procedure, mice were anesthetized with isoflurane (induction at 3 % and then maintained at 1.5 %) during the image acquisition. The echocardiography protocol followed the procedures outlined in previous studies [ ]. The acquired ultrasonic images were analyzed using the Vevo Lab 3.2.6 software. The echocardiographic measurements were averaged over three cardiac cycles to ensure accuracy.

4.3.

## Histological analysis
Mice were euthanized by cervical dislocation under prior sedation by inhalation of isoflurane (2.5 %). The heart tissues were fixed in 4 % paraformaldehyde for at least 24 h at room temperature. Following dehydration, the tissues were embedded in paraffin and sectioned at a thickness of 4–5 μm for staining. H&E staining (Servicebio, China) was used to assess routine histological changes, and Masson's trichrome staining (Servicebio, China) was used to evaluate collagen deposition in the heart tissues. Additionally, FITC-conjugated WGA (Sigma, 1:500) staining was used to measure the cross-sectional area of the cardiomyocytes.

4.4.

## Fluorescent in situ hybridization [ ]
After fixation with 4 % paraformaldehyde, heart tissue was washed, dehydrated, and embedded,and then hearts were cut into transverse sections with a thickness of 4–5 μm. Cell slides were fixed with 4 % paraformaldehyde and then washed with PBS. An RNA probe against circSamd4 was designed by Servicebio. Briefly, tissue sections or cell slides were deparaffinized and then heated in 90–95 °C pretreatment solution for 10 min, followed by protease incubation at 40 °C for 35 min. The RNA probe against circSamd4 was hybridized at 40 °C for 2–4 h. After the preamplifier hybridization, amplifier hybridization and AP-conjugated anti-RNA probe hybridization steps, probes were visualized by Fast-Red substrate at 40 °C for 40 min. Immunofluorescence assays were performed subsequently to detect cardiac myocytes, endothelial cells and fibroblasts, using antibodies against cardiac Troponin T (cTnT; Servicebio, GB15364-100; 1:100), viementin (Servicebio, GB11192-100, 1:100) and CD31(Servicebio, B11063-2-100, 1:100), respectively. Images were taken with a Leica STELLARIS5 confocal laser-scanning microscope.

4.5.

## CircRNA microarray analysis
To assess the expression of circRNAs in mouse heart tissue, circRNA microarray analysis was conducted using the Arraystar Mouse circRNA Array V2 (Arraystar, USA), facilitated by Aksmics BioTech, Inc. (Aksmics, China). Total RNA was extracted from the heart tissues of five sham-operated and five TAC-operated mice using QIAzol Lysis Reagent (Qiagen, 74904, Germany), and the RNA concentration was determined using a NanoDrop ND-1000 spectrophotometer. The RNA samples were then treated with RNase R to remove linear RNAs and enrich for circular RNAs. Microarray hybridization and subsequent data collection were performed according to the protocols established by ArrayStar. Differentially expressed circRNAs were identified post-quantile normalization of the raw data, using a t-test and p-value adjustment, with significance thresholds set at p < 0.05 and a log2FC ≥ 0.5.

4.6.

## Cell isolation, culture, and treatment
Primary adult mouse CFs were isolated using previously described protocols with minor modifications from protocols previously described [ , ]. Briefly, cardiac ventricles from adult mice were separated and cut into 1 mm3 fragments. Then, these pieces were digested in 0.1 % collagenase type II (Worthington, LS004176, USA) for 30 min at 37 °C, followed by 0.25 % trypsin (Gibco, 25200056, USA) for three 5 min period. Collagenase type II digestion steps were repeated 2–3 times until the tissues were completely digested. The cells were combined, centrifuged, and resuspended in fresh 10 % FBS (Gibco, 10099141C, USA) and DMEM (Gibco, C11995500BT, USA). After 90 min incubation, the non-adherent cells were removed and the attached cells were further incubated at 37 °C in a 5 % CO2 humidified incubator.
Neonatal mouse CFs (NMCFs) and neonatal mouse cardiomyocytes (NMCMs) were isolated from 1-to 3-day-old C57BL/6 mice, as previously described in our reports [ , , ]. Briefly, neonatal mouse hearts were harvested, rinsed with ice-cold phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, BI, 02-024-1ACS, Israel), and minced into 1 mm3 fragments under sterile conditions. These fragments were then digested with 0.25 % trypsin at 4 °C for 12 h on a horizontal shaker set at 20–30 rpm. The following day, the digestion supernatant was collected and combined with an equal volume of 10 % FBS-supplemented DMEM, and the sediment was resuspended in collagenase type II-containing L15 medium (Solarbio, LA9510, China). This mixture was then shaken at 37 °C for 30 min at 20–30 rpm. The resulting supernatant was combined with that obtained during the initial digestion step. This combined suspension was filtered through a 70 μm cell strainer and centrifuged at 400g for 5 min to pellet the cells. The cells were then resuspended in fresh 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS)-DMEM. CFs were separated from CMs using the differential attachment method. Briefly, cells were plated in T25 flasks and incubated at 37 °C in a 5 % CO2 humidified incubator for 30 min, allowing CFs to adhere. Non-adherent cells, predominantly CMs, were collected and seeded in to the wells of the plates.
Cells were passaged when the confluence of (CFs) confluence exceeded 80 %. CFs were grouped and treated differently for the second passage as their confluence approached 80 %. Before treatment, the CFs were serum-starved in serum-free DMEM for 4 h. Subsequently, they were treated with 10 ng/mL TGF-β1 (Sino Biological Inc. 80116-RNAH, China) for 24 h to induce activation. For siRNA transfection, CFs were transfected either with siRNA-circSamd4 (si-circSamd4) or its negative control (si-NC), and with siRNA-Serpine1 (si-Serpine1) or its negative control (si-NC) (RiboBio, China), using Lipofectamine™ RNAiMAX (Invitrogen, 13778–150, USA) for 24 h. The transfection concentration was set to 50 nM. For adenoviral infection, CFs were infected with either circSamd4-overexpressed adenovirus (Ad-circSamd4) or its negative control (Ad-Ctrl) (HANBIO, China) for 24 h, followed by replacement with fresh culture medium. The MOI was set at 100 for all the adenoviruses. The siRNA sequences are listed in .
Neonatal mouse cardiomyocytes were cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10 % FBS and 1 % penicillin-streptomycin supplement under 5 % CO2. For inducing cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, the cells were cultured in DMEM with 1 % FBS overnight and followed by 100 μM phenylephrine (PE, Sigma, P6126, USA) for 48 h. The procedures for transfecting siRNA and infecting adenovirus are consistent with those used for cardiac fibroblasts.

4.7.

## Flow cytometry sorting
At the end of the AAV infection experiment, the mouse heart tissue was prepared as a single-cell suspension using the aforementioned cardiac fibroblast isolation method, and blood cells were lysed. Mouse Fc block (553141, BD Pharmingen, USA) was added to the cells to reduce non-specific staining. The cells were then incubated in the dark at room temperature for 30 min, followed by the addition of membrane molecule antibodies (CD31-FITC, 558738; CD45-BV421, 563890; and MefSK4-APC, 130-120-802, BD Pharmingen, USA) at appropriate dilution ratios. The cells were washed with PBS and filtered through a 70 μm cell strainer, and sorted using Beckman's multicolor flow cytometer. Cells from the mouse cardiac fibroblast population (CD45−CF31−MefSK4+) were collected for RT-qPCR analysis [ ]. Data were collected on a multicolor flow cytometer (BD FACSAria, USA) and analyzed using the FlowJo software.

4.8.

## Dual-luciferase reporter assays
Dual-luciferase reporter assays were performed as described in previous literature [ , ]. First, the bioinformatic analysis software tools, including RNAhybrid and miRanda software, were used to analyze the binding sites between circSamd4 and miR-1894-3p and between miR-1894-3p and the 3′ UTR of Serpine1. A circSamd4 fragment containing WT or mutated (Mut) miR-1894-3p binding sites was amplified and cloned into the psiCHECK-2 vector at the 3′ end of the Renilla gene using primers containing NotI or XhoI sites and T4 DNA ligase (psiCHECK2-circSamd4-WT/Mut) (TsingKe Biotech, China). And partial 3′UTR fragments of the Serpine1 coding region containing the indicated miR-1894-3p binding sites, or Mut sites were cloned into the psiCHECK2 dual luciferase reporter plasmid (psiCHECK2-Serpine1-WT/Mut) (TsingKe Biotech, China). HEK293T cells were seeded in 24-well plates and cultured until they reached 80 % confluence. Cells were co-transfected with the miR-1894-3p mimic or its negative control (mimic-NC) and a luciferase reporter plasmid (psiCHECK2-circSamd4-WT/MUT or psiCHECK2-Serpine1-WT/MUT) using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, L3000-008, USA) for 24 h. The final concentration of miR-1894-3p mimics and mimics-NC was 50 nM, and the plasmid concentration was 1 μg/mL. After-transfection, dual-luciferase activity was measured using a Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay Kit (Vazyme, DL101-01, China) following the manufacturer's instructions.

4.9.

## RNA pull-down assay [ , ]
An RNA pull-down assay using MS2-capturing protein (MS2-CP) was performed to identify the miRNAs or mRNAs that interacting with circSamd4. Firstly, two overexpression vectors were constructed, including one carrying MS2-circSamd4 and the other carrying MS2-CP-Flag (Geneseed, China). Next, these two vectors were co-transfected into CHO cells to induce MS2-circSamd4 and MS2-CP expression. After specific binding between MS2-labelled circSamd4 and MS2-CP, an anti-Flag antibody was used to pull down the MS2- CP-MS2-circSamd4 complex and RT-qPCR or western blotting assays were performed to identify the captured products.

4.10.

## RNA isolation and real-time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR)
Total RNA was extracted from the tissues and cells using an RNA Extraction Kit (Accurate Biotechnology, AG21024, China) according to the manufacturer's instructions. RNA was extracted from CFs using the PARIS Kit (Invitrogen, AM1921, USA). Reverse transcription was performed to synthesize the cDNA using a Reverse Transcription Kit (TOYOBO, FSQ101, Japan). Gene expression was detected by qRT-PCR using the SYBR Green Master Mix (Vazyme, Q711-03, China) and a CFX96 detection system (Bio-Rad, USA). Relative gene expression was analyzed by the 2ΔΔCt method, and U6 and Gapdh were used as the nuclear and cytoplasmic endogenous controls, respectively. Primer sequences are listed in .

4.11.

## Protein isolation and western blotting
Protein isolation was performed using RIPA solution (Beyotime, P0013B, China), and a BCA Kit (Beyotime, P0010, China) was used to quantify the protein concentrations. The protein samples were denatured at 95 °C for 10 min. Equal quantiles of protein samples per line were separated using 10 % sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) gel and transferred onto polyvinylidene difluoride membranes (Millipore, IPVH00010, USA). Then, the membranes were blocked with 5 % bovine serum albumin (BSA, Biofroxx, 4240GR250, China) in Tris-buffered saline with 1 % Tween (Biofroxx, 1247ML100, China)-20 buffer (TBST) at 37 °C for 1h at room temperature. After washing with TBST three times, the membranes were incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C with gentle shaking. The next day, after washing thrice with TBST, the membranes were incubated with the appropriate secondary antibodies at room temperature for 2 h. Finally, the immunoblots were visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) kit (Bio-Rad, 170–5060, USA) and analyzed using ImageJ software. Detailed information regarding the antibodies used is provided in .

4.12.

## Adeno-associated virus (AAV) construction and infection
AAV9 vectors carrying a periostin (Postn) promoter driving the expression of shRNA targeting circSamd4 (AAV9-Postn-sh-circSamd4) and its negative control AAV (AAV9-Postn-sh-Ctrl) were designed and constructed by Genechem Co., Ltd. (Genechem, China). AAV9 carrying a Postn promoter induced the overexpression of circSamd4 (AAV9-Postn-circSamd4), and its negative control AAV (AAV9-Postn-Ctrl) was designed and constructed by Hanbio Biotechnology (HANBIO, China). AAV9 carrying a Postn promoter that drives the expression of shRNA targeting Serpine1 (AAV9-sh-Serpine1) and its negative control AAV (AAV9-Postn-sh-Ctrl) were designed and constructed by Hanbio Biotechnology Co., Ltd (HANBIO, China). The sequences of sh-circSamd4 and sh-Serpine1 were 5′-TTCTCCGAACGTGTCACGT-5′ and 5′-GCTATGGGATTCAAAGTCAAT-3′, respectively. AAV9-Postn-miR-1894-3p sponge was constructed by Hanbio Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (HANBIO, China). The sequences of the AAV9-Postn-miR-1894-3p sponge were“CTCCCTTCACGAGTCCCTTGCTACCCTTGCACATCCTCC.
CTTCACGAGTCCCTTGCTCTTCACTCCCTTCACGAGTCCCTTGC”. According to their group, these AAV (5 × 1011 v. g.) were injected into the tail vein of mice 7 d post-surgery.

4.13.

## Immunofluorescence assay
The cardiac fibroblasts/cardiomyocytes were seeded in 24-well plates or confocal Petri dish and cultured to approximately 70 % confluence. After treatment, the cells were fixed for 30 min in 4 % paraformaldehyde and subsequently permeabilized with 0.5 % Triton X-100 (Biofroxx, 1139ML100, China) for 10 min at room temperature. After washing three times with PBS, cells were incubated with α-SMA (ABclonal, A17910, China)/α-actinin (Sigma, A7811, USA) primary antibody overnight at 4 °C. The next day, after three washes, the cells were incubated with Alexa Fluor 488 or Alexa Fluor 594 secondary antibodies for 2 h at room temperature, and the nuclei were counterstained with DAPI (Sigma, D9542, USA) for 10min at room temperature. Fluorescence images were captured using an IX83 fluorescence microscope (Olympus, Japan) or Leica stellaris 5 confocal laser scanning microscope (Leica, Germany). The fluorescence intensity was quantified using ImageJ software. Detailed information regarding the antibodies used is provided in .

4.14.

## EdU incorporation assay
EdU incorporation into the newly synthesized DNA can be used to detect proliferating cells [ ]. To assess the CF proliferation, we used the EdU incorporation assay using the Cell-LightTM EdU Apollo® 567 In Vitro Imaging Kit (RiboBio, C10310-1, China) according to the manufacturer's manual. Briefly, the CFs were plated in 24-well plates. At the end of the treatment, cells were added to a final-concentration of 50 μM EdU in each well for 2 h in a CO2 incubator at 37 °C. The cells were fixed in 4 % paraformaldehyde and permeabilized with 0.5 % Triton X-100. The cells were then incubated with Apollo staining solution for 30 min and counterstained with Hoechst solution for 30 min at room temperature. Finally, the fluorescence was visualized using a fluorescence microscope (Olympus, Japan). ImageJ software was used to calculate the ratio of EdU-positive cells to the total number of Hoechst-stained cells.

4.15.

## Collagen gel contraction assay
The CFs were embedded in a complex of rat-tail type I collagen (Solarbio, C8062, China) gel and culture medium, and the contractile capacity of the CFs in the gel was assessed by measuring the gel area. The collagen gel contraction assay was performed as previously described [ ]. ImageJ software was used to calculate the gel area.

4.16.

## Scratch assay
A scratch assay was performed to determine the migration ability of CFs in vitro. Briefly, CFs were seeded in 6-well plates and cultured to 80 % confluence. After 24 h of treatment with siRNA or adenovirus, a scratch wound across the well was made using a 200 μL pipette, and the cellular debris and floating cells were removed with PBS. Next, the culture medium was replaced with a low-serum culture medium, and TGF-β1 was added to the medium. Images of wound healing were captured at the time of scratching and at the end of treatment. The ImageJ software was used to quantify the scratch areas.

4.17.

## RNA-sequencing (RNA-seq)
To analyze the global effect of knockdown circSamd4 on gene expression patterns of CFs, we performed RNA-seq on three groups of CFs (si-NC, si-NC + TGF-β1, and si-circSamd4+TGF-β1). Total RNA was extracted from CFs using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA). Sequencing libraries were generated using the NEBNext® Ultra™ RNA Library Prep Kit (NEB, USA). Libraries were sequenced using the Illumina's NovaSeq6000 platform. Raw RNA-seq reads were trimmed using TrimGalore (https://github.com/FelixKrueger/TrimGalore). Clean reads were mapped to the GRCm38/mm10 mouse genome using the HISAT2 alignment tool [ ]. Genes were annotated using an ensemble database. The expression of each gene was quantified using Cuffdiff [ ].

4.18.

## Bioinformatic analysis
Differentially expressed circRNAs and mRNA were identified using DESeq2 [ ]. GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were performed using ClusterProfiler [ , ]. We used the Mfuzz package in R for trend analysis to define the gene expression dynamics after circSamd4 knockdown. Based on the ceRNA theory [ ], the interactions between circRNAs and miRNAs were predicted using RNAhybrid and miRanda, and miRNA-mRNA interactions were predicted using TargetScan, miRDB, miRTarBase and miRWalk databases. CircRNA-miRNA-mRNA interactions were visualized using the Cytoscape software. Spearman's correlation analysis was performed to determine the correlation between SERPINE1 expression and the expression of human cardiac fibrosis marker genes.
The landscape of circRNA expression in the human heart was obtained from Roger S–Y Foo's Lab [ ]. Single-cell data of Serpine1 were obtained from published human heart failure single-cell data (https://singlecell.broadinstitute.org/single_cell/study/SCP1849/) [ ]. Bulk RNA-seq of human myocardial tissue samples was obtained from the public data (GSE141910) [ ]. The raw sequencing reads shown in have been deposited in the NCBI Sequence Read Archive under BioProject accession number PRJNA1288693.

## Statistical analysis
Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation. The normality of the data was validated via the Shapiro–Wilk test. Data from two groups were analyzed using an unpaired two-tailed Student's t-test. For the analysis of more than two groups, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Bonferroni post hoc tests was used. Otherwise, we used the nonparametric Mann-Whitney test to compare the two groups and the Kruskal–Wallis test followed by Dunn's post hoc test to correct for multiple comparisons. GraphPad Prism (version 8.0) software was used for the statistical analyses. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.
