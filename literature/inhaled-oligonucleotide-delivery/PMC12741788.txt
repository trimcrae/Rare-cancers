## ABSTRACT
The mouse model of oxygen‐induced retinopathy (OIR) recapitulates key pathological features observed in human retinal neovascular diseases, including retinopathy of prematurity (ROP). Recent research has highlighted the involvement of long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) in retinal neovascular diseases, but their specific roles remain unclear. Using human retinal microvascular endothelial cells (ECs) and OIR mice, we found upregulation of lncRNA DANCR in ROP models. DANCR overexpression enhanced EC proliferation, migration, and angiogenesis, while suppressing apoptosis via autophagy activation. RNA pull‐down and RNA immunoprecipitation assays confirmed that DANCR directly bound to Annexin A2 (ANXA2). Critically, ANXA2 knockdown partially reversed the DANCR‐driven pro‐angiogenic effects by upregulating the p38 MAPK/mTOR pathway. Intravitreal RNA interference‐DANCR reduced ANXA2 expression, attenuated the severity of retinal vascular and glial cell pathology, and improved visual function. Collectively, lncRNA DANCR exacerbated retinal neovascularization by activating autophagy via the ANXA2/p38 MAPK/mTOR axis, identifying this pathway as a novel therapeutic target for ROP.

This study reveals that the long non‐coding RNA DANCR drives pathological retinal neovascularization in oxygen‐induced retinopathy via an ANXA2‐dependent p38/MAPK/mTOR autophagy pathway. In hypoxic endothelial cells and a murine OIR model, DANCR promotes angiogenesis. Mechanistically, it interacts with ANXA2, inhibits phosphorylation of p38 and mTOR, activates autophagy, and thereby promotes angiogenic processes. Knockdown of DANCR, particularly in combination with aflibercept, attenuated neovascularization, underscoring its potential as a therapeutic target for ischemic retinopathies.

3.2

## 
DANCR Upregulation Inhibited EC Apoptosis Through Autophagy Activation
To elucidate the mechanism of DANCR under hypoxia, the potential influence of DANCR on autophagy was initially assessed by detecting the expression of the ratio of LC3B II to LC3B I (ΔLC3B) and P62, two established autophagy markers. The results of western blot analysis revealed elevated autophagy levels (upregulated ΔLC3B and downregulated P62 expression) in ECs subjected to hypoxia compared to those exposed to normoxia. Furthermore, these autophagy levels were significantly augmented in hypoxia+oeDANCR ECs compared to hypoxia+oeNC ECs (Figure  ).
 Subsequently, the potential protective role of DANCR‐mediated autophagy against EC injury was explored. As depicted in Figure  , DANCR overexpression significantly increased cell viability by CCK8 analysis (Figure  ). Additionally, TUNEL detection of cell apoptosis suggested a significant reduction in the TUNEL‐positive cells and a significant increase in cell density in the hypoxia+oeDANCR group compared to the hypoxia+oeNC group (Figure  ). DANCR overexpression reduced levels of apoptosis markers of BAX and cleaved Caspase‐3 by western blot analysis (Figure  ), whereas inhibition of cell autophagy using an autophagy inhibitor (3‐MA) significantly reversed the pro‐survival impact of DANCR overexpression in ECs (Figure  ).
Altogether, these data revealed that upregulation of DANCR potentially inhibited ECs' apoptosis through autophagy activation.

## 
DANCR Bound ANXA2 to Mediate Hypoxia‐Induced EC Migration and Angiogenesis
LncRNAs can bind to proteins to perform various biological roles, thus acting as a guide, signal, scaffold, and bait. To explore potential binding partners for DANCR, bioinformatics prediction tools such as lncBOOK 2.0 (https://ngdc.cncb.ac.cn/lncbook/omics/interaction) and POSTAR3 (http://111.198.139.65/) were first utilized, identifying potential binding partners for DANCR and revealing a possible interaction with ANXA2. To identify potential interacting proteins, RNA pull‐down assays were conducted in conjunction with western blotting, which indicated that ANXA2 was selectively isolated in the biotin‐labeled sense group in both HUVECs and ECs (Figure  ). Moreover, to confirm this interaction further, RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assays also revealed that ANXA2 enriched DANCR in HUVECs and ECs (Figure  ).
 Transwell cell migration and capillary‐like tube formation assays were conducted to investigate further the influence of the DANCR/ANXA2 axis on ECs' biological functions. The knockdown efficiency of the shANXA2 lentiviral vector was validated through RT‐qPCR (Figure  ) and western blotting assays (Figure  ). Figure  illustrated that hypoxia stimulation improved cell migration ability compared to normoxia. Under hypoxia, oeDANCR treatment enhanced cell migration ability relative to oeNC ones, whereas ANXA2 silence significantly reversed the migration ability of DANCR‐induced ECs. Simultaneously, the results of the tube‐formation assay in hypoxia‐exposed ECs also revealed that tube length and branch points were increased by DANCR overexpression but decreased by further ANXA2 knocking down (Figure  ).
These findings indicated that ANXA2 was a direct interacting protein of DANCR and could suppress the migration ability and angiogenesis of ECs promoted by DANCR overexpression.

## 
DANCR Promoted Angiogenesis via ANXA2‐Mediated Autophagy Activation Through the p38 MAPK/mTOR Pathway in ECs

Furthermore, the mechanism by which DANCR regulates neovascularization through ANXA2 was explored. Western blot results informed that ANXA2 knockdown significantly abrogated the autophagy activation of oeDANCR ECs, as evidenced by downregulation of ΔLC3B and upregulation of P62 (Figure  ). Additionally, treatment with 3‐MA, an autophagy inhibitor, did not reduce ANXA2 expression (Figure  ), indicating that DANCR activated autophagy in ECs under hypoxic conditions by modulating ANXA2, rather than regulating the expression of ANXA2 through the activation of autophagy. Subsequently, the involvement of ANXA2 in regulating autophagy effects on ECs' angiogenesis was assessed. Under hypoxic conditions, ELISA analysis demonstrated that overexpression of ANXA2 (oeANXA2) increased VEGF secretion in ECs, whereas treatment with 3‐MA inhibited VEGF expression (Figure  ). Moreover, the tube‐formation assay in hypoxia‐exposed ECs also demonstrated that branch points and tube length were increased by ANXA2 overexpression but decreased by further addition of 3‐MA (Figure  ). In brief, these results validated that activation of autophagy was involved in the pro‐angiogenic effects induced by ANXA2 overexpression and hypoxia.
 The MAPK/mTOR pathway was broadly acknowledged as a key regulator of autophagy [ ]. Our western blot analysis revealed that, relative to the shNC group, the phosphorylation levels of p38 and mTOR were elevated in ECs of the shANXA2 group (Figure  ). Then, SB203580, a specific inhibitor of p38 MAPK, was employed to elucidate the role of p38 further. Given that SB203580 inhibits the catalytic activity of p38 MAPK without affecting its phosphorylation by upstream kinases, the impact of SB203580 on the p38 MAPK pathway was evaluated by measuring the expression of HSP27, which serves as a substrate [ ]. As depicted in Figure  , SB203580 inhibited the expression of p‐HSP27/HSP27 but did not affect the expression of ANXA2, indicating that p38 MAPK is a downstream molecule of ANXA2. Additionally, inhibition of p38 MAPK resulted in the activation of autophagy in ECs, as evidenced by the upregulation of Beclin‐1 (an essential component of the autophagy initiation complex) [ ] and the downregulation of P62, indicating that ANXA2 activates autophagy through p38 (Figure  ). In conjunction with the previous findings, these results demonstrated that regulating the p38 MAPK/mTOR pathway is closely associated with autophagy inhibition upon ANXA2 knockdown. Consequently, these data revealed that DANCR interacting with ANXA2 activated autophagy via targeting p38 MAPK/mTOR axis to promote the ability of tube‐formation in ECs (Figure  ).

## Silence of DANCR Reduced the Degree of Retinal Lesions in the Oxygen‐Induced Retinopathy (OIR) Mice
To further explore the endothelial DANCR contribution to retinal pathological neovascularization, the vascular response to retinal hyperoxia‐hypoxia was investigated utilizing the OIR mouse model. We first established the temporal expression profile of Dancr in vivo, which paralleled our earlier cellular findings. Compared to P7, Dancr expression in mouse retinal microvascular endothelial cells (mRMECs) showed no significant difference at P12 but was markedly elevated at P17 (Figure  ). Aflibercept, a recombinant VEGF receptor fusion protein, served as the positive control in this study. Our findings revealed significantly elevated gene expressions of DANCR and ANXA2 in the OIR group relative to the control group. Conversely, the OIR + DANCR‐silencing RNA (OIR + siDANCR) group showed a marked reduction in gene levels compared to the OIR + negative control‐silencing RNA (OIR + siNC) group. Meanwhile, gene expression of DANCR and ANXA2 was not affected by aflibercept treatment (Figure  ). The OIR group exhibited significantly higher expression levels of ANXA2 compared to the normoxic (control) group, consistent with previous findings [ ]. Conversely, siDANCR treatment reduced ANXA2 expression (Figure  ). Similarly, ANXA2 expression was not altered in the aflibercept‐treated (Afl) group.
 The OIR group had a higher retinal vaso‐obliteration ratio and disorganized and extensive neovascular plexus than controls. The OIR + siDANCR group demonstrated a notable decrease in non‐perfusion areas and neovascularization compared to the OIR + siNC group (Figure  ; Figure  ). Meanwhile, H&E staining revealed a significant increase in tuft numbers in both the OIR and OIR + siNC groups, while knockdown of DANCR notably reduced the count of neovascular nuclei (Figure  ). The effects of siDANCR on vasculature were comparable to those observed with aflibercept. Of interest, eyes that received both siDANCR and aflibercept injections exhibited superior results compared to those treated with either siDANCR or aflibercept alone (Figure  ).
Retinal glial cells comprise microglia, astrocytes, and Müller cells. The interplay between pathological retinal angiogenesis and the activation or dysfunction of retinal glial cells significantly influences the progression of OIR. In reaction to oxygen‐induced injury, nonactivated, ramified morphologies of microglia/macrophages, both in the neovascularized and avascular areas, exhibited retraction of cellular processes. In contrast, treatment with siDANCR partially restored dendritic morphologies of microglia/macrophages (Figure  ). Next, the effects of DANCR on retinal macroglial cells following oxygen‐induced injury were further explored. As shown in Figure  , the OIR group exhibited increased intensity of glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP) and glutamine synthetase (GS), markers for astrocytes and Müller cells, respectively, in the retina, which was reversed by siDANCR treatment. While siDANCR treatment demonstrated a partial inhibition of oxygen‐induced activation or damage to glial cells, aflibercept had no significant impact on these processes. Furthermore, the combined treatment of siDANCR and aflibercept did not have an enhanced protective effect on glial cells.
 ERG analysis was further employed to evaluate the influence of DANCR on retinal functional characteristics (Figure  ). OIR mice exhibited a notable decrease in scotopic a‐ and b‐wave amplitudes of scotopic 3.0 compared to normal controls. Conversely, compared to the negative control, DANCR silencing and aflibercept significantly enhanced both a‐ and b‐wave amplitudes, and the combined siDANCR and aflibercept treatment showed superior efficacy compared to treatment with siDANCR or aflibercept alone (Figure  ). The peak time in the OIR group was prolonged, while no significant differences were observed in the other groups (Figure  ). These findings indicated that DANCR knockdown combined with aflibercept may offer a promising therapeutic strategy for ROP.

## Discussion
The evolutionary trajectory of perinatal medicine and neonatal intensive care has paradoxically increased the population at risk for ROP through improved birth and survival rates among extremely preterm infants [ , ]. This clinical advancement underscores the critical need to elucidate molecular regulators of retinal neovascularization, a hallmark pathological feature driving ROP progression. Emerging evidence positions long non‐coding RNAs (lncRNAs) as key modulators in ocular angiogenesis by orchestrating downstream signaling cascades [ , ]. This study is the first to associate DANCR with RNV and ROP.
Consistent with prior studies linking lncRNAs to retinal vascular disorders [ , ], our data identified DANCR as a hypoxia‐responsive regulator of endothelial cell behavior. While the precise mechanism of hypoxic induction of DANCR requires further investigation, we speculated that hypoxia‐responsive factors such as hypoxia‐inducible factor‐1α (HIF‐1α) or activator protein 1 (AP‐1) might directly regulate DANCR transcription, given its rapid upregulation under hypoxic conditions [ , ]. DANCR not only influenced ROP by regulating the proliferation and angiogenesis of ECs but also exacerbated pathological angiogenesis by regulating autophagy and apoptosis mechanisms. To elucidate the molecular mechanism, we employed bioinformatics tools (lncBOOK 2.0 and POSTAR3) to identify potential protein partners of DANCR. From the list of candidates, ANXA2 was prioritized for experimental validation based on a compelling convergence of evidence. Its well‐established functional role in pathological angiogenesis and vascular permeability, particularly in retinal diseases [ , ], its high prediction score for interaction with DANCR, and our preliminary experimental data confirming a robust interaction, guided this strategic focus. This approach ensured our investigation was targeted toward a binding partner with predefined biological relevance to the pathological context of ROP. Notably, unlike previous reports on DANCR's role in cancer [ ], this study uncovered its unique interaction with ANXA2 in ECs, revealing a previously unrecognized RNA‐protein partnership driving angiogenesis, where DANCR might function as a scaffold or a decoy for ANXA2 in the pathological process of ROP, a mechanism not previously described in ROP. The direct interaction between DANCR and ANXA2 could influence ECs' migration and angiogenesis by regulating the stability or function of ANXA2. This mechanism could involve the localization of ANXA2 on the cell membrane or its role in intracellular signaling pathways. Nevertheless, the specific sequence and domain involved in the interaction between DANCR and ANXA2 still require further investigation.
The pathophysiological interaction between autophagy activation and angiogenesis has been identified as a dynamic regulatory axis that controls pathological neovascularization, with temporal coordination occurring from initiation to resolution [ ]. Hypoxic reprogramming triggered compensatory angiogenesis and concomitantly activated autophagic responses through canonical HIF‐dependent and HIF‐independent pathways [ ]. Our current findings significantly extend this paradigm by identifying ANXA2 as a critical molecular nexus integrating DANCR‐mediated angiogenic signaling with autophagic regulation, a mechanistic convergence previously unrecognized in retinal vascular disorders. Our findings position the DANCR/ANXA2 axis as a molecular nexus integrating hypoxic signaling with retinal neovascularization. Mechanistically, DANCR operates synergistically with the HIF‐VEGF pathway through autophagic amplification of VEGF secretion, establishing a positive feedback loop that augments angiogenic signaling. This multi‐level regulation explains the superior efficacy of combining DANCR silencing with VEGF inhibition, revealing a previously unrecognized regulatory architecture in ROP pathogenesis. Our previous research also demonstrated that ANXA2 facilitated retinal neoangiogenesis by activating the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway [ ]. Furthermore, evidence has shown the link between autophagy and ANXA2 by altering cellular localization [ ]. Notably, it is essential to recognize that autophagy and apoptosis are double‐edged swords, as supported by previous studies [ ]. Jiang et al. reported that DANCR triggered proliferation and autophagy in hepatocellular carcinoma [ ]. At the same time, Wu et al. proved that DANCR enhanced breast cancer cell malignant proliferation but inhibited apoptosis and autophagy [ ]. DANCR's binding to ANXA2 under hypoxia drove EC proliferation and angiogenesis by suppressing apoptosis via autophagy activation. This dual role also highlights autophagy as a survival mechanism in hypoxic ECs, while implying the importance of homeostasis of autophagy levels at specific pathological stages.
In this study, silencing ANXA2 triggered activation of the p38 MAPK/mTOR signaling axis, a critical pathway mediating cellular stress responses. The p38 MAPK cascade, recognized as a central transducer of environmental stress signals, becomes activated through phosphorylation events that propagate downstream through effectors including MAPK‐activated protein kinase 2 (MK2) and HSP27, ultimately converging with mTOR signaling components [ ]. Notably, mTOR complex 1 (mTORC1) serves as a master regulator of autophagic processes through its phosphorylation‐mediated inhibition of Unc‐51‐like kinase 1 (ULK1), the essential autophagy‐initiating kinase [ ]. Our data further demonstrated that p38 MAPK inhibition enhanced the expression of Beclin‐1, a key autophagy initiation protein that acts downstream of ULK1 to promote autophagosome formation [ ]. This Beclin‐1 upregulation and decreased P62 levels provided direct evidence for enhanced autophagic flux in this regulatory pathway. Beyond this established autophagy regulation, the mTOR pathway engages additional downstream mechanisms that may contribute to DANCR's functions. mTOR complex 2 (mTORC2) activates AKT through phosphorylation, promoting cell survival and proliferation while inhibiting pro‐apoptotic proteins [ ]. This AKT activation can further phosphorylate and inhibit TSC2, relieving inhibition on mTORC1 and creating a positive feedback loop [ ]. Additionally, mTORC2 phosphorylates PKCα, regulating actin cytoskeleton organization, which is a function particularly relevant given ANXA2's known role in connecting the cell membrane with cytoskeletal components [ ]. Previous studies have established functional relationships between ANXA2, AKT, and PKCα during angiogenesis [ , ], suggesting these pathways may represent critical downstream effectors of the DANCR/ANXA2 axis. These molecular insights suggested a hypoxia‐responsive regulatory mechanism in which DANCR‐mediated ANXA2 activation triggers autophagy by suppressing the p38 MAPK/mTOR pathway, leading to increased Beclin‐1 expression and subsequent autophagic flux demonstrated through LC3B lipidation and P62 degradation, which collectively promote endothelial cell survival and neovascularization. This discovery gained clinical relevance when considering compensatory mechanisms during anti‐VEGF therapy, where therapeutic resistance frequently emerges through upregulation of alternative angiogenic pathways [ ]. Such adaptive responses may involve either activation of VEGF‐independent growth factors (e.g., basic fibroblast growth factor) or enhanced vascular stabilization through platelet‐derived growth factor‐mediated pericyte recruitment. Our findings proposed that targeting this newly identified DANCR‐ANXA2‐p38/mTOR‐autophagy axis could provide a strategic approach to circumvent resistance mechanisms in anti‐angiogenic therapies.
Given that OIR models were the most classic and mature models of ROP [ ], we developed this model to investigate further the role of DANCR in retinal neovascularization in vivo. Not surprisingly, DANCR knockdown markedly diminished the ANXA2 expression. Dallacasagrande et al. [ ] reported that the blockade of ANXA2 efficiently prevented retinal neovascularization, which aligns with our observations. The interaction between pathological retinal neovascularization and the activation or dysfunction of retinal glial cells is momentous in the progression of OIR. Retinal glial cells, including macroglia (consisting of Müller cells and astrocytes) and microglia, become activated and persist throughout OIR and extend beyond the recovery of retinal vessels [ ]. Previous studies showed that activated microglia aggregated around neovascular clusters, releasing pro‐inflammatory cytokines including interleukin‐1β (IL‐1β), tumor necrosis factor‐α (TNF‐α), and inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS) along with angiogenic factors like VEGF, which modulated the endothelial‐to‐pericyte ratio and drove the complex interplay between inflammation and pathological angiogenesis [ , ]. Our findings are consistent with the abovementioned studies, demonstrating increased retinal microglial density and their activation under oxygen‐induced damage. Beyond vascular ECs, DANCR silencing attenuated glial cell activation (e.g., reduced GFAP and GS expression), highlighting its broader role in preserving retinal homeostasis. Intriguingly, siDANCR treatment and combination therapy could reverse microglial activation, whereas monotherapy with aflibercept did not significantly affect microglial cells. Research on the effects of aflibercept treatment on microglial cells is scarce. Arima et al. reported that in diabetic macular edema, anti‐VEGF agents combined with ROCK inhibitors had a synergistic effect in suppressing microglial activation. However, VEGF blockade alone was insufficient to suppress microglial activation in the retinal tissue of mice [ ]. Zhang et al. demonstrated in their study using a chick model that intravitreal injection of a sustained‐release formulation of aflibercept did not induce significant microglial response or cell death [ ]. Meanwhile, the suboptimal therapeutic response observed in certain patients receiving intravitreal injections of anti‐VEGF agents, like aflibercept, may be attributed to complex mechanisms, including VEGF‐independent pathways. This highlights the need for exploring combination therapies to enhance treatment outcomes [ ]. In our study, the combination of DANCR‐siRNA and aflibercept exhibited superior efficacy in treating retinal neovascularization compared to the effectiveness of single‐agent therapy. The synergistic effect of DANCR silencing with aflibercept suggests a combinatorial strategy to overcome limitations of monotherapy (e.g., VEGF resistance or rebound neovascularization).
Extensive population‐based studies have elucidated a significant reduction in vision and ERG responses among ROP patients [ , ]. In this study, we confirmed that OIR induced structural and functional deficits in the rod and cone pathways, which were corroborated by our ERG data. Despite this, following the silencing of DANCR, we observed a notable improvement in visual function, as reflected in the electroretinographic responses. Reduced ANXA2 expression and improved retinal function in OIR mice after DANCR knockdown position DANCR as a promising therapeutic target for ROP. While our current study focused on the OIR model, the temporal upregulation of DANCR specifically during the pathological neovascularization phase suggests its potential relevance to proliferative ROP stages in humans. Our data indicate that DANCR's pro‐angiogenic functions are executed cell‐autonomously in endothelial cells. However, the observed attenuation of glial cell activation following DANCR silencing indicates that its functional impact extends beyond ECs, likely through paracrine mechanisms involving the retinal neurovascular unit. Beyond the therapeutic potential demonstrated by siRNA‐mediated knockdown, developing clinically viable DANCR‐targeting strategies warrants consideration of several approaches. Antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) represent a promising modality for lncRNA targeting, with several ASO‐based drugs already approved for neurological and neuromuscular disorders [ , ]. The eye presents a favorable target for ASO therapy due to its relative immunological privilege and the feasibility of local intravitreal administration, which could minimize systemic exposure and off‐target effects. However, challenges remain in optimizing delivery efficiency, stability, and duration of effect in retinal tissues [ ]. Alternatively, small molecule inhibitors that disrupt the specific DANCR‐ANXA2 protein interaction could be explored through high‐throughput screening strategies, though identifying compounds that specifically target RNA‐protein interfaces remains technically challenging. Future work could explore ASO efficacy in preclinical ROP models, assessing optimal dosing regimens, and evaluating potential off‐target effects. These are the essential steps toward translating DANCR targeting into a viable therapeutic strategy for ROP. While our OIR model replicates key features of ROP, differences in human retinal development may limit translational applicability. Future studies are required to validate the expression and functional roles of DANCR and ANXA2 in clinical ROP samples further.

## Conclusions
This study identified that DANCR exacerbated retinal pathological neovascularization in ROP through an ANXA2‐dependent p38/MAPK/mTOR‐mediated autophagy axis, establishing it as a novel therapeutic target. The upregulated DANCR expression observed in hypoxia‐exposed ECs and retinal tissues of the oxygen‐induced retinopathy murine model suggests its pathophysiological relevance in ischemic retinopathies. Functional characterization revealed DANCR's dual role in enhancing EC survival under hypoxic stress by simultaneously promoting pro‐angiogenic processes (viability, proliferation, and tube‐forming capacity) while activating cytoprotective autophagy to mitigate apoptosis. Mechanistic investigations revealed that DANCR exerts these effects through direct interaction with ANXA2, subsequently initiating a signaling cascade involving p38 MAPK and mTOR pathway inhibition, which are molecular switches known to orchestrate autophagy activation and angiogenic responses. Importantly, the therapeutic potential of targeting this axis was substantiated in vivo, where DANCR knockdown not only reduced pathological neovascularization but also had a synergistic effect with aflibercept to achieve better functional and structural improvements. The findings advanced our understanding of lncRNA‐mediated retinal vascular dysregulation and laid the groundwork for precision interventions targeting the neurovascular unit in oxygen‐induced retinopathies.
