## Visual Abstract
 

## Highlights
•USP9X is a crucial deubiquitinating enzyme involved in cardiac fibrosis, and its conditional deletion in myofibroblasts reduces myocardial fibrosis and improves heart function in MI mice.
•USP9X deletion also prevents scarring in lung and kidney fibrosis models, and its pharmacologic inhibition with Degrasyn reduces organ scarring after injury.
•USP9X activates ARK5 by deubiquitinating K29 and K33 chains, promoting fibroblast activation and differentiation through the RhoA/MRTF-A pathway.
•USP9X knockout partially mitigates the adverse effects of ARK5 gene delivery on cardiac remodeling following MI.
•Overall, USP9X regulates ARK5 kinase activity and enhances pathological cardiac fibrosis.

## Summary
USP9X plays a crucial role in myocardial fibrosis. This study showed increased USP9X expression in myocardial infarction models, associated with collagen deposition and myofibroblast activation. Myofibroblast-specific USP9X knockout and pharmacologic inhibition with Degrasyn both reduced fibrosis and improved cardiac function. Mechanistically, USP9X was found to bind and deubiquitinate AMPK-related kinase 5, thereby activating it and promoting transforming growth factor-β1–induced myofibroblast transformation via the Rho kinase pathway. These findings highlight USP9X as a potential therapeutic target for fibrotic diseases.

Heart failure (HF) is a clinical syndrome that encompasses a range of cardiovascular disorders. It is characterized by high rates of morbidity and mortality caused by left ventricular (LV) remodeling, with cardiac fibrosis being the primary pathological alteration. Reparative scar formation that occurs after myocardial infarction (MI) serves as an example of beneficial cardiac fibrosis. It replaces necrotic cardiomyocytes to preserve myocardial continuity and avoid ventricular rupture. Yet, in the end, this “replacement fibrosis” weakens contractility and causes either localized or systemic systolic dysfunction. The disproportionate buildup of extracellular matrix (ECM) components is a characteristic of myocardial fibrosis. Collagen turnover during cardiac remodeling is determined by the balance between its synthesis and breakdown; in the postinjury state, myofibroblasts are primarily responsible for this regulation. In addition to producing a wide array of matricellular proteins and serving as the primary source of structural ECM proteins in fibrotic hearts, activated myofibroblasts can also regulate matrix remodeling by producing proteases such matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) and their inhibitors. , Targeting myofibroblasts or their detrimental roles in the advancement of HF remains unachievable through current therapeutic approaches.
Ubiquitin (ub), an 8.5-kDa protein that has been conserved throughout evolution, controls intracellular protein homeostasis and enzyme activity by post-translational modification of substrate protein stability and location. The existence of deubiquitinating enzymes (DUBs), which can cleave ubiquitin from changed proteins, renders ubiquitination a reversible process. To date, around 100 DUBs have been shown to be implicated in the control of intracellular signal transduction and a range of illnesses, including cancer, infectious diseases, and cardiovascular diseases. , , As a member of the ubiquitin-specific protease family, ubiquitin-specific peptidase 9 X-linked (USP9X) regulates processes such as apoptosis, inflammation, proliferation, oxidative stress, and drug resistance by precisely recognizing, recruiting, and binding substrates for targeted stabilization and deubiquitination. Previous research has shown that USP9X is dysregulated in various cancer types, with a unique ability to either promote or inhibit tumorigenesis in a context-dependent manner. , , Emerging evidence also suggests a potential role of USP9X in cardiovascular disease; eg, USP9X mediates SR-A1 deubiquitination to inhibit foam cell formation and atherosclerosis and stabilizes MIB1 to prevent NOTCH1 activation, thereby protecting valve leaflets from calcification. Notably, despite its potential to modulate the transforming growth factor (TGF)-β signaling cascade by deubiquitination, the role of USP9X in cardiac fibrosis remains largely unexplored.
This study aimed to characterize the role of USP9X in post-MI cardiac fibrosis. A significant increase in USP9X expression was observed in TGF-β1-stimulated cardiac fibroblasts (CFs) in vitro and in the hearts of post-MI mice. To examine the function of USP9X in cardiac fibrosis, both genetic silencing and pharmacological inhibition approaches were applied. Additionally, coimmunoprecipitation combined with mass spectrometry was conducted to identify USP9X-binding proteins and explore the underlying mechanisms.

## Methods

## Bioinformatics analysis
Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data sets from wild-type mouse hearts post-MI (GSE136088) and microarray data from TCF21 lineage-traced CFs (GSE111059) were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus database. The UMAP reduction dimension analysis was utilized to minimize the data complexity for the following cell cluster pattern analysis based on previous reports. A volcano plot was created to show the most differentially expressed genes (log2-fold change) vs log10 P value. DUB expression from GSE111059 database was visualized using the R package “ggplot2” (R Foundation for Statistical Computing).

## Animal experiment
The animal experiment procedures were approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of Huazhong University of Science and Technology (IACUC Number: 4168) and adhered to National Institutes of Health Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals. USP9Xflox/flox mice were generated via clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats (CRISPR)/Cas9 technology (GemPharmatech) and then crossed with tamoxifen-inducible periostin-Cre (PostnMCM) mice (Jackson Laboratory). Tamoxifen was administered at a dose of 35 mg/kg, dissolved in corn oil, and given intraperitoneally for 5 consecutive days. USP9Xflox/flox/PostnMCM/tamoxifen mice were used as conditional knockouts (MF-KO), while USP9Xflox/flox/tamoxifen littermates served as wild-type (WT) controls. Adeno-associated virus serotype 9 (AAV9) encoding AMPK-related kinase 5 (ARK5) or GFP vector (GFP) were constructed by Genomeditech. Mice were injected with AAV9 through tail vein (2 × 1011 particles/mouse). After 2 weeks, the mice were randomly divided into 4 groups: AAV-GFP WT (n = 10), AAV-ARK5 WT (n = 10), AAV-GFP MF-KO (n = 13), and AAV-ARK5 MF-KO (n = 13).
The model of MI was established as previously described. Briefly, MI was created by permanent ligation of the left anterior descending artery with a 7-0 Prolene suture; mice undergoing a sham operation experienced the identical treatment, but without the ligation. The grouping was as follows: WT-Sham (n = 15), WT-MI (n = 17), MF-KO-Sham (n = 15), MF-KO-MI (n = 17), Sham (n = 13), Degrasyn (n = 10), MI (n = 17), and MI+ Degrasyn (n = 17). The Degrasyn (MedChemExpress) group was treated with Degrasyn (Deg) at a dose of 25 mg/kg, administered via intraperitoneal injection on alternate days.
Unilateral ureteral obstructed (UUO) models were established by unilateral ureteral ligation based on a previous research. The left ureter was exposed and sutured at 2 locations near the renal hilum, followed by excision. The obstructed kidneys of mice were removed at 7 days after UUO and collected for analyses. The grouping was as follows: WT-UUO (n = 10), MF-KO-UUO (n = 10), Sham (n = 8), Deg (n = 8), UUO (n = 10), and UUO+Deg (n = 10). As previously detailed, the mouse lung fibrosis model was established by intratracheal instillation of Bleomycin (MedChemExpress). On Day 14, mice were sacrificed. The grouping was as follows: WT-Bleomycin (n = 10), MF-KO-Bleomycin (n = 10), phosphate buffered saline (n = 8), Deg (n = 8), Bleomycin (n = 10), and Bleomycin+Deg (n = 10). Randomization and blinding were performed for the animals. When necessary, adult mice were anesthetized using pentobarbital and humanely sacrificed through carbon dioxide overdose. A schematic of the animal experiment is shown in .

## Echocardiography
For echocardiography, mice were anesthetized by the inhalation of 3% isoflurane. M-mode echocardiography was performed by a VisualSonics 770 echo system (FUJIFILM VisualSonics). Left ventricular internal dimension at end diastole (LVIDd) and left ventricular internal dimension at end systole (LVIDs) were measured using M-mode echocardiography. Ejection fraction and fractional shortening were automatically calculated using echocardiographic software.

## Histological examination
Heart, lung, and kidney tissues were fixed in 4% formaldehyde, dehydrated, transparent, and embedded with paraffin. Then, the tissues were cut into 4-μm-thick sections for subsequent experiments. The sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin, Sirius Red, and Masson’s trichrome staining (Solarbio) according to the manufacturer’s specifications.
Immunohistochemistry staining was performed as described previously. Antibody α-SMA (1:1,000, Proteintech) and USP9X (1:500, Proteintech) were used as primary antibodies.
Immunofluorescent staining was performed with different antibodies. The samples were then incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C. USP9X and α-SMA antibodies were used at a ratio of 1:200. Secondary antibodies conjugated with fluorophore were utilized for detection.

## Isolation and culture of mouse CFs
CFs were isolated from C57BL/6 mice, as previously described. The CFs were then cultured in DMEM supplemented with 20% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin-streptomycin at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2.

## Cell culture and infection
HEK-293T and National Institutes of Health/3T3 cells were obtained from the Stem Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences. Cells were exposed to TGF-β1 (10 ng/mL) at 37 °C for 24 hours. For transfection, cells were seeded into plates at 70% confluence and then transfected with gene-specific siRNA (10 nmol/L) or plasmid using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen) according to the manufacturer's directions.
Genomeditech created recombinant adenoviruses that expressed only GFP (Ad-GFP) and ARK5 (Ad-ARK5). The levels of expressed proteins were measured using Western blot analysis.

## Real-time quantification polymerase chain reaction
Total RNA from heart tissue and cells were isolated and purified using TRIzol reagent and reverse-transcribed to cDNA using PrimeScript RT reagent Kit (Takara). Real-time quantification polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) was performed in triplicate using a polymerase chain reaction (PCR) detection kit (Takara) and a CFX96 system (Bio-Rad Laboratories). The sequences of the PCR primers are shown in .

## LC-MS/MS analysis
To discover proteins binding to USP9X, cell lysates were removed and immunoprecipitated with anti-USP9X antibody. Then, the LC-MS/MS analysis was carried out by Oebiotech Co, Ltd. Finally, we screened the substrate proteins that may bind to USP9X based on the score and mass of identified proteins.

## Western blotting and immunoprecipitation analysis
Proteins from tissue or cell lysates were collected in the RIPA buffer (Servicebio). Western blotting was used to detect the protein levels of targets, as stated. Immunoprecipitation was performed as described previously. Antibodies against USP9X (55054-1-AP), α-SMA (14395-1-AP), mechanosensitive transcription factor myocardin-related transcription factor-A (MRTF-A) (21166-1-AP), Ubiquitin (10201-2-AP), glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate dehydrogenase (60004-1-Ig), PCNA (10205-2-AP), and RhoA (10749-1-AP) were purchased from Proteintech. Antibodies against ARK5 (sc-271828) were purchased from Santa Cruz. Antibodies against Collagen I (ab138492) were purchased from Abcam. Mouse IgG (B900620), and antibodies against Flag (20543-1-AP), MYC (16286-1-AP), and HA (51064-2-AP) were obtained from Proteintech. Rabbit IgG was obtained from Servicebio.

## Immunofluorescence staining
Immunofluorescence was conducted as described previously. CFs were diluted with α-SMA or MYPT-A antibodies in 5% bovine serum albumin at 4 °C overnight. Finally, 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole was used to counterstain nuclei.

## Wound Healing Assay
A wound healing experiment was used to assess cells’ migratory capacity. Following transfection, the cells were gently scratched with a sterile 200 mL micropipette tip, then replaced with fresh serum-deprived medium and incubated under hypoxic conditions for 24 hours. At 0 and 24 hours, images were captured. The migration distances were measured with ImageJ software.

## In vivo ubiquitylation assays
Cells were transfected with siRNA and corresponding plasmids as indicated for 24 hours and then incubated with MG132 (Solarbio) before harvesting. Cells were lysed with NETN buffer solution. S-protein agarose (Novagen) was added and placed in a rotating incubator at 4 °C overnight. Following 5 washings, the beads were boiled with SDS loading buffer, and then protein ubiquitinations were evaluated by Western blotting.

## RNA-sequencing analysis
Cardiac fibroblasts were transfected with si-NC or si-ARK5 and cultured with TGF-β1 for 24 hours. Total RNA from fibroblasts was isolated and purified using TRIzol reagent according to the manufacturer's instructions. RNA library preparation, sequencing, and analysis were performed by OE Biotech Co, Ltd. DESeq was used to identify differentially expressed genes according to the conditions of P < 0.05 and fold change >1.5. Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analysis in DEGs was performed.

## RhoA activation assay
RhoA activation was determined using the Rho Activation Assay Biochem kit (Cytoskeleton, Inc) as described previously. Rhotekin-RBD agarose beads were used to selectively separate and extract the active form of RhoA from cell lysates. The active GTP-RhoA was subsequently analyzed by Western blotting.

## Isolation of nuclear and cytosolic extract
A commercially available kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific) was used to separate nuclear and cytosolic protein fractions in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions.

## Statistical analysis
All data from the experiments are reported as mean ± SEM. The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was used to confirm that the data were normally distributed. The unpaired, 2-tailed Student's t-test was used to compare 2 groups with normally distributed data, whereas the 2-tailed Mann-Whitney U test was used for non-normally distributed data. For univariate comparisons among several groups, data with a normal distribution were analyzed using a 1-way analysis of variance followed by Tukey’s post hoc test for multiple pairwise comparisons. Otherwise, data were analyzed using the Kruskal-Wallis test followed by the Dunn’s post hoc test. For multivariable comparisons among groups, a 2-way analysis of variance followed by the Tukey’s post hoc test was used. Statistical analysis was conducted using GraphPad Prism 9.0 (GraphPad Software, Inc). A value of P < 0.05 indicates statistically significant.

## Results

## Increased expression of USP9X is associated with severe cardiac fibrosis in mice
To assess the contribution of fibroblasts following MI, we collected a single-cell RNA sequencing data set (GSE136088) of mouse hearts. Two weeks following MI, we observed more transcriptional heterogeneity in the fibroblast population ( ). In response to ischemia, fibroblasts' transcriptional profile revealed significantly enhanced expression of activated fibroblast markers such as Postn and Acta2 ( ). In recent years, there has been a rise of evidence pointing to the involvement of DUBs in the fibrotic process. , , , We next examined the GSE111059 data set, which used a lineage tracking system with Tcf21MCM/+; R26EGFP mice to identify the great majority of tissue-resident cardiac fibroblasts in the wounded mouse heart ( ). Of these, 6 candidates showed more than a 1.2-fold increase in mRNA levels compared with the control; these candidates were chosen for subsequent quantitative PCR validation and found that USP9X transcription was considerably elevated in MI mice heart ( ). LV lysates from the infarct zone of mice undergoing MI were examined at various time intervals for USP9X expression. In parallel, the fibrotic area's positive control for α-SMA and Collagen I expressions were measured. After MI, the expression of USP9X peaked at 7 days and remained elevated until 14 days, coinciding with the expression of α-SMA and Collagen I ( ). Immunohistochemical analysis revealed a significant increase in the number of USP9X-positive cells in the infarct, border, and remote zones at 14 days post-MI, with the highest levels observed in the border zones ( ). Additionally, USP9X expression in the ischemia border zone of MI hearts was up-regulated and colabeled with exacerbated α-SMA–marked cardiac fibroblasts according to immunofluorescence staining ( ). These findings indicate that elevated USP9X expression is linked to cardiac fibrosis in mice with cardiac damage. 

## Myofibroblast-specific USP9X deletion attenuates post-MI cardiac dysfunction, fibrosis, and alleviates organ fibrosis in vivo
To evaluate the role of USP9X in response to MI, we established a mouse model with conditional ablation of USP9X in myofibroblasts (USP9X MF-KO). PostnMCM mice have been routinely used to manipulate gene expression in activated myofibroblasts within the injured heart. , To investigate the role of USP9X in regulating cardiac function, echocardiographic assessments were conducted ( ). MF-KO mice showed preserved cardiac function, as evidenced by a smaller reduction in LV ejection fraction ( ) and LV fractional shortening ( ), indicating a slower progression of LV dysfunction following MI. Notably, MF-KO mice also displayed improved dilative remodeling 14 days post-MI, with significantly lower LVIDd ( ) and LVIDs ( ) compared with WT mice. Echocardiographic analyses showed no significant differences of cardiac function between Sham groups. These findings indicate that myofibroblast-specific USP9X deletion alleviates ventricular enlargement and dysfunction during MI. Next, we investigated whether the retained cardiac function in USP9X MF-KO mice is attributable to reduced reactive fibrosis and scar formation in response to MI. Masson's trichrome staining demonstrated considerably lower myocardial scar regions in USP9X MF-KO-MI mice ( ). Additionally, interstitial fibrosis in the border zone of USP9X MF-KO hearts was markedly decreased post-MI ( ). Hematoxylin and eosin staining revealed that myocardial tissue disarrangement and inflammatory cell infiltration were significantly improved in the USP9X MF-KO hearts ( ). We also observed decreased expression of genes critical for myofibroblast formation (Acta2) ( ), collagen biosynthesis (Col1a1, Col3a1, and Lox) ( ), and other ECM components (Fn1 and Postn) ( and ) in USP9X MF-KO hearts. MMPs are enzymes that degrade the ECM, with their activity tightly regulated by tissue inhibitors of metalloproteinases (TIMPs). Concerning fibrosis-associated enzymes, USP9X MF-KO hearts exhibited a significant reduction in Timp1 ( ) mRNA levels and showed an apparent increase in Mmp2 ( ) and Mmp9 ( ) mRNA levels. Consistent with the RT-qPCR results, immunofluorescence staining also demonstrated an up-regulated expression of MMP2 and MMP9 in USP9X MF-KO hearts ( ). Moreover, immunostaining revealed a significant reduction in the abundance of the primary fibrogenic marker α-SMA in USP9X MF-KO hearts ( ). In line with this, WT-MI mice showed substantially increased protein levels of Collagen I and α-SMA, USP9X MF-KO mice showed significantly lower α-SMA and Collagen I expression, demonstrating USP9X plays a vital role in cardiac myofibroblast activation in vivo ( ). We next investigated whether the observed reduction in fibrosis in USP9X MF-KO mice might be attributable to decreased cardiomyocyte cell death. TUNEL staining combined with cardiac troponin T–positive cell labeling indicated that USP9X knockout did not affect the cardiomyocyte apoptosis in border areas of myocardial tissues after MI ( ). These results suggest that USP9X deficiency attenuates the fibroblast-mediated profibrotic response and alleviates adverse cardiac remodeling following MI. 
Finally, as fibroblast activation plays a significant role in the development of fibrosis in several tissues, we investigated the possibility that USP9X MF-KO would provide protection against the development of fibrosis in other organs. First, we determined if USP9X MF-KO was efficient in reducing in vivo experimental lung fibrotic processes. Masson and Sirius Red staining demonstrated that USP9X MF-KO reduced the severity of Bleomycin-induced fibrosis by reducing structure consolidation and collagen deposition in comparison to the USP9X-WT group ( ). Similarly, USP9X MF-KO significantly prevented UUO-induced kidney fibrosis ( ). Based on these findings, it appears that USP9X is crucial to modulate organ fibrosis.

## Pharmacological inhibition of USP9X improves heart function and reduces organ fibrosis
We further investigated whether pharmacological inhibition of USP9X could reverse fibrosis in mice following MI. Degrasyn, a partially selective inhibitor of deubiquitinating enzymes, has been shown to inhibit USP9X deubiquitination. , , To elucidate the role of USP9X in cardiac fibrosis, MI-induced mice were administered Degrasyn intraperitoneally for 2 weeks. Data from echocardiography indicated that Degrasyn administration protected mice from MI-induced cardiac dysfunction ( ), as evidenced by preserved ejection fraction ( ) and fraction shortening ( ). Degrasyn treatment also significantly reduced the LVIDd ( ) and LVIDs ( ) of MI mice, with no significant changes observed in the Sham group. Histological evaluation revealed that the Degrasyn-treated MI mice exhibited reduced fibrosis ( ), decreased collagen deposition ( ), and lower protein expression of myofibroblast markers (α-SMA) ( ). These changes were not observed in either group of mice subjected to the Sham operation. Furthermore, terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase dUTP nick end labeling staining colabeled with cardiac troponin T indicated that Degrasyn treatment did not affect the cardiomyocyte apoptosis in the border region of myocardial tissue following MI ( ). 
To investigate the prospect of employing Degrasyn as an antifibrotic treatment method, we treated pulmonary and renal fibrosis mice with Degrasyn. Consistent with our data in USP9XMF-KO mice, early beginning of Degrasyn treatment decreased the development of histological markers of kidney fibrosis ( ). Also, Degrasyn therapy lessened lung fibrosis brought on by Bleomycin ( ). These findings show that the USP9X inhibitor Degrasyn has the potential to treat cardiac fibrosis, and that targeting USP9X provides an intervention strategy for fibrosis therapy.

## USP9X regulates cardiac myofibroblast activation in vitro
Next, we examined the expression of USP9X in cardiac fibroblasts following exposure to TGF-β1. After 24 hours of exposure, USP9X protein and mRNA levels increased substantially, matching the expression pattern of Acta2 and Col1a1 ( ). To investigate the role of USP9X in TGF-β1–induced myofibroblast activation, cardiac fibroblasts were exposed to TGF-β1 for 24 hours after being infected with siRNA targeting USP9X. RT-qPCR assays revealed that TGF-β1 stimulation induced the up-regulation of fibrosis-associated genes, including Col1a1, Col3a1, Acta2, Postn, and Timp1, which was subsequently reversed by USP9X siRNA treatment ( ). Additionally, the TGF-β–mediated up-regulation of Mmp2 and Mmp9 in fibroblasts was further amplified following the introduction of USP9X siRNA ( ). Notably, USP9X knockdown significantly reduced the protein expression of Collagen I and α-SMA ( ). Transfection of cells with USP9X siRNA dramatically decreased TGF-β1–induced α-SMA expression, as evidenced by immunofluorescence analysis ( ). Additionally, we observed a reduction in cell migration in TGF-β1-treated USP9X knockdown fibroblasts ( ). These data support a need for USP9X in TGF-β1–induced myofibroblast transformation and fibrotic response. 

## USP9X directly interacts with ARK5
Conventionally, the deubiquitinating enzyme USP9X is believed to be a crucial component in the regulation of SMAD4 function. First, we investigated the impact of USP9X on TGF-β/SMAD signaling. Western blot analysis revealed that there was no alteration in the expression of SMAD4 in cardiac fibroblasts following USP9X siRNA transfection ( ). To determine the substrate proteins controlled by USP9X when exhibiting antifibrosis effects, we screened candidate substrate proteins of USP9X using Co-IP paired with mass spectrometry ( ). Among the putative USP9X binding proteins, ARK5 (also named NUAK1) caught our attention because it has been demonstrated to be crucial in regulating organ fibrosis ( ). The effective coimmunoprecipitation of these 2 proteins was shown by co-IP with antibodies against USP9X or ARK5 and subsequent immunoblotting with antibodies ( and ). To determine whether USP9X affects ARK5 ubiquitination, we first overexpressed FLAG-tagged ARK5 and HA-tagged ubiquitin protein in HEK293T cells treated with control or USP9X siRNA. Western blot analysis revealed higher levels of total ARK5 ubiquitination in USP9X-deficient cells than in control cells ( ). HEK293T cells were then transfected with WT USP9X or the catalytically inactive mutant C1566S USP9X, and cells expressing USP9X-WT had lower levels of ARK5 ubiquitination than cells expressing USP9X-C1566S ( ). We then attempted to identify the regulatory mechanism of USP9X in ARK5 ubiquitination. As shown in and , USP9X primarily deubiquitinates ARK5 via chains K29 and K33. 

## USP9X influences myofibroblast transformation by activating ARK5
Because the stability of ARK5 is not considerably affected by controlling the level of ubiquitination through USP9X knockdown in CFs ( ), we next investigated how USP9X-mediated ubiquitination affects ARK5 function. Thus far, the most well studied substrate of ARK5 kinase activity is MYPT1 (PPP1R12A), the myosin phosphatase targeting component of PP1β. As shown in , knockdown of USP9X decreased the MYPT1 phospho-Ser445 levels, confirming that the deubiquitinate ARK5 by USP9X is important for ARK5 activation. Next, we investigated whether USP9X increases myofibroblast transformation by preferentially targeting ARK5 and the downstream signaling pathway. As expected, knockdown of ARK5 decreased the p-MYPT1S445 ( ), as well as α-SMA protein levels ( ); moreover, CFs were infected with Ad-ARK5 or empty vector GFP (Ad-GFP) together with siRNA-USP9X or siRNA-control. Following TGF-β1 stimulation, overexpression of ARK5 considerably increased the protein levels of α-SMA in comparison with coinfection with Ad-GFP, and was significantly decreased by coinfection with siRNA-USP9X ( ). These in vitro data suggest that USP9X mediates TGF-β1–induced myofibroblast transformation by increasing ARK5 activation. 

## ARK5 functions through the Rhoa/MRTF-A mechanotranscriptional pathway
Next, we explored the downstream mechanisms through which ARK5 regulates myofibroblast transformation. RNA samples taken from cardiac fibroblasts infected with si-ARK5 were used for RNA sequencing (RNA-seq). Rhoa signaling-related pathways were found to be among the most abundant DEGs according to KEGG analysis ( ). Rhoa signals have a crucial role in controlling cytoskeletal dynamics and producing actin-myosin contractility, which in turn facilitates the fibroblast phenotypic transition. Thus, we investigated whether Rho kinase activity is regulated by ARK5 and contributes to fibroblast activation mediated by USP9X-activated ARK5. The ratio of GTP-RhoA to total-RhoA indicated that RhoA was effectively activated by TGF-β1. ARK5-deficient significantly reduced RhoA activation ( ). Importantly, RhoA activity was dramatically elevated by ARK5 overexpression (OE), and this impact was significantly reduced by coinfection with siRNA-USP9X ( ). After that, we concentrated on the MRTF-A, which was demonstrated to be activated in response to the development of stress fibers and mediated via the Rho kinase pathway. First, we investigated whether MRTF-A is activated downstream of USP9X/ARK5 and discovered that TGF-β1 increases MRTF-A's nuclear translocation, which can be inhibited by ARK5 knockdown ( and ). In addition, USP9X-KD abolished ARK5-OE–induced MRTF-A nuclear translocation in TGF-β1 cultured cardiac fibroblasts ( and ). These data indicate a fundamental role for RhoA/MRTF-A–dependent mechanotranscriptional pathway in USP9X/ARK5-mediated responses. 

## USP9X accelerates MI-induced cardiac fibrosis by regulating ARK5
Next, we investigated whether ARK5 is a functional substrate for USP9X in the heart and whether it promotes cardiac fibrosis in MI mice. We created AAV9-encoding ARK5 particles and delivered them to mice via tail vein injection. Our findings indicate that after MI, ARK5 overexpression led to increased levels of α-SMA and Collagen I proteins ( ). Overexpression of ARK5 in the heart aggravated MI-induced cardiac remodeling, as evidenced by worsened cardiac dysfunction ( , ) and increased myocardial fibrosis ( ). However, USP9X knockdown partially reversed these effects. Together, these results indicate that USP9X mediates cardiac fibrosis and dysfunction through deubiquitination and activating ARK5. 

## Discussion
In this study, we demonstrate that USP9X expression is significantly elevated during cardiac fibrosis following MI. Both genetic deletion of USP9X in myofibroblasts and pharmacological inhibition using Degrasyn markedly reduced myocardial fibrosis, alleviated cardiac dysfunction, and attenuated fibrosis in lung and kidney injury models. Furthermore, USP9X was found to mediate TGF-β1–induced cardiac fibroblast activation, with its genetic inhibition suppressing profibrotic phenotypes. Mechanistically, we showed that USP9X directly interacts with ARK5, facilitating its activation and deubiquitination, which subsequently activates the RhoA/MRTF-A pathway and promotes fibroblast activation. Thus, our findings identify USP9X as a potential therapeutic target for the treatment of cardiac fibrosis.
Cardiac fibroblasts play a crucial role in regulating the fibrotic process in response to various pathological stimuli, such as ischemic injury. To facilitate tissue repair, signaling molecules and physical cues induce quiescent fibroblasts to differentiate into myofibroblasts, which express contractile proteins like α-SMA. This enables them to coordinate biomechanical remodeling and contraction by exerting traction forces on the abundant ECM that they secrete. We gathered a single-cell sequencing data from infarcted mice in the Gene Expression Omnibus database and discovered that cardiac fibroblast populations experienced heterogeneity 14 days following MI, as evidenced by a marked increase in the expression of myofibroblast markers Postn and Acta2. Ubiquitination is involved in the post-translational alteration of proteins implicated in key signaling pathways for fibroblast activation. The identification of specific DUBs and E3 ligases involved in fibrosis is crucial because, as demonstrated by Lear et al, these enzymes and their downstream targets represent druggable targets. In this study, we found that USP9X was elevated during the pathological myocardial fibrosis. Through the deubiquitination of vital proteins, USP9X regulates multiple signaling pathways and is intimately linked to neurological diseases, atherosclerosis, and malignancies. However, the role of USP9X in cardiac fibrosis remains unclear. To investigate this, we examined the impact of elevated USP9X expression using myofibroblast-specific USP9X knockout mice. Our data demonstrate that deletion of USP9X in myofibroblasts reduces the size of fibrotic scars in the infarcted region of mouse hearts 14 days post-MI. The formation of fibrotic scars in post-MI hearts involves the stimulation of profibrotic growth factors, along with the activation of cardiac fibroblasts, which subsequently secrete large amounts of ECM to replace damaged cardiomyocytes. The balance between MMPs and TIMPs is essential for scar formation in post-MI hearts. Our findings indicate that USP9X deletion in myofibroblasts enhances the production of MMP2 and MMP9 while down-regulating TIMP1, suggesting that USP9X modulates ECM remodeling in fibrotic scars by regulating MMP and TIMP expression. This balance is associated with a reduction in infarct scar area and an improvement in heart systolic function.
Previous studies have shown that USP9X inactivation ameliorates liver fibrosis by destabilizing Neuropilin-1. To explore whether USP9X contributes to fibrosis in other organs, we established models of UUO-induced renal fibrosis and bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis. USP9X knockout significantly attenuated fibrosis in both models, highlighting its broad role in fibrotic diseases.
Research on antifibrotic therapies is critically needed because organ transplantation remains the only definitive way to prolong and enhance the quality of life for patients with fibrosis. Degrasyn, a partially selective deubiquitinating enzyme inhibitor, has been recognized as a potential chemosensitizer caused by its capacity to block USP9X deubiquitination. , , In this study, Degrasyn administration effectively reversed myocardial fibrosis and dysfunction post-MI, representing a novel therapeutic strategy targeting DUBs. However, several limitations must be considered when evaluating the therapeutic potential of Degrasyn as a USP9X inhibitor for the treatment of fibrosis. Primarily, the specificity of Degrasyn poses a significant challenge, because it is not exclusively selective to USP9X and may interact with other deubiquitinating enzymes. This lack of selectivity could lead to off-target effects, thereby complicating the interpretation of our results. Consequently, the observed effects on cardiac fibrosis might not be solely attributable to the inhibition of USP9X. Therefore, future studies should aim to develop more selective USP9X inhibitors or utilize genetic approaches to more definitively isolate and understand the specific effects of USP9X inhibition.
TGF-β, a key promoter of fibroblast-to-myofibroblast transition, is significantly up-regulated post-MI. Consistent with this, our studies revealed that genetic inhibition of USP9X attenuated TGF-β–induced transformation of cardiac fibroblasts in vitro, suppressing both collagen production and cellular migration capabilities. DUBs’ function is tightly linked to that of the substrate proteins. We identified ARK5 as a USP9X substrate through LC-MS/MS analysis and further validated this interaction using Co-IP assays. ARK5 is a Ser/Thr kinase from the AMPK-related family, which includes 12 kinases with sequence homology to the catalytic domain of the AMPK-α subunit. Together, these kinases govern cell adhesion, polarity, metabolism, and the response to various stimuli, including energy, osmotic, and oxidative stress. , The pathological function of ARK5 was initially discovered to be linked to the development and progression of tumors, displaying characteristics that were both tumor-suppressive and carcinogenic. , A recent groundbreaking study, which conducted transcriptome analysis on transplanted kidney biopsy samples, revealed that ARK5 expression was elevated in fibrotic tissues and correlated with reduced function in human transplanted kidneys. Furthermore, fibroblast-specific ARK5-deficient mice or pharmacological suppression of ARK5 minimizes scarring and prevents fibrosis after injury. ARK5 inhibition disrupts the positive feedback loop generated by YAP/TAZ-ARK5, inhibiting fibroblast activation and hence reducing organ fibrosis. Our findings are consistent with prior research indicating that USP9X deubiquitinates ARK5 by catalyzing the unique Lys29/Lys33-linked polyubiquitin chains. Unlike other substrates of DUBs, USP9X cannot affect the stability of ARK5. Previous research has shown that AMPK family ubiquitin modifications appear to modulate LKB1-mediated kinase activity. , , The PP1β subunit, MYPT1, is currently the most extensively studied ARK5 substrate. According to research by Banerjee et al, ARK5-mediated activation of PLK1 through PP1MYPT1 inhibition connects ARK5 activity to the cell cycle. Therefore, we investigated the phosphorylation level of MYPT1 to confirm whether the ubiquitination modification impacts the kinase activity of ARK5. Interestingly, we discovered that following USP9X knockdown, p-MYPT1 expression drastically dropped, indicating that USP9X might have an impact on ARK5's kinase activity. Despite evidence that ARK5 may be a profibrotic kinase in injury models, other studies have revealed that the enzyme has completely distinct effects on fibroblast activation in an in vitro setting. According to our research, there was a considerable rise in ARK5 expression under TGF-β1 induction, and blocking ARK5 effectively decreased the expression of α-SMA. Additionally, myofibroblast-specific USP9X gene deletion partially mitigated the exacerbation of cardiac fibrosis caused by AAV9-mediated ARK5 overexpression after MI.
After identifying the critical function of ARK5 kinase activity in controlling myofibroblast phenotypes, RNA-Seq was used to identify the putative signaling route underpinning ARK5's biological effects. KEGG analysis revealed that fibroblasts transfected with si-ARK5 significantly disrupted the RhoA signaling pathway. The RhoA/ROCK system affects multiple cell types, including fibroblasts, cardiomyocytes, immune cells, and vascular cells, potentially contributing to fibrosis. Suppressing RhoA/ROCK-1 activity hindered the VSMC transition to myofibroblasts, hence preventing vascular fibrosis. In the pressure overload model, cardiomyocyte-specific RhoA signaling activates fibrogenic signaling via undiscovered paracrine mechanisms. Inhibiting the RhoA-ROCK pathway reduced the fibrosis of the pressure-overloaded heart. Myocardial and pulmonary fibrosis mouse models reveal RhoA/ROCKs-activated MRTF/SRF-mediated profibrotic gene transcription targets several genes, including pro-tissue necrotic cytokines and the fibroblast-myofibroblast differentiation marker, α-SMA. Silencing MRTF-A led to decreased α-SMA expression and TGF-β1–induced CF differentiation. In the current work, we discovered that TGF-β1 activated RhoA and MRTF-A nuclear translocation, which were blocked by ARK5 deficiency. USP9X knockdown prevented ARK5-OE–induced MRTF-A nuclear translocation in TGF-β1–cultured cardiac fibroblasts. According to our results, RhoA signaling is crucial for the regulation of myofibroblast transformation by USP9X-activated ARK5.
In conclusion, we identified that USP9X plays a role in deubiquitinating and regulating ARK5 kinase activity, which improves pathological cardiac fibrosis. Mechanistically, USP9X-activated ARK5 activates the RhoA/MRTF-A pathway, promoting the transformation of cardiac fibroblasts into myofibroblasts. These findings shed light on the role of USP9X in regulating intracellular signaling transduction.PerspectivesCOMPETENCY IN MEDICAL KNOWLEDGE: The development of postinfarction fibrosis significantly impairs cardiac function because of the irreversible nature of cardiomyocytes. Effective treatment of myocardial fibrosis remains a persistent challenge for cardiologists and researchers. USP9X, a crucial deubiquitinating enzyme, plays a key role in the differentiation of fibroblasts into myofibroblasts. In this study, we utilized MF-KO mice to demonstrate that targeting USP9X and its downstream pathways presents a novel approach for combating cardiac fibrosis by effectively addressing critical molecular targets.
TRANSLATIONAL OUTLOOK: Our conditional MF-KO mouse model offers novel tools that could enhance the development of improved antifibrotic therapies.
