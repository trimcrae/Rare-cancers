## Abstract
Advanced prostate cancer remains challenging, driven in part by Epidermal Growth Factor (EGF) signaling that promotes migration, invasion, and angiogenesis. We evaluated LA3IK (LAIAVK), a novel MIEN1-based hexapeptide, for its ability to inhibit EGF-mediated tumor progression in androgen-independent, EGFR-overexpressing PC3 prostate cancer cells. Our full assessment included mechanistic studies of receptor dimerization, downstream signaling pathways, and functional assays for cancer cell behaviors. The results show that LA3IK selectively disrupts EGFR–ERBB2 heterodimerization, reducing phosphorylation of EGFR at Y1068 and downstream signaling through NF-κB, Src, and STAT3. This leads to impaired EGF-driven migration and invasion while sparing proliferation. Mechanistically, LA3IK induces a tryptophan blue shift in EGF and inhibits EGFR–ERBB2 interaction. Transcriptomic analyses revealed downregulation of angiogenesis-related genes ANGPTL4 and VEGFC. Crucially, LA3IK exhibited cancer-specificity, inhibiting EGF signaling in prostate tumors while preserving physiological EGFR function in healthy liver tissues. This tissue-specific action underscores LA3IK’s favorable safety profile. We therefore report LA3IK as the shortest peptide to date that effectively inhibits EGF-mediated tumorigenesis in advanced prostate cancer with minimal off-target effects, highlighting its potential as a precise therapeutic agent.

## Introduction
Epidermal Growth Factor (EGF) and its receptor EGFR play pivotal roles in prostate cancer progression and metastasis. In normal prostate tissue, androgens maintain cellular homeostasis by downregulating EGFR expression . However, this regulatory mechanism is often disrupted in prostate cancer, leading to increased EGFR signaling and subsequent tumor growth, invasion, and metastasis. Enhanced EGFR expression correlates with disease relapses and progression to androgen independence, modulating pathways implicated in proliferation, survival, and invasion, particularly metastasis to bone . Recent advancements in targeted therapies have highlighted the potential of small molecule inhibitors and peptides to disrupt these aberrant signaling pathways. EGFR inhibitors like gefitinib and erlotinib have shown efficacy in blocking EGFR-mediated signaling, potentially reducing cancer cell proliferation and survival . In recent years, the development of peptides targeting the EGF and its receptor EGFR has emerged as a promising approach in cancer therapy. Several peptides have been reported to inhibit EGF-EGFR interactions and subsequent signaling pathways. Among these, Disruptin (SVDNPHVC) inhibits EGF-dependent EGFR dimerization by disrupting Hsp90 binding to EGFR . TE-64562 (RKKRRQRRRG-RRRHIVRKRTLRRLLQER), a cell-penetrating peptide derived from EGFR residues 645–662 and conjugated to the Tat sequence, has demonstrated the ability to inhibit EGFR dimerization and reduce both phosphorylated and total EGFR levels in various cancer cell types . GE11 (YHWYGYTPQNVI), a dodecapeptide, binds to EGFR with high affinity and competes with EGF for binding, suggesting a similar interaction site . Additionally, there are reports of peptides targeting EGF and induce apoptosis in cancer cells , . However, shorter peptides are increasingly preferred due to their cost-effective production, reduced toxicity and lower immunogenicity.
Our study investigated the potential of the Migration and Invasion Enhancer 1 (MIEN1)-based peptide LA3IK (LAIAVK) in inhibiting EGF-mediated prostate cancer progression. We focused on the roles of MIEN1 and the EGFR signaling pathway in promoting proliferation, migration, invasion, Epithelial-Mesenchymal Transition (EMT), and angiogenesis in prostate cancer cells. The MIEN1-based peptide was designed to interact with MIEN1 and inhibit its downstream signaling pathways . Given the promising results of LA3IK in breast cancer cells, there is a strong basis for investigating its effects in prostate cancer cells. Both cancers share common signaling pathways, including EGFR and NF-κB, which play crucial roles in cancer progression and metastasis. Our experiments demonstrated that the LA3IK peptide effectively inhibited EGF-induced activation of NF-κB, src, and B-catenin pathways. In advanced stages of the disease, heightened signaling through these pathways is frequently observed, playing a crucial role in promoting androgen-independent growth, facilitating metastasis, and contributing to therapy resistance. The NF-κB pathway controls the progression of prostate cancer to androgen-independent growth, making it a critical target for therapeutic intervention . By extending the investigation of LA3IK to prostate cancer cells, we can assess whether its inhibitory effects on the EGFR/NF-κB pathway are conserved across different cancer types. This could provide valuable insights into the peptide’s broader applicability as a potential therapeutic agent for multiple cancers that share similar signaling pathway dysregulations.

## Materials & methods

## Antibodies, protein, drug and primers
Primary antibodies used in this study included phospho-EGFR Y1068 Rabbit mAb (Cat# 3777), phospho-EGFR Y1173 Rabbit mAb (Cat# 4407), phospho-EGFR Y992 Rabbit mAb, (Cat# 2235), and total EGFR Rabbit mAb(Cat# 4267), all obtained from Cell Signaling Technology and used at a 1:1000 dilution. Additional antibodies from Cell Signaling Technology included CXCL1/CXCL2 (E5M6D) Rabbit mAb (Cat# 24376S and Cat# 24376 T), JunB (C37F9) Rabbit mAb (Cat# 3753S), FosB (5G4) Rabbit mAb (Cat# 2251S), EGF Receptor (EGFR1) Mouse mAb (Cat# 2256S), HER2/ErbB2 (29D8) Rabbit mAb (Cat# 2165S), IL-8 (E5F5Q) XP® Rabbit mAb (Cat# 94407S), and EGR3 Rabbit mAb (Cat# 2559S), all used at 1:1000 dilution. The β-actin antibody (Mouse mAb, Cat# sc-47778) was obtained from Santa Cruz Biotechnology and used at a 1:4000 dilution. Secondary antibodies included goat anti-mouse IgG-HRP (Cat# 1031–05) and goat anti-rabbit IgG-HRP (Cat# 4050–05), both purchased from Southern Biotech and used at a 1:2000 dilution. Human recombinant EGF was obtained from Gibco (Cat# PHG0311), and mouse recombinant EGF was purchased from R&D Systems (Cat# 2028-EG-200). For immunoprecipitation experiments, A/G Magnetic Beads (Thermo Scientific, Cat# 88,803) were used according to the manufacturer’s protocol. PCR primers were synthesized by Integrated DNA Technologies.

## Peptide synthesis
Peptides were synthesized using Fmoc (N-(9-fluorenyl) methoxycarbonyl)/tBu solid-phase peptide synthesis chemistry, with Fmoc-linker AM resin serving as the supportive matrix. Following synthesis, the crude peptides were purified using a reversed-phase preparative HPLC technique. Purification was performed on a C18 column (250 × 4.6 mm) using a suitable gradient of water and acetonitrile, with 0.1% TFA as a modifier, at a flow rate of 1 ml/minute. Eluted fractions were monitored at 214 and 220 nm, and the appropriate fractions containing the desired peptide were pooled and lyophilized . The molecular mass of the purified peptides was subsequently confirmed through MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry analysis. ## Cell culture and peptide treatment methods
All cell lines used in this study, including PC3, 22RV1, C4-2B, COS-7, and HUVEC, were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC). PC3 cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin. 22RV1 cells were maintained in RPMI-1640 medium with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin–streptomycin. C4-2B and Cos-7 cells were cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin–streptomycin. HUVEC cells were grown in endothelial cell growth medium (EGM-2) supplemented with 2% FBS and appropriate growth factors. All cell lines were kept at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO₂. Mycoplasma testing was done routinely using a PCR-based mycoplasma detection kit Plasmocin Prophylactic (InvivoGen). Cells were serum-starved for 24 h and treated with peptides in 0.1% fetal bovine serum media. In molecular and functional assays (RNA-seq, PCR, migration-invasion, ELISA), LA3IK and EGF were pre-incubated for 15 min at 4 °C. For all experiments, EGF and LA3IK were preincubated together for 15 min on ice prior to cell treatment to allow complex formation. Cells were then stimulated with the EGF or EGF–LA3IK mixture for another 15 min.

## Cell proliferation and selectivity index (SI) assays
To evaluate the effect of LA3IK and EGF on cell proliferation, PC3 cells were subjected to the trypan blue exclusion assay. Cells were seeded in 24-well plates at a density of 2 × 104 cells per well and treated with EGF (10 ng/ml) alone, EGF (10 ng/ml) in combination with the LA3IK peptide (80 μM), or left untreated for 24 and 48 h. After treatment, cells were harvested, mixed with 0.4% trypan blue solution (Corning, Product Number 25–900-CI), and counted using a hemocytometer. The total number of viable cells per ml was recorded and used to compare proliferation across treatment groups. To assess the selectivity of LA3IK in comparison to erlotinib, human prostate cancer cell lines (PC3, 22Rv1, and C4-2B) and normal human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) were cultured as described previously. Cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 5 × 103 cells per well and treated the next day with increasing concentrations of either LA3IK or erlotinib for 48 h. Cell viability was assessed using the MTT assay, where MTT reagent (5 mg/ml) was added and incubated for 4 h, followed by solubilization of formazan crystals in DMSO. Absorbance was measured at 570 nm using a microplate reader. The selectivity index (SI) for each compound was determined by dividing the IC₅₀ value in HUVECs by the IC₅₀ value in each prostate cancer cell line (PC3, 22Rv1, and C4-2B). A higher SI indicates greater cancer cell selectivity with reduced toxicity to normal cells.

## EGF-mediated wound healing assay and transwell invasion
The wound healing assay was performed using the Culture-Insert 4 Well in μ-Dish 35 mm, high ibiTreat( Ibidi USA). PC3 cells were seeded into wells and cultured until full confluency, then serum-starved for 24 h in RPMI 1640 media. Upon removing the inner well of the μ-Dish and washing away detached cells and debris, uniform 500 µm gaps were created between cell monolayers. Cells were then treated with either EGF alone, EGF + LA3IK peptides, or left untreated as a control. Photomicrographs were taken at 0-, 2-, 4-, and 6-h post-treatment using a phase-contrast microscope. The cell-free gap area was measured in a blinded manner to avoid observer bias, and the increase in cell area was calculated and plotted over time to assess wound closure. The invasive potential of PC3 cells under different treatment conditions was assessed using a Transwell invasion assay. Transwell plates (Corning Life Sciences) with polyethylene terephthalate membrane inserts (6.4 mm diameter, 8 μm pore size) were used for this purpose. PC3 cells (8 × 104) suspended in RPMI medium containing 0.1% serum were added to the upper chamber, with treatments including EGF alone, EGF + LA3IK peptide, or no treatment (control). The lower chamber contained DMEM with 10% FBS as a chemoattractant. After 24 h of incubation at 37 °C and 5% CO2, invaded cells on the lower surface of the membrane were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min, followed by a PBS wash. The membrane inserts were then further fixed with ice-cold methanol for 20 min and stained with 0.25% (w/v) crystal violet. Non-invaded cells were removed from the upper surface using a cotton swab. Random fields were imaged under a microscope, and the number of invaded cells was quantified using ImageJ software. Random fields were chosen for imaging, and the number of invaded cells was counted using ImageJ software. The results were expressed as an average number of cells per field.

## Reverse transcription
Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol (Invitrogen #15596026) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Briefly, cells were lysed in TRIzol, and chloroform was added (200 μl per 1 ml TRIzol). After centrifugation (12,000 × g, 15 min, 4 °C), the aqueous phase was mixed with an equal volume of isopropanol containing GlycoBlue (for RNA pellet visualization). RNA was precipitated by centrifugation (12,000 × g, 10 min, 4 °C), washed twice with 75% ethanol, air-dried, and resuspended in nuclease-free water. RNA concentration and purity were assessed using a Thermo Scientific NanoDrop 2000c Spectrophotometer. A260/A280 ratios between 1.9 and 2.1 indicated high purity RNA, while A260/230 ratios greater than 2.0 suggested minimal contamination from organic compounds or chaotropic salts. Complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized from 1 μg of total RNA using the Superscript-III First Strand Synthesis kit (Invitrogen), following the manufacturer’s instructions. The RT reaction was carried out at 25 °C for 10 min,50 °C for 15 min, and then terminated at 98 °C for 5 min. Complementary DNA was stored at − 20 °C.

## Real-time PCR
Real-time PCR was performed in duplicate using SYBR Green Master Mix (Invitrogen) on a Realplex2 Mastercycler ep gradient S thermal cycler (Eppendorf). Each 20 μl reaction was subjected to the following thermal cycling conditions: an initial denaturation at 95 °C for 15 min, followed by 40 cycles of denaturation at 95 °C for 30 s, annealing at 54 °C for 1 min, and extension at 72 °C for 45 s, with a final hold at 4 °C. The 18S rRNA gene was used as an internal control for normalization. Relative gene expression was calculated using the comparative Ct (ΔΔCt) method , . Primer sequences used were as follows: 18S (Forward: GTA ACC CGT TGA ACC CCA TT; Reverse: CCA TCC AAT CGG TAG TAG CG), ANGPTL4 (Forward: GAT GGC TCA GTG GAC TTC AAC C; Reverse: TGC TAT GCA CCT TCT CCA GAC C), and VEGF-A (Forward: CAA GAC AAG AAA ATC CCT GTG G; Reverse: GCT TGT CAC ATC TGC AAG TAC G).

## RNA-seq library preparation and sequencing
RNA-seq library preparation and sequencing began with total RNA extraction from PC3 cells. RNA concentration was quantified using a Qubit fluorometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific), and only samples with an RNA Integrity Number (RIN) value of 8.0 or higher were used for library preparation. RNAseq libraries were prepared using Illumina Stranded mRNA Library Prep kits (Illumina, Cat. No. 20040534) following the manufacturer’s instructions. One microgram of total RNA served as the starting material for each library. Poly-A-tailed RNA was purified using poly-T oligo beads provided in the kit. The purified RNA samples were then fragmented and converted to double-stranded DNA for sequencing. Individual libraries were uniquely indexed using the Illumina RNA UD Indexes (Illumina, Cat. No. 20040555) and pooled in equimolar ratios. The pooled libraries were sequenced on an Illumina NextSeq 550 sequencing system, generating 75-bp paired-end reads.

## RNA-seq data analysis
Low-quality reads were trimmed, and adapter contamination was removed using Trim Galore (v0.5.0) to ensure high-quality input data for downstream analysis. The cleaned, trimmed reads were then aligned to the human reference genome (hg38) using HISAT2 (v2.1.0) , allowing for accurate mapping of RNA sequences. Gene expression levels were quantified with StringTie (v1.3.3b) , employing gene models (v27) from the GENCODE project to provide a comprehensive annotation framework. Finally, differentially expressed genes were identified using DESeq2 (v1.22.1) enabling us to analyze the expression patterns across treatment conditions at various time points . Genes with a log2 fold change ≥ 0.25 were considered upregulated, those with a log2 fold change ≤ -0.25 were deemed downregulated, and an adjusted p-value ≤ 0.05 was set as the threshold for statistical significance – .

## Western blot analysis
Western blot analysis was performed to assess protein expression and modification in response to EGF and LA3IK peptide in PC3 cells. Cells were lysed using RIPA buffer supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitors, and protein concentrations were determined by bicinchoninic acid method. Equal amounts of protein (40 μg) were separated on 4–12% Bis–Tris precast gels using SDS-PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes using the iBlot2 Dry Blotting System (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Membranes were blocked with 5% BSA in TBST, followed by overnight incubation at 4 °C with primary antibodies targeting proteins of interest. After washing, membranes were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies. Protein bands were visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence and the Invitrogen iBright CL1500 Imaging System (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Densitometry analysis was performed using the built-in software of the iBright system, with analysis frames manually positioned around each band of interest. Background subtraction was applied, and densitometry values were exported for further analysis. The ratios of phosphorylated proteins to total proteins or other loading controls were calculated using Microsoft Excel, and graphical representations were generated using GraphPad Prism 10 software.

## Tryptophan (Trp) fluorescence assays
The interaction between human epidermal growth factor (EGF) and LA3IK peptide was investigated using a tryptophan fluorescence shift assay on a Cary 60 UV–Vis Spectrofluorometer. EGF (2.5 μM in PBS, pH 7.4) was titrated with increasing concentrations of LA3IK peptide (0–100 μM). Fluorescence spectra were recorded at room temperature in a quartz cuvette with a 1 cm path length, with excitation at 295 nm to selectively excite tryptophan residues and avoid fluorescence contributions from nearby tyrosines. Emission was measured from 300 to 400 nm, with the slit width set to 10 nm for both excitation and emission. Background spectra of the LA3IK peptide alone were subtracted from the LA3IK-EGF spectra to isolate the EGF fluorescence changes. Smoothening of spectra was performed to reduce noise. Changes in the emission maximum (initially ~ 357 nm) and fluorescence intensity were monitored as a function of LA3IK concentration. LA-6(LASAVK) peptide was used as a control, and identical experiments were performed with this peptide. Data analysis was conducted using GraphPad Prism software, plotting fluorescence changes against peptide concentration to evaluate potential interactions between EGF and the peptides .

## Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) assay
PC3 cells (1 × 10⁷) were cultured in 150-mm dishes until 70–80% confluency. Cells were harvested in ice-cold PBS and lysed in RIPA buffer supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitor cocktails. Lysates were rotated at 4 °C for 30 min and clarified by centrifugation at 18,000 × g for 15 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was collected, and protein concentration was determined using the Pierce BCA Protein Assay Kit. For each Co-IP reaction, 500 µl of lysate (1 mg/ml) was incubated with the indicated primary antibody—MIEN1, ERBB2, or EGFR—for 2 h at 4 °C with gentle rotation. Following antibody binding, 5 µl of pre-washed Protein A/G magnetic beads were added and incubated for an additional 2 h at 4 °C. Beads were then washed three times with 1 × TBST for 10 min each at 4 °C. As controls, lysates were also incubated with beads alone (no antibody) to account for non-specific binding, and 10% of each lysate was reserved as input for direct analysis by Western blotting. Bound proteins were eluted by boiling the beads in SDS sample loading buffer and analyzed by SDS-PAGE followed by Western blotting using antibodies against MIEN1, ERBB2, and EGFR. To determine the composition of protein complexes, Co-IP experiments were performed in both forward and reverse directions: MIEN1 was immunoprecipitated and probed for ERBB2, and vice versa; similarly, ERBB2 was immunoprecipitated and probed for EGFR and MIEN1, and reverse experiments were conducted accordingly. To assess the effect of the LA3IK peptide on EGF-mediated protein interactions, cells were treated with LA3IK in the presence or absence of human recombinant EGF, followed by immunoprecipitation using an antibody against EGFR. Co-immunoprecipitation of ERBB2 was then evaluated to determine disruption of the EGFR–ERBB2 complex. A mouse-specific monoclonal antibody against EGFR (Cat# 2256S) and a human-specific HER2/ErbB2 (29D8) rabbit monoclonal antibody (Cat# 2165S), both from Cell Signaling Technology, were used to distinguish the closely migrating protein bands of EGFR (~ 175 kDa) and ERBB2 (~ 185 kDa).

## Immunofluorescence staining and confocal microscopy
PC3 cells were seeded on 22-mm glass coverslips and cultured in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin–streptomycin. Following cell attachment, cultures were serum-starved for 24 h. Cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min at room temperature and permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 for 15 min. Non-specific binding was blocked by incubating the cells with 5% bovine serum albumin for 1 h at room temperature. The cells were then incubated overnight at 4 °C with the respective primary antibodies. After washing, species-specific secondary antibodies conjugated to FITC or Texas Red were applied for 1 h at room temperature. Nuclei were counterstained with 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI) for 10 min. Finally, coverslips were mounted on glass slides using an anti-fade mounting medium (Invitrogen). Fluorescence images were acquired using an inverted Zeiss LSM 880 Airyscan confocal microscope, with all image acquisition settings kept constant across experimental groups.

## ELISA
Quantification of secretory ANGPTL4 and VEGF in the culture medium was performed using a Human Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA) development kit (DY3458 and DVE00) following the manufacturer’s instructions (R&D Systems). Briefly, 100 μl of diluted capture antibody was coated onto a 96-well microplate and incubated overnight at room temperature. After washing, the wells were blocked with 300 μl of reagent diluent (1% BSA in PBS). After another wash, 100 μl of standards and samples were added and incubated for 2 h at room temperature. Subsequently, 100 μl of detection antibody was added and incubated for another 2 h, followed by 100 μl of streptavidin-HRP for 20 min, protected from light. After washing, 100 μl of substrate solution was added for 20 min, and 50 μl of stop solution was used to terminate the reaction. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm, with the readings subtracted by those taken at 540 nm, using a Biotek Synergy 2 Plate Reader. For VEGF quantification, the conditioned medium was collected in 1% FBS to ensure VEGF stability. A total of 200 μl of culture medium from cells treated with EGF, EGF + LA3IK, or untreated controls was incubated with the precoated capture antibody in microplate strips at room temperature. After washing, 200 μl of human VEGF conjugate was added and incubated for 2 h. Following a wash, 200 μl of substrate was added and incubated for 20 min at room temperature, and then 50 μl of stop solution was added. Absorbance readings for VEGF were taken at 450 nm and subtracted by readings at 540 nm, like the ANGPTL4 measurements.

## Transient transfection and peptide treatment in COS-7 cells
COS-7 cells, at 70 to 80% confluency, were transiently transfected with a plasmid of interest in 35 mm glass-bottom MatTek dishes. The plasmid was transfected into the cells using Lipofectamine 2000 and incubated for approximately 12 h. A total of 2 μg DNA (1:1) ratio of mCherry and eGFP-tagged EGFR plasmid was used to express both fluorescent constructs evenly. After 12 h of transfection, the cells were treated with different concentrations of peptide (LA3IK) at varying incubation times (18–24 h). Before measuring with PIE-FCCS, the media was changed to Opti-MEM I Reduced Serum Medium (Thermo Fisher Scientific) without phenol red. For each system, measurements were taken for both the ligand-free and ligand-stimulated state, using recombinant human EGF (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO). Receptor-expressing cells were stimulated with 500 ng/ml of ligand (EGF) and incubated for 15 min prior to data collection. The acquired local density of receptors expressed was in the range of 100–3000 receptors/μm2.

## ERBB2 silencing in PC3 cells via transient siRNA transfection
PC3 cells (ATCC) were maintained in RPMI with 10% fetal bovine serum at 37 °C, 5% CO₂. One day prior to knockdown, 2 × 105 cells were seeded per well of a 6-well plate. ERBB2 was transiently depleted with 5 nM ON-TARGETplus SMARTpool siRNA (Dharmacon) using Lipofectamine RNAiMAX (Thermo Fisher) according to the manufacturer’s rapid-forward protocol; a non-targeting siRNA served as control. Assays were carried out 24 h post-transfection.

## Pulsed interleaved excitation fluorescence cross-correlation spectroscopy (PIE-FCCS)
Live-cell pulsed interleaved excitation fluorescence cross-correlation spectroscopy (PIE-FCCS) was employed to investigate the effect of LA3IK peptide on EGFR oligomerization in COS-7 cells. Cells were plated on MatTek glass-bottom dishes and maintained in Opti-MEM at 37 °C. For stimulation, cells were incubated with 500 ng/ml ligand for 15 min prior to imaging. Measurements were performed on a customized Nikon Eclipse Ti inverted microscope equipped with a supercontinuum white light laser (SuperK, NKT Photonics) for pulsed interleaved excitation and time-correlated single-photon detection. To ensure that only membrane-bound receptors were analyzed, the excitation beams were focused on the flat peripheral membrane regions of live cells, avoiding fluorescence from cytosolic organelles and vesicles. Emitted photons were collected through a 50 µm pinhole for confocal detection, separated into green (520 nm) and red (612 nm) emission channels, and detected using single-photon avalanche diode (SPAD) detectors. Laser powers were set at 400 nW (488 nm) and 800 nW (561 nm), and six 10-s measurements were averaged per cell. Signals were recorded using a time-correlated single-photon counting (TCSPC) card and analyzed with a MATLAB script as described previously , . Data analysis involved auto- and cross-correlation of the two-color PIE-FCCS data to determine the oligomerization state (fraction correlated, fc), diffusion dynamics (effective diffusion coefficient, Deff), and receptor expression density. Oligomerization states were classified based on fc : < 0.09 for monomers, 0.09–0.17 for dimers, and > 0.17 for higher-order oligomers , . Increased fc values and decreased Deff indicated the formation of stable higher-order oligomers, providing insights into receptor dynamics under the influence of LA3IK and ligand stimulation.

## invivo assays
Male BALB/c mice were purchased from Charles River and housed in a temperature- and light-controlled environment (12-h light/12-h dark cycle) with ad libitum access to food and water. An acute dose-finding study was conducted using Hardesty et al.’s method . The mice were divided into treatment groups and received either PBS, EGF (0.2 µg/g), or a combination of two doses of LA3IK (40 mg/kg and 60 mg/kg) along with EGF (0.2 µg/g) via intraperitoneal (IP) injection. The EGF dose was chosen based on Yang et al. as this dose was demonstrated to cause maximal phosphorylation of EGFR at Y1173 after 30 min of administration. Thirty minutes post-injection, the mice were euthanized using CO2 inhalation in a gradually filled chamber, Whole livers were promptly dissected and snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen. Liver tissue (100 mg) was homogenized in 500 µl of radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) buffer supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitors (10 μl/ml; Sigma-Aldrich). The liver homogenates were sonicated using a Vibra Cell sonicator, to ensure complete homogenization. The homogenates were then centrifuged at 4 °C for 5 min at 16,000 g. The supernatants were aliquoted and stored at − 80 °C until future analysis. Protein concentrations were assessed using the bicinchoninic acid protein assay. Subsequently, 20 µg of protein samples were prepared for Western blot analysis following the previously established protocols. Blood was collected via cardiac puncture and allowed to clot at room temperature before centrifugation to isolate serum, which was stored at − 80 °C. Mouse EGFR levels in serum were measured using a commercial ELISA kit (R&D Systems, Cat# MEGFR0), following the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, 50 μl of Assay Diluent RD1-34 and 50 μl of serum or standard were added per well and incubated for 2 h at room temperature. After washing, 100 μl of Mouse EGFR Conjugate was added and incubated for another 2 h. Plates were washed again, and 100 μl of Substrate Solution was added for 30 min, followed by 100 μl of Stop Solution. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm with correction at 540 nm.

## Ethics declarations
All animal experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) of the UNT Health Science Center (Protocol #2024–0006) and conducted in accordance with institutional, national, and ARRIVE guidelines and regulations. Male BALB/c mice (Charles River Laboratories) were housed under specific pathogen-free conditions with a 12-h light/dark cycle and provided autoclaved food and water ad libitum. Anesthesia and euthanasia were performed in a separate dedicated procedure room. Anesthesia was induced and maintained with 2–3% isoflurane in oxygen. At study endpoints, mice were euthanized by CO₂ inhalation at a displacement rate of 30–70% of the chamber volume per minute, and death was confirmed by the absence of heartbeat and respiration. Three mice per experimental group were used to ensure sufficient statistical power while minimizing animal use.

## Statistical analysis
All data are presented as mean ± SD from at least three independent experiments. Statistical analysis was performed using unpaired t-tests, Mann–Whitney tests, or one-way and two-way ANOVA, as appropriate. Analyses were done in GraphPad Prism (v10.0), with p ≤ 0.05 considered significant.

## Data visualization tools

## Results

## Experimental validation demonstrates that MIEN1 interacts with ERBB2, not EGFR, in prostate cancer cells, providing a rationale for targeting EGFR–ERBB2 signaling with the MIEN1-based peptide LA3IK
Using immunoprecipitation (IP) and reciprocal co-immunoprecipitation (co-IP) assays, we demonstrated that MIEN1 directly interacts with ERBB2, a known heterodimerization partner of EGFR (ERBB1). When MIEN1 was immunoprecipitated from PC3 cell lysates, ERBB2 was consistently detected in the co-immunoprecipitated complex (IP: MIEN1; co-IP: ERBB2), indicating a physical association between the two proteins (Fig.  A). To confirm the specificity and directionality of this interaction, we performed the reverse experiment by immunoprecipitating ERBB2 and probing for MIEN1. MIEN1 was consistently detected in ERBB2 immunoprecipitates (IP: ERBB2; co-IP: MIEN1), further validating the reciprocal nature of the MIEN1–ERBB2 interaction. (Fig.  A). These results provide the first experimental evidence of a direct association between MIEN1 and ERBB2 in prostate cancer cells. In contrast, when EGFR was immunoprecipitated, MIEN1 was not detected in the complex (IP: EGFR; co-IP: MIEN1), indicating that MIEN1 does not associate with EGFR in PC3 cells. However, consistent with previously reported receptor interactions, ERBB2 was readily detected in the EGFR immunoprecipitates (IP: EGFR; co-IP: ERBB2), confirming EGFR–ERBB2 heterodimerization under these conditions. (Fig.  B). Together, these findings demonstrate that MIEN1 specifically associates with ERBB2 in PC3 prostate cancer cells, independent of EGFR.
 Since LA3IK has been shown to inhibit MIEN1-driven oncogenic signaling and attenuate EGF-stimulated NF-κB activation, we next investigated whether this peptide could also interfere with EGFR-driven signaling via the EGFR–ERBB2 axis . ERBB2 functions as a critical co-receptor that, despite lacking direct ligand-binding ability, stabilizes and amplifies EGFR signaling. Given that LA3IK suppresses signaling initiated by MIEN1, another ERBB2-associated binding partner, we reasoned that it might similarly destabilize EGFR–ERBB2–mediated signaling. Based on this framework, we tested whether LA3IK treatment could suppress EGF-induced transcriptional responses by interfering with ERBB2-dependent pathways in PC3 cells.
As a first step, we characterized the activation dynamics of EGFR following EGF stimulation to establish a baseline response in this system. PC3 cells were treated with recombinant human EGF (10 ng/ml), and lysates were collected at 0, 5, 10-, 20-, 30-, and 60-min post-treatment. Western blot analysis using a phospho-specific antibody against EGFR Y1068 revealed a rapid and transient activation of the receptor. Phosphorylation of EGFR at Y1068 was detectable by 5 min, peaked at around 10–15 min, and declined thereafter, indicating a sharp but short-lived activation in response to EGF (Fig.  C). We also performed densitometry to confirm this observation (Fig.  D). We thus established 15 min as the optimal time point for peak EGFR activation and provided a basis for investigating downstream signaling and transcriptional responses. To characterize EGF-induced transcriptional changes, we performed RNA sequencing on PC3 cells collected at 0 h, 30 min, 1 h, and 6 h post-EGF stimulation. The transcriptomic analysis revealed a dynamic gene expression landscape. Early-response genes included canonical immediate early genes such as EGR2, EGR3, FOS, FOSB, JUN, and JUNB, as well as EGF-responsive genes like ERG1, which is implicated in cancer progression (Fig.  E). Western blot analysis at 30 min and 1-h post-stimulation confirmed increased protein expression of select targets (EGR3, CXCL2, CXCL8, FOSB, and FOSB2), consistent with the RNA-seq results (Fig.  F, G and H).

## Co-treatment of LA3IK with EGF Inhibits EGFR activation and downstream signaling in PC3 cells compared to EGF alone
To evaluate whether the MIEN1-based peptide LA3IK impairs EGF-mediated signaling, we assessed the phosphorylation status of key components in the EGFR signaling cascade in PC3 prostate cancer cells following EGF stimulation, with or without LA3IK treatment. Cells were stimulated with the EGF or EGF–LA3IK mixture for 15 min, corresponding to the peak of EGFR phosphorylation. Western blot analysis revealed robust phosphorylation of EGFR at Tyr1068 in cells treated with EGF alone. This phosphorylation was markedly reduced in cells co-treated with LA3IK and EGF, while control (untreated) cells showed minimal basal phosphorylation (Fig.  A). Densitometric analysis confirmed a significant decrease in phospho-EGFR (Tyr1068) levels in the LA3IK-treated group compared to EGF alone, normalized to total EGFR (Fig.  B). Downstream of EGFR, we observed a similar trend in Src activation. Phosphorylation of Src at Tyr416 was strongly induced by EGF and substantially reduced in the presence of LA3IK (Fig.  C), with densitometric quantification showing decreased phospho-Src levels in LA3IK-treated cells (Fig.  D). We next examined NF-κB signaling by probing for phosphorylation of the p65 subunit at Ser536. EGF stimulation enhanced p65 phosphorylation, which was attenuated in cells treated with LA3IK (Fig.  E). This reduction was quantitatively confirmed by densitometry (Fig.  F). Similarly, phosphorylation of STAT3 at Ser727 was also reduced upon LA3IK treatment relative to EGF alone (Fig.  G and H). We examined IκBα phosphorylation, a key regulator of NF-κB activation. Phosphorylation of IκBα increased following EGF stimulation but was reduced in LA3IK-treated cells (Fig.  I), indicating inhibition of NF-κB activation. This observation was confirmed by densitometric analysis (Fig.  J).
 Interestingly, β-catenin phosphorylation at Ser33/37/Thr41 showed a distinct pattern. EGF-treated cells displayed decreased phosphorylation at these sites, consistent with β-catenin stabilization. In contrast, both untreated cells and those treated with EGF + LA3IK maintained higher levels of phosphorylated β-catenin, suggesting that LA3IK may counteract EGF-induced β-catenin dephosphorylation and stabilization (Fig.  K). Phosphorylation of Src at the inhibitory site Ser527 remained unchanged across all conditions (Fig.  K), with quantification confirming no significant differences (Fig.  L). Consistent with observations in PC3 cells, LA3IK treatment significantly reduced EGF-induced phosphorylation of EGFR at Tyr1068 in 22Rv1 cells (Fig.  M), as confirmed by densitometric analysis (Fig.  N). In contrast, C4-2B cells did not show EGFR phosphorylation at Tyr1068 in response to EGF stimulation, consistent with previous reports . Nonetheless, EGF treatment activated downstream signaling, as evidenced by increased Src phosphorylation at Tyr416, which was markedly inhibited by LA3IK (Fig.  O), with quantification confirming this effect (Fig.  P). Together, these findings demonstrate that LA3IK disrupts EGF-induced activation of EGFR and multiple downstream effectors, including Src, STAT3, NF-κB, and β-catenin pathways.

## LA3IK inhibits EGF-induced migration and invasion without affecting proliferation in PC3 cells
To evaluate the effects of EGF stimulation and LA3IK inhibition on prostate cancer cell behavior, we performed scratch wound assays to assess migration. PC3 cells treated with EGF (10 ng/ml) exhibited enhanced migration, with wound width decreasing from 500 µm at 0 h to an average of 121 µm after 6 h, representing ~ 78% wound closure. Untreated control cells showed an average wound closure of 63%, whereas cells co-treated with EGF and LA3IK (80 µM) displayed only 34% wound closure over the same period (Fig.  A). Thus, LA3IK significantly impaired EGF-induced migration, reducing wound closure by ~ 56% relative to EGF treatment alone, demonstrating its efficacy in inhibiting EGF-stimulated migratory behavior in PC3 cells (Fig.  B). To further assess the impact of LA3IK on the invasive potential of PC3 cells, we performed a matrigel-coated transwell invasion assay. As expected, EGF treatment (10 ng/ml) significantly enhanced the invasiveness of PC3 cells, increasing the number of invading cells from an average of 164.33 ± 33.45 in the untreated control group to 275.67 ± 26.68, representing approximately a 1.68-fold increase. Co-treatment with LA3IK resulted in a dose-dependent reduction in EGF-induced invasion. At 50 μM, LA3IK reduced the number of invading cells to 194.0 ± 8.89, corresponding to a 29.6% inhibition. Increasing the concentration to 80 μM further decreased invasion to 125.67 ± 16.48 cells, representing a 54.4% inhibition. At 90 μM, LA3IK markedly suppressed invasion to 79.33 ± 11.94 cells, reflecting a 71.2% inhibition relative to the EGF-treated group (Fig.  C and 3D). Representative micrographs confirmed these findings, showing fewer migrated cells in the LA3IK-treated groups compared to EGF alone. To confirm that these inhibitory effects were not due to reduced proliferation, we performed Trypan Blue exclusion assays over a 48-h period. Viable cell counts in PC3 cells showed no significant difference across control, EGF-treated, LA3IK-treated, or EGF + LA3IK groups (p > 0.05), indicating that LA3IK does not impair basal cell proliferation under these conditions (Fig.  E). Selectivity Index (SI) analysis was performed to evaluate the selectivity of the peptide LA3IK relative to erlotinib across three prostate cancer cell lines (22Rv1, PC3, C4-2B) and one normal cell line (HUVEC). The SI was calculated as the ratio of IC₅₀ in HUVEC cells to the IC₅₀ in each cancer cell line.LA3IK demonstrated higher SI values than erlotinib in the 22Rv1 and PC3 prostate cancer cell lines, indicating greater selectivity towards these cancer cells over normal endothelial cells. Specifically, the SI for LA3IK in 22Rv1 and PC3 cells was significantly higher than that of erlotinib (p < 0.05), indicating greater selectivity. In contrast, erlotinib showed a higher SI in the C4-2B cell line compared to LA3IK, suggesting better selectivity of erlotinib in this particular model (Fig.  F).
 

## Temporal dynamics of angiogenesis-related gene activation during EGF stimulation in PC3 prostate cancer cells
RNA sequencing analysis of PC3 prostate cancer cells treated with EGF (10 ng/ml) revealed a complex, time-dependent upregulation of angiogenesis-related genes. The heatmap visualization showed a clear sequential activation of these genes over time. At the 0-h baseline, angiogenesis-related genes, including ANGPTL4, VEGFC, EPAS1, ROBO4, EPHA2, EPHB3/B4, MMP1, MMP14, SERPINE1, COL4A1, and COL4A2, were minimally expressed. By 30 min, very few genes were activated, with expression levels remaining close to baseline. By 1 h, an increase in angiogenesis-related gene expression was observed, indicating the initiation of the angiogenic response. By the 6-h mark, nearly all angiogenesis-related genes were significantly upregulated, marking the peak of the angiogenic signature in EGF-treated PC3 cells (Fig.  A). Some of these genes are specifically linked to EGFR signaling, while others are indirectly associated through crosstalk. For instance, EPAS1 (Endothelial PAS Domain Protein 1) plays a crucial role in the EGFR-MET crosstalk and is involved in resistance to tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) . EPAS1 also regulates hypoxia-inducible factor 2-alpha (HIF-2α), which is linked to increased expression of the angiogenic factor thymidine phosphorylase . Similarly, SHC1(Src homology containing domain 2 containing transforming protein1) mediates the activation of key signaling cascades downstream of EGFR, including the RAS/MAPK and PI3K pathways, both essential for prostate cancer progression and angiogenesis, and is associated with poor prognosis in prostate cancer . Additionally, AAMP binds to the intracellular domain of EGFR, promoting its dimerization, trans-autophosphorylation, and downstream signaling. EGFR activation stimulates the production of VEGF, a critical factor in angiogenesis . Furthermore, BSG (CD147) is positively correlated with EGFR and p53 expression and is well-established for its role in promoting angiogenesis through various studies . Notably, co-treatment with LA3IK resulted in the downregulation of angiogenesis-related genes. After 6 h of LA3IK treatment, a reduction in EGFR-related genes was observed. EPAS1 expression decreased by 22% in response to EGF stimulation upon LA3IK treatment, while SHC1 expression showed a 20.4% decrease. BSG also exhibited a 23.1% decrease in gene expression (Fig.  B). Principal-component analysis of the RNA-seq data recapitulates these biological states, resolving the samples into three discrete clusters that correspond exactly to EGF-naïve (0 h), EGF-driven (6 h) and EGF + LA3IK (6 h) conditions (Fig.  C).A subset of these results was validated through qPCR for key players such as ANGPTL4 and VEGFC and confirmed via both qPCR and ELISA assays. ANGPTL4 expression showed a significant 36.2% decrease (fold change: 0.63), and VEGFA expression was reduced by 21% (fold change: 0.79) (Fig.  D and E). Furthermore, LA3IK treatment inhibited the secretion of VEGFA and ANGPTL4 from PC3 cells in response to EGF, demonstrating the compound’s ability to suppress key pro-angiogenic and pro-tumorigenic mediators. These findings highlight the potential of LA3IK as an anti-tumorigenic agent (Fig.  F and G).
 

## LA3IK modifies the EGF microenvironment and inhibits EGFR–ERBB2 interaction without affecting EGFR oligomerization
To investigate whether LA3IK directly alters the structural environment of EGF, we performed a tryptophan fluorescence shift assay. Human EGF contains two conserved tryptophan residues (Trp49 and Trp50) located near the C-terminus (Fig.  A). The emission spectrum of EGF alone displayed a maximum at 357 nm when excited at 295 nm. Upon treatment with LA3IK, we observed significant quenching of tryptophan fluorescence along with a 4–6 nm blue shift in the emission maximum (Fig.  B). In contrast, LA-6, a control peptide that does not inhibit NF-κB signaling, did not induce any shift, and the λmax remained unchanged (Fig.  C and D). These findings suggest that LA3IK specifically interacts with EGF and alters the microenvironment surrounding the tryptophan residues. The observed blue shift indicates that these residues transitioned from a polar (aqueous) to a less polar (hydrophobic) environment, consistent with conformational changes in EGF or direct interaction of LA3IK near Trp49 and Trp50. Such alterations reduce EGF’s binding efficiency to EGFR and modulate downstream receptor signaling. The specificity of this effect was supported by the lack of response with LA-6. To assess whether this conformational change affects EGFR receptor dynamics, we evaluated EGFR oligomerization using fluorescence correlation spectroscopy in EGFR-transfected COS-7 cells. At basal state, the fraction correlated (fc) value was 0.04, reflecting monomeric EGFR. Following EGF stimulation, the fc increased to 0.21, indicating ligand-induced multimerization. Co-treatment with LA3IK did not alter this increase, suggesting that LA3IK does not disrupt EGFR oligomerization (Fig.  E). Despite not affecting EGFR oligomerization, LA3IK significantly reduced EGFR–ERBB2 heterodimer formation. When cells were treated with EGF or preformed EGF–LA3IK complex, co-immunoprecipitation of EGFR followed by ERBB2 immunoblotting revealed a marked reduction in the EGFR–ERBB2 interaction in the EGF plus LA3IK-treated cell lysates (Fig.  A). Quantification across distinct biological replicates showed a decrease in normalized EGFR–ERBB2 interaction from a mean of 1.36 (EGF alone) to 0.88 (EGF + LA3IK), a level comparable to that of untreated cells (mean: 0.86) (Fig.  B). ERBB2 was transiently knocked down in PC3 cells using ERBB2-specific siRNA, and knockdown was confirmed by Western blot (Fig.  C). Cells were then stimulated with EGF in the absence or presence of LA3IK, and EGFR phosphorylation at Y1068 was analyzed. LA3IK did not reduce this phosphorylation, indicating that LA3IK primarily disrupts EGFR–ERBB2 heterodimer formation (Fig.  C, D and F). To visually confirm this specific disruption of the receptor complex, we performed confocal immunofluorescence microscopy. Consistent with the co-immunoprecipitation data, EGF stimulation induced a pronounced co-localization of EGFR and ERBB2 at the plasma membrane (Fig.  G, middle row). However, this EGF-induced spatial interaction was markedly abrogated in cells co-treated with LA3IK, resulting in a clear reduction in co-localized signal (Fig.  G, bottom row), a phenotype resembling the minimal co-localization observed in untreated cells (Fig.  G, top row).These directly corroborates the biochemical data, providing a spatial context for LA3IK’s mechanism of action (Fig.  H).
 

## LA3IK does not interfere with the protective function of phosphorylated EGFR in the mouse liver following EGF injection
We investigated the effects of intraperitoneal (IP) injection of EGF and LA3IK peptide on the phosphorylation states of EGFR in the miceliver . The mice were divided into four groups, each receiving the same dose of EGF (0.2 µg/g). Two of these groups also received EGF in combination with two different doses of LA3IK (40 mg/kg and 60 mg/kg). The control group received PBS (Fig.  A). Our results showed no significant changes in the phosphorylation states of the tyrosine residues in Y1068 and Y1173 in liver tissue following treatment with EGF and LA3IK. This finding suggests that the LA3IK peptide does not interfere with normal EGFR signaling in the liver, indicating a favorable safety profile for this peptide in this context. Western blot analysis for Y1068 revealed no significant changes in its phosphorylation state (Fig.  B and 7C). In mouse liver, Y1068 phosphorylation of the EGFR plays a critical role in hepatocyte function and liver health. This phosphorylation site is essential for hepatocytes to effectively respond to regenerative stimuli, promoting normal liver regeneration processes and maintaining proper metabolic function . Phosphorylation at Y1173 exhibited a similar pattern (Fig.  D and 7E). This site is particularly important for liver physiology, as it facilitates protective responses against environmental insults and promotes hepatocyte survival and regeneration. Enhanced EGFR signaling through Y1173 is vital for counteracting hepatic inflammation and fibrosis induced by harmful environmental stressors, such as polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs) . We were unable to detect other phosphorylated forms of EGFR, such as Y992, with the antibodies. Furthermore, ELISA-based quantification showed that serum EGFR levels remained unchanged among the EGF-treated, EGF plus LA3IK-treated, and control groups, indicating that short-term systemic EGF administration does not significantly influence soluble EGFR levels in mouse serum (Fig.  F).
 

## Discussion
In this study, we identify LA3IK as the shortest peptide reported to date capable of inhibiting EGF-mediated oncogenic signaling in prostate cancer cells. Using PC3 cells, a model characterized by androgen independence, high metastatic potential, and elevated EGFR expression, we demonstrate that LA3IK suppresses EGF-driven tumor progression. Compared to longer peptides or small-molecule inhibitors, LA3IK’s minimal length may offer distinct advantages, including reduced immunogenicity, faster clearance, and potentially fewer off-target effects, making it a promising therapeutic lead . We experimentally demonstrated that MIEN1 interacts with ERBB2, but not EGFR, prompting the hypothesis that an MIEN1-based peptide LA3IK might indirectly influence EGFR signaling by modulating EGFR–ERBB2 interactions. Importantly, ERBB2 is unique within the EGFR (or ERBB or HER) family in that it lacks known ligands and cannot form ligand-dependent homodimers. Instead, it must either heterodimerize with other HER receptors upon ligand binding or, under conditions of overexpression, assemble into ligand-independent homodimers . Given this dependency on heterodimerization for its function, disrupting EGFR–ERBB2 complexes represents a targeted strategy to impair oncogenic signaling . Given the well-established role of EGFR–ERBB2 heterodimerization in amplifying EGF signaling, we examined whether LA3IK could interfere with this interaction. Our findings show that LA3IK selectively disrupts EGFR–ERBB2 complex formation without affecting EGFR oligomerization, revealing a precise mechanism by which LA3IK attenuates EGF-mediated signaling. Supporting this, we confirmed that EGFR phosphorylation at Y1068 peaks at approximately 10- 15 min of EGF stimulation, consistent with rapid receptor activation dynamics. This early receptor activation was followed by robust transcriptional induction of immediate early genes (IEGs) within 30 min, with sustained expression at 1 h, as revealed by RNA-seq. Comparable to EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) such as gefitinib, erlotinib, and lapatinib, which block ATP binding and receptor autophosphorylation, LA3IK inhibits EGFR Y1068 phosphorylation and downstream activation of critical signaling nodes – . Specifically, phosphorylation of NF-κB (Ser536), Src (Y416), and STAT3 (Ser727) was decreased, while phosphorylation of β-catenin (Ser33/37/Thr41) was increased. This β-catenin modification, associated with GSK3β-mediated degradation, suggests that LA3IK disrupts not only upstream receptor activity but also downstream transcriptional programs promoting tumor progression . Consistent with these observations in PC3 cells, we further tested LA3IK in 22Rv1 and C4-2B prostate cancer cell lines to assess whether its activity extends across diverse cellular contexts. In 22Rv1 cells, LA3IK effectively inhibited EGFR Y1068 phosphorylation upon EGF stimulation, mirroring our results in PC3 cells. However, in C4-2B cells, which express EGFR but lack EGF-induced phosphorylation at Y1068, LA3IK treatment still reduced phosphorylation of Src at Y416 . This suggests that LA3IK’s activity is not entirely dependent on EGFR phosphorylation and may modulate oncogenic pathways such as Src signaling independently of EGFR activation. Functionally, LA3IK selectively inhibits EGF-induced migration and invasion of PC3 cells without affecting cell viability or proliferation. The dose-dependent reduction in wound closure and invasion, coupled with preserved proliferation, underscores LA3IK’s potential as a targeted anti-metastatic agent. This contrasts with many anti-cancer agents that concurrently impair migration and proliferation, often leading to unwanted cytotoxicity. For example, while gefitinib inhibits EGF-induced migration, it does so at concentrations that also suppress cell growth . Natural compounds such as curcumin and resveratrol exhibit similar dual effects , . The Selectivity Index calculation indicates that LA3IK possesses superior selectivity in targeting 22Rv1 and PC3 prostate cancer cells relative to erlotinib, which may suggest its potential as a more targeted therapeutic agent in these contexts. The higher SI values support LA3IK’s preferential cytotoxicity to cancer cells while sparing normal endothelial cells, which is an important consideration for minimizing off-target toxicity. Temporal analysis revealed time-dependent induction of angiogenesis-related genes following EGF stimulation. Genes such as ANGPTL4, VEGFC, EPAS1, and MMPs were minimally expressed at baseline but became strongly upregulated by 6 h post-stimulation, reflecting robust pro-angiogenic signaling. Notably, co-treatment with LA3IK significantly reduced the expression of these genes, as validated by qPCR and ELISA, highlighting LA3IK’s potential anti-angiogenic activity.
A key mechanistic insight arose from fluorescence spectroscopy, which revealed a 4–6 nm blue shift in tryptophan emission when EGF was pre-incubated with LA3IK. This shift implies a conformational change in EGF, likely altering the microenvironment of Trp49 and Trp50 without affecting the EGFR-binding interface. The control peptide LA-6, which does not inhibit NF-κB signaling, induced no such shift, confirming LA3IK’s specificity. In contrast to peptides like disruptin, which reduce EGFR dimerization and promote receptor degradation, LA3IK does not alter EGFR oligomerization, suggesting a ligand-targeted rather than receptor-targeted mechanism . Previous studies by Victorovich et al. reported two distinct tryptophan fluorescence peaks (331 nm and 357 nm) for human EGF, whereas we observed a single peak at 357 nm in our smoothed spectra . This supports the notion that LA3IK affects EGF’s microenvironment at a site distinct from the dimerization interface, explaining its effect on EGFR signaling without altering oligomerization. Furthermore, since Tyr44 and Leu47 mediate hydrophobic, and Glu5, Asp11, Asp17, and Arg41 mediate electrostatic interactions with EGFR, the two tryptophans are not directly involved in EGF–EGFR binding . This may account for the absence of significant changes in EGFR oligomerization despite altered EGF conformation. Additionally, because LA3IK is an MIEN1-based peptide—a cancer-specific protein with minimal expression in normal cells, its effects are unlikely to manifest in non-tumorigenic cells such as COS-7, which lack MIEN1 expression. Mechanistically, LA3IK differs from other EGF/EGFR-targeting peptides. While GE11 competes with EGF for EGFR binding and TE-64562 inhibits dimerization and promotes receptor downregulation, LA3IK targets EGF itself, reducing its functional engagement with EGFR and ERBB2 without affecting receptor abundance or oligomerization , . This novel ligand-targeting approach may offer a unique therapeutic window. These complexes are critical drivers of aggressive tumor behavior, therapy resistance, and poor prognosis in cancers such as prostate and head and neck squamous cell carcinoma , . Current strategies increasingly focus on selectively targeting these heterodimers to avoid disrupting normal ErbB receptor function , . LA3IK’s ability to reduce EGFR–ERBB2 interactions to almost the basal levels support this direction and highlights its potential as a next-generation therapeutic agent . Together, the co-immunoprecipitation and confocal microscopy data provide evidence that LA3IK selectively disrupts the EGFR-ERBB2 heterodimer. The co-IP results biochemically quantify the loss of this specific protein–protein interaction, while the confocal images visually capture the consequent dissolution of receptor co-localization at the plasma membrane.
In in vivo, EGFR signaling plays vital roles in normal tissues such as the liver, where phosphorylation at Y1068 and Y1173 is known to regulate hepatocyte survival, regeneration, and metabolism . While LA3IK effectively inhibits EGFR signaling in cancer cells, it does not reduce hepatic EGFR phosphorylation in our model, as robust activation at both Y1068 and Y1173 was observed in all EGF-treated groups, regardless of LA3IK co-administration. This observation is consistent with the high baseline expression of EGFR in hepatocytes and suggests that LA3IK may not interfere with EGFR activity in normal liver tissue under the tested conditions. To further assess the systemic context, we evaluated soluble EGFR levels in serum across four groups—EGF alone, EGF + LA3IK (40 mg/kg), EGF + LA3IK (60 mg/kg), and PBS-treated controls—and found no significant differences, indicating that LA3IK does not alter systemic EGFR levels. Our liver microsome and plasma stability assays confirmed that LA3IK remains stable for over 2 h, supporting its functional relevance in short-term in vivo assays . Given that EGF’s half-life is less than one hour, our 30-min co-injection experiment likely reflects active engagement of both molecules throughout the study . Nevertheless, we acknowledge that direct evidence of cell-type specificity requires comparative analysis across multiple tissues, which will be the focus of future studies. Despite these promising findings, limitations remain. As a small peptide, LA3IK currently requires a higher concentration for effective biological activity. We are actively pursuing structural optimizations, including β-alanine substitutions, to enhance binding affinity and reduce the effective dose by at least tenfold. Furthermore, the broader relationship between MIEN1 and EGFR signaling warrants deeper investigation. Ongoing studies aim to expand these findings across additional prostate cancer cell lines and patient-derived models to better define clinical relevance.
