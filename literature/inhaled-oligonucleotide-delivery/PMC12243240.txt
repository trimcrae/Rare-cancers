## Background
Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is typically characterized by chronic airway inflammation and emphysema. Macrophage polarization plays an important role in COPD, while the precise molecules and mechanisms underpinning it have yet to be fully elucidated. Pulmonary decrease of myotubularin-related protein 14 (MTMR14) expression conduces to the progression of COPD in our prior publication, while the further analysis reveals the differential expression of MTMR14 in alveolar macrophages, whose function and related mechanisms are worth further research. Our study aims to investigate the role and mechanism of MTMR14 in macrophages of COPD.

## Methods
The expression and potential role of MTMR14 in COPD macrophages was explored via bioinformatic analysis and clinical detection, as well as in vivo and vitro experiments. By constructing animal model with Mtmr14 knockout and cell model with the knockdown or over-expression of MTMR14, the effect of MTMR14 on polarization direction of macrophages and the related signaling pathways were elaborated. Indirect co-culture was performed to probe the influence of MTMR14 in the crosstalk between macrophages and alveolar epithelium. The regulation of ubiquitin-proteasome system on MTMR14 expression was investigated via (co-)immunoprecipitation and cycloheximide chase assay.

## Results
Based on analysis from open-access single-cell sequencing data, MTMR14 was down-regulated in macrophages of COPD patients, which was confirmed in clinical specimens, animal and cell models. Meanwhile, MTMR14 was functionally enriched in inflammatory response and macrophage activation. Correspondingly, the knockout of MTMR14 aggravated the pulmonary function decline, emphysema, inflammation and pro-inflammatory macrophage polarization in mice exposed by cigarette smoke (CS). Mechanically, MTMR14 negatively regulated the M1 polarization of macrophages under CS extract (CSE)-stimulation through PI3K/Akt and NF-κB pathways. In addition, damage from macrophages on alveolar epithelium was intensified by the down-regulation of MTMR14 in the formation of emphysema. Finally, TRIM21 was found to down-regulate MTMR14 through ubiquitin-proteasome system in CSE-stimulated macrophages.

## Conclusions
Our findings underscore the mitigative role of MTMR14 on macrophage polarization towards pro-inflammatory phenotype, offering a promising target for prevention and intervention for COPD in clinical settings.

## Background
Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a prevalent chronic respiratory illness marked by persistent respiratory inflammation, structural damage to the terminal airways and alveoli, and an irreversible decline in lung function. COPD has emerged as one of the leading causes of chronic respiratory diseases-attributable deaths worldwide, mainly attributed to the interplay between the body and various environmental insults like cigarette smoke, air pollutant and specific infection [ , ]. As the most abundant immune cells in lung, macrophages, along with the airway epithelium, constitute the first barrier of the lower respiratory tract against external threats [ , ]. Some studies have found that the number of macrophages in the airway, lung parenchyma, alveolar lavage fluid and sputum of COPD patients is increased compared with the control group, and it is positively correlated with the severity of the disease [ ]. However, the researches cannot fully elucidate the exact mechanism by which macrophages are involved in COPD up to now.
Pulmonary macrophages are involved in the defense against inhaled foreign matter, initiation of immune response, regulation of inflammation, and clearance of aberrant cells, thus the dysfunction of macrophages, particularly abnormal polarization, has been thought to have a significant impact on the development of COPD [ , – ]. Specifically, M1 macrophages mainly participate in airway inflammation and the destruction of tissues [ , ], while M2 macrophages mainly emerge at the late stage of the disease and lead pulmonary structures into remodeling [ , ]. Nonetheless, the key molecules and precise mechanisms underlying macrophage polarization in COPD remain to be clarified.
Phosphatases such as phosphatase tensin homolog (PTEN) and protein phosphatase 2 A (PP2A) play an important role in the development of COPD [ , ], while the studies on phosphatases in COPD macrophages are still limited. As one member of myotubularin (MTM)-related protein family, MTMR14 can basically function as a phosphoinositol phosphatase [ ], and several studies have revealed that MTMR14 is also involved in the modulation of a range of biological processes such as autophagy, apoptosis, and inflammation [ – ]. In COPD, we have found that the pulmonary expression of MTMR14 was significantly reduced and in airway epithelium MTMR14 regulated mitochondrial damage and mitophagy to alleviate COPD [ ]. Interestingly, our preliminary study also pointed out that MTMR14 might regulate the function of macrophages in COPD as well. MTMR14 is reduced in macrophages of COPD and potentially related to their activation, but the specific function and molecular mechanisms need to be further studied.
Therefore, this research aims to explore MTMR14-related molecular and pathological mechanisms in the dysfunction of macrophages in COPD, which may further reveal MTMR14 as a potential target in the clinical prevention and treatment of COPD. Firstly, the expression of MTMR14 was evaluated by bioinformatic analysis and verified in clinical specimens, animal and cell models. Next, the effect of MTMR14 on COPD progression was investigated via animal model with Mtmr14 knockout. Moreover, in vitro analysis of the potential mechanism of MTMR14 on the dysfunction of macrophages in COPD and the regulation of ubiquitination on MTMR14 expression was conducted.

## Methods

## Bioinformatic analysis
To comprehensively observe the expression of MTMR14 in COPD, the open-access data of single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNAseq) were obtained from Gene Expression Omnibus (GSE136831) and re-analyzed [ ]. After excluding the cases without cigarette smoke exposure in COPD group or aged less than 40, there were finally 15 controls and 17 COPD subjects. The bioinformatic analysis basically followed the process of Sauler, Maor et al. [ ]. Briefly, after clustering, differential cell expression, and cell population composition, the expression levels of MTMR14 around all the cell types were manifested. The change of specific genes was evaluated by wilcoxon test. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were functionally enriched in Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG). All the analysis was conducted in R studio (version 4.3.2) with packages Seurat, dplyr, multtest, clusterProfiler, topGO, and ggplot2.

## Collection of clinical specimens
All the participants were prospectively enrolled into this study from Tongji Hospital, Wuhan, China, from 2022 to 2024. Based on their smoking history and lung function tests, participants were divided into three groups: non-smokers, smokers, and individuals with COPD. COPD diagnosis was made according to the Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease (GOLD) 2021 guidelines. The process of pulmonary function tests followed the previous literature [ ]. Age- and gender-matched controls were selected from non-smokers and smokers without COPD. Participants were excluded if they had other chronic respiratory conditions, active tuberculosis, or had recently received treatment with inhaled or oral corticosteroids. Table listed the detailed information regarding the clinical patients included in this study.
After consent process, patients were instructed to fast and refrain from drinking for at least 4 h prior to the collection of Bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) to prevent vomiting. Bronchoscope was selected and strictly sterilized. Sterile normal saline, pre-warmed to 37℃ to minimize airway irritation, was used as the lavage fluid. After local anesthesia with lidocaine, the bronchoscope was gently inserted through the nostril or mouth along the posterior pharyngeal wall into the larynx. At the lavage site, an appropriate volume of normal saline (generally 10 ~ 20 mL) was injected through the working channel of bronchoscope to ensure full contact with the alveoli, and then immediately aspirated using the suction tube. This lavage procedure would be repeated 2 ~ 3 times. The lavage fluid was immediately placed in 4℃ and filtered using a 70-µm filter, and the filtrate was centrifuged. The supernatant was aliquoted and stored at -80℃, while the sediment was resuspended in PBS and centrifugated for slides.

## Establishment of model mice
Mtmr14 knockout (Mtmr14-KO) mice and wild-type (WT) littermates were obtained from GemPharmatech Co., Ltd. (Nanjing, China). All mice (8 ~ 12 weeks old) were maintained in a specific pathogen-free environment with a 12-hour light/dark cycle at a temperature of approximately 23℃, and provided with clean padding, ample water and food. Body weights were recorded twice a week to ensure that the animals were maintaining a healthy weight throughout the study.
The mice were randomly assigned to four groups, each consisting of six animals: Air + WT, CS + WT, Air + Mtmr14-KO, and CS + Mtmr14-KO. Throughout the experiment, the mice were exposed to either room air or cigarette smoke (CS) from Marlboro Red cigarettes for about 3 h per day (12 cigarettes/45 minutes/session, repeated 4 times daily), for a total of 5 days per week over a period of 6 months. Upon completion of the exposure, all mice were anesthetized with an intraperitoneal injection of 1% sodium pentobarbital at a dosage of 100 mg/kg to minimize pain and distress during surgical procedures and euthanasia. Lung function parameters, specifically forced expiratory volume at 0.05 s (FEV0.05s) and forced vital capacity (FVC), were assessed using the FlexiVent system (SCIREQ, Canada). In addition, we also measured the compliance and resistance of lung tissues in mice. Respiratory system compliance (Crs) primarily reflects its ability to distend during inspiration, while respiratory system resistance (Rrs) represents the impediment encountered by airflow during respiratory process [ ]. BALF was collected to measure cytokine levels, and total cell counts were performed to differentiate cell types using Liu’s staining method (Baso, Zhuhai, China). The left lungs were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde and then embedded in paraffin, while the right lungs were stored at -80℃.

## Histological analysis
The extent of peribronchial inflammation and mean linear intercept (MLI) was assessed using at least five random photomicrographs of hematoxylin and eosin (HE)-stained sections from each mouse. The evaluation of peribronchial inflammation was performed following a method previously described in the literature [ ].
For immunohistochemical (IHC) staining, paraffin-embedded sections of human or mouse lungs were stained with anti-ZO1 (1:100), anti-Occludin (1:100), or anti-E-cadherin (1:200) antibody. For immunofluorescent (IF) staining, the sections were subjected to the incubation with anti-MTMR14 (1:200) or anti-TRIM21 (1:200) antibody with anti-CD68 (1:200) or anti-F4/80 (1:200) antibody as the marker of macrophages, and then the fluorescent secondary antibodies of corresponding species (Servicebio, Wuhan, China) were applied for staining. Imaging was then captured using the Nikon Spot imaging system (USA) and the semi-quantitative analysis was conducted by ImageJ software.

## Flow cytometry
The cells of mouse lung tissues were collected for flow cytometry to classify macrophage polarization. To isolate pulmonary macrophages, fresh lung samples were sectioned into small pieces, and single cell suspensions were prepared using mechanical dissociation and collagenase. CD11b PC5.5 and F4/80 PE were used to distinguish macrophages, while CD86 PB450 and CD206 APC identified them as M1 and M2, respectively. The steps were in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions, and data analysis was performed using the FlowJo software as instructed.

## Cell culture and treatment
Human monocyte-macrophage cell line THP-1 and human alveolar epithelium cell line A549 were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium with 10% fetal bovine serum in a humidified incubator at 37℃ containing 5% CO2, while mouse macrophage cell line RAW264.7 was cultured in DMEM (high glucose) medium.
Cigarette smoke extract (CSE) was prepared by bubbling the smoke from two burning cigarettes for approximately 10 min to 20 ml of culture medium. The pH was adjusted to 7.4 and the solution was then filtered through a 0.22-µm filter to eliminate bacteria. The supernatant of THP-1 was collected and filtered before adding into the medium of A549. Phorbol 12-myristate 13-acetate (PMA), MG-132, and Cycloheximide (CHX) were purchased from MedChemExpress (Shanghai, China). The cells were treated with the stimulus at different concentrations and duration as described respectively.

## Transfection for cells
In accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions, well-developed cells were counted for the infection of lentiviral vector, purchased from the OBiO Biotechnology Co. Ltd. (Shanghai, China). As for the transfection of small interfering RNA (siRNA) targeting mouse Mtmr14 (5’-AGCCUGCUGAGAUCCUCUA-3’), human TRIM21 (5’-GCAGAGCAUACCUGGAAAU-3’) or negative control, the transfection reagents (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) were mixed with siRNA for 10 min and added into the medium of cells.

## Transcriptome sequencing and data analysis
Total RNA was extracted for RAW264.7 cells transfected with siRNA (Mtmr14-knockdown or negative control). Transcriptome sequencing was performed at Bioyigene Technology Co., Ltd (Wuhan, China). DEGs were determined based on the parameters of |logFC| > 1 and P-adjusted < 0.05. GO and KEGG enrichment was performed in R studio as mentioned above.

## Protein extraction and western blotting
Proteins were extracted from lung tissues or cells using RIPA lysis buffer containing protease and phosphatase inhibitors, and stored in freezers of -80℃. In western blotting (WB), the protein lysate was separated by SDS-PAGE, transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes, and then incubated with primary antibodies for an overnight period. Detection was carried out using a chemiluminescent method (Bio-Rad, CA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The intensity of each band was quantified using ImageJ software.
In immunoprecipitation (IP) and co-IP, cell lysates were incubated with anti-Flag or anti-IgG antibodies on a rotator at 4℃ overnight. Subsequently, magnetic beads were introduced for the IP process at 4℃ for a duration of 2 h. The proteins that had bound to the magnetic beads were then eluted and subjected to further analysis by mass spectrometry (MS) or WB. The mass spectrometry analysis was performed by Wuhan SpecAlly Technology Co., Ltd (Wuhan, China).

## Enzyme linked immunosorbent assay
The levels of TNF-α, IL-1β and IL-6 in cell supernatants or mouse BALF were determined respectively with Enzyme linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits following the manufacturer’s protocol.

## Transepithelial electrical resistance
A549 was seeded on transwell chamber with 0.4-µm micropore (Labselect, Anhui, China) and stimulated by the supernatant of THP-1. Once the cells grew full of the chamber, transepithelial electrical resistance (TEER) was measured by Millicell (USA) ERS 3.0 Digital Voltohmmeter in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions.

## Antibodies and other reagents
Antibodies against iNOS was procured from Abcam (Cambridge, MA, USA) and Affinity Biosciences (OH, USA). Antibodies against CD68, F4/80, MTMR14, Arg-1, Akt, NF-κB p65, E-cadherin, TRIM21, Flag, Pan-ubiquitin, proSP-C, β-Actin, IgG and GAPDH were acquired from Proteintech (Wuhan, China). Antibodies against p-Akt and p-p65 were procured from Cell Signaling Technology (Danvers, MA, USA). Antibodies against ZO-1 and Occcludin were procured from ABclonal (Wuhan, China). Fluorescent antibodies against CD11b with PC5.5, F4/80 with PE, CD86 with PB450 and CD206 with APC were procured from Biolegend (San Diego, CA, USA). ELISA kits of mouse IL-6 were obtained from R&D Systems (Minneapolis, MN, USA). ELISA kits of human IL-6 and mouse IL-1β were purchased from Dakewe Bio-engineering (Shenzhen, China). ELISA kits of human TNF-α and IL-1β were obtained from 4Abio Bio-technology (Jiangsu, China). ELISA kit of mouse TNF-α was purchased from Mmbio Bio-technology (Jiangsu, China). Magnetic beads were procured from MedChemExpress (Shanghai, China). DNA extraction kit was sourced from TIANGEN Biotech (Beijing, China).

## Statistical analysis
Data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD) unless otherwise indicated. Statistical significance was determined using Student’s t-test for comparisons between two groups or one-way ANOVA for multiple group comparisons. All statistical analyses and graph generation were performed using GraphPad Prism 9 software (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, United States). A two-sided P-value less than 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

## Results

## MTMR14 was down-regulated in macrophages of COPD
In the previous study, we have found that MTMR14 was down-regulated in the lung tissues of COPD patients [ ]. In order to further explore the key cells of MTMR14 in COPD, we re-analyzed the scRNAseq data of COPD (GSE136831), in which the expression of MTMR14 was notably concentrated in alveolar macrophages and significantly reduced in the COPD patients (Fig.  A-B), while the DEGs between MTMR14− and MTMR14+ alveolar macrophages were functionally enriched in inflammation response and macrophage differentiation (Fig.  C). The decrease of MTMR14 expression in macrophages was verified in non-smokers and COPD patients by immunofluorescence of lung tissues and BALF (Fig.  D, Fig. A), which was equivalent to that in CS-exposed mice (Fig.  E, Fig. B). Correspondingly, macrophages RAW264.7 and THP-1 expressed much lower MTMR14 under the concentration-gradient inducement of CSE (Fig.  F).

 

## Mtmr14 deficiency aggravated lung damages in CS-exposed mice
To test if the decrease of MTMR14 expression participants into the development of COPD, we established the Mtmr14-KO mice (Fig. A-C) and performed 6-month CS exposure on them with WT mice. Compared with the CS-stimulated WT mice, the pulmonary function decline (Fig.  A-B), emphysema (Fig.  C-D), peribronchial inflammation (Fig.  E-F), and the inflammatory cell counts in BALF (Fig.  G) were all aggravated in CS-stimulated Mtmr14-KO mice. As for macrophages, both the results of flow cytometry and WB found more macrophages with M1 markers in CS-stimulated Mtmr14-KO mice (Fig.  A-B), while the cytokines related to M1 macrophage polarization also increased significantly (Fig.  C). Therefore, it could be speculated that the decrease of MTMR14 expression reinforced the polarization of macrophages into M1 during the development of COPD.

 

 

## MTMR14 negatively regulated M1 polarization through PI3K/Akt and NF-κB pathways
To prove the above conjecture, macrophages with the over-expression (OE) or knockdown (KD) of MTMR14 were established respectively. Stimulated by CSE, the M1 macrophages reflected by iNos and cytokines were even more in Mtmr14-KD RAW264.7 than the control sublines (Fig.  A-B). Meanwhile, the M1 polarization was much suppressed in the RAW264.7 over-expressing Mtmr14 with the significant decrease of M1-related cytokines (Fig.  C-D). The similar phenomenon occurred to THP-1 with the knockdown or over-expression of MTMR14 (Fig.  E-H). The negative regulation of MTMR14 on M1 polarization of macrophages was verified in COPD.

 

DEGs between macrophages with and without the knockdown of Mtmr14 were analyzed to find out the specific mechanism in this negative regulation. GO enrichment further indicated the influence of MTMR14 on immune response and inflammation (Fig.  A), while KEGG enrichment resulted in two signaling pathways closely related to M1 polarization, including PI3K-Akt and NF-κB (Fig.  B). Compared with the control sublines, the phosphorylation of Akt and p65 induced by CSE was elevated in the MTMR14-KD macrophages sublines (Fig.  C, E) and suppressed in the MTMR14-OE sublines (Fig.  D, F). In sum, MTMR14 negatively regulates the M1 polarization of macrophages mainly through PI3K/Akt and NF-κB pathways.

 

## MTMR14 regulated the crosstalk between macrophages and alveolar epithelium
Emphysema, represented by the destruction of alveolar wall and the abnormally elevated compliance of respiratory system, was worsened in the Mtmr14-KO mice (Fig.  B-D), but the expression level of MTMR14 showed no difference in the alveolar epithelium of COPD patients (Fig. A) and CSE-stimulated A549 (Fig. B). Since macrophages play an important role in the formation of emphysema, they could also be the functional cells of MTMR14 in CS-related emphysema. The co-culture experiment was designed as Fig.  A to test the crosstalk between macrophages and alveolar epithelium. TEER was measured for the epithelium barrier function of A549, which was damaged by the supernatant of CSE-stimulated macrophages. The deterioration was worsened by the knockdown of MTMR14 (Fig.  B) in macrophages and rescued by its over-expression (Fig.  C). Correspondingly, the intercellular junction markers showed the consistent changes in alveolar epithelium (Fig.  D-E) and verified in the lung tissues of model mice by WB and IHC (Fig.  F-G). Therefore, macrophages with the down-regulated MTMR14 in COPD further damages the conjunction among alveolar epithelium and may aggravate emphysema.

 

## TRIM21 down-regulated MTMR14 through ubiquitin-proteasome system in macrophages
IP-MS showed the potential combination of MTMR14 and the E3 ubiquitin ligase Tripartite-Motif Protein 21 (TRIM21) in macrophages (Table ), which was verified by co-IP (Fig.  A). Thus, we guessed that MTMR14 might be down-regulated by TRIM21 through the ubiquitin-proteasome system. Firstly, the proteasome inhibitor MG-132 elevated the expression of MTMR14 (Fig.  B) while IP directly proved the ubiquitination of MTMR14 (Fig.  C). Secondly, in the lung tissues of COPD patients and CS-stimulated mice, macrophages expressed more TRIM21 on IF (Fig.  D-E). The stimulation of CSE also elevated the expression of TRIM21 in macrophages (Fig.  F). Finally, the knockdown of TRIM21 truly weakened the ubiquitination of MTMR14 (Fig.  G) and prolonged its half-life duration in macrophages (Fig.  H). Thereout, we can conclude that the down-regulation of MTMR14 in COPD macrophages is caused by TRIM21 through the ubiquitin-proteasome system.

 

## Discussion
In the present study, we confirmed the decrease of MTMR14 expression in pulmonary macrophages of COPD patients and experimental COPD models. We found that MTMR14 negatively regulated the M1 polarization of macrophages under CSE-stimulation and alleviated inflammation through PI3K/Akt and NF-κB pathways. Damage from macrophages on alveolar epithelium was intensified by the down-regulation of MTMR14, which might contribute to the formation of emphysema. Moreover, we have proven that TRIM21 down-regulated MTMR14 through ubiquitin-proteasome system in CSE-stimulated macrophages. These critical findings offer a promising target for clinical prevention and therapy for COPD.
As one of the most important roles in classical immune system, macrophages are very plastic and possesses many functions such as engulfing foreign pathogens, presenting antigens, secreting cytokines, and clearing abnormal cells [ , ]. As for the phenotype of macrophages during activation, the classic classification is the polarization system, which divides macrophages into M1 and M2 mainly based on its marker molecules [ , ]. In brief, M1 macrophages is pro-inflammatory and promotes immunity, while M2 macrophages is anti-inflammatory and promotes repair [ ]. As a system directly contacting with external environments, lung has abundant macrophages, which is either tissue-resident or bone marrow-derived [ ]. During the development and acute exacerbation of COPD, the number of macrophages increases even more with their phenotype changing dramatically [ , ]. The early stage of COPD is characterized by chronic airway inflammation, and gradually turns into the destruction and abnormal repair of lung tissues. It can be speculated that macrophages with M1 polarization participants in the early stage and results in the long-lasting inflammation, which was verified in this research. In the lung tissues of CS-stimulated mice and CSE-stimulated macrophage cell lines, the markers and cytokines related to M1 increased significantly while the polarization was negatively regulated by MTMR14. In COPD, the decrease of MTMR14 expression in macrophages aggravates airway inflammation through M1 polarization. MTMR14 is found as a potential target in M1 macrophage polarization of the chronic inflammation in COPD.
As structural cells, airway epithelium releases various cytokines in response to damage from cigarette, recruiting neutrophils and other inflammatory cells to trigger local inflammation [ ]. This process primarily serves as an early event of COPD, leading to increased mucus secretion and airway remodeling [ ]. In contrast, macrophages actively undergo pro-inflammatory activation by engulfing inhaled harmful particles and can induce systemic inflammatory responses through circulatory system [ , ]. The secretory profiles of airway epithelium and macrophages also differ. Macrophages release a broader range of inflammatory mediators and can also secrete multiple matrix metalloproteinases (MMP), thus being more directly associated with the development of emphysema [ ]. Moreover, induced by infections, the inflammatory response of macrophages is closely related to acute exacerbations of COPD, further exacerbating respiratory symptoms and pulmonary dysfunction in patients [ ]. We have discovered that MTMR14 exerts protective effects on both airway epithelium and macrophages in COPD, which may hold significant clinical value.
Nevertheless, M2 macrophages are also noteworthy in COPD [ ]. Some studies believed that the macrophages contributing to emphysema were mainly M2-like [ ]. In this research, the M2 marker Arg-1 and CD206 also increased in the lung tissues of CS-stimulated mice, which was consistent with recent researches in human bodies [ , ], while Mtmr14 knockout showed no significant influence on M2 macrophages in vivo. However, M2 polarization did not occur in CSE-stimulated macrophages in vitro. Under the complex regulatory network in vivo, macrophages can manifest higher heterogeneity and polarize into different directions [ ]. In fact, the double-positive macrophages have been found with markers of both M1 and M2 [ ]. Relatively, the macrophages are rather identical in vitro and show single stable polarization under the certain stimulation. In regard of COPD, Liu Jiaxin et al. have found that macrophages co-cultured with CSE-stimulated bronchial epithelial cells would majorly turn into M2, but this conclusion is still controversial since the similar cellular experiment showed the polarization into M1 in another study [ , ]. In addition, supernatants from bronchial epithelial cells treated by both CSE and lipopolysaccharide can induce macrophages to express several markers of polarization, most of which belong to M2 [ ]. Therefore, the stimuli and their duration should be considered more carefully in macrophage experiments, and in this research short-term direct stimulation of CSE majorly induce macrophages into pro-inflammatory M1 rather than M2.
In response to diverse stimuli, the signaling pathways within macrophages are activated, thereby regulating target genes to execute various functions and manifest specific phenotype [ ]. The present study revealed that MTMR14 modulates M1 macrophage polarization by influencing the PI3K/Akt and NF-κB signaling pathways. As phosphoinositol phosphatase, MTMR14 potentially regulates PI3K/Akt [ ]. For instance, in cases of cardiac hypertrophy, MTMR14 has the ability to interact directly with Akt, thereby dephosphorylating it, which was proven with transgenic mice and bi-directional co-IP experiments [ ]. Other Akt phosphatases such as PTEN, although well-recognized, remains poorly studied in the context of COPD macrophages. One study has suggested that down-regulation of PTEN promotes M2 macrophage polarization in emphysema by activating PI3K/Akt pathway [ ]. The discrepancy among conclusions might be attributed to the isoforms of Akt. In details, the knockout of Akt1 promotes various features of M1 polarization, including nitric oxide synthesis, IL-12β expression, responsiveness to LPS, and bacterial clearance capacity, while the knockout of Akt2 results in a phenotype similar to M2, such as up-regulation of M2 marker molecules like Arg-1 [ ]. For example, PTEN inhibition facilitated the activation of Akt1 while inhibiting Akt2 in periodontitis, which also induces the M2 polarization of macrophages [ ]. As for NF-κB, MTMR14 deletion further promoted its activation after hepatic ischemia-reperfusion [ ]. More studies are needed to clarify the mechanism of MTMR14 on these two pathways and the relationship between them in macrophages [ ]. There have emerged many drugs focusing on signaling pathways to regulate macrophages in diseases, but their clinical effects were limited [ , ]. One of their possible drawbacks is the compensatory effects between different signaling pathways. Therefore, the common upstream molecules of these pathways such as MTMR14 can be better targets and show more powerful effects.
Besides inflammation, another important trait of COPD is the structural destruction of alveolar walls, which results in pulmonary emphysema. Macrophages also contribute to this pathological process [ ]. In the lung tissues of COPD patients, activated macrophages obviously gather around the lesion of emphysema [ ]. Polarized macrophages can secret various enzymes such as elastase and metalloproteinase to destroy the airway walls directly, while their inhibitors from macrophages are down-regulated in response to CS [ , ]. The current research found that the supernatant of CSE-stimulated macrophages can destroy epithelial barrier and the related intercellular junction molecules of alveolar epithelial cells, which corresponds to the formation of emphysema. Furthermore, the knockdown of Mtmr14 in CS-stimulated mice worsened emphysema, but at the cellular level CSE showed no significant influence on the expression of MTMR14 in alveolar epithelial cells. Thus, we guessed that MTMR14 participants in emphysema through macrophages. As a matter of fact, the over-expression of MTMR14 in macrophages alleviated its damage on alveolar epithelial conjunction, and vice versa. This further highlights the potential clinical effect of MTMR14 in emphysema of COPD.
The ubiquitin-proteasome system plays a crucial role in modulating protein expression levels [ ]. Ubiquitination on MTMR14 has been firstly proved in this research. Furthermore, ubiquitin-proteasome system regulated MTMR14 in macrophages and TRIM21 is the upstream ubiquitinase of MTMR14. TRIM21-mediated ubiquitination has been found to be involved in the pro-inflammatory phenotype, polarization direction, and cytokine secretion of macrophages in obesity, breast cancer, and myocardial infarction [ – ], but the function of TRIM family is still poorly understood in COPD. The increase and insoluble accumulation of ubiquitinated proteins leads to cellular senescence and emphysema [ , ], while the abnormal activation of ubiquitin-proteasome system results in skeletal muscle atrophy of COPD [ , ]. This research can help explore the role of ubiquitin-proteasome system in macrophage phenotype and the development of COPD. Given the crucial role of TRIM21 in various diseases, its small molecule inhibitors are currently under development. For example, Bezafibrate shows the ability to block the interaction between TRIM21 and EPHX1, thereby inhibiting tumor proliferation and gemcitabine resistance [ ]. Since MTMR14 expression is mainly influenced by TRIM21-mediated ubiquitination, the focused intervention might be plausible to regulate MTMR14 for the therapy of COPD.
There are still several limitations in this research. Firstly, the phenotype of macrophages is sophisticated, for which the classic polarization system is rather limited. Researchers have pointed out that the polarization of macrophages, especially in the lung tissues from COPD patients, is not really polarized but present a spectrum-like distribution [ ]. The relationship of MTMR14 with other polarization directions and even other phenotype of macrophage needs more research to clarify the role of macrophages in COPD. Secondly, as for the crosstalk between macrophages and alveolar epithelial cells, the erosion of epithelial conjunction and its role in emphysema need to be verified in vivo. Furthermore, while many molecules from macrophages such as MMP-9 and MMP-12 have been found as the mediators of emphysema [ , ], the specific molecules regulated by MTMR14 in macrophages are yet to know. Thirdly, the modification site of TRIM21-mediated ubiquitination on MTMR14 is still unclear. Last but not the least, since we have proven the importance of MTMR14 in airway epithelium and macrophages of COPD so far [ ], the administration of drugs focused on MTMR14 or TRIM21 such as recombinant proteins, over-expression plasmid, and small molecule inhibitors might be a powerful treatment on COPD, for which airway drug delivery may be a feasible method to avoid the hypersensitivity reactions and off-target effects of systemic administration of drugs [ ].

## Conclusions
In summary, this study has elucidated the role of MTMR14 in macrophages with M1 polarization in the context of COPD. The effects of MTMR14 on macrophages are associated with both the two major pathological changes in COPD: airway inflammation and emphysema. These findings highlight the clinical translational value of MTMR14 for the prevention and intervention of COPD. Additionally, the ubiquitination of MTMR14 have been firstly confirmed in this research, which deepens the understanding of this molecule. Future researches need to focus on the development of intervention strategies targeting MTMR14 to achieve its therapeutic application for COPD.
