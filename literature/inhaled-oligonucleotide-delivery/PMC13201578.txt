Microglial/macrophage activation and neuroinflammation are key events following ICH, but the molecular mechanisms of mediator regulatory function are not well understood. Cluster of differentiation 204 (CD204), a macrophage scavenger receptor, regulates multiple features of microglial/macrophage physiology. However, its function in regulating the innate immune response and microglial/macrophage M1/M2 polarization in ICH has not been addressed. In the current study, we aimed to investigate whether CD204 is involved in the regulation of M1/M2 microglial/macrophage polarization and neuroinflammation following ICH. The ICH mouse model was employed. CD204 siRNA was intracerebroventricularly administered, and tamibarotene was intraperitoneally administered for intervention. The neurobehavioral assessments, RT-PCR, Western blot, immunohistochemistry, Nissl staining, and ELISA were performed. CD204 is upregulated and regulates microglial/macrophage M1/M2 polarization and neuroinflammation in ICH. Mechanistically, inhibition of CD204 markedly decreases microglial/macrophage M2-like polarization while increases M1-like polarization, which exacerbates the brain injury after ICH. Tamibarotene exerts beneficial effects in ICH mice through CD204 activation. CD204 plays an important role in M1/M2-macrophage/microglial polarization, providing novel potential therapeutic targets for the regulation of immune and inflammatory disorders in ICH.

## Background
Intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) is a severe neurological disorder associated with high mortality and significant long-term disability in survivors . Notably, the pathophysiology of ICH is highly complex, and it serves as a major risk factor for various neurological complications, such as epilepsy, neurodegenerative diseases, and mental disorder . ICH severely affects not only the lives of the patient, but also their families and have a substantial impact at various domains, such as political, economic, socio-cultural. However, there remains a lack of comprehensive understanding regarding the involvement of this notorious disease.
As the brain’s resident immune cells, microglia rapidly respond to ICH by migrating to and congregating at the injury site . Extensive research demonstrates that activated microglia/macrophages modulate the injured brain’s microenvironment through their phenotypic diversity – . In recent studies, the diverse activation states of microglia/macrophages have gradually become a research focus; nevertheless, the traditional M1/M2 dichotomy continues to provide researchers with a practical framework to investigate their roles in central nervous system disorders , . Microglia/macrophages exhibit two primary polarization states: the classical (M1) and alternative (M2) activation phenotypes , . Following injury, pro-inflammatory (M1) microglia/macrophages exhibit upregulated secretion of inflammatory cytokines . Meanwhile, the anti-inflammatory phenotypes secrete neurotrophic factors and anti-inflammatory cytokines, which are associated with tissue repair , . Therefore, modulating microglial/macrophage polarization toward the anti-inflammatory phenotype may mitigate ICH-induced neurological damage, highlighting its therapeutic potential.
CD204, a polymorphic membrane protein expressed by phagocytic cells, plays an essential role in controlling neuroinflammation through its actions in microglia and macrophages . Emerging evidence indicates CD204 potentially modulates nuclear factor kappa-B signaling through interactions with multiple ligands . For instance, research demonstrates that tamibarotene-induced CD204 activation suppresses the M1-like polarization observed in stimulated microglia , . Besides, CD204 drives M2 macrophage polarization through modulation of arginine and proline metabolism, leading to AMPK/mTOR pathway activation and subsequent gastric cancer progression . CD204-dependent signal transduction constitutes a prerequisite for the optimal engulfment of early apoptotic cells mediated by M2c-polarized macrophages . However, the role of CD204 in modulating innate immunity and controlling microglial/macrophage M1-M2 phenotypic switching during intracerebral hemorrhage remains unexplored.
In the present study, we hypothesized that CD204 is a key modulator of microglial/macrophage M1/M2 polarization and neuroinflammation in the early stage of ICH. Our investigation revealed that CD204 blockade promotes a phenotypic shift toward M1-like polarization in microglia/macrophages, consequently aggravating neuroinflammatory responses and exacerbating cerebral injury post-ICH. Conversely, CD204 signaling activation enhances M2-like polarization while suppressing M1-like polarization in microglia/macrophages, ultimately providing neuroprotective effects in ICH.

## Materials and methods

## Compliance with ethical standards
Male C57BL/6 mice were used in this study. All animal procedures were approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital, Zhejiang University School of Medicine. All methods were carried out in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations. All methods are reported in accordance with ARRIVE guidelines.

## Animal preparation
Male C57BL/6 mice (6–8 weeks old, weighing 20–25 g; sourced from SLAC Laboratory Animal Co., Ltd) were utilized in the current research. Housing conditions included a thermostatically controlled environment with regulated humidity, a 12-hour light/dark cycle, and unrestricted availability of food and drinking water.
ICH induction was conducted in accordance with established experimental methodologies . Briefly, mice were anesthetized with 2% isoflurane and ventilated with 1% isoflurane. The autogenous blood was acquired from the tail artery to establish the ICH model. The mice were then placed on the stereotaxic frame. A 0.2-mm craniotomy was performed 2.5 mm lateral to midline and anterior to the right coronal suture. A 26-gauge hypodermic needle was stereotaxically advanced 3.5 mm in the ventral direction into the right basal ganglia, followed by infusion of 30 µL autologous whole blood at a constant rate of 3.0 µL/min using a calibrated microinjection system.
Euthanasia was induced by an overdose of anesthetic administered via intraperitoneal injection of 0.4 mL of a 1% sodium pentobarbital solution (approximately 150 mg/kg) followed by CO₂ inhalation.

## Intracerebroventricular (i.c.v.) injection
Prior research has established that intracerebroventricular (i.c.v.) administration of siRNA effectively suppresses target gene expression in the brain, achieving knockdown efficiencies between 50% and 80%. The i.c.v. delivery of siRNA was carried out following an established protocol . Briefly, a burr hole with a diameter of 1 mm was created on the right hemisphere of the skull (1.0 mm lateral and − 0.25 mm anterior-posterior (AP) relative to bregma). Either CD204 siRNA or a scrambled siRNA control (500 pmol, Thermo Fisher Scientific), complexed with a transfection reagent (Engreen Biosystem Co., Ltd.) in a final volume of 2 µl, was infused into the ipsilateral ventricle (coordinates: 1.0 mm lateral and − 0.25 mm AP to bregma, 2.5 mm dorsoventral (DV) below the skull surface). Infusion proceeded at a rate of 0.5 µl/min. To minimize backflow, the injection needle was left in place for an additional 5 min before withdrawal. The burr hole was subsequently sealed with bone wax, and the surgical incision was sutured. Following the procedure, mice were individually housed in recovery cages. The i.c.v. siRNA injections were administered one day prior to and 10 min after the induction of ICH.

## Tamibarotene administration
Tamibarotene was administered intraperitoneally (IP) 1 h, 1 day and 2 days after ICH at a dose of 1 milligrams per kilogram . Control animals received intraperitoneal injections of a 0.5% carboxymethyl cellulose solution (Merck, CAS No. 9000-11-7).

## Assessment of neurobehavioral function
Neurobehavioral evaluations were conducted 3 days after ICH induction by two investigators blinded to the experimental conditions, following an established protocol. Three behavioral tests were employed to assess functional deficits , .
In the forelimb placing test, mice were gently restrained by the torso to allow unrestricted movement of the forelimbs. Each mouse underwent ten trials per forelimb. The percentage of successful placements of the forelimb onto the edge of the countertop following vibrissae stimulation was recorded.
For the cylinder test, animals were individually placed into a transparent cylinder (8 cm in diameter, 25 cm in height) and allowed to rear freely until 20 independent wall contacts were achieved. The initial forelimb used for each contact was documented. Asymmetry was quantified using the formula: (number of right forelimb contacts − number of left forelimb contacts)/(number of right + left + both forelimb contacts), with higher positive scores indicating more pronounced left-sided hemiparesis.
The modified Garcia test was administered using a 21-point scoring system encompassing seven sub-tests: spontaneous activity, axial sensation, vibrissae proprioception, limb symmetry, lateral turning, forelimb walking, and climbing ability.

## Quantitative RT-PCR
Total RNA was extracted from both sham-operated and injured brain tissues with TRIzol reagent (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) following the supplier’s recommended protocol . Subsequently, 1 µg of RNA from each sample was reverse-transcribed into complementary DNA (cDNA) using the PrimeScript™ RT Reagent Kit (Takara Bio Inc, Shiga, Japan). Quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) was then carried out using SYBR® Premix Ex Taq™ (Takara Bio Inc) on a 7300 Plus Real-Time PCR System (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Each 20 µl reaction mixture contained 10 µl of PCR master mix, 5 pmol each of forward and reverse primers, 1 µl of cDNA template, and nuclease-free water to adjust the volume. The amplification protocol comprised an initial denaturation at 95 °C for 20 s, followed by 40 cycles of denaturation at 94 °C for 15 s, annealing at 56 °C for 30 s, and extension at 72 °C for 25 s. All reactions were performed in triplicate. Threshold cycle (CT) values were recorded, and the relative mRNA expression of target genes was determined using the 2^−ΔΔCT method, with β-Actin serving as the endogenous control. Gene expression results are presented as fold changes relative to the sham-operated control group. The nucleotide sequences of all primers used are provided in Supplemental Table .

## Western blotting
Western blot analysis was performed as previously described . Briefly, brain tissue was perfused with 0.1 mM phosphate-buffered saline (pH 7.4) after euthanasia and ipsilateral basal ganglia sampled. The following primary antibodies were utilized: a rabbit polyclonal anti-CD204 (dilution 1:1000; Abcam, ab314227) and a mouse monoclonal anti-β-Actin (dilution 1:2000; Abcam, ab8226). Corresponding horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibodies included goat anti-rabbit IgG and goat anti-mouse IgG (dilution 1:5000; ZSGB-BIO).

## Immunofluorescence
Following established protocols , mice were subjected to deep anesthesia and transcardially perfused with 20 mL of ice-cold 0.1 mol/L phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), followed by perfusion with 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA). Brains were carefully extracted and post-fixed in 4% PFA overnight at 4 °C, then cryoprotected in 30% sucrose solution for 72 h at the same temperature. Subsequently, the brain tissue was sectioned coronally at a thickness of 10 μm for further analysis. Sections were rinsed with PBS and blocked with 10% donkey serum supplemented with 0.3% Triton X-100 for 1 h at room temperature. Thereafter, they were incubated overnight at 4 °C with the following primary antibodies: anti-CD204 (1:100, Abcam, ab314227), anti-Iba1 (1:500, Abcam, ab5076), anti-CD16 (1:100, Invitrogen, MA1-7633), anti-CD68 (1:200, Bioss, bs-6049R), anti-Arg-1 (1:500, Proteintech, 16001-1-AP), and anti-CD206 (1:500, Abcam, ab64693). After thorough washing with PBS, the sections were incubated with species-appropriate fluorescent secondary antibodies for 1 h at 37 °C. Finally, nuclei were counterstained with DAPI (Abcam, ab104135), and imaging was performed using a fluorescence microscope (Leica, Mannheim, Germany). Three brain sections were examined per mouse, and each section was examined under three fields (perihematoma area) of vision to acquire the means numbers of target cells using Image J software (NIH).

## Nissl staining
Nissl staining was conducted in accordance with the manufacturer’s protocol . Coronary Sect. (20 μm thickness) were deparaffinized and rehydrated, followed by incubation in Nissl Staining Solution (C0117, Beyotime) at 37 °C for 5 min. Morphologically intact neurons were identified by the presence of a large cell body, abundant cytoplasm, and prominent Nissl substance. In contrast, damaged cells exhibited shrunken somata, pyknotic nuclei, and a noticeable reduction or absence of Nissl bodies. For each animal, quantitative analysis was performed across five nonconsecutive sections (spaced 100 μm apart) from the ipsilateral cortex, with five randomly selected fields evaluated per section. Cell counts were carried out using ImageJ software, and data from three mice per group were included for statistical analysis.

## ELISA
The concentrations of IL-1β, IL-10, and TNF-α were quantified using ELISA following a previously established methodology . Brain tissue homogenates from mice were prepared in accordance with the instructions provided with the commercial ELISA kits (BOSTER, China). The total protein concentration of each sample was determined using a BCA Protein Assay Kit (Beyotime, China) to ensure accurate normalization. Final cytokine levels are expressed as picograms per milligram of total protein.

## Statistical analysis
Data are represented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Following verification of normal distribution (Shapiro-Wilk test) and homogeneity of variance (Levene’s test), between-group differences were assessed by one-way ANOVA with Tukey’s post hoc multiple comparisons. The statistical analyses were implemented in SPSS 22.0 (IBM), adopting P < 0.05 as the significance criterion.

## Results

## Temporal mRNA expression changes in microglial and macrophage
Microglia/macrophage polarization states (M1/M2) after ICH are conventionally defined by their unique surface marker expression and cytokine/chemokine production profiles. Microglial/macrophage activation states were analyzed via RT-PCR using perilesional tissue RNA collected at 12 h, 1 d, 3 d, and 7 d after ICH or sham surgery (Fig.  A–F). Biomarkers characteristic of M1 polarization state, including CD16 (Fig.  A), CD68 (Fig.  B), and iNOS (Fig.  C), were gradually increased over time and arrive peak at 3 days after ICH, then declined on day 7. Expression levels of the examined M2-associated marker genes, including ARG1 (Fig.  D), CD206 (Fig.  E), and IL-10 (Fig.  F), began to be significantly upregulated at 12 h, 3 days, and 1 day after ICH, respectively, and remained elevated for at least 7 days after ICH. These results showed activation of M1 and M2 microglial/macrophage phenotypes in the acute and subacute periods after ICH.

 

## Dynamic changes and cellular localization of CD204 following ICH induction
Initial quantitative assessment of CD204 protein expression was conducted in lesioned tissues across multiple post-ICH time intervals. No statistically significant differences were observed in protein expression among multiple time points in the sham-operated group, and according to previous studies, postoperative day 3 represents a critical stage when brain edema reaches its peak. As illustrated in Fig.  A,B, the temporal expression profile of CD204 demonstrated: (i) marked elevation by 12 h post-ICH, (ii) peak expression at day 3, and (iii) complete resolution to sham levels by day 14. Immunofluorescent colocalization studies were implemented to characterize CD204 expression patterns with microglia/macrophages (identified by IBA-1 immunoreactivity) at 72 h following hemorrhagic insult. Figure  C,D shows CD204 was basally expressed in sham microglia/macrophages but significantly upregulated in activated cells near the injury site at day 3 post-ICH.

 

## CD204 suppression exacerbates neurological deficits post-ICH
To delineate CD204’s mechanistic role in ICH progression, targeted suppression of CD204 expression was achieved through siRNA transfection. CD204 protein expression was significantly reduced following siRNA treatment, as evidenced by Western blot analysis (Fig.  A). Post-ICH functional recovery was monitored employing the Forelimb Placing Score, Cylinder Test performed 3 days after ICH. When compared to the sham group, ICH mice exhibited significantly worse in all neurobehavioral tests (Fig.  B,D). Notably, CD204 gene silencing resulted in marked exacerbation of post-ICH neurological impairments, with siRNA-treated animals demonstrating significantly worse neurobehavioral performance compared to control siRNA group at day 3, based on Modified Garcia Score (Fig.  B), Forelimb Placing Score (Fig.  C), and Cylinder Test (Fig.  D). Neuronal loss was significantly increased in ICH-affected brain regions compared to sham-operated controls (Fig.  E), and CD204 siRNA significantly elevated the level of devastaed neuron at 3 days post-ICH, versus vehicle siRNA controls (Fig.  E). Results demonstrate the beneficial role of CD204 upregulation in post-ICH recovery.

 

## CD204 affected microglial/macrophage M1-M2 polarization following ICH
CD204’s role in microglial/macrophage M1/M2 polarization and neuroinflammation post-ICH was investigated by assessing its expression in cells within ICH-affected regions. Effects of CD204 siRNA on microglial/macrophage polarization were assessed at 72 h post-ICH by co-staining IBA-1 with M1 (CD16, CD68) and M2 (Arg-1, CD206) markers in perihematomal regions. The percentage of CD16 or CD68 and IBA-1 double-positive M1-like cells was significantly increased on day 3 after ICH, which was further elevated in the CD204 siRNA group (Fig.  A,B,D,E). M2-like cell populations (Arg-1+/Iba-1 + and CD206+/Iba-1+) were significantly increased at day 3 post-ICH versus sham, but decreased following CD204 siRNA treatment compared to control siRNA group (Fig.  A,B,D,E). qRT-PCR results confirmed group-dependent variations in CD68, CD16, Arg-1, and CD206 mRNA levels, consistent with immunofluorescence data (Figs.  C,F and C,F). Integration of these findings reveals that CD204 serves as a molecular regulator of microglial polarization post-ICH, and its inhibition perturbs the physiological transition between activation states.

 

 

## Effect of CD204 on inflammation
ICH induced significant increases in TNF-α, IL-1β and IL-10 mRNA levels as measured by RT-PCR (Fig.  A–C). In response to CD204 siRNA administration, the upregulated levels of IL-1β and TNF-α were markedly increased, and the upregulation level of IL-10 was markedly decreased. Moreover, complementary to transcriptional profiling data, ELISA-measured protein concentrations of TNF-α, IL-1β and IL-10 have similar changes in all experimental conditions (Fig.  D–F). Results confirm CD204’s involvement in post-ICH inflammation regulation, with its suppression altering the inflammatory balance.

 

## CD204 activation attenuated ICH-associated functional impairments
To delineate CD204’s neuroprotective mechanisms, therapeutic intervention with the CD204 agonist tamibarotene was initiated at 1 h, 24 h, and 48 h post-ICH. Tamibarotene treatment significantly increased CD204 protein levels as detected by Western blot (Fig.  A), versus the vehicle control group. Tamibarotene treatment significantly repressed the transcriptional activation of classical M1 markers (CD16, CD68, iNOS), exhibiting lower expression levels than vehicle-treated animals (Fig.  E–G). Besides, comparative analysis revealed that tamibarotene treatment induced significant upregulation of characteristic M2-like phenotype markers (ARG1, CD206, and IL-10), as demonstrated in Fig.  H–J. Pharmacological intervention with tamibarotene markedly improved neurological function by day 3 following ICH, using the Modified Garcia Score (Fig.  B), Forelimb Placing Score (Fig.  C), and Cylinder Test (Fig.  D).

 

However, silencing CD204 via siRNA effectively blocked tamibarotene’s regulatory effects on CD204 protein abundance (Fig.  A), M1-like markers (Fig.  E–G), and M2-like markers (Fig.  H–J) in ICH mice. In addition, Modified Garcia Score (Fig.  B), Forelimb Placing Score (Fig.  C), and Cylinder Test (Fig.  D) showed worsened neurological outcomes in the experimental group receiving Tamibarotene with CD204-targeting siRNA, relative to the Tamibarotene plus scrambled siRNA control cohort. Collectively, these data indicate that CD204 facilitates phenotypic polarization of microglia/macrophages, attenuates neurological damage, and enhances functional recovery after ICH.

 

## Discussion
Neuroinflammation is widely regarded as a key modifiable factor in brain injury pathogenesis after ICH . In this study, we show that CD204 upregulation exerts neuroprotective effects in an ICH mouse model by modulating microglial/macrophage M1/M2 polarization and attenuating neuroinflammatory responses. At the mechanistic level, CD204 facilitates the induction of microglial/macrophage M2 polarization. CD204 blockade shifts the microglial/macrophage polarization balance from neuroprotective M2 toward detrimental M1 phenotype, resulting in worsened functional outcomes and cerebral pathology following ICH.
ICH represents a dynamic neurological disorder that significantly influences both immediate tissue damage and progressive neurodegeneration . ICH is pathophysiologically distinct from other CNS injuries due to the presence of blood components—particularly thrombin, hemoglobin, and iron—which trigger unique inflammatory cascades . Therapeutic interventions for this condition remain particularly challenging due to its pathophysiological heterogeneity and intricate molecular cascades . Of note, microglial/macrophage activation and neuroinflammation are hallmark features of ICH pathophysiology . As the resident innate immune cells in the CNS, microglia usually respond around hematoma after ICH, where they can even sustain for a long time following ICH . ICH is well-established to induce a robust immune response, driven by the upregulation and secretion of multiple inflammatory cytokines and chemokines, which collectively amplify neuroinflammatory cascades . Microglia and macrophages exhibit phenotypic plasticity in CNS pathologies, adopting diverse activation states contingent upon varying pathophysiological contexts . Following CNS insults such as ICH, these cells undergo dynamic polarization across a continuum spanning pro-inflammatory M1 to anti-inflammatory M2 phenotypic extremes . The classically activated M1 phenotype is generally associated with pro-inflammatory responses, while the alternatively activated M2 phenotype typically mediates anti-inflammatory functions . Multiple signaling cascades, exemplified by the CD204 pathway, coordinately regulate the dynamic equilibrium of M1/M2 polarization states , , . In this study, we demonstrate for the first time that CD204 specifically modulates microglial/macrophage polarization in the context of ICH-induced neuroinflammation. Moreover, we show that the protective effects of CD204 are closely linked to the resolution of hemorrhage-induced secondary brain injury.
Emerging evidence implicates CD204 as a critical regulator of inflammatory processes across diverse pathological conditions, including autoimmune disorders, allergic responses, cerebrovascular accidents, oncogenesis, metabolic syndrome, and physiological homeostasis , – . In fracture repair, researchers observed delayed intramembranous ossification in CD204 knockout mice . Furthermore, macrophage MSR1 mediated PI3K/AKT/GSK3β/β-catenin signaling increased ability to enhance osteoblast differentiation potential . CD204 activation after subarachnoid hemorrhage can result in improved neurological deficits and attenuated brain edema, blood brain barrier permeability, while CD204 inhibition can suppress neurological function and aggravate brain edema, blood brain barrier permeability . During tumor-protective immunity, CD204, functioning as a non-Toll-like receptor pattern recognition molecule, serves as an endogenous negative modulator of TLR4-dependent immune responses. This regulatory mechanism holds significant translational potential for the design of TLR-focused immunomodulatory therapies . Experimental stroke in mice induces characteristic neuroinflammation accompanied by neurological dysfunction. CD204 blockade markedly intensifies the inflammatory cascade, reduces microglial reactivity, and amplifies neurological impairment in this setting . Conversely, the neuroinflammatory cascade initiated by CD204 post-spinal cord injury demonstrates reparative potential, contributing to exacerbating damage rather than functional neuronal recovery . Genetic ablation of CD204 confers protection against metabolic syndrome in murine models, manifested by reduced hepatic foam cell accumulation, attenuated liver inflammation, ameliorated dyslipidemia and glucose intolerance, along with modified hepatic lipid metabolic pathways . These observations indicate that the functional role of CD204 may vary across different disease contexts. The underlying mechanisms for these discrepancies are likely associated with distinct disease pathogenesis and require further investigation. Based on existing literature, CD204 is known to interact with ligands such as oxidized lipids and apoptotic cells, potentially influencing signaling cascades including NF-κB and MAPK pathways , . Despite its emerging importance in neuroinflammation, the functional contribution of CD204 to ICH pathogenesis remains poorly characterized. To address this knowledge gap, we systematically investigated CD204-mediated mechanisms using a well-established murine ICH model.
Consistent with prior findings, quantitative analysis revealed a marked upregulation of CD204 expression in peri-hematomal brain tissue following ICH induction. CD204 exhibited rapid upregulation following ICH induction, showing statistically significant increases within 12 h of injury onset, achieving peak expression at the 3-day timepoint, and subsequently demonstrating gradual attenuation. All the data suggested that CD204 is involved in the acute and subacute stages of ICH. A previous report demonstrated that CD204 is expressed at a low level in normal brain , . However, under neuropathological conditions, CD204 expression is markedly upregulated in response to diverse CNS insults . Western immunoblotting of brain lysates provided biochemical validation of these findings, confirming elevated CD204 protein expression patterns in ICH-affected tissue. The well-established enrichment of CD204 in reactive microglia positions it as a critical mediator of inflammatory signaling within the CNS microenvironment. Corroborating these observations, immunofluorescence analysis demonstrated predominant CD204 localization within activated microglia and macrophages in peri-hematomal regions post-ICH.
Consequently, we delineated CD204’s pathophysiological role in modulating hemorrhage-induced neuroinflammation. Post-ICH microglial activation demonstrated pronounced M1 polarization, characterized by significant upregulation of pro-inflammatory markers iNOS, CD16, and CD86 in peri-hematomal regions , . Consistent with established literature , our findings demonstrate that CD204 inhibition exacerbates neuroinflammation post-ICH by polarizing microglia toward a pro-inflammatory phenotype, evidenced by elevated CD16/CD68 expression and concomitant suppression of anti-inflammatory markers Arg1/CD206. CD204 inhibition demonstrably exacerbates post-ICH neuroinflammation by dysregulating microglial/macrophagic polarization dynamics. Pharmacological activation of CD204 significantly alleviated ICH-induced neuroinflammation and secondary brain injury, contributing to improved neurological outcomes, by polarizing microglia/macrophages away from pro-inflammatory states toward anti-inflammatory functional phenotypes. CD204 activation demonstrably attenuates biosynthesis of key inflammatory mediators , . However, all observed therapeutic benefits were abrogated following with CD204 inhibition. While CD204 knockdown significantly attenuated the effects of tamibarotene, this does not rule out the involvement of other parallel or compensatory mechanisms. We also systematically investigated the underlying molecular mechanisms of CD204 governing this phenomenon regarding the modulation of microglial/macrophage phenotypic polarization. Inhibition of CD204 abolished the role of tamibarotene, whose therapeutic effect attenuated ICH-induced neuroinflammation and secondary neuronal damage, ultimately enhancing functional recovery through microglial/macrophage phenotype reprogramming from pro-inflammatory (M1) to anti-inflammatory (M2) states.
While our findings demonstrate CD204 activation confers neuroprotection by reducing neuroinflammation and improving functional recovery in mice ICH models, several limitations warrant consideration. First, CD204 likely participates in multiprotein complexes mediating post-ICH neuroinflammation. Systematic identification of its binding partners through co-immunoprecipitation or proximity ligation assays would clarify these molecular associations. Second, while our findings suggest that CD204 is involved in mediating the effects of tamibarotene, the precise mechanism by which it acts upstream of inflammatory cascades in ICH remains unclear. Further studies using conditional knockout models or selective agonists are necessary to delineate the specificity and centrality of these CD204-dependent effects. Third, oxidative stress and white matter injury are important factors that influence neurological recovery following ICH. However, our study design prioritized elucidation of CD204-mediated microglial/macrophage polarization mechanisms, deliberately circumventing analysis of oxidative stress and axonal injury components. Therefore, systematic investigation of CD204’s mechanistic contributions to ICH pathophysiology represents a critical direction for future research.

## Conclusions
CD204 upregulation in ICH confers neuroprotection by shifting microglial/macrophage polarization toward M2 phenotypes. Conversely, CD204 inhibition promotes detrimental M1 polarization, intensifying secondary damage and neurological impairment.
