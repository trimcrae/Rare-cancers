A powerful driver of stem cell activity and hence tissue regeneration, Wnt signaling can promote fibroblast proliferation and activation, leading to fibrosis, while prolonged Wnt signaling is potentially carcinogenic. In the lung, Wnt signaling is essential for alveolar stem cell activity and alveolar regeneration, which is impaired in lung fibrosis. Our study offers an attractive strategy for treating pulmonary fibrosis, with general implications for regenerative medicine.

## Graphical abstract
 
Xi and colleagues have developed a lung epithelial-specific, reversible Wnt activation strategy that promotes alveolar regeneration and inhibits lung fibrosis in both preventive and therapeutic settings. This novel approach offers a promising therapeutic strategy for regenerative medicine and the treatment of fibrotic diseases.

## Introduction
Wnt/β-catenin signaling is an evolutionarily conserved signaling essential for embryonic development, tissue homeostasis, and regeneration. , , Dysregulation of Wnt signaling has been implicated in developmental defects, cancer, fibrosis, and neurodegenerative disorders. , , Numerous Wnt targeting therapies have been investigated for decades, but so far, they are not clinically available.
Wnt/β-catenin signaling is essential for adult stem cell activity and thus tissue regeneration after injury. However, systemic prolonged Wnt activation may lead to undesirable effects, such as tumorigenesis, and also promote fibroblast proliferation and activation into myofibroblasts, leading to fibrosis. , Indeed, Wnt/β-catenin signaling is activated in the fibroblasts of fibrotic diseases, including idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), systemic sclerosis-associated pulmonary fibrosis, and liver cirrhosis. , , Therefore, tissue-specific, transient, and reversible Wnt activation is required to develop regenerative therapies and avoid pro-fibrotic effects.
To overcome the limitations of conventional drug molecules, recently gene editing has been harnessed to target β-catenin, the central effector of Wnt signaling. The stability of β-catenin is controlled by the destruction complex, comprising of Axin, APC, casein kinase 1 (CK1), and glycogen synthase kinase 3α/β (GSK3α/β). , In the absence of Wnt ligands, β-catenin was phosphorylated by CK1 and GSK3α/β at a series of highly conserved Ser/Thr residues, leading to β-catenin recognition by β-TrCP and subsequent ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation. When a Wnt ligand binds to its receptors Frizzled (Fzd) and LRP5/6, β-catenin phosphorylation is inhibited and thereby β-catenin accumulates, translocates into the nucleus to form complexes with TCF/LEF, and activates Wnt target genes expression.
DNA base editing was used to destroy the phosphorylation site of β-catenin to upregulate Wnt/β-catenin signaling in the inner ear of mice. Compared with permanent, irreversible DNA editing, RNA editing is transient and reversible, as it only corrects the cell function at the RNA level without altering the DNA sequence. The CRISPR-Cas13 family of RNA-targeting endonucleases have been developed for RNA degradation in various organisms. , RNA base editors were also developed by fusing catalytically inactivate Cas13 with an adenine or cytidine deaminase. , , , , RNA base editors such as RNA Editing for Specific C to U Exchange or C > U RNA Editor were used to introduce a T41I or S33F mutation to the phosphorylation sites of β-catenin to activate Wnt signaling. However, these approaches have not yet been applied in vivo. Instead, CasRx (Cas13d), as one of the most compact RNA-guided, RNA-targeting Cas enzymes, exhibits high knockdown efficiency and specificity in vitro , , and can be delivered by adeno-associated virus (AAV) vectors for in vivo applications. , , 
IPF is a chronic and progressive lung disease characterized by excess accumulation of extracellular matrix (ECM) deposited by (myo)fibroblasts. , Currently, the two medicines approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), pirfenidone and nintedanib, only alleviate symptoms, rather than reverse the disease. Lung transplantation remains the only cure. IPF is still a huge unmet medical need.
IPF arises as a result of repeated alveolar epithelial injuries which leads to aberrant wound healing response. , Alveolar type II cell (AT2s) are alveolar stem cells, which self-renew and give rise to alveolar type I cells (AT1s) during homeostasis. They can proliferate quickly after injury and differentiate into AT1s to repair the damaged alveolar regions. However, there is a significant loss of AT2 cells in the lungs from IPF patients, , , as well as the bleomycin-induced mouse lung fibrosis model. , And AT2s from IPF lungs have impaired renewal capacity, which results in severe fibrosis. 
Wnt/β-catenin signaling is essential for AT2 stemness and expands the AT2 pool after injury. , , , , AT2-specific β-catenin deletion exacerbates bleomycin-induced lung fibrosis. Moreover, Wnt activation seems to attenuate experimental emphysema, which exhibits impaired alveolar repair capacity as well. These all suggest that the activation of Wnt/β-catenin signaling specifically in lung epithelium can promote alveolar repair and regeneration, and potentially treat lung fibrosis and other alveolar diseases.
Here, we developed a lung epithelial-specific Wnt activation strategy by intratracheal (IT) instillation of AAV6 vector to selectively deliver CasRx-gRNA into lung epithelium, where it reversibly activates Wnt signaling by knocking down Axin1 and Axin2, the essential scaffold of the β-catenin destruction complex. Lung epithelial-specific β-catenin stabilization promotes AT2 proliferation and ameliorates bleomycin injury-induced lung fibrosis in both preventive and therapeutic settings, without inducing any obvious toxicities in the lung, liver, and small intestine.

## Results

## CasRx-mediated Axin1 and Axin2 knockdown activates Wnt/β-catenin signaling in vitro
As Axin is the scaffold protein and the rate-limiting factor of the β-catenin destruction complex, , we sought to knockdown Axin to activate Wnt/β-catenin signaling using CRISPR-CasRx. There are two vertebrate Axin genes, Axin1 and Axin2. Both act as negative regulators of canonical Wnt signaling. Axin1 is constitutively expressed, whereas Axin2 is induced by canonical Wnt signaling and, therefore, functions as part of a negative feedback loop to restrain Wnt activity. We designed guide RNAs (gRNAs) specifically targeting Axin1 or Axin2 using Cas13design tool as previously described and transfected the gRNA constructs together with CasRx in a mouse cell line, Neuro-2a cells, to examine the knockdown efficiency ( A). Of 15 gRNAs targeting Axin1 and 5 gRNAs targeting Axin2, gRNA14 targeting Axin1 and gRNA22 targeting Axin2 showed the highest knockdown efficiency against Axin1 and Axin2, approximately 78% and 70%, respectively, compared with non-targeting (NT) guide ( B and 1C). Immunoblotting of Neuro-2a cell lysates with Axin1- and Axin2-specific antibodies confirmed the protein levels were decreased ( D). We then performed RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) to explore any off-target transcriptional changes. Consistent with previous reports, CasRx-mediated RNA knockdown revealed no significant off-target effects, but efficiently reduced Axin1 and Axin2 mRNA levels ( E and 1F). 
Next, we used TOPFlash reporter to examine Wnt/β-catenin signaling activity in Neuro-2a cells. CasRx-mediated RNA editing targeting either Axin1 or Axin2 induced a 2-fold increase in TOPFlash activity; however, the combination of both gRNAs induced a 200-fold activity, similar with that induced by a β-catenin mutant (S33A, S37A, T41A, T45A, hereinafter referred to as “β-catenin (S > A)”) that is resistant to proteasomal degradation and thus constitutively active ( G). Consistent with TOPFlash activity, both cytoplasmic and nuclear β-catenin protein is stabilized by combination of both gRNAs ( H). These data show that dual Axin1 and Axin2 knockdown mediated by CasRx is able to stabilize β-catenin and induce Wnt/β-catenin activity in cultured cells.

## AAV6 shows high infection efficiency and specificity in lung epithelial cells
Recombinant AAVs are widely used for in vivo gene delivery, due to high infection efficiency and long-lasting expression. , , , To deliver CasRx and gRNA specifically in lung epithelium, we chose IT injection of AAVs. AAV5, AAV6, and AAV9 have been shown to efficiently infect lung epithelial cells. , , , So, we administrated 8- to 12-week-old C57BL/6 mice IT with 1 × 1011 viral genomes (vg) of AAV5, AAV6, and AAV9 expressing GFP (AAV-GFP) and examined the infection efficiency by flow cytometry and immunofluorescence staining 14 days after virus injection ( A). AAV6 showed the greatest infection efficiency and GFP expression level among the three AAVs, with 10% of all the cells in the lung expressing GFP ( B) and the mean GFP intensity of AAV6 group was 10-fold of AAV9 group and 4-fold of AAV5 group ( C). The results of immunofluorescence staining were consistent with that of flow cytometry ( D), showing that AAV6 exhibits the greatest infection efficiency in the lung. 
To further examine the infected cell types, we used cell surface markers to separate alveolar epithelial cells (EPCAM+ ITGB4−), airway epithelial cells (EPCAM+ ITGB4+), immune cells (CD45+), endothelial cells (CD31+), and fibroblasts (EPCAM−CD45−CD31−), as previously reported. IT delivery of AAV5, AAV6 and AAV9 specifically targets lung epithelial cells, but not immune cells, endothelial cells, or fibroblasts ( E). Both AAV5 and AAV6 achieved roughly 50%–60% infection efficiency in alveolar and airway epithelial cells ( E). These results collectively support that AAV6 has the greatest infection efficiency and specificity in lung epithelium among the three AAVs we tested.
Immunofluorescence staining of AAV6-infected lung sections showed that most of GFP signal colocalized with SPC-expressing AT2s in alveoli ( F). In the airway epithelial cells, GFP signal was detected in acetylated-tubulin-labeled ciliated cells and CC10+ club cells ( G). These results show AAV6 mainly targets AT2 cells, ciliated cells, and club cells in the lung, consistent with previous reports. 
It has been shown that AAV6.2 (AAV6 F129L) and AAV6.2FF (AAV6 F129L, Y445F, and Y731F) have higher transgene expression in lung epithelial cells. , However, their infection efficiencies were inferior to AAV6 in our hands ( A and S1B). We also performed a time course experiment by harvesting the lungs at days 7, 14, 21, and 28 after AAV6 delivery to determine the expression dynamics. The expression levels peaked at day 21 and decreased at day 28, with minimal differences between day 14 and 21. Therefore, we chose day 14 for further experiments ( C).

## AAV6-mediated Wnt activation promotes AT2 proliferation after bleomycin injury
We then investigated whether IT AAV6 delivery of CasRx could knock down Axin1 and Axin2 and activate Wnt/β-catenin signaling in lung epithelium in vivo. As we have observed, the AAV-GFP expression level decreased over time. To lineage trace the cells that were infected by AAVs, we introduced a Cre-loxP system ( A). Specifically, an AAV6 vector encoding a Cre recombinase under CMV promoter was IT delivered into Ai9 mice, which have a loxP-flanked STOP cassette followed by red fluorescent protein tdTomato at Rosa26 locus. The tdTomato is expressed only after Cre-mediated recombination. 
Due to the 4.7-kb limit of package size for AAVs, the Cre recombinase cannot be packed with CasRx in the same virus. We had to separate CasRx and Cre in two vectors. We first tested dual-AAV injection in Ai9 mice. We mixed 1 × 1011 vg of AAV6-Cre and 1 × 1011 vg of AAV6-GFP for IT injection and the lungs were harvested after 14 days ( A). AAV6-Cre induced approximately 19.5% tdTomato+ cells in all the live cells, and AAV6-GFP labeled approximately 11.1% of live cells. Dual infection did not affect either virus infection, with tdTomato+ cells approximately 19.5% and GFP+ cells approximately 13.5% ( B–S2D). There were 13.1% cells expressing both tdTomato and GFP, indicating that approximately 70% of tdTomato+ cells were expressing GFP ( D). Thus, the dual AAV system can efficiently lineage label the infected cells.
Next, CasRx and gRNAs targeting Axin1 (g14) and Axin2 (g22) were constructed in AAV6 vector (referred as AAV6-CasRx-Axin1/2) and co-injected with AAV6-Cre into Ai9 mice. Fourteen days later, tdTomato+ AT2s were flow sorted and Axin1/2 knockdown efficiency was determined by qPCR analysis. Compared with NT control, CasRx-Axin1/2 downregulated both Axin1 and Axin2 ( B). As expected, phosphorylated β-catenin (Ser33/Ser37/Thr41) in the tdTomato+ AT2s decreased and the total β-catenin protein level increased ( C), at a similar extent as the positive control, β-catenin (S > A) ( D and 3E). These results show that CasRx-mediated Axin1/2 mRNA degradation stabilizes β-catenin by preventing its phosphorylation and degradation, similarly as a constitutively active β-catenin mutant.
Then we examined whether stabilization of β-catenin could enhance AT2 proliferation using an organoid culture system. TdTomato+ AT2s were flow sorted 14 days after AAV6-Cre and AAV6-CasRx-Axin1/2 or AAV6-CasRx-NT co-injection into Ai9 mice and then cultured in three-dimensional organoids ( F). In AAV6-CasRx-Axin1/2 group, there were significantly more and larger colonies ( G–3I). We added 5-ethynyl-2′-deoxyuridine (EdU) in the culture medium to label the dividing cells. The immunofluorescence staining of EdU labeling showed that CasRx-mediated Axin1/2 knockdown increased AT2 proliferation in organoid culture ( J and 3K).
Wnt/β-catenin activity is required to expand the AT2 pool after injury. , , , , Therefore, we further tested whether CasRx-mediated Axin1/2 knockdown could promote AT2 proliferation in vivo after bleomycin injury. AAV6-CasRx-Axin1/2 and AAV6-CasRx-NT were co-injected with AAV6-Cre into Ai9 mice followed by bleomycin injury 14 days later. Bleomycin is a commonly used reagent to induce pulmonary fibrosis in mice due to causing DNA damage. A single administration of bleomycin through IT injection damages alveolar epithelial cells, inducing inflammatory response and fibroblast activation, migration, and proliferation, leading to the accumulation of ECM components in the lung parenchyma. After bleomycin injury, the mice were given EdU every 2 days by intraperitoneal injection starting from day 1 ( L). The proliferation of AT2s was detected by flow cytometry and immunofluorescence staining at day 8. There were more EdU+ tdTomato+-proliferating AT2s in the CasRx-Axin1/2 group, as well as in the constitutively active β-catenin (S > A) group ( M). Consistent with flow cytometry results, there were more proliferating AT2s and more total number of tdTomato+ AT2s detected by staining in the damaged alveolar regions ( N and 3O). These data show that AAV6-mediated delivery of CasRx-Axin1/2 efficiently activate Wnt/β-catenin signaling in AT2s and promote AT2 proliferation in organoids and in vivo.

## Lung epithelial-specific Wnt activation prevents bleomycin-induced lung fibrosis
We then tested whether enhancing AT2 proliferation by Wnt activation could ameliorate lung fibrosis. AAV6-CasRx-Axin1/2 and AAV6-CasRx-NT, as well as the positive control AAV6-β-catenin (S > A) and its vehicle control AAV6-GFP were IT delivered followed by bleomycin injury 14 days later ( A). The mice were harvested 14 days after bleomycin. Compared with CasRx-NT or GFP, CasRx-Axin1/2 and β-catenin (S > A) downregulated ECM gene expression including Collagen 1a1 (Col1a1), Collagen 1a2 (Col1a2), Collagen 3a1 (Col3a1), and Fibronectin 1 (Fn1) ( B–4E). Consistent with the transcriptional expression, the protein content of Fibronectin was decreased by AAV-induced Wnt activation ( F and 4G). Hydroxyproline content, which indicates the amount of collagen, was significantly decreased by CasRx-Axin1/2 or β-catenin (S > A) treatment ( H). In addition, Ashcroft scoring was performed to quantify the severity of lung fibrosis, as previously described. The mean Ashcroft score was significantly lower and the fibrotic areas were smaller in the CasRx-Axin1/2 and β-catenin (S > A) group ( I and 4J). Moreover, myofibroblasts accumulation was decreased by lung-epithelial-specific Wnt activation as examined by ACTA2 and PDGFRβ immunofluorescence staining ( K and 4L). In collective, these results demonstrate that Wnt/β-catenin signaling activation in lung epithelial cells, mostly AT2s, inhibits bleomycin-induced lung fibrosis. 
Bleomycin injury significantly damages alveolar epithelial cells, including both AT1s and AT2s. Then we asked whether Wnt activation promotes alveolar repair and regeneration after injury. The SPC protein, hallmark of AT2s was upregulated by CasRx-Axin1/2 and β-catenin (S > A) compared with CasRx-NT or GFP ( F and 4G). Immunofluorescence staining of lung sections showed that SPC+ AT2 areas increased from approximately 15% in the control groups to approximately 25% in the CasRx-Axin1/2 or β-catenin (S > A) group ( M and 4N). There were more SPC+ AT2s in the alveoli as well ( M and 4O), consistent with more AT2 proliferation after bleomycin injury ( N). In addition, both Rage+ AT1 cell area and AT1 cell number were increased in CasRx-Axin1/2 or β-catenin (S > A) group ( M–4O), suggesting more AT2-AT1 differentiation. These data show that Wnt activation in lung epithelium could promote alveolar repair and regeneration and prevent lung fibrogenesis.

## Lung epithelium-specific Wnt activation after bleomycin injury ameliorates bleomycin-induced lung fibrosis
The forementioned results show that Wnt activation in lung epithelium prevents fibrogenesis. To further determine whether epithelial-specific Wnt activation has any therapeutic effects after fibrosis initiation, the AAVs were injected 6 days after bleomycin injury, when inflammatory response and alveolar damage already happened ( A). The expression of ECM genes, Col1a1, Col1a2, Col3a1, and Fn1 in CasRx-Axin1/2 and β-catenin (S > A) were all downregulated compared with control groups ( B–5G). Hydroxyproline content also significantly decreased ( H). Masson’s trichrome stain showed that Wnt activation after injury reduced the deposition of collagen fibers and Ashcroft score ( I and 5J). And there were significantly less myofibroblast accumulation ( K and 5L). These all demonstrate that activating Wnt/β-catenin signaling in lung epithelium after bleomycin injury is able to attenuate the severity of fibrosis. The SPC protein level as well as AT2 and AT1 covered areas, AT2 and AT1 cell number were restored by Wnt activation after injury ( F, 5G, and 5M–5O), indicating that activating Wnt/β-catenin signaling in surviving AT2s after injury could promote alveolar regeneration and further attenuate lung fibrosis. 

## Wnt activation in lung epithelium does not induce obvious side effects within 12 months
AAV is reported to achieve long-term transgene expression in quiescent cell types, up to a few years. Prolonged Wnt activation may lead to tumor formation. To test whether long-lasting expression of CasRx-Axin1/2 or β-catenin (S > A) cause any side effects, the mice were monitored over 12 months after AAV infection and the lung, liver, and small intestine were harvested ( A, top). The body weight of mice increased steadily during the course of 12 months, with no significant difference among the groups ( A, bottom). AAV6-GFP expression in the lung sections was decreased from 2 to 4 months, and undetectable in AT2s at 12 months ( B). To monitor the changes of Wnt/β-catenin activity, tdTomato+ AT2s from the AAV6-CasRx-Axin1/2 and AAV6-Cre co-injected Ai9 mice were harvested at different time points and blotted for β-catenin. Upregulation of β-catenin protein levels was not observed at 2, 4, and 12 months ( C), in contrast with 2 weeks ( C), suggesting CasRx-mediated RNA editing induced a transient Wnt activation, which cannot persist for 2 months. 
We further examined the lung morphology using hematoxylin and eosin staining. There were no obvious changes in lung morphology and structure after Wnt activation ( D and A). Moreover, AAV administration did not induce immune cell infiltration ( D). The liver and small intestine also showed normal morphology at 2, 4, and 12 months ( E, 6F, B, and S3C) and no GFP signals were detected at 2 and 4 months ( D and S3E), suggesting lung-specific delivery. In summary, IT delivery of AAV specifically targets lung epithelial cells without affecting other organs and does not induce obvious tumor formation.

## Discussion
In this work, we showed that AAV-mediated lung epithelial-specific delivery of CasRx-Axin1/2 effectively activates Wnt/β-catenin signaling in lung epithelium and ameliorates bleomycin-induced lung fibrosis in both preventive and therapeutic settings. Among different AAV serotypes, we found that AAV6 exhibits high infection efficiency and specificity in lung epithelium. AAV6-mediated CasRx-Axin1/2 expression in lung epithelial cells downregulates Axin1 and Axin2, thus stabilizing β-catenin and promoting AT2 cell proliferation after injury. More interestingly, increased AT2 proliferation prevents bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis and attenuates fibrosis after its initiation. Taken together, our work provides a potential strategy for promoting alveolar regeneration and treating pulmonary fibrosis.
Wnt signaling has been reported to be activated in fibrotic diseases. Many attempts have been made to treat lung fibrosis via inhibiting Wnt signaling, including small molecules inhibiting β-catenin dependent gene transcription, ICG-001 and iCRT3, and β-catenin siRNA. , , , However, although Wnt signaling activity in fibroblasts is involved in fibroblast proliferation and differentiation into myofibroblasts, , which leads to fibrosis, it is also essential for maintaining alveolar stem cell activity. , , Blocking AT2 self-renewal would impair alveolar repair and regeneration after injury, leading to more severe lung fibrosis. Therefore, how to specifically activate Wnt signaling in AT2s but not in fibroblasts is critical for therapeutic use.
Here, we used IT delivery to achieve lung epithelial-specific Wnt activation. Coincidentally, a recent study aimed at the same goal but used a different strategy, using lung epithelial-specific Fzd agonists (Fzdag). The researchers revealed that AT2 cells and fibroblasts express distinct Fzds to respond to Wnt ligands. Fzd5 and Fzd6 are expressed in AT2s, Fzd1 in fibroblasts and Fzd4 in endothelial cells. Using Fzd agonists, which engages the Fzd and Lrp5/6 to form a ternary complex to mimic the Wnt ligands, , they were able to activate canonical Wnt signaling in AT2s with Fzd5ag or Fzd6ag, and demonstrate that canonical Wnt activation in AT2s could promote AT2 proliferation and survival of mice after bleomycin injury, which is consistent with our results. However, no obvious effect on fibrosis was observed in their study.
The different effects observed in their study and our work could be due to differences in the potency of Wnt activation or the disease severity induced by a single dose versus three doses of bleomycin injury. In their study, Fzd5ag or Fzd6ag induced a robust Wnt activation in AT2 organoid culture, comparable with the effects of the GSK3α/β inhibitor CHIR99021. In our TOPFlash assay, CHIR99021 could induce a 4,000-fold induction (data not shown), whereas CasRx-Axin1/2 or β-catenin (S > A) activates approximately 200- or 800-fold, respectively, considerably less potent than CHIR99021. Stronger activation of Wnt signaling in their study might hinder AT2 differentiation into AT1s. In our study, we observed more AT2 proliferation after CasRx-Axin1/2 treatment, as well as more AT1 expansion, which was not reported in their study. Furthermore, our study used a single dose of bleomycin injury, while they used three doses of bleomycin. A different severity degree of injuries may evoke varying levels of inflammation and alveolar damage, potentially contributing to the disparate outcomes.
As prolonged Wnt activation may lead to tumor formation, we used an RNA editing tool that specifically affects genes at the RNA level, rather than altering DNA, thereby limiting the duration of its effects. AAV6-CasRx-mediated Wnt activation stabilized β-catenin at 2 weeks ( C), but not at 2 months ( C), contrasting with AAV6-GFP expression, which persisted for at least 4 months ( B). This highlights the transient regulatory potential of RNA editing. Additionally, Axin2, a downstream target of the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, is likely induced in the edited cells, which may counterbalance the effects of RNA editing and further restrict the duration of Wnt activation. Therefore, RNA editing targeting Axin offers potential safety advantages over overexpression of active β-catenin.
CasRx belongs to Cas13 family, which has been recently expanded, comprising Cas13a, Cas13b, Cas13c, Cas13d, Cas13X, Cas13Y, and Cas13bt. , , , , , , , As the fourth reported Cas13 enzyme, the size of CasRx is small, suitable for package into AAVs. Since its discovery, CasRx has been used to knock down target genes for in vivo applications. , , But it is worthy to note that studies have pointed out the collateral cleavage activity of Cas13 system in recent years, both in human cells and mouse model. , , , The protein variants of Cas13d and Cas13X, named hfCas13d and hfCas13X, have been developed to lower collateral cleavage while preserve high knockdown efficiency. RNA-seq analysis in our study revealed satisfying knockdown specificity, without obvious off-target effects, except for one gene being downregulated by the gRNA targeting Axin2.
The excellent safety profile together with high transduction efficiency has established AAVs as a widely used platform for in vivo gene therapy. A number of AAV-based therapies have been approved by FDA in the past few years. AAVs have been applied to treat SFTPB deficiency in mouse model and to generate severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2-susceptible mice model. Our work showed that IT delivery of AAV5/6/9 specifically target lung epithelial cells, without affecting other cell types in the lung or other organs, possibly due to physical barrier of the epithelial layer. Thus, intrapulmonary AAV delivery provides advantages for targeting lung epithelium; however, it cannot be used for endothelial or mesenchymal cell targeted delivery.
The lung is a highly quiescent tissue in homeostasis, but responds quickly after injury by activating regional stem/progenitor cells to participate in epithelial repair and regeneration. Multiple epithelial stem/progenitor cell populations have been reported, including basal cells, club cells, pulmonary neuroendocrine cells, AT2s, and distal airway stem/progenitor cells such as bronchioalveolar stem cells, , , lineage-negative epithelial progenitors (LNEPs), or distal airway stem cells, , , , and H2K1high club-like stem cells. Among those, AT2s are the stem cells in the alveoli. Activation of Wnt/β-catenin signaling promotes AT2 proliferation and also promote alveolar differentiation of the distal airway progenitors, including LNEPs in mouse and respiratory airway secretory cell in human, which could be involved in alveolar regeneration after severe injury.
Acute and chronic pulmonary diseases are a leading cause of morbidity and mortality globally. Unfortunately, there are currently limited treatment options. Loss of functional lung tissue is a characteristic of these diseases. Thus, alveolar regeneration holds promise to treat people with severe lung diseases, including IPF, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, and acute infection-induced pneumonia. Here, we proposed a potential treatment strategy for lung fibrosis by promoting epithelial regeneration using AAV6-based RNA editing. Our work demonstrated that promoting AT2 proliferation and alveolar regeneration can ameliorate lung fibrosis in both preventive and therapeutic settings. This strategy may also be applied to other acute and chronic lung injury-induced diseases.

## Materials and methods

## Animals
All animal studies were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of ShanghaiTech University and were done in compliance with ethical guidelines and the approved protocols. The animals used in this study were housed in the Animal Facility at the National Facility for Protein Science in Shanghai or the Animal Core Facility at ShanghaiTech University under pathogen-free conditions at room temperature, maintained in a 12 h light/dark cycle room with water and food ad libitum. C57BL/6J mice were purchased from Shanghai JiHui Experimental Animal Feeding Co. Ltd. The Ai9 mouse line was previously described and was a gift of Dr. Harold A. Chapman. We used 8- to 12-week-old mice.

## AAV production and IT injection
AAV5-GFP, AAV6-GFP and AAV9-GFP were purchased from PackGene Biotech. The CMV-CasRx-U6-gRNA (a gift of Dr. Hui Yang) and CMV-β-catenin(S > A) (a gift of Dr. Guisheng Zhong) plasmids were constructed into AAV6 vector and packed by PackGene Biotech. Before IT injection, the mice were placed in a gas anesthesia device and fully anesthetized with isoflurane (R510-22-10, RWD), then suspended on a surgical stent with continuously isoflurane supply. Next, a 22G retention needle was inserted into the trachea, and the virus was inhaled as the mice breathing. Each mouse received a single IT injection of 1 × 1011 vg AAV diluted in PBS to a volume of 50 μL.

## Bleomycin injury model
Mice were given pharmaceutical-grade bleomycin (1.6 mg/kg body weight, Nippon Kayaku) which was diluted to a volume of 50 μL in sterile saline via IT injection. All mice were randomized based on their weights to minimize variance between experimental and control groups before the study. AAVs were administrated 14 days before bleomycin injury for preventive studies and 6 days after bleomycin for therapeutic studies. The mice were sacrificed at days 14 or 17 after bleomycin injury.

## Lung cell isolation and flow cytometry
Mouse lung cells were isolated as previously described. The antibodies and reagents used in the flow cytometry include BV421-conjugated rat anti-mouse EPCAM (1:500, 118225, Biolegend), BUV395-conjugated rat anti-mouse CD45 (1:300, 564279, BD), Alexa Fluor 647-conjugated rat anti-mouse Integrin β4 (1:150, 123608, Biolegend), BV711-conjugated rat anti-mouse CD31 (1:500, 102449, Biolegend), and fixable viability dye eFluor 780 (1:2,000, 65-0865-14, Thermo Fisher Scientific). Cell sorting and analysis were performed on BD fluorescence-activated cell sorting Aria cytometers. CD45+ cells were distinguished as immune cells, CD31+ cells were as endothelial cells, EPCAM+ Integrin β4+ cells were as airway epithelial cells, EPCAM+ Integrin β4− cells were as alveolar epithelial cells, and CD45−CD3−EpCAM− cells were as fibroblasts.

## AT2 organoid culture
The AAVs were administered into Ai9 mice, and 7 days later, tdTomato+EPCAM+ Integrin β4− cells were flow sorted to enrich infected mouse lung AT2s for organoid culture. Eight-thousand tdTomato+ AT2 cells were resuspended in AT2 Maintenance Medium (AMM) without CHIR99021, mixed 1:1 with Matrigel (Corning, 356231), seeded in a 40-μL droplet in the center of a well in a 24-well plate and cultured in AMM without CHIR99021 for 10 days.

## EdU proliferation assay
To detect AT2 proliferation in organoids, EdU (A10044, Thermo Fisher Scientific) was added in the culture medium at the final concentration of 10 μM from day 7 to day 10. The proliferating AT2s were detected using the Click-iT EdU Alexa Fluor 647 imaging kit (C10340, Thermo Fisher Scientific). To detect the proliferation of AT2s after bleomycin injury, 100 μg EdU (A10044, Thermo Fisher Scientific) was injected intraperitoneally per mice once every 2 days starting from day 1. The mice were harvested at day 8 after injury. The proliferating AT2s were detected using the Click-iT EdU Alexa Fluor 488 imaging kit (C10337, Thermo Fisher Scientific) and the Click-iT EdU Alexa Fluor 488 flow cytometry assay kit (C10425, Thermo Fisher Scientific).

## Tissue preparation and immunofluorescence
Freshly dissected mouse left lungs were inflated with 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) plus 50% Optimal Cutting Temperature (OCT) and fixed in 4% PFA for 1 h at room temperature. After fixation, the lungs were washed with PBS repeatedly and incubated with 15% sucrose plus 50% OCT overnight, then embedded and frozen in OCT. Cryosections (6 μm thick) were cut and fixed for 5 min in 4% PFA at room temperature, then incubated with 0.1% sodium borohydride (213462, Sigma Aldrich) in PBS to reduce aldehyde-induced background fluorescence for 3 × 10 min intervals, and subsequently blocked in PBS plus 1% BSA, 5% horse serum, and 0.1% Triton X-100 for 1 h, followed by primary antibodies staining. The antibodies used in this study are rabbit anti-pro-SPC (1:3,000, AB3786, Millipore), goat anti-pro-SPC (1:1,000, sc-7706, Santa Cruz Biotechnology), rat anti-RAGE (1:200, MAB1179–100, R&D), rabbit anti-CC10 (1:500, sc-25555, Santa Cruz Biotechnology), mouse anti-acetylated tubulin (1:400, T7451, Sigma Aldrich), mouse anti-ACTA2-conjugated FITC (1:200, F3777, Sigma Aldrich), and goat anti-PDGFRβ (1:100, AF1042, R&D).
For whole mount staining of AT2 organoid, pre-warmed 4% PFA was added to the well containing the droplet to fix for 30 min. Then the droplets were washed with PBS and followed by primary and secondary antibodies staining for 48 h.

## Quantification of immunofluorescence
To quantify the area of ACTA2+ cells, PDGFRβ+ cells, SPC+ AT2s, and RAGE+ AT1s, whole lobes were imaged with Nikon Ti2-E CSU-W1 Sora Camera (Nikon) and quantified using ImageJ machine learning. ACTA2+ smooth muscle cells around airways and blood vessels were excluded to quantify the areas of ACTA2+ myofibroblasts. SPC+ AT2 covered areas were manually outlined and measured by ImageJ. The ImageJ “multi-point” function was used to count SPC+ AT2s and RAGE+ AT1s. For each mouse, at least two sections separated by over 300 μm depth were quantified.

## Histology analysis and Ashcroft scoring
Trichrome stain was performed on the frozen lung sections according to the standard procedure of Masson Stain Kit (60532ES74, Yeasen). The fibrotic area was quantified by ImageJ machine learning. Ashcroft scoring were following a previous described modified method that assigned a scale ranging from 0 to 8, with higher scores indicating more extensive fibrosis. For long-term safety analysis of AAVs, the sections of lung, liver and small intestine were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (60524ES60, Yeasen).

## Hydroxyproline assay
Snap-frozen lungs were ground into tissue powder and the entire amount of powder was used for hydroxyproline assay to quantify the content of collagen. This assay was performed following the standard procedure of Hydroxyproline Assay Kit (MAK008, Sigma Aldrich). The measured absorbance was normalized to saline control.

## Cell culture and transfection
Neuro-2a cells were cultured in DMEM with 10% FBS (VS500T, Ausbian), 1% penicillin/streptomycin (15140122, Thermo Fisher Scientific), and 1% glutamax (35050061, Thermo Fisher Scientific) in a 37°C incubator under 5% CO2. One day before transfection, cells were plated in a 24-well plate (3524, Corning) and then on the next day, the cells were transfected with 1 μg plasmid expressing CMV-CasRx-U6-gRNA (none targeting or targeting Axin1 and Axin2) according to the procedure of Lipofectamine 2000 (11668019, Thermo Fisher Scientific). The cells were harvested 48 h later and were lysed for RNA extraction and qPCR analysis. The gRNA sequences used in this study are listed in . 

## RNA extraction and qPCR analysis
The cells and tissue powder were lysed using Trizol (15596026, Thermo Fisher Scientific) and total RNA was extracted using RNA MicroPrep kit (R2062, Zymo Research). Complementary DNA was synthesized using reverse transcription enzyme (R222-01, Vazyme). qPCR analysis was performed according to the procedure of SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Q711-03, Vazyme). Relative gene expression was calculated using the ΔΔCT method and normalized to HPRT. Primers are as listed in . 

## RNA-seq analysis
For transcriptome-wide RNA-seq, RNA was extracted as described above. Strand-specific libraries were constructed by the TruSeq RNA sample preparation kit (Illumina). Then RNA-seq was performed by the Illumina Novaseq 6000 sequencing platform by the service of Genergy Biotechnology Co. Ltd. The quality of raw data was controlled by FastQC v0.11.2 with default parameters. The expression of each transcript was calculated by fragments per kilobase of exon model per million mapped reads. Differential expression genes (DEGs) were determined using the MA-plot-based method with Random Sampling model in the DEGseq package. DEGs were defined as |log2FC|≥1 and padjusted ≤ 0.05. The data were presented in volcano plots.

## Immunoblot
The cells and tissue powder were lysed in RIPA buffer (50 mM Tris-HCl PH7.5, 150 mM NaCl, 1% deoxycholate, 0.1% SDS, 1% Triton X-100) supplemented with a Protease Inhibitor Cocktail (11697498001, Roche), phenylmethyl sulfonyl fluoride and a Phosphatase Inhibitor Cocktail (524625-1SET, Sigma Aldrich). The lysates were quantified using Pierce BCA protein assay kit (23227, Thermo Fisher Scientific). The primary antibodies are Axin1 (1:100, 2087S, Cell Signaling), Axin2 (1:100, A2513, ABclonal), β-actin (1:100, sc-47778, Santa Cruz Biotechnology), β-catenin (1:100, sc-59737, Santa Cruz Biotechnology), phospho-β-catenin (1:100, sc-57535, Santa Cruz Biotechnology), GAPDH (1:100, 5174S, Cell Signaling), Lamin A/C (1:100, sc-376248, Santa Cruz Biotechnology), fibronectin (1:500, F3648, Sigma Aldrich) and pro-SPC (1:400, AB3786, Millipore). The secondary antibodies are goat anti-mouse-conjugated horseradish peroxidase (HRP) (1:5,000, G21040, Thermo Fisher Scientific), rabbit anti-goat-conjugated HRP (1,:5000, 31402, Thermo Fisher Scientific), and goat anti-rabbit-conjugated HRP (1:5,000, G21234, Thermo Fisher Scientific).

## Luciferase reporter assay
Neuro-2a cells transfected with CMV-CasRx-U6-gRNA plasmid were reseeded 48 h later into a new 24-well plate and then were transfected with 400 ng M50 Super 8XTOPFlash (a gift of Dr. Wenqing Xu) and 25 ng Renilla (a gift of Dr. Wenqing Xu) and waited for another 48 h. Luciferase assay was performed according to the procedure of Dual Luciferase Reporter Assay System kit (E1910, Promega). The luciferase activities were measured by Spark Multimode Microplate Reader (Tecan, Switzerland) and the firefly luciferase activity was normalized to that of Renilla luciferase.

## Nuclear and cytoplasmic protein extraction
To detect the cytoplasmic and nuclear β-catenin levels, cytoplasmic and nuclear protein was extracted following the procedure of Nuclear and Cytoplasmic Protein Extraction kit (P0027, Beyotime). The extracted proteins were blotted as discussed above.

Custom scripts in R version 4.1.3 were used for RNA-seq data analysis and plotting in . GraphPad Prism was used for statistical analysis in all other figures.
