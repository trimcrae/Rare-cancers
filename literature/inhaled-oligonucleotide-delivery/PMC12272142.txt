## ABSTRACT

Orthohantavirus puumalaense causes hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome in Europe, with Puumala virus (PUUV) as its primary representative. Muju virus (MUJV), harbored by Craseomys regulus, an Arvicolinae rodent species endemic to the Republic of Korea (ROK), is also a genotype of O. puumalaense. However, their genomic diversity and zoonotic potential remain largely unknown. To investigate their prevalence, 185 voles were collected from 23 regions of the ROK between 2012 and 2023. Serological assays detected anti‐PUUV immunoglobulin G antibodies in five samples (3.1%), whereas reverse‐transcription polymerase chain reaction confirmed MUJV RNA in identical specimens (2.7%). Amplicon‐based nanopore sequencing facilitates near‐complete genome recovery, enabling high‐resolution comparative analysis. Phylogenetic analysis revealed distinct genetic lineages in Gangwon and Jeollabuk Provinces. Evolutionary rate estimates indicated greater sequence divergence in the S and L segments than in the M segment. A zoonotic risk assessment revealed that most MUJV variants exhibited moderate‐to‐high spillover potential. The molecular detection of MUJV in Cheorwon, Gangwon Province, expands its known geographic range and provides the first molecular evidence of MUJV circulation in this region. These findings highlight the need for continued surveillance and seroprevalence studies of MUJV to assess its potential for human exposure and public health relevance in the ROK.

## WGS of MUJV
We performed amplicon‐based NGS to generate six near‐complete genome sequences of MUJV from lung tissues of C. regulus collected in the ROK (Supporting Information S1: Table  ). The genome coverage rates ranged from 81.3% to 99.9% for the S segment, 84.7% to 96.8% for the M segment, and 98.0% to 99.5% for the L segment. The number of mapped reads and average depths are listed in Supporting Information S1: Table  . Gaps in the tripartite genomes were resolved using an in‐house nested RT‐PCR.

## Genomic Characterization of MUJV
Phylogenetic analysis of the MUJV S, M, and L segments showed a clear geographic separation, forming two well‐supported groups corresponding to the Gangwon and Jeollanam Provinces, ROK (Figure  ). The estimated evolutionary rates of MUJV were 8.4E‐03, 7.0E‐03, 2.6E‐03, and 1.8E‐02 substitutions per nucleotide per year for the S (N protein), M (glycoproteins Gn and Gc), and L (RdRp) segments, respectively (Supporting Information S1: Table  ). Nucleotide and amino acid similarities of MUJV are shown in Supporting Information S1: Table  .

## Zoonotic Risk Assessment of MUJV
We assessed the zoonotic potential of well‐characterized, complete coding genomes representative of O. puumalaense derived from both humans and rodents (Figure  and Supporting Information S1: Table  ). In this study, MUJV variants were identified as having either three medium (23.1%) or nine high (69.2%) grades of potential zoonosis scores, whereas only one variant (7.7%) was classified as having low zoonotic potential.

## Discussion
Our study presents a comprehensive nationwide investigation of MUJV in C. regulus voles across the ROK, encompassing 23 regions across multiple provinces, from 2012 to 2023. Despite extensive sampling, MUJV RNA was exclusively detected in five voles (2.7%) from Gangwon Province, specifically in Cheorwon, Hongcheon, Inje, and Pyeongchang. This pattern is consistent with previous reports documenting MUJV exclusively in the Gangwon and Jeollabuk Provinces [ ]. Detection in Cheorwon expands the known distribution of MUJV within the Gangwon Province and serves as the first molecular evidence for its presence in C. regulus in this region. C. regulus, which is endemic to the Korean Peninsula, exhibits distinct habitat preferences for mountain forests, bamboo woodlands, rocky slopes, and riverbanks, indicating that landscape features play a key role in shaping its distribution [ ]. Although C. regulus is widely distributed across the ROK, its regional abundance varies, which may influence the capture rates and virus detection. The restricted detection of MUJV outside Gangwon Province may reflect regional differences in C. regulus population density and habitat suitability. Given the broad distribution of C. regulus, continued surveillance in undersampled regions is essential to further elucidate the distribution and prevalence of MUJVs.
In this study, six complete MUJV genome sequences were obtained from voles in the Gangwon and Jeollabuk Provinces, ROK, and the previously reported MUJV 00‐18 sequence from Muju was incorporated for phylogeographic analysis [ ]. Phylogenetic analyses revealed a well‐supported divergence between MUJV lineages from these regions, which is consistent with previous reports [ , ]. Earlier studies generated valuable genomic data but were constrained by incomplete sequences, limiting phylogenetic analyses. The initial characterization included only the S and M segments because the L segment remained unsequenced [ ]. Full‐length genomes were obtained from samples in the Gangwon Province (MUJV 11‐1 from Inje, MUJV 11–4 and 11–5 from Pyeongchang), but the data set remained geographically restricted, preventing broader phylogeographic assessments [ ]. To enhance the phylogeographic resolution, we expanded the genomic data set by adding sequences, including MUJV 00‐18 from Muju in Jeollabuk Province. The estimated evolutionary rates for the S, M, and L segments were 8.4 × 10⁻³, 2.6–7.0 × 10⁻³, and 1.8 × 10⁻² substitutions per site per year, respectively, with the M segment exhibiting the least divergence. Because MUJV and HOKV appear to have evolved faster than PUUV, further sampling is required to refine these estimates and better resolve their evolutionary dynamics.
In the ROK, approximately 400 cases of HFRS are reported annually, with fatality rates ranging from 1% to 4%. Around 70% of these cases are caused by HTNV, while the remainder are linked to SEOV or remain unclassified [ ]. Although PUUV is the primary etiological agent of HFRS in Europe, no human infections attributed to MUJV have been documented in the ROK, and its zoonotic potential remains uncertain [ , ]. Nevertheless, the phylogenetic proximity of MUJV to PUUV has raised questions about its possible risk to humans. To explore this potential, we applied a genome composition‐based machine learning model developed by Mollentze et al. (2021), which estimates zoonotic potential using features such as codon usage, dinucleotide bias, and amino acid composition [ ]. Among the 13 MUJV variants analyzed, 12 were predicted to have moderate or high potential for human infectivity. While informative, these predictions are based on genomic signatures rather than experimental data and do not consider key factors such as receptor binding, tropism, or ecological context. Moreover, the model was not specifically optimized for hantaviruses, which may affect its predictive accuracy. Despite these limitations, the results, together with the close evolutionary relationship between MUJV and PUUV, support the need for enhanced surveillance and further investigation. Serological studies, receptor interaction assays, and virus isolation will be essential to clarify the zoonotic potential of MUJV and to guide public health preparedness in the ROK.
In conclusion, we report six whole‐genome sequences of MUJV obtained from C. regulus collected in the ROK, providing the first molecular evidence of MUJV circulation in Cheorwon, Gangwon Province. Zoonotic risk evaluation suggested a moderate‐to‐high likelihood of human spillover, underscoring the need for further investigation. This report advances our understanding of the evolutionary patterns and genetic diversity of MUJV and contributes to more robust hantavirus surveillance, risk assessment, and public health preparedness in the ROK.
