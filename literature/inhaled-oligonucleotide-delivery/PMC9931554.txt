## Abstract
Myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) are a group of immature myeloid cells that play an important role in diseases. MDSCs promote Th17 differentiation and aggravate systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) progression by producing arginase-1 to metabolize arginine. However, the metabolic regulators remain unknown. Here, we report that MDSC derivative polyamines can promote Th17 differentiation via miR-542–5p in vitro. Th17 polarization was enhanced in response to polyamine treatment or upon miR-542–5p overexpression. The TGF-β/SMAD3 pathway was shown to be involved in miR-542-5p-facilitated Th17 differentiation. Furthermore, miR-542–5p expression positively correlated with the levels of polyamine synthetases in peripheral blood mononuclear cells of patients with SLE as well as disease severity. In humanized SLE model mice, MDSC depletion decreased the levels of Th17 cells, accompanied by reduced expression of miR-542–5p and these polyamine synthetases. In addition, miR-542–5p expression positively correlated with the Th17 level and disease severity in both patients and humanized SLE mice. Together, our data reveal a novel molecular pathway by which MDSC-derived polyamine metabolism enhances Th17 differentiation and aggravates SLE.
Keywords: myeloid-derived suppressor cells, systemic lupus erythematosus, polyamine, Th17, miR-542-5p, TGF-β/SMAD3

## Introduction
Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) is mainly characterized by the presence of immune complexes consisting of autoantibodies and antigens deposited in local tissues or circulating in body fluids. , The immune pathogenesis of SLE is mainly associated with dysfunction of cell types involved in innate and adaptive immune functions, including dendritic cells, B cells, and T cells. The disease involves multiple systems and organs, such as lupus nephritis (LN). , 
The main function of T helper 17 (Th17) cells, a subgroup of helper T cells, is to combat infection by clearing extracellular bacteria and fungi. However, these cells have been implicated in many disease states, including autoimmunity, chronic inflammation, and cancers. , The central modulator of Th17 cells is the orphan nuclear receptor γ (RORγt). RORγt directs the transcription of genes encoding IL-17A and IL-17F. , It has become increasingly clear that Th17 cells play an important role in the progression of autoimmune diseases, such as SLE. , 
Myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) are a heterogeneous population of immature myeloid cells with well-documented immunosuppressive functions in cancers. , In recent years, the interaction between MDSCs and Th17 cells has been studied in the settings of autoimmune diseases and cancers. We have shown that MDSCs can promote Th17 differentiation and aggravate disease progression by producing interleukin-1β (IL-1β) , or arginase-1 (Arg-1) to metabolize arginine (ARG). However, the detailed mechanism by which MDSCs affect Th17 cells in autoimmune diseases is not completely understood.
Polyamines, including putrescine (PUT), spermidine (SPD), and spermine (SPM), are synthesized by utilizing glutamine, arginine, and proline as substrates, and methionine is also used for spermine synthesis, which is essential for cell proliferation, inflammation control, transmembrane transport, and tissue repair. , Previously, Adhami hypothesized that polyamines convert DNA to an immunogenic structure to stabilize autoantigens. However, it is not clear whether polyamines alter the differentiation of Th subsets. Although spermidine shifts Th17 cell polarization toward Tregs in the intestine, a recent study showed that Th1 and Th2 cells have active polyamine synthesis, but Th17 and Treg cells seldom utilize arginine, and how polyamine-mediated hypusinated eIF5A influences downstream transcription factors is still an enigma. 
MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are important regulatory noncoding RNAs that are approximately 19–22 nucleotides in length. miRNAs play an important role in many biological activities. , They also alter Th17 cells’ fate in the pathogenesis of autoimmune diseases. In SLE mice, miR-155, miR-30a, miR-130b, miR-148a-3p, and other miRNAs were reported to affect disease severity, including kidney damage, through multiple genes and pathways. , , Therefore, miRNAs represent new targets for disease treatment due to their unique regulatory roles at the levels of gene transcription and posttranscription. , 
In the current work, we show that polyamines released by MDSCs promote Th17 differentiation via miR-542-5p upregulation. miR-542-5p overexpression in human naive CD4+ T cells increased Th17 polarization by activating the TGF-β/SMAD3 pathway. Finally, we confirmed that increased polyamine production and miR-542-5p upregulation by MDSCs are associated with disease severity in patients with SLE and in humanized SLE mice. These findings provide molecular insights into the interaction between MDSCs and Th17 cells and implicate polyamine metabolism or miR-542 as potential therapeutic targets for SLE.

## Results

## Ornithine and ornithine decarboxylase derived from MDSCs increase Th17 polarization
Our previous studies have shown that MDSCs promote Th17 cell differentiation and SLE disease progression in an Arg-1-dependent manner. ORN (ornithine), the downstream product of ARG, is further metabolized by ODC1 (ornithine decarboxylase 1) and other enzymes to produce proline and polyamines. Specifically, PUT, SPD, and SPM are sequentially synthesized by the activity of ODC1, spermidine synthase (SRM), and spermine synthase (SMS) ( A). We hypothesized that these downstream metabolites produced by MDSCs are involved in MDSC-enhanced Th17 differentiation. To address this hypothesis, the cells were categorized into four groups: Th17, Th17 + ORN, Th17 + MDSCs, and Th17 + MDSCs + DFMO. Under conditions that induce human Th17 cell differentiation, we showed that Th17 + ORN or Th17 + MDSCs similarly upregulated IL-17A and RORγt at the protein and mRNA levels in naive T cells. Moreover, DFMO, which inhibits the metabolism of ORN, clearly decreased the expression of IL-17A and RORγt in Th17 + MDSCs + DFMO compared with the Th17 + MDSCs group ( B–1I). To confirm that the effect of DFMO on impeding Th17 differentiation was based on MDSCs, we compared the protein and RNA levels of IL-17A and RORγt between the Th17 and Th17 + DFMO groups, and there was no significant difference ( ). Consistent with this finding, ORN or MDSC metabolism in mice caused a similar increase in the level of differentiated Th17 cells, since DFMO alone, at the concentration tested, did not enhance or impair the expression of IL-17A and RORγt in Th17 group ( ).
 Collectively, our data indicate that MDSCs facilitate Th17 cell differentiation by ORN and the derivatives catalyzed by the enzyme ODC1.

## Polyamine treatment increased Th17 polarization
To elucidate the effect of polyamine on Th17 polarization, we performed Th17 polarization assay using Th17 polarizing medium with or without three different polyamines (PUT, SPD, or SPM, 2.5 μM) (containing anti-human CD3 mAb, anti-human CD28 mAb, 2-mercaptoethanol, TGF-β, IL-6, IL-23, IL-1β, anti-human IFN-γ mAb, and anti-mouse IL-4 mAb culture for 6–7 days). The results showed that polyamine supplementation in the medium significantly increased CD4+IL-17A+ or CD4+RORγt+ cells, enhanced IL-17A and RORγt mRNA expression, and increased IL-17A levels in cell supernatants ( ), indicating that SPD, SPM, and its metabolic precursor PUT produced by MDSCs act as the primary modulators of Th17 polarization. Total polyamine assay was performed, and polyamines levels in Th17 and naive CD4+ T cells were almost the same, but higher in MDSC compared with Th17. Besides that, polyamines concentration was also markedly increased in Th17 + MDSC group compared with Th17 group. We used DFMO-treated MDSC for 24 h to block polyamines synthesis; then we added them to the Th17 differentiation group (Th17 + MDSC treated with DFMO for 24 h), and it showed a lower polyamines level than Th17 + MDSC group. However, although polyamines concentration was lower in Th17 + MDSC treated with DFMO for 24 h than Th17 + MDSC group, there was no significant statistical difference ( A and S3B). It suggested that polyamine precursors derived from MDSC were metabolized into polyamines in Th17 cells. Moreover, we extracted total RNA from Th17 cells polarized from human naive CD4+ T cells or Th17 cells cocultured with MDSCs. qPCR analysis revealed that the expression levels of the genes encoding the polyamine synthetases ODC1, SRM, and SMS were elevated in Th17 cells cocultured with MDSCs ( C–S3E). The same results were also observed in parallel mouse models ( F–S3H). As DFMO is an irreversible inhibitor of ODC1, we used DFMO-treated MDSC for 24 h, and then tested the Th17 differentiation proportion; mRNA and protein level of both IL-17A and RORγt were elevated in Th17 + MDSC group compared with Th17 group, while both IL-17A and RORγt were decreased in Th17 + DFMO-treated MDSC group compared with Th17 + MDSC group ( ).Taken together, these data support coordinated regulation of the polyamine pathway and suggest that these polyamine synthetases drive the production of high levels of polyamines in MDSCs, promoting Th17 cell differentiation.
 

## Positive correlation between miR-542–5p expression and MDSC metabolites
Considering that miRNAs have been shown to play a crucial role in CD4+ T cell differentiation in SLE, we examined whether the effect of polyamines on Th17 polarization involves changes in specific miRNAs. Three experimental groups were set up during human Th17 differentiation, which included naive CD4+ T cells (Th17 group), T cells cocultured with MDSCs (Th17 + MDSCs group), or T cells cocultured with MDSCs in the presence of nor-NOHA (an Arg-1 inhibitor, Th17 + MDSCs + nor-NOHA group). To identify miRNAs involved in the activity of the polyamines produced by MDSCs, we assessed the miRNA profiles. A total of 9,256 miRNAs (7,768 known miRNAs and 1,488 unknown miRNAs) were detected in the three samples. Seventy-four miRNAs were differentially expressed between the Th17 and Th17 + MDSC groups: 63 miRNAs increased in the Th17 alone group, whereas 11 decreased in the Th17 + MDSC group. Seventy-five miRNAs were differentially expressed between the Th17 + MDSC and Th17 + MDSC + nor-NOHA groups, with 38 miRNAs increased and 37 decreased in the nor-NOHA treatment group. There were nine overlapping miRNAs among the 63 upregulated miRNAs in the Th17 + MDSC group and Th17 group, while there were 37 downregulated miRNAs in the Th17 + MDSC + nor-NOHA group and Th17 + MDSC group ( and A and S5B). In addition, we examined the expression of the nine miRNAs in SLE patients, which were all elevated compared with healthy controls (HCs). miR-542–5p, miR-6826–5p, miR-576–5p, miR-1273g-3p, and miR-6128 elevated significantly (p 0.05, C–S5J), especially miR-542–5p and miR-6826–5p. Among these overlapping miRNAs, miR-542-5p was previously reported to be associated with myeloid cell differentiation , and SLE disease progression. We therefore selected miR-542-5p to further analyze its potential function in Th17 differentiation.
To determine the role of miR-542-5p in polyamine-induced Th17 differentiation, we purified mRNA from the cells used in the experiments above, amplified miR-542-5p, and examined its relative expression by qPCR. We found that hsa-miR-542-5p expression increased in the human or mouse Th17 + MDSC group (independent of ORN treatment) compared with the Th17 group. In contrast, hsa-miR-542-5p expression significantly decreased in the DFMO-treated group compared with the untreated coculture group ( A and 3B). Similarly, hsa-miR-542-5p expression was significantly upregulated in the polyamine treatment group compared with the control group ( C). These results suggest that miR-542-5p is upregulated by the polyamines produced by MDSCs.
 

## Upregulation of miR-542–5p increased Th17 polarization
To evaluate the function of hsa-miR-542-5p, we overexpressed or silenced hsa-miR-542-5p in human naive CD4+ T cells (the relative expression of hsa-miR-542-5p posttransfection is shown in A and S6B) and then stimulated the cells with CD3/CD28 for 72 h. Activated T cells were defined as CD44+CD69+ cells ( C), and statistical analysis showed no significant difference in the number of activated cells upon overexpression or silencing of hsa-miR-542-5p ( D and S6E). During T cell proliferation, RNA level of miR-542–5p was downregulated ( F), which further illustrates that Th17 differentiation promotes miR-542–5p expression. Upon polarization of human naive CD4+ T cells to Th17 cells, IL-17A and RORγt were elevated at both the protein and mRNA levels in the hsa-miR-542-5p overexpression group ( D–3F) but decreased in the hsa-miR-542-5p silencing group when compared with the control group ( H–3J). Thus, our data indicate that while T cell activation was independent of hsa-miR-542-5p expression, miR-542-5p positively regulates Th17 polarization.

## miR-542–5p is associated with TGF-β/SMAD3 signaling
Considering that miR-542-5p plays an important role in Th17 differentiation, we sought to determine the underlying molecular mechanism. Since the TGF-β/SMAD3 pathway is the classical signaling pathway critical for Th17 differentiation, , we selected the SMAD3, SMAD7, and SMURF1 genes and examined their potential relationship with the expression level of hsa-miR-542-5p using a dual-luciferase reporter assay. The miRWalk database (http://mirwalk.umm.uni-heidelberg.de/) was used to identify the binding site of miR-542–5p downstream of the TGF-β/SMAD3 pathway. The binding sites for miR-542-5p in SMAD genes are shown in A. We found that SMAD3 and SMURF1 gene transcription were significantly reduced by miR-542-5p overexpression ( B).
 

## Increased Th17 polarization occurs via polyamine/miR-542–5p/TGF-β/SMAD3 signaling
To determine the role of miR-542-5p in Th17 differentiation, we overexpressed or silenced hsa-miR-542-5p in human naive CD4+ T cells and then polarized them to Th17 cells. mRNAs were purified and used to quantify SMAD3, SMAD7, and SMURF1 expression, and we found that SMAD3 and SMURF1 expression was decreased in the hsa-miR-542-5p mimic group, which was consistent with the use of miRWalk hsa-miR-542-5p that also significantly downregulated SMAD7 levels ( C). By measuring the mean fluorescence intensity (MFI), we detected SMAD2/3, phosphorylated SMAD2/3 (pSMAD2/3), and SMURF1 protein levels in the same groups by flow cytometry. Interestingly, although SMAD3 mRNA expression was downregulated by miR-542-5p overexpression, no significant change in the SMAD3 protein level was observed. However, the level of SMURF1 protein decreased, whereas the levels of pSMAD2/3 protein and pSMAD2/3 protein/total SMAD2/3 increased ( D–4F). In contrast, when hsa-miR-542-5p was silenced, SMAD3 and SMURF1 mRNA expression increased, while the MFI for the pSMAD2/3 and pSMAD2/3/total SMAD2/3 proteins decreased ( G–4J). hsa-miR-542-5p was also overexpressed or silenced in human naive CD4+ T cells, and similar results were obtained ( A–S7H).
To verify function of polyamine in miR-542–5p/TGF-β/SMAD3 axis, we performed a study in which miR-542–5p NC or inhibitor was transfected in human naive CD4+ T cells; then, three different polyamines (PUT, SPD, or SPM) were directly added to the Th17-polarizing medium subsequently. We found that the level of pSMAD2/3 protein increased in polyamines groups compared with Th17 polarization alone, whereas pSMAD2/3 protein level decreased in inhibitor groups compared with NC groups by MFI calculate in flow cytometry. pSMAD2/3/total SMAD2/3 proteins also decreased by MFI test in miR-542–5p silenced group compared with NC group ( I–S7K). These data together suggest that polyamines and hsa-miR-542-5p play an important role in Th17 polarization via TGF-β/SMAD signaling.

## Polyamine synthetase and miR-542–5p expression are increased in PBMCs from SLE patients
We next isolated peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) from a total of 18 SLE patients (3 males and 15 females, aged 19 to 64 years) and 18 HCs (three males and 15 females, aged 17 to 65 years, ). All patients were diagnosed with active SLE according to their SLE Disease Activity Index (SLEDAI) scores, which ranged from 0 to 24. Detailed clinical and laboratory characteristics of these patients are presented in . Compared with the HCs, the SLE patients showed a significant increase in the RNA levels of ODC1, SRM, SMS, and miR-542–5p expression in PBMCs, which were also positively associated with the SLEDAI scores ( A–5D). We used Spearman rank tests to verify the correlation between polyamine synthetase levels and miR-542-5p expression. The results showed a significant positive relationship for all three polyamine synthetases with miR-542-5p ( E–5G). Therefore, both the expression of polyamine synthetase and miR-542-5p correlated positively with SLE severity.
 

## miR-542–5p and MDSC-derived polyamines increased Th17 polarization in humanized SLE mice
To determine the role of polyamines and miR-542-5p in SLE development, we created a humanized SLE mouse model via intravenous injection of PBMCs from SLE patients with active disease (SLEDAI ≥ 9; double-stranded DNA [dsDNA] ≥ 1:10) into NSG mice. The NSG mice were administered PBMCs plus NC, PBMCs plus miR-542-5p inhibitor, MDSC-depleted PBMCs plus control, or MDSC-depleted PBMCs plus miR-542-5p mimics ( A). Within 4 to 5 weeks, all mice developed LN-like symptoms indicated by elevation of urine albumin ( B). Mice that received nonmodified PBMCs plus NC showed exacerbated symptoms compared with those that received unmodified PBMCs plus miR-542-5p inhibitor, whereas mice administered MDSC-depleted PBMCs plus miR-542-5p mimics showed higher urine albumin levels than those receiving MDSC-depleted PBMCs plus control, indicating that miR-542-5p is essential for LN pathogenesis in vivo. Treatment with a miR-542-5p inhibitor or MDSC depletion inhibited LN progression in NSG mice to a similar extent. When compared with MDSC-depleted PBMCs, nonmodified PBMCs showed increased RNA expression of ODC1, SRM, and SMS in the mouse kidney and spleenocytes ( C and A), which are representative of SLE target organs and peripheral lymphoid tissue. The most common pathological manifestations of LN were cell proliferation in capillaries with inflammatory cell infiltration (or crescent formation) and tubular vacuolar degeneration. Histological analysis of the SLE model mouse kidney tissue revealed increased inflammatory cell infiltration in the group injected with nonmodified PBMCs plus negative control and serious vacuolar degeneration of the renal tubules in the group injected with nonmodified PBMCs plus NC or MDSC-depleted PBMCs plus miR-542-5p mimics but not in those injected with MDSC-depleted PBMCs or nonmodified PBMCs plus miR-542-5p inhibitor ( D). Compared with mice that received unmodified PBMCs plus the negative control, those that received unmodified PBMCs plus the miR-542-5p inhibitor showed markedly reduced hIL-17A and hRORγt expression at both the mRNA and protein levels in the kidney and spleenocytes. A similar result was observed in mice that received MDSC-depleted PBMCs plus miR-542-5p mimics compared with those that received MDSC-depleted PBMCs plus control ( E, 6F, and B–S7G). These findings indicate that the function of miR-542-5p in promoting LN is dependent on polyamine synthetases.
 

## Discussion
Given the well-documented immunoregulatory roles of MDSCs in autoimmune diseases, attempts have been made to design therapeutic applications by targeting this cell population. , , , In the current work, we show that MDSCs can exacerbate SLE progression through an amino acid metabolism-driven Th17 immune response. Furthermore, we identified miR-542-5p in Th17 cells as the target miRNA that is regulated by MDSC-derived polyamine and promotes Th17 differentiation through the TGF-β/SMAD3 signaling pathway. Additionally, we uncovered a positive correlation of disease severity with the expression of polyamine synthetase and miR-542-5p in patients with SLE. Using both an in vitro assay and a humanized SLE mouse model, we further demonstrated that MDSCs from SLE patients promote Th17 cell differentiation and disease progression in a polyamine- and miR-542-5p-dependent manner ( ).
 Th17 cells have been well documented for their proinflammatory effects in the pathogenesis of several autoimmune disorders. Emerging studies highlight the importance of amino acid metabolism in Th17 cells differentiation regulation. , Our previous studies of SLE patients and SLE mouse models showed that MDSCs can promote Th17 differentiation and aggravate SLE progression by producing Arg-1. Indeed, Arg-1 is believed to be detrimental rather than protective in autoimmune diseases, which may be due to the products of ARG decomposition that can potentiate disease progression. , An algorithm to characterize cellular metabolic states based on single-cell RNA sequencing and flux balance analysis and in vivo experiments has shown that polyamine metabolism is related to Th17 polarization. , However, which cells manipulate polyamine levels remains to be determined. It is also unclear whether pathological or physiological conditions drive the enhanced polyamine levels that we believe lead to Th17 cell polarization.
ARG is an essential amino acid in both culture media and organisms. The literature shows that polyamines produced by ARG have been proven to enhance the immunosuppressive function of dendritic cells. The aim of this manuscript is to confirm whether Arg-1 or its metabolites participate in this process. ODC1 is a key enzyme that metabolizes ORN (Arg-1 metabolites), producing proline and polyamines with other enzymes. There seemed to be a direct link between polyamine synthesis and T cell proliferation, , promoting macrophage format to M2 and conferring immunomodulatory properties to dendritic cells. , , It was demonstrated that increased levels of polyamines can be detected 24 h postactivation in T cells and that ODC inhibition with DFMO is sufficient to inhibit activation-induced proliferation by using modern mass spectrometry techniques. Furthermore, DFMO, as a polyamine-blocking therapy, has been used to target MDSCs in tumors and inflammatory arthritis. , 
In vitro studies to identify polyamine synthesis and MDSCs focusing on the mechanism are still absent. In our research, the expression of ODC1, SRM, and SMS was almost the same in Th17 cells compared with MDSCs but was clearly elevated in the MDSC + Th17 group. The total polyamine assay in the culture supernatant showed that the MDSC, ORN + Th17 and MDSC + Th17 groups exhibited higher polyamine levels than the Th17, Th17 + DFMO, and Th17 + MDSC + DFMO groups. This result suggested that polyamine precursors derived from MDSCs were metabolized into polyamines in Th17 cells. Taken together, we hypothesized that under the Th17 differentiation system, Arg-1 secreted by MDSCs decomposes ARG to produce ORN, which may further catabolize to produce polyamines, such as to promote the differentiation of Th17 cells. In our study, adding MDSCs or ORN to human or mouse Th17-polarizing medium induced a significant increase in Th17 cells, whereas the presence of DFMO (to inhibit the decomposition of ORN) decreased the ability of MDSCs to promote Th17 differentiation, which is similar to Nir’s study. However, the concentration of DFMO (20 nM) used in our study was much less than that of Nir (100–200 μM), which was unable to inhibit Th17 differentiation ( ); therefore, we postulated that this low DFMO concentration can affect polyamine metabolism only in MDSCs, not in Th17 cells. This finding supports our postulation that Arg-1 produced by MDSCs metabolizes ARG into ORN, after which the downstream metabolites rather than Arg-1 itself stimulate Th17 differentiation. This notion is also supported by increased expression of ODC1, SRM, and SMS during MDSC-promoted Th17 differentiation. Furthermore, directly adding PUT, SPD, or SPM to human Th17 cell culture can significantly increase the expression of IL-17A and RORγt at the mRNA and protein levels, indicating that polyamines derived from MDSCs are crucial for inducing Th17 polarization. In addition, two recent studies have investigated how endogenous polyamine metabolism affects Th17 differentiation, and our current work emphasized exogenous polyamine produced by MDSCs. However, to date, the detailed molecular composition of polyamine transport systems is still undefined in mammalian cells, and antizyme, which inhibits polyamine uptake, also promotes ODC degradation; therefore, it is difficult to verify how polyamine transport influences Th17 polarization.
miR-542 is a multifunctional miRNA involved in many biological processes, including the differentiation of myeloid cells. , In this study, we showed that miR-542-5p levels were upregulated during Th17 differentiation in the presence of MDSCs but downregulated when MDSCs were treated with nor-NOHA. It appears that polyamines, a key product of MDSCs, promote Th17 differentiation by upregulating the expression of miR-542-5p. Additional evidence also comes from the study showing that ORN, MDSCs, or three different polyamines, when added to the Th17 differentiation medium, increase the expression of miR-542-5p, as well as Th17 cells. Moreover, we overexpressed or silenced miR-542–5p and then polarized the cells to Th17 cells. Increased miR-542-5p expression in human naive CD4+ T cells via overexpression also resulted in increased Th17 cells associated with elevation of IL-17A and RORγt expression.
Our work also reveals that miR-542-5p overexpression can downregulate the transcriptional activity of SMAD3, SMAD7, and SMURF1 genes and decrease the protein expression of SMURF1. Although this overexpression has no effect on total SMAD3 expression, it increased the levels of phosphorylated SMAD2/3. SMAD2 and SMAD3 form a hetero-oligomeric complex after their direct activation via phosphorylation by TGF-β type I receptor (TGFβRI), and SMURF1 is an E3 ubiquitin ligase for Smad7. Formation of the SMAD7-SMURF1-TGFβRI complex promotes the proteasomal degradation of TGFβRI, which prevents docking and phosphorylation of the SMAD2/3 complex and diverts these proteins for degradation via ubiquitin-mediated proteasomal degradation. , It is possible that miR-542-5p affects the formation of the SMAD7-SMURF1-TGFβRI complex, which consequently alters Th17 cell differentiation.
Accumulating evidence supports the regulatory roles of miRNAs in disease pathogenesis, including SLE. , Our studies have shown that the expression levels of hsa-miR-542-5p, ODC1, SRM, and SMS positively correlate with disease severity in SLE patients and that the expression levels of these three polyamine synthetases also positively correlate with that of hsa-miR-542-5p. Based on these findings, we propose that the levels of these three polyamine synthetases determine the expression of hsa-miR-542-5p, which is closely related to the severity of SLE. To date, a variety of humanized mouse models have been successfully constructed by transplanting human cells into immunodeficient mice. , In our study, we also validated this finding using a humanized mouse model of SLE. NSG mice receiving SLE patient-derived PBMCs that were transfected with miR-542-5p developed symptoms indicating LN-like disease, which coincided with Th17 cell activation. However, silencing miR-542-5p significantly inhibited disease progression, further supporting an important role of miR-542-5p in SLE development.
In summary, our study provides new insights into MDSC-enhanced Th17 differentiation in SLE by revealing a previously unknown role of MDSC-derived polyamines and T cell-associated miR-542–5p in inflammatory conditions. Our study highlights the importance of the MDSC-T cell interaction in shaping autoimmune T cell responses and suggests that polyamine and miR-542–5p may be potential targets for the treatment of Th17-driven autoimmune disorders such as SLE.

## Materials and methods

## Study design
For human Th17 cell polarization in vitro, healthy pregnant women who were planning to deliver at the First Hospital of Jilin University from May 2018 to June 2020 gave informed consent at their first pregnancy check-up to participate in the present study. Umbilical cord venous blood samples were collected immediately after birth from healthy full-term newborns.
For comparison between SLE patients and HCs, patients fulfilling the 1982 American College of Rheumatology criteria for the classification of SLE were randomly recruited between September 2012 and November 2013 at the First Hospital of Jilin University. The exclusion criteria were pregnancy and acute infection. Disease activity at the time of blood collection was evaluated with the SLEDAI. HCs were randomly recruited, and an effort was made to match them to the SLE patients by age and sex.
For the humanized SLE model mouse construct (described in the next subsection), patients fulfilling the 1982 American College of Rheumatology criteria for the classification of SLE with an SLEDAI ≥10 and LN were randomly recruited from December 2019 to March 2020.
For all experiments using clinical samples, researchers were blinded to the disease status of all samples. All participants provided written informed consent, and all procedures in this study were approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Hospital of Jilin University.

## Mice
6- to 8-week-old male C57BL/6 mice were obtained from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology, and 4- to 6-week-old nonobese diabetic/severe combined immunodeficient IL-2Rγnull (NSG) mice were purchased from the Nanjing Biomedical Research Institute of Nanjing University. All mice were maintained under specific pathogen-free conditions at the animal facility of the First Hospital of Jilin University. Mice were killed via 5% isoflurane inhalation. All animal experiments were approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Hospital of Jilin University on Laboratory Animal Care (No. 2018–018) and were performed according to the guidelines of the Institute for Laboratory Animal Research of the First Hospital of Jilin University Laboratory Animal Center (Changchun, China).
Humanized NSG SLE model mice were prepared as previously described. Briefly, PBMCs were prepared from SLE patients with active disease (SLEDAI ≥ 10; dsDNA ≥ 1:10) and untreated LN. PBMCs or PBMCs – MDSCs (CD11b+ HLA-DR− cell-surface phenotype) were intravenously injected into NSG mice (5 to 10 × 106 cells per mouse). All mice were transfected with siRNA 1 week later for 3 weeks. Mice were sacrificed, and blood and urine were collected from isoflurane-anesthetized mice through cardiac and urinary bladder punctures, respectively. PBMCs, spleen, and kidney were separated immediately. PBMCs and spleen were divided into two parts: one part for flow cytometry (FCM) and one part for the extraction of total genomic DNA for real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) analysis. The kidney was used for qPCR and histopathological analysis. Serum and urine samples were collected in tubes and stored at −80°C until analysis.

## Cell preparation
Mononuclear cells (MNCs) were isolated from C57BL/6 mouse spleens and human umbilical cord venous blood by red blood cell lysis and density gradient centrifugation using Lymphoprep (Axis-shield), respectively. Naive CD4+ T cells were purified from MNCs using a naive CD4+ T cell isolation kit (Miltenyi Biotec) according to the manufacturer’s instructions, and the cell purity after separation was >95% (detected by FCM). MDSCs were separated by cell sorting using an Aria II fluorescence-activated cell sorter (BD, Mountain View, CA, USA). Many studies have confirmed that MDSCs are augmented in the spleen of tumor-bearing mice. WT mice and RM-1 tumor-bearing C57BL/6 mice were used to isolate mouse naive CD4+ T cells and MDSCs, respectively.

## Th17 cell polarization
Purified naive CD4+ T cells (2 to 4 ×105 per well) were cultured under Th17 differentiation conditions in 96-well round-bottom plates. Cells collected from mice were cultured in medium containing 5 μg/mL anti-mouse CD3 mAb (17A2, BioXcell), 1 μg/mL anti-mouse CD28 mAb (37.51.1, eBioscience), 50 μM 2-mercaptoethanol (S6250, Sigma), 5 ng/mL human TGF-β (R&D), 20 ng/mL mouse IL-6 (Peprotech), 5 μg/mL anti-mouse IFN-γ mAb (XMG1.2, BioXcell), and 5 μg/mL anti-mouse IL-4 mAb (11B11, BioXcell) for 3 days. Cells collected from humans were cultured in medium containing 5 μg/mL anti-human CD3 mAb (OKT3, eBioscience), 1 μg/mL soluble anti-human CD28 mAb (CD28.2, eBioscience), 50 μM 2-mercaptoethanol (Sigma), 2.5 ng/mL human TGF-β (R&D), 20 ng/mL human IL-6 (R&D), 10 ng/mL human IL-23 (R&D), 10 ng/mL human IL-1β (R&D), 5 μg/mL anti-human IFN-γ mAb (285-IF-100, R&D), and 5 μg/mL anti-mouse IL-4 mAb (MP4-25D2, BioXcell) for 6–7 days.
To determine the role of MDSCs and polyamines in Th17 cell differentiation, MDSCs (at a 1:1 ratio to naive CD4+ T cells) were added in the presence or absence of ornithine (ORN, 100 nM, Sigma), difluoromethylornithine (DFMO, 20 nM, Tocris), putrescine (PUT, 2.5 μM, Sigma), spermidine (SPD, 2.5 μM, Sigma), or spermine (SPM, 2.5 μM, Sigma).

## Total polyamine assay
Cell culture supernatants from each experiment were kept at −80°C until analysis. A total polyamine assay (Abcam) was performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions. All samples were assayed in duplicate.

## Flow cytometric analysis
FCM was used to isolate or deplete MDSCs and confirm the phenotype of T cells. Various combinations of the following fluorochrome-conjugated mAbs were used: human/mouse CD11b APC/Cy7 (M1/70), mouse Gr-1 PerCP-Cy5.5 (RB6-8C5), mouse CD4 FITC (GK1.5), mouse IL-17A APC (TC11-18H10.1), and mouse CD62l BV421 (MEL-14) from Biolegend; human HLA-DR PE (TU36), human IL-17A BV510 (N49-653), human/mouse RORγt PE (Q21-559), human CD45RA PFITC (HI100), and human pSMAD2/3 AF647 (104) from BD Pharmingen; human CD4 FITC (OKT4) from eBioscience; and anti-Smad2 + Smad3 antibody (EPR19557-4), anti-Smurf1, FITC-conjugated rabbit anti-goat IgG, and goat anti-rabbit IgG H&L AF405 from Abcam. A Fixable Aqua Dead Cell Stain Kit (Invitrogen) and PI (Solarbio) were used to discriminate dead and live cells.
For intracellular staining, 0.2 μL of leukocyte activation cocktail with GolgiPlug (BD Pharmingen) was added to each well and incubated for 4–6 h, after which the cells were stained for surface antigens, fixed and permeabilized with methanol, and stained with fluorochrome-conjugated mAb against the respective intracellular proteins. All samples were collected on an Aria II fluorescence-activated cell sorter (BD, Mountain View, CA, USA) and analyzed by the FlowJo 10.0 software package (TreeStar, Ashland, OR, USA).

## Statistical analyses
GraphPad Prism 8.0 and SPSS version 21.0 software were used for statistical analyses. All data are presented as the mean ± SD. Differences in variables between groups were analyzed using the independent samples, Student’s t test, correlation analyzed using the Spearman rank test, and p < 0.05 was used to indicate statistical significance.
