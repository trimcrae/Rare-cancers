The ability to dive deep into specific rare cell populations
is
critical for understanding tissue physiology and pathology across
various biological domains. As single-cell RNA-seq flourishes, many
newly discovered cell subtypes are defined by their transcriptomic
markers. However, our ability to retrieve and analyze cells based
on their nucleic acid markers remains underdeveloped. Here, we present
Double Emulsion PCR-Initiated Cell sorting and Transcriptomic Sequencing
(DEPICT-seq), a high-throughput droplet nucleic acid cytometry method
that integrates batch cell fixation for cellular information preservation,
double emulsion digital PCR-based cell sorting to target nucleic acid
markers of interest, and in-depth full-length transcriptomic analyses
at single-cell resolution. We utilize DEPICT-seq to isolate and characterize
T cell receptor (TCR)-engineered T cells within a mixed population
and also demonstrate a variation of the workflow by incorporating
an RNase H-dependent PCR step to enrich full-length TCR sequences
for paired single-cell TCR sequencing and transcriptomic profiling.

cell subtypes is intricately linked to cell profiling methods. − 
However, our ability to simultaneously retrieve and analyze
cell
subtypes based on nucleic acid markers remains underdeveloped. For
instance, fluorescence in situ hybridization coupled with fluorescence-activated
cell sorting (FACS) has facilitated cell isolation through the direct
detection of RNA markers, yet the hybridization
probes and cascades of signal amplification precluded downstream single-cell
RNA-seq library construction. , Emulsion PCR has the
potential to achieve cell sorting with single-molecule sensitivity , and distinguish transcript-splicing isoforms. , However,
most droplet-based analysis or screening studies focus on digital
nucleic acid analysis, single-cell genotyping, or a limited subset
of mRNA transcripts in a cell (Figure S1), without access to each cell’s holistic phenotypic profiles. , , − These single-cell droplet sorting methods suffer from mRNA degradation
during cell lysis or thermocycling, which impairs full transcriptomic
analysis after sorting. − The recent nucleic acid cytometry
in semipermeable capsules provides a new technology route with its
analytical versatility and promises great potential, although its transcriptomic analysis power is yet to be
demonstrated. FIND-seq − is among the first attempts to offer a complete workflow
from RNA cytometry to whole transcriptomic sequencing: Fresh cells
are processed right after tissue dissociation by using two-step sequential
microfluidic droplet devices. The single cells are lysed along with
a compartmentalized agarose bead matrix, where their mRNAs are captured
by agarose-conjugated poly dT oligonucleotides and reverse-transcribed
into cDNA upon emulsion breakage. Droplet digital polymerase chain
reaction (dPCR) is then performed on cDNA immobilized on agarose beads
that get re-encapsulated in a single emulsion (SE). The SE droplets
are sorted based on dielectrophoresis implemented in custom microfluidic
instrumentations. Currently, FIND-seq adoption is restricted to a
select few laboratories. , − , − 
Here, we present a nucleic acid cytometry analytical
workflow that
integrates cellular state preservation, target detection, and in-depth
single-cell analysis, termed Double Emulsion PCR-Initiated Cell sorting and Transcriptomic Sequencing (DEPICT-seq). The
procedure is technodissemination friendly by design and has the potential
to greatly upgrade our ability to comprehend unique rare cell subpopulations
and their contributions to tissue physiology and pathology. First,
DEPICT-seq substitutes the initial droplet single-cell encapsulation
step with cell fixation, which enables both sample preservation and
multistep biochemical conversion to be performed in a massively parallel
manner. Second, DEPICT-seq uses double emulsion (DE) dPCR for sorting,
which is compatible with the flow cytometry core infrastructure in
most research institutions. Lastly, to enhance single-cell transcriptomic
analysis, we conducted a benchmark comparison among three whole transcriptomic
amplification methods, followed by a cross-contamination analysis
to demonstrate the purity of the library. As a variation of the workflow,
we added an RNase H-dependent PCR (rhPCR) step to enrich full-length TCR sequences from the DEPICT-seq cDNA
library, achieving paired analysis of TCR sequences and cellular phenotypes
following single cells sorted by the defined RNA marker. This demonstrates
the adaptability and versatility of DEPICT-seq in meeting domain-specific
requirements and accommodating diverse sample preparation methods.

## Experimental Section
Experimental details for standardized
procedures, including cell
culture, device fabrication, cell pretreatment condition test, sequencing,
and analysis, are included in the Supporting Information.

## Methanol Fixation
Cells were resuspended in ice-cold
PBS-BSA-DEPC (1× PBS and 0.5% BSA (Thermo Fisher, 37525) and
1% diethylpyrocarbonate (DEPC; Sangon Biotech, B600154)). The cells
were then passed through a 40 μm strainer into a 15 mL falcon
tube. Four milliliters of ice-cold methanol (Thermo Fisher, M/4000/17)
was then dropwise added to 1 mL of cells. Cells were fixed at −20
°C for 20 min.

## In Situ Reverse Transcription
After two additional
washes (500 g, 4 °C, 3 min) with 1 mL of ice-cold PBS-BSA-RRI
(1× PBS, 0.5% BSA, and 0.8 U/μL RiboLock (Thermo Fisher,
EO0382)), the fixed cells were filtered through a 40 μM cell
strainer and then resuspended in 100 μL of reverse transcription
(RT) mix: 0.5 mM dNTP (Thermo Fisher, R0191), 10 U/μL Maxima
H minus reverse transcriptase (Thermo Fisher, EP0753), 2 U/μL
RnaseOUT (Thermo Fisher, 10777019), 1× RT buffer (Thermo Fisher,
EP0753), 5 mM dithiothreitol (Thermo Fisher, 707265 ML), and 2 μM
STRT-P1-T31 oligo (Table S1, GENEWIZ).
The RT was incubated as follows (with a heated lid set to 60 °C):
50 °C for 10 min, 3 cycles of [8 °C for 12 s, 15 °C
for 45 s, 20 °C for 45 s, 30 °C for 30 s, 42 °C for
2 min, 50 °C for 3 min], and 50 °C for 5 min and then stored
at 4 °C.

## Double Emulsion Generation
DE droplets were generated
using four syringe pumps for cell suspension and inner, oil, and outer
sheath solutions. The oil phase was composed of an HFE-7500 fluorinated
oil (3M) and a 2% PEG–PFPE amphiphilic block copolymer surfactant
(Zhejiang ThunderBio Innovation Ltd.). The carrier phase contained
1% Tween-20 (Bio-Rad, 1610781) and 2% Pluronic F68 (Thermo Fisher,
24040032) in 1× PBS. The PCR mix contained 1× AceTaq Mg2+ Plus buffer (Vazyme, P401), 0.2 U/μL AceTaq DNA Polymerase
(Vazyme, P401), 1% Tween-20, 2% PEG8000 (Promega, V3011), 0.1% Triton
X-100 (Thermo Fisher, 85111), 1.2 μM forward primer, 1.2 μM
reverse primer, and 0.6 μM probe (Table S1). The typical flow rates were 400:120:120:2000 μL/h
(oil/cells/reagents/outer).

## Single-Cell DE-PCR
Given the shell permeability of
DE droplets, the outer phase would be replaced by DE-PCR outer buffer
(1× AceTaq Mg2+ Plus buffer, 1% Tween-20, 2% Pluronic
F68). Finally, a 20 μL droplet pellet was placed in 20 μL
of DE-PCR outer buffer with an additional 2.4 mM MgSO4 (NEB,
B1003S) to increase Taq activity. PCR reactions were performed as
follows: 95 °C 5 min denature, followed by 40 cycles of [95 °C
30 s, 60 °C 30 s, 72 °C 30 s].

## DE Flow Cytometry
Single-cell DEs were analyzed via
FACS using the sdDE-FACS workflow. , 

## Nucleic Acid Release and Recovery
Droplets were sorted
into a dry PCR plate and left for 1 min to evaporate flow cytometry-derived
aerosolized droplets; the change in interfacial tension spontaneously
drove DE breakage. Then, 10 μL of cell lysis mix containing
50 mM Tris pH 8.0 (Thermo Fisher, AM9855G), 50 mM NaCl (Thermo Fisher,
AM9760G), 0.02% SDS (Thermo Fisher, AM9823), 0.4 μg/μL
proteinase K (Thermo Fisher, AM2546) was mixed with each sample and
incubated at 55 °C for 2 h. One microliter of 100 μM PMSF
(Sigma-Aldrich, P7626) was added to inactivate proteinase K and incubated
at room temperature for 10 min. The biotinylated cDNA was collected
by MyOne C1 Dynabeads (Thermo Fisher, 65001).

## Transcriptomic Amplification
MyOne C1 Dynabeads were
resuspended in 10 μL of modified FLASH-seq WTA mix (1.2 mM dNTP mix, 1 U/μL RnaseOUT, 5 mM dithiothreitol,
0.45 mM dCTP (Thermo Fisher, R0151), 1.84 μM FS-TSO (Table S1), 4% Ficoll-400 (Sigma-Aldrich, F5415),
9.2 mM MgCl2 (Thermo Fisher, AM9530G), 0.25 U/μL
Maxima H minus reverse transcriptase, and 1× Kapa HiFi HotStart
Master Mix (Roche, KK2602); the WTA PCR reaction was carried out under
the following conditions: 50 °C 60 min for incubation and 95
°C 3 min denature, followed by 5 cycles of [98 °C 30 s,
65 °C 45 s, 72 °C 3 min]. The beads were removed, and extra
cycles were carried out under the following conditions: 20 cycles
of [98 °C 30 s, 65 °C 20 s, 72 °C 3 min].

## Results

## Design of DEPICT-seq
A pivotal challenge in the investigation
of specific cellular subtypes lies in achieving efficient target detection
and comprehensive molecular RNA profiling. To mitigate the loss of
transcriptomic information due to mRNA degradation, to enhance the
accuracy and sensitivity of nucleic acid–based sorting, and
to ensure single-cell transcriptomic sequencing quality, we devised
a four-step workflow, as illustrated in : (1) information preservation, wherein cell
methanol fixation and permeabilization are performed in bulk, concurrently facilitates in situ RT − while inactivating cellular components that may impede subsequent
dPCR. Biotin-modified poly(T) oligonucleotides were employed to capture
and convert polyadenylated mRNA into more stable biotinylated cDNA,
critical for purification during the later single-cell library prep;
(2) compartmentalization, the fixed cells and PCR master mix are coflowed
into a single-layer microfluidic device, achieving high-throughput
encapsulation by DEs; (3) detection, target-specific DE-dPCR exponentially
amplified nucleic acid during thermocycling, generating high sensitivity
fluorescence signals; and (4) sorting and analysis, DEs containing
target-specific fluorescence are sorted using widely available commercial
flow cytometers, such as SONY SH800. Subsequently, biotinylated nucleic
acids are purified with streptavidin beads, followed by whole transcriptome
amplification for comprehensive analysis.
 

## Cell Fixation and In Situ RT for Improved Compatibility with
qPCR and RNA-seq
The utilization of single-cell dPCR within
minute droplets (<1 nL) has previously encountered challenges stemming
from nonspecific probe hydrolysis, therefore leading to unintended
background fluorescence, induced by high-concentration cell lysates. − Our initial endeavor involved screening various fixation formulations
to identify an optimal protocol that not only enhances detection efficiency
by inactivating endogenous inhibitory components but also maximizes
transcript integrity. We analyze a sample of Jurkat cells and qPCR
TaqMan probe targeting CD45 to identify a specific transcript produced
by these cells (Table S1). Fixation and
permeabilization procedures allow for the conversion of delicate mRNA
molecules into more stable cDNA, which are retained within the fixed
cells ( A).
Previous literature has described successful reverse transcription
polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) from single cells in droplets,
achieved through either diluting cell lysates before RT or employing
physical or chemical inactivation methods. , − To evaluate the impact of fixation formulations on signal detection,
we compared the effects of live cell lysates, alkaline-lysis cell
lysates, heat-inactivation cell lysates, and paraformaldehyde (PFA)
fixation alongside methanol-fixed cells on probe integrity. After
probe incubation, increased fluorescence signals attributable to probe
cleavage were observed in all groups except the methanol-fixed cells ( B and S2), with live cell lysates
exhibiting the most pronounced probe cleavage. While alkaline lysis,
heat inactivation, and PFA fixation partially mitigated cell lysate-induced
inhibition, methanol fixation exhibited superior efficacy in eliminating
high-concentration cell lysate-induced probe cleavage (1 × 105 cells per μL, equivalent to 1 cell in a 35 μm
droplet). We then proceeded to assess the PCR performance on methanol-fixed
cells, revealing a highly correlated fluorescence signal with cell
density ( C).
To elucidate the impact of fixation on transcriptomic integrity, single
live or postfixation cells were sorted into well plates and subjected
to standard FLASH-seq. Profiling of cDNA
revealed that methanol fixation, when combined with a commercial protein
RNase inhibitor, led to substantial mRNA degradation, consistent with
prior findings. , We postulated that methanol may
accidentally inactivate the commercial protein RNase inhibitor, allowing
extracellular and intraorganellar ribonucleases to infiltrate the
cytoplasm and degrade RNA before enzyme denaturation occurs. Upon
substitution of the commercial protein RNase inhibitor with DEPC,
mRNA degradation during fixation was mitigated ( D). The methanol fixation-based cell encapsulation
method, which simultaneously fixes and permeabilizes cells, offers ease of handling, maximizes transcript
preservation, and holds promise as a preferred storage method for
single-cell transcriptomic samples.
 

## Single-Cell Encapsulation and DE-dPCR
To compartmentalize
post-RT cells, we designed a one-layer, one-step microfluidics chip
( A and S3), which obviates the need for intricate 3D
alignment in multilayer microfluidic chip assembly, , , thereby enabling accessible chip fabrication
on a homemade soft lithography platform (Figure S4). Following device bonding, an extended baking process ensures
the hydrophobic recovery of PDMS to facilitate proper encapsulation
at the first junction. Plasma treatment is administered to render
the second junction hydrophilic ( B). The visible demarcation line between
the cell solution and the dPCR Mastermix facilitates fine-tuning of
each inlet’s flux ratio ( B). The droplets generated at the first nozzle traverse
the serpentine channel for proper mixing and arrive at the second
nozzle, where excess oil is sheared by the outer phase to yield thin-shell
DEs and oil droplets ( B). Both thin-shell DEs and HFE7500 oil-in-water single droplets
are generated, yet a simple gravitational separation step allows for
efficient enrichment of the DEs above the droplet pellet (Figure S5). To achieve flow cytometry compatibility,
we produce DEs of sufficiently small dimensions (33.46 ± 0.47
μm in inner diameter and 42.08 ± 0.70 μm in outer
diameter; C).
Comparative analysis demonstrates that DEs exhibit greater thermal
stability than SEs (Figure S6). The Jurkat
and NIH3T3 cell mixtures and TaqMan PCR mix targeting CD45 were introduced
to yield DEs. Following thermocycling, DEs undergo slight swelling
(42.26 ± 1.03 μm in inner diameter and 50.15 ± 1.07
μm in outer diameter; D,E), likely to maintain osmotic equilibrium across
the inner and outer aqueous phases. Subsequent digital image analysis
of post-PCR DEs reveals that the biological membrane remains intact
and forms a distinct, spatially confined fluorescence pattern ( F), likely attributable
to nonspecific adsorption of fluorescent probes to intracellular contents. In contrast, the fluorescence emanating from
PCR amplicons is uniformly distributed throughout the entire DE volume
( G). Consequently,
profiling the fluorescence across the entire DE affords a distinct
and highly sensitive means of accurately discerning partitions carrying
cells and segregating them from those containing cell-free nucleic
acid molecules. 
 

## Flow Cytometry for Rare Cell Target Sorting
Building
upon the microscopy features, we conducted flow cytometry on post-PCR
DEs containing either Jurkat cells, NIH3T3 cells, or a mixture of
both, employing a flow cytometry instrument ( A). Following a brief pickup delay, DEs were
gated based on the forward scatter area (FSC-A) versus the side scatter
area (SSC-A) to analyze larger particles and discard signals arising
from residual oil droplets ( B). Next, gating was applied based on fluorescence
signal (FAM) intensity to identify DEs carrying cells ( C), which differs from the
Dim DEs with weak and diffuse fluorescence signals (Figure S7A). In the final step, gating was performed based
on FAM-H versus FAM-A to isolate DEs with fluorescence distributed
throughout the entire droplet volume ( D). To ensure high positive predictive value
of our method, about 30% positive droplets were inadvertently excluded
(Figure S7B,C). Improvements in signal
acquisition (e.g., using an imaging flow cytometer) or enhancement
of the signal-to-noise ratio of the assay (e.g., using double-quenched
probes) might help better separate positive droplets from negative
ones. Figure S7C showcases RNA cytometry
of NIH3T3 cells spiked with various dilutions of Jurkat cells, highlighting
the potential of DE-based RNA cytometry in identifying low-abundance
cell subtypes. Subsequent examination of each well under the microscope
confirmed reliable sorting of positive DE droplets (Figure S8). , 
 

## Optimization of Single-Cell mRNA Profiling
Following
sorting, biotinylated cDNA from target cells is purified with streptavidin
beads. Previous investigations have demonstrated the efficacy of SMART-seq-like
methodologies for constructing libraries from cDNA bound to streptavidin
beads. − To optimize library construction efficiency from
streptavidin bead-collected cDNA, we compared three modified transcriptomic
library approaches, comprising two template switch-based methods,
namely, SMART-seq3 -like and FLASH-seq -like methods, alongside a TdT tailing -based method (Figure S9A). Analysis was conducted on 10 samples (4 FLASH-seq-like samples,
3 SMART-seq3-like samples, and 3 TdT tailing-based samples). Subsequent
analysis demonstrated that the FLASH-seq-like method exhibited a superior
exon mapping ratio (Figure S9C) and greater
library complexity (Figure S9B–E). Given its advantages in both labor and performance, we adopted
the FLASH-seq-like method as the standard protocol for DEPICT-seq.
To validate the specificity and accuracy of DE-based dPCR flow cytometry,
we sorted 16 DEs carrying cells and amplified the cDNA released from
the DEs exhibiting either positive or negative amplicon fluorescence
signals. In a mixed species experiment, the Jurkat and NIH3T3 populations
distinctly segregate ( A). This attests to the recoverability of cDNA from each droplet
postsorting, devoid of major material loss or cross-contamination
from emulsion breakage. Our analysis
shows that all positive DEs exclusively contain Jurkat cells, whereas
negative DEs encompass not only NIH3T3 cells but also Jurkat cells
( A, B). This
observation suggests that partial DEs harboring Jurkat cells might
not exhibit fluorescence signals, aligning with flow cytometry results
( E).
 

## Characterization of Genetically Engineered Cells
Previous
studies indicate that the activation potency of TCR-T cells may not
directly correlate with high pMHC binding affinity, presenting challenges
for low-affinity TCR-T cell isolation using pMHC multimer staining
methods. By targeting the transfection
payload of sequence of recombinant TCR-T cells rather than cell surface
TCRs, we used DEPICT-seq to isolate TCR-T cells in an artificial spike-in
cell mixture (TCR-T/NIH3T3 = 1:100). To gauge both potential technical
biases and the protocol’s efficacy in delineating cell populations,
we integrated TCR-T single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data obtained
from standard FLASH-seq (Live, n = 7) and DEPICT-seq
(n = 9) with Jurkat (n = 50, 10×
Genomics Chromium) and human peripheral
blood mononuclear cell (PBMC_1K_V3, 10× Genomics Chromium) scRNA-seq
data. The larger cell population enabled us to annotate major cell
types based on population-specific markers ( C and S10). tSNE
visualization demonstrated that different methods yielded inferred
biological states maintaining consistent population structures and
biological information ( C), and the increasing sequence depth enhanced the library
complexity of DEPICT-seq ( D). Notably, DEPICT-seq performed better with higher
RNA quantities (Figure S11), suggesting
an input-sensitive optimum. As shown in E, ribosomal reads constituted a substantial
portion of detected transcripts (37.2%) from 10× Genomics, which
was 80 times higher than those from DEPICT-seq (0.45%), and the significantly
lower ribosomal reads of DEPICT-seq might result from multiple washes
after streptavidin bead collection and mRNA capture efficiency between
protocols.
To enhance the utility of DEPICT-seq in T-cell biology
research, we adopted rhPCR to amplify
full-length TCR sequences from the cDNA library post DE sorting (Figure S12A,B). Both full-length TCRα and
TCRβ sequences were successfully retrieved, allowing for the
reconstruction of TCRs recognizing specific MHC–antigen complexes
(Figure S12C). In addition to TCR discovery,
paired single-cell TCR sequencing and transcriptomic profiling enable
the joint analysis of T-cell clonality and phenotype. This approach
uses the TCR sequence as a unique barcode for tracking T-cell clones
over time or across PBMCs and different tissues.

## Discussion
In this work, we introduce DEPICT-seq, a
democratized method that
integrates cellular information preservation, detection, and isolation
based on specific nucleic acid markers, and comprehensive transcriptomic
sequencing analysis and elucidate vital details in its development:
Leveraging the TaqMan system, DE-dPCR enables nucleic acid marker-assisted
cell sorting in a highly specific manner, encompassing both endogenous
(DNA, including SNP, mRNA, spliced transcripts, noncoding RNA, etc.)
and exogenous (natural viral RNA, transfected plasmids, artificial
oligos, etc.) targets. After amplification, the droplets containing
the target exhibit diffuse fluorescence, enabling the absolute quantification
of positive cells and facilitating sorting. Although the reason for
the intracellular enrichment of fluorescent dyes in DE-dPCR remains
unclear (another assay utilizing Cy3 dye also exhibits a similar effect),
this characteristic enables accurate recapitulation of the total cell
count, thereby keeping the original frequency information on cell
subtypes.
Compared to the method based on semipermeable capsules or agarose beads, , DEPICT-seq
decouples the first sample fixation and RT steps from the downstream
microfluidic DE formation and droplet sorting steps, which allows
for decentralized sample preparation and microfluidic-specialized
streamline processes. Longitudinal studies may also benefit since
samples could go through cellular information preservation at separate
time points, while the library preparation steps can be performed
on the same day to allow for homogeneous quality control. Our modified
single-cell FLASH-seq workflow greatly
diminishes the labor, time, and cost of DEPICT-seq, thereby producing
high-quality transcriptional data. Fixed cells have been demonstrated
to be compatible with various additional in situ molecular biology
reactions, such as T4 ligation and Tn5
tagmentation. , These capabilities enhance the
potential of DEPICT-seq for epigenomic profiling of targeted cell
subpopulations or for utilizing the split-pool method to prebarcode
fixed cells, enabling high-throughput profiling of desired cell subpopulations.
We further demonstrated a variation of the DEPICT-seq workflow
for paired single-cell TCR sequencing and transcriptomic profiling.
The ability to isolate specific T-cell subpopulation based on RNA
markers and track their in vivo biology could provide valuable insights
into the etiology and pathology of immune-mediated diseases or track
infused therapeutic subpopulation during cell therapies for mechanistic
studies and improved engineering strategies. Combined with antigen-specific cell transfections, , viral payload-targeted DEPICT-seq can potentially decode the interaction
between the TCR and presented antigens in biological or clinical samples.

## Conclusions
In summary, DEPICT-seq is capable of isolating
cell subpopulations
defined by nucleic acid signatures, followed by deep molecular profiling.
This approach is akin to conducting a wet-lab experimental BLAST (Basic
Local Alignment Search Tool) query on initial shallow single-cell
RNA-seq of a large number of cells, which invites the revelation of
lead markers that trigger deeper analysis using DEPICT-seq to uncover
additional RNA markers. This iterative loop, from RNA-seq to RNA-targeted
cell sorting, can be integrated with downstream chemical or genetic
perturbations and biochemical experiments, presenting a novel and
potent method for investigating biologically significant subpopulations.
