## Abstract
Septic cardiomyopathy (SCM) is a prevalent and severe complication associated with sepsis. This study explores the effects of sevoflurane and pentobarbital on lipopolysaccharide (LPS) -induced SCM and elucidates underlying mechanisms. The SCM model was established using an intraperitoneal injection of 10 mg/kg LPS. Pentobarbital and sevoflurane were administered thirty minutes post-model establishment. Following the echocardiographic assessment, mice were euthanized 24 h after the modeling, and cardiac samples were collected. Gene sequencing and western blot were utilized to identify potential hub genes and signaling pathways. Sevoflurane markedly reduced LPS-induced myocardial injury and cardiac dysfunction compared to the pentobarbital intervention. Transcriptome sequencing revealed that numerous genes exhibited differential expression following intervention with sevoflurane and pentobarbital, with predominant enrichment in the signaling pathways, such as the extracellular region and matrix, tumor necrosis factor (TNF), and p53 signaling. Sevoflurane significantly induced TATA-box binding protein-associated factor, RNA polymerase I subunit D (TAF1D) expression and attenuated cardiomyocyte death, oxidative stress, and the secretion of IL-6 and TNF-α compared to the pentobarbital group (p < 0.05). Furthermore, oe-TAF1D significantly exacerbated cardiomyocyte death, oxidative stress, and inflammatory responses, which were alleviated with si-TAF1D (p < 0.05). Sevoflurane mitigates sepsis-induced cell death, oxidative stress, and inflammatory responses by up-regulating TAF1D, consequently diminishing cardiac injury and preserving cardiac function.

## Introduction
Sepsis is a critical systemic inflammatory response syndrome principally characterized by the dysregulation of the body’s response to inflammation, resulting in damage and eventual failure of multiple organ system-wide . Sepsis ranks among the conditions with the highest morbidity and mortality rates globally. According to the Global Burden of Disease Study 2017, 48.9 million cases of sepsis occurred worldwide in 2020, with 11 million deaths, representing approximately 20% of all global deaths . A retrospective study revealed that among patients admitted to intensive care units, the in-hospital mortality rate was 32% for those diagnosed with sepsis and escalated to 56% for those suffering from septic shock . Sepsis results from a complex interplay of various pathological mechanisms, primarily characterized by a homeostatic imbalance between pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory responses. The uncontrolled inflammatory response triggered by sepsis leads to the secretion of a plethora of detrimental cytokines and chemokines. These mediators induce vascular endothelial cell damage and cell death by activating the coagulation cascade, promoting the excessive production of reactive oxygen species, and compromising mechanical barriers , . Collectively, these processes culminate in multiorgan dysfunction.
Studies have shown that more than 40% of patients with bacteremia present with elevated serum troponin- a marker of myocardial injury- highlighting the significant impact of sepsis on heart health . Over 50% of septic patients in the intensive care unit exhibit myocardial damage, which results in either systolic or diastolic cardiac dysfunction . Patients with sepsis who develop cardiac dysfunction experience a 20% to 50% increase in mortality rates . The primary characteristic of septic cardiomyopathy (SCI) is ventricular systolic dysfunction, which is manifested by biventricular dilatation, reduced ejection fraction, lowered systemic vascular resistance (SVR), and diminished cardiac output/cardiac index . Nonetheless, the pathways through which sepsis induces cardiac dysfunction are intricate and not well understood. Research indicates that the pathological mechanism of SCI encompasses a diverse range of factors, including dysregulation of inflammatory mediators, mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress, calcium dysregulation, and endothelial dysfunction. Plasma TNF-α concentrations in septic patients are inversely correlated with cardiac function in a dose-dependent manner, whereas monoclonal anti-TNF antibodies have been shown to transiently enhance ventricular function in individuals experiencing septic shock , . Mitochondrial ultrastructural damage is commonly observed in cardiomyocytes from both septic patients and animal models . Persistent mitochondrial dysfunction in these cardiomyocytes leads to substantial production of reactive oxygen species (ROS), which subsequently exacerbates mitochondrial damage and perpetuates a deleterious cycle of worsening mitochondrial dysfunction.
Sevoflurane and pentobarbital are extensively utilized anesthetics in clinical settings, with their safety and efficacy being well-documented. Clinical research has established that sevoflurane provides a cardioprotective effect during cardiac surgeries, impacting hemodynamic and cardiovascular parameters to a lesser extent than other anesthetics . Sevoflurane demonstrates a wide range of anti-inflammatory actions by interacting with immune cells, including neutrophils and macrophages. Xi Jia Sun et al. found that sevoflurane mitigates the signaling of nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB) triggered by LPS via the Toll-like receptor 4 signaling pathway, thereby exerting anti-inflammatory effects . Sevoflurane effectively decreases the migration and adhesion of immune cells, including neutrophils, to vascular endothelial cells and suppresses neutrophil apoptosis in vivo . Thomas J. Gerber et al. discovered that sevoflurane enhances macrophage phagocytosis and diminishes the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines induced by endotoxins through the upregulation of nitric oxide synthase expression . Nonetheless, the protective mechanism by which sevoflurane shields against sepsis-induced cardiac injury remains largely undefined. Therefore, this study employed both cellular and animal models to investigate and compare the impacts of sevoflurane and pentobarbital interventions on LPS-induced cardiac injury. We further utilized advanced techniques such as gene sequencing and bioengineering to elucidate the specific mechanisms by which sevoflurane protects cardiac function by reducing cardiomyocyte apoptosis, decreasing ROS release, and attenuating inflammatory responses.

## Material and methods

## Study design
The research design is shown in Fig.  . Firstly, we compared the effects of sevoflurane and pentobarbital on cardiac injury in an LPS-induced sepsis mouse model and found that sevoflurane significantly improved cardiac dysfunction and myocardial injury (Fig.  A). Secondly, we employed both translational and transcriptome sequencing to investigate the differentially expressed genes and pathways enriched following interventions with sevoflurane and pentobarbital. Additionally, we utilized Western blot analysis to validate the potential pivotal role of TAF1D in these processes (Fig.  B). Thirdly, we employed both translational and transcriptome sequencing to investigate the differentially expressed genes and pathways enriched following interventions with sevoflurane and pentobarbital. Additionally, we utilized western blot analysis to validate the potentially pivotal role of TAF1D in these processes. Finally, we elucidated the mechanism by which sevoflurane attenuates cardiomyocyte apoptosis, reduces the release of ROS, and diminishes the inflammatory response through the upregulation of TAF1D expression (Fig.  C).
 

## Experimental grouping and animal modeling
Male C57BL/6 mice, aged 8–12 weeks, weighing 20–30 g, were obtained from the Hunan SJA Laboratory Animal Co., Ltd. These mice were housed in micro-isolated cages maintained at 24 °C with a light–dark cycle of 12:12 h and were allowed to eat and drink freely. Animal care and use throughout the experiments were approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of the Second Xiangya Hospital. All animal experiments were conducted in accordance with the relevant designated guidelines and regulations and in compliance with the ARRIVE Guidelines. A total of 42 mice were included in this study, and no mice died. The animal experiment has four parts: Part I: 24 mice were randomly divided into control (n = 6), LPS (n = 6), LPS + pentobarbital (n = 6), and LPS + sevoflurane (n = 6) groups. Echocardiography was performed 12 h after modeling, euthanasia was carried out one day after modeling, and cardiac specimens were retained for histological analysis. Part II: 6 mice were randomly divided into LPS + pentobarbital (n = 3) and LPS + sevoflurane (n = 3) groups; euthanasia was performed one day after modeling, and cardiac specimens were taken for transcriptome (1 ug for each sample) and proteomics (20 ug for each sample) sequencing. Part III: 12 mice were randomly divided into control (n = 3), LPS (n = 3), LPS + pentobarbital (n = 3), and LPS + sevoflurane (n = 3) groups, euthanized one day after modeling, and cardiac specimens were retained for Western blot.
The control group received an intraperitoneal injection of saline at a dose of 10 mg/kg. In the LPS group, mice were administered 10 mg/kg of LPS intraperitoneally. For the sevoflurane group, mice underwent a 30-min exposure to 2% sevoflurane inhalation following a 30-min post-intraperitoneal injection of LPS at 10 mg/kg as previously described , . In the pentobarbital group, 1% pentobarbital at a dosage of 80 mg/kg was administered following a 30-min post-LPS injection. Mice were modeled for one day and then underwent functional cardiac ultrasound, followed by euthanasia and perfusion to collect cardiac specimens.

## Echocardiography
Echocardiography was performed 12 h after modeling, as previously described . Three consecutive cardiac cycles were measured for each parameter, and the mean value was calculated. The ultrasound system measured left ventricular end-systolic volume (LVESV), left ventricular end-diastolic volume (LVEDV), output per beat (SV), cardiac output (CO), left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), left ventricular shortening fraction (LVFS), left ventricular internal diameter in diastole (LVIDd), and left ventricular internal diameters in systole (LVIDs).

## Hematoxylin and eosin (HE) staining
HE staining was conducted as previously described . Briefly, mouse myocardial tissues were fixed in 10% paraformaldehyde for 24 h, subsequently embedded in paraffin, and then sectioned. The sections were stained sequentially with hematoxylin and 5% eosin. Finally, the slides were mounted with neutral resin and air-dried before microscopic examination.

## Western blot analysis
Western blot analysis was conducted as previously described . 30 μg of protein from each sample was used for Western blot analysis. The primary antibodies were monoclonal rabbit anti-RCAN1 (Biorbyt, orb213860, 1:200 dilution), monoclonal rabbit anti-CNTN2 (Biorbyt, orb214642, 1:200 dilution), monoclonal rabbit anti-KLHL41 (Biorbyt, orb540149, 1:200 dilution), monoclonal rabbit anti-TAF1D (Biorbyt, orb35890, 1:200 dilution), monoclonal rabbit anti-TAF1D (Biorbyt, orb35890, 1:200 dilution) and monoclonal mouse anti-β-actin (Proteintech, 60,008–1-Ig, 1:20,000 dilution). Secondary antibodies were rabbit anti-mouse IgG (Bio-Rad, 1:2000 dilution) and goat anti-rabbit IgG (Bio-Rad, 1:5000 dilution). Western blots were analyzed using an enhanced chemiluminescence system (Amersham), followed by an analysis of the relative density of the bands using NIH Image J. The original blot/gels used in this paper are shown in Fig. .

## Cell culture and treatments
The cell culture and treatment were adapted from previous research . H9C2 cells (Purchased from the company Abiowell) were chosen for the follow-up in vitro studies, which were structured into two parts. The first part consisted of four groups: the control group, the LPS group, the group treated with LPS and pentobarbital, and the group treated with LPS and sevoflurane. The second part consisted of six groups: the control group, the LPS group, the group treated with LPS and Vector, the group treated with LPS and oe-TAF1D, the group treated with LPS, sevoflurane, and si-NC, and the group treated with LPS, sevoflurane, and si-TAF1D.

## Real-time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR)
The expression of the TAF1D gene in H9C2 cells was assessed using RT-qPCR, as delineated in prior studies . Briefly, we extracted RNA using the TRIzol method. Next, we prepared the reaction mixture for the reverse transcription process, followed by the preparation of the PCR reaction mix for amplification using the SYBR method. Finally, we detected the PCR products through agarose gel electrophoresis. The RT-PCR-related information (reaction systems, concentrations, primer designs, etc.) for each step is detailed in Tables S1-S4. Concentration was determined by measuring absorbance at 260 nm and 280 nm using a UV spectrophotometer, facilitating the calculation of purity-adjusted concentrations.

## Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)
Supernatants from mouse and cell cultures were analyzed using ELISA to quantify the levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6 and TNF-α) and oxidative stress markers (lactate dehydrogenase and malondialdehyde) as previously described . The assays were performed in accordance with the protocols provided by the ELISA kits from Wuhan Huamei Biological Co., Ltd. and Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute. Optical density (OD) values at a wavelength of 450 nm were measured using a multi-mode reader.

## Cell counting Kit-8 assays (CCK8)
The proliferative capacity of different groups of H9C2 cells was assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (Beijing, YT-CJ-2NB) according to a previously established protocol . H9C2 cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 5 × 103 cells per well. The optical density (OD) of each well was measured sequentially at 450 nm using an ELISA plate reader.

## Apoptosis assays for flow cytometry
Apoptosis in H9C2 cells was assessed using the Annexin V-APC/PI Dual Staining Apoptosis Detection Kit (Jiangsu, KGA1030-1007), following the manufacturer’s recommended protocol, as described previously . Initially, cells underwent EDTA-free trypsin digestion after intervention grouping. Subsequently, cells underwent two PBS washes and were centrifuged at 1000 rpm for 5 min, resulting in an approximate yield of 4.8 × 105 cells. These cells were then resuspended in 500 µL of binding buffer. Following resuspension, 5 µL of Annexin V-APC was added and thoroughly mixed. This step was succeeded by the addition of 5 µL of Propidium Iodide, with careful mixing to ensure uniformity. The staining procedure was conducted for 10 min at room temperature, protected from light exposure. Subsequently, apoptosis was analyzed using a flow cytometer within one hour.

## Sample preparation and sequencing
Mouse tissues were rapidly frozen in liquid nitrogen immediately after collection. They were then pulverized to a fine powder under liquid nitrogen using a mortar and pestle. Subsequently, the powdered tissues were solubilized in 400 µL of lysis buffer. The recovery of ribosomal footprints (RFs) and the construction and sequencing of circRNA, LncRNA, and mRNA libraries are described in Supplementary Methods. Briefly, ribosomal RNA was removed following the extraction of total RNA from the samples. The mRNA was then purified and fragmented, and complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized using a reverse transcriptase hybridization system. The purified double-stranded cDNA underwent amplification through PCR libraries after end repair, A-tailing, and adapter ligation. The amplified cDNA was purified with VAHTS DNA Clean Beads and sequenced using an Illumina Nova sequencer.

## Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and enrichment analysis
RNA differential expression analysis was performed by DESeq2 software between two different groups , . Genes or transcripts exhibiting a false discovery rate (FDR) below 0.05 and an absolute fold change of 2 or greater were classified as differentially expressed. Proteins exhibiting FDR below 0.05 and an absolute fold change of 1.2 or greater were classified as differentially expressed. Subsequent pathway enrichment analysis for these differentially expressed genes was conducted using the Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway databases , .

## Results

## Effect of sevoflurane and pentobarbital on LPS-induced cardiac injury
Initially, we conducted histopathological assessments of four mouse groups using HE staining (Fig.  A and B). We observed significant cellular edema, disorganized myofilament arrangement, degradation, and varying degrees of necrosis in the cardiomyocytes of septic mice, which were mitigated following treatment with sevoflurane. Cardiac function in the model mice was evaluated using echocardiography (Fig.  A and D). We observed that the LVEF and LVFS were significantly reduced, whereas the LVIDd and LVIDs were significantly elevated in septic mice compared to the control group (p < 0.01). Importantly, in septic mice treated with sevoflurane, there was a significant increase in LVEF and LVFS and a significant decrease in LVIDd and LVIDs when compared to those treated with pentobarbital (p < 0.01). These findings indicated that sevoflurane significantly alleviates cardiac injury in septic mice.
 

## Transcriptome and translatome sequencing, differential gene, and enriched pathway analysis following intervention with sevoflurane and pentobarbital
Next, we conducted transcriptome and translatome sequencing analyses to thoroughly investigate the specific mechanisms through which sevoflurane ameliorates cardiac dysfunction (Figs. – ). The transcriptome sequencing results revealed 607 differentially expressed mRNAs following the intervention between the two groups, with 225 genes up-regulated and 382 genes down-regulated (Fig. A–C). GO enrichment analysis found that these differentially expressed genes were primarily associated with pathways of extracellular matrix and region, extracellular region part, and extracellular space (Fig. D). KEGG enrichment analysis found that these differentially expressed genes were primarily associated with pathways of viral protein interaction with cytokine and cytokine receptors, cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, Mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling, and TNF signaling (Fig. E). The translational sequencing results revealed 1,130 differentially expressed proteins following the intervention between the two groups, with 571 proteins up-regulated and 559 proteins down-regulated (Fig. A–C). GO enrichment analysis found that these differentially expressed proteins were primarily associated with pathways of the extracellular matrix and region, extracellular region part, and cytoplasm (Fig. D). KEGG enrichment analysis found that these differentially expressed proteins were primarily associated with pathways of viral protein interaction with cytokine and cytokine receptors, focal adhesion, MAPK signaling, and TNF signaling (Fig. E). A total of 132 differentially expressed cirRNAs following the intervention between the two groups, with 66 cirRNAs up-regulated and 66 cirRNAs down-regulated (Fig. A–C). GO enrichment analysis indicated that these differentially expressed cirRNAs were primarily associated with pathways of intracellular, intracellular part, and organelle, and organelle (Fig. D). KEGG enrichment analysis indicated that these differentially expressed cirRNAs were primarily associated with pathways of growth hormone synthesis, secretion and action, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, cAMP signaling, and MAPK signaling (Fig. E). Integrating the aforementioned sequencing outcomes with existing literature, we investigated the expression of potential hub genes within animal models (Fig.  ). Western blot analysis revealed a significant upregulation in the expression of proteins CNTN2 and TAF1D in the cardiac tissues of mice treated with sevoflurane compared to those subjected to pentobarbital intervention (p < 0.05). CNTN2 is a glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI)-anchored neuronal membrane protein that functions as a cell adhesion molecule. It primarily plays a pivotal role in the formation of axonal junctions within the nervous system . TAF1D is essential for the transcriptional activity of RNA polymerase I and has the capacity to interact with various cellular proteins to assemble diverse protein complexes. TAF1D plays a pivotal role in several biological processes by regulating transcription, fostering cellular proliferation, and sustaining the structural and functional stability of cells . Therefore, we hypothesize that TAF1D may be the critical gene mediating the protective effects of sevoflurane.
 

## Sevoflurane reduced cardiac injury by attenuating cardiomyocyte apoptosis, oxidative stress, and inflammatory responses through upregulation of TAF1D
Finally, the results of RT-qPCR (Fig.  A) and western blot (Fig.  B) suggested that TAF1D expression was significantly up-regulated in H9C2 cells after sevoflurane intervention compared to pentobarbital intervention (p < 0.01). Cell survival and cytotoxicity were assessed using CCK-8 (Fig.  C) and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) release assays (Fig.  D), respectively. These assays demonstrated a significant decline in cell survival and an elevation in cytotoxicity among LPS-induced septic mice relative to the control group (p < 0.01). Notably, these detrimental effects were significantly alleviated following sevoflurane administration (p < 0.01). Flow cytometric analysis revealed that sevoflurane treatment significantly reduced apoptosis levels when compared to the response seen with pentobarbital treatment (p < 0.01) (Fig.  E). ELISA data further indicated that sevoflurane administration substantially decreased the secretion of inflammatory cytokines, including IL-6 and TNF-α, as well as levels of ROS response, in comparison to the pentobarbital-treated group (p < 0.05) (Fig.  F).
 Furthermore, we employed both small interfering RNA (si-RNA) (Fig.  A) and over-expressed RNA (oe-RNA) (Fig.  B) to modulate the expression of the TAF1D protein (p < 0.01). The overexpression of TAF1D significantly enhanced cell survival and reduced cytotoxicity under LPS-induced conditions (p < 0.01) (Fig.  C and D). Furthermore, the cytoprotective effects of sevoflurane were substantially attenuated by the downregulation of TAF1D expression (p < 0.01). Likewise, the protective efficacy of sevoflurane in mitigating LPS-induced apoptosis, inflammation, and ROS responses was significantly diminished following the downregulation of TAF1D expression (p < 0.05) (Fig.  E and F).
 

## Discussion
This study assessed the impacts of two anesthetics, sevoflurane and pentobarbital, on LPS-induced cardiomyocyte injury and cardiac dysfunction. We found that sevoflurane exhibited a more substantial protective effect than pentobarbital. Further analysis involving sequencing and in vivo experiments identified TAF1D as a critical mediator in the cardioprotective mechanisms of sevoflurane. Additionally, in vitro experiments clarified the specific pathways by which sevoflurane attenuates LPS-induced cytotoxicity, apoptosis, inflammatory response, and ROS generation by up-regulating TAF1D expression in cardiomyocytes.
Currently, numerous clinical and foundational studies have explored the cardioprotective effects of volatile anesthetics, particularly sevoflurane, on cardiac tissue. For example, in a comprehensive meta-analysis incorporating 15 clinical trials, Feng Li et al. demonstrated that sevoflurane significantly enhances cardiac index and output, diminishes the necessity for vasoconstrictors and positive inotropic drugs, abbreviates ICU stays, and lowers the frequency of postoperative atrial fibrillation following cardiac surgery. Yiqi Weng et al. revealed that sevoflurane significantly decreased serum concentrations of cardiac troponin I (cTnI), creatine kinase MB (CK-MB), IL-6, TNF-α, and HMGB1 in children undergoing transplantation, thereby attenuating postischemic myocardial mechanical dysfunction and myocardial injury. In a recent randomized controlled trial, Alberto Zangrillo et al. demonstrated that preoperative administration of sevoflurane significantly enhanced hemodynamic stability and decreased the incidence of myocardial infarction within 48 h post-surgery while also reducing long-term cardiac mortality. Our findings broadly concur with previous research, indicating that sevoflurane notably alleviates cardiac injury and sepsis-induced dysfunction.
As a commonly utilized clinical inhalation anesthetic, sevoflurane exhibits multifaceted cardioprotective mechanisms through various biochemical and physiological pathways . These include the reduction of inflammation, antioxidant activity, stabilization of mitochondrial function, modulation of ion channels, inhibition of apoptosis, and the preservation of energy production within cardiomyocytes. In this study, through sequencing mouse heart tissues and comparing the effects of sevoflurane and pentobarbital interventions, we identified numerous differentially expressed genes and associated enriched pathways, including extracellular matrix and region, extracellular region part, and extracellular space, cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, Mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling, and TNF signaling. The cardiac extracellular matrix forms a complex z-crosslinked network comprised of both fibrillar and non-fibrillar components within the extracellular space. This matrix plays a critical role in signaling and providing a mechanical framework essential for preserving the structural integrity and functional capacity of cardiac cells . MAPK is a serine/threonine-specific protein kinase activated by diverse extracellular stimuli . It orchestrates significant changes in crucial intracellular processes, fostering cell growth, apoptosis, and transformation. Research indicates that MAPK is a principal signaling pathway implicated in the development of cardiac hypertrophy and heart failure . The role of the TNF family in myocardial diseases is multifaceted and debated. TNF-α, on the one hand, heightens the expression of adhesion molecules, initiates hypertrophic responses and ROS release, and promotes apoptosis . Conversely, TNFR2 offers cardioprotective effects, safeguarding cardiomyocytes against apoptosis and boosting cellular resilience to oxidative stress through the activation of endothelial/epithelial tyrosine kinases .
Through the analysis of differentially expressed genes and subsequent validation in vivo experiments, we identified TAF1D as the pivotal gene through which sevoflurane mediates its protective effects. TAF1D, an integral member of the SL1 protein complex, plays a central role in RNA polymerase I transcription and the regulation of proteins post-transcriptionally within cellular processes . In our current study, we discovered that sevoflurane mitigates apoptosis, inflammation, and the release of ROS, ultimately diminishing LPS-induced cardiac injury by up-regulating the expression of TAF1D. Uncontrolled inflammatory response is one of the key pathologic mechanisms of sepsis-induced cardiac injury. Ibrahim Akin et al. found that LSP enhances the expression of pro-inflammatory and chemotactic cytokines, including TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, CCL2, CCL5, and IL-8, contributing significantly to cardiac damage . Numerous clinical investigations have shown that sevoflurane administration effectively suppresses the expression of pro-inflammatory factors and cytokines, thereby mitigating cardiac injury following various conditions , . Research also demonstrated that sevoflurane facilitates microglial migration, enhances their activation and polarization, improves phagocytic efficiency, and promotes their proliferation via the NF-κB signaling pathway . Sophia Koutsogiannaki et al. proposed that sevoflurane delivers anti-inflammatory protection by attenuating neutrophil apoptosis via interactions between the Fas death domain and the Fas-related death domain . Excessive production of ROS is a critical pathological mechanism underlying cardiac injury induced by sepsis. Mayumi Shiomi et al. discovered that sevoflurane reduces cardiac infarct size by modulating reactive oxygen species-mediated cellular autophagy through AMPK signaling pathways . R A Bouwman et al. demonstrated that sevoflurane suppresses the production of ROS by activating PKC-α, which in turn enhances the recovery of contractile function following cardiac ischemia .
This work has several obvious strengths. First of all, our study, combining bioinformatics analysis and in vitro and in vivo experiments, demonstrated for the first time the specific mechanism by which sevoflurane attenuates LPS-induced cardiac injury by reducing apoptosis, pro-inflammatory factors, and ROS production through upregulation of TAF1D. Moreover, transcriptome sequencing revealed that numerous genes displayed differential expression following intervention with sevoflurane and pentobarbital, with significant enrichment observed in signaling pathways, including the extracellular region and matrix, TNF, and p53 signaling pathways, which may provide partial data support for further studies. However, our study has several limitations. Firstly, the publicly accessible databases lack sequencing data for LPS-induced sepsis models treated with sevoflurane and pentobarbital interventions. Thus, our investigation into target genes and pathways solely relied on our sequencing data without incorporating bioinformatics sequencing results from other public sources. Secondly, we have conducted comprehensive sequencing analyses encompassing both cirRNAs and lncRNAs. However, we have yet to succeed in integrating these datasets to elucidate the specific roles these RNAs play in regulating the expression and function of the hub gene, TAF1D. Meanwhile, the regulatory mechanisms upstream and downstream of TAF1D still need to be clarified, and they have not been investigated in the current study. Last but not least, our investigation into the mechanism of TAF1D is presently confined to cellular experiments. The specific function of sevoflurane in up-regulating TAF1D to mitigate LPS-induced cardiac injury will be further validated through comprehensive animal studies, including gene knockout and viral transfection techniques.

## Conclusion
Our findings demonstrate that post-treatment with sevoflurane in vivo and in vitro sepsis models reduce cytotoxicity, cardiac cell death, inflammation, and ROS release by up-regulating TAF1D. Future research will necessitate broader in vitro and in vivo investigations to delineate the specific upstream and downstream pathways through which sevoflurane modulates TAF1D and thereby attenuates cardiac injury, which will help to clarify its expected therapeutic value in clinical sepsis cardiomyopathy.
