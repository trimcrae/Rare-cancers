The family of clonally variant adhesive proteins called P falciparum erythrocyte membrane protein 1 (PfEMP1) is an important reason for the high virulence of this parasite species [ ]. The P falciparum parasites export PfEMP1 to the surface of the infected erythrocytes (IEs), causing them to adhere to various endothelial receptors. This sequestration enables the IEs to evade recognition and destruction in the spleen, leading to higher levels of parasitemia than other malaria parasite species. IE sequestration is further associated with malaria morbidity and mortality due to tissue-specific inflammation, circulation dysfunction, and organ failure. The PfEMP1 variant being expressed during an infection is an important determinant of disease severity and clinical outcome as different PfEMP1 proteins mediate adhesion to different receptors with diverse tissue distribution. Important examples are cerebral malaria in childhood, which is caused by parasites expressing PfEMP1 variants that can bind to both EPCR and ICAM–1 [ ], and placental malaria (PM), which is caused by parasites expressing VAR2CSA–type PfEMP1 that bind exclusively to uniquely sulfated chondroitin sulfate (CSA), which is normally only expressed on the syncytiotrophoblast and in the placental intervillous space [ ].
People living in areas with stable transmission of P falciparum parasites acquire substantial protective immunity to malaria during childhood, and severe disease among adults is therefore uncommon. This protection is largely mediated by immunoglobulin G (IgG) to asexual-stage antigens [ ], not least the variant IE surface antigens [ ] among which PfEMP1 dominates [ , ]. Nevertheless, women in such areas become highly susceptible to PM during their first pregnancy, since VAR2CSA is antigenically distinct from all other PfEMP1 variants [ ]. PM can cause intervillositis and placental insufficiency, particularly if the placental IE load is high. These are risk factors for prematurity and delivery of babies who are small for gestational age [ ]. Although acquisition of immunity to PM does not happen until the immune system is exposed to VAR2CSA–expressing parasites, substantial protection is rapidly acquired after exposure, and PM is therefore highly concentrated among women of low parity [ , ]. These features make VAR2CSA an attractive target antigen for vaccines designed specifically to protect against PM [ ]. So far, focus has been on the minimal CSA-binding region of VAR2CSA [ ] to induce neutralizing IgG [ ]. Other potential effector functions of VAR2CSA–specific IgG, such as opsonization for phagocytosis and antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC), have not received much attention.
ADCC is mediated by Fcγ receptor III (FcγRIII)–positive effectors such as natural killer (NK) cells, neutrophils, and subsets of macrophages and γδ T cells [ ]. The affinity of FcγRIII for IgG is highly dependent on the glycosylation at a conserved site (Asn297) in the Fc region of the antibody. Thus, IgG that lacks fucose on 1 or both of the heavy chains at that position (Fc–afucosylated IgG) has markedly increased affinity compared to the majority (∼94%) of plasma IgG, which typically contains fucose [ ]. This can make the difference between a strong and an absent ADCC response. Thus, it is potentially clinically significant that PfEMP1–specific (including VAR2CSA-specific) IgG in plasma of people naturally exposed to P falciparum infection is often substantially Fc-afucosylated, although there is marked variation among individuals [ ].
Previous studies report that NK cells can kill IEs opsonized by VAR2CSA-specific IgG in vitro and that the killing depends on FcγRIIIa [ ]. However, the importance of the Fc–afucosylation of the opsonizing IgG for the IE killing and the putative clinical relevance of the observed ADCC response have not been assessed before and are therefore studied here.

## MATERIALS AND METHODS

## Ethics Statement
Samples collection in the original studies were approved by the Institutional Review Board of the Noguchi Memorial Institute for Medical Research (NMIMR-IRB 038/10-11); the Regional Research Ethics Committees, Capital Region of Denmark (protocol H-4-2013-083); the Comité Consultatif de déontologie et d’éthique of the Institut de Recherche pour le Développement (Marseille, France); the Comité d’éthique de la Faculté des Sciences de la Sante (Université d’Abomey Calavi, Benin; FSS 026/2007/CE/FSS/UAC); and the Academic Medical Center Institutional Medical Ethics Committee of the University of Amsterdam. A declaration of free willingness to participate in the study and written informed consent were obtained from all participants or guardians before enrollment.

## Study Participants
We used plasma samples and data collected as part of independent and previously published studies in Ghana [ , ] and Benin [ ]. The samples used here were an availability-based subset of the samples originally collected. No other selection criteria were applied. Plasma samples from healthy adults without malaria exposure were used as negative controls. Infection by P falciparum was diagnosed by microscopy and polymerase chain reaction [ ]. PM was diagnosed at delivery in blood and biopsies from placenta [ ]. Low birth weight (LBW) was defined as a birth weight <2500 g. Maternal anemia was defined as a hemoglobin level <11 g/dL.

## Recombinant Proteins
The full-length ectodomain of the VAR2CSA-type PfEMP1 IT4VAR04 was produced in baculovirus-transfected Sf9 insect cells, as described elsewhere [ ]. Recombinant variants of the previously described human monoclonal VAR2CSA-specific IgG1 antibody PAM1.4 [ ] were produced in HEK293F cells, and used either as it was (with high levels of Fc-fucosylation; PAM1.4FUC) or glycoengineered for Fc-afucosylation (PAM1.4AFUC) as described previously [ ].

## Mass Spectrometric IgG Fc-Glycosylation Analysis (GlYcoLISA)
Determination of Fc-fucosylation of VAR2CSA-specific plasma IgG was performed by glycosylation of antibody liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry immunosorbent assay (GlYcoLISA) as part of an earlier study [ ] and as described in detail elsewhere [ ]. In brief, VAR2CSA-specific IgG was affinity-purified from plasma samples using flat-bottomed 96–well Nunc Maxisorp plates (Thermo Fisher Scientific) precoated with VAR2CSA. Bound IgG was then eluted and analyzed by mass spectrometry after proteolytic cleavage using trypsin. The level of Fc-fucosylation was calculated as the sum of the relative intensities of glycoforms containing the glycotrait [ ].

## Fucose-Sensitive Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay for Antigen-Specific IgG
VAR2CSA-specific fucose-sensitive enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) for antigen-specific IgG (FEASI) was performed as described previously for severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) spike–specific IgG [ ]. FEASI involves 2 ELISAs—1 to quantify levels of antigen-specific (in this case, VAR2CSA-specific) IgG (IgG ELISA) and a second to assess the affinity of FcγRIIIa for VAR2CSA-specific IgG (FcγRIIIa-IgG ELISA). For both ELISAs, the 96-well plates were coated with recombinant full-length VAR2CSA (75 ng/well) and incubated with 4 serial dilutions of plasma. Bound IgG was detected with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)–conjugated anti-human IgG (Dako; 1 μg/mL) and with biotinylated FcγRIIIaV158 (Sanquin; 1 μg/mL) followed by streptavidin-poly-HRP (Sanquin; 0.1 μg/mL), respectively, as detailed previously [ ].
VAR2CSA-specific IgG levels were calculated and expressed as arbitrary units (AU) using a serially diluted Ghanaian plasma sample (set at 100 AU) as calibrator. Ratios of AU values between the 2 ELISAs were used to calculate the percentage of VAR2CSA-specific IgG fucosylation according to a simple linear regression as described elsewhere [ ]. Calculated Fc-afucosylation values ≤0% or ≥100% were assigned values of 1% and 99%, respectively.

## Malaria Parasite Culture and Selection for IE Surface Expression of VAR2CSA
The IT4 clone of P falciparum was maintained in vitro as described elsewhere [ ]. The genotypic identity of the parasites and the absence of Mycoplasma contamination in the cultures were regularly verified. Late-stage IEs were selected for surface expression of VAR2CSA using protein A-coupled DynaBeads coated with PAM1.4 and IE surface expression monitored by flow cytometry as described elsewhere [ ].

## Antibody-Mediated Inhibition of IE Adhesion to CSA
The inhibition of IE adhesion to CSA by plasma samples collected at delivery was evaluated using a static adhesion assay as previously described [ ]. In brief, late-stage VAR2CSA+ IEs were preincubated in test plasma and then incubated in CSA-coated Petri dishes. After removal of unbound cells by washing, adherent cells were fixed, Giemsa-stained, and quantified by light microscopy.

## ADCC Assay
Antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity in response to VAR2CSA-specific IgG was quantified by degranulation-induced CD107a expression on FcγRIIIa+ green fluorescent protein+ NK92 cells [ ] as described previously [ ]. The IgG was either bound to plastic-immobilized recombinant VAR2CSA [ ] or to native antigen on the surface of plasma-opsonized VAR2CSA+ IEs. For the latter assay, NK92 cells were mixed with opsonized IEs and incubated (4 hours, 37°C, 5% carbon dioxide) in the presence of antihuman CD107a-PE (H4A3 clone, BD Biosciences), brefeldin A (10 μg/mL, Sigma-Aldrich), and monensin (2 µM, Sigma-Aldrich). The NK92 cells were then collected and stained with near-IR fixable Live/Dead dye (Invitrogen). A CytoFLEX S (Beckman Coulter Life Sciences) flow cytometer was used for data acquisition, and data were analyzed with FlowLogic software (version 8.3; Inivai Technologies, Australia).

## Statistical Analysis
Data were analyzed and plotted using GraphPad Prism version 10.2.2 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, California). The sample sizes and specific statistic tests are indicated in the figures. P values <.05 were considered statistically significant. Levels of IgG, Fc–fucosylation of VAR2CSA-specific IgG, and inhibition of IE adhesion were categorized into 3 groups (low, medium, and high) based on tertiles with models comparing low and high responders. Multiple regression models adjusted for age, parity, VAR2CSA-specific IgG levels, and PM were used to evaluate the effect of those parameters on pregnancy outcome (PM, maternal anemia, and LBW).

## RESULTS

## Marked Fc-Afucosylation of Antigen-Specific IgG Can Be Reliably Determined by FEASI
The standard assay to determine Fc-afucosylation of antigen-specific IgG employs liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry analysis of affinity-purified IgG (GlYcoLISA) [ ]. However, in the present study, we optimized and validated an ELISA–type assay called FEASI, developed for SARS-CoV–2 spike-specific IgG [ ]. We previously observed extreme Fc-afucosylation (<20% Fc-fucosylation) of VAR2CSA–specific IgG determined by GlYcoLISA [ ], but the performance of the FEASI with markedly Fc-afucosylated (<60% Fc-fucosylation) IgG was not evaluated in the study describing the FEASI assay [ ]. We therefore first verified the suitability of FEASI for the study of markedly Fc-afucosylated VAR2CSA-specific IgG, using plasma samples with previously determined Fc-fucosylation by GlYcoLISA [ ]. The readout of the FEASI assay did not depend on levels of VAR2CSA-specific IgG ( ), and the results obtained with FEASI and GlYcoLISA were positively correlated ( ). Supplemental experiments with the human VAR2CSA-specific monoclonal IgG1 antibody PAM1.4 [ ], produced recombinantly and glycoengineered to be either fully (98%) Fc-fucosylated (PAM1.4FUC) or markedly Fc-afucosylated (PAM1.4AFUC, 13% Fc-fucosylation), were confirmatory. We mixed PAM1.4FUC and PAM1.4AFUC at 5 different ratios, and although the mixtures were indistinguishable by IgG ELISA ( ), they were different in the FcγRIIIa-IgG ELISA ( ), confirming the sensitivity of the FEASI assays to varying Fc-fucosylation levels. In both sets of experiments, the ability of the FEASI assay to resolve Fc-afucosylation was limited at Fc-fucosylation levels <50%. This was expected, since IgG with only 1 heavy chain Fc-afucosylated can bind FcγRIIIa as well as completely Fc-afucosylated IgG [ ]. It should also be noted that individual B–cell clones can secrete IgG with varying degrees of Fc-afucosylation [ ].
 

## NK–Cell Degranulation is Enhanced in Response to VAR2CSA+ IEs Opsonized by Fc–Afucosylated IgG
We previously reported that Fc-afucosylated VAR2CSA-specific plasma IgG bound to plastic-immobilized recombinant VAR2CSA elicits a strong NK–cell ADCC response, whereas corresponding fucosylated IgG does not [ ]. However, the importance of Fc–afucosylation for NK–cell responses to IgG-opsonized IEs was not investigated directly. To do so, we used 2 pools of plasma from women naturally exposed to P falciparum during pregnancy to opsonize VAR2CSA+ IEs. Each pool consisted of the 5 plasma samples with the most and the least pronounced Fc–afucosylation of VAR2CSA-specific IgG, respectively, among the samples assessed in our earlier study ( ) [ ]. Although the levels of VAR2CSA-specific IgG were similar in the 2 pools ( ), VAR2CSA-positive IEs opsonized by IgG from the Fc-afucosylated pool induced a stronger NK–cell ADCC response, assessed as degranulation-induced surface expression of CD107a, than the latter ( ). Furthermore, both the levels ( ) and the degree of Fc-afucosylation of VAR2CSA-specific IgG ( and ) in individual plasma samples correlated with NK–cell degranulation in response to VAR2CSA-specific plasma IgG bound to plastic-immobilized recombinant VAR2CSA. Together, these findings demonstrate directly that IgG Fc-afucosylation is an important determinant of whether NK cells can destroy IgG-opsonized IEs in vitro by ADCC, in addition to the degree of IgG opsonization.
 

## Fc-Afucosylation of VAR2CSA-Specific IgG Is Associated With Outcome of P falciparum–Exposed Pregnancies
The above data, in combination with previous observations [ , ] and our unpublished data, suggest that ADCC against IgG-opsonized IEs and therefore also Fc-afucosylation of VAR2CSA-specific IgG are significant determinants of protective immunity to PM. To consolidate our previous results [ ] and test our hypothesis regarding clinical protection conferred by Fc-afucosylated VAR2CSA-specific IgG, we used plasma samples from 262 Beninese pregnant women ( ), collected as part of an earlier study (Strategies to Prevent Pregnancy-Associated Malaria [STOPPAM]), where pregnancy outcome and other clinical data were available [ ]. Among these women, parity was associated with protection against LBW, after adjusting for age, VAR2CSA-specific IgG levels, and placental infection (adjusted odds ratio [aOR], 0.52; P = .01). On average, each previous pregnancy increased the weight of the newborn by 56 g, after controlling for the above factors. About 9% of the women had microscopically detectable P falciparum parasitemia at the time of inclusion in the original study, and this was a risk factor for LBW after adjusting for age, parity, VAR2CSA-specific IgG levels, and placental infection (aOR, 4.27; P = .01). On average, women infected at inclusion delivered babies that were 293 g smaller than those born to uninfected women, after controlling for the above factors. The levels ( ) and degree of Fc-afucosylation ( ) of VAR2CSA–specific plasma IgG varied markedly among these women and were significantly associated with parity. The ability of plasma IgG to inhibit adhesion of VAR2CSA+ IEs to CSA in vitro also varied considerably, and it was positively correlated with the levels ( ), but not the degree, of Fc-afucosylation ( ) of VAR2CSA-specific IgG. Peripheral parasitemia was detected during the pregnancy in some of the women. The number of parasitemic episodes significantly influenced levels (rs = 0.37; P < .05) and IE adhesion–inhibitory capacity of VAR2CSA-specific IgG (rs = 0.14; P < .05), but it did not significantly influence the degree of Fc-afucosylation of VAR2CSA-specific IgG or birth weight.
 All of the above interwoven associations illustrate that the P falciparum exposure-dependent risk of adverse pregnancy outcome is complex and multifactorial. To disentangle the relative importance of levels, Fc-afucosylation, and neutralizing (IE adhesion-inhibition) capacity of VAR2CSA-specific IgG for pregnancy outcome, we calculated the aORs of PM, maternal anemia, and offspring LBW ( ). The aORs of PM did not significantly depend on any of these 3 features of VAR2CSA-specific IgG, and neither did the risk of being anemic at delivery (both models adjusted for age and parity). It may be that assessment of the impact of VAR2CSA-specific IgG on placental parasite burden rather than just presence/absence of IEs in the placenta would have provided a clearer picture. Unfortunately, we did not have the histopathology data to do that. In contrast, the aOR of delivering an LBW baby was reduced among women with marked Fc–afucosylation of their VAR2CSA-specific IgG (unadjusted OR, 0.14 [95% confidence interval {CI}, .04–.39]; P = .001). This risk remained after adjusting for age, parity, VAR2CSA-specific IgG levels, placental infection, and parasitemic episodes (aOR, 0.15 [95% CI, .02–.58]; P = .02) ( ). Specifically, women with marked (upper tertile) Fc-afucosylation of their VAR2CSA-specific IgG were 84% less likely to deliver an LBW baby than women with limited (lower tertile) Fc-afucosylation of their VAR2CSA–specific IgG. These findings show that Fc-afucosylation of VAR2CSA-specific IgG is a critical determinant of pregnancy outcome, specifically the risk of delivering an LBW baby, which is a major adverse consequence of PM and an important cause of PM–related morbidity and mortality. Indeed, our data indicate that the degree of Fc–afucosylation of VAR2CSA-specific IgG is the strongest predictor of clinical protection against LBW.

## DISCUSSION
Placental P falciparum malaria remains a serious threat to mother-child health in areas with stable transmission of these parasites (reviewed in [ ]). The recently available malaria vaccines [ , ], possibly complemented by targeted monoclonal antibody prophylaxis [ ], may be the long-anticipated tools that will boost the long–advertised malaria endgame. However, their impact on PM may be jeopardized by the fact that PM is often the result of women already being infected at conception rather than being due to infections acquired after becoming pregnant [ , ]. Although this difficulty may potentially be overcome by regular radical malaria cure followed by revaccination [ ], or monoclonal antibody–based prophylaxis of young females of childbearing age, the practicality of such interventions remains unclear. VAR2CSA-based vaccination is potentially an attractive alternative for prevention of PM [ , ]. However, the 2 vaccines tested to date induced antibody responses that were focused on the allelic variant used for immunization. Furthermore, their ability to inhibit adhesion of VAR2CSA+ IEs to CSA was limited, although the immunogen in both vaccines included the minimal binding region containing the receptor-binding domains [ , ]. The reasons for these deficiencies remain unclear but may include underrepresentation of functionally important and structurally complex epitopes in the vaccine immunogens [ ]. Consistent with this, a recent meta-analysis concluded that IgG responses to native VAR2CSA antigens are significantly associated with protection from LBW, whereas levels of IgG to the recombinant constituent domains of VAR2CSA are not [ ].
In addition to the above difficulties, we recently reported that whereas naturally acquired VAR2CSA-specific IgG is often very markedly Fc–afucosylated, corresponding vaccine-induced IgG is fully Fc–fucosylated, similar to most plasma IgG [ ]. This difference is potentially clinically important, as Fc-afucosylated IgG efficiently triggers FcγRIII-dependent ADCC responses against opsonized targets, whereas Fc-fucosylated IgG does not [ ]. NK cells have been shown to efficiently lyse IgG–opsonized IEs by ADCC in vitro [ ]. This effector function has been associated with clinical protection against malaria in children (see [ , ] and our unpublished data).
Here, we validate a convenient ELISA-based alternative to mass spectrometry (FEASI) [ ] for the robust quantification of IgG Fc-afucosylation ( and – ). We confirm that Fc-afucosylation is a critical hallmark of ADCC and the relevance of our earlier approach to assessing the role of Fc–afucosylation of PfEMP1-specific IgG. Thus, NK-cell ADCC against IgG-opsonized VAR2CSA+ IEs (instead of IgG bound to plastic-immobilized PfEMP1) was highly dependent on the degree of Fc-afucosylation of the opsonizing IgG ( and ). Most importantly, we found that Fc–afucosylation of VAR2CSA-specific IgG was a strong predictor of LBW, whereas levels of this specific IgG per se were not ( ).
The ability of VAR2CSA-specific IgG to inhibit the adhesion of VAR2CSA+ IEs to their cognate receptor (CSA) has so far been considered the main effector function of VAR2CSA–specific IgG. Consequently, current VAR2CSA-based vaccines are specifically targeting the minimal CSA-binding region of VAR2CSA [ , ]. However, cell-mediated destruction of IEs opsonized by VAR2CSA-specific IgG likely contributes to protection [ ], an inference that is reinforced and extended by the present work. Specifically, we show that the clinical importance of Fc-afucosylation of VAR2CSA-specific IgG can exceed its neutralizing capacity. In contrast, VAR2CSA–specific IgG per se may be a marker of exposure to P falciparum and risk of PM more than a correlate of protection. Indeed, high levels of VAR2CSA–specific IgG have been associated with increased offspring risk of malaria during the first year of life [ ]. Our study warrants a reconsideration of current approaches to PfEMP1-based vaccination. In addition to the issue of potential underrepresentation of epitopes eliciting broadly cross–reactive antibodies in short recombinant antigens, vaccination with recombinant VAR2CSA minimal binding region antigen does not elicit the Fc-afucosylation required for efficient activation of effector cells via FcγRIII [ ]. The precise molecular interactions triggering this posttranslational modification of the IgG Fc domain remain unclear, but they appear to occur in responses to foreign antigens displayed on host cellular membranes [ ]. PfEMP1 on the IE surface (see [ ] and our unpublished data), pathogen–derived antigens on the surface of enveloped viruses [ ], and allo-antigens such as RhD recognized during pregnancy [ ] can all induce Fc–afucosylated IgG. Thus, it is plausible that Fc–afucosylated VAR2CSA-specific IgG could be induced by vaccination with recombinant enveloped viruses displaying VAR2CSA or with var2csa mRNA vaccines.
Until recently, almost all PfEMP1-specific research has focused on characterizing the interaction between PfEMP1 and their cognate host receptors, to facilitate the development of vaccines interrupting it. The work toward prevention of PM by VAR2CSA-based vaccination is a primary example, and our study suggests that a reorientation of these efforts should be considered. Our findings also add a new layer of complexity to the evolutionary arms race between P falciparum and the human immune system. The profound and selective Fc-afucosylation of PfEMP1-specific IgG seems to be an evolutionary adaptation to enhance the elimination of IgG–opsonized IEs by ADCC. If so, this mechanism should be considered in the future development of improved PfEMP1-based vaccines.
