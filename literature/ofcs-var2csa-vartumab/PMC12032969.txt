Embedded on the surface of infected erythrocytes, RIFIN and STEVOR proteins are involved in cytoadherence and immune evasion, but the extent of family-wide sequence diversity across strains has yet to be comprehensively investigated in light of improved resolution of the subtelomeric genome sequences. Using a k-mer frequency approach, we analyzed long-read genomic sequence data from 18 geographically diverse P. falciparum genome assemblies, including lab strains and clinical isolates. We hypothesized that k-mer sequence comparison can identify existing RIFIN and STEVOR subgroups, identify novel subgroups, and generate more robust and reliable estimates of family-wide sequence diversity. Full-length RIFIN and STEVOR proteins shared on average 49.5% and 61.1% amino acid k-mer similarity, respectively, which fell to 25.1% and 20% in the hypervariable regions alone. Despite this diversity, we identified 11 RIFINs and five STEVORs that were conserved across strains above expected thresholds. A subset of these strain-transcendent genes was similar and syntenic to genes in related Plasmodium species, suggesting an ancient origin. Additionally, in silico structural predictions from AlphaFold showed that three-dimensional structures of RIFIN receptor-binding regions were more conserved than their sequences suggested. Evolutionarily constrained RIFINs and STEVORs may have critical functions in parasite survival or pathogenesis. This study provides a framework for investigating diversity in highly variable multi-gene families and highlights the potential of strain-transcendent RIFIN and STEVOR proteins as vaccine candidates.

## Introduction
Significant portions of the Plasmodium falciparum genome are dedicated to multi-gene families encoding variant surface antigens (VSAs) ( ). VSAs are displayed on the surface of infected erythrocytes (IEs) and mediate adhesion to host cells. The most well-studied VSAs belong to the P. falciparum erythrocyte membrane protein-1 (PfEMP1) family, encoded by approximately 60 var gene paralogs ( ). In comparison, much less is known about the repetitive interspersed family (rif) and subtelomeric variant open reading frame (stevor) genes. These loci encode approximately 150–200 RIFIN and 30 STEVOR proteins, respectively ( ; ; ). RIFIN and STEVOR proteins share a similar structure consisting of a semiconserved N-terminus, a hypervariable loop, and a conserved, intracellular C-terminus ( ). RIFINs are broadly subtyped into RIFIN-As (surface-exposed) and RIFIN-Bs (intracellular) by the presence of a 25-amino acid insertion in the semiconserved domain of RIFIN-As ( ; ).
The majority of RIFINs and STEVORs have unknown functions, but recent in vitro studies have shed light on the roles of a handful of members during malaria infection. RIFINs are involved in multiple host binding interactions via their hypervariable regions; one member forms rosettes via blood antigen A and glycophorin A ( ; ), while others impair the host immune system via the inhibitory receptors LILRB1, LILRB2, and LAIR1 on B- and NK-cells ( ; ; ). The STEVOR family has been implicated in rosetting ( ; ), erythrocyte invasion ( ), and membrane deformability ( ; ; ) via the semiconserved region. Much remains to be discovered about these gene families, but they are shown to have several important functions in malaria pathogenesis.
The diversity of VSAs allows P. falciparum to evade the host immune system and enhances its virulence. Thus, grappling with the extent of rif and stevor genetic diversity is critical for understanding malaria pathogenesis and developing novel interventions. However, the paralogy and high recombination rate of these families pose challenges for genetic analysis. Genetic diversity among PfEMP1s has been characterized extensively, but such rigor has not been applied to the RIFIN or STEVOR families ( ; ). Previous attempts to analyze these families in-depth were limited by the available technologies, bioinformatic tools, and parasite genomic data, leading to a stagnation in progress for over a decade ( ; ; ; ; ; ).
Recent advancements in DNA sequencing technology have dramatically improved the resolution of the P. falciparum subtelomeric genome regions, inviting a deeper investigation of VSA sequence diversity ( ). In this study, we used an alignment-free k-mer frequency approach to analyze long-read genomic sequence data from 18 P. falciparum isolates of diverse geographical origins. Our goal was to comprehensively profile the sequence and structural diversity within the RIFIN and STEVOR families. We hypothesized that k-mer sequence comparison can identify existing RIFIN and STEVOR subgroups, identify novel subgroups, and generate more robust and reliable estimates of family-wide diversity. We also sought to expand the known set of conserved RIFIN and STEVOR peptides, proteins, and subgroups, which possibly serve essential functions and may be promising targets to further investigate as vaccine candidates.

2.

## Methods
2.1.

## Sequence selection
We downloaded the amino acid sequences for proteins annotated as “RIFIN” or “STEVOR” in 18 P. falciparum isolates from the PlasmoDB database (release 66) excluding pseudogenes ( ). This was a comprehensive snapshot of publicly available, annotated whole-genome sequences as of 2024. Six strains were common laboratory clones: 3D7, 7G8, Dd2, HB3, IT4, and GB4. The remaining 12 were short-term culture adapted clinical isolates that were sequenced, assembled, and annotated by Otto et al. ( ) and Moser et al. ( ). Together, these isolates spanned diverse geographical origins, including multiple regions of Africa, Southeast Asia, and South America. We used STRIDE, a Hidden Markov Model sequence classification tool for RIFINs and STEVORs ( ), to confirm existing annotations, resolve ambiguous annotations, and classify RIFIN sequences into the major A and B subgroups.

2.2.

## Genetic diversity and comparative genomic analyses
We performed preliminary multiple sequence alignments of full-length RIFIN and STEVOR amino acid sequences using MAFFT v7.450 with the E-INS-I option ( ) and MUSCLE v3.8.31 with default parameters ( ), respectively. We visualized and curated alignments with Jalview ( ). The PEXEL motif and C-terminal transmembrane domain delineated the variable and constant protein regions, which were separated using Jalview.
We counted the frequency of all possible amino acid k-mers of length three (i.e., 3-mers) in each RIFIN and STEVOR sequence. For all pairs of sequences, we calculated the fractional common k-mer count, or the percentage of k-mer frequencies present in the shorter sequence that are shared by both sequences ( ). Edgar similarity (k = 3) has generally been shown to correlate with alignment-based percent identity, but the two metrics are more discordant for low-identity sequence pairs ( ).
We defined identical RIFINs and STEVORs as pairs of sequences sharing at least 99% Edgar similarity. Based on the PfEMP1 VAR2CSA, the best existing paradigm of conserved VSAs, we defined “strain-transcendent” RIFIN and STEVOR sequences as those conserved at one of three potential thresholds: 75% Edgar similarity across at least 17/18 P. falciparum isolates, 80% Edgar similarity across 16/18 isolates, or 85% Edgar similarity across 15/18 isolates. We determined synteny to the extant P. falciparum relatives P. praefalciparum (strain G01) and P. reichenowi (strain CDC) using annotations from PlasmoDB.
We normalized k-mer frequency tables by protein sequence length, then conducted principal component analyses to visualize the relationships and clustering patterns of the sequences in two-dimensional space. We used the MEME Suite version 5.5.6 ( ) to search for ungapped amino acid motifs (length six or greater) that were differentially enriched in identified subgroups compared to the remaining sequences.
All data analyses and visualization were conducted using the R Project for Statistical Computing, version 4.3.2 ( ).

2.3.

## Protein structure predictions
We downloaded three-dimensional structural predictions for each 3D7 RIFIN and STEVOR from Google DeepMind’s AlphaFold database ( ), then used TM-align ( ) with default parameters to compute pairwise structural similarities. The TM-score is a length-normalized measure of structural similarity ranging from zero (no similarity) to one (identical). A TM-score greater than 0.5 is commonly cited for identifying protein pairs that are in the same fold ( ). We also calculated the root mean square deviation (RMSD) of each protein pair, which measures the average distance between the atoms of aligned 3D structures in Angstroms (Å). We used ChimeraX ( ) to isolate the hypervariable region from each structure and repeated the same steps. ChimeraX was also used to visualize protein structures and compute structural alignments (“matchmaker” command).

3.

## Results
3.1.

## Curation of RIFIN and STEVOR protein sequences
We accessed genome annotations for 18 globally distributed P. falciparum isolates from the PlasmoDB database and investigated the amino acid (AA) sequence diversity within the subtelomeric gene families rif and stevor.
We initially extracted 3418 AA sequences from PlasmoDB with annotations containing “RIFIN” (n = 2410) or “STEVOR” (n = 456), plus 552 sequences with mixed or putative designations ( ). The STRIDE sequence classification tool successfully disambiguated most putative RIFIN/STEVOR sequences, and we removed 49 sequences STRIDE could not confidently annotate ( ). The average lengths of the protein sequences were 341 AA residues (standard deviation, SD: 34 AA) for RIFINs and 298 AA (SD: 19 AA) for STEVORs, but 60 RIFINs and 11 STEVORs were unexpectedly short (<250 AA) due to truncations at either terminus ( ). We removed these truncated sequences, limiting all further analyses to 2793 RIFINs and 505 STEVORs ( ).
The P. falciparum isolates studied here contained 110 to 291 rif genes (average: 155) and 13 to 51 stevors (average: 28) ( ). The distribution of RIFIN subtypes per genome was 69.6% RIFIN-A (SD: 2.7%), 28.1% RIFIN-B (SD: 2.9%), and 2.3% unclassifiable RIFINs (RIFIN-U, SD: 1.2%) on average ( ). Two polyclonal isolates, ML01 and TG01 contained approximately double the number of rif and stevor loci compared to monoclonal isolates and accounted for the highest number of putative or mixed annotations ( ).

3.2.

## Extensive within-strain and between-strain variability of the RIFIN and STEVOR families
To investigate the AA sequence diversity within the RIFIN and STEVOR families, we initially performed multiple sequence alignment of the protein sequences, but observed poor quality alignments due to the substantial variability in residues and length ( ). Therefore, we instead counted the frequency of AA 3-mers (peptides of length three) in each sequence and calculated the fractional common k-mer count (Edgar similarity) between all pairs of sequences ( ).
Heatmaps of pairwise Edgar similarities revealed two distinct clusters of RIFINs, consistent with the major RIFIN-A/RIFIN-B subtypes ( ). Among STEVORs, Edgar similarities revealed a relatively conserved group and a separate set of elements, each cluster composed of highly similar sequences with low similarity to all other STEVORs ( ). We refer to these groups as STEVOR-A and STEVOR-B, respectively.
The sequence diversity in both families was extensive; the average pairwise Edgar similarity among full-length RIFINs and STEVORs was 49.7% (SD: 8.2%) and 60.5% (SD: 13.5%), respectively ( ). RIFIN and STEVOR members within the same genome were equally diverse compared to members between genomes ( ). For both families, members within the same A/B group were significantly more similar than those between groups ( ).
We then extracted specific regions of the RIFIN and STEVOR sequences and examined their diversity. These include the semiconserved region, variable region 1, and hypervariable region ( ). The average pairwise Edgar similarity among semiconserved regions of RIFINs (53.7% [SD: 13.2%]) and STEVORs (60.3% [SD: 17.0%]) coincided with full-length protein comparisons ( , , ). However, the average Edgar similarity of hypervariable regions fell to 25.0% (SD: 8.6%) and 20.1% (SD: 14.0%) for RIFINs and STEVORs, respectively ( ).
We hypothesized that RIFINs and STEVORs with known virulence mechanisms would be more conserved, but these proteins exhibited considerable sequence diversity. For example, the RIFIN-A PF3D7_1245800, which binds to the immune inhibitory receptor LILRB1 ( ; ), shared an average of 71.5% (SD: 10.7%) of Edgar similarity among its most similar homologs from the 17 other P. falciparum genomes, including 56.8% (SD: 17.5%) similarity in the hypervariable region ( ). The RIFIN-A PF3D7_0100400, shown to mediate blood type A rosetting ( ), shared an average Edgar similarity of 71.7% (SD: 3.3%) across the full-length protein, and 59.3% (SD: 11.7%) in the hypervariable region, with its most similar counterparts from the other isolates ( ). The average hypervariable region similarities among homologs for the rosetting STEVORs PF3D7_0617600 and PF3D7_1040200 ( ) were 64.6% (SD: 23.8%) and 42.3% (SD: 15.5%), respectively ( , ).

3.3.

## Strain-transcendent RIFINs and STEVORs exist in all major subgroups
We searched for RIFIN/STEVOR sequences that were highly conserved across P. falciparum isolates, hypothesizing that functionally important paralogs were maintained despite extensive family-wide genetic diversity. Pairs of genomes shared between zero and 13 identical RIFINs and zero to nine identical STEVORs, with an average of one fully conserved gene in both cases ( ). The three Latin American isolates shared zero to one identical RIFINs with the African and Asian isolates on average. In contrast, the African polyclonal isolates ML01 and TG01 had four to seven RIFINs and STEVORs in common with other African isolates on average.
We referenced the level of sequence similarity among VAR2CSA, the most conserved member of the PfEMP1 family which is present in all P. falciparum genomes, to inform additional thresholds for conservation. The VAR2CSA protein sequence is conserved across at least 75% of its length in 17/18 isolates studied here, 80% across 16/18 isolates, and 85% across 15/18 isolates, according to Edgar similarity. We considered RIFIN or STEVOR sequences that met these thresholds to be “strain-transcendent”.
We identified 11 RIFINs and five STEVORs that qualified as strain-transcendent, including sequences from all major A/B subgroups ( – , ). The strain-transcendent groups featuring PF3D7_0401600 (RIFIN-U), PF3D7_0413200 (RIFIN-A), PF3D7_0900300 (RIFIN-B), and PF3D7_0900400 (RIFIN-A) were conserved across all 18 isolates studied ( ). One strain-transcendent RIFIN-A was absent from the 3D7 reference genome (PfIT_040006900). The RIFIN-A PF3D7_0900400 represented a large group with intragenomic redundancy, approximately four times the size of the other strain-transcendent groups (≥85% similarity to PF3D7_1100300, PF3D7_1150300, PF3D7_1300400 and their homologs in other isolates).
Seven RIFINs – PF3D7_0413200 (RIFIN-A), PF3D7_0713000 (RIFIN-A), PF3D7_1372600, PfIT_040006900 (RIFIN-A), PF3D7_1000600 (RIFIN-B), PF3D7_0114700 (RIFIN-B), PF3D7_0401600 (RIFIN-U) – and two STEVOR-Bs – PF3D7_0221400 and PF3D7_0832000 – had conserved genomic locations (synteny) in the majority of P. falciparum isolates examined ( – ). Three of these RIFINs – PF3D7_0413200 (RIFIN-A), PfIT_040006900 (RIFIN-A), PF3D7_0401600 (RIFIN-U) – and both STEVOR-Bs also had ancient origins, with syntenic orthologs of up to 95% similarity in P. praefalciparum (gorilla host) and P. reichenowi (chimpanzee host), the closest extant relatives of P. falciparum ( ). Fourteen of the strain-transcendent genes identified were in the highly diverse subtelomeric genomic regions, and two rif genes were located more centrally on the chromosomes ( ).

3.4.

## Novel subgroups within the RIFIN and STEVOR families
To further examine the subgrouping of the RIFIN and STEVOR families, we conducted principal component analyses (PCA) using AA 3-mer frequencies. For RIFINs, the first two dimensions clearly separated the established A/B subgroups ( ). Some crossover between clusters indicated potential hybrid sequences. The remaining RIFIN-U sequences formed a divergent cluster, including the strain-transcendent member PF3D7_0401600. Principal component plots drawn from full-length STEVOR sequences showed a mostly homogenous group of STEVOR-As and a constellation of distinct and/or solitary STEVOR-B sequences ( ), consistent with . Two clusters of STEVOR-Bs were anchored by the strain-transcendent members PF3D7_0221400 and PF3D7_0832000.
RIFIN and STEVOR sequences were not distinguishable by their geographic origin, as sequences from each of the 18 P. falciparum isolates were equally dispersed across all principal component plots ( – ). We also found no stratification of RIFIN or STEVOR sequences common to laboratory isolates versus recent clinical isolates ( – ).
To identify more subtle subgroups, we examined the clustering patterns of specific protein sub-sequences. PCA analysis of RIFIN and STEVOR semiconserved regions reproduced the separation of the major A/B subgroups ( , ). Isolating STEVOR semiconserved regions also emphasized the clustering of STEVOR-B sequences into smaller groups of highly similar sequences ( ). Considering RIFIN hypervariable regions alone, RIFIN-As and RIFIN-Bs were not clearly distinguishable, except for a distinct cluster of RIFIN-A sequences (RIFIN-A2) including the strain-transcendent member PF3D7_0900400 ( ). Analysis of STEVOR hypervariable regions also revealed a novel cluster of STEVOR-As (STEVOR-A2) which included the strain-transcendent member PF3D7_0700400, and tighter clustering of STEVOR-B sequences ( ). The RIFIN-A2 and STEVOR-A2 subgroups had specific motifs in their hypervariable region that were enriched relative to the remaining RIFIN/STEVOR sequences, according to MEME ( – ).
Examining additional axes of variation from PCA results identified hidden relationships between sequences. Considering full-length sequences, PC4 highlighted the RIFIN-A2 subgroup ( ). In the semi-conserved regions, PC5 revealed a distinct cluster of RIFIN-Bs (RIFIN-B2, ). Considering hypervariable regions alone, another RIFIN subgroup was evident along PC3/PC4 (RIFIN-U2, ). The proposed RIFIN-B2 and RIFIN-U2 subgroups were also characterized by differentially enriched MEME motifs ( ). Among STEVOR hypervariable regions, PC3 and PC5 further highlighted the distinctness of the two strain-transcendent STEVOR-B subgroups ( ). 3.5.

## RIFIN and STEVOR structural diversity
The three-dimensional protein structures of P. falciparum antigens may be more conserved than their amino acid sequences suggest ( ). To assess the degree of structural diversity within the RIFIN and STEVOR families, we calculated pairwise structural similarity scores (TM-scores) using AlphaFold predictions for all 157 RIFINs and 31 STEVORs from the 3D7 reference genome. Full-length RIFIN and STEVOR proteins were structurally diverse, with average pairwise TM-scores of 0.44 (SD: 0.08) and 0.48 (SD: 0.1), respectively ( , ). PCA analyses showed that RIFIN-A and RIFIN-B structures were not clearly distinguishable, and the strain-transcendent RIFIN PF3D7_0401600 folded distinctly from the rest of the RIFINs ( ). Small clusters of RIFINs and STEVORs appeared to share similar structures ( , , , ).
We then compared the structural similarity of RIFIN hypervariable regions alone. These regions consist of helical segments forming a loop in the extracellular space that interacts with host receptors ( ; ). RIFIN hypervariable regions shared an average structural similarity of 0.66 (SD: 0.12) ( ), and PCA visualization supported the presence of three clusters, not specific to the RIFIN-A/B division, each containing two or more strain-transcendent RIFINs ( ). For STEVORs, the human binding portion is within the semiconserved region ( ). Semiconserved region structures were approximately as diverse as full-length STEVOR proteins (average TM-score: 0.37), but pairs of STEVOR hypervariable loops were more similar (average TM-score: 0.59, Additional File 1: – ).
Visualization of three-dimensional structural alignments emphasized how pairs of RIFINs ( ) and STEVORs ( ) varied in terms of the helices formed by the semi-conserved regions, but the hypervariable regions folded into more consistent loop structures.

4.

## Discussion
The subtelomeric var, rif, and stevor genes are the most diverse within the Plasmodium falciparum genome. In this study, we provided comprehensive estimates of RIFIN and STEVOR amino acid sequence diversity, capitalizing on the availability of high-quality sequencing data and robust, alignment-free comparative genomic approaches. Despite extensive sequence variability between RIFINs and STEVORs from the same or different isolates, we identified a subset of strain-transcendent RIFIN and STEVOR proteins that were conserved well beyond expectations. Clustering of hypervariable regions, rather than full-length sequences, revealed three novel subgroups: RIFIN-A2, STEVOR-A2, and STEVOR-B2. These findings refine and expand upon subgroup definitions established in prior studies ( ; ; ). Finally, we observed that the hypervariable regions of RIFIN and STEVOR proteins fold into consistent loop structures that were more similar than expected given the high degree of sequence diversity. Identifying and characterizing RIFIN and STEVOR subgroups is likely to be a key step towards understanding these families’ roles in malaria pathogenesis and evaluating their potential as targets for novel vaccines or therapeutics.
Our study significantly expands the current knowledge of strain-transcendent VSAs. The best existing paradigm is var2csa, a var gene that is at least 75% conserved across all P. falciparum isolates ( ). Here, we used this model to identify evolutionarily constrained RIFINs and STEVORs among geographically diverse genomes, including clinical isolates and distant Plasmodium relatives. Earlier studies identifying conserved RIFIN/STEVOR subgroups were based on a limited set of reference genome sequences and included pseudogenes, which may not be subjected to immune selection pressure ( ; ). A subset of the loci had syntenic orthologs in the primate-infecting species P. praefalciparum and P. reichenowi, which diverged from P. falciparum between 40 and 60 thousand and 3–5 million years ago, respectively ( ; ). VAR2CSA serves a specialized function in placental sequestration ( ; ) and appears to have a central role in regulating var gene switching ( ); likewise, the 16 loci identified here may have specialized roles in parasite survival or pathogenesis.
The RIFIN PF3D7_0401600 in particular had several parallels to VAR2CSA. Its sequence and structure diverged significantly from all other family members, but it was conserved by at least 96% k-mer similarity across all 18 isolates analyzed. Interestingly, PF3D7_0401600 has repeatedly been reported as predominantly transcribed in laboratory lines ( ; ; ; ) and controlled human malaria infections ( ; ). Its expression is epigenetically regulated, rapidly shifting between strong activation and stable repression in a cyclical fashion reminiscent of var2csa ( ; ; ). Together, this evidence suggests that PF3D7_0401600 may have a central role in malaria infection and deserves further attention.
VAR2CSA is the leading candidate for a PfEMP1-based malaria vaccine; as such, the strain-transcendent RIFIN and STEVOR proteins are also intriguing considerations for the development of novel interventions. RIFINs and STEVORs are broadly immunogenic ( ), and anti-RIFIN antibodies lead to rapid parasite clearance in children ( ) and prevent host-parasite interactions ( ; ; ; ). Additionally, serological responses to some members, including the strain-transcendent STEVOR-B PF3D7_0832000, have been associated with protection from severe malaria ( ; ). Future studies should investigate the transcription and surface expression of these conserved rif and stevor loci during natural infections, their ability to elicit protective antibody responses, and their interactions with host binding targets.
We found that 3D7 RIFIN and STEVOR sequences with known roles in rosetting or immune evasion were not especially conserved, consistent with prior studies ( ; ). However, recent studies have shown that PfEMP1 proteins with diverse sequences can bind to human host endothelial proteins in a common fashion ( ; ) and seminal research on RIFIN structural biology suggests a similar phenomenon ( ; ; ). Our data support this theory, as the predicted three-dimensional structures of RIFIN hypervariable regions were surprisingly similar. Taken together, VSA structures may be more conserved than their sequences suggest, particularly in the host binding regions. This fact highlights the balance between antigenic escape and preservation of function. Additional studies are needed to untangle the relationship between RIFIN/STEVOR sequence, structure, and function in better detail.
The P. falciparum isolates selected for this analysis spanned three continents – Africa, Asia, and America – and were sequenced with long-read technology to ensure the accuracy of the highly variable subtelomeric regions. Among these isolates were two polyclonal infections from Africa, which contained approximately twice as many rif and stevor genes compared to monoclonal infections and shared several identical sequences with other African isolates. Polyclonal infections are common in high-transmission settings, so including and characterizing these isolates broadens the applicability of our findings. Despite the geographical distance between isolates, we observed an overall consistent degree of RIFIN and STEVOR sequence diversity, aligning with previous studies ( ; ; ; ; ). Expanding the availability of long-read subtelomeric genome sequences will be crucial for confirming these findings and advancing our understanding of global VSA diversity.
This study introduces alignment-free sequence comparison methods to the study of malaria VSAs. Our approach offers an attractive alternative to sequence alignment for comparing highly variable biological sequences, where the homology of aligned positions cannot be assumed. Methods employing k-mer counting are robust to large variations in sequence and length, as well as genomic recombination events. Additionally, PCA may be more appropriate for studying variant surface antigen diversity compared to phylogenetic trees due to high recombination rates and the lack of a bifurcating evolutionary trajectory. PCA also provides multiple axes of variation, with the potential for deeper insights about patterns of sequence similarity or divergence. This approach can readily be extended to obtain robust comparative genomics conclusions for additional Plasmodium multi-gene families or other organisms.

5.

## Conclusions
Despite high genetic diversity within the RIFIN and STEVOR protein families, strain-transcendent sequences are remarkably conserved across time and geographical origin. Such evolutionary constraint suggests that these sequences may serve important functions in parasite fitness or pathogenesis. Strain-transcendent RIFINs and STEVORs are promising candidates for further in vitro and clinical studies, as well as consideration for the next generation of malaria vaccines.
