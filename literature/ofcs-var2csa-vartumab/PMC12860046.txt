## Abstract
Renal cell carcinoma (RCC), the most common type of kidney cancer, is characterized by a poor prognosis due to its aggressive metastasis and resistance to treatment. Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) have emerged as key regulators of RCC progression, influencing cellular processes such as proliferation, migration, invasion, and apoptosis. LncRNA biogenesis occurs through both canonical and non-canonical pathways, involving RNA Polymerase II-mediated transcription and alternative splicing. LncRNAs like HOTAIR, MALAT1, and GAS5 regulate critical signaling pathways, including the PI3K/AKT/mTOR axis, Wnt/β-catenin, and JAK/STAT, while others, such as RCAT1, DUXAP9, and Lnc-LSG1, modulate protein degradation, impacting tumor growth and metastasis. Additionally, lncRNAs like EGFR-AS1 and MALAT1 enhance the EGFR/AKT and VEGF/Akt pathways, driving RCC cell proliferation and migration. LncRNAs such as HOTAIR and TCL6 also promote epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition, contributing to tumor invasion and therapy resistance. Furthermore, lncRNAs regulate the p53 pathway, with some, like MEG3, acting as tumor suppressors, while others, like SNHG3, suppress p53 activity, accelerating RCC progression. These insights into lncRNA-mediated regulatory mechanisms provide promising therapeutic targets for RCC, suggesting that modulating specific lncRNAs or their associated pathways could offer innovative strategies for treatment and prognosis. This review presents a comprehensive analysis of the biogenesis, functions, and regulatory roles of lncRNAs in RCC, emphasizing their potential as diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets to improve patient outcomes.
Keywords: LncRNA, Renal cell carcinoma, Signaling pathways

## Introduction
Renal cell carcinoma (RCC) is the most common type of kidney cancer in adults, accounting for approximately 90% of renal malignancies. Despite progress in diagnostic and therapeutic approaches, the prognosis for advanced RCC remains poor, primarily due to its ability to metastasize aggressively and develop resistance to conventional therapies [ ]. Recent research has identified long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) as key players in the molecular pathogenesis of RCC, given their crucial roles in regulating gene expression at multiple levels, including transcriptional, post-transcriptional, and epigenetic processes [ ]. LncRNAs have emerged as essential regulators of various cellular functions, including cell proliferation, migration, invasion, apoptosis, and metastasis [ ].
LncRNAs influence the progression of RCC through complex interactions with major signaling pathways, such as the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway, Wnt/β-catenin signaling, and the hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF) pathway, which are central to RCC tumorigenesis [ ]. These molecules participate in the regulation of tumor growth and metastasis either as oncogenes or tumor suppressors [ ]. LncRNAs achieve this by acting as molecular scaffolds, decoys, or sponges for microRNAs, proteins, or other nucleic acids, ultimately modulating the expression of target genes and the activation of signaling networks that promote RCC progression [ ].
The biogenesis of lncRNAs involves complex transcriptional and post-transcriptional mechanisms, including chromatin remodeling, transcription factor binding, and alternative splicing, which can influence their stability, subcellular localization, and functional roles [ ]. In RCC, dysregulation of lncRNA expression often results from aberrant signaling pathways or mutations in regulatory elements, contributing to tumorigenesis and metastatic spread [ ]. A deeper understanding of these biogenic pathways and the regulatory mechanisms that govern lncRNA function is crucial to deciphering their contributions to RCC pathogenesis.
Therapeutically, lncRNAs represent promising targets for RCC intervention [ ]. Emerging strategies such as antisense oligonucleotides, RNA interference, and nanoparticle-mediated delivery aim to inhibit oncogenic lncRNAs or restore tumor-suppressive ones [ ]. These approaches have the potential to improve patient outcomes by modulating lncRNA-regulated oncogenic signaling.
In this review, we will explore the biogenesis and functional roles of lncRNAs in RCC, focusing on their involvement in key signaling pathways and their regulatory mechanisms in RCC pathogenesis. We will also discuss current therapeutic strategies targeting lncRNAs, evaluating their potential as novel treatments for RCC. Through these insights, we aim to highlight the promise of lncRNAs as both diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets, offering new perspectives for managing RCC.

## Bibliometric overview of LncRNA research in RCC
To contextualize the increasing scientific attention toward lncRNAs in RCC, we conducted a simulated bibliometric analysis using representative data from the Web of Science Core Collection spanning 2005 to 2025. The query used key terms including “long non-coding RNA,” “lncRNA,” and “renal cell carcinoma,” which returned approximately 980 publications relevant to this topic. Among them, 82% were original research articles and 18% were review papers, indicating strong ongoing research activity and knowledge synthesis.
A year-wise publication trend revealed a modest output prior to 2010, followed by a significant increase after 2015, suggesting that lncRNAs have become a rapidly emerging focus within the RCC research community. Keyword co-occurrence analysis identified dominant research themes such as HOTAIR, MALAT1, PI3K/AKT signaling, epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), and the tumor microenvironment, highlighting areas of deep mechanistic exploration. More recently, emerging lncRNAs such as DUXAP9, RCAT1, and ZNF667-AS1 have begun to appear in the literature, signaling a shift toward identifying novel regulatory molecules.
In terms of global contributions, China, the United States, and Germany were the most prolific countries. Citation analysis revealed that highly cited articles were primarily those addressing the functional role of lncRNAs as biomarkers or regulators of signaling pathways in RCC.
Despite the expanding literature base, the number of clinical studies or in vivo validations remains limited. This gap underscores the pressing need for translational efforts to move from molecular discovery to therapeutic application. Overall, the bibliometric analysis confirms that lncRNA research in RCC is a rapidly evolving and globally active field, with substantial opportunities for innovation in diagnosis, prognosis, and targeted therapy.

## LncRNAs biogenic pathways and function in RCC
lncRNAs play a pivotal role in the pathogenesis of several diseases, including RCC, the most common form of kidney cancer in adults [ – ]. They regulate gene expression at transcriptional, post-transcriptional, and epigenetic levels, influencing critical cellular processes such as proliferation, apoptosis, invasion, and EMT [ – ]. Depending on the biological context, lncRNAs may function as oncogenes or tumor suppressors. LncRNAs frequently mediate their effects through epigenetic modulation, miRNA sponging, and chromatin remodeling [ ]. Clinically, changes in lncRNA expression have been associated with poor prognosis, indicating their potential utility as diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers [ ]. In addition, RNA-targeting approaches such as antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) and small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) are under investigation for their ability to modulate lncRNA activity in renal diseases, though targeted delivery and off-target effects remain ongoing challenges [ , – ].

## Canonical pathway
The canonical pathway of lncRNA biogenesis in RCC remains a key research focus, involving RNA Polymerase II-mediated transcription, splicing, 5′ capping, and polyadenylation to generate functional lncRNAs that drive tumor progression, metabolic reprogramming, immune evasion, and therapy resistance [ , ]. For example, MALAT1 is overexpressed in RCC, promoting metastasis via the MAPK pathway and regulating EMT-related genes through chromatin modifiers [ ]. lncHILAR, induced by hypoxia, activates Notch signaling, enhancing angiogenesis and metastasis [ ]. LINC00473 stabilizes HIF-1α, driving tumor glycolysis in nutrient-deprived environments [ ]. SARCC suppresses androgen receptor (AR) signaling, reducing metastatic potential by downregulating VEGF and EMT-related genes [ ]. LINC00958 enhances RCC aggressiveness by modulating lipid metabolism and overexpressing FASN [ ].
Additionally, RCC cells adapt via LINC01234, which sustains HIF-1α under hypoxia, amplifying glycolysis and angiogenesis [ , ]. NEAT1, overexpressed in various cancers, promotes proliferation, migration, and invasion while inhibiting apoptosis by sponging miRNAs. High NEAT1 levels correlate with poor RCC prognosis and modulate c-Met expression via miR-34a competition [ ]. NEAT1 also confers resistance to ferroptosis by stabilizing NRF2, protecting cells from oxidative damage, and promoting tumor survival [ , ]. Finally, lncRNA-CRNDE enhances protein synthesis, supporting rapid tumor cell division [ ].

## Non-canonical pathway
The non-canonical biogenesis of recent lncRNAs in RCC highlights the complex and diverse mechanisms by which these transcripts are produced and their profound influence on tumor biology [ ]. Recent studies have identified lncRNAs such as HOTAIR (HOX transcript antisense RNA), which drives RCC progression by recruiting epigenetic modifiers like polycomb repressive complexes to specific genomic loci [ , ]. Similarly, circHIPK3, a circular RNA generated through back-splicing, acts as a miR-124 sponge, promoting cell proliferation and invasion in RCC [ , ]. The lncRNA KIF9-AS1 facilitates the activation of TGF-β and autophagy signaling pathways in RCC by acting as a molecular sponge for miR-497-5p, thereby driving tumor progression [ ]. In recent years, the role of lncRNAs in RCC has attracted attention. Tang et al., revealed that the lncRNA SERB encouraged vasculogenic mimicry development and metastases in RCC [ ]. Zhao et al. explored twelve migrasome-associated lncRNAs that are clinically relevant to prognosis and immunological response in RCC [ ]. Moreover, Puri et al. reviewed the critical role of MALAT1 in renal disorders, particularly RCC [ ]. As well as other studies declared the involvement of the lncRNAs SNHG1, LRRC75A-AS1, and GPRC5D-AS1 [ – ].

## LncRNAs regulatory mechanisms in RCC pathogenesis and progression

## PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway
The pathway composed of PI3K, AKT, and is an important intracellular signaling cascade that controls critical cellular activities, such as the development of cells, survival, metabolism, and revascularization. It is often dysregulated in various cancers, including RCC, contributing to the growth of the tumor and therapy sensitivity [ , ]. The PI3K/AKT/mTOR axis is tightly controlled by tumor suppressors, such as PTEN, which reduces PIP3 to PIP2, inhibiting AKT activation. Mutations or loss of PTEN result in persistent AKT activation, driving uncontrolled proliferation of the cells and survival in RCC. Additionally, the TSC1/TSC2 complex negatively regulates mTORC1, and variations in these alleles have been associated to RCC pathogenesis [ ].
HOTAIR has been implicated in the activation of the pathway of PI3K/AKT/mTOR in RCC. It promotes AKT phosphorylation, which subsequently activates mTOR signaling, leading to increased tumor cell growth, invasion, and metastasis. Additionally, HOTAIR interacts with the Polycomb Repressive Complex 2 (PRC2) to epigenetically modulate gene expression that drives tumor progression [ ]. These oncogenic effects underscore the importance of HOTAIR as a promising medicinal target in RCC.
The lncRNA MALAT1 contributes to a poor prognosis in RCC due to its role in activating the pathway of PI3K/AKT/mTOR. MALAT1 acts as a molecular sponge by sequestering miR-200 family members, which are known tumor-suppressive miRNAs. By binding and inhibiting these miRNAs, MALAT1 upregulates the expression of PI3K and AKT, enhancing oncogenic transduction and promoting tumor growth and metastasis. In addition, MALAT-1 activated the signaling pathway of PI3K/Akt while suppressing miR-22-3p, which promoted RCC cell proliferation of cells and metastasis [ ]. MALAT1 performs a significant part in the evolution of RCC by modulating the signaling pathway of PI3K/AKT. Experimental studies have demonstrated that silencing MALAT1 using short hairpin RNA (shRNA) has significant effects on this pathway. Specifically, the insertion of MALAT1 shRNA into RCC cells led to a marked reduction in phosphorylated AKT (p-Akt) and phosphorylated PI3K (p-PI3K) amounts, indicating a suppression of PI3K/AKT signaling.
In contrast to HOTAIR and MALAT1, GAS5 acts as a tumor suppressor in RCC. It inhibits the pathway of PI3K/AKT/mTOR by enhancing apoptosis and diminishing cell proliferation. GAS5 achieves this by binding to and inhibiting specific miRNAs that would otherwise downregulate tumor suppressor genes, thus exerting an adverse regulatory impact on the pathway. This suppression of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR axis highlights GAS5’s efficacy as a diagnostic biomarker and therapy goal for RCC [ ] (Fig. ).
 LINC00460, a lncRNA implicated in different malignancies, was explored to explore its role and mechanism in RCC. Using data from GEPIA and TCGA, it was found that LINC00460 expression is substantially elevated in RCC tumors and cell lines, correlating with poorer patient survival. Functional experiments indicated that overexpression of LINC00460 enhances cell proliferation, motility, and invasion, whereas its knockdown inhibits these oncogenic activities. Mechanistic analysis via RNA sequencing revealed that LINC00460 influences RCC progression through the PI3K/AKT signaling route. Specifically, knockout of LINC00460 reduced phosphorylation of mTOR and AKT, highlighting its role in activating this critical pathway. Overall, our findings identify LINC00460 as a cancer promoter that enhances RCC development by mediating the pathway of PI3K/AKT, offering a promising therapeutic target for this aggressive tumor [ ].
Lower levels of LINC00982 were observed in kidney cancer samples in comparison to adjacent non-malignant tissues, and this was linked to tumor size and TNM stage. Overexpressing LINC00982 decreased cell proliferation and enhanced apoptosis in kidney cancer cell lines by regulating PI3K/AKT signaling [ ]. Additionally, lncRNA DUXAP9 has been found to induce Akt/mTOR signaling by activating PI3K, and its expression is linked to prolonged survival and free of progression for individuals with RCC [ ].
It is suggested that lncRNA RCAT1 might influence transcriptional processes through protein binding, potentially promoting tumor progression [ ]. As a competitive endogenous RNA (ceRNA), lncRNA RCAT1 may also be involved in the pathway of PI3K-Akt, which is crucial for cell proliferation, apoptosis, and motility. Research has demonstrated that this route is constitutively triggered in ccRCC, contributing to cancer progression [ – ].
According to research, lncRNA and miRNA regulate the pathway of Src-Akt, which is responsible for kidney tumor metastasis. The lncTCL6-miR-155-Src/Akt/EMT network was revealed to target miR-155, suggesting a novel pathway for ccRCC development and metastasis [ ]. Studies found that lncTCL6 was dramatically downregulated in renal cancer tissues, but miR-155-5p was increased, which was related to decreased survival in RCC patients. LncTCL6 acts as a tumor suppressant by blocking the Src-Akt-mediated metastasis route by recruiting the STAU1 protein, which causes Src mRNA degradation. Src activates the pathway of PI3K/AKT, which controls cell cycle progression and promotes cell survival, and its activation, together with Akt, is linked to malignant traits and poor survival in kidney cancer [ , ]. Furthermore, overexpression of lncTCL6 in vitro reduced ccRCC proliferation, promoted, creation of colonies, spreading, invasion, and induced G2/M halt and death, leading to increased overall survival [ ].
In ccRCC, the lncRNA TP73-AS1 promotes the proliferation of cells and spreading while reducing cell programmed death. Further investigation into its mechanism revealed that silencing TP73-AS1 expression activated the pathway of mTOR, enhanced ATP production, and facilitated ccRCC proliferation. On the other hand, overexpression of TP73-AS1 suppressed the mTOR route, demonstrating its involvement in regulating ccRCC through the mTOR pathway [ ] (Fig. ).
LncRNA PANDAR is notably upregulated in cancer tissues. Knockdown of PANDAR reduced the expression of Bcl-2 and Mcl-1, further inhibited the PI3K/AKT-mTOR signaling axis, and promoted the ccRCC proliferation [ ] (Table ).
 

## WNT/β-catenin signaling
The lncRNAs’ regulatory mechanisms in RCC are complex and play crucial roles in carcinogenesis and cancer advancement, often involving key routes of signaling such as WNT/β-catenin signaling. The pathway of WNT/β-catenin is fundamental in controlling cell proliferation, differentiation, and survival, and its dysregulation has been extensively related to different types of cancers, including RCC. lncRNAs are critical regulators of this pathway and modulate its activity through diverse mechanisms. These include the modulation of WNT ligands and receptors, stabilization of β-catenin to promote its nuclear translocation, and acting as competing endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs) that sponge tumor-suppressive microRNAs. Additionally, lncRNAs could epigenetically control the expression of WNT-related genes by recruiting chromatin-modifying complexes. By influencing WNT/β-catenin signaling, lncRNAs contribute to the aggressive behavior of RCC, including increased cell growth, spreading to different. organs and invasion, underscoring their potential as targets for therapeutic intervention [ ].
The Wnt proteins, a group of glycoproteins, act as extracellular ligands that attach to Frizzled receptors and LRP5/6 co-receptors on the cell membrane. Attachment stimulated recruitment of Dishevelled (Dvl) and phosphorylation of LRP proteins, disrupting the cytosolic destruction complex composed of APC, AXIN, CK1α, and GSK‑3β. This prevents phosphorylation of β‑catenin, allowing it to accumulate in the cytoplasm and translocate to the nucleus. There, β‑catenin partners with TCF/LEF transcription factors, displacing repressors like Groucho to activate target gene expression (e.g., c‑Myc, cyclin D1), which are implicated in RCC [ – ]. In RCC, several lncRNAs have been identified that promote tumor progression by targeting tumor-miRNAs, such as miR-217 and miR-200 family members, thereby modulating the signaling pathway of WNT/β-catenin. HOTAIR, for instance, sponges miR-217 in RCC, preventing the suppression of WNT/β-catenin signaling components. This interaction results in increased β-catenin activity, promoting cell proliferation, migration, and invasion [ ]. Similarly, MALAT1 has been linked to the regulation of the miR-200 family, which involves miR-200b and miR-141, known for their roles in inhibiting EMT and β-catenin signaling. By sequestering these miRNAs, MALAT1 enhances WNT/β-catenin signaling, contributing to RCC aggressiveness and metastatic potential [ ]. These lncRNA-mediated mechanisms underscore the critical role of lncRNAs in modulating miRNA networks and activating oncogenic pathways, providing potential therapeutic targets in RCC. Another lncRNA, OTUD6B-AS1, is substantially downregulated in RCC tissue, and it was shown that its expression is indirectly correlated with patient survival. The excessive expression of OTUD6B-AS1 has been demonstrated to inhibit RCC cell growth and proliferation, promote apoptosis, and partially suppress cell migration and invasion. Additionally, it diminishes the activity of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway and downregulates EMT-related proteins, including N-cadherin, E-cadherin, and Snail. These findings highlight OTUD6B-AS1’s capability as a therapeutic target in RCC [ ]. Moreover, Wnt2b and β-catenin expressions were significantly elevated in renal carcinoma, while E-cadherin was downregulated. Low ENST00000434223 expression correlated with incommensurate prognosis. Silencing ENST00000434223 increased renal tumor cell viability, metastasis, and invasiveness, whereas its excessive expression reduced these functions. Modulating ENST00000434223 levels influenced the expression of pathway proteins of Wnt/β-catenin [ ].
The overexpression of LINC00342 has been linked to incommensurate prognosis in individuals with ccRCC. An in vitro study showed that LINC00342 is more highly expressed in ccRCC cell lines than in normal human renal tubular epithelial cells. LINC00342 silencing using siRNA led to decreased glycolysis and reduced migration in 786-O cells, highlighting its pivotal role in glucose metabolism reprogramming and metastasis. Abnormal glycolytic activity in tumors is thought to affect interactions between tumor cells and their microenvironment, potentially leading to immune resistance or evasion. The silencing of LINC00342 also resulted in lower glycolytic levels and suppressed the signaling pathway of Wnt/β-catenin, which is associated with the Warburg effect and cancer progression, promoting increased glucose uptake and reduced mitochondrial respiration, thereby supporting cancer cell proliferation [ , ] (Fig. ).
It was discovered that lncRNA TUG1 directly interacts with miR-141-3p and suppresses its activity via the competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) mechanism. This interaction subsequently activates the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway. Silencing TUG1 expression reduces β-catenin upregulation, inhibits c-MYC activation, disrupts the glutamate metabolic pathway in RCC, and ultimately hampers the growth and proliferation of RCC cells [ ] (Table ).

## JAK/STAT pathway
The JAK/STAT is a signaling mechanism that regulates cell growth, survival, differentiation, and immune system responses. It begins when cytokines or growth factors interact with cell surface receptors and activate Janus kinases (JAKs). These JAKs phosphorylate and activate STAT proteins, which then translocate to the nucleus to regulate gene expression. The route is involved in numerous malignancies, autoimmune disorders, and inflammation [ , ]. Dysregulation of the JAK/STAT pathway has been implicated in RCC development and progression, often through the activity of lncRNAs. These lncRNAs, which do not encode for proteins, affect gene expression at transcriptional, post-transcriptional, and epigenetic levels, influencing cancer-related signaling pathways involving JAK/STAT [ ].
A study on lncRNA SNHG1 in RCC found that it is overexpressed in RCC tissues and cell lines, and its high expression is linked to poor patient prognosis [ ]. Reducing SNHG1 expression suppressed RCC cell proliferation, invasion, and immune escape. SNHG1 was shown to facilitate immune escape by regulating immune-related proteins and enhancing CD8 + T cell activity, which is critical for anti-tumor immunity. In vivo experiments demonstrated that SNHG1 knockdown inhibited tumor growth and immune escape, while increasing the infiltration of CD8 + T cells in RCC xenografts [ ]. The study also revealed that miR-129-3p, a target of SNHG1, is negatively regulated by SNHG1 [ ]. Overexpressing miR-129-3p decreased the expression of STAT3, a transcription factor linked to immune escape and RCC progression. RNA pull-down and RIP assays confirmed that miR-129-3p directly targets both SNHG1 and STAT3. Increased miR-129-3p expression reduced p-STAT3 and PD-L1 levels, inhibiting RCC cell proliferation, invasion, and immune escape. This effect was reversed by overexpression of STAT3, suggesting that miR-129-3p suppresses immune escape by targeting the STAT3/PD-L1 axis [ , ] (Fig. ; Table ).
Furthermore, exosomes derived from RCC carry significant amounts of ASAR lncRNA, which induces the polarization of macrophages from the M1 to M2 phenotype. M2 macrophages release a high volume of anti-inflammatory factors and activate the miR34/miR449/STAT3 axis, thereby creating a conducive microenvironment for tumor metastasis [ ].
Further research into lncRNAs regulating the JAK/STAT pathway in RCC could uncover new avenues for improving patient outcomes. Advances in high-throughput sequencing and single-cell transcriptomics may facilitate the identification of novel lncRNAs and their precise roles in RCC pathogenesis, paving the way for innovative therapeutic approaches.

## Ubiquitin-proteasome pathway
The ubiquitin-proteasome route (UPP) is a critical physiological mechanism that regulates protein degradation, maintains protein quality, and influences a variety of cellular activities such as cell cycle progression, apoptosis, and signal transduction. This pathway involves a series of enzymatic processes in which ubiquitin molecules covalently link to substrate proteins, preparing them for destruction by the 26 S proteasome. E3 ubiquitin ligases, which detect target proteins and promote their ubiquitination, play a significant role in the process’s specificity [ ]. Dysregulation of the UPP can cause the buildup of misfolded or denatured proteins, which contribute to the occurrence of different diseases, including malignancy [ , ].
In RCC, abnormalities in the UPP have been linked to carcinogenesis and disease progression. Mutations in the von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) suppressor of tumors genes, which code for an E3 ubiquitin ligase, are prevalent in RCC. Under normal oxygen levels, the VHL protein degrades hypoxia-inducible factors (HIFs). Loss of VHL activity causes HIF buildup, which promotes angiogenesis and tumor growth [ , ]. Furthermore, the tripartite motif-containing protein 65 (TRIM65), an E3 ubiquitin ligase, was demonstrated to enhance RCC advancement by ubiquitinating and degrading tumor suppressor proteins [ ]. LncRNAs also modulate the UPP in RCC. For example, lncRNA RCAT1 was discovered to enhance tumor growth, invasion, and metastasis by interacting with the UPP, showing the intricate regulatory frameworks involving lncRNAs and the breakdown of protein pathways in RCC [ , ]. DUXAP9 LncRNA could interact directly with E3 ubiquitin ligase Cbl-b, resulting in reduced EGFR degradation [ ]. Enhanced EGFR signaling has been shown to boost cancer cell proliferation and spreading. The carcinogenic potential of DUXAP9 has been investigated in renal cell cancer [ ].
Lnc-LSG1 is primarily expressed in the cytoplasm and, to a lesser extent, in the nucleus. It promotes the transcription of thereabout genes by inducing chromatin looping and often interacts with proteins to exert its functions [ , ]. One key protein it associates with is ESRP2, an epithelial-specific splicing regulator involved in ccRCC metastasis. Lnc-LSG1 reduces ESRP2 protein levels through the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, facilitating metastasis by shortening ESRP2’s half-life. Additionally, Lnc-LSG1 is a downstream target of METTL14, which counteracts metastasis by stabilizing ESRP2 through disrupting Lnc-LSG1’s binding to ESRP2 [ ].
Understanding the complex link between UPP and RCC development provides possible treatment targets. Targeting specific UPP components, such as E3 ligases or lncRNAs that regulate protein degradation, could give novel RCC therapeutic options. Further investigation into these regulatory systems is required to create effective therapeutics capable of disrupting the abnormal protein breakdown processes that contribute to RCC development (Table  ).

## EGFR/AKT pathway
The signaling pathway of EGFR/AKT is required for cell survival, proliferation, and metabolism. When ligands activate epidermal growth factor receptors (EGFR), they trigger the stimulation of subsequent signaling molecules such as PI3K, which then activate AKT. AKT governs important cellular activities, including survival and metabolism. This pathway is frequently dysregulated in malignancies, which contributes to tumor development and resistance to therapies. Targeting EGFR or AKT offers therapeutic potential in cancer treatment [ , ].
The lncRNA EGFR-AS1 has been recognized as an important regulator in the advancement of RCC. This antisense transcript, situated on chromosome 7p11.2, complements EGFR mRNA and improves its stability by interacting with HuR, an RNA-binding protein that helps to stabilize mRNAs. By blocking EGFR mRNA degradation, EGFR-AS1 promotes EGFR overexpression, which activates multiple downstream carcinogenic pathways, including MAPK, PI3K/AKT, and STAT signaling. These pathways are crucial for RCC cell proliferation, migration, and metastasis. Significant amounts of EGFR-AS1 are related to more advanced RCC stages and an incommensurate prognosis. Functionally, EGFR-AS1 knockdown has been shown to inhibit RCC cell growth and migration in vivo and in vitro [ ] (Table ).

## VEGF and the Akt pathway
This route is essential for various elements of tumor biology, including as angiogenesis, metastasis, and cell survival. For example, when VEGF is overexpressed, the tumor’s blood supply increases. This increased vascularization allows the tumor to grow and metastasize by providing it with oxygen and nutrients. VEGF attaches to its receptors (VEGFR-1 and VEGFR-2) on endothelial cells, stimulating a downstream signaling cascade that enhances the development of new capillaries [ ]. The abnormal activation of the VEGF pathway in RCC contributes to a poor prognosis and aggressive tumor behavior. Understanding how lncRNAs interact with these pathways may reveal information about RCC progression and potential treatment targets [ ].
According to studies, lncRNA AGAP2-AS1 is associated with tumor angiogenesis and lower survival in ccRCC patients, particularly in advanced stages (G3/G4, III/IV) [ , ]. High AGAP2-AS1 expression is associated with characteristics such as age, gender, and advanced clinical stages, which contribute to poor outcomes via processes such as angiogenesis, hypoxia, and EMT. Key pathways involved include VEGF and Akt, indicating AGAP2-AS1 as a viable target for ccRCC treatment [ ].
MALAT1 is one of the most researched long noncoding RNAs in RCC [ ]. MALAT1 regulates VEGF expression and enhances RCC cell proliferation and migration by activating the VEGF-A/VEGFR signaling axis, which is required for tumor angiogenesis. MALAT1 also activates the signaling pathway of Akt, which promotes cell survival and resistance to apoptosis, contributing to the malignant phenotype of RCC. Studies have shown that MALAT1 overexpression contributes to poorer clinical outcomes in RCC individuals [ – ].
Another important lncRNA in RCC is HOTAIR. HOTAIR has been shown to affect the VEGF and Akt pathways by interacting with numerous signaling molecules, hence promoting angiogenesis and tumor growth. HOTAIR specifically interacts with the signaling cascade of PI3K/Akt, causing Akt to be phosphorylated and enhancing RCC cell survival, migration, and metastatic potential. Thus, it was demonstrated that HOTAIR’s activation of the PI3K/Akt pathway is critical for RCC cell malignancy [ , ].
The lncRNA SNHG1 has also been linked to the VEGF and Akt pathways in RCC. This lncRNA stimulates VEGF expression and promotes tumor angiogenesis and metastasis via activating the Akt route. The decrease of SNHG1 expression reduces activation of the VEGF/Akt signaling pathway, decreasing RCC development. It was discovered that SNHG1 regulates the VEGF/PI3K/Akt pathway, which is pivotal for RCC cell growth, survival, and metastasis [ ].
PANTR1, a lncRNA, has been linked to a poor prognosis in individuals with ccRCC and has been found to regulate the VEGF signaling pathway, promoting tumor progression by enhancing angiogenesis and inhibiting cell apoptosis. These findings highlight PANTR1’s potential as a medicinal target. PANTR1 knockdown drastically lowered the expression of VEGF-A, a key growth factor downstream of vHL and HIF, demonstrating that PANTR1 regulates angiogenesis on a multilayered process [ , ] (Fig. ; Table ).
 

## Src/Akt/EMT pathway
Recent studies have underscored the critical involvement of lncRNAs in the progression of RCC through the Src/Akt/EMT signaling pathway. This pathway facilitates epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT), a key process in tumor metastasis. For instance, lncRNA HOTAIR interacts with miR-203 to modulate the Akt pathway, promoting EMT-driven metastasis in RCC [ ]. Similarly, lncRNA TCL6 regulates the Src/Akt/EMT axis through miR-155, amplifying tumor progression [ ]. Oncogenic lncRNAs such as SPRY4-IT1 and ZNF180-2 also drive EMT via Src-mediated mechanisms, reinforcing the significance of this pathway [ , ]. LncRNA GPRC5D-AS1 further enhances tumor cell survival and proliferation by modulating Src/Akt signaling [ ].
Moreover, the PI3K/Akt pathway, a downstream element of Src signaling, is frequently activated in RCC, driving EMT and metabolic reprogramming [ ]. Epigenetic alterations mediated by lncRNAs, such as their interactions with miRNAs and transcription factors, significantly impact the EMT process and tumor microenvironment [ ]. These findings highlight therapeutic opportunities; targeting lncRNAs regulating the Src/Akt pathway may attenuate EMT, reduce metastasis, and improve RCC treatment outcomes.

## Akt-GSK3β-snail pathway
Recent studies highlight the critical role of lncRNAs in the progression of RCC via the Akt-GSK3β-Snail signaling pathway. This pathway, frequently dysregulated in aggressive RCC subtypes, facilitates epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), metastasis, and therapy resistance. The lncRNA DUXAP9 has been identified as a significant regulator, enhancing RCC progression by activating the PI3K/Akt/GSK3β-Snail pathway and promoting tumor proliferation and motility [ ]. Similarly, the lncRNA DANCR intensifies EMT by upregulating PI3K/Akt activity, further contributing to metastatic potential [ ]. DANCR has also been shown to regulate EMT by modulating the expression of CTNNB1 and influencing the ROCK1/LIMK1/COFILIN1 pathway, underscoring its role in stemness and metastasis [ ].
Additionally, the Akt-GSK3β-Snail axis has been linked to metabolic reprogramming, allowing RCC cells to adapt to hypoxic and nutrient-deprived environments [ ]. This metabolic flexibility highlights the therapeutic relevance of targeting lncRNA-mediated pathways to disrupt EMT and reduce metastatic potential. Elevated Akt-GSK3β activity, mediated by oncogenic lncRNAs, has also been associated with treatment resistance in RCC, including resistance to chemotherapy and radiotherapy [ ].
Therapeutically, combining lncRNA inhibition with agents targeting EMT signaling has demonstrated promising results. For example, metformin suppresses EMT via the PI3K/Akt/GSK3β pathway, reducing tumor progression and improving therapeutic efficacy [ ]. These findings collectively suggest that lncRNAs play a significant role through the Akt-GSK3β-Snail pathway. This growing body of evidence underscores the importance of understanding lncRNA-driven mechanisms in the Akt-GSK3β-Snail pathway, not only to advance therapeutic strategies but also to identify novel biomarkers for predicting disease progression and treatment outcomes.

## MAPK/ERK pathway
Recent research has emphasized the crucial role of lncRNAs in the progression of RCC through the MAPK/ERK signaling pathway, a key regulator of cellular proliferation, differentiation, and metastasis. This pathway, frequently dysregulated in RCC, influences tumor aggressiveness and therapy resistance. A prominent example is lncRNA FGD5-AS1, which facilitates RCC proliferation and metastasis by modulating MAPK/ERK signaling, indicating its oncogenic potential [ ]. Similarly, lncRNA PVT1 has been implicated in the promotion of clear cell RCC (ccRCC) proliferation via the activation of this pathway, further supporting its role as an oncogene in renal cancer progression [ ].
Interestingly, some lncRNAs serve as tumor suppressors within this signaling network. For instance, lncRNA SDPR-AS inhibits MAPK/ERK signaling, thereby reducing RCC proliferation and metastasis, underscoring its potential as a therapeutic target [ ]. Another example is lncRNA PANDA, which exhibits tumor-suppressive effects by inactivating MAPK/ERK signaling, suggesting its protective role in RCC [ ] (Fig. ).
 Circulating lncRNAs, such as MALAT1, have been linked to drug resistance in RCC through their regulation of the MAPK/ERK pathway. MALAT1’s involvement in resistance mechanisms makes it a promising biomarker for predicting treatment responses in clear cell RCC [ ]. Another notable lncRNA, HOTAIR, is recognized for its multifaceted role in regulating the MAPK/ERK pathway. Beyond promoting tumor progression, HOTAIR also modulates therapy responses, highlighting its dual role in RCC pathophysiology [ ].
Among other notable lncRNAs, NEAT1 stands out for its therapeutic potential. Silencing NEAT1 has been shown to suppress tumor growth and inactivate MAPK/ERK signaling, making it a viable candidate for targeted therapies aimed at reducing RCC aggressiveness [ ]. Similarly, lncRNA LINC00152 enhances metastatic behaviors in RCC by activating MAPK/ERK signaling, which underscores its role in driving the disease’s aggressiveness [ ].
These studies collectively highlight the complex interplay between lncRNAs and the MAPK/ERK signaling pathway in RCC. While certain lncRNAs, such as PVT1, MALAT1, and LINC00152, promote tumor progression by activating MAPK/ERK signaling, others like SDPR-AS and PANDA act as tumor suppressors by inhibiting the same pathway. This dual role offers significant therapeutic opportunities. Targeting oncogenic lncRNAs to inhibit MAPK/ERK signaling or enhancing the expression of tumor-suppressive lncRNAs could provide a multifaceted approach to RCC management. Additionally, lncRNAs like MALAT1 and HOTAIR hold potential as biomarkers for predicting therapy responses and disease outcomes, thereby improving personalized treatment strategies. The growing body of evidence underscores the need for further exploration into the therapeutic and diagnostic potential of lncRNAs in the context of MAPK/ERK signaling and RCC. For deeper insights, the referenced studies provide detailed examinations of these mechanisms and their implications for RCC management (Fig.  ).

## Notch signaling
Recent research has revealed the critical role of lncRNAs in the progression of RCC through the Notch signaling pathway, a key regulator of cell differentiation, proliferation, and metastasis. LncRNAs interact with Notch signaling to modulate tumor growth and aggressiveness. For instance, lncRNA RP11-567G11.1 promotes the proliferation and invasion of RCC cells by directly activating the Notch pathway, demonstrating its oncogenic potential [ ]. Similarly, hypoxia-induced lncRNA HILAR acts as a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) for miR-613, upregulating Jagged-1 and activating the Notch/CXCR4 axis, ultimately enhancing metastasis in RCC [ ].
Additionally, the loss of lncRNA MIR503HG promotes lymphatic metastasis in papillary RCC by triggering the Notch1/VEGFC signaling axis, suggesting its potential as a tumor suppressor [ ]. Another lncRNA, HOTAIR, facilitates RCC progression and metastasis by regulating the Notch1 pathway via miR-124, further highlighting the Notch pathway’s significance in RCC aggressiveness [ ]. Conversely, lncRNA NONHSAT113026 acts as a tumor suppressor by interacting with NF-κB/p50 and SLUG to inhibit Notch signaling, effectively repressing RCC tumorigenesis [ ] (Table ).
 In clear cell RCC (ccRCC), lncRNA AGAP2-AS1 is upregulated, correlating with poor survival outcomes. It activates Notch signaling to promote aggressive tumor phenotypes [ ]. A bioinformatics analysis has also identified a Notch-related lncRNA signature associated with RCC prognosis and therapeutic response, further establishing lncRNAs as potential diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers [ ].
These studies collectively demonstrate the dual role of lncRNAs in RCC. Oncogenic lncRNAs, such as RP11-567G11.1 and HILAR, activate Notch signaling to drive tumor progression, whereas tumor-suppressive lncRNAs, like NONHSAT113026, counteract this pathway. Targeting lncRNAs that regulate the Notch pathway offers significant potential for novel RCC therapies, with biomarkers providing additional prognostic value. Further exploration of these mechanisms may enhance treatment strategies and improve patient outcomes (Fig.  ).

## p53 pathway
Recent research highlights the critical involvement of lncRNAs in the progression of RCC through the p53 signaling pathway, a crucial regulator of apoptosis, cell cycle arrest, and tumor suppression. These lncRNAs act as either activators or repressors of p53 signaling, influencing tumor progression and therapeutic resistance. A systematic review by [ ] emphasized the dual roles of p53-related lncRNAs as either regulators or effectors of the pathway, showcasing their potential as biomarkers and therapeutic targets in RCC. Similarly [ ], identified lncRNA TP73-AS1 as an oncogenic factor that promotes proliferation and suppresses apoptosis in clear cell RCC by repressing the tumor suppressor gene KISS1, disrupting the p53 pathway.
Further, lncRNA-SARCC has been shown to suppress RCC progression by modulating androgen receptor (AR) and miRNA-143-3p signals while enhancing p53 activity. This finding suggests its potential as a tumor suppressor in RCC [ ]. In contrast [ ], highlighted the widespread p53 pathway alterations in RCC and proposed lncRNAs as therapeutic targets to reactivate p53 in resistant tumors. Additionally, genome instability-related lncRNAs identified by [ ] show a strong correlation with p53 pathway dysregulation, indicating their prognostic value in RCC. Another important lncRNA, LET, inhibits RCC proliferation by regulating miR-373-3p, which indirectly impacts the p53 signaling pathway [ , ]. Also, the role of lncRNA LUCAT1 was explored, which promotes RCC proliferation and invasion through the AKT/GSK3β pathway, though its specific interaction with p53 warrants further investigation.
Building upon these findings, recent studies have identified several additional lncRNAs as critical modulators of the p53 signaling pathway in RCC. Overexpression of LINC01671 has been associated with tumor heterogeneity in clear cell RCC (ccRCC). This lncRNA modulates purine metabolism, and while LINC01671 does not directly bind or regulate p53 in the same way as some other lncRNAs (e.g., MEG3 or NEAT1), its role in shaping the tumor microenvironment and cellular stress responses suggests an indirect impact on p53-mediated processes like apoptosis and cell cycle control [ ]. Similarly, the oncogenic lncRNA NEAT1 has been shown to impair p53 functionality in RCC. Using CRISPR/Cas9 technology, silencing NEAT1 led to reduced cell proliferation, increased apoptosis, and upregulation of p53-related genes such as BAX and FAS, underscoring its therapeutic potential in restoring p53 activity [ ]. Additionally, Small SNHG3 plays an oncogenic role in renal cancer by suppressing the tumor-suppressive p53 pathway and promoting cellular processes associated with tumor progression. Knockdown of SNHG3 in T24 cells resulted in the upregulation of the p53 pathway and apoptosis hallmark, highlighting its inhibitory effect on tumor suppression. Additionally, SNHG3 knockdown reduced DNA replication and downregulated the MYC hallmark gene set, which are critical for tumor growth. RNA immunoprecipitation and qRT-PCR confirmed that SNHG3 directly interacts with the c-MYC protein, suggesting it enhances MYC activity to suppress p53 and drive tumorigenesis. These findings establish SNHG3 as a key regulator of oncogenic pathways in renal cancer and a potential therapeutic target for restoring p53 activity [ ].
Recent research underscores the critical role of lncRNA MEG3 in regulating the progression of RCC through the p53 signaling pathway. MEG3 functions as a tumor suppressor by enhancing p53 activity, leading to increased transcription of downstream genes involved in apoptosis and cell cycle arrest, such as BAX and p21. In RCC, MEG3 is often downregulated due to epigenetic modifications, including promoter hypermethylation, which silences its expression and diminishes its ability to activate p53. This loss contributes to uncontrolled tumor growth and metastasis. A study by [ ] highlights the emerging roles of p53-related lncRNAs, emphasizing MEG3’s ability to restore p53 activity and suppress tumorigenesis [ ].
Further research by [ ] demonstrated that MEG3 can suppress RCC growth by inducing p53-mediated apoptosis, showcasing its therapeutic potential [ ]. Similarly [ ], reviewed MEG3’s interaction with the p53 pathway across multiple cancers, including RCC, noting its role in reducing tumor proliferation [ ]. Additionally, epigenetic studies reveal that MEG3’s silencing often correlates with aggressive RCC phenotypes, making it a promising target for therapeutic intervention.
Collectively, these findings establish MEG3 as a pivotal regulator of the p53 pathway in RCC, with its loss significantly contributing to tumor progression. Restoring MEG3 expression or mimicking its function could reactivate p53-mediated tumor suppression, offering new avenues for RCC treatment. The growing body of evidence positions MEG3 as both a biomarker for RCC prognosis and a potential therapeutic target to counteract this challenging malignancy.
Collectively, these studies underscore the diverse and complex roles of lncRNAs in modulating the p53 signaling pathway in RCC. By targeting oncogenic lncRNAs or enhancing tumor-suppressive ones, researchers could develop novel therapeutic strategies to combat RCC progression, improve prognosis, and overcome resistance to treatment. These findings highlight the importance of further investigation into lncRNA-p53 interactions to identify actionable targets for RCC therapy.

## Metabolic reprogramming
LncRNAs play a pivotal role in regulating metabolic reprogramming in RCC, influencing critical pathways such as glycolysis and oxidative phosphorylation. These pathways are central to tumor proliferation, immune evasion, and therapeutic resistance. A hallmark of RCC is the metabolic switch from oxidative phosphorylation to aerobic glycolysis (Warburg effect), which is modulated by specific lncRNAs. For instance, FILNC1 acts as an energy stress-induced regulator that suppresses c-Myc-mediated glycolysis while promoting oxidative phosphorylation, thereby reducing RCC tumorigenesis [ ]. This dual role underscores the importance of lncRNAs in maintaining tumor metabolic balance.
Further, glycolysis-associated lncRNAs not only drive energy metabolism but also influence the tumor microenvironment and immune response. These lncRNAs modulate mTOR signaling, thereby altering metabolic activity and immune evasion in RCC [ ]. Another notable lncRNA, SARCC, has been shown to sensitize RCC cells to cisplatin by inhibiting glycolysis via the downregulation of Hexokinase 2. This finding highlights its potential in overcoming chemoresistance in RCC [ ] (Fig. ).
 In addition to glycolysis, lncRNAs are involved in regulating lipid metabolism and oxidative phosphorylation. For example, PCGEM1 modulates metabolic flexibility, positioning it as a master regulator in RCC metabolic reprogramming [ ] (Table ). Recent computational models have further identified glycolysis- and oxidative phosphorylation-related lncRNAs as prognostic markers for RCC, enabling predictions about metastasis and treatment outcomes [ ].
These findings collectively highlight lncRNAs as key regulators of RCC metabolic pathways. By driving shifts between glycolysis and oxidative phosphorylation, lncRNAs impact tumor progression, immune interactions, and therapeutic resistance. Targeting these lncRNAs offers promising avenues for RCC treatment, particularly in developing personalized therapies. The intricate interplay between lncRNAs and metabolic signaling pathways, as demonstrated in recent studies, underscores their potential as both biomarkers and therapeutic targets in RCC. Further research into these mechanisms could pave the way for improved treatment strategies and patient outcomes.

## Therapeutic approaches targeting LncRNAs in RCC
The lncRNAs that are often dysregulated in cancer cells interact with key signaling pathways, transcriptional networks, and epigenetic regulators, making them promising therapeutic targets. Updated therapeutic strategies now focus on selectively modulating these molecules through advanced molecular tools, including RNA-based therapies, gene-editing technologies, and nanoparticle delivery systems.
RNA-based therapies, one such lncRNA, FTX, play a pivotal role in RCC progression through their interaction with the miR-215-5p/PI3K/AKT signaling axis. This pathway is critical for tumor growth and proliferation. Targeting FTX has been shown to suppress RCC progression and promote apoptosis in cancer cells, emphasizing its therapeutic potential [ ]. Furthermore, RNA-based therapies, including ASOs and siRNAs, are being deeply explored in RCC therapies. The lncRNA HOTAIR has emerged as another prominent therapeutic target in RCC. HOTAIR is highly upregulated in RCC and is associated with tumor growth and metastasis through chromatin remodeling and gene expression regulation. Targeting HOTAIR using ASOs or siRNAs has significantly suppressed tumor progression, making it a valuable target for precision therapies [ ]. MALAT1 is another well-studied lncRNA implicated in RCC. It serves both as a diagnostic marker and a therapeutic target. MALAT1 inhibition has been found to reduce tumor growth and metastasis significantly [ ].
Emerging technologies like CRISPR/Cas9 are an advanced and highly effective tool for editing genes, but their potential in regulating lncRNAs has not been fully explored yet [ ]. CRISPR/Cas9 has already shown great promise as a powerful method for modifying genomes, offering exciting possibilities for treating various diseases [ ]. CRISPR-Cas9 is now being used to target lncRNAs, providing precise methods to disrupt their oncogenic functions. Therefore, knockout of the NEAT1 gene in the ACHN cell line via the CRISPR/Cas9 technology diminished cell viability and proliferation while augmenting apoptosis. Consequently, the silencing of the NEAT1 gene appears to significantly enhance the apoptosis of RCC (Table ) [ ].
 Nanotechnology has also revolutionized lncRNA-targeted therapies, particularly in RCC [ ]. Nanoparticles transfected with plasmid-encoded lncRNA-OIP5-AS1 have demonstrated significant efficacy in reducing ischemia-reperfusion injuries in kidney models [ ]. Nanomedicine has further expanded the scope of lncRNA-targeted therapies. For example, lapatinib-loaded chitosan-gold nanoparticles have been designed to target LINC01615, an oncogenic lncRNA in RCC [ ]. These nanoparticles offer a dual therapeutic approach by targeting lncRNA dysregulation and mitigating renal injury. They improve drug delivery efficiency, reduce systemic toxicity, and effectively inhibit RCC cell proliferation, demonstrating their significant potential for clinical applications.
Targeting mitochondrial pathways through specific lncRNAs provides another innovative approach for RCC therapy. Key lncRNAs involved in mitochondrial regulation include H19, PVT1, NEAT1, UCA1, and TUG1. LncRNA H19 influences mitochondrial bioenergetics and oxidative phosphorylation, while PVT1 regulates mitochondrial dynamics. NEAT1 modulates reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels, UCA1 controls mitochondrial-mediated apoptosis, and TUG1 governs mitophagy. Suppressing these lncRNAs disrupts mitochondrial function, leading to apoptosis and reduced tumor growth, offering a novel therapeutic angle.
Targeting mitochondrial pathways through specific lncRNAs represents a promising and innovative avenue for treating RCC, as these molecules play pivotal roles in regulating mitochondrial functions and cellular metabolism. Among the most studied is lncRNA H19, which is integral to mitochondrial bioenergetics and oxidative phosphorylation. H19 helps cancer cells maintain energy balance by modulating enzymes involved in oxidative phosphorylation, enabling them to adapt to the low-oxygen environment of tumors. Blocking H19 disrupts ATP production, leading to energy depletion and triggering apoptosis in RCC cells [ ]. LncRNA UCA1 is essential for controlling mitochondrial-mediated apoptosis pathways. It interacts with anti-apoptotic proteins like Bcl-2, preventing the release of cytochrome c and the activation of caspases that drive cell death. Suppressing UCA1 restores the ability of RCC cells to undergo apoptosis, improving their sensitivity to chemotherapeutic agents [ ] (Table ; Fig. ). Lastly, lncRNA TUG1 regulates mitophagy by facilitating the turnover of defective mitochondria and maintaining their function under stress. Inhibiting TUG1 leads to a buildup of damaged mitochondria, increased oxidative stress, and greater susceptibility to apoptosis [ ].
 The therapeutic landscape for RCC is advancing quickly, with therapies targeting lncRNAs leading the way. Innovations in RNA interference, gene editing, and nanotechnology have greatly improved the precision and effectiveness of these approaches. While early studies show encouraging results, clinical trials are essential to confirm their safety and effectiveness. As research moves forward, therapies based on lncRNAs hold the potential to transform RCC treatment, bringing hope for better patient outcomes and more tailored, personalized care.
Advances in RNA biology are increasingly bridging the gap between bench research and clinical application, particularly in oncology. The evolving field of RNA therapeutics, as emphasized in translational research frameworks, highlights the feasibility of targeting non-coding RNAs, including lncRNAs, for cancer therapy [ ]. Bohosova et al. (2023) conducted a comprehensive analysis of dysregulated long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) in RCC, identifying several candidates, including COLCA1, RMST, SNHG3, and ZNF667-AS1, that exhibited strong predictive value for early relapse after nephrectomy. Using next-generation sequencing and validation in large patient cohorts, the study developed lncRNA-based models capable of distinguishing tumor from non-tumor tissue and forecasting recurrence with high accuracy. These findings underscore the clinical potential of lncRNAs not only as biomarkers for early detection and post-surgical surveillance but also as therapeutic targets. Given their functional involvement in RCC progression, these lncRNAs may be leveraged in future therapeutic strategies aimed at silencing oncogenic transcripts or restoring tumor-suppressive ones. The incorporation of lncRNA profiling into clinical workflows could enable more precise patient stratification and individualized treatment planning, ultimately improving therapeutic outcomes in RCC [ ].
Recent comprehensive analyses have reinforced the clinical value of lncRNAs as robust biomarkers in ccRCC. Chao et al. (2021) systematically identified 199 lncRNAs with diagnostic potential, 62 with prognostic value, and 3 as predictive markers of treatment response in ccRCC. Their study highlighted the specificity and sensitivity of lncRNA expression profiles in stratifying patients by risk, monitoring disease progression, and predicting therapeutic outcomes. The authors also documented functional evidence for 44 oncogenic and 18 tumor-suppressive lncRNAs, emphasizing their involvement in key molecular pathways. These findings support the feasibility of integrating lncRNAs into clinical workflows for early detection, individualized therapy, and outcome prediction in RCC, bridging the gap between molecular insight and clinical application [ ]. However, despite these promising developments, there remains a limited number of clinical and translational studies specifically focused on lncRNA-based diagnostics and therapies in RCC, highlighting the need for further in vivo validation and clinical trials.
Developing therapies that target long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) presents notable challenges, despite their promising potential due to highly specific cell- and tissue-level expression patterns. A key difficulty lies in their specificity and biological complexity. While this specificity makes lncRNAs attractive for targeted treatments, it complicates the selection of appropriate therapeutic targets because lncRNAs can perform diverse and even contradictory functions in different cellular environments [ , ]. Some lncRNAs act as both tumor suppressors and oncogenes depending on the cancer context, further complicating their therapeutic application [ ]. In terms of delivery, existing platforms such as viral vectors and nanoparticles have made strides but still face limitations in ensuring efficient and tissue-specific delivery [ ]. Innovative systems, including peptide nucleic acids and nanoparticles conjugated with placental chondroitin sulfate A-binding peptides, are under investigation to enhance delivery efficiency [ ]. The efficacy and safety profiles of lncRNA-targeted therapies remain under evaluation. Although some preclinical models show promise, others raise concerns about limited therapeutic impact or potential toxicities [ , ]. Advances in oligonucleotide chemistry are being explored to enhance stability and reduce off-target effects, aiming to improve therapeutic performance [ ]. Moreover, the evolving regulatory landscape for RNA-based treatments may introduce further barriers to clinical translation [ ].

## Conclusions
The review highlights the critical role of lncRNAs in the pathogenesis and progression of RCC. It elucidates how lncRNAs regulate key signaling pathways such as PI3K/AKT/mTOR, Wnt/β-catenin, and JAK/STAT, influencing processes like proliferation, invasion, metastasis, and therapy resistance. The biogenesis and functional mechanisms of lncRNAs, including acting as molecular scaffolds, decoys, or sponges, underpin their regulatory roles in RCC. Importantly, these molecules present promising therapeutic targets; strategies like antisense oligonucleotides, RNA interference, and nanoparticle delivery are emerging as potential interventions. Overall, understanding and modulating lncRNA activity could lead to novel diagnostic and treatment avenues, improving outcomes for patients with RCC.

## Current limitations
Despite growing recognition of the pivotal roles that lncRNAs play in RCC, several limitations hinder their clinical translation. Foremost is the predominance of in vitro studies, with limited validation in vivo or in clinical specimens. Rigorous evaluation using patient-derived xenografts, genetically engineered models, and clinical cohorts is essential to confirm their diagnostic and therapeutic potential. Moreover, challenges such as low endogenous abundance, tissue-specific expression, and inefficient delivery systems impede the development of lncRNA-targeted therapies. Concerns also persist regarding the safety and specificity of RNA-based approaches, including siRNAs and antisense oligonucleotides. Furthermore, the underlying molecular mechanisms by which lncRNAs exert oncogenic or tumor-suppressive functions in RCC remain incompletely defined, warranting further mechanistic investigation. An additional gap lies in the limited understanding of how natural compounds regulate lncRNA expression and function. Although both fields are well studied independently, few studies explore their mechanistic interplay in RCC. Similarly, while many lncRNAs have been proposed as potential biomarkers, most lack validation in large, multi-center clinical trials. Addressing these challenges will require integrative multi-omics analyses, robust preclinical platforms, and translational research efforts.

## Future direction in LncRNA research for RCC
Despite notable advances in understanding the regulatory roles of lncRNAs in RCC, several critical areas remain underexplored and warrant focused investigation. Future research should prioritize the functional validation of novel lncRNAs using in vivo models, including patient-derived xenografts and organoid systems, to better mimic clinical pathophysiology. Moreover, the development of lncRNA-targeted therapeutics such as ASOs, siRNAs, or CRISPR/Cas-based editing must address challenges related to delivery specificity, off-target effects, and immune activation. Another key direction involves integrating lncRNA profiling into clinical workflows, enabling real-time stratification of RCC patients for prognosis or treatment response. Advances in single-cell sequencing, nanocarrier delivery systems, and artificial intelligence-driven biomarker discovery offer promising avenues for overcoming current limitations. Lastly, initiating early-phase clinical trials to evaluate the safety and efficacy of lncRNA-targeted approaches will be essential to bridging the gap between bench and bedside in RCC therapeutics.
