## Abstract
Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality, emphasizing the need for novel therapeutic strategies. Decorin (DCN), a chondroitin sulfate proteoglycan (CSPG), has been proposed as a tumor suppressor, yet its precise role in HCC and the tumor microenvironment (TME) remains underexplored. Through integrated analyses of bulk RNA and single-cell RNA sequencing datasets, we identified a distinct tumor stromal subset highly expressing DCN and associated chondroitin sulfate (CS) synthases. Our findings revealed that DCN expression is significantly downregulated in HCC tissue, but upregulated in peri-tumor stroma, where it correlates with better prognosis and reduced capsular invasion. Western blot analysis demonstrated that CS-DCN, the glycosylated form of DCN, plays a dominant role in this context. Single-cell clustering analysis identified a unique stromal subset in HCC characterized by elevated expression of DCN, CSPGs, and CS synthases, associated with extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling and protective barrier functions. A six-gene DCN-associated signature derived from this subset, including DCN, BGN, SRPX, CHSY3, CHST3, and CHPF, was validated as a prognostic marker for HCC. Furthermore, functional assays demonstrated that CS-DCN significantly inhibited HCC cell proliferation and invasion. Our study highlights the critical role of DCN in HCC TME and provides insights into its therapeutic potential. Modulating CSPG pathways, particularly on CS-DCN-expressing stromal cells, may offer a promising approach for improving HCC treatment and patient outcomes.

## Introduction
Although the global incidence of liver cancer has declined with the widespread adoption of hepatitis B vaccination, it remains the third leading cause of cancer-related deaths. According to the CANCER TODAY 2022 database (https://gco.iarc.fr/today/en), liver cancer accounted for over 860,000 new cases and more than 750,000 deaths in 2022 [ ]. Approximately 85% of primary liver cancer cases are hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). Surgical resection is a viable option only for early-stage HCC, while patients with advanced HCC face limited treatment options, including first-line multi-target tyrosine kinase inhibitors (e.g., Sorafenib, Lenvatinib) and second-line inhibitors (e.g., Regorafenib, Cabozantinib). Although immune-checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)-based therapies are now used to treat HCC, only about 30% of patients experience an objective response, and the 3-year overall survival (OS) rate remains well below 50%, highlighting a continued need for novel therapeutic strategies [ ].
In addition to targeting exhausted immune cells, understanding the alterations in other cellular components of the tumor microenvironment (TME), such as cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), extracellular matrix proteins, and glycans, holds great potential for advancing HCC treatments [ , ]. Previous studies have reported the ectopic expression of several proteoglycans (PGs) in the TME of HCC, including glypican-3 (GPC3) [ ], versican (VCAN) [ , ], syndecan-1 (SDC1) [ , ], which are positively correlated with HCC progression. Therefore, investigating the aberrant expression and functions of PGs in the TME is of high diagnostic and prognostic value. For instance, GPC3 is virtually absent in normal liver tissue but is highly expressed in HCC tumors and detected at elevated levels in the serum of HCC patients, making it a promising diagnostic marker and a target for chimeric antigen receptor (CAR)-T cell therapy in clinical trials [ , ]. Thus, aberrant PGs in the TME are emerging as attractive targets for the development of novel HCC therapies.
PGs that contain one or more chondroitin sulfate (CS) side chains, known as chondroitin sulfate proteoglycans (CSPGs), play diverse roles in regulating cell behavior. CS side chains exhibit varying affinities for binding to growth factors, proteases, cytokines, and chemokines [ , ], thereby modulating critical signaling processes within the cancer microenvironment. Notably, the discovery of parasite protein VAR2CSA binds to a unique oncofetal CS structure on various types of cancer cells including HCC cells, highlights its potential as a diagnostic and therapeutic tool [ , ]. However, current research on different CS types and CS synthases indicates distinct and sometimes opposing effects on HCC progression. Several studies suggest that CS accumulation, particularly chondroitin-6-sulfate, promotes HCC progression and metastasis [ , , ]. In contrast, other findings reveal that specific CS-modifying enzymes can exert opposing effects. For instance, CHSY1, which mediates CS polymerization, enhances HCC malignancy via hedgehog signaling activation [ ], while CHPF, another CS-synthesizing enzyme, suppresses HCC progression by modifying CS structure on Decorin (DCN) [ ]. These findings suggest that different CSPGs and their co-expressed modifying enzymes may uniquely influence cancer progression.
DCN, a small leucine-rich CSPG, is downregulated in HCC tissues compared to nontumor liver, and its loss has been shown to promote tumorigenesis. Acting as a tumor suppressor, DCN inhibits HCC cell proliferation, induces apoptosis, and may interfere with receptor tyrosine kinases, including PDGFR, EGFR, and IGF-IR [ – ]. Although DCN shows therapeutic potential in HCC treatment, the specific TME cell types responsible for DCN production and the impact of DCN glycosylation on HCC progression remain unexplored. In this study, we reanalyzed bulk and single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) datasets to identify DCN-producing cell clusters and examine their co-expressed CSPG and CS enzymes. Our findings reveal a distinct Decorin-expressing tumor stromal subset with potential prognostic value for HCC.

## Results

## Upregulation of CS-DCN in tumor tissue is associated with good prognosis in HCC
Using the GEPIA database (http://gepia2.cancer-pku.cn/#index), we observed a sharp decline in DCN expression across multiple tumor types compared to peri-tumor tissue, particularly in liver cancers, urinary cancers, and reproductive organ cancers (Fig.  A). To further characterize DCN expression in normal and cancerous liver tissue, we examined the protein expression patterns of DCN using the Human Protein Atlas (HPA) database (https://www.proteinatlas.org/ENSG00000011465-DCN/pathology). Immunohistochemistry (IHC) analysis showed that DCN is expressed in specific normal stromal cells, while it is undetectable in normal hepatocytes. Conversely, DCN is highly expressed in peri-tumor stromal tissue (Fig.  B).
 According to previous studies, chondroitin sulfate-linked DCN (CS-DCN), with a molecular weight of over 100 kDa, is the predominant secreted form. Additionally, a 40 kDa core protein of DCN (core-DCN) can be detected in tissue and cultured cells [ – ]. We performed Western blot analysis to distinguish these two DCN forms in primary HCC and paired peri-tumor tissues (Fig.  C). The results indicated that both core-DCN and CS-DCN levels were reduced in tumor tissue, with a more pronounced decrease in CS-DCN compared to core-DCN (Fig.  D).
To investigate the correlation between CS-DCN or core-DCN levels and HCC prognosis, we analyzed 60 HCC tumor samples from a local hospital tissue bank. Long-exposure Western blot images revealed heterogeneous expression levels of core-DCN and CS-DCN (Fig.  E). Based on the mean intensity of the blots, samples were classified into high-expression and low-expression groups. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis demonstrated that HCC patients with high CS-DCN expression had significantly better survival rates compared to those with low CS-DCN expression (Fig.  F). In contrast, there was no statistically significant difference in survival between high and low core-DCN groups. Further analysis of the relationship between CS-DCN expression and clinicopathologic features of HCC patients revealed a significant correlation between low CS-DCN expression and capsular invasion (two-tailed Fisher’s exact test, P = 0.013) (Table  and Supplementary Table ). Collectively, these findings suggest that CS-DCN may possess tumor-suppressive properties in HCC.
 

## Identification of tumor stromal cluster that highly expresses DCN
The peri-tumor tissue of HCC contains various cell types, including fibroblasts, monocytes, and neutrophils, all of which play roles in influencing tumor progression and patient outcomes [ – ]. Given the observed upregulation of DCN in HCC peri-tumor tissue compared to the tumor core (Fig.  A), we next sought to determine if any specific cell type exhibits high DCN expression in HCC or healthy liver tissue. To explore this, we analyzed the single-cell RNA sequencing dataset SCP2154 with samples from 37 normal livers and 12 HCC tumors. Cells were categorized into seven types: B cells, endothelial cells, epithelial cells, hepatocytes, lymphocytes, myeloid cells, and stromal cells according to the original article, with erythrocytes excluded from analysis (Fig.  A) [ ]. Different cell distribution was observed between normal livers (n = 149,349; Fig.  B) and HCC samples (n = 25,869; Fig.  C). We found predominant DCN expressions in stromal cells of both normal and tumor samples (Fig.  D). Notably, only a small subset (23%) of stromal cells in normal livers expressed DCN, whereas a substantial portion (65%) of stromal cells in tumors showed positive DCN expression (Fig.  E). This result is in concordance with the scRNA-seq DCN enrichment analysis for normal liver cells available from The HPA database, where the scores represent the mean correlation between DCN and three other reference transcripts, with hepatic stellate cells having the highest value (Figure A). These findings suggested that higher DCN expression in HCC peri-tumor tissues may result from more tumor stromal cells accumulating in the tumor margin compared to the tumor core.
 

## Pathways associated with tumor stromal cells in HCC
To identify pathways associated with tumor stromal cells in HCC, we conducted Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) with 830 gene sets from the WikiPathways and two customized gene sets related to CS synthases and CSPGs on the SCP2154 dataset (see Methods) (Fig.  F). Among the upregulated pathways enriched in tumor stromal cells, the “Type I Collagen Synthesis” and “Lung Fibrosis” pathways suggested a role of tumor stromal cells on fibrosis, which is commonly linked to poor prognosis in cancer [ ]. We also observed positive enrichment in pathways related to angiogenesis, vascular remodeling, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition, as well as stem cell-like properties along with interactions with cancer stem cells. Interestingly, the CSPG gene set was upregulated in tumor stromal cells, indicating not only DCN but the entire CSPG gene set was prominently expressed in tumor stromal cells. We further analyzed the expression levels of individual CSPG and CS synthase genes, categorizing them into functional groups by elongation, linker, and modification. A heatmap displaying Z-score normalized gene expression levels revealed that several CSPGs, including DCN, CSPG4, and BGN, were sharply upregulated in tumor stromal cells (Fig.  G). Additionally, key CS enzymes such as CHST3, CHSY1, CHPF, and CHSY3 also showed increased expression in these cells. We used the CellChat package to analyze DCN-expressing stromal cells intercellular communication with other cell population [ , ]. The results indicated that the stromal cells with high DCN expression exhibited the strongest outgoing interaction strength among all cell types, specially targeting epithelial cells, lymphocytes, hepatocytes, and myeloid cells in liver tissue (Figure A and B). Moreover, these cells revealed distinct outgoing and incoming signaling profiles compared to DCN-low stromal cells (Figure C). These results suggest the DCN-expressing stromal cells may serve as critical modulators in liver tissue through active intercellular communication.
Cell-cell communication was analyzed by CellChat, obtaining the interaction strength and numbers of interaction pairs among cell subtypes. (A) Circle plot showing the cellular interaction in both secreted signaling and extracellular matrix-receptor. The thickness of the lines connecting cells indicates the numbers of interaction. (B) The strength of incoming and outgoing interactions in each cell subtypes. (C) The relative strength of individual signaling factor involved in the cell subtypes.

## The DCN-expressing stromal subset in HCC tumor
To further characterize the DCN-expressing liver stromal cells in normal and HCC tissues, we performed clustering analysis and identified six distinct clusters in the stromal cells of the SCP2154 dataset (Fig.  A). Clusters 0 and 5 were predominantly composed of stromal cells from HCC samples, whereas clusters 1, 2, 3, and 4 were primarily associated with normal liver tissue (Fig.  B and S3A). DCN was predominantly expressed in the majority of cells within cluster 5, followed by clusters 4, 0, and 2, while only a small proportion of cells in clusters 1 and 3 exhibited DCN expression (Fig.  C and S3B). Cluster 5 emerged as a tumor-specific subset exhibiting significantly higher DCN expression compared to other clusters.
 To characterize these stromal subclusters, we performed GSEA using the 830 WikiPathways gene sets along with the CS synthase and CSPG gene sets (Fig.  D). GSEA results revealed distinct functional signatures for cluster 5, including upregulation of pathways related to CSPG synthesis, active extracellular matrix remodeling, wound healing, and developmental processes. In contrast, cluster 0 showed enrichment in pathways linked to high cellular activity, proliferation, and stress response, potentially reflecting a role in tumor progression and adaptation. Further analysis of individual gene expression profiles highlighted that cluster 5 exhibited markedly elevated expression of CSPGs and CS synthases (Fig.  E). Specifically, DCN and BGN were prominently expressed, alongside multiple CS synthase elongation and modification enzymes. These findings suggest that stromal cluster 5, representing a DCN-secreting subset of tumor stromal cells in the tumor microenvironment, might play a unique role in HCC.

## The 6-gene DCN-associated signature predicts patient outcomes in HCC
Given the correlation between higher CS-DCN levels and poor survival, and the higher gene expression of DCN, CSPGs, and CS synthases in the tumor stromal cluster 5, we proposed a 6-gene DCN-associated signature to predict patient survival in HCC (Fig.  A). For the CSPG components (DCN, SRPX, and BGN), genes were selected from the intersection of three datasets: known CSPG genes, markers of stromal cluster 5, and genes significantly correlated with DCN expression (p < 0.05) from GEPIA data. The CS synthase genes (CHSY3, CHST3, and CHPF) were chosen based on the GSEA for tumor stromal cells with the top three enrichment scores in the CS synthase gene set. These six genes were predominantly expressed in the stromal subset of HCC tumors (Fig.  B). We assessed the prognostic value of this signature using TCGA-LIHC cohort survival data, and the scores were calculated using ssGSEA with the 6-gene gene set. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis showed that patients in the high-scoring group had significantly better overall survival compared to the low-scoring group (Fig.  C). This finding was further validated in an independent cohort of Asian HCC patients (ICGC-LIRI-JP dataset), confirming the prognostic power of the 6-gene signature (Fig.  C). Together, these data suggest a potential tumor-suppressive role of this stromal signature in HCC.
 To explore the functional role of CS-DCN, we produced and purified CS-chain-containing DCN from HEK293 cells (Fig.  D). Treatment of HA22T and PLC5 cells with CS-DCN at 2 µg/ml significantly inhibited both cell proliferation (Fig.  E) and invasion (Fig.  F). These findings were consistent with the prognostic significance of the 6-gene DCN-associated signature, where higher scores predict better survival outcomes in HCC patients.
To validate the broader prognostic relevance of this signature, we analyzed its performance across multiple cancer types using bulk RNA sequencing data. The 6-gene DCN-associated signature demonstrated significant prognostic utility in gastric cancer (GSE84337; Fig.  A), breast cancer (TCGA-BRCA; Fig.  B), renal papillary cell carcinoma (TCGA-KIRP; Fig.  C), bladder cancer (TCGA-BLCA; Fig.  D), and renal clear cell carcinoma (TCGA-KIRC; Fig.  E). Interestingly, in contrast to HCC, lower signature scores were correlated with improved survival outcomes in these cancers, suggesting a context-dependent role of CS-related stromal responses in different tumor microenvironments.
 

## Discussion
In this study, we investigated the distribution of DCN-expressing cells and proposed a unique tumor-suppressive function of the DCN-expressing stromal subset in HCC. DCN was significantly downregulated in tumor tissues compared to peri-tumor areas, particularly in its chondroitin sulfate-modified form (CS-DCN), which correlated with better overall survival in HCC patients. Low CS-DCN expression was linked to capsular invasion, suggesting a tumor-suppressive role. Besides, single-cell RNA sequencing analysis revealed that DCN expression was predominantly localized to stromal cells, with a higher proportion in tumor-associated stromal cells compared to normal liver stromal cells. Further clustering identified a tumor-specific subset of cancer-associated fibroblasts (cluster 5) characterized by high DCN, CSPGs, and CS synthase expression. Pathway analysis showed this subset was enriched in extracellular matrix remodeling, wound healing, and CSPG pathways. A 6-gene DCN-associated signature derived from cluster 5, including CSPG components (DCN, SRPX, BGN) and CS synthases (CHSY3, CHST3, CHPF), predicted better survival outcomes in HCC.
Although the tumor-suppressive role of DCN has been highlighted in previous studies [ – ], most analyses rely on bulk RNA sequencing comparisons between surgical resected peritumoral tissue and tumor core. However, peritumoral liver tissue is often affected by chronic inflammation, cirrhosis, and adipose accumulation, making it biologically distinct from healthy liver tissue. Recent evidence underscores the critical differences between these environments. For instance, single-cell RNA sequencing has identified specific NK cell subsets in healthy liver that are absent in both peritumoral and HCC tissues [ ]. Gene expression differences between biopsies obtained percutaneously versus surgically further emphasize the need for appropriate control tissue selection in HCC studies [ ]. Oxidative damage, as indicated by increased 8-oxo-dG levels, and alterations in DNA repair enzyme expression are more pronounced in peritumoral liver tissue than in healthy liver [ ]. Similarly, stem cell markers and protein quality control factors are differentially expressed in HCC and surrounding tissue compared to normal liver [ ]. Our analysis revealed that stromal cells expressing DCN are upregulated in the peritumor liver compared to the normal liver, potentially acting as a protective mechanism against disease progression. In contrast, DCN expression is markedly downregulated in cancerous tissue. These findings suggest a novel role for DCN in the dynamic progression of HCC and provide insights into its context-dependent tumor suppressive functions.
Our data revealed a negative correlation between CS-DCN expression in primary tumor tissue and capsular invasion, an aggressive feature of HCC. DCN plays a critical role in extracellular matrix (ECM) integrity, particularly in collagen fibrillogenesis and matrix organization. It binds specific collagen sequences, regulating fibril formation, spacing, and stability [ , ]. Importantly, the CS chain on DCN further enhances its ability to mediate collagen assembly, as its loss has been associated with impaired fibril formation and reduced collagen organization [ , ]. These findings suggest that stromal cells expressing high levels of CS-DCN in both tumor and peritumoral tissues contribute to the formation of a robust collagen-rich ECM barrier. This barrier likely functions as a defensive mechanism, limiting capsular invasion and potentially suppressing disease progression. The increased presence of CS-DCN-expressing stromal cells in peritumoral areas could represent an adaptive response of the liver to restrict tumor spread. These results highlight the potential of targeting stromal remodeling and DCN-associated ECM pathways as a therapeutic strategy for mitigating aggressive HCC phenotypes.
The additional five genes in the proposed DCN-associated signature exhibit diverse roles across HCC and other cancers. BGN (Biglycan), positively correlated with DCN expression in HCC, has been implicated in promoting hepatic fibrosis by regulating ECM deposition and activating hepatic stellate cells [ ]. SRPX (Sushi Repeat Containing Protein, X-Linked) remains largely unexplored in the context of HCC, though the Human Protein Atlas database suggests its high expression may be associated with better survival rates in HCC patients. Interestingly, studies in glioblastoma and ovarian cancer suggest SRPX may act as a protumor factor [ , ], indicating a possible tumor-type-dependent function. The CS synthases, CHPF (Chondroitin Polymerizing Factor) and CHSY3 (Chondroitin Sulfate Synthase 3) are responsible for elongating CS chains and show upregulation in HCC. CHPF has been identified as a tumor suppressor in HCC [ ], but promoting malignancy in breast cancer [ , ]. Meanwhile, CHSY3’s role in HCC remains unexplored, though it has been linked to poor prognosis in gastric cancer [ ] and stomach adenocarcinoma [ ]. These findings suggest that while these genes collectively contribute to the suppressive function of the signature in HCC, their roles in other cancers may vary, reflecting a context-dependent impact of CS-related pathways on tumor biology.
Survival analysis of HCC and other cancers using our signature (Figs.  C and ) revealed the distinct suppressive role in HCC, whereas its prognostic value appears less favorable or even opposite in other cancers such as gastric cancer (GC), breast cancer (BRCA), and kidney cancers (KIRC, KIRP), and bladder cancer (BLCA). This divergence may reflect differences in tumor microenvironmental context, particularly in the origin and function of cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) and the structural characteristics of the tumor. In HCC, CAFs are mainly derived from hepatic stellate cells and resident fibroblasts, which contribute to forming a fibrous capsule around the tumor. In this encapsulated environment, CSPGs, such as DCN, may help maintain mechanical stability and tissue integrity, potentially restraining tumor expansion and invasion [ ]. In contrast, many other tumor types such as GC, BRCA, KIRC, KIRP, and BLCA are non-encapsulated and are often associated with CAFs derived from endothelial or epithelial lineages [ , – ]. In these contexts, CSPGs may instead facilitate extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling and promote tumor progression and metastasis. Thus, the roles of CS synthases and CSPGs are highly context-dependent, potentially acting as tumor suppressors in encapsulated environments like HCC, but promoting malignancy in more invasive tumor settings.

## Limitations of scRNA-seq and bioinformatic
Our utilization of scRNA-seq analysis in this study granted the resolution to compare and evaluate gene expressions as well as perform clustering at the single-cell level. However, several limitations should be addressed. First, although the batch effect of the different samples has been corrected using Harmony, which performs well for integrating different batches and cell types [ ], variation from unknown sources may still exist [ ]. In addition, the continuous differentiation of cells regarding the tumor-expressing DCN stromal subset has yet to be explored with trajectory analysis, which requires raw file unavailable from the integrated dataset.

## Conclusion
This study identifies a distinct Decorin-expressing stromal subset in HCC, highlighting its tumor-suppressive role. CS-DCN was found to correlate with better patient outcomes and reduced capsular invasion, suggesting its role in strengthening the ECM barrier. Single-cell RNA sequencing revealed a unique stromal cluster with elevated DCN, CSPGs, and CS synthases, contributing to ECM remodeling and cancer suppression. The proposed six-gene DCN-associated signature predicts better prognosis in HCC, but exhibits diverse roles across other cancers. These findings underscore the potential of targeting CSPG pathways, particularly CS-DCN, as a novel therapeutic strategy for improving HCC management.

## Materials and methods

## Human HCC tissue samples
Post-surgery tumor and peri-tumor tissue samples and clinical data of 60 HCC patients were collected from Chung Shan Medical University Hospital in Taichung, Taiwan, for Western blot and overall survival analysis. This study adheres to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki and conforms to all relevant ethical guidelines for biomedical research. Additionally, all data analyses involving patient samples were in accordance with the institutional regulations and relevant ethical norms, as approved by the Ethical Committees of Chung Shan Medical University Hospital, and all participants provided informed consent prior to tissue collection (approval number: CS218075).
This study involved the analysis of publicly available datasets, and no new human participants were involved. Therefore, no additional ethical approvals were required.

## Western blot
Total protein lysates (20–40 µg) of HCC tumor and peri-tumor tissue were separated by 8% SDS-PAGE and transferred to PVDF membrane, then blotted with antibodies against human DCN (R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN, Catalog #: AF143) and Actin (Santa Cruz, sc-47778). Total protein was measured with stain-free technology (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA). The intensity of signals on Western blotting was quantified by ImageJ software (version: 1.53 g, U. S. National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD) (Wayne Rasband).

## Single-cell RNA data processing
Public single-cell RNA-seq data of normal and tumor liver specimens (SCP2154) were downloaded from the Broad Institute Single-Cell Portal (https://singlecell.broadinstitute.org). For visualization, UMAP coordinates and the annotated cell types were extracted from the original dataset. Quality control, dimension reduction, and cell clustering followed the recommendations of the Seurat version 5 package [ ]. SCTransform [ ] was used as the normalization method with default parameters. Batch effect correction was achieved with the RunHarmony function from the harmony package (v1.0) [ ]. To investigate the properties of the stromal cells, we extracted the stomal cell subset and applied the FindNeighbors function using dimensions 1 to 15, followed by the FindClusters function with a resolution of 0.6.

## Single-sample gene set enrichment analysis
SsGSEA was used to determine the enrichment score of targeted gene sets for each sample [ ]. The scores represent the degree of a particular gene set coordinately up- or down-regulated within a sample [ ]. The enrichIt function from the ‘escape’ R package was used to perform ssGSEA on scRNA-seq data, which uses the ‘gsva’ R package and applies the Poisson distribution for enrichment calculations. The CS synthase gene set was derived from KEGG_GLYCOSAMINOGLYCAN_BIOSYNTHESIS_CHONDROITIN_SULFATE gene set of KEGG Legacy Pathways from Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB) [ , ]. The CSPG gene set included PG genes with predominant glycosaminoglycans on their core proteins [ ]. The enrichment scores of the CSPG and CS synthase gene sets for each cell type in scRNA-seq data and the scores of the 6-gene DCN-associated signature for each patient in bulk RNA-seq datasets were calculated using ssGSEA.

## Gene set enrichment analysis
GSEA [ , ] was conducted to investigate the significance of gene expression patterns within the stromal subset. Raw counts of the stromal cells were extracted and combined into pseudo-bulk RNA-seq data based on biosample and stromal cluster class. Then, the raw counts were normalized to transcripts per million (TPM). We performed GSEA using 830 gene sets from the WikiPathways [ ] downloaded from the MSigDB and two custom gene sets, CSPG and CS synthase. GSEA was performed with 1,000 permutations and default parameters to assess the enrichment of the gene sets. GSEA results of each of the six stromal cluster were filtered by significantly upregulated pathways in cluster 0 and 5, the two main tumor clusters.

## Cell-to-cell communication analysis
Cell-to-cell communication analyses were conducted using CellChat package (version 2.1.0 in R [ , ]), on the single-cell RNA dataset, SCP2154, focusing on interactions involving DCN-overexpressing stromal cells and other tumor microenvironmental cells types. Stromal cells expressing DCN higher than the median among DCN-positive cells were classified as the “stromal cell-DCN high” group, while those with expression lower than the median were classified as the “stromal cell-DCN low” group. Interactions between the stromal cell-DCN high group and other cell types were performed according to the CellChat vignette guidelines.

## Survival analysis
For the survival analysis of our 60 HCC samples, patients are split into high and low groups by CS-DCN or core-DCN expression. Bulk RNA-seq data and clinical survival data from five TCGA (LIHC, BRCA, KIRP, BLCA, and KIRC) datasets [ ], GSE84427 (gastric cancer) [ ], and ICGC-LIRI-JP dataset (HCC, n = 203), were used for survival analysis of the 6-gene DCN-associated stromal signature. The raw count matrices were retrieved and normalized to TPM. The signature scores calculated by ssGSEA allowed the division of patients into two high and low scoring groups. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis was conducted for the two groups. Log-rank test was used to compare the survival distributions of the two groups.

## Cell culture
HA22T cells, PLC5 cells, and HEK293 cells were purchased from Bioresource Collection and Research Center (Hsinchu, Taiwan), and cultured in DMEM containing 10% FBS in 5% CO2 at 37 °C. For transfection experiments that required condition medium, HEK293 cells were cultured in serum-free medium in 5% CO2 at 37 °C. Condition medium was collected after 24 h of incubation.

## Recombined DCN protein purification
HEK293 cells were transfected with c-terminal His-tagged DCN plasmid using TransIT-X2 Transfection Reagent (Mirus Bio, Madison, WI). The His-tagged DCN in concentrated conditional medium were purified via Profinity IMAC chromatography system (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA). The purity and concentration of the obtained protein were analyzed by SDS-PAGE and total protein was quantified with BSA as control.

## Cell viability assay
Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8; Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO) was used to calculate the cell viability. In brief, 2 × 103 HA22T cells or PLC5 cells were seeded into 96-well plates and allowed to attach for 6 h. Cultured medium was replaced without or with 0.4 ug/ml and 2ug/ml of HEK293 secreted DCN. CCK8 reagent was added for 2 h, and the absorbance of O.D. 450 nm was measured. Experiments were independently repeated three times, and the data are presented as the increase in absorbance at 450 nm after subtracting the background from the medium control.

## Cell invasion assay
Matrigel (BD Biosciences) coated Transwell inserts for 24-well plate were used to measure cell invasion. Matrigel was coated on 80 μm filter with or without recombinant DCN (2ug/ml) for 24 h. HA22T cells or PLC5 cells in 0.1 ml serum-free DMEM were seeded in inserts, and 0.6 ml DMEM containing 10% FBS and EGF (20 ng/ml) was added in the lower wells and incubated for 24 h. Cells that moved to the other side of the filters were stained by crystal violet and counted with ImageJ software. Values were shown as the number of cells per microscopic field over five fields of each filter. Three independent experiments were performed.
