## Summary
We introduce a protocol for generating 3D organoids from circulating tumor cells (CTCs), enabling longitudinal functional and molecular analyses in pancreatic cancer patients, including those with unresectable disease, which constitutes the majority of cases. We outline the process for isolating and characterizing CTCs from the blood of pancreatic cancer patients and provide detailed instructions for initiating, passaging, and phenotyping CTC-derived organoids. Additionally, we describe techniques for utilizing these organoids in drug screening with a focus on stemness-related pathways.
For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Tang et al.

## Before you begin
Circulating tumor cells (CTCs) are cancer cells that are released from primary tumors as well as secondary lesions into the bloodstream. They reflect the heterogeneity of the originating tumor and carry crucial information about metastasis and immune invasion. CTC-derived 3D organoid models provide a powerful preclinical tool for academic research and personalized medicine. However, developing CTC-derived models specific to pancreatic cancer has been challenging due to the low CTC counts and their limited viability. In this protocol, we utilize an innovative VAR2-based platform to efficiently capture CTCs from pancreatic cancer patients. The Plasmodium falciparum-derived VAR2 protein binds to oncofetal chondroitin sulfate (ofCS) glycosaminoglycans that are strongly expressed on many carcinoma cells including pancreatic cancer. , This markedly improves the CTC capture rate and enables the creation of long-term in vitro 3D organoid cultures. These cultures can be further expanded and used in drug screening studies as well as in vivo validation studies.

## Preparation of macrophage-conditioned medium (MCM)

Timing: 7 days

1.Peripheral blood mononuclear cell (PBMC) isolation from whole blood.a.Preheat Dulbecco’s Phosphate-Buffered Saline (DPBS) to room temperature (RT).
b.Transfer 40 mL of freshly drawn whole blood from a healthy donor into a 50 mL conical tube and top up to 50 mL with sterile DPBS.Note: The blood should be processed as soon as possible, ideally within 2 h of collection.

c.Pipette 20 mL of Ficoll Paque Premium into each of two 50 mL conical tubes.
d.Carefully transfer 25 mL of blood on top of the Ficoll layer using a serological pipette.CRITICAL: Perform this step slowly; the cleaner the interface, the better the PBMC isolation will be.
Note: Tilting the conical tube at a 45° angle can assist with this step. If used, gently return the tube to an upright position afterward.

e.Centrifuge at RT at 1,200 × g for 25 min with the brake off.
f.Carefully collect the middle layer (interface, 1–2 mL) from each tube, which contains the PBMCs, and transfer it into a fresh 50 mL conical tube.
g.Add DPBS up to 50 mL and centrifuge at 400 × g for 5 min with the brake on.
h.Aspirate the supernatant, add 10 mL of DPBS to each tube, and centrifuge again at 400 × g for 5 min.
i.Discard supernatant, the PBMCs are ready to use.

2.Generation of M2-like macrophages.a.Resuspend the PBMC pellet in 24 mL of macrophage culture medium.
b.Transfer 12 mL of the suspension into each of two T75 flasks.
c.Place the flasks in an incubator at 37°C and 5% CO2 to allow cells to attach.
d.On day 1, after adherence, remove non-adherent cells along with the medium and replenish with fresh macrophage medium containing M-CSF at 50 ng/mL.
e.On days 2 and 3, repeat step 2d.
f.On day 4, M2-like macrophages are ready ( ).

3.Collection of macrophage-conditioned medium (MCM).a.Remove the medium from M2-like macrophages and add 6 mL of basic organoid culture medium.
b.After 2 days, harvest the MCM, aliquot it, and store at −80°C for future use.

Note: Test the MCM on established cultures prior to use, ensuring it induces mesenchymal or fibroblast-like morphological changes in epithelial cultures. Apply the MCM during the first 7 days of CTC organoid initiation to enhance cell viability.

## Step-by-step method details

## CTC isolation by recombinant VAR2 (rVAR2)

Timing: 4.5 h

This section provides step-by-step guidelines for the isolation of CTCs from the blood of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) patients using rVAR2 and the IsoFlux platform ( ).1.rVAR2-SpyC complex preparation.a.Thaw rVAR2-SpyTag and SpyCatcher-biotin from −80°C on ice.
b.Mix rVAR2-SpyTag and SpyCatcher-biotin at a molar ratio of 0.8:1.
c.Incubate at RT for 1 h, to produce the VAR2–SpyC complex, and store on ice until use.

2.Beads preparation.a.Thoroughly mix beads by vortexing. Transfer Sera-Mag SpeedBeads Streptavidin-blocked magnetic particles (for CTC characterization) or anti-biotin Dynabeads (for CTC organoid culture) to an Eppendorf LoBind 1.5 mL tube, using either 2.5 μL Sera-Mag beads or 4.5 μL Dynabeads per 5 mL blood.
b.Wash beads by adding 1 mL of Pierce protein-free (PBS) blocking buffer (PF buffer).
c.Place the tube on a neodymium magnet (10 × 12 mm) for pelleting the beads, then remove the buffer.
d.Repeat step b and c two more times. Resuspend beads in 30 μL of PF buffer per 5 mL blood sample and store on ice until use.

CRITICAL: Always use freshly prepared rVAR2-SpyC complex and beads to minimize contamination by WBCs.
Note: Beads attached to CTCs reduce cell viability and must be removed before culture. Anti-biotin Dynabeads can be removed with DNase but have high fluorescent background, affecting CTC counts. Sera-Mag speedbeads show low fluorescence but cannot be removed before culture. Use beads as needed for specific applications.
3.Red blood cell lysis.a.Receive fresh blood in EDTA tubes and divide it into aliquots of 5 mL in 50 mL conical tubes.CRITICAL: At least 40 mL blood should be used to establish long-term CTC cultures, ideally from advanced PDAC patients.
CRITICAL: The blood should be processed as soon as possible after collection to preserve the highest cell viability, which is key for culturing CTCs.

b.Dilute the 10X stock RBC lysis buffer with dd H2O to make a 1X solution.i.Add 45 mL of RBC lysis buffer to each aliquot of blood.
ii.Invert the tube three times.
iii.Incubate for 10 min at RT.

c.Centrifuge at 4°C, 400 × g for 8 min, and wash the pellet with 10 mL of DPBS twice.
d.Resuspend the cell pellet in 500 μL CTC isolation buffer 1 and transfer it to a 1.5 mL protein LoBind tube.Note: Use protein LoBind tips and tubes from this step through all subsequent steps involving CTCs. The tubes should be precoated with isolation buffer 1 before use.

4.Incubation with rVAR2-SpyC complex and beads.a.Add the VAR2–SpyC complex to cell suspension at a concentration of 130 nM.
b.Rotate at 4°C with slow rotation (360° at 10 rpm) for 30 min.
c.Add 1 mL of CTC isolation buffer 1 and centrifuge at 4°C, 400 × g for 8 min.
d.Resuspend cell pellet in 500 μL of CTC isolation buffer 1 and add 30 μL of prepared beads.
e.Rotate at 4°C with slow rotation (360° at 10 rpm) for 30 min.

5.Capture CTCs using the IsoFlux device.a.Switch on the IsoFlux machine 30 min before use.
b.Press load protocol and select number of samples.Note: Each sample corresponds to 5 mL of blood, and each run can hold a maximum of four samples.

c.Add 3 mL of binding buffer to sample well of an Isoflux cartridge.
d.Load the cartridge into the machine and prime it before loading the samples.Note: It takes 5–8 min to prime the machine. Load samples and start the run as soon as possible to avoid drying out the cartridge.

e.Load the samples into wash well of the cartridges and start the run.CRITICAL: Avoid any bubbles while loading samples, and ensure that the total volume loaded in well 1 does not exceed 1.2 mL.
Note: It takes 40–50 min to complete the run.

f.Add 100 μL of CTC isolation buffer 2 to the recovery tube of the cartridge and transfer the cells to a 1.5 mL protein LoBind tube.
g.Add another 100 μL of CTC isolation buffer 2 to wash the recovery tube and transfer the buffer to the same tube used in step f.Note: The tubes should be precoated with isolation buffer 2 before use.

h.These cells are ready for characterization and culture.Note: As VAR2 can bind ofCS in various carcinoma types, this protocol may apply broadly, though retrieval and culture conditions may need optimization for each cancer type.

 

## Characterization and enumeration of CTCs

Timing: 2 h

This section provides step-by-step guidelines for characterizing CTCs by staining with specific cancer and WBC markers.6.Fixation of samples.a.Prepare glass slides by drawing a circle with a diameter of 20 mm using water-repellent Dako pen.
b.Place the tube on a neodymium magnet to pellet the cells bound to the beads, and discard the buffer.Note: A portion of isolated CTC sample corresponding to 2–5 mL of blood is sufficient for characterization and enumeration.

c.Add 200 μL of 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) to the pellet and mix well by pipetting up and down.
d.Incubate at RT for 10 min while gently tilting.
e.Place the tube on the neodymium magnet, and after the beads have fully pelleted, discard the PFA.
f.Resuspend the pellet in 100 μL of binding buffer using a 1,000 μL tip, and transfer the suspension to the prepared glass slide.Note: Ensure no beads are left in the tube. If necessary, add a small amount of additional binding buffer to transfer all the beads from the tube to the slide.

g.Place the glass slide on a neodymium magnet for 1 min to allow beads-bound cells to settle. Then, carefully remove the liquid by gently pouring it onto a paper tissue.

7.Blocking of samples.a.Block the samples by adding 100 μL of binding buffer containing 10% Flebogamma, 10% normal donkey serum (NDS), and 10% Fc receptor block. Incubate for 20 min at RT.
b.Place the slides on a magnet for 1 min and discard the liquid. Wash the cells three times with 100 μL of DPBS.

8.Staining samples with CD45 antibody.a.Add 100 μL of binding buffer containing 1% Alexa Fluor 561-CD45 antibody, 1% Flebogamma, 1% NDS, and 1% Fc receptor block. Incubate the slide on a magnet for 1 min, then discard the liquid. Wash the cells with 100 μL of DPBS three times.
b.Place the slides on a magnet and discard the liquid. Wash the cells with 100 μL of DPBS three times.

9.Permeabilization of cells.a.Permeabilize the cells by adding 100 μL binding buffer containing 0.2% Triton X-100. Incubate for 10 min at RT.
b.Place the slides on a magnet and discard the liquid. Wash the cells three times with 100 μL of DPBS.

10.Staining samples with the intracellular cancer marker anti-Cytokeratin antibody.a.Add 100 μL of binding buffer containing 1% FITC-Cytokeratin antibody, 1% Flebogamma, 1% NDS, and 1% Fc receptor block. Incubate for 30 min at RT.
b.Place the slides on a magnet and discard the liquid. Wash the cells three times with 100 μL DPBS.
c.Seal the samples using one drop of Mounting Medium with 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI).

11.Enumeration.a.Image samples using a high-content analysis system, e.g., the Operetta CLS (PerkinElmer).
b.Enumerate CTCs defined as Cytokeratin+CD45–DAPI+ cells. Contaminating WBCs are Cytokeratin–CD45+DAPI+ cells ( ).

## CTC organoid culture

Timing: 2–4 months

This section provides a step-by-step protocol for initiating, passaging, and further culturing CTC organoids as adherent and sphere cultures, adapted and modified from a published method. 12.Initiation of CTC organoid culture ( ).a.Add 50 μL growth factor-reduced Matrigel in an adherent 96-well plate to form a concave matrix surface (bowl-shaped).Note: Place Matrigel on ice to thaw for 1 h before starting.
CRITICAL: It is important to form a bowl-shape matrix surface to prevent cells from attaching to the adherent surface of the well.

b.Place the 96-well plate into a 37°C incubator for 60 min to allow the Matrigel to solidify.
c.Add 12 μL of reconstituted Release Buffer (DNase I) to each 200 μL suspension of CTC beads.
d.Incubate for 20 min at RT with gentle tilting and rotation.Note: To maximize cell release after incubation, thoroughly pipet the samples 5 - 10 times using a 200 μL LoBind tip.

e.Pre-coat a protein LoBind Eppendorf tube for 5 min with CTC isolation buffer 2.
f.Place the sample on the neodymium magnet for 1 min, then collect the supernatant with released cells into the tube from step e.
g.Resuspend the bead fraction in 200 μL CTC isolation buffer 2 and repeat step f.
h.Centrifuge the tube containing the supernatant fraction at 300 × g for 5 min at 4°C.
i.Discard supernatant and resuspend cells in 10 μL of DPBS.
j.Transfer cells onto the concave Matrigel surface of the 96-well plate from step a and b, then add another 50 μL of GF-reduced Matrigel. It is recommended to use one well / 40 mL blood.
k.Incubate in a 37°C incubator for 60 min to solidify the Matrigel.
l.Add 100 μL of pre-warmed CTC organoid culture medium mixed 1:1 with macrophage-conditioned medium (MCM), supplemented with 10 μM Y-27632 for the first three days.
m.Culture cells at 37°C and 5% CO2.
n.Replenish the medium with the same ingredients, except Y-27632, every three days until the cells cover the entire well.Note: MCM is only used during the first 7 days of culture.
Note: The initiation time of CTC organoids varies among different patients and is highly dependent on CTC counts, contained cancer stem cells as well as the patient’s stage and overall status.

13.Passage CTC organoids.a.Carefully remove the medium from wells containing CTC organoids, ensuring not to disturb or damage the Matrigel layer at the bottom.
b.Add 200 μL of ice-cold cell recovery solution to each well of the 96-well plate, gently transferring the Matrigel into a 1.5 mL Eppendorf tube. Wash the well with an additional 200 μL of ice-cold cell recovery solution and transfer it to the same tube.
c.Incubate on ice for 1 h. Centrifuge at 300 × g for 5 min at 4°C.
d.Aspirate the supernatant and add 1 mL TrypLE Express (1X) without phenol red for 15 min at 37°C to further dissociate cells.
e.Centrifuge at 300 × g for 5 min at 37°C.
f.Aspirate the supernatant and resuspend the pellet with 200 μL Matrigel.
g.Add 100 μL of Matrigel containing cells into each well of the 96-well plate.
h.Incubate in a 37°C incubator for 60 min to solidify the Matrigel.
i.Add 100 μL organoid medium.
j.Culture the CTCs at 37°C and 5% CO2.
k.Replenish the medium with the same ingredients, excluding Y-27632, every three days until the cells cover almost the entire well.

Note: CTC organoids can be further expanded into 12-well or 6-well plates. For long-term storage, they should be frozen in liquid nitrogen using fetal bovine serum (FBS) with 10% dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO).
Note: During dissociation, “clouds” of DNA may appear in the solution, which originate from cells that have lysed during the process. To release any trapped cells, pipette up and down to break apart these DNA aggregates.
Note: It is not recommended to maintain organoids in culture for more than one month.
14.Characterization of CTC organoids.a.Image CTC organoids in 96-well plates using bright field microscopy, e.g., Operetta CLS High-Content Analysis System ( A and 5B).
b.Carefully remove the medium from the 96-well plate containing CTC organoids without disturbing the Matrigel or the organoids themselves.
c.Wash organoids once with 100 μL of DPBS.
d.Add 200 μL of 4% PFA and incubate for 45 min at 37°C.
e.Wash the organoids three times with 100 μL of DPBS.
f.Block the sample with 100 μL 5% BSA. Incubate for 1 h at 37°C.
g.Aspirate the blocking buffer and add 100 μL of DPBS containing 1% Alexa Fluor 561-CD45 antibody and 5% BSA. Incubate for 1 h at 37°C.
h.Wash the organoids three times with 100 μL of DPBS.
i.Permeabilize the organoids by adding 100 μL of DPBS containing 0.2% Triton X-100. Incubate for 10 min at RT.
j.Wash the organoids three times with 100 μL of DPBS.
k.Block the organoids again with 100 μL of 5% BSA. Incubate for 1 h at 37°C.
l.Aspirate blocking buffer and add 100 μL of DPBS containing 1% FITC-Cytokeratin antibody and 5% BSA. Incubate for 1 h at 37°C.
m.Wash the organoids three times with 100 μL of DPBS.
n.Add Mounting Medium with DAPI.
o.Take pictures using a high-content analysis system, e.g., Operetta CLS ( C).

CRITICAL: Ensure that all steps are performed at RT or 37°C to keep the Matrigel solidified. If the temperature drops, the Matrigel can liquefy, potentially washing away all organoids during the process.
15.Expansion of CTC organoids as adherent cultures.a.Repeat the steps 13a-e.
b.Aspirate the supernatant and resuspend the cell pellet with 1 mL complete culture medium.
c.Seed the cells in a 12-well adherent culture plate at a density of 1 × 105 cells per well in 1 mL complete culture medium.
d.Culture cells in an incubator at 37°C and 5% CO2.
e.Replenish with the complete medium every three days.
f.Once cells reach approximately 70% confluency, take bright-field images using a high-content analysis system, e.g., Operetta CLS ( A).
g.Detach and further expand cells in standard adherent culture conditions.

Note: Once the cell number reaches 107, samples can be collected and stored in liquid nitrogen in FBS with 10% DMSO for later use.
Note: It is not recommended to maintain the cells in adherent culture for more than one month.
16.Expansion of CTC organoids as 3D sphere culture.a.Repeat the steps 13a-e.
b.Aspirate the supernatant and resuspend the cell pellet with 1 mL sphere culture medium.
c.Seed the cells in a 24-well sphere culture plate, 1,000 cells/well in 1 mL sphere culture medium.
d.Culture the cells in an incubator at 37°C and 5% CO2 for 7 days.
e.On day 7, take pictures in bright field using a high-content analysis system, e.g., Operetta CLS ( B).

Note: It is not recommended to maintain cells in 3D sphere culture for more than 2 weeks. For long-term use, it is preferable to store cells from different culture conditions in liquid nitrogen.
 

## Screening for stemness-targeting compounds

Timing: 4 days

This section provides a step-by-step protocol for using cells derived from CTC organoids to screen a library of stemness-targeting compounds.17.Remove the medium from the T75 flask after the cells reach 80–90% confluence.
18.Wash the cells once with 10 mL of DPBS.
19.Add 3 mL of TrypLE Express (1X) to detach the cells.
20.Add 7 mL of complete culture medium and collect the cells into a 50 mL conical tube.
21.Centrifuge at 300 × g for 5 min at 37°C.
22.Resuspend the cells in 5 mL sphere culture medium and measure the cell concentration.
23.Seed the cells in sphere culture medium with 4% MethoCult H4100 at a density of 1,000 cells per 50 μL per well into 384-well ultralow-adhesion microplates.
24.Load the stemness-targeting compound library at 0.1 μL per well.
25.Culture the cells in an incubator at 37°C and 5% CO2 for 4 days.
26.Add 10 μL of CellTiter-Glo and measure the chemiluminescence using the BioTek Synergy Neo2 multimode reader (Agilent).
27.Compounds that show a 50% reduction in cell viability compared to the vehicle are considered promising.

## Expected outcomes
This protocol describes the isolation of CTCs from the blood of PDAC patients, the generation of CTC organoid cultures, and their application in drug screening. Using the rVAR2 isolation platform, a median of 65 CTCs (Cytokeratin+CD45–DAPI+) can be retrieved from 5 mL of blood, with fewer than 2,000 remaining WBCs (Cytokeratin–CD45+DAPI+) ( ), as detailed in our original publication. The success rate for forming CTC organoid cultures varies, with the most important factors being the quantity of CTCs and the level of WBC contamination. The higher the number of CTCs retrieved and the lower the WBC contamination, the greater the success rate for establishing CTC cultures will be. Once the total CTC count reaches 600, there is a high chance of establishing long-term cultures. The growth rate of CTC organoid cultures also varies, which may partly be attributed to individual genetic differences. The initiation step typically takes one to two months. Successfully initiated organoids exhibit a typical three-dimensional structure composed of multiple cell layers ( ). Immunofluorescence analysis shows that the cells are cytokeratin positive and CD45 negative, indicating that they are cancer cells originating from epithelial cells ( ). These cells can be further passaged and expanded into adherent and sphere cultures ( ) and stored in liquid nitrogen for long-term use. The expanded CTCs can then be utilized for screening stemness-targeting compounds, exemplifying one of their applications. Compounds that demonstrated a 50% reduction in cell viability are considered promising, including the voltage-gated potassium (KCNH2) and voltage-gated calcium channel (CACNA2D2) inhibitor amiodarone hydrochloride, AMP-activated protein kinase inhibitor dorsomorphin dihydrochloride, MEK inhibitor PD-0325901, histone methyl transferase inhibitor BIX-01294, and stearoyl-coenzyme A desaturase 1 (SCD1) inhibitor A939572. The SCD1 inhibitor is further validated in in vitro and in vivo studies. 

## Limitations
The success rate of generating CTC-derived organoid models is highly dependent on the number of retrieved CTCs and their viability. The lengthy processing time critically affects their viability. For some patients, it may not be possible to generate 3D organoids if the CTC count is low. The use of the IsoFlux machine for CTC isolation limits the number of samples to four (20 mL blood) in one run, which further increases processing time and reduces cell viability. Additionally, the growth rate of CTC organoid cultures varies, partially due to individual genetic differences and sample variations.

## Troubleshooting

## Problem 1
MCM is less effective or not effective at all (related to the “ ” section).

## Potential solution
The MCM is freshly prepared from the blood of healthy donors, and there is considerable batch-to-batch variation. MCM serves two major purposes in the initiation of CTC organoid culture: mimicking clinical heterogeneity and simulating metastatic conditions. After MCM treatment, cancer cells exhibit a myofibroblast-like morphology with upregulation of EMT-associated genes (SLUG, VIM, and ZEB1). It is highly recommended to test each batch of MCM on known cells by assessing morphology and measuring the expression of EMT-associated genes before using it for CTC culture.

## Problem 2
High levels of remaining WBCs in retrieved CTCs are a concern (related to the “ ” section).

## Potential solution
If still a high number of WBCs remains among retrieved CTCs, it may be due to two reasons: 1) the WBCs in the original blood sample are significantly higher than usual; or 2) the specificity of rVAR2 is diminished. In the first case, it is recommended to pre-wash the bead-cell suspension once or twice using a neodymium magnet before running it through the IsoFlux machine. In the second case, it is advised to aliquot and store rVAR2 proteins at −80°C and use them within one year after production, as older rVAR2 can exhibit nonspecific binding to WBCs. Ideally, test the specificity of rVAR2 every month in blood from a healthy donor using FACS to ensure lowest binding to WBCs.

## Problem 3
Organoids do not form after the first passage (related to step 13 of the “ ” section).

## Potential solution
Passage the organoids in a timely manner, as it is difficult to recover them once they become starved. It is advisable to dissociate the organoids into small pieces containing approximately 10 cells instead of single cells. Overdigestion can severely affect organoid viability. Adding a ROCK inhibitor during passaging can help avoid anoikis. Initially, it is beneficial to seed the cells at a relatively high density (1:2 split). During later passages, cells can be seeded more sparsely. Change the culture medium regularly, every three days.

## Problem 4
Organoids are lost during the process of changing the medium (related to the “ ” section).

## Potential solution
Before CTC organoids are ready to be passaged, the medium will need to be changed multiple times. The Matrigel may become thinner, and some organoids might disintegrate in the medium and get lost during medium removal. To minimize this issue, always warm the buffer and medium to 37°C before use. If the culture period exceeds one month, add an additional layer of Matrigel on top.
