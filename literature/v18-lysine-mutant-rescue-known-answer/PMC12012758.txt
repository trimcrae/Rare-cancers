## ABSTRACT
Histone methylation is an important epigenetic modification process coordinated by histone methyltransferases, histone demethylases and histone methylation reader proteins and plays a key role in the occurrence and development of cancer. This study constructed a risk scoring model around histone methylation modification regulators and conducted a multidimensional comprehensive analysis to reveal its potential role in breast cancer prognosis and drug sensitivity. First, 144 histone methylation modification regulators (HMMRs) were subjected to differential analysis and univariate Cox regression analysis, and nine differentially expressed HMMRs associated with survival were screened out. Next, a risk scoring model consisting of eight HMMRs was constructed using the LASSO regression algorithm, exhibiting independent predictive values in training and validation cohorts. Then, immune analysis shows that patients in the high‐risk group divided by the risk scoring model has weakened the immune response. In addition, through functional analysis of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between high‐risk and low‐risk groups, we confirmed that the DEGs mainly affected the nucleoplasm and tumour microenvironment. Finally, drug sensitivity analysis demonstrated that our model could be useful for drug screening and identify potential drugs for treating BRCA patients. In conclusion, these eight HMMRs may be key factors in the prognosis and drug sensitivity of BRCA patients.

This study developed a risk scoring model based on histone methylation modification regulators (HMMRs) to assess their role in breast cancer (BRCA) prognosis and drug sensitivity. The model, constructed using LASSO regression, identified eight key HMMRs associated with survival, which showed independent predictive values. Immune and functional analyses revealed altered immune responses and tumour microenvironment impacts in high‐risk patients, whereas drug sensitivity analysis highlighted potential therapeutic options for BRCA treatment.

## Construction and Validation of Risk Scoring Model
To further evaluate the prognostic significance of the nine HMMRs, we used LASSO regression to screen the features of the nine HMMRs and constructed a risk scoring model based on eight HMMRs according to the minimum λ value (Figure  , λmin=0.0031) and the coefficient of each gene (Figure  ) as follows:

RS=0.208×ExpMECOM−0.487×ExpCHD2−0.234×ExpKDM4B+0.292×ExpWDR5+0.008×ExpCBX2+0.447×ExpNCAPD3+0.206×ExpPHF20L1+0.557×ExpPHF20

 Based on this model, we calculated the risk score of each BRCA sample, which ranged from 0.587 to 3.550. Then, all BRCA samples were divided into high‐risk and low‐risk groups according to the median risk score (1.775). The results show that the survival time of patients in the high‐risk group is generally lower than that of patients in the low‐risk group (Figure  ). In addition, we analysed the differences between the two risk groups by PCA. The results show that there is a clear distinction between the two risk groups, which indicates that the risk groups divided by our model are reasonable (Figure  ). Further, we used Kaplan–Meier survival analysis to verify the predictive performance of the model. The results show that the survival rate and overall survival time of patients in the high‐risk group are lower than those in the low‐risk group (Figure  ). Finally, we used the risk scoring model to predict the survival of patients. The results show that the AUC values of the model for 1‐year, 3‐years and 5‐years are 0.672, 0.662 and 0.683, respectively (Figure  ). These results indicate that the risk scoring model we constructed has certain validity and reliability in predicting patient survival.
To further validate the performance of our risk scoring model in predicting prognosis, we used the GSE20685 samples as external validation cohorts. The above risk scoring model was used to predict patient survival. The results show that the AUC values for 1‐year, 3‐years and 5‐years are 0.811, 0.690 and 0.652, respectively (Figure  ). It can be concluded that our risk scoring model also has good predictive performance on the validation cohorts.

3.3

## Independent Prognostic Value of Risk Scoring Model
To explore the independent predictive value of the risk scoring model for the prognosis of BRCA patients, we performed univariate Cox and multivariate Cox regression analysis in combination with the clinical characteristics of the patients. The results show that patient age, tumour stage and risk score are adverse factors associated with patient prognosis, and these factors are significantly independent in the prognostic evaluation of BRCA patients (P<0.01) (Figure  ). The expression analysis of the eight HMMRs in the model shows that there are significantly different in the expression values of the eight HMMRs, tumour stage (including T, M, and N stage) and the survival status of the patients between the high‐risk and the low‐risk groups (P<0.01) (Figure  ). In addition, we integrated the clinical information data, risk score and the results of multivariate Cox regression analysis of BRCA patients to construct a nomogram (Figure  ). The results show that compared with age and the tumour stage, the risk score has a greater impact on the prediction results, and the higher risk score is associated with worse survival rates. Finally, to verify the accuracy of the model prediction, we further drew a calibration curve, and the results show that the 5‐year survival rate predicted by the risk scoring model is highly consistent with the ideal 5‐year survival rate (Figure  ). These results not only verify the predictive accuracy of the model but also further confirm the application value of the model in clinical practice.
 
3.4

## Comparison of Immune Cells and Immune Activity Between High‐Risk and Low‐Risk Groups
In order to explore the differences in immune status between high‐risk and low‐risk groups, we used single‐sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) to evaluate the immune status of 28 immune cells and 13 immune‐related signalling pathways in different risk groups. The results show that the infiltration levels of some immune cells in the high‐risk group are significantly reduced (Figure  ), including activated B cell, natural killer cell, central memory CD4 T cell, effector memory CD8 T cell, macrophage, plasmacytoid dendritic cell etc. Among them, activated B cell, natural killer cell, T cells and other immune cells play a key role in antitumour immune responses, and the infiltration level of these immune cells in the high‐risk group is low, which means that the patient's immune response ability is weakened. The activity of the CCR (C‐C chemokine receptor) immune pathway, HLA (human leucocyte antigen) immune pathway, T_cell_co‐stimulation immune pathway and Type_II_IFN_reponse immune pathway are impaired in the high‐risk group of patients (Figure  ), further illustrating that the overall immune response ability of the high‐risk group is reduced. The above results show that exploring the differences in immune status between high‐risk and low‐risk groups will help understand changes in the patient's immune response ability and provide a basis for further research and treatment.
 
3.5

## GO and KEGG Analysis of Differentially Expressed Genes Between High‐Risk and Low‐Risk Groups
In order to understand the specific functional characteristics of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between high‐risk and low‐risk groups in BRCA, we calculated the differences in gene expression between different risk groups and obtained 4854 DEGs, including 2734 downregulated genes and 2120 upregulated genes. Then, we performed functional enrichment analysis on the upregulated and downregulated genes, respectively. The results of GO analysis show that the downregulated genes are mainly involved in organelle organisation, cell cycle and other biological processes, which means that cell proliferation, structural and functional remodelling may be inhibited, and they are mainly distributed in the nuclear lumen and nucleoplasm of the cells (Figure  ). The upregulated genes are mainly involved in the regulation of response to stimulus, the regulation of signalling and the regulation of cell communication, reflecting the adaptability of cells in responding to external stimuli and maintaining of physiological functions, and they are mainly distributed in the endomembrane system, nucleoplasm and vesicle (Figure  ). In addition, KEGG pathway analysis results show that the downregulated genes are mainly involved in the cell cycle, protein processing in endoplasmic reticulum and nucleocytoplasmic transport (Figure  ), whereas upregulated genes are mainly involved in the ribosome (Figure  ). The above results clearly present the specific functional characteristics of DEGs. Therefore, we can determine that most of the DEGs between the high‐risk and low‐risk groups divided by the risk scoring model are closely related to the nucleoplasm as well as the tumour microenvironment.
 
3.6

## Drug Sensitivity Analysis and Screening of Therapeutic Drugs
In order to explore the relationship between the high‐risk and low‐risk groups divided by the risk scoring model and the efficacy of commonly used BRCA drugs, we selected six chemotherapy drugs, namely paclitaxel, gemcitabine, 5‐fluorouracil, etoposide, temozolomide and cisplatin. Subsequently, we analysed the drug sensitivity of these drugs in the high‐risk and low‐risk groups (Figure  ). The results show that the IC50 values of patients in the high‐risk group for the above chemotherapy drugs are significantly lower than those of patients in the low‐risk group (P<0.05), which means that the high‐risk group patients are more sensitive to these drugs. This suggests that our risk scoring model is helpful in selecting appropriate therapeutic drugs for BRCA patients.
 In addition, we used the cMap online tool to screen candidate drugs for the treatment of BRCA. First, GO analysis found that the proportion of upregulated and downregulated genes distributed in the nucleoplasm was relatively high, and they play a vital role in maintaining normal cell functions. Therefore, we selected upregulated and downregulated genes distributed in the nucleoplasm to screen effective candidate drugs. Second, we identified the top 20 antagonist drugs based on the connectivity scores of cMap (ranging from −100 to 100) (Table  ). Some of these drugs have shown significant activity in the treatment of BRCA, such as ruxolitinib, valproic acid, PRIMA‐1MET, tyrphostin‐1 and genipin. Therefore, the candidate drugs screened according to our risk scoring model may provide new directions and drug options for the treatment of BRCA.

## Discussion
In this study, we explored the impact of HMMRs on BRCA. Initially, nine differentially expressed HMMRs associated with survival (S_DEHs) were obtained by differential expression analysis and univariate Cox regression analysis of 144 HMMRs. Subsequently, utilising the LASSO regression algorithm, we developed a robust risk scoring model incorporating eight significant HMMRs, including two protective factors (CHD2 and KDM4B) and six risk factors (MECOM, WDR5, CBX2, NCAPD3, PHF20L1 and PHF20). This model demonstrated good prognostic performance across both training and validation cohorts. Then, we found that the overall immune response ability of patients in the high‐risk group divided by the risk scoring model is reduced, and the DEGs between the high‐risk and low‐risk groups are mainly distributed in the nucleoplasm, playing an important role in multiple processes such as the cell cycle. In addition, patients in the high‐risk group divided by our risk scoring model are more sensitive to chemotherapy drugs. Finally, we also found some potential drugs that may be used to treat BRCA among which ruxolitinib, valproic acid, PRIMA‐1MET, tyrphostin‐1 and genipin may have good therapeutic effects on BRCA.
Among the eight HMMRs that make up the risk scoring model, MECOM belongs to HMTs, KDM4B belongs to HDMs and CHD2, WDR5, CBX2, NCAPD3, PHF20L1 and PHF20 belong to HMRPs. Recent studies have found that WDR5 is overexpressed in a variety of cancers and has been confirmed to be a key target for the development of anticancer therapies. The potential role of WDR5 is as an inhibitor and degrader, opening up new possibilities for the innovation and discovery of anticancer drugs [ , ]. In addition, some studies have also clearly pointed out that WDR5 is essential for the growth and metastasis of BRCA cells, and inhibiting or degrading the overexpression of WDR5 may become an effective strategy for the treatment of triple‐negative breast cancer (TNBC) [ ]. Our results also show that WDR5 is a risk factor and its overexpression implies a poor prognosis. Other studies have found that the abnormal expression of CBX2 in hepatocellular carcinoma can lead to the silencing of a large number of genes [ ]. In colorectal cancer, CBX2 plays an important role as a tumour suppressor. The loss of CBX2 can inhibit the MAPK signalling pathway, thereby inhibiting the growth and metastasis of colorectal cancer [ , ]. Related studies have found that high expression of CBX2 is significantly associated with poor prognosis of BRCA [ ], and reducing the expression of CBX2 can inhibit the growth and invasion of BRCA cells through the PI3K/AK signalling pathway [ ]. In this study, CBX2 is a risk factor, and its enrichment in tumours shows that it is associated with poor prognosis. NCAPD3 plays an oncogenic role in many cancers. In pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, overexpression of NCAPD3 is associated with shortened overall survival of patients. In colorectal cancer, NCAPD3 promotes tumour progression [ ]. Our study found that NCAPD3 is a risk factor associated with poor prognosis of BRCA. Studies have shown that high expression of PHF20L1 is associated with lower overall survival in ovarian cancer. In BRCA patients, high expression of PHF20L1 is also associated with poor prognosis [ ]. Our study shows that PHF20L1 is a risk factor in BRCA, indicating that it may be an oncogene. Previous studies have shown that PHF20 is highly expressed in glioblastoma and is closely associated with poor prognosis of glioblastoma patients [ ]. Overexpression of PHF20 is associated with poor prognosis and immune status of BRCA patients, whereas reducing the expression level of PHF20 can effectively inhibit the activity and progression of BRCA cells [ ]. In this study, PHF20 is upregulated in tumours and is associated with poor prognosis of BRCA. In addition, studies have shown that the increased expression of MECOM indicates a good prognosis in ovarian cancer [ ]. And the downregulation of MECOM expression is associated with poor overall survival in lung adenocarcinoma [ ]. KDM4B can inhibit the proliferation and metastasis of BRCA cells by inhibiting the expression of PHGDH [ ]. Research on CHD2 has mainly focused on neurodevelopmental diseases such as encephalopathy and epilepsy, but there is relatively little research in the field of cancer [ ]. These are similar to our results, but the research on MECOM, KDM4B and CHD2 in BRCA is relatively limited. In our study, MECOM was confirmed to be a risk factor, which may play a role in promoting the occurrence and development of BRCA. KDM4B and CHD2 are protective factors and have a good prognosis for BRCA.
Our study not only provides a reference value for the prognosis assessment of BRCA patients but also screens some potential candidate drugs, such as ruxolitinib, valproic acid, PRIMA‐1MET, tyrphostin‐1 and genipin. Previous studies have shown that ruxolitinib inhibits the proliferation of tumour cells by inhibiting the JAK‐STAT signalling pathway and exhibits antitumour activity in various cancers [ , ]. Valproic acid can regulate histone deacetylase activity, thereby affecting the growth and survival of tumour (including BRCA) cells [ , ]. PRIMA‐1MET is a compound that can restore the function of mutant p53 protein. It promotes the apoptosis of tumour cells by restoring the function of p53, thereby inhibiting tumour growth [ , ]. Tyrphostin‐1 is a tyrosine kinase inhibitor that affects the proliferation and survival of tumour cells by inhibiting cell signalling pathways [ ]. Genipin also plays a role in cell signalling and may have an inhibitory effect on the growth of BRCA cells [ ]. Our study shows that these drugs have good therapeutic effects on BRCA patients and therefore may have important reference values for exploring and developing treatment options for BRCA patients.

5

## Conclusion
This study reveals the key role of histone methylation modification regulators in predicting the prognosis of BRCA patients. On the one hand, we constructed a risk scoring model based on eight HMMRs, which opens up a perspective for the prognosis prediction of BRCA. On the other hand, we screened candidate drugs related to BRCA and provided theoretical support for the targeted therapy of BRCA.
