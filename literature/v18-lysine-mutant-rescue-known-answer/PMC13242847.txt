The development of HCC usually goes through the precancerous stage, which is a key transition stage for the transformation of various chronic liver diseases to liver cancer. Hepatic precancerous lesions mainly include Dysplastic foci (DF) and Dysplastic nodules (DN), in which DF is typically characterized by a diameter of less than 1 mm and defined by cytological alterations, and DN is commonly observed in cirrhotic livers and are classified into low-grade dysplastic nodules (LGDN) and high-grade dysplastic nodules (HGDN) based on the degree of cellular atypia. However, the early biochemical and molecular events underlying the progression from precursor conditions, such as cirrhosis, to HCC have not been fully elucidated.
In recent years, with the prevalence of metabolic diseases, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) and its progressive form MASH have rapidly emerged as a significant cause and precursor condition for HCC. MASLD/MASH is considered the hepatic manifestation of the metabolic syndrome. Without timely intervention, it can gradually progress to liver fibrosis and even cirrhosis. Although HBV/HCV infection, chronic hepatitis, and cirrhosis have traditionally been the main causes of HCC, several studies have confirmed that MASLD/MASH has become the main cause promoting the development of fibrosis, cirrhosis, and HCC, , and has been confirmed as a new precancerous state of HCC. Therefore, the early identification and monitoring of liver inflammation and fibrosis in patients with MASLD/MASH are crucial to prevent the development of precancerous lesions.
Although liver biopsy remains the gold standard for the diagnosis of HCC, its clinical application is limited due to its invasiveness and the risk of needle tract metastasis. Alpha-fetoprotein is commonly used for early screening of HCC; However, studies have shown that it has limited specificity in distinguishing MASLD/MASH-related HCC from HCC related to viral hepatitis. Consequently, there is an urgent need to develop new, non-invasive, and highly specific biomarkers to enable accurate diagnosis and risk assessment of precancerous lesions.
In this context, epigenetic modifications—especially dynamic, reversible histone modifications—offer promising directions for studying precancerous lesions of HCC, as they can be altered in the early stages of the disease. The pathogenesis of HCC is a complex process involving genetic and epigenetic changes. Epigenetic modifications include DNA methylation, histone modification, and non-coding RNA. Among them, histone modification, a core mechanism, plays a key role in HCC by regulating processes such as chromatin structure, gene transcription and DNA repair. There are various forms of histone modification, including acetylation, methylation, phosphorylation, and ubiquitination. Increasing evidence suggests that histone methylation plays a particularly important role in HCC.
Therefore, this article focuses on the research progress of histone methylation modification in the early, progressive, and prognostic stages of MASH-liver fibrosis/cirrhosis-HCC, in order to provide a new epigenetic perspective and target clues for early intervention and treatment of HCC.

## Histone Methylation
Histone methylation and demethylation are key mechanisms for dynamic regulation of chromatin structure, which determine transcriptional repression (heterochromatin formation) or activation (euchromatin formation), respectively. This process is co-catalyzed by histone methyltransferases (HMTs) and histone demethylases (HDMs). HMTs are categorized into two major classes based on their target residues: arginine methyltransferases and lysine methyltransferases. The function of histone methylation is highly determined by the specific lysine or arginine residue sites and their methylation state (mono-, di-, or tri-methylation). Generally, modifications such as H3K4me2/3, H3K9me1, H3K27me1, H3K36me1/3, H3K79me1/2, and H4K20me1 are associated with transcriptional activation, while H3K9me2/3, H3K27me2/3, and H4K20me3 are associated with transcriptional repression. , The transfer of one to three methyl groups from S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) to the side-chain nitrogen of lysine or arginine residues is catalyzed by HMTs. During the methylation reaction, SAM is converted to S-adenosine homocysteine (SAH), which inhibits methyltransferase activity. Under cellular stress or disease states, methylation patterns can be disrupted, and elevated SAM levels can suppress proto-oncogene expression through promoter methylation. Histone arginine methylation is a ubiquitous post-translational modification, and aberrant histone arginine methylation is closely related to tumorigenesis and metastasis. Arginine residues may be methylated by different types of arginine methyltransferases (PRMTs), and their methylation modification can be mainly removed by peptidyl arginine deiminase 4 (PADI4) by converting them to citrulline. On the other hand, lysine-specific methylation is catalyzed by lysine methyltransferases, while HDMs mediate its demethylation. According to the mechanism of action, HDMs can be divided into two categories: nucleophilic catalysis and oxidation-dependent catalysis. They can remove methyl marks on histone lysine residues in a site-specific manner. Studies have shown that disruption of the above histone methylation balance occurs early in the progression from MASLD/MASH to HCC and continues to influence subsequent pathological processes. In HCC, specific histone post-translational modifications (PTMs) exert their effects on epigenetic regulation through complex interactions. For example, activating histone PTMs (H3K27ac, H3K4me3) and an eraser (KDM6B) that inhibits PTMs can promote chromatin opening, leading to the upregulation of proto-oncogenes and thereby promoting HCC development. In contrast, repressive PTMs (H3K9me2/3, H3K27me3) downregulate tumor suppressor genes and miRNAs by altering chromatin accessibility. In addition, other PTM erasers (eg, HDAC1, KDM5B) inhibit key tumor suppressor pathways by removing activated methylation or acetylation marks. The ultimate shared consequence of these epigenetic abnormalities leads to increased tumor proliferation, vascular invasion, distant metastasis, and reduced overall survival of patients. 

## Histone Methylation in MASH
MASH, a progressive form of MASLD, is characterised by hepatic steatosis, inflammation, hepatocyte injury, and fibrosis. Studies have shown that histone methylation plays a key role in the initiation and progression of MASH and may further promote hepatocellular carcinogenesis. Apoptosis, regulated by histone methylation-dependent mechanisms involving caspases, Bcl-2 family proteins, and the JNK pathway, plays a critical role in MASH. Caspase-2-activated site-1 protease has been identified as a driver of lipogenesis and steatohepatitis. , The Cytokeratin-18 (CK-18) fragment generated by caspase-3 activation has been established as a key independent predictor of MASH. 

## H3K4 Methylation: Driving Lipid Accumulation and Inflammation
Aberrant H3K4 methylation has been implicated as a key mechanism driving hepatic steatosis and inflammatory responses. The lysine methyltransferase KMT2D catalyzes the H3K4me1 modification, which, in turn, promotes IDI1 expression, thereby driving lipid accumulation and inflammation. KMT2D can also regulate liver steatosis in mice induced by overfeeding by activating the nuclear receptor peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma 2 (PPARγ2). PPARγ2 is a key adipogenic transcription factor that stimulates fatty acid uptake and lipid droplet formation, thereby promoting hepatic steatosis. Under conditions of nutritional excess, the activated kinase ABL1 phosphorylates and activates the nuclear receptor PPARγ2, which in turn recruits the KMT2C/D complex to multiple targets, including its own gene, to promote the formation of fatty liver by catalyzing H3K4 methylation. In addition, the methyltransferase SET7/9 has been shown to upregulate H3K4me3 levels at the promoters of inflammatory genes induced by tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) and to promote the recruitment of p65 factors, thereby amplifying the NF-κB-mediated inflammatory cascade. This mechanism is closely related to MASH deterioration. 

## H3K9 Methylation: Regulation of the Balance Between Metabolism and Inflammation
The methylation status of H3K9 is essential for maintaining liver metabolic homeostasis. H3K9me1/me2 catalyzed by histone methyltransferase KMT1C is significantly down-regulated in MASLD/MASH, affecting cytochrome P450 expression, thereby affecting the clearance of fatty acids, steroids, and other metabolites, indicating that KMT1C down-regulation is involved in the pathogenesis of MASH. Overexpression of histone demethylase KDM7A activates the expression of diacylglycerol acyltransferase 2 (DGAT2) by eliminating H3K9me2 and H3K27me2 on the promoter. It promotes NF-κB-mediated inflammatory cascade, which aggravates the occurrence of hepatic steatosis and MASH. On the other hand, the histone lysine methyltransferase KMT1B catalyzes H3K9me3 up-regulation in MASH, and its deficiency restores the normal expression of Sirt-1 and inhibits NF-κB-dependent inflammatory responses, suggesting that KMT1B plays an important role in liver inflammation. The histone demethylase JMJD2B facilitates LXRα-dependent lipogenesis and hepatic inflammation by specifically removing H3K9me2 and H3K9me3 marks in the vicinity of LXR response elements. Notably, KDM4B has been shown to upregulate PPARγ2 expression by removing H3K9me2 and H3K9me3 modifications at the PPARγ2 promoter. This epigenetic regulation has been demonstrated to enhance fatty acid uptake and lipid droplet accumulation in both cellular and animal models, thereby contributing to the progression of MASH. , As a transcriptional coactivator of ChREBP, Phf2—a histone demethylase—enhances hepatic lipid metabolism by catalyzing the removal of H3K9me2 at specific gene promoters and activating the Nrf2 signaling pathway, thereby attenuating the progression of MASH, which highlights the functional complexity of H3K9 demethylation. 

## H3K27 Methylation: A Key Node in the Regulation of Inflammation
The increase of H3K27me2/3 has been identified as a key driver of hepatic inflammation. This modification is mainly catalyzed by methyltransferase EZH2 (KMT6A), which can lead to chromatin compaction and gene silencing, thus promoting MASH. , By removing H3K27me, demethylase KDM6B increases chromatin accessibility in macrophages, promotes the recruitment of transcription factors to the STING promoter, and then activates the TBK1/IRF3/IFN-α pathway or the NF-κB pathway, which eventually leads to chronic inflammation. 

## Dual Roles of Arginine Methylation
The arginine methyltransferase PRMT5 plays a dual role in the MASH process. On the one hand, the PRMT5/MEP50 complex promotes growth hormone transcription by increasing H3R2me3 levels at the OXR1A promoter, inducing oxidative stress in hepatocytes, and accelerating the progression of MASH. On the other hand, inhibition of PRMT5 may increase liver TG levels and accelerate the progression of MASLD to MASH. ## Histone Methylation in Liver Fibrosis/Cirrhosis
The persistence of MASH-induced inflammatory injury drives the progression to liver fibrosis and ultimately cirrhosis. In this process, the activation and differentiation of hepatic stellate cells are the core steps, and an increasing number of studies have shown that aberrant histone methylation plays a key role in epigenetic regulation.

## H3K4 and H3K79 Methylation: An Amplifier of Profibrotic Signals
In hepatocytes overexpressing STING, histone methylation mediated by the H3K4-specific methyltransferase WDR5 and the H3K79 methyltransferase DOT1L was found to enhance the binding of interferon regulatory factor 3 (IRF3) to the NLRP3 promoter. Thus, it promotes NLRP3 transcription, which strengthens the epigenetic basis of liver fibrosis by regulating pyroptosis and inflammation in hepatocytes. In addition, ASC-2 forms the ASC-2 complex (ASCOM) with the H3K4 methyltransferases MLL3 and MLL4. In addition to its established role in PPARγ-dependent adipogenesis, the ASCOM complex has been implicated in the progression of fibrosis by catalyzing H3K4me3. 

## H3K9 Methylation: An Epigenetic Switch That Regulates HSC Activation
The differentiation of HSC into a profibrotic fibroblast-like phenotype requires the suppression of their PPARγ expression. Following its binding to methylated DNA, MECP2 recruits histone methyltransferases that elevate H3K9 methylation levels at the PPARγ promoter, thereby repressing transcription of the gene and promoting HSC activation. On the other hand, demethylase KDM4C upregulates ALKBH5 expression by decreasing H3K9me3 level, thereby inhibiting the m6A modification of profibrotic factor Snail1. However, in fibrotic mice, KDM4C expression is significantly downregulated, further accelerating fibrosis. 

## H3K27me3: A Core Epigenetic Driver of Fibrosis Progression
Ezh2-mediated H3K27me3 modification is an early epigenetic event that occurs during fatty acid-induced liver injury and is deeply involved in liver inflammation and fibrosis. The mechanisms are complex and diverse: Long non-coding RNA H19 is highly expressed in chronic liver disease. Mechanistically, H19 functions as a molecular scaffold that directly binds to EZH2 through a conserved binding motif, recruiting the Polycomb Repressive Complex 2(PRC2) complex to specific genomic loci to mediate H3K27me3 reprogramming. , The H19-EZH2-PRC2 axis subsequently epigenetically silences multiple tumor suppressor pathways—including the p16INK4a/Rb and PTEN pathways—while concurrently derepressing Wnt/β-catenin and EMT signaling through H3K27me3-mediated repression of negative regulators (eg` Dkk1), collectively driving HSC activation and proliferation. The H19-EZH2 interaction represents a potentially druggable node, involving disrupting the H19-EZH2 protein-RNA interface using antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) against H19 or stapled peptides blocking the EZH2 binding motif. Notably, H19 is liver-enriched and its expression is minimal in most normal adult tissues, suggesting a favorable therapeutic window.
A result that tested epigenetic targeting of EZH2 in STAM MASH mice suggested that EZH2/ KMT6A-regulated H3K27me3 modifications play a role in the progression of liver fibrosis caused by MASH. 
In summary, the persistence of repressive methylation mediated by EZH2 in the fibrotic microenvironment not only maintains the activation state of HSCs and promotes fibrosis progression by silencing protective genes, but also creates favorable epigenetic conditions for the malignant transformation of hepatocytes.

## Histone Methylation in HCC
The progression from MASH to HCC is a complex pathological process involving multiple cellular signaling pathways, immune disorders, and remodeling of gene expression profiles. Notably, aberrant histone methylation patterns serve as a key epigenetic driver, spanning the entire process from initial steatosis and inflammation to fibrosis and, eventually, malignant transformation.
MASH usually begins with a disorder of lipid metabolism, with excessive lipid accumulation in hepatocytes triggering oxidative stress and chronic inflammatory responses. These pro-inflammatory environments activate HSCs and promote their transformation into fibrosis-related myofibroblasts. This process leads to excessive deposition of extracellular matrix (ECM) and fibrosis, and to liver cirrhosis through gradual destruction of liver tissue structure. At this time, chronic inflammation, repeated injury, and hepatocyte regeneration increase the risk of gene mutations and provide conditions for malignant transformation of hepatocytes. Chronic injury and angiogenesis in the cirrhosis stage further aggravate the proliferation of abnormal cells and the formation of the tumor microenvironment, which ultimately promotes the occurrence of HCC. Studies have shown that approximately 2% of MASH cases will progress to MASH-HCC each year. Behind this series of pathological events, aberrant histone methylation plays a key role, providing a new perspective on the molecular mechanism of MASH-HCC and identifying potential therapeutic targets.
At the molecular level, HCC development often results from dysregulation of the hepatocyte cycle or dysregulation of the interaction between progenitor cells and oncogenes. Metabolomics and methylation analysis of MASH model mice, such as TSOD and STAM, showed that the process was accompanied by extensive methylation activation in hepatocellular carcinoma. , Among them, the elevated SAM/SAH ratio indicates that the balance between SAM synthesis and catabolism is disrupted, leading to SAM accumulation in the mouse liver, which may potentially drive the development of MASH-related HCC. , Notably, phosphorylation of La-associated protein 1, an mRNA-binding protein sensitive to SAM, has been identified as a potential key factor in the progression of MASH to HCC. Increasing evidence indicates that mutations in genes encoding epigenetic modifiers or their dysregulation are prevalent in HCC. These alterations result in an aggressive gene expression profile and are closely associated with poor clinical outcomes in patients. 

## Mechanisms of Histone Methylation in MASH-HCC Precancerous Lesions and Early Transformation
During the early stages of MASH-to-HCC transformation, specific histone methylation modifications create critical epigenetic conditions for malignant conversion by activating oncogenes and disrupting cell cycle and metabolic homeostasis.

## H3K4 Methylation: Driving Cell Proliferation and Malignant Transformation
Enhanced H3K4 methylation correlates with the activation of oncogenes and cell cycle regulators. The methyltransferase SETD7 not only serves as a core component in TLR4 signaling activation but also synergizes with HIF2 and NOTCH signaling under hypoxic conditions to induce TLR4 expression, thereby promoting the generation of tumor-initiating stem-like cells. Furthermore, under the catalysis of KMT7/SETD7, substantial accumulation of H3K4me2 directly drives HCC cell cycle progression from G1 to S phase, accelerating cancer cell proliferation and differentiation. Another crucial methyltransferase, SMYD5, is overexpressed in HCC. It catalyzes H3K4 and H3K36 methylation while specifically mediating trimethylation of lysine 22 on ribosomal protein RPL40. This enhances protein translation efficiency, activates the mTOR signaling pathway, and ultimately promotes the development of hepatocellular carcinoma. 

## H3K9 and H3K27 Methylation: Mediating Gene Silencing and Early Events
In addition to activating modifications, repressive methylation also plays a crucial role in early transformation. In MASH-associated HCC, metabolic stress-induced dysregulation of the Na/K-ATPase (ATP1A1) signal promotes increased H3K9me3, which, in turn, further facilitates tumourigenesis and progression. Concurrently, H3K27me3, a key repressive mark, has been shown to be enriched as an early event in p16INK4a silencing during tumour progression. This indicates that abnormal histone methylation plays a significant role in the precancerous stage of hepatocellular carcinoma.

## Mechanisms of Histone Methylation in the Progression of Primary Liver Cancer
During the progression of hepatocellular carcinoma, multiple histone methylation modifications influence tumour proliferation, invasion and metastasis by regulating the expression of key genes.

## H3K4 Methylation: Promoting Gene Transcription and Tumour Malignancy
Activating methylation modifications such as H4K20me3, H3K9me3, and H3K4me3 are frequently significantly elevated in hepatocellular carcinoma tissues, where they induce the expression of epithelial-mesenchymal transition markers, thereby promoting tumour progression. The demethylase KDM5B, a member of the JmjC domain protein family, functions to remove methyl groups from histone H3K4. Its expression is upregulated in hepatocellular carcinoma tissues, where it modulates PTEN transcriptional activity by regulating H3K4me3 on the PTEN gene promoter, thereby promoting hepatocellular carcinoma cell proliferation, EMT, migration, and invasion. Furthermore, the methyltransferase SMYD3 catalyses H3K4me3, enhancing the promoter activity of sphingosine 1-phosphate receptor 1 (S1PR1) and thereby promoting the growth and migration of hepatocellular carcinoma cells both in vivo and in vitro. 

## H3K9 Methylation: Regulating Chromatin State and Oncogene Signalling
Lysine methyltransferases EHMT2 (G9a) and KMT1A primarily mediate histone H3K9 methylation to induce heterochromatin formation, and G9a overexpression is closely associated with malignant pathological features in HCC. In Myc-driven liver tumours, G9a interacts with c-Myc, not only participating in c-Myc-dependent gene repression but also stabilising c-Myc protein levels, thereby jointly promoting HCC growth and invasion. Conversely, the demethylase KDM3A reduces H3K9me1/2 levels, thereby activating the PI3K/AP-1 and JAK2-STAT3 signalling pathways. It further regulates the expression or activity of β-catenin and C-Myc genes, accelerating HCC progression. ZNF143, overexpressed in HCC, promotes proliferation by transcriptionally activating the demethylase MDIG, thereby reducing H3K9me3 enrichment at the CDC6 promoter and relieving inhibition of CDC6. LncRNA TRERNA1 promotes HCC metastasis by recruiting the EHMT2/SNAI1 complex to mediate H3K9me2 modification in the CDH1 promoter region. Furthermore, EHMT2 suppresses RRAGC gene expression in HCC via a reactive oxygen species-dependent mechanism, indicating its role as a key regulator of stress response gene expression in hepatocellular carcinoma cells. 

## H3K27 Methylation: Mediating Gene Silencing and Signalling Pathway Activation
High H3K27 methylation serves as a universal transcriptional repressive marker. EZH2 mediates gene silencing via H3K27me3, is frequently overexpressed in HCC, and participates in regulating cell cycle arrest and DNA damage responses. EZH2 further suppresses transcription of Wnt signalling antagonists via H3K27me3, thereby activating Wnt/β-catenin signalling and enhancing tumour invasiveness. In HCC, expression of the long non-coding RNAs TCAM1P-004 and RP11-598D14.1 is suppressed by EZH2-mediated H3K27me3 modification, leading to loss of their tumour-suppressing functions. This disrupts the regulation of the p53, MAPK, and HIF-1α pathways, thereby promoting HCC proliferation and progression. Research further reveals that abnormal expression of IKK complex subunits enhances the activity of H3K27 methyltransferases SUZ12 and EZH2, promoting methylation at H3K27 sites within the HOTAIR promoter region, which is closely associated with HCC development. Furthermore, studies have demonstrated that HSC-induced nicotinamide N-methyltransferase (NNMT) exhibits elevated expression in HCC. NNMT modulates histone H3K27 methylation patterns and activates transcription of CD44, thereby further promoting the formation of CD44v3 splice variants, revealing NNMT’s pivotal role in HCC metastasis. ## Other Methylation Types: Argininylation and Synergistic Carcinogenesis
In addition to lysine methylation, arginine methylation also contributes to HCC progression. Both lysine methyltransferase SMYD4 and arginine methyltransferase PRMT5 are highly expressed in HCC and synergistically promote the proliferation of hepatocellular carcinoma cells. SMYD4 induces monomethylation of PRMT5, enhancing its interaction with MEP50. This subsequently promotes PRMT5-catalysed symmetric dimethylation of H3R2 and H4R3 at target gene promoters, activating DVL3 expression whilst simultaneously suppressing E-cadherin, RBL2, and miR-29b-1-5p expression. Similarly, the arginine methyltransferase PRMT2, overexpressed in HCC tissues, catalyzes asymmetric methylation of H3R8 (H3R8me2a). This enhances the accessibility of the Bcl2 promoter to STAT3, thereby promoting Bcl2 gene expression and accelerating HCC progression. 

## Mechanisms of Histone Methylation in the Prognosis of Primary Liver Cancer
Specific histone methylation patterns are closely associated with disease progression and therapeutic resistance in hepatocellular carcinoma, revealing not only their potential as prognostic biomarkers but also partially elucidating the molecular basis for variations in patient response to treatment.

## As a Biomarker for Prognostic Prediction
Research indicates that nuclear levels of H3K9me2, H3K9me3, and the hypoxia-inducible factor HIF-1α are significantly elevated in HCC tissue compared to adjacent non-cancerous tissue, with H3K9me2 expression positively correlated with HIF-1α. The high expression of these molecules correlates significantly with tumour dedifferentiation and poor patient prognosis, indicating that H3K9 methylation and the HIF-1α pathway jointly constitute a prognostic indicator system for assessing HCC invasiveness. Furthermore, the H4M score, constructed from multiple histone H4 methylation states, can effectively distinguish different tumour immune microenvironments. Patients with low scores typically exhibit an “immunological inflammatory” TME profile, demonstrating greater sensitivity to immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy and consequently improved prognosis. Conversely, high scores indicate an immunosuppressive microenvironment and poorer survival outcomes. Thus, the H4M score holds promise as a robust tool for guiding HCC immunotherapy and predicting prognosis. 

## Key Mechanisms Mediating Therapeutic Resistance
Abnormal histone methylation constitutes a significant factor contributing to treatment resistance in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). The long non-coding RNA Lnc-PDZD7 exhibits elevated expression in HCC and correlates with poor patient prognosis and suboptimal response to transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) therapy. Its molecular mechanism involves Lnc-PDZD7 acting as a competitive endogenous RNA to sequester miR-101, thereby releasing miR-101’s inhibitory effect on EZH2. Subsequently, the elevated EZH2 catalyzes H3K27me3 modification in the ATOH8 gene promoter region and synergises with DNA methylation to jointly suppress its expression. This ultimately enhances the stem-like characteristics of hepatocellular carcinoma cells and diminishes their sensitivity to chemotherapy. This discovery reveals the pivotal role of EZH2-mediated histone methylation in driving therapeutic resistance in HCC.

## Discussion

## Critical Synthesis: Stage-Specific Epigenetic Transitions in MASLD-HCC
A critical examination of the evidence presented in this review reveals several overarching principles governing histone methylation dynamics during MASLD-HCC progression. First, the transition from benign steatosis to malignancy is characterized by a progressive accumulation of repressive histone marks, particularly H3K27me3 and H3K9me3, which creates a permissive epigenetic environment for oncogenic transformation. This observation aligns with the “epigenetic priming” hypothesis, wherein early metabolic stress establishes chromatin states that predispose hepatocytes to malignant transformation upon subsequent genetic or environmental insults. 
However, this linear model oversimplifies the complexity of disease progression. A more nuanced interpretation is that histone methylation modifications function as stage-specific “switches” rather than continuous gradients. During the MASH phase, H3K4me1/3-driven activation of lipogenic and inflammatory genes predominates, reflecting the primary pathological processes of steatosis and necroinflammation. , In contrast, the fibrotic stage is marked by a shift toward H3K9me3-mediated heterochromatin formation and H3K27me3-dependent silencing of anti-fibrotic genes, which locks HSCs into an activated phenotype. , Notably, the transition to HCC involves the reactivation of H3K4me3 at oncogene promoters concurrent with persistent H3K27me3 silencing of tumor suppressors—a “bivalent” chromatin state reminiscent of embryonic stem cells that confers both proliferative capacity and phenotypic plasticity. 
This stage-specificity has profound implications for biomarker development. For instance, H3K27me3 enrichment at the p16INK4a promoter may serve as an early predictive marker of malignant conversion, whereas the H4M score—integrating multiple H4 methylation states—appears more informative for prognostic stratification in established HCC. The challenge lies in identifying which methylation events are causally driving progression versus those that are merely correlative consequences of cellular transformation.
Furthermore, the intimate connection between histone methylation and metabolic reprogramming warrants critical attention. The SAM/SAH ratio, which reflects cellular methylation capacity, is disrupted in MASH and may itself drive oncogenic histone methylation patterns. , This creates a feed-forward loop wherein metabolic dysfunction alters the epigenetic landscape, which in turn reprograms metabolism to support malignant growth-a paradigm consistent with the emerging concept of “metabolic-epigenetic crosstalk”. , Whether targeting this metabolic-epigenetic axis can reverse established epigenetic aberrations remains a critical unanswered question.

## Context-Dependent and Conflicting Roles of Histone Methylation Modifiers
While this review has systematically summarized the pro-tumorigenic roles of histone methylation dysregulation in MASLD-HCC progression, it is crucial to acknowledge that the field of epigenetics is characterized by significant context-dependent effects and conflicting evidence. Several histone methylation modifiers exhibit dual or even opposing functions depending on the disease stage, cellular microenvironment, and specific molecular contexts.
EZH2: Oncogene versus context-dependent tumor suppressor. EZH2 is widely recognized as an oncogenic driver in HCC, where its overexpression promotes tumor proliferation, metastasis, and immune evasion through H3K27me3-mediated silencing of tumor suppressor genes such as p21, CHD5, and DLC1. , However, emerging evidence suggests that EZH2 may also exert context-dependent tumor-suppressive functions. In certain liver injury models, EZH2-mediated H3K27me3 is essential for maintaining hepatocyte differentiation and preventing Mallory-Denk body formation; failure of EZH2/H3K27me3 to methylate DNA in hepatocyte nuclei is associated with increased cell proliferation and liver pathology. Furthermore, the role of EZH2 in immune regulation appears contradictory: while EZH2 generally suppresses anti-tumor immunity by downregulating NKG2D ligands and CXCL10, Liu et al reported that EZH2 negatively regulates PD-L1 expression in hepatoma cells by inhibiting IRF1, suggesting a potential immune-activating effect in specific contexts. These discrepancies may reflect differences in tumor microenvironment composition, baseline immune infiltration, or the specific HCC etiology, highlighting that EZH2 targeting requires careful consideration of disease stage and tumor immune context.
PRMT5: Cell-type-dependent dual roles in MASH progression. Protein arginine methyltransferase 5 (PRMT5) exemplifies the complexity of histone methylation regulation across different cell types within the liver. On the one hand, the PRMT5/MEP50 complex promotes growth hormone transcription by increasing H3R2me3 levels at the OXR1A promoter, thereby inducing oxidative stress in hepatocytes and accelerating MASH progression. On the other hand, inhibition of PRMT5 in myeloid cells has been shown to increase hepatic triglyceride levels and accelerate the progression from MASLD to MASH, suggesting that PRMT5 maintains lipid homeostasis in hepatocytes while simultaneously driving pro-inflammatory responses in macrophages. , This cell-type-dependent differential effect indicates that PRMT5 may serve distinct, even opposing, functions in hepatocytes versus immune cells, complicating its therapeutic targeting in MASLD/MASH.
KDM6B (JMJD3): Demethylase-dependent versus independent oncogenic functions. The H3K27 demethylase KDM6B also exhibits multifaceted roles in cancer. While KDM6B-mediated removal of H3K27me3 can activate tumor suppressor genes such as p16INK4A and p14ARF, triggering cellular senescence and apoptosis, KDM6B is also upregulated via NF-κB binding to its promoter region by TNF-α, and in turn upregulates the MAPK pathway, thereby promoting cancer cell growth and survival in a catalytically independent manner. Furthermore, KDM6B increases chromatin accessibility in macrophages, promotes transcription factor recruitment to the STING promoter, and activates the TBK1/IRF3/IFN-α or NF-κB pathways, leading to chronic inflammation. , , These findings suggest that KDM6B can function as either a tumor suppressor or promoter depending on the signaling context and cell type.
The existence of these conflicting roles carries profound implications for therapeutic development. First, they underscore the danger of extrapolating findings from one disease stage or cell type to another. Second, they highlight the need for biomarker-driven patient stratification-therapeutic efficacy may depend on the baseline epigenetic state of the tumor and its microenvironment. Third, they suggest that combination therapies targeting multiple epigenetic nodes may be necessary to overcome compensatory mechanisms and context-dependent resistance.

## Crosstalk Between Histone Methylation and Other Epigenetic Layers in MASLD-HCC
Histone methylation does not operate in isolation but engages in extensive crosstalk with DNA methylation, non-coding RNAs, and metabolic signaling, forming an integrated epigenetic network that drives MASLD-HCC progression.

## Histone Methylation and DNA Methylation
The interplay between histone methylation and DNA methylation is bidirectional and mutually reinforcing. EZH2-mediated H3K27me3 recruits DNA methyltransferases to promoter regions, establishing de novo DNA methylation that permanently silences tumor suppressor genes. Conversely, DNA methylation at CpG islands can recruit methyl-CpG-binding domain proteins, which in turn interact with H3K9 methyltransferases to reinforce heterochromatin formation. , In MASH-related HCC, this positive feedback loop is exemplified by the silencing of ATOH8: EZH2-catalyzed H3K27me3 at the ATOH8 promoter synergizes with DNMT3a-mediated DNA methylation to suppress its expression, enhancing cancer stemness and chemoresistance. Pharmacological co-targeting of EZH2 and DNMTs through combined tazemetostat and decitabine may therefore overcome epigenetic resistance more effectively than single-agent therapy. 

## Histone Methylation and Non-Coding RNAs
LncRNAs serve as critical bridges linking histone methylation machinery to chromatin. Beyond the H19-EZH2 axis, lncRNA TRERNA1 recruits the EHMT2/SNAI1 complex to the CDH1 promoter, mediating H3K9me2 deposition and E-cadherin silencing to promote HCC metastasis. Conversely, some lncRNAs antagonize histone methylation: lncRNA HOTAIR is repressed by EZH2-mediated H3K27me3 at its own promoter, forming a negative feedback loop. Additionally, circular RNAs have emerged as modulators of histone methylation. For instance, circRNA-LRIG3 forms a ternary complex with EZH2 and STAT3, promoting EZH2-mediated methylation of STAT3 at K180, which activates STAT3 signaling and enhances HCC metastasis. These examples illustrate that the lncRNA/circRNA-histone methylation axis constitutes a hierarchical regulatory network where RNAs both instruct and respond to histone marks.
The integration of these epigenetic layers creates multiple nodes for therapeutic intervention, yet the context-dependent nature of individual modifiers (as discussed in Context-Dependent and Conflicting Roles of Histone Methylation Modifiers) underscores the need for precision approaches. For example, targeting EZH2 alone may be insufficient if compensatory DNA methylation or lncRNA-mediated feedback loops maintain gene silencing. Conversely, combination strategies that simultaneously disrupt histone methylation and its crosstalk partners may achieve more durable epigenetic reprogramming.

## Unanswered Questions and Future Directions
Despite substantial progress, several critical questions remain unresolved. First, what determines the specificity of histone methylation targeting? While writers, erasers, and readers have been identified, the mechanisms directing these enzymes to specific genomic loci in response to metabolic stress are poorly understood. Second, how do histone methylation dynamics differ across distinct hepatic cell types? The majority of current evidence derives from bulk tissue analyses or hepatocyte-centric models, yet HSCs, Kupffer cells, and endothelial cells each exhibit unique epigenetic landscapes that collectively shape disease progression. Single-cell multi-omics approaches will be essential to resolve these cell-type-specific contributions. , Third, can epigenetic modifications be reversed once established? Unlike genetic mutations, histone methylation is theoretically reversible; however, the stability of aberrant methylation patterns and the potential for “epigenetic memory” following transient metabolic insults remain controversial. Longitudinal studies tracking methylation changes during disease regression could address this question. , Fourth, what are the optimal strategies for clinical translation? The systemic toxicity of epigenetic drugs, the challenge of liver-specific delivery, and the need for predictive biomarkers to identify responsive patient subpopulations represent formidable barriers. The development of PROTAC-based degraders, nanoparticle delivery systems, and liquid biopsy-based methylation biomarkers may help overcome these hurdles. , 
Finally, the integration of histone methylation data with artificial intelligence-driven predictive models holds promise for personalized risk stratification and treatment selection. Multi-modal algorithms incorporating clinical, genomic, epigenomic, and metabolomic features may ultimately enable precision epigenetic medicine for MASLD-HCC. ,
