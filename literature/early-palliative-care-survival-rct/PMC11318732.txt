## Abstract
Metastatic uveal melanoma (UM) patients often initially present with limited symptoms despite a poor prognosis, complicating communication with patients and caregivers. Early Together (NCT04728113) is a randomized Phase III trial that integrates early palliative care through systematic joint visits involving the palliative care team and the medical oncologist, compared with standard oncological care, in 162 metastatic UM patients beginning systemic treatment. This collaboration aims to enhance patient functioning, improve quality of life and facilitate coping mechanisms. Patients' quality of life and needs for information, care and support are assessed over 12 months using validated questionnaires in both groups. Investigators receive training throughout the study to improve communication skills in addressing uncertainty and poor prognosis. This article describes the trial protocol.
Keywords: : communication training, early palliative care, metastatic uveal melanoma, psychological care needs, quality of life

## Article highlights

## Background
Patients with metastatic Uveal Melanoma, a rare subtype of melanoma, face a poor prognosis while presenting few or no physical symptoms.
Methods:
We here describe the protocol of the first prospective, comparative Phase III trial dedicated to metastatic UM patients focusing on early integration of palliative care to meet patient's specific needs.
The study uses internationally validated questionnaires to objectively quantify the patient's needs for information, care and support over time.
The use of a dedicated information booklet, including a question prompt list, aims to improve patients' understanding and facilitate their active participation in care.
Concomitant training of physicians aims to improve their communication skills on poor prognosis and uncertainty with the patients.

## Discussion/conclusion
Our results will contribute to guidelines on the optimal timing to introduce palliative care in metastatic uveal melanoma and possibly other diseases with poor outcomes and few symptoms.

## Introduction
Uveal melanoma (UM) is a rare melanoma but the most frequent ocular primary tumor in adults with approximately five new cases per million and year in Europe and the USA [ ]. Despite effective local treatment with radiation therapy or enucleation, up to half of uveal melanomas eventually develop clinical metastases, depending on the size and genomics of the primary tumor; liver being the first site of metastasis in 90% of patients [ ]. Before tebentafusp [ ], median progression-free (PFS) and overall survival (OS) in metastatic UM patients were as poor as 3 and 12 months respectively [ ]. Tebentafusp significantly improves OS in HLA A*02:01 positive first-line metastatic patients over the investigator's choice (21.6 months versus 16.9 months) in a randomized Phase III trial [ ], and this survival benefit remains significant after 3 years of follow-up [ ]. Standard medical management in metastatic UM patients usually introduces palliative care for patients with advanced disease suffering from physical, functional, or psychological distress, as recommended by ASCO guidelines [ ].
Palliative care is an essential part of cancer treatment [ ] and aims to meet the psychological, social, physical, informational and practical needs of cancer patients [ , ], to prevent and relief suffering and improve the quality of life.
Early integration of palliative care (EPC) is internationally recommended to facilitate the progressive transition from curative to palliative care and to individually tailor the intensity of care [ ]. An anticipated, collegial and multidisciplinary strategy of patient care in Stage IV non-small-cell lung cancer was reported to reduce resuscitation acts and improve quality of life and survival [ ]. According to a recent review of 7 randomized trials in 1614 advanced cancer patients [ ], EPC significantly improved health-related quality of life and symptom relief, with no difference in levels of depressive symptoms or survival.
Metastatic UM patients represent a unique population in the context of early palliative care. Indeed, these patients are mostly referred to medical oncologists for systemic treatment in the absence or with very limited physical symptoms. Liver function remains in normal range and patients are asymptomatic for a long time before late and terminal liver failure.
The discrepancy between a good physical condition and the short-term poor prognosis further increases the complexity of the announcement of the diagnosis of metastases and the palliative situation. In the same way, disease progression and therapeutic failures constitute recurrent bad news, as the median PFS is 3 months, whatever the oncological treatment. Stopping anticancer treatments and switching to palliative care is a complex situation for the patients, the family members, and care providers. A recent pilot study has highlighted the difficulties faced by these patients, identifying more than 30% of high scores of anxiety and poor quality of life, and 14% of depression [ ].
Moreover, each patient has its own needs, evolution of understanding and capability to actively participate in treatment decisions [ ]. Many studies have demonstrated that high-quality communication between physicians and patients is beneficial both for physicians and patients [ , ]. This bidirectional process of communication on their current medical condition, the complexity of treatments and life expectations, will help patients cope with uncertainty and have a more realistic anticipation of the future [ ].
Previous studies showed the efficacy of communication skills training (CST) programs to improve the communication skills of physicians such as breaking bad news to cancer patients, assessing psychosocial issues and talking with patient relatives [ ]. Communication between physician and patient can also be promoted by using tools such as Question Prompt Lists (QPL), a structured list of questions for the patients or caregivers to ask the medical team about the disease [ , ].
To provide patient-centered care, there is an obligation to understand whether the patient's needs for information, care and support are met and whether an earlier introduction of palliative care, adapted to the patient's needs, could improve their global quality of life.
In this study, we plan to address the effectiveness of an early introduction of palliative care in metastatic UM patients. Prior to Early Together, a feasibility study was conducted at Institut Curie in 47 metastatic UM patients from June 2016 to June 2018 [ ]. Among these patients, 25 (53%) had an early joint consultation with a medical oncologist and a palliative care specialist after a median time of 3 months from the suspicion of metastasis. This delay covers the time needed from the diagnosis of metastases (imaging and biopsy) and staging to the initiation of the systemic treatment. This joint consultation was performed as a systematic visit with the medical oncologist and the palliative care team (including physician, nurse, psychologist and nutritionist) in 14 patients (30%) to address patients' and caregivers' specific needs. Most patients had no physical symptoms at that moment, with an ECOG performance status of 0–1 in 41 patients (87%). Patients showed increased malnutrition and pain, but also reported anxiety and depression over time, from 17% at the time of diagnosis of metastases to 50% patients at 3 months and 80% at 12 months. This preliminary study not only demonstrated the feasibility of an early joint consultation in daily clinical practice for metastatic UM patients, but also highlighted the unmet needs of these patients in terms of information and palliative care. The Early Together trial builds upon the expertise gained in this pilot study.
The “Early Together” trial is set up in two French comprehensive cancer centers, members of the French UM network Melachonat labelled by the National Institute of Cancer (INCa): Institut Curie, Paris and Centre Antoine Lacassagne, Nice. We plan to train all investigators for communication skills focused on dealing with uncertainty and poor prognosis, study the integration of palliative care as soon as the medical oncologist starts the management of the metastatic disease, with a systematic joint consultation with a palliative care specialist and (iii) objectively quantify the patient's needs for information, care and support over time using internationally validated questionnaires, both in the experimental and in the standard arm. To our knowledge, Early Together is the first study focused on the early integration of palliative care and patient needs in metastatic UM.

2.

## Methods
2.1.

## Study aims & hypothesis
We hypothesize that patients randomly assigned to the early palliative care arm (i.e. the experimental group) will report decreased levels of psychological needs, in terms of information and support needs over time, when compared with patients receiving standard oncology care (i.e. the standard arm).
The primary objective of the study is to determine whether the early introduction of palliative care demonstrates a benefit on patients' psychological care needs at 6 months (M6), versus oncological standard of care in patients with metastatic UM.
The secondary objectives for both groups are:
▪To assess and compare the impact of the intervention on patient's other care needs at 6 months (M6) and 12 months (M12), on symptoms and quality of life, beliefs and perception of prognosis and communication about end-of-life care.
▪To assess and compare patient's symptoms, functioning and overall quality of life.
▪To describe and compare the impact of the intervention on the type and number of lines of oncological treatment and on Progression-free and Overall Survival;
▪To describe and compare the impact of the intervention on medical interventions, treatment of symptoms, observance, prevention and treatment of adverse events, outpatient visits and hospital admissions;
▪To evaluate the impact of the disease burden that may affect the patient's partners or relatives.

2.2.

## Design of the study
Early Together is a bi-centric, prospective, randomized Phase III trial conducted at Institut Curie (Paris, France) and Centre Antoine Lacassagne (Nice, France), two French reference centers for uveal melanoma. The study was ethically validated without prior phase I or II studies, because Early Together is not a drug trial and therefore represents limited risks and similar Phase III trials have already evaluated the effectiveness of early palliative care in frequent and highly symptomatic tumor types such as pancreatic or lung cancer [ , ].
The study design is depicted in . The estimated total study duration is 48 months, including a recruitment period of 36 months and a follow-up period of 12 months for each participating patient.
 
2.3.

## Patients' eligibility criteria
Inclusion criteria are: adult patients ≥18 years; metastatic UM with no planned surgery or loco-regional treatment of metastases in a curative intent; systemic treatment is planned or started for less than 2 months. The performance status must be ECOG 0 or 1; no uncontrolled symptoms; liver function tests in the normal range or ≤ grade 2 according to CTCAE v 4.0; patients have to be able to sign the informed consent and fill the questionnaires.
Non-inclusion criteria are: patients <18 years, symptoms requiring palliative care before any - specific treatment for metastases, any psychopathological disorder or prior medical condition not compatible with the study procedures.

2.4.

## Randomization
The randomization will assign patients to “Introduction of palliative care when medically needed” (Arm A: Standard Group) or “Initial concomitant management by the oncologist and the palliative care team” (Arm B: Early Together Group); this randomization is proposed to be 1:2. Assignments will be computer-generated via Ennov Clinical software.

2.5.

## Schedule & content of visits
Both the standard group (n = 54 patients) and the experimental group: (“Early Together” group, n = 108 patients) will benefit from oncological visits at months 0, 3, 6, 9, 12 (+/- 14 days): ECOG performance status, weight, vital signs, concomitant medications, physical examination, tumor evaluation (MRI and/or CT) by RECIST 1.1 criteria, laboratory tests, anticancer treatment: date/type/schedule/adverse events (CTCAE v 4.0).
For patients in the standard group, palliative care is introduced according to current international guidelines and clinical practice, upon: insufficient relief of disease-related symptoms; need for additional medical and/or psychological support, performance status >2; symptomatic grade >2 LFTs requiring corticosteroids, discontinuation of the disease-specific treatment.
Patients in the experimental group will benefit at months 0, 6 and 12 from an “Early Together Visit” by the oncologist and the palliative care physician as a team. First, the patient meets the medical oncologist for an explanation of the metastatic disease and the systemic treatment strategy. Then, the medical oncologist introduces the palliative care physician as being part of the medical team for global patient management. Within two weeks maximum, the patient meets the palliative care team for a visit with open questions about disease understanding, a discussion in which the physician tailors the delivered information to the patient's needs and level of acceptance, questions about social situation, evaluation of psychological status and need for psychological follow-up, clinical examination, symptoms and medication prescriptions if needed. Caregivers can actively participate. The uncertainty about the evolution of the disease and its prognosis despite the absence of symptoms can be addressed. In addition to these “Early Together” visits, the patients have a palliative care consultation every 6 weeks (+/- 7 days). Teleconsultations are allowed for these interim visits. Patients also receive a specific question prompt list about palliative care. Patients can choose questions they wish to ask the physicians.

2.6.

## Questionnaires
Selected questionnaires have demonstrated strong psychometric properties, are validated in French and their use is recognized among samples of adults with cancer. All questionnaires will be filled at baseline, 6 and 12 months by the patients after the oncological consultation, in the 2 groups. The Prognostic Treatment Perception Questionnaire (PTPQ) questionnaire is filled at 3, 6, 9 and 12 months [ ].
-The Supportive Care Needs Survey (SCNS) [ ], has proven validity and reliability [ ], is applicable to all types of cancer and covers a wide range of content. The short version of 34 items was recently validated psychometrically in French [ ]. The SCNS-SF34 is composed of 34 items addressing physical and daily living needs (items 1–5), psychological needs (items 6–17), patient care and support needs (items 18–22), sexuality needs (items 15-16-31), health system and information needs (items 23–34). Here, the main study end point is the score of the psychological needs (items 6–17) obtained in the two arms at 6 months after inclusion. A score from 0 to 100 is calculated for each scale, a higher score indicates higher expressed needs or unmet needs.
-The European Organization for Research and Treatment (EORTC) QLQ-C30 30-item questionnaire is validated and widely used for assessing symptoms, functioning difficulties and overall quality of life in cancer patients (Aaronson et al., 1993). A score from one to 100 is calculated, lower scores denote lower quality of life.
-The Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS) [ ] 14-item is validated for the assessment of symptoms of anxiety and depression in palliative care and oncology. Anxiety and depression scores range from 0 (no distress) to 42 (maximum distress).
-Patients' information preferences, perception of their prognosis and goal of therapy and communication about end-of-life care will be measured with the 13-item Prognosis and Treatment Perceptions Questionnaire (PTPQ) [ ]. PTPQ assesses patient beliefs regarding: 1) the likelihood of cure, 2) the importance and helpfulness of knowing about prognosis, 3) the primary goal of cancer care, 4) preferences for information about treatment and 5) satisfaction with the quality of information received about prognosis and treatment.
-Patients' self-efficacy will be measured with the 10-item General Self-Efficacy scale (GSE) [ , ]. The GSE is designed to assess optimistic self-beliefs regarding coping with a variety of difficult demands in life. A higher score reflects a higher generalized sense of self-efficacy.

2.7.

## Training of physicians
Throughout the study, investigators from Institut Curie (Paris) and Centre Antoine Lacassagne (Nice) will attend communication skills training (CST) workshops provided by an expert team in the field [ ]. Both medical oncologists and palliative care physicians will be trained throughout the 4 years of the study duration. This CST teaches physicians to talk with patients about prognosis in a way that promotes hope and well-being, to assess and restructure patient's expectations and understandings about prognosis and symptoms.
The CST will involve:
(1)teaching the use of a patient-communication tool, the Question Prompt List (QPL) [ ], about treatment, survivorship, early palliative and end-of-life questioning;
(2)teaching effective communication skills adapted to treatment, survivorship, early palliative and end-of-life questioning through group training sessions;
(3)strengthening the transfer of learned skills in clinical practice through individual sessions that aim to identify physician-specific facilitators and barriers.

2.8.

## Sample size & power considerations
The 6-month standardized Likert summated score obtained at the “Care and Support Needs” domain of the SCNS-SF34 will be compared with the Student's t-test, in an Intent To Treat (ITT) analysis. For a 10-point mean score difference expected between groups (within standard deviation of 20 points) and a two-sided type 1 error of 5%, the inclusion of 54 patients in the standard care group and 108 patients in the Early Together group will provide the study 85% of power. A total of 162 patients is needed and all the patients included will be analyzed (ITT). Multiple imputations will be performed when the main criterion is missing; sensitivity analysis will be conducted as well.

2.9.

## Data analysis plan
Statistical data will be presented in the form of percentages (qualitative variables), mean and standard deviation or median and range (quantitative variables) and survival curve estimates calculated with the Kaplan-Meier method (censored data). All estimations will be presented with a 95% confidence interval. Different statistical methods will be used to compare data: qualitative variables with the Chi2 or Fisher test, quantitative variables with the Student's t-test or with a non-parametric test (Wilcoxon) and survival data with a Logrank test and a Cox model. Concerning the secondary end point, the modalities of analysis of the different questionnaires will be applied according to the advice of the respective authors. All tests will be bilateral tests with a 5% threshold. All analyses will be performed with the R software [ ].

2.10.

## Actual Status
As of 1 January 2024, 140 patients have been included, of which 94 in the experimental arm and 46 in the standard arm. Enrolment started in July 2020 and was slower than initially foreseen, due to the Covid-19 pandemic.
Inclusions should be completed by July 2024, and analyses should be conducted in the following year.
Seven communication training sessions of 2 to 3 h each have been organized for the caregivers so far.

3.

## Discussion
The Early Together trial aims to validate an innovative model of care for metastatic UM patients, a rare melanoma of very poor prognosis. It offers a personalized approach centralized on patient expectations and needs and a regular follow-up in relation to any type of symptoms, psychological, existential as well as physical ones.
We propose in this study an early and joint consultation in the metastatic patient's care journey, allowing integration of the palliative care team further upstream. In the context of a palliative situation with no physical symptom to focus on, regular and systemic follow-up by the palliative care team may help the patients and their relatives adjust to the current medical condition and cope with negative outcomes and uncertainty. Indeed, it takes time to integrate and accept an inescapable end and the continuity of supportive care consultations helps to reduce the time lag between a projected end of life, inconceivable at the time of diagnosis of liver metastasis because it is not physically experienced as such, and the organization and management of this coming end within a relatively short period of time. It may thus enhance the quality of life and help toward appropriate life projects. Finally, it may strengthen and consolidate the role of a person of trust in helping the patients complete their last wills. We thus expect that this model of care gives the patients a sense of care cohesion through a deep and continuous multidisciplinary approach, and that psychological isolation and distress will be decreased.
However, we are also aware that, in this particular disease where patients have few or no physical symptoms, completing questionnaires and talking about uncertainty and prognosis could cause anxiety and increase patient concerns. Indeed, poor communication with cancer patients may be detrimental to both patients and care providers. The communication skills training (CST) of physicians that we have included in the project aims to minimize deleterious effects by adapting communication as best as possible to the needs of each patient. The two-arm randomization in this trial is essential to compare the new approach with current practice. Such CSTs have demonstrated improved emotional support and work satisfaction for medical teams.
During the Early Together trial, Tebentafusp, a bi-specific T-cell engager, has become the standard of care for metastatic UM patients who are positive for HLA-A*02:01 [ ]. Tebentafusp significantly increases the median overall survival, even in patients with progressive disease, giving hope to patients affected by this rare melanoma [ ]. Interestingly, we will be able to assess how the approval of Tebentafusp affects patient's needs and quality of life over time, and whether it modifies the communication between physicians and patients.
At a societal level, our project aims to participate in a new vision of early palliative care, integrated into a holistic model of care. At the end of this project, we plan to present and publish guidelines on the optimal timing to introduce metastatic UM patients to palliative care, which will hopefully be the first step toward international recommendations. Our project could then serve as an example to evaluate and implement this model of care in other diseases with poor outcomes in the absence of symptoms.

4.

## Conclusion
The Early Together trial will provide valuable information on how early introduction of palliative care affects patients' needs and quality of life in the particular context of metastatic uveal melanoma. Early joint consultations between the palliative care team and the medical oncologist, combined with communication skills training for all investigators, aim to offer a personalized approach centralized on patient expectations and needs. Our results will contribute to guidelines on the optimal timing to introduce palliative care in metastatic uveal melanoma and possibly other diseases with poor outcomes and few symptoms.

The study was approved by the internal review board of Institut Curie on Nov 6, 2019, and by an independent ethics committee (Comité de protection de personnes Ouest VI), in line with French regulations. The initial approval date was March 3, 2020. EARLY TOGETHER is a French national trial that was registered before the first patient enrolment on the French National Cancer Institute (INCa) on April 23rd, 2020 (RECF4233 Essai clinique cancer Peau - Mélanomes/Mélanomes oculaires ouvert aux inclusions (e-cancer.fr), in compliance with French law, and on https://www.clinicaltrials.gov under the identifier NCT04728113. Five amendments have been subsequently approved. Amendment 1 (16 July 2020) amendment 3 (30 June 2022), and amendment 5 (17 October 2023) aimed to update the list of investigators. Amendment 2 (24 February 2021) aimed to adapt the protocol to the Covid-19 pandemic, allowing more flexibility in the visits (± 14 days for each official visit and the possibility of teleconsultations). We also modified the timing of the first PTPQ questionnaire, to be completed at M3 instead of M0. The reason for this modification is to prevent patients from facing metastasis announcement and prognostic questioning concomitantly. Amendment 4 (22 December 2022) aimed to extend the duration of the trial by 12 months, to compensate for the delay due to the Covid-19 pandemic. All patients received an information note. Informed consent was obtained from all the participants and their legal guardians. The trial is sponsored by Institut Curie (26 rue d'Ulm, 75005 Paris) and conducted in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki, with Good Clinical Practices as defined by the International Conference on Harmonization (ICH–E6, 17/07/96), with the European directive 2001/20/CE on the conduct of clinical trials, with French laws related to informatics and freedom, bioethics and research involving the human person and with the European Parliament and Council Regulation on the protection of individuals from the processing of personal data and the disclosure of such data, as part of the research.

## Patient & public involvement
Institut Curie is labeled as an “Integrated Cancer Research Site” (SIRIC) by the National Institute of Cancer (INCa) and patient involvement and health democracy are a priority for this label. Prior to this project, the research team asked for advice from the health democracy group of the Institut Curie, which is largely comprised of expert patients. These expert patients have identified physician-patient communication as a priority to improve and promote the management of metastatic UM. They highlighted the difficulty for patients to understand and integrate all medical information and therapeutic issues. Our project builds upon this necessity to facilitate the communication between doctor and patient to improve the psychological adaptation of patients to this medical context.
