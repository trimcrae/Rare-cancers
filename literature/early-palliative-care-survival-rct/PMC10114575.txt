## ABSTRACT

## Introduction:
Palliative care (PC) has been classically synonymous with end-of-life care offered to patients with advanced incurable cancers with an aim to improve their quality-of-life (QoL). However, there is growing evidence to suggest that early integration of PC (EIPC) along with cancer-directed management improves patient-reported outcomes. In this study, we aim to evaluate the impact of EIPC on QoL of cervical cancer patients treated with curative intent.

## Material and Methods:
Patients with locally advanced cervical cancer (stage IB2 to IIIB) having eastern cooperative oncology group (ECOG) performance score 0–2 were randomized into EIPC arm and standard of care arm of concurrent chemoradiation by a computer-generated table of random numbers. QoL was assessed by functional assessment of cancer therapy – general (FACT-G) questionnaire twice in both arms. The first assessment was done prior to starting treatment and second assessment at three months after treatment completion. The mean scores on physical, mental, emotional, social, and functional well-being subscales of FACT-G scale were calculated in both arms, and Wilcoxon test was used to evaluate differences in QoL scores within and in between the arms. This trial was registered with Clinical Trials Registry of India (CTRI) vide CTRI/2017/05/008704.

## Results:
Fifty patients were enrolled in each arm. However, only 42 and 45 patients in EPIC and standard oncological care arm were evaluated in the final analysis. Comparing QoL scores between the two time points in each arm, the mean pre-treatment scores in EIPC arm was significantly higher than post-treatment scores in the domains of physical and emotional well-being, whereas social and functional well-being scores improved significantly after treatment as compared with that as baseline. However, when compared between two arms, the difference of mean scores pre- and post-treatment were almost similar for physical and functional well-being but statistically significant differences were found only in social and emotional well-being scales.

## Conclusions:
Our study is a feasibility study done in an attempt to test the validity of EIPC in cervical cancers. The results are inspiring to conduct robust studies in the future to explore this new domain of integration of palliative services in curable cancers.

## Introduction
Palliative care (PC) has been traditionally associated with end-of-life care offered to patients suffering from advanced incurable cancers with an aim to improve their quality-of-life (QoL) when curative treatment is no longer feasible. However, there is growing evidence to suggest that integration of PC with oncological management improves patient-reported outcomes, in terms of QoL betterment, alleviation of symptom burden, greater satisfaction for the care offered, etc.[ ]
Lately, an interesting topic of research has been the early integration of PC (EIPC) with standard oncological care (SOC). Such EIPC addresses not only QoL and symptomatic relief of patients, which are the principal aims of PC, but also facilitates greater clarity to patients regarding intent of treatment.[ ] The course of illness needs shared decision-making with the patient so that patients can perceive the prognosis better and discuss their preferences with the clinicians.[ ]
EIPC has been tested previously in newly diagnosed incurable non-small cell lung cancers and non-colorectal gastrointestinal cancers.[ ] Evidence suggests improvement in QoL and reduction in depression scores compared with SOC alone.[ ] However, role of EIPC in gynaecological malignancies has been largely unexplored.
Cervical cancer is one of the most common cancers in women in India and majority of our patients present with locally advanced disease. The physical symptoms of bleeding, abdominal pain, bowel irregularities, etc., in locally advanced cervical cancer are mostly similar in case of curative and palliative cases, just that these exacerbated in the latter. The physical symptoms do not just produce physical suffering; but are often associated with psychosocial and emotional trauma in terms of feeling secluded in family or neighbourhood, treatment related anxiety, fear of death, etc. Being a disease largely prevalent in the low and middle income countries (LMICs), these issues are quite overburdening and are further compounded by poor socioeconomic conditions.[ ] Hence, upfront PC or EIPC could help important to address these myriad issues. Based on this hypothesis, we embarked on this study to evaluate whether EIPC improves QoL of cervical cancer patients, deemed fit for radical treatment. This pilot study was initiated to study the impact of EIPC on the QoL in locally advanced cervical cancers treated with curative intent chemo- radiotherapy.

## Materials and Methods
The aim of this prospective pilot study was to evaluate the impact of EIPC on QoL of cervical cancer patients treated with curative intent.

## Study setting
This study was conducted in a tertiary care centre since January 2018 where patients with locally advanced cervical cancer, planned for treatment with radical chemoradiation, and brachytherapy were enrolled. Patients with the following criteria were included (i) locally advanced cases of cancer cervix-staged IB2 to IIIB, as per FIGO 2015 staging,[ ] (ii) ECOG performance status 0–2,[ ] (iii) no previous pelvic radiotherapy, (iv) no previous exposure to chemotherapy within 2 years from registration, and (v) absence of any uncontrolled systemic ailments or derangements.

## Study design and data collection
Patients were randomized into EIPC arm (Arm A) and SOC arm of concurrent chemoradiation (Arm B) by a computer-generated table of random numbers. QoL was assessed by the FACT-G questionnaire. The English and Hindi translated version (appendix) validated for use by from FACIT organization were used.[ ] The FACT-G is a patient-reported outcome measure used to assess health-related QoL in patients undergoing cancer therapy. The FACT-G Version 4 has 27 questions, each of which is answered using a 5-point Likert scale ranging from 0 (Not at all) to 4 (Very much). Questions are phrased so that higher numbers indicate a better health state, leading to some items being reverse scored. Questions measure the respondents’ health state over the last 7 days in four subscales: physical well-being (PWB; 7 questions), social/family well-being (SWB; 7 questions), emotional well-being (EWB; 6 questions), and functional well-being (FWB; 7 questions). Scoring the FACT-G is performed through a simple sum of item scores, multiplied by the total number of questions in each sub-scale and then divided by the number of items answered. Each subscale is scored, with a score range 0–28 for PWB. SWB, FWB, and 0–24 for EWB, and a total score for the FACT-G is obtained by adding each of the subscale scores (score range 0–108). The higher the score, the better the QoL. QoL was assessed twice in both the arms. The first assessment was done prior to start of treatment and second assessment was done at 3 months after treatment completion.
Our gynaecological oncology unit and PC units are housed in the same out- patient complex. The PC team consists of PC specialist doctor, dedicated nurse, and social worker. Patients in the EPIC arm were referred to palliative clinic for registration prior to initiation of treatment. Usually, visits to PC unit were scheduled every 2 weeks; however additional visits were permitted depending upon patients’ wishes and/or PC specialist discretion. The PC specialist took special care to address the physical needs (pain medication etc.), emotional, social and psychological needs of these patients. Various coping strategies were suggested to patients and their caregivers to help them deal with the illness better. Patients assigned to SOC were allowed to visit the PC unit only if the treating clinician decided so.

## Sample size
A sample size of convenience was chosen for this study. Based on the average number of cervical cancer patients registered per month at our OPD, we planned to recruit a total of 100 patients in this study, 50 in each arm.

## Data analysis
Baseline demographics like age, menopausal status, marital status, parity, etc., were compared between the two arms by simple descriptive statistics. The mean scores on physical, mental, emotional, social/family and functional well-being subscales of FACT-G scale were calculated in the two arms in the above time frames. Wilcoxon test was used to evaluate the differences in QoL scores within and in between the arms. A P value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Ethical concern
This trial was approved by Institute Ethics Committee and was registered with Clinical Trials Registry of India (CTRI) vide CTRI/2017/05/008704. A written informed consent was taken from all patients prior to enrolment in this study.

## Results
Fifty patients were enrolled in each arm. Of these, 42 and 45 patients in the EPIC and SOC arm were evaluable as shown in the consort diagram [ ]. The median age was 49 years (range 27–66 years) and median age of marriage was 21 years (range 16–33 years). 53 patients (60.9%) were postmenopausal. Most patients (59, 67.8%) belonged to low socio-economic background (income, education and occupation were assessed to stratify socio-economic classes). Squamous cell carcinoma was the most common histology (78, 89.7%) followed by adenocarcinoma (7, 8.0%). The commonest stage was IIB (34, 39%) followed by IIIB (30, 34.4%).

## Discussion
The present study reports the effect of EIPC on QoL issues in locally advanced cervical cancer. As compared to SOC, EIPC has a favourable impact on QoL of patients, particularly their social and emotional well-being. EIPC has been an active area of research in last few years. The burden of incurable cancers extends beyond the physical agony and it is only natural to incorporate PC as soon as possible in management of such patients. The body of evidence is quite meagre as of now; however, the results have been promising so far.
The first high quality randomized study investigating the benefit of early PC was done by Temel et al.[ ] in 2010 in metastatic non-small cell lung cancer patients. It reported a significant improvement in QoL scores on FACT-Lung scale with EIPC. The ENABLE (Educate, Nurture, Advise, Before Life Ends) trials in 2004 and 2013 demonstrated the feasibility and benefit with telehealth early PC model.[ , ] Following these footsteps, the ENABLE III randomized trial was conducted between two arms- PC and SOC within 1-2 months of being informed of an advanced cancer diagnosis, cancer recurrence, or progression [with a prognosis between 6 and 24 months] (i.e., early group) versus the same instituted 3 months later (i.e., delayed group). The authors reported no significant differences in QoL and mood of patients or resource utilization; however, survival 1-year after enrolment was improved in early group compared with the delayed group.[ ] Though we did not report survival in this study, but our study showed a positive impact on QoL with EIPC in advanced cervical cancer. In a recent trial evaluating EIPC in advanced cancer with a life expectancy of around 1 year, the authors concluded EIPC improved QoL near the end of life too.[ ]
The appropriate time to introduce PC was investigated in a retrospective study on incurable pancreatic cancers. The author suggested that PC consultations need to be sought early in the course of illness rather than late in the trajectory.[ ] The most quoted study of recent years comes from Temel et al. where the impact of EIPC on improvement in QoL was proven beyond doubt. The study also showed that EIPC can alleviate depression and facilitate better understanding and communication with physicians.[ ] The mechanism by which EIPC improves these outcomes was investigated in a subsequent study next year. Greer et al.[ ] demonstrated that EIPC significantly increased patient use of approach-oriented coping strategies slightly reduced use of avoidant strategies. One of the recent studies from Belgium again showed modest improvement in QoL of patients with survival less than a year with EPIC.[ ] Several other reviews have also supported this premise.[ ] The unifying feature of all these studies on EIPC is that they have all dealt with incurable cancers. Our study stands unique in this regard since it deals with curable cervical malignancies. Our pre-treatment scores in all domains were higher than post treatment scores. This is likely due to the effect of treatment rather than EIPC. The complexity of the gamut of non-physical issues faced by women from mostly rural background led us to believe that radiotherapy treatment alone may address the disease, but not the patient as a whole. We could not afford the much-needed elaborate discussions on patient’s anxieties and insecurities in a busy OPD. Hence, we sought the help of our palliative unit to reach out to our patients better. Patients were sent to palliative clinic right from the time of registration and they continued visits to same during weekly radiotherapy check-ups. After few months of such collaboration, we found that the patients were more settled and competent to carry through the treatment. We wanted to quantify the results of such conjoined effort, formally termed as EIPC. This is how this study was conceptualised.
Our results show significant betterment in social and emotional well-being subscales of QoL with EIPC. The study by Temel et al.[ ] have also recorded better overall QoL scores and lesser depression in patients receiving EIPC. Similarly, the results of other studies on EIPC also echo with our results. We have demonstrated the results of subscales to evaluate the effects of EIPC on them individually. A total score of FACT-G would have given a composite idea and overlooked this interesting finding. The lack of significant difference in physical well-being subscale (PWB) is probably due to the effects of curative treatment which was same for both arms. Quite naturally, this translated into lack of significant difference in functional well-being (FWB) too.
Our study has quite a few limitations. We have not correlated the effect of stage, socio-economic status, care giver support, etc., on QoL. Our study is also underpowered to come to definite conclusions.

## Conclusions
It is a feasibility study done in an attempt to test the validity of EIPC in curable cancers. This study highlights the positive impact of EIPC in locally advanced cervical cancer which has led to better QoL, especially emotional and social well-being. The results are inspiring to conduct robust studies in the same line and also assess whether it impacts the survival in the long-term. Physicians treating patients with advanced cancer and/or those with a significant symptom burden should consider referring patients to PC early in their treatment course. The significance of this study lies in exploring a new area of EIPC, hitherto unknown.
