## Background:
In 2023, medical assistance in dying (MAiD) accounted for 4.7% of all deaths in Canada. Little is known about the role or attitudes of surgeons in this practice. This study aimed to assess surgeon: 1) experience with MAiD, 2) knowledge of eligibility criteria, 3) attitudes toward MAiD in ethically complex scenarios, and 4) interest in participating in MAiD assessment and/or provision.

## Methods:
Following ethics approval, this cross-sectional study was conducted via an anonymous online survey distributed to 1040 practising staff surgeons at the University of Toronto (November 21, 2024, to February 7, 2025).

## Results:
The response rate was 11.4% (n = 119), primarily from general surgery (31.9%, n = 38), obstetrics and gynaecology (23.5%, n = 28), and orthopaedic surgery (10.1%, n = 12). Nearly half (49%, n = 58) of surveyed surgeons had been approached by patients requesting MAiD, typically responding with referrals to hospital MAiD teams (27.2%, n = 33) or other physicians (14.3%, n = 17). While 31.9% (n = 38) of surgeons self-reported at least “good” knowledge of MAiD eligibility criteria and 37.8% (n = 45) reported at least “good” knowledge of the assessment process, 74.8% (n = 89) answered a multiple-choice question assessing eligibility criteria for patients with terminal illness (Track 1) and those with non-terminal illness (Track 2) incorrectly. Surgeons were more likely to support MAiD in scenarios describing Track 1 patients than those describing Track 2 patients. Regarding MAiD discussions, 44.5% (n = 53) supported initiating conversations, while 29.4% (n = 35) opposed this practice, citing concerns around coercion and inadequate training. With additional training, 29.4% (n = 35) of respondents expressed interest in becoming a MAiD assessor and 25.4% (n = 28) indicated they would consider providing MAiD.

## Conclusion:
These findings highlight the evolving role of surgeons in MAiD, revealing knowledge gaps and varying comfort levels among them. While concerns about coercion and inadequate training exist, a notable proportion of surgeons are open to greater MAiD involvement, suggesting the need for enhanced education and institutional support.

## Background:
Laparoscopic cholecystectomy remains one of the procedures most commonly performed by general surgeons in Canada. Significant complications after cholecystectomy are rare but can have devastating consequences on the patient, surgeon, and healthcare system. This is especially true for common bile duct injuries (BDIs). This study aimed to provide a 20-year review and examination of trends in Canadian medicolegal data related to postcholecystectomy complaints.

## Methods:
This was a 20-year retrospective review of the Canadian Medical Protective Association’s (CMPA) repository of medicolegal cases involving cholecystectomy-related complaints between 2003 and 2022. Cases included all those in which the physician contacted the CMPA for assistance, including civil legal actions, medical regulatory complaints, and hospital complaints. Data analysis was limited to descriptive statistics using SAS software.

## Results:
A total of 488 cases were closed, with initial events occurring from 1991 to 2021. There was a trend of decreasing complaints during the study period (p = 0.0004), with the majority of patients identifying as female (66%) and being generally healthy (ASA grade I/II 73.6%). The top 5 complications leading to complaint initiation were BDI (35.9%), leak (15.2%), bowel injury (12.7%), sepsis (10.9%), and hemorrhage (10.2%). A total of 555 physicians were involved, with 484 operating surgeons named in complaints. Most surgeons (70%) had been in practice for 11 years or more. The mean time from incident to case closure was 63.2 ± 42.3 months. Cases of BDI were significantly more likely to result in a favourable medicolegal outcome for the patient (77%) compared to all cholecystectomy cases (54%).

## Conclusion:
This study demonstrated that the majority of postcholecystectomy complaints involved generally healthy female patients. Unsurprisingly, BDI was the most common complication resulting in a complaint, and these cases were more likely to result in a successful claim for the patient. Surgeons later in practice were more likely to be named in a complaint than their colleagues early in practice.

## Background:
Compared with the traditional open approach, minimally invasive vascular surgery techniques may offer benefits to patients undergoing lower extremity arterial reconstruction. We sought to determine whether endoscopically assisted lower extremity bypass for chronic limb-threatening ischemia is as safe and effective as traditional approaches.

## Methods:
From 2021 to 2024, we performed a retrospective cohort study of endoscopically assisted femoropopliteal or femorotibial bypass in 10 patients (mean age 72.4 years; 3 female) in an academic hospital in Ontario, Canada. Patients were selected based on the presence of Rutherford grade 4 to 6 ischemia and anatomic suitability for in situ bypass, age over 18 years, and ability to tolerate surgery; no patients withdrew from the study. We recorded patient demographics such as medical comorbidities (e.g., diabetes, coronary artery disease). We also documented outcomes (e.g., 1-year amputation-free survival, major adverse cardiac or limb events, major revascularization). Follow-up with arterial duplex scanning was performed at 1 month and 3 months, and then yearly after surgery; data were collected up to 1 year after surgery.

## Results:
Nine of the 10 patients were amputation-free at 1 year, 6 achieved primary patency at 1 year, all 10 achieved primary-assisted patency at 1 year, and there were no deaths within 1 year of surgery.

## Conclusion:
Our outcomes suggest that endoscopically assisted lower extremity bypass for chronic limb-threatening ischemia is safe and effective.

## Background:
The early postoperative period is critical for detecting complications in emergency general surgery (EGS) patients, yet little research has examined postoperative healthcare utilization in this population. Immigrants may be particularly vulnerable owing to language barriers, limited healthcare access, and cultural factors that affect postoperative care. This study examined postoperative length of stay (LOS), emergency department (ED) visits, readmissions, reoperations, and mortality among immigrant and non-immigrant EGS patients in British Columbia, Canada.

## Methods:
Emergency general surgery procedures in British Columbia between 2012 and 2020 were analyzed using population-based linked immigration and hospital discharge summary data. Postoperative care LOS, ED visits within 30 days of discharge, odds of readmission and reoperation within 30 days of discharge, and 1-year mortality were compared between immigrants and non-immigrants. Subanalyses examined years since immigration and ethnic minority status.

## Results:
Of 75 545 EGS procedures, 15.6% were performed in immigrants. Immigrants had shorter LOS (relative risk [RR] 0.71, 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.70–0.73) and fewer ED visits (RR 0.75, 95% CI 0.71–0.83) but higher odds of readmission (odds ratio [OR] 1.13, 95% CI 1.07–1.19), reoperation (OR 1.39, 95% CI 1.21–1.59), and mortality (OR 1.12, 95% CI 1.03–1.22). Recent immigrants and visible minorities had even lower LOS and ED utilization.

## Conclusion:
Immigrants undergoing EGS experience shorter hospital stays and lower ED utilization but face higher risks of readmission, reoperation, and mortality. These findings suggest potential disparities in postoperative care and highlight the need for targeted interventions to improve discharge planning and postoperative communication for vulnerable immigrant populations.

## Background:
Timely access to surgery remains a significant barrier to care across healthcare systems, with delays leading to prolonged wait times, increased hospital stays, and healthcare costs. Semi-urgent surgical programs may offer a benefit by managing cases requiring timely intervention without emergent admission. Such programs have the potential to improve patient outcomes and experience, and augment value-based care. This scoping review reports on semi-urgent programs across surgical disciplines in order to highlight benefits to such models as well as barriers to implementation.

## Methods:
A systematic search identified 4106 studies using terms related to semi-urgent pathologies, procedures, and surgical programs across surgical subspecialities, yielding 3125 abstracts, 242 full-text articles, and 44 included studies. Data extraction followed a thematic analysis. Themes included implementation models and barriers, workflow optimization, economic efficiency, and patient outcomes.

## Results:
Semi-urgent programs were found to result in reduced hospital stays and improved patient satisfaction. Economic evaluations demonstrated significant cost-effectiveness, with some programs saving up to $1.13 million annually with a dedicated Saturday operating room. Savings per case ranged from $12 923 to $21 866. Barriers to implementation included resource limitations, staffing constraints, and difficulties in standardizing protocols, with inconsistent outcomes from less formalized pathways.

## Conclusion:
Semi-urgent surgical programs have the potential to improve surgical access while optimizing resource utilization. Strategies to overcome barriers to implementation are essential for broader adoption.

AmmeterDerek JohnsonGarrett AskinNicole HelewaRamzi HyunEric HochmanDavid 1From the Max Rady College of Medicine of the University of Manitoba (Ammeter, Johnson, Helewa, Hyun, Hochman); the Neil John Maclean Library of the University of Manitoba (Askin)Nov-Dec2025686suppl2S29S29–S30© 2025 CMA Impact Inc. or its licensorsThis is an Open Access supplement distributed in accordance with the terms of the Creative Commons Attribution (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0) licence, which permits use, distribution and reproduction in any medium, provided that the original publication is properly cited, the use is noncommercial (i.e., research or educational use), and no modifications or adaptations are made. ## Background:
Appendectomy is among the most common emergency abdominal operations. Despite advances in minimally invasive techniques, pain control is a common postoperative concern. Transverse abdominus plane (TAP) block has been proposed as an alternative to port site local anesthetic injection. We synthesized randomized controlled trials (RCTs) comparing efficacy and safety of TAP block versus port site local anesthetic injection in laparoscopic appendectomy.

## Methods:
Relevant databases were searched from inception until December 2024 for RCTs comparing TAP block and port site local anesthetic injection in laparoscopic appendectomy. Two authors independently identified trials, extracted data, and assessed risk of bias (ROB 2.0) and evidence certainty (GRADE).

## Results:
Four RCTs were included. Resting pain scores within the first 24 hours were significantly lower in the TAP block group (standardized mean difference −4.07, 95% confidence interval [CI] −6.78 to −1.37, 4 RCTs, 233 patients, low certainty). Average patient satisfaction scores within the first 24 hours were also significantly better in this group (mean difference [MD] −0.79, 95% CI −1.03 to −0.55, 1 RCT, 80 patients, low certainty). There was no significant difference in total opioid use (MD −4.40 mg morphine equivalent, 95% CI −10.82 to 2.02, 3 RCTs, 153 patients, moderate certainty), length of stay (MD −0.03 days, 95% CI −0.40 to 0.33, 3 RCTs, 177 patients, low certainty) or postoperative nausea and vomiting (relative risk 1.15, 95% CI 0.77–1.71, 3 RCTs, 153 patients, moderate certainty). No adverse reactions to either anesthetic technique were reported in any study. Evidence quality was limited primarily by risk of bias and unexplained statistical heterogeneity.

## Conclusion:
Transverse abdominus plane block is a promising technique to reduce laparoscopic appendectomy pain. Current evidence supports a statistically significant, though modest, improvement in early postoperative pain control and patient satisfaction with TAP block compared with port site local anesthetic. There was no corresponding reduction in total opioids used, hospital length of stay, or postoperative nausea and vomiting.

Canadian Journal of SurgeryCan J Surg07. Use of artificial intelligence versus traditional statistical modelling in predicting anastomotic leakage in patients undergoing colorectal surgery: a scoping review
AnantSwati WadhwaArushi LeeJeremy AuerRebecca FallavollitaPascal 1From McMaster University (Anant); the University of Ottawa (Wadhwa, Lee, Auer, Fallavollita)Nov-Dec2025686suppl2S30S30© 2025 CMA Impact Inc. or its licensorsThis is an Open Access supplement distributed in accordance with the terms of the Creative Commons Attribution (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0) licence, which permits use, distribution and reproduction in any medium, provided that the original publication is properly cited, the use is noncommercial (i.e., research or educational use), and no modifications or adaptations are made. See: https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-nd/4.0/

## Background:
Anastomotic leak (AL) is a devastating complication of colorectal surgery, causing significant morbidity and mortality. Traditional risk assessment models (e.g., logistic regression or nomograms) have identified various risk factors for AL; however, there is no consensus on best models for stratifying AL risk. Recently, artificial intelligence (AI) models have shown promise in accurately predicting AL by analyzing large patient data sets. This study aimed to compare the performance of traditional and AI-based predictive models for AL and guide future development of AI-driven surgical decision-making tools for AL.

## Methods:
MEDLINE and Embase databases were searched from database inception to March 2024 for observational cohort studies outlining the development and/or validation of conventional or AI-based risk prediction models for AL following colorectal surgery. Primary outcomes included the model performance through area under the curve (AUC), concordance-index, sensitivity, and specificity, while secondary outcomes focused on the specific risk factors, calibration, and power of studies. Data extraction followed the CHARMS checklist.

## Results:
In all, 1882 abstracts were reviewed, and 53 studies were included for analysis. Eight studies (15%) focused on AI-based models, while 45 studies (85%) described traditional predictive models for AL. Models based on AI used more predictors per model than traditional models, but this was not statistically significant (21 v. 6, p = 0.24). Among AI-based models, the highest AUC observed was 0.92 (95% confidence interval [CI] 0.91–0.93), attained through an artificial neural network. Frequently assessed variables included patient ASA status, body mass index, age, sex, and operative time. Among traditional models, the highest observed AUC was 0.95 (95% CI 0.91–0.93) using a logistic regression analysis. Commonly included prognostic variables were male sex, ASA grade, and body mass index.

## Conclusion:
Artificial intelligence models have demonstrated promising reliability and accuracy for predicting AL using comprehensive patient cohorts. Further research is needed to externally validate models and establish widespread applicability for AL risk stratification.

## Background:
Current evidence suggests that, during colonoscope insertion, water assistance is a superior technique to air insufflation when performed by experienced endoscopists. Little is known about trainee performance. The purpose of this randomized controlled clinical trial was to compare outcomes of the 2 methods during colonoscope insertion when performed by general surgery residents.

## Methods:
Senior residents at 1 Canadian centre were eligible to participate in the study. Each patient was randomly assigned to receive either water infusion or air insufflation during colonoscope insertion. The primary outcome was cecal intubation rate, and secondary outcomes were time to cecal intubation, transverse colon intubation rate, time to transverse colon intubation, patient comfort level, sedation dosing requirement, and complication rate. After each procedure, residents completed a 4-question survey rating the difficulty of the procedure, their own performance, their perception of the patient’s comfort, and their overall satisfaction with the learning experience.

## Results:
A total of 142 colonoscopies were completed. The overall cecal intubation rate was 55% (n = 78). The cecum was intubated in 35/57 (61%) water infusion cases and in 43/85 (51%) air insufflation cases (p = 0.204). There were no significant differences between intubation methods for any of the clinical secondary outcomes. Water infusion was associated with superior resident-reported outcomes in all 4 domains: difficulty of scope (95% confidence interval [CI] 0.083–0.852; p = 0.018), resident performance (95% CI 0.122–0.669; p = 0.005), patient comfort (95% CI 0.115–0.969; p = 0.013), and satisfaction with learning experience (95% CI 0.119–0.619; p = 0.004).

## Conclusion:
This study did not show a superior cecal intubation rate with water infusion compared to air insufflation during colonoscope insertion when performed by general surgery residents. There was a trend favouring water for most clinical outcomes. All resident-reported outcomes were significantly improved with water infusion.

## Background:
Acute uncomplicated appendicitis is traditionally managed with emergent surgery. Owing to operating room and resource availability restraints, patients may have delays to operation. The literature on the impact of these delays remains equivocal, with emerging evidence suggesting small delays may not adversely affect outcomes. The aim of this systematic review and meta-analysis was to assess postoperative outcomes of emergent versus delayed appendectomy.

## Methods:
A comprehensive search of Ovid MEDLINE, Ovid Embase, Scopus, Web of Science Core Collection, and Cochrane Library (via Wiley) was performed on January 15, 2024. Studies were included if they contained cohorts of emergent versus delayed appendectomy, outcomes presented as binomial data, and predominantly adult populations, and were of prospective design. We used the following definition of emergent versus delayed appendectomy: < 4 to 12 hour or overnight versus > 4 to 12 hour or next day. The primary outcome was intraoperative complication rate, and secondary outcomes included postoperative abscess, surgical site infection (SSI), conversion to open, and length of stay. A random-effects model with restricted maximum likelihood was used.

## Results:
Among 11 232 unique records, 5 studies were included, of which 3 were prospective cohort studies and 2 were randomized controlled trials. A total of 4250 patients were included, of whom 1928 were female (mean age 36.81 years ± 15.19 years). Results revealed no significant difference in the incidence of intraoperative perforation (odds ratio [OR] 1.57, 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.93–2.61, p = 0.09), abscess (OR 1.54, 95% CI 0.58–4.10, p = 0.39), SSI (OR 1.35, 95% CI 0.71–2.56, p = 0.36), or conversion to open (OR 0.81, 95% CI 0.64–1.03, p = 0.09).

## Conclusion:
These results suggest a window of safety during which delays to the operating room do not significantly affect complication rates. This may help guide decision-making in resource-limited settings.

## Background:
Plant-rich diets are optimal for individual and planetary health, but their adoption in healthcare settings has been limited, in part by perceptions of patient preferences and aversions. We aimed to design an inpatient menu for a tertiary care hospital that decreased the environmental impacts associated with food served, and then evaluated its acceptability for inpatients during a hospital-wide implementation.

## Methods:
An interdisciplinary team created novel dishes for lunch and dinner incorporating exclusively plant-sourced or low-impact animal proteins. Potential dishes were evaluated based on nutritional adequacy, operational feasibility, and acceptability. The successful novel dishes were integrated into a new menu, which was compared with the original menu with respect to protein diversity, nutritional adequacy, greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions, and land use. During the novel menu implementation (October 2023 to March 2024), for all new dishes served, meal uptake was measured, in-person patient surveys were conducted, and food waste was estimated using visual plate waste audits.

## Results:
Of 41 novel recipes, 19 were selected for menu integration. On the new menu, the proportion of plant-based protein dishes offered as default options increased from 0% to 40%, with no significant difference in nutritional content. The new menu had 39% lower GHG emissions and 51% lower land use impacts. During the implementation, 157 dishes were evaluated, 2929 patient surveys were administered, and 55 231 trays were assessed for food waste. On average, new dishes were served to 57.8% to 72.6% of inpatients, and 83.7% of patients found the dishes acceptable or better. Food waste was significantly lower with new dishes (p < 0.001).

## Conclusion:
We demonstrated patient acceptability of an inpatient menu that significantly improved food-related environmental impacts, met nutritional standards for hospitalized patients, and was amenable to production within existing operational limits. Incorporating more plant-rich menus in hospitals can therefore meaningfully contribute to healthcare decarbonization.

## Background:
As global surgery emerges as an academic field, there is a growing need for consensus-driven learning objectives to guide education and training. Existing curricula vary widely, and previous efforts to define learning objectives have been geographically limited or lacked multidisciplinary input. This study aimed to achieve international consensus on core learning objectives for global surgery education.

## Methods:
A modified Delphi consensus study was conducted with an international panel of global surgery experts. A scoping review informed an initial list of learning objectives, categorized into 14 domains based on the Consortium of Universities for Global Health (CUGH) framework. Panellists rated objectives over 3 iterative survey rounds, with consensus defined as ≥ 80% agreement within ± 1 Likert point of the median.

## Results:
Sixty-one experts from 26 countries across all World Health Organization regions participated, representing surgery (40.1%), anesthesiology (14.8%), obstetrics and gynecology (14.8%), general practitioners with and without enhanced surgical skills (16.4%), and allied health fields (6.6%). Across 3 Delphi rounds, 120 learning objectives reached consensus, covering key domains such as the global burden of surgical disease, surgical system strengthening, ethics and equity, health policy, and sustainable development. Three novel CUGH domains — Institutionalization and Sustainable Development, Planetary Health, and Decolonizing Global Health — were integrated into the framework.

## Conclusion:
This Delphi consensus provides a structured, globally relevant framework for global surgery education, supporting curriculum development and competency-based training. These findings underscore the importance of aligning global surgery education with evolving healthcare priorities while ensuring adaptability across diverse educational settings.

## Background:
Understanding surgeons’ perspectives on post-discharge opioid prescribing is crucial for developing strategies to optimize pain management while mitigating opioid-related harms. Thus, the objective of this study was to explore the perceptions, attitudes, and beliefs of general surgeons practising in North America on opioid prescribing after postoperative hospital discharge.

## Methods:
This was a qualitative descriptive study involving 1-to-1 semi-structured interviews conducted between July 2021 and July 2023. General surgeons from academic and non-academic centres across the US and Canada were purposefully selected for maximum variation in geographic, demographic, and practice characteristics. Interviews were audio-recorded, transcribed verbatim, and continued until thematic saturation.
