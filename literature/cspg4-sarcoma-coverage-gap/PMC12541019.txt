To view a copy of this licence, visit http://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-nd/4.0/.In the placenta, fetal-derived pericytes wrap the villous capillaries, directly interacting with endothelial cells to orchestrate the branching angiogenesis required to meet the demands of the growing fetus. In animal models of preeclampsia (PE), there is a positive correlation between reduced αSMA-expressing pericyte coverage and reduced vascular branching. Transforming Growth Factor β-1 (TGFβ-1) signalling is critical to placental development, is altered in placental pathology and alters pericyte function in other organs. However, the factors that influence placental pericyte function are under-investigated. In the present study, we investigated the in vitro effects of TGFβ-1 signalling on the population dynamics and functional measures of isolated term human placental pericytes, including angiogenic and inflammatory secretions, extracellular matrix (ECM) production, and phagocytic capacity. TGFβ-1 treatment promoted a proangiogenic phenotype with increased pro-angiogenic secretion of VEGFA and MMP-2 and reduced vessel stabilizing secretion of (ANG-1), without affecting the production of ECM components. Pericyte secretion of inflammation-associated adhesion molecule sVCAM-1, cytokine IL-6, chemokine MCP-1, and their phagocytosis capacity were attenuated with TGFβ-1 treatment. While some effects were mediated via the type I receptor ALK5, others were not, suggesting that TGFβ-1 signalling in placental pericytes may additionally occur via ALK1. Thus, it is likely that locally secreted TGFβ-1, contributes to the regulation of villous pericyte properties and their barrier function, and may implicate dysregulated TGFβ-1 signalling as a mechanism for the compromised placental fetal vascular branching observed in many placental pathologies. These findings are timely with emerging evidence that TGFβ is involved in the pathogenesis of PE.

## Introduction
Pericyte cells are mural cells that interact with the vascular endothelium, playing a pivotal role in the formation and maintenance of blood vessels . Their dysfunction is implicated in vasculature-related diseases , . While pericytes play a role in vascular dynamics, their distribution and density vary among organs and vascular beds . In the fetal-derived placenta, the villi are the site of nutrient and waste exchange between the mother and the developing fetus . They are comprised of a mesenchymal core containing pericyte-wrapped endothelial cells that make up fetal blood vessels, and two layers of trophoblast cells (villous cytotrophoblast and syncytiotrophoblast), with the syncytiotrophoblast in direct contact with maternal blood . In the context of villous tree development and placental pathology, most studies have focused on the endothelial and trophoblast populations, with pericytes notably less investigated.
The continuum of mural cell phenotypes extending from terminal arterioles through post-capillary venules, along with the overlapping expression of genes, has made it tempting to speculate that mural cells may differentiate into one another as the vessels grow and remodel . Vascular sprouting involves endothelial cell proliferation, migration and contribution to the formation of new endothelial tubes, followed by the recruitment of pericytes to stabilize the vessel . Importantly, while pericytes proliferate during angiogenesis, the extent to which new pericytes develop by (1) division of pre-existing pericytes, (2) recruitment (proliferation, migration and differentiation) from the pre-existing vascular smooth muscle cells associated with a nearby larger vessel or (3) differentiation from immature mesenchyme has not been elucidated in most tissue . As poor pericyte coverage of vessels can increase vessel dilation, contribute to vessel instability and negatively impact blood barrier function , it is logical to presume that compromised pericyte coverage or function could significantly impact placental health. Unfortunately, in vivo analysis of pericytes in human placental pathologies is limited, with reduced pericyte coverage of fetal-derived vessels described only in placentas from pregnancies affected by gestational diabetes . However, in rodents, our investigations of placenta samples collected from mouse models of preeclampsia (PE) and fetal growth restriction (FGR) , revealed compromised alpha-smooth muscle actin (αSMA)-expressing pericyte coverage and impaired fetal vessel expansion , , with similar findings reported in pregnancy-associated hypertension models – and a Streptozotocin mouse model of diabetes . Supporting these findings, pericyte dropout is associated with restricted vessel growth in human 3D models of placental microvasculature .
Pericytes express proangiogenic factors, including vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGFA) , and matrix metalloproteinase-2 (MMP-2) , . Vascular growth and vessel stabilization are regulated through angiopoietin-Tie receptor signalling , , . Specifically, vessel stabilization is mediated by pericyte-secreted angiopoietin-1 (ANG-1), which maintains vascular quiescence, suppresses plasma leakage, inhibits vascular inflammation, and prevents endothelial cell death . Conversely, during new vessel growth, ANG-1 interactions are disrupted by endothelial-secreted angiopoietin-2 (ANG-2) . In the presence of VEGF, this leads to pericyte dissociation , initiating vessel sprouting. However, in the absence of VEGF, it leads to vessel regression . We recently demonstrated that placental pericyte secretion of proangiogenic factors is influenced by oxygen concentration, with VEGFA secretion increased and ANG-1 reduced in low oxygen tension , suggesting that alterations to the placental microenvironment affect pericyte function. However, it remains unknown which physiological and pathological factors contribute to pericyte dropout and/or compromised function.
Among the many factors and pathways critical to the development of the placenta is the transforming growth factor β-1 (TGFβ-1) signalling cascade , which is important for regulating extravillous trophoblast cell differentiation and establishing maternal tolerance . Later in pregnancy, it regulates villous vascularization and angiogenesis . The mechanisms by which TGFβ-1 signalling alters the function of trophoblast and villous endothelial cells are well described , but remain unexplored in placental pericytes. Moreover, TGFβ-1 altered the function of brain pericytes and fidelity of kidney and retinal pericytes , , suggesting that it may similarly affect placental pericytes. As TGFβ-1 is upregulated in PE placentas , – , and our animal model PE studies report alterations to both the placental fetal capillaries and pericyte coverage, we questioned whether TGFβ-1 may alter properties of placental pericytes. TGFβ-1 binds to a heteromeric receptor complex, consisting of a type I and type II receptor . Pericytes in other organs express the type I receptor ALK5 , with activation by TGFβ-1 triggering the phosphorylation of SMAD2 and SMAD3, leading to their nuclear translocation and the transcriptional regulation of target genes , . Interestingly, specific deletion of the type II receptor Tgfbr2 and Alk5 in mouse mural cells leads to embryonic lethality between E12.5 and E16.5, which is slightly later than in conventional knockout mice . Thus, embryonic lethality, during the window of placental vascular expansion, supports the possibility that TGFβ-1 signalling via ALK5 may be important for placental pericytes.
The vascular basement membrane is composed of layers of complex extracellular matrix (ECM) proteins that form a sheet-like support for the endothelial cells, separating them from the underlying tissue , . As part of the vessel stabilization process, pericytes secrete ECM components, collagen , laminin , , and fibronectin . While the placental pericyte contribution to the villous vascular basement membrane has not been explored, corresponding changes to the ECM components and pericyte coverage are reported in placentas from animal models of maternal diseases of pregnancy , , . ECM production, including the synthesis of collagens and fibronectin in some cell types, is regulated by TGFβ , but whether this is the case in placental pericytes is unknown. Supporting the possibility is that TGFβ-1 stimulation in human cardiac pericytes induced expression of Col1a2 , the gene that encodes the pro-alpha2 chain of type I collagen.
While unexplored in placental pericytes, the inflammatory phenotype of brain pericytes is attenuated by TGFβ-1 treatment . Brain pericytes display macrophage-like, non-professional antigen-presenting cell characteristics , including immune properties like pinocytosis, phagocytosis and immunomodulatory potential , supporting the idea that pericytes participate in immune response and contribute to the removal of toxic cellular by-products . While human placental pericytes poorly stimulate allogeneic cluster of differentiation (CD)4 T cell responses (relative to endothelial cells), it has been proposed that their cytokine and chemokine secretion (reviewed in ) may be necessary for potentiating inflammatory responses and recruiting immune cells to the site of inflammation.
Thus, as TGFβ-1 signalling is established in other pericyte populations , , , we questioned whether it would alter placental pericyte angiogenic function. Furthermore, with evidence supporting TGFβ-1 modulation of the brain pericyte inflammatory phenotype , we questioned whether placental pericytes also have an inflammatory phenotype modulated by TGFβ-1. Therefore, this study aimed to confirm TGFβ-1/ALK5 signalling and investigate its impact on placental pericyte proliferation, angiogenic and inflammatory secretion, phagocytosis, and ECM production.

## Results

## Placental pericytes heterogeneously express pericyte markers
Commercially available, isolated human term placental pericytes are isolated from tissue samples based on CD31-/CD34-/CD105+/CD146+ expression (Promocell), ensuring that the purified cells are free from endothelial or smooth muscle cell contamination. However, with no single unique marker for pericyte cells, validated pericyte markers were assessed, including: platelet-derived growth factor receptor-beta (PDGFRβ), a receptor involved in pericyte recruitment during angiogenesis , ; chondroitin sulfate proteoglycan 4/ neural-glial antigen 2 (CSPG4/NG2), involved in pericyte recruitment during vascular morphogenesis ; and αSMA, a structural protein expressed in capillary junctional pericytes but not quiescent pericytes . Nestin was also included, as its expression reportedly differentiates type-2 pericytes that attach to the walls of small and large blood vessels, and, in vitro, can induce endothelial cells to form new vessels, whereas type-1 pericytes do not. Like pericytes from other organs , – , when grown in monolayer culture, isolated human term placental pericytes exhibit heterogeneous morphology and expression of pericyte markers. Specifically, some pericytes had elongated projections, while others had none (Fig.  a). Pericytes expressed ACTA2/αSMA, PDGFRB/PDGFRβ, and CSPG4/NG2 (Fig.  b and c). They were homogeneously positive for PDGFRβ and Nestin (NES) and heterogeneously positive for αSMA and NG2 (Fig.  c), suggesting that the population was predominantly comprised of type II pericytes. Importantly, while most cells were NG2 positive, its localization differed among cells (Fig.  c). As NG2 is associated with directional migration in other cell types and is found concentrated at the leading edge of migrating cells , it is possible that the differential localization identified migrating cells . Importantly, as studies suggest that pericytes can differentiate into adipocytes , osteoblasts , fibroblasts , , and phagocytes, pericyte differentiation was considered. Pericyte differentiation into adipocytes requires an adipogenic stimulus , and soft tissue pericytes do not readily differentiate into osteoblasts . Furthermore, pericyte differentiation into fibroblast cells requires differentiation of human pluripotent stem cells into pericytes, followed by a secondary differentiation into fibroblast cells in commercially available fibroblast culture medium , . Finally, while it is noted that pericytes can differentiate into phagocytes, evidence suggests that pericytes, in fact, possess phagocytic ability . Thus, the sorting selection, combined with our morphological and histological assessments and the absence of factors required for pericyte differentiation into adipocytes or fibroblast cells, provides strong evidence that the cells were pericytes. 

## Increased pSMAD2 levels in placental pericytes confirm TGFβ-1 signalling via canonical receptor ALK5
Because TGFβ-1 signals via Type I receptor ALK5, in developing brain pericytes , placental pericyte expression of the Type I and Type II receptors TGFBR1/ALK5 (ICC/IF and RT-PCR) and TGFBR2 (RT-PCR; Fig.  a and b) was confirmed. To assess TGFβ-1 signalling in placental pericytes, a concentration of 10 ng/mL was selected, as this concentration has been shown to effectively alter brain pericyte function . To confirm that TGFβ-1 signalled via ALK5, RepSox, the TGFβR1 kinase (ALK5) inhibitor II (CAS 446859-33-2; Calbiochem) , was used as it has been previously shown to inhibit TGFβ-1 signalling via ALK5 . 2 M of ALK5 inhibitor was the concentration determined to effectively inhibit TGFβ-1 signalling in placental pericytes without affecting viability or proliferation. Confirming TGFβ-1 signalling, phosphorylated (p)SMAD2 was elevated in TGFβ-1-treated pericytes (p = 0.0019), while TGFβ-1 + ALK5 inhibition attenuated phosphorylation of SMAD2 (Fig.  c and d). Cell counts and Ki67 immunofluorescent staining were used as measures of cell health and viability. Neither TGFβ-1 treatment nor TGFβ-1 + ALK5 inhibition significantly altered cell number or the percentage of Ki67-positive cells (Supplemental Fig.  a and b). 

## TGFβ-1 signalling altered the secretion of angiogenic factors from placental pericytes
Expression of pericyte markers was assessed to determine whether TGFβ-1 signalling altered their expression or fidelity. To account for the fact that cell morphology can change the intensity of fluorescent staining, both the area and the mean fluorescent intensity (MFI) of each marker were assessed and normalized to cell number. TGFβ-1 treatment did not significantly alter the area or MFI of Nestin (Supplemental Figs. and a), αSMA (Supplemental Figs. and b), or NG2 (Supplemental Figs. and c). Noteworthy is that TGFβ-1 + ALK5 inhibition significantly increased the NG2 staining area (p = 0.0298) and non-significantly reduced the MFI compared with the TGFβ-1 treatment group. As migrating cells are reported to have concentrated NG2 at their leading edge , it is suggested that reduced area and increased MFI in the TGFβ-1 + ALK5 inhibition group (Supplemental Fig.  ) were likely related to their capacity to migrate. Conversely, TGFβ-1 treatment resulted in a trending reduction in PDGFRβ staining area (p = 0.0582) and a non-significant increase in MFI (Supplemental Figs. and d). However, as all cells remained PDGFRβ-positive, it was concluded that the difference was likely due to a TGFβ-1-induced shift in cell morphology, rather than a loss of PDGFRβ and fidelity (Supplemental Figs. and ). 
Thus, with fidelity confirmed, the effect of TGFβ-1 treatment on placental pericyte secretion of VEGFA , and ANG-1 was assessed based on the rationale that pericytes contribute to the angiogenic secretome , , . Furthermore, as TGFβ-1 treatment of placental pericytes has been shown to upregulate the expression of MMP2 , the secretion of total (pro-enzymatic and active forms) proangiogenic MMP-2 and MMP-9 was also assessed, with results normalized to cell number. TGFβ-1 treatment doubled the secretion of VEGFA with trending significance (p = 0.0507; Fig.  a) and tripled total MMP-2 (p = 0.0105; Fig.  b); both in an ALK5-independent manner. Conversely, TGFβ-1 treatment reduced ANG-1 secretion by more than 50% (p = 0.0359; Fig.  c) in an ALK5-dependent manner. Placental pericytes did not express detectable levels of MMP-9.

## TGFβ-1 signalling did not alter ECM production in placental pericytes
Reduced placental vascular collagen IV is associated with reduced pericyte coverage in an animal model of PE , suggesting pericytes may play a role in abnormal ECM composition in pathological placentas. Given that pericytes from other organs produce collagen , laminin , , and fibronectin , with a possible role for TGFβ-1 in ECM production , the effects of TGFβ-1 treatment on ECM production were assessed. ICC was performed for each ECM component (collagen, laminin, and fibronectin) with the area and MFI of ECM normalized to cell number. While the results revealed that placental pericytes expressed collagen IV, fibronectin, and laminin, TGFβ-1 treatment did not alter their production (Fig.  a, b, c and Supplemental Figs.  , , , respectively). 

## TGFβ-1 signalling altered the inflammatory secretome of placental pericytes
Pericytes from other organs are known to secrete inflammatory factors, including cytokines and chemokines, in response to injury or inflammation, which can contribute to the recruitment of immune cells, the disruption of blood barriers and, in a context-dependent manner, aid or disrupt placental angiogenesis , . As TGFβ-1 alters the secretion of immunogenic factors in brain pericytes and suppresses the synthesis of genes encoding proinflammatory proteins in placental pericytes , the secretion of proinflammatory factors (IL-6, IL-8, sVCAM-1, CX3CL1, and MCP-1) was assessed. Results revealed that TGFβ-1 treatment reduced secretion of IL-6 by 36% (p = 0.0319) and MCP-1 by 53% (p = 0.0146), both in an ALK5 independent manner (Fig.  a and b), while sVCAM-1 secretion was attenuated by TGFβ-1 (p = 0.0003) in an ALK5 dependent manner (Fig.  c). Neither CX3CL1 nor IL-8 secretion was altered by TGFβ-1 signalling (Fig.  d and e).

## Placental pericytes displayed potential for multiple TGFβ-1 signalling pathways
TGFβ-1 signals through both ALK5 and ALK1 . However, ALK5 is predominantly expressed in pericytes , and as such, pericyte studies have focused on ALK5 . As the results of this study identified placental pericyte functions altered by TGFβ-1 in both ALK5-dependent and independent manners, the expression of the type I receptor ALK1/ACVRL1 was evaluated to determine whether signalling through an alternate receptor was feasible. RT-PCR analysis revealed that placental pericytes express the type I receptor ALK1/ACVRL1 (Fig.  ), suggesting that TGFβ-1 may also signal via ALK1.

## TGFβ-1 signalling attenuated placental pericyte phagocytosis
Brain pericytes display phagocytic activity , and based on this property, it has been suggested that pericytes function as part of the blood barrier and serve to remove cellular debris and toxic circulating plasma proteins, including immunoglobulins, fibrin and albumin, in response to injury . Because this process is attenuated by TGFβ-1 treatment , the possibility that the function and its regulation are conserved in placental pericytes was explored using flow cytometry and microscopy. Placental pericytes, like brain pericytes, phagocytosed FluoSpheres beads (Fig.  ), with a 42% reduction (p = 0.0500) in the TGFβ-1-treated group (Fig.  a, b, c).

## Discussion
Vascular branching and angiogenesis within the placenta are crucial for the exchange of nutrients and waste between the mother and the fetus , with endothelial and pericyte cell populations playing a key role in vessel growth and stability . Pericytes from other organs have angiogenic, inflammatory and phagocytic properties , and while the angiogenic contribution of placental pericytes relative to other villous populations has been characterized , little is known about their other properties or the factors that regulate them. Given that TGFβ-1 signalling is critical to placental development, altered in pathology, and shown to alter pericyte functions in other organs, we questioned whether TGFβ-1 signalling modulates placental pericyte properties. Building on previous placental pericyte studies that showed oxygen concentration alters their angiogenic secretome , the current study demonstrates that TGFβ-1 promotes a proangiogenic phenotype and inhibits inflammatory and phagocytic properties.
Placental angiogenesis involves the same tightly regulated process as in any other organ . However, when dysregulated, it is the most common pathological characteristic of placentas with pregnancy complications – . Angiogenic sprouting yields new blood vessels from pre-existing ones , , with VEGFA initiating the process by activating the receptor VEGFR2 on vascular endothelial cells, triggering vascular growth and endothelial proliferation , beginning a remodelling process. Specifically, pericytes secrete MMPs that, when active, degrade the vascular basement membrane prior to disengaging from the capillary wall. This allows for the emergence of a sprouting endothelial cell, endothelial proliferation and migration towards an angiogenic stimulus, with guidance by endothelial tip cells or pericytes . This current study demonstrates for the first time that the secretion of two key factors in this process, VEGFA and MMP-2, by placental pericytes is regulated by TGFβ-1 in an ALK5-independent manner, supporting a TGFβ-1-induced proangiogenic phenotype. Whether the MMP-2 was pro-enzymatic or active was not differentiated in this study. However, as most MMPs are secreted in their pro-enzymatic state and require activation, it is reasonable to speculate that this may be the case in placental pericytes . This finding is like that of retinal pericytes, where TGFβ-1 treatment increased the secretion of VEGFA and MMP-3 . Importantly, TGFβ-1 also acts on endothelial cells in a proangiogenic capacity to induce new capillary beds and tube-like structures . After vessel sprouting and angiogenesis, newly formed vessels require maturation and stabilization. Once proliferation stops, endothelial cells adhere to each other, and a lumen is formed. Recruited pericytes, together with endothelial cells, deposit a basement membrane , followed by pericyte secretion of ANG-1 to stabilize the vessel . Thus, the ALK5-dependent reduction of vessel-stabilizing ANG-1 secretion by TGFβ-1, identified in this study, further demonstrates a TGFβ-1-driven proangiogenic phenotype. Although results confirm that placental pericytes, like pericytes from other organs, produce ECM components, this particular vessel-stabilizing property, was not regulated by TGFβ-1. Importantly, the cytotrophoblast, syncytiotrophoblast and Hofbauer cells (placental fetal macrophages) within the placental villi all produce TGFβ-1 , making it tempting to speculate that locally secreted TGFβ-1 contributes to the regulation of both the endothelial and pericyte roles in branching angiogenesis. Whether dysregulation of this signalling pathway in pericytes alone is sufficient to compromise placental vascular branching and downstream fetal growth has not been explored.
Throughout gestation, the placenta produces and responds to inflammatory stimuli . Starting in early pregnancy, the secretory activity of placental cell populations contributes to elevated levels of local and systemic cytokines and inflammatory molecules, which are thought to support development . While this immune response is well studied in the context of invasion and implantation, the physiological role of most placental-produced cytokines is less investigated . TGFβ-1 exhibits both pro- and anti-inflammatory effects, with a proinflammatory response in the presence of proinflammatory factors but otherwise represents a potent anti-inflammatory cytokine . In the context of placental development, it is also important to consider that inflammation and angiogenesis are tightly interwoven , with the two capable of potentiating each other. IL-8, IL-6, sVCAM-1, MCP-1 and CX3CL1, the factors assessed in this study, play a role in inflammation-induced angiogenesis. Notably, IL-6 and MCP-1 have both been shown to induce VEGF expression in other cell populations – . Our results showed that the proinflammatory response to TGFβ-1 was diverse; CX3CL1 and IL-8 were not altered; IL-6 and MCP-1 were reduced in an ALK5-independent manner, and sVCAM-1 secretion was attenuated entirely by TGFβ-1 in an ALK5-dependent manner. When considered together with the angiogenic results, these findings suggest that increased VEGFA secretion was a direct effect of TGFβ-1, rather than a downstream response to IL-6 or MCP-1. The reduced secretion of sVCAM-1, IL-6, and MCP-1 might impact the microvascular environment in an inflammatory capacity, but this was not explored. In the chorionic villi, TGFβ-1 is thought to regulate tissue homeostasis by maintaining an anti-inflammatory phenotype in Hofbauer cells . Thus, it is possible that TGFβ-1 acts in an anti-inflammatory capacity on the multiple cell types within the villi. Moreover, as pericytes in other organs recruit immune cells and MCP-1 and IL-6 can both act to recruit monocytes and macrophages, further studies exploring whether TGFβ-1 signalling inhibits pericyte recruitment of immune cells would be of value. Supporting the possibility that the response of pericytes to TGFβ-1 may be organ dependent is that TGFβ-1-treated brain pericytes had increased IL-6 .
Phagocytosis and inflammation are interconnected, with phagocytosis being critical to embryonic development and tissue remodelling, triggering host defence and inflammation, as well as the removal of apoptotic cells . During development, phagocytosis clears apoptotic cells, thereby preventing the accumulation of cellular debris that can induce inflammation. Phagocytosis involves (1) receptor recognition and binding of the pathogen, (2) actin polymerization at the site of contact, (3) membrane extensions fusing and pulling the particle towards the center of the cell, with particle internalization changing cell morphology , and (4) pathogen digestion . Importantly, the receptor type that participates in particle recognition affects the downstream functional response. Specifically, phagocytosis mediated by the Fc receptor is strongly proinflammatory, complement receptor-mediated phagocytosis is noninflammatory, and the uptake of apoptotic cells is anti-inflammatory. This study provides evidence that placental pericytes, like brain pericytes , phagocytose and that TGFβ-1 treatment reduces this activity. Based on the rationale that excessive debris can induce inflammation, which can disrupt organ development by disrupting cell differentiation , it stands to reason that in vivo placental pericytes function to clear cellular debris at the site of branching angiogenesis. While the receptor mechanism was not explored in this study, TGFβ-1 suppressed phagocytosis in myeloid cells was mediated via reduced expression of Fc receptors . Together with the assessment of inflammatory cytokines, our results suggest that TGFβ-1 acts on placental pericytes in a manner that reduces their proinflammatory potential. Whether the differences identified in the morphological assessment were attributed to pericyte phagocytosis was not explored.
Due to the inherent barrier function of the placenta and the localization of pericytes wrapped around capillaries, an attenuated inflammatory response mediated by TGFβ-1 could be concerning in placental pathologies and maternal diseases, where TGFβ-1 signalling is upregulated. Specifically, it raises questions about the balance of inflammatory responses necessary to sustain placental development and provide barrier protection, as well as whether elevated TGFβ-1 signalling disrupts this cycle through modulation of pericytes and other placental immune cells. Supporting this idea is that TGFβ-1 can enhance viral infectivity during pregnancy (reviewed in ). Given that pericytes may be vulnerable to placental infection, evidenced by preferential infection of cytomegalovirus towards pericytes in the placental milieu , further elucidating the immunogenic properties of placental pericytes is critical.
This study aimed to investigate whether the effect of TGFβ-1 signalling in placental pericytes was mediated through ALK5. While secretion of ANG-1 and sVCAM-1 was ALK5-dependent, the remaining measures were not. Notably, ALK5 inhibition blocks all signalling via that receptor, including by other TGFβ superfamily ligands. Thus, it is possible that inhibiting ALK5 prevented the action of other ligands and/or facilitated alternative signalling via ALK1. While this study did not assess downstream measures of ALK1 signalling or signalling of other TGFβ superfamily members, the results presented here identify a role for TGFβ-1 in regulating placental pericyte function. Thus, future studies investigating alternative signalling are warranted. Additionally, the assessment of placental pericyte produced growth factors and integrins should be considered as TGFβ-1-treated placental pericytes have significant transcriptional changes in genes associated with integrins and growth factor expression .
While it has been previously established that TGFβ-1 plays a critical role in the placenta and acts on vascular endothelial cells, the findings presented here confirm TGFβ-1 signalling in placental pericytes via both ALK5-dependent and independent signalling pathways and demonstrate functional consequences for pericyte properties. While the in vivo outcomes of pericyte modulation by TGFβ-1 on placental physiology are difficult to predict based on primary culture in vitro, results illustrate a proangiogenic, anti-inflammatory phenotype mediated by TGFβ-1 signalling, supporting the vital role of pericytes in placental microvascular homeostasis. Together, we believe that these findings will lay the groundwork for understanding the properties and functions of placental pericytes, as well as the factors that regulate them in both normal placental development and pathological conditions.

## Methods

## Tissue culture
Tissue culture experiments utilized commercially available FACS-isolated human term placental pericytes (PromoCell), which were cultured in ready-to-use pericyte growth medium (PromoCell). The cells were seeded at 3.0 × 103 cells/cm2 in 5% CO2 at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere for 48 h, allowing them to establish before experimental treatment. Cells were treated for 48 h with recombinant human TGFβ-1 (R&D Systems) and the TGFβR1 kinase (ALK5) inhibitor II , (RepSox, CAS 446859-33-2; Calbiochem), with a vehicle control group treated with only the DMSO carrier. Specifically, groups consisted of (1) a vehicle group (DMSO), (2) 2 M ALK5 kinase inhibitor , (3) 10 ng/mL TGFβ-1 , and (4) 10 ng/mL TGFβ-1 and 2 M ALK5 kinase inhibitor. Experiments were performed using supernatants collected for ELISA analysis, and cells were either fixed in Image-iT Fixative Solution (Life Technologies) for immunofluorescence analysis or collected for protein or RNA analysis. Experiments were performed with biological replicates (n = 3).

## Immunofluorescence
Immunocytochemistry/Immunofluorescent (ICC/IF) staining was performed to evaluate the expression of the pericyte markers: nestin (NES, Invitrogen, MA1110) , neural/glial-antigen 2 (NG2, Invitrogen 14-6504-82) , alpha-smooth muscle actin (αSMA, Abcam, ab124964) , platelet-derived growth factor receptor beta (PDGFRβ, Invitrogen, MA515143) , TGFβ-1 receptor (TGFβR1, Invitrogen PA598192) . Proliferation (Ki67, Abcam ab16667) and ECM component production, collagen IV (Abcam ab6586) , fibronectin (Abcam ab23750) , and laminin (Abcam ab11575) were also evaluated. Fixed placental pericytes (nestin, αSMA, PDGFRβ, fibronectin, and Ki67) were permeabilized (1 × PBS with 0.1% TritonX). To show that TGFβR1 staining was localized to the plasma membrane, 7.5 μg/mL wheat germ agglutinin (WGA) AF 594 (Life Technologies, W11262) staining was performed. All ICC/IF analyses included a 1 × PBS and 5% goat serum block and incubation with the primary antibody overnight at 4 C. Before imaging, cells were washed, incubated with a secondary antibody (1:500, 1 h at room temperature) and counterstained with ready-to-use Nuc-Blue Fixed stain (Life Technologies). See Supplemental Materials and Methods for antibody details and Supplemental Images Document for negative control images. Images (phase contrast and fluorescence) were collected using the EVOS M7000 microscope (Life Technologies), with the light, exposure and gain optimized for each antibody and applied to each captured image. Cell number, proliferation, pericyte marker expression and extracellular matrix production were analyzed using Celleste Imaging Analysis Software V.6, on raw images. Cell numbers and proliferation were assessed by counting the number of Ki67-positive cells and normalized relative to the total number of nuclei. The pericyte marker and ECM component staining areas and MFIs were normalized to cell count (presented as an area relative to 100 cells).

## Western blot
Cell lysates were isolated with RIPA buffer containing 0.1 mM Sodium orthovanadate (Sigma) and cOmplete Mini Protease inhibitor cocktail tablets (Miltenyi Biotec). Protein concentrations were measured using a DC protein assay kit (Bio-Rad). SDS-page gels (BioRad; 4–20%) were loaded with 14.75 μg of protein/well and semi-dry transferred to 0.2 μm PVDF membrane (BioRad), using standard procedures . Immunodetection included phosphorylated SMAD2 (CellSignalling C#3108S, 1:1000) as a marker of TGFβ-1 signalling and GAPDH (CellSignalling C#2118S, 1:1000) as a reference. A secondary horseradish peroxide conjugated goat anti-rabbit antibody (CellSignalling C#7074S, 1:2000) and ECL Substrate-Luminol Solution (Ultrasense, Froggobio) were used to detect the primary antibody. See Supplemental Images Document for full gel images. Bands were quantified using densitometry analysis on ImageJ software Version 1.53 k.

## RNA isolation, cDNA synthesis, RT-PCR, and visualization
Placental pericyte RNA was isolated using the Aurum Total RNA Mini Kit (Bio-Rad) , and reverse-transcribed with the iScript RT kit (Bio-Rad). Qualitative RT-PCR was used to amplify and detect mRNA expression according to the manufacturer’s protocol (iTaq Universal SYBR Green Supermix, BioRad) and BioRad primers (see Supplemental Materials and Methods for details) on a QuantStudio 5 thermocycler (Thermo Fisher). PCR reactions were conducted in technical triplicate on placental pericyte biological replicates (n = 3) for each gene using primers (Biorad) for pericyte markers (ACTA2, PDGFRβ, and CSPG4), TGFβ-1 receptors (TGFBR1, ACVRL1, and TGFBR2), and reference genes (PPIA and YWHAZ). PCR products, including the no template control (NTC), were visualized by electrophoresis in a 2% agarose gel, stained with SYBR safe DNA stain (Thermo Fisher) and imaged (Gel Logic 200 Imaging System) to confirm the expression of pericyte markers and TGFβ-1 receptors. See Supplemental Images Document for full gel images.
ELISA: ELISA assays, specific to secreted angiogenic and immunogenic factors (Invitrogen; Supplemental Materials and Methods for details), were performed in technical triplicate using biological replicate supernatants (n = 3). Pericyte media was assessed for factor secretion, given that proprietary media (PromoCell; C#C280140) has an undisclosed composition, and this was subtracted from the sample results. All assays were performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions using a microtiter plate spectrophotometer (SpectraMax ABS Plus, Molecular Devices). Optical densities from the blank were subtracted from those of the standards and samples. Concentration data for each ELISA were retrieved from interpolating the standard curve derived from a serial dilution of protein standards. In case treatment altered cell number, all secreted factor concentrations were normalized to cell count; the data presented describe the secreted factor concentration per 100,000 cells.

## Phagocytosis assay
To assess pericyte phagocytosis , FACS-isolated primary human placental pericytes (PromoCell) were cultured in ready-to-use pericyte growth media (PromoCell), seeded at 3.0 × 103 cells/cm2 and allowed to establish in 5% CO2 in 37 °C humidified air for 48 h before TGFβ-1 treatment. Cells were treated for 24 h with or without recombinant human TGFβ−1 (10 ng/mL; R&D Systems). After 22 h, cells were incubated in the presence or absence of a 1:1000 dilution of fluorescent ultraclean polystyrene microsphere beads (FluoSpheres carboxylate, 1.0 μm diameter, yellow-green 505/515). After 2 h, cells were washed (twice with 1xPBS to remove the beads that had not been phagocytosed), trypsinized and processed for FACS analysis to determine the percentage of the population that phagocytosed beads. Specifically, cells were pelleted and resuspended in FACS Buffer (1 × PBS with 1 mM EDTA, 25 mM HEPES, and 1% w/v Bovine Serum Albumin (BSA) and analyzed using the Sony SH800H cell sorter and analyzer (SONY Biotechnology) and the data were analyzed using FlowJo™ software (FlowJo™ v11, Becton, Dickinson & Company, https://flowjo.com/flowjo/overview) at the Queen’s Cardiopulmonary Unit. Internalization of beads was confirmed via microscopy.

## Statistical analysis
Analysis was performed with GraphPad PRISM 10.5.0 software. Normal (Gaussian) distribution was assessed using a Shapiro–Wilk normality test, given the limited sample size. Where data with three experimental groups demonstrated normality, analysis was conducted via an ordinary one-way ANOVA alongside Šídák’s multiple comparisons test with a single pooled variance . Where data from three experimental groups demonstrated an abnormal distribution, a Kruskal–Wallis test with Dunn’s multiple comparisons test was performed . Similarly, where data with two experimental groups did not demonstrate a normal distribution, a Mann–Whitney analysis with a one-tailed p-value was performed, given the directionality of the phagocytosis analysis had been previously described , . All data is presented with a significance determined by p ≤ 0.05 and mean ± SEM, with a 0.099 ≤ p ≥ 0.05 considered trending.
