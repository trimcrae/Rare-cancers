Oligodendrocyte precursor cells (OPCs) were reported to involve in engulf synapses and remodel neural circuits. This study aimed to elucidate the role of OPCs-mediated phagocytosis of GABAergic synapses in a mouse model of bone cancer pain. Male C3H mice were utilized to establish a model of bone cancer pain. In vivo fiber photometry was used to monitor the activity of GABAergic neurons, and chemogenetic techniques were applied to modulate neuronal excitation. The phagocytosis of GABAergic synapses by OPCs was visualized via immunofluorescence-based 3D reconstruction and immunoelectron microscopy. Interventions targeting lipocalin-2 (LCN2) and its receptor SLC22A17 were carried out with adeno-associated virus (AAV), siRNA, and pharmacological tools. On the 21st postoperative day, mice with bone cancer displayed significant pain and anxiety-like behaviors. Tumor-bearing mice exhibited a compensatory increase in calcium activity among GABAergic neurons within the anterior cingulate cortex (ACC). Compared with sham-operated mice, OPC phagocytosis of GABAergic synapses was higher in the tumor-bearing mice than controls. Concurrently, a pronounced upregulation of LCN2 expression was observed in the tumor group. Administration of LCN2-neutralizing antibodies or AAV-mediated intervention markedly alleviated pain and anxiety-related behaviors in mice with bone cancer. Moreover, the LCN2 receptor SLC22A17 expression was significantly increased. Targeted inhibition of SLC22A17 induced cytoskeletal remodeling and decreased their phagocytic capacity of OPCs. Collectively, LCN2/SLC22A17 signal was involved in OPCs-mediated phagocytosis of GABAergic synapses and contributed to cancer pain and anxiety development.

## Introduction
Cancer pain is a common malignant pain in cancer patients, and it is difficult to manage clinically due to its heterogeneity [ ]. Majority cancer patients (about 33%) experience cancer pain which profoundly results in negative emotions such as anxiety and depression [ ]. However, clinical treatments for cancer pain-related mood disorders are limited. Therefore, it is particularly important to investigate the underlying mechanism of pain-related emotion for better strategy development.
Chronic depression and anxiety may result in compromised synaptic function of neurons within the cerebral cortex and limbic system [ ]. Anterior cingulate cortex (ACC) serves as a critical hub within the neural circuits responsible for the regulation of pain and anxiety [ , ]. A previous Study in a murine model of bone cancer pain showed abnormalities in both the volume and functional activity of the ACC with functional magnetic resonance imaging [ ]. GABAergic neurons in the ACC constitute a key component of the descending pain inhibitory system, and their excitability is known to modulate emotional states [ ]. However, whether these GABAergic neurons directly encode and regulate pain and associated negative emotions remains unclear.
The processes of synaptic formation, pruning, and maturation are crucial for the development and maintenance of neural circuits [ ]. Dysregulated synaptic pruning may lead to excitatory-inhibitory (E-I) imbalance within the central nervous system [ ]. Glial cells mediated phagocytosis is essential for the clearance of synapses and the preservation of brain function, with aberrant synaptic pruning is frequently associated with neural damage [ ]. Research indicates that oligodendrocyte progenitor cells (OPCs) are involved in the phagocytosis of cortical input synapses during adolescence and adulthood. OPCs exhibit a more robust capacity for synaptic pruning compared to microglia [ , ]. In contrast, mature oligodendrocytes do not participate in synaptic phagocytosis [ ].
Lipocalin-2 (LCN2) was shown to facilitate synapse formation and modulate anxiety-like behavior in rodent models [ ]. As a secreted protein, LCN2 serves as a crucial regulator of central neuronal excitability [ ]. Solute carrier family 22 member 17 (SLC22A17), a receptor for LCN2, is a cell surface protein classified among endocytic receptors and is implicated in endocytosis [ ]. Previous studies identified a strong correlation between SLC22A17 and psychiatric disorders [ ]. Nonetheless, the potential role of the LCN2/SLC22A17 involved in mediating synaptic phagocytosis via oligodendrocyte precursor cells in cancer pain and related emotional disorders remains unknown.

## Materials and methods

## Animal
Male, 5–6 weeks, 18–22 g, C3H mice were purchased from Vital River Experimental Animal Corporation (Beijing, China). All animals were kept in specific pathogen-free (SPF) environment: room temperature of 22 ± 2 °C, relative humidity of 40–60%, and 12-hour light/12-hour dark cycle. All animal experimental protocols were approved by the Ethics Committee of Drum Tower Hospital, Medical School of Nanjing University and compliance with ARRIVE guildeline.

## Bone cancer pain model
Anesthesia was induced with 3% isoflurane and maintained at 2% throughout the surgical procedure. Following anesthetic induction, local infiltration injection was administered at the surgical site using 0.25 to 0.5% bupivacaine. Then, a knee arthrotomy was performed on the mice using ophthalmic scissors. After exposing the knee joint, the knee ligaments were transected using microscissors. The femoral patellar plane of the mouse femur was drilled with a needle. Mice in the tumor group were injected with α-MEM containing 2 × 105 NCTC 2472 osteosarcoma cells (ATCC, USA), whereas those in the sham group were injected with α-MEM alone. Bone wax was applied to seal the hole on the femoral patellar plane, and the incision was closed with sutures. Finally, the mice were allowed to recover on a thermal blanket.

## Paw withdrawal mechanical threshold
After 3 weeks, both group mice were subjected to plantar stimulation using VonFery needles of different stimulation intensities. The positive response was defined as either paw lift or lick. Paw withdrawal mechanical threshold (PWMT) was the minimum VonFery value in five tests.

## Number of spontaneous flinches
Mice were allowed to rest for 30 min. The number of mice foot-lift events within two minutes was then counted.

## Elevated plus maze
The maze was 50 cm above the ground and has two open arms (30 × 5 cm) and two closed arms (30 × 5 cm). Mouse was placed in the middle, facing one side of the open arm. Then, camera was used to capture their behavior and software was used to record the trajectories of mice within 5 min. In between tests, the maze device was cleaned with alcohol.

## Open field test
Mouse was put in a 40 × 40 × 30 cm acrylic plate chamber and allowed to travel freely for 5 min. The trajectory map was captured using software. Use alcohol to clean the chamber in between two test sessions.

## Western blot
Cervical dislocation was performed post-anesthesia for euthanasia. Following euthanasia, the mouse’s head was separated from the body, and the skin and soft tissues were removed. Using ophthalmic scissors, a circular incision was made through the skull at its base. The excised skull segment was then lifted with tweezers to expose the brain, which was carefully extracted using curved tweezers. The brain was subsequently sectioned with a mouse brain matrix, and the anterior cingulate cortex was dissected according to stereotaxic coordinates obtained from a standard mouse brain atlas.
Freshly sampled ACC brain region tissues were harvested under terminal anestheisa homogenized and centrifuged, and the concentration of protein in the supernatants was measured with BCA assay. Electrophoresis sample proteins were transferred proteins to PVDF membranes. After blocking with skim milk, the membranes were incubated at 4 ℃ with the primary antibody (Supplemental Table ). After TBST washing, the membranes were incubated with secondary antibody (Supplemental Table ) at room temperature. Finally, ECL was used to visualize bands.

## Immunofluorescence
The mice were deeply anesthetized with isoflurane and subsequently perfused transcardially with normal saline, followed by 4% paraformaldehyde. Brain tissue was then removed and fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 24 h. After formaldehyde fixation and sucrose gradient dehydration, the tissue was embedded and cut into 20 μm thickness. Sections were blocked with BSA and then incubated with the primary antibody (Supplemental Table ) at 4 °C. The sections were washed with phosphate buffered saline (PBS) and incubated with secondary antibody (Supplemental Table ) for 1 h at room temperature. Finally, DAPI was added dropwise for nuclear staining before covered with cover glass.

## Immunoelectron microscopy
Mouse ACC brain region was freshly collected and fixed overnight with immunoelectron microscopy fixation solution (Servicebio, China) at 4 °C. Following fixation, the tissue samples were rinsed with phosphate buffer (PB), dehydrated through a graded series of ethanol, and embedded in resin. Ultrathin sections with a thickness of 70–80 nm were subsequently obtained using an ultramicrotome. After washing with TBS, the sections were blocked with 1% BSA blocking solution for 30 min at room temperature, and then incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against NG2 and GAD67. The sections were washed again with TBS and then incubated with colloidal gold secondary antibodies for 2 h at room temperature. Finally, uranium salts counterstaining and drying of the sections were performed. Image acquisition was performed under a transmission electron microscope.

## Enzyme linked immunosorbent assay
According to the manufacturer’s instruction, tissue was homogenized with PBS to obtain the supernatant and serum was obtained by centrifugation of fresh blood. LCN2 concentration in both supernatant and serum was measured by LCN2 enzyme linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits (mlbio, China).

## Quantitative real–time PCR
Total RNA was extracted from mouse ACC brain tissue using Trizol (Vazyme, China). Next, cDNA synthesis was performed using reverse transcription kit (Vazyme, China). Finally, qRT-PCR was performed and the relative expression was determined by the 2−ΔΔCt method. The sequences of qPCR primers were shown in the table below (Supplemental Table ).

## Virus administration
Mice were anesthetized with isoflurane and fixed in a stereotaxic apparatus. Following disinfection, the scalp was incised to expose the skull, and the coordinates were adjusted based on the landmarks of the bregma and lambda. The injection location was AP, + 0.45 mm, ML, ± 0.25 mm and DV, − 1.12 mm. Adeno-associated virus 200nl was pre-aspirated using a microsyringe: rAAV-hSyn-EGFP-5’miR-30a-shRNA(scramble)-3’-miR30a-WPREs or rAAV-hSyn-EGFP-5’miR-30a-shRNA(LCN2)-3’-miR30a-WPREs (5.28 × 1012 vg/ml, BrainVTA, China). The skull marker was drilled using an electric cranial drill, and AAV was injected slowly at a rate of 0.1 µl/min via a microsyringe pump. After the injection, the needle was left in place for 10 min to prevent viral backflow. Finally, the scalp was sutured layer by layer, and the mice were placed on a thermal blanket for recovery. General postoperative recovery of the mice was assessed by monitoring body weight, activity, wound condition, appearance, and diet.

## Chemogenetic modulation
GABAergic neurons in the ACC were selectively transduced by stereotaxic injection of AAV2/9-mDlx-hM3D(Gq)-EGFP-ER2-WPRE-pA or a control (AAV2/9-mDlx-EGFP-WPRE-pA; ≥1 × 1013 vg/ml, Taitool Bioscience). After 21 days, mice received an intraperitoneal injection of clozapine-N-oxide (CNO; 1 mg/kg) before behavioral tests.

## Sparse labeling
For sparse labeling, a diluted solution of AAV2/9-mGAD65-Cre-pA (20,000-fold dilution) was mixed at a 1:1 ratio with AAV2/9-hSyn-DIO-mGFP-WPRE-pA (≥ 1 × 1013 vg/ml, Taitool Bioscience, China) and co-injected into the ACC.

## Drugs administration
LCN2 neutralizing antibody (0.25 mg/kg, R&D systems, USA) and SLC22A17 siRNA (0.15 nmol/g, Gene Adv, China) were delivered via intracerebroventricular injection under surgical anesthesia. The injection location: AP, − 0.3 mm; ML, ± 1 mm; DV, -2.5 mm. Oligodendrocyte precursor cells inhibition was achieved by adding clemastine fumarate (0.03 mg/ml, Proteintech, China) to drinking water.

## Fiber-photometry
Mice ACC brain regions were injected with virus (200nl) via stereotaxic instruments: rAAV-hSyn-GCaMp6s-WPRE-hGHpA (5.07 × 1012 vg/ml, BrainVTA, China), AAV2/9-mDlx-GCaMP6s-WPRE-pA (≥ 1 × 1013 vg/ml, Taitool Bioscience, China). After virus injection, the fiberoptic implant was inserted at the same position and secured with dental cement. GCaMP fluorescent signals were recorded with a fiber photometry system (Inper, China).

## Golgi-cox staining
Dendritic spine staining was performed utilizing the Rapid GolgiStain Kit (FD Neurotech, China) in accordance with the manufacturer’s protocol. Brain slices were sectioned to be a thickness of 150 μm [ ]. Following Golgi staining, images were acquired using a microscope.

## Transcriptomic analysis
Total RNA was isolated from the tissue or cells using Trizol reagent (Vazyme, China). The purity and integrity of total RNA were assessed using a spectrophotometer and a microfluidic capillary electrophoresis system. mRNA was enriched and purified from total RNA using oligo(dT) magnetic beads. The purified mRNA was fragmented under elevated temperature and used as a template for double-stranded cDNA. The double-stranded cDNA underwent end repair, adapter ligation, size selection, and PCR enrichment to construct the cDNA library. The qualified library was subsequently used for high-throughput sequencing. RNA sequencing was performed using illumina platforms (Metware Biotechnology Co., Ltd, China). The screening standard for differential genes were significance p-value < 0.05 and differential fold |log2FoldChange|>1. Enrichment analysis of differentially expressed genes was performed using the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG).

## Primary OPCs culture and treatment
Primary oligodendrocyte precursor cells were extracted from the neonatal mouse cerebral cortex and cultured in DMEM/F12 medium containing 2% B27 (Gibco, USA), 30 ng/ml PDGF-AA and 10 ng/ml bFGF (GenScript, China). Oligodendrocyte precursor cells were purified by the differential adherent method and then seeded in poly-D-lysine-coated flasks [ ]. Purified oligodendrocyte precursor cells were intervened with LCN2 (MCE, USA) [ ]. For phagocytosis assays, OPCs were incubated with fluorescent latex beads (Merck, Germany) for 1 h following LCN2 treatment [ ]. To visualize lysosomes, cells were stained with Lyso-Tracker Green (Beyotime, China) for 30 min. After removal of the probe, nuclei were counterstained with Hoechst 33,342 (Beyotime, China). For siRNA transfection, OPCs were transfected using an siRNA-specific transfection reagent (Gene Adv, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. F-actin was labeled with FITC Phalloidin (ABclonal, China). To disrupt the actin cytoskeleton, OPCs were exposed to 5 µM cytochalasin D (MCE, China) for 30 min [ ].

## Statistical analysis
Graphical abstract and schematic diagram were drawn with aid of Figdraw. A p < 0.05 was considered to be of statistical significance. After confirming that the data met the assumptions of normality and homogeneity of variance, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was conducted to compare differences between the two groups. To assess the effects following drug administration, two-way ANOVA was used, followed by post-hoc comparisons using the Bonferroni correction. For data that did not meet the assumptions of normality or homogeneity of variance, the Mann-Whitney U test was used for comparisons between two groups, while the Scheirer-Ray-Hare test was applied to analyze group differences after drug administration. Post-hoc comparisons were subsequently performed using the Bonferroni test.

## Results

## GABAergic neurons regulated hyperalgesia and anxiety-like behaviors in a mouse model of bone cancer
The methodology outlined by Schwei et al. was employed to develop a murine model of bone cancer pain [ ]. According to the results of the previous study, pain behavior and anxiety behavior were tested after model establishment [ ]. In comparison to the sham group, the PWMT values in the tumor group were significantly decreased, while the NSF values were elevated on the 21st day. Furthermore, during anxiety assessments, tumor-bearing mice exhibited a notable decrease in both the frequency and duration of entries into the central area and open arms on postoperative day 21. (Fig.  A–E). c-Fos immunostaining was utilizd to identify brain regions activity. On the 21st day post-surgery, there was a substantial increase in c-Fos positive cells in the ACC of tumor group relative to the sham group (Supplemental Fig.  A and B). Fiber photometry recordings of calcium activity revealed that ACC neurons in the tumor group showed elevated responses to subthreshold stimuli (Supplemental Fig.  C–E). Additionally, the number of inhibitory neurons was significantly reduced in the tumor group (Fig.  F and G). Fiber photometry further showed that calcium activity in ACC GABAergic neurons was significantly enhanced in tumor-bearing mice following subthreshold stimulation (Fig.  H–J). Chemogenetic activation of GABAergic neurons markedly attenuated mechanical allodynia and spontaneous pain behaviors. It also increased the time and entries into the center zone of the open field and the open arms of the elevated plus maze (Fig.  K–P).

 

## Bone cancer induced oligodendrocyte precursor cells to phagocytose GABAergic synapses
To investigate the role of synaptic plasticity in pain and anxiety-like behaviors, we assessed synaptic changes using western blot (WB), immunofluorescence (IF), and Golgi-Cox staining. Compared to the sham group, tumor-bearing mice showed a significant reduction in inhibitory synapses and an increase in excitatory synapses (Supplemental Fig.  A–D). Golgi-Cox staining further revealed a decrease in dendritic spine density in the tumor group (Supplemental Fig.  E and F). Given that synaptic loss is often linked to neuroinflammation and aberrant glial activation [ ], we evaluated glial reactivity in the BCP model using immunofluorescence. On postoperative day 21, oligodendrocyte precursor cells displayed increased branching, and microglia exhibited more amoeboid morphology relative to sham group (Fig.  A–C). Three-dimensional immunofluorescence reconstruction showed enhanced phagocytosis of GABAergic synapses by OPCs in the tumor group (Fig.  D). This was corroborated by immuno-electron microscopy, as evidence of the presence of GABAergic synaptic material within OPCs in bone cancer mice (Fig.  E). Furthermore, tumor-bearing mice exhibited a reduction in dendritic spines on GABAergic neurons, and oligodendrocyte precursor cells were observed to engulf these spines (Fig.  G–J). To examine the contribution of OPCs to anxiety-like behaviors, mice were administered clemastine fumarate (0.03 mg/ml) in drinking water after modeling [ ]. Clemastine treatment ameliorated anxiety-like behaviors in tumor-bearing mice (Supplemental Fig.  B–E), reduced OPCs proliferation, and enhanced myelin sheath maturation in the BCP model (Supplemental Fig.  F). Additionally, Edu staining confirmed that clemastine effectively suppressed OPCs proliferation in tumor mice (Supplemental Fig.  G).

 

## Bone cancer induced an increase of LCN2 expression in ACC
To investigate the key molecule regulating the enhancement of phagocytosis in oligodendrocyte precursor cells, RNA sequencing was employed. Transcriptomic analysis showed a significant upregulation of LCN2 expression and an enrichment of phagocytosis pathway (Fig.  A–C). Western blot and qPCR analyses confirmed a marked elevation in LCN2 levels in the tumor group compared to sham group (Fig.  D and F). ELISA further indicated elevated LCN2 concentrations in both serum and ACC tissue from bone cancer mice (Fig.  G). Immunofluorescence colocalization of LCN2 with neurons and glial cells further revealed that LCN2 was predominantly expressed in neurons within the ACC of tumor-bearing mice (Fig.  H and I).

 

## LCN2 knockdown attenuated phagocytosis of GABAergic synapses by oligodendrocyte precursor cells
To investigate the impact of LCN2 on pain and anxiety-related behaviors in BCP, the administration of LCN2 neutralizing antibody via brain stereotactic injection was conducted [ ]. Mice treated with the LCN2 antibody showed significant improvement in pain and anxiety-like behaviors compared to those receiving control IgG (Supplemental Fig.  B–G). Since LCN2 was primarily expressed in neurons, we next used an adeno-associated virus (AAV) vector carrying short hairpin RNA against LCN2 (shRNA-LCN2) under a neuron-specific promoter to knock down LCN2 expression in the ACC of bone cancer mice (Fig.  A and B). Compared to scramble shRNA-treated mice, mice receiving shRNA-LCN2 spent more time and entered more frequently into the open arms of the elevated plus maze and the center zone in the open field test (Fig.  C–F). Treatment with shRNA-LCN2 resulted in a marked reduction in LCN2 protein and mRNA expression levels in bone cancer mice (Fig.  G–K). Furthermore, LCN2 knockdown significantly decreased the phagocytosis of GABAergic synapses in the BCP model (Fig.  L and M).

 

## SLC22A17 knockdown attenuated OPCs-mediated phagocytosis of GABAergic synapses
SLC22A17 serves as a receptor for LCN2. To further investigate the role of SLC22A17 in oligodendrocyte precursor cells, we conducted an examination of SLC22A17 expression. In comparison to the sham group, the expression of SLC22A17 was elevated in the tumor group, and there was a significant increase in SLC22A17 expression within oligodendrocyte precursor cells in the tumor group (Fig.  A–D). Knockdown of SLC22A17 via siRNA in bone cancer mice resulted in a significant increase in PWMT (Fig.  E and F) [ ]. Additionally, SLC22A17 siRNA treatment led to an increased duration and frequency of entries into the open arms of the elevated plus maze, as well as increased time spent and frequency of entries into the central area of the open field test (Fig.  G and H). Furthermore, SLC22A17 siRNA treatment reduced both protein and mRNA expression levels of SLC22A17 in mice with bone cancer (Fig.  I–K). SLC22A17 siRNA reduced phagocytosis of GABAergic synapses in bone cancer mice (Fig.  L and M).

 

## Cytoskeletal reorganization enhanced the phagocytic capacity of oligodendrocyte precursor cells
To further examine the effect of LCN2 on oligodendrocyte precursor cells, primary OPCs were treated with increasing concentrations of recombinant LCN2 protein [ ]. Treatment with 10 µg/ml LCN2 significantly increased both lysosomal volume and phagocytic uptake of fluorescent beads in OPCs (Fig.  A–C). Furthermore, LCN2 treatment upregulated SLC22A17 protein expression in OPCs (Fig.  D and E). Knockdown of SLC22A17 via siRNA markedly attenuated LCN2-enhanced beads phagocytosis and SLC22A17 protein expression (Fig.  F–I). RNA sequencing revealed that SLC22A17 knockdown significantly enriched genes related to the cytoskeleton pathway (Fig.  J). Phalloidin staining demonstrated an increase in filopodia in oligodendrocyte precursor cells following treatment with LCN2 recombinant protein (Fig.  K and L). Treatment with the actin polymerization inhibitor cytochalasin D significantly abrogated LCN2-induced phagocytosis (Fig.  M and N). In addition, SLC22A17 knockdown significantly reduced the activity of RAC1, a key GTPase regulating cytoskeletal dynamics (Fig.  O and P).

 

## Discussion
In this study, we demonstrated that oligodendrocyte precursor cells contribute to the development of pain and anxiety-like behaviors through the phagocytosis of GABAergic synapses. Specifically, we found that in the ACC of mice with bone cancer, neurons excessively secrete LCN2, which binds to SLC22A17 on OPCs. This further drove OPCs cytoskeletal remodeling, which triggered the phagocytosis of GABAergic synapses and resulting in pain and anxiety-like behaviors. Furthermore, the knockdown of LCN2 or SLC22A17 was shown to mitigate these behaviors, indicating that the LCN2/SLC22A17 signal may serve as a potential therapeutic target for alleviating negative emotional comorbidities associated with cancer pain.
Previous studies showed that the excitatory-inhibitory (E-I) imbalance in the central nervous system is a key mechanism underlying pain and anxiety [ ]. Modulating this neural circuit imbalance has been shown to improve pain and anxiety-like behaviors in animal models [ ]. In the present study, mice with bone cancer indeed exhibited both pain and anxiety-like behaviors. Using c-Fos to map activated brain regions, we observed significant neuronal activation in the anterior cingulate cortex. This result is consistent with functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) study reporting abnormal functional activity in the ACC of mice with bone cancer [ ]. Similarly, we detected a significant enhancement in neuronal calcium signals within the ACC of tumor-bearing mice. Furthermore, we found a marked reduction in the inhibitory synaptic marker Vgat and a concurrent increase in the excitatory synaptic marker Vglut2 in the ACC of tumor-bearing mice, indicating a shift toward excitatory dominance. Such E-I imbalance in the ACC has been previously linked to pain and anxiety, as demonstrated in chronic pain models such as the spared nerve injury (SNI) model [ ]. Additionally, we noted a significant decrease in the number of GABAergic neurons in the ACC of bone cancer mice. Interestingly, the remaining GABAergic neurons displayed heightened excitability. Chemogenetic activation of these neurons significantly alleviated pain and anxiety behaviors, suggesting a potential compensatory mechanism through increased excitability of GABAergic neurons—a phenomenon also observed in the SNI model [ ]. Collectively, these findings indicate that E-I imbalance in the ACC may play a critical role in mediating cancer-induced pain and anxiety.
Oligodendrocyte precursor cells are thought to play a role in the myelination process by differentiating into oligodendrocytes [ ]. Recent findings indicate that OPCs can contribute to the remodeling of neural circuits via synapse phagocytosis, with a mechanism different from that of oligodendrocytes formed as precursor cells [ , , ]. In this study, we found that OPCs were capable of engulfing GABAergic synapses. Notably, OPCs contained more intracellular synapses compared to microglia, paralleling findings related to the phagocytosis of cortical input synapses by adolescent OPCs [ ]. The administration of clemastine fumarate, an inhibitor of OPCs [ ], resulted in a marked reduction of anxiety-like behaviors in bone cancer mice and facilitated the differentiation of OPCs. Research has also demonstrated that selective photoactivation of OPCs can induce anxiety-like behavior by disrupting E-I balance through modulation of adjacent interneuron activity [ ]. These findings suggest that OPCs play a significant role in the regulation of pain and anxiety.
Lipocalin 2 is a class of secreted proteins expressed in both glial cells and neurons [ , , ]. Previous research has demonstrated that LCN2 plays a pivotal role in stress response, disrupts cortical neural activity, and induces anxiety-like behaviors [ ]. In this study, based on sequencing data, we found that LCN2 expression was significantly elevated in the ACC of bone cancer mice, where it was primarily expressed in neurons. The administration of LCN2 neutralizing antibodies markedly alleviated pain and anxiety-like behaviors in these mice. SLC22A17, known as the receptor for LCN2, was also found to be significantly upregulated in oligodendrocyte precursor cells in bone cancer mice. Furthermore, inhibition of SLC22A17 resulted in a significant reduction in the phagocytosis of GABAergic synapses by OPCs.
During phagocytosis, the engagement of cell surface receptors with target initiates downstream signaling cascades [ ]. This activation drives localized actin polymerization and pseudopod extension, enabling the engulfment of the target material [ ]. The newly formed phagosome is then internalized, transported into the cell, and ultimately fuses with lysosomes for content degradation. In this study, we demonstrated that treatment of primary oligodendrocyte precursor cells with recombinant LCN2 protein enhanced their phagocytic capacity. Conversely, siRNA-mediated knockdown of SLC22A17 significantly reduced beads phagocytosis by OPCs. Further analysis revealed an enrichment of cytoskeleton-related pathways following LCN2 treatment, accompanied by a marked increase in filopodia formation. Given that pseudopod extension and fusion are essential for phagosome formation, these morphological changes are indicative of enhanced phagocytic activity [ ]. Moreover, we observed a significant increase in the activity of RAC1, a key GTPase regulating cytoskeletal dynamics, consistent with previously reported mechanisms in bacterial phagocytosis by macrophages [ ].
Several questions remain unresolved in this study. First, while clemastine administration provides supportive pharmacological evidence, these findings still warrant genetic validation to confirm causality. Conditional OPC ablation has not yet been carried out owing to time constraints, and we will subsequently perform cell-specific OPC depletion to consolidate our observed phenotypes. Second, given that synaptic phagocytosis by OPCs is a dynamic process, real-time visualization of OPC-neuronal interactions via two-photon microscopy would yield more compelling evidence, and this experimental approach will be incorporated in our subsequent studies. Third, SLC22A17 is currently the most extensively characterized receptor for LCN2 and is known to mediate its endocytosis. However, a recent study has identified MC4R as an additional receptor for LCN2 in the central nervous system, where it contributes to the regulation of appetite [ ]. Given time limitations in the present study, we were unable to evaluate whether the MC4R pathway is involved in mediating the effects of LCN2 observed in our work. Finally, our study lacks electrophysiological assessments of the ACC brain region, and functional electrophysiological data would directly link structural synapse loss to behavior; subsequent studies will include such functional assessments to further corroborate our conclusions.
In conclusion, the present study demonstrated that LCN2/SLC22A17 signal may contribute to the development of cancer pain and anxiety by facilitating OPCs engulfment of GABAergic synapses. These findings provide novel insights and potential therapeutic strategies for the treatment of pain and associated negative emotional states in cancer.
