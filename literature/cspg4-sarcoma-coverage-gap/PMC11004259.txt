No use, distribution or reproduction is permitted which does not comply with these terms.Hematopoiesis is a finely orchestrated process, whereby hematopoietic stem cells give rise to all mature blood cells. Crucially, they maintain the ability to self-renew and/or differentiate to replenish downstream progeny. This process starts at an embryonic stage and continues throughout the human lifespan. Blood cancers such as leukemia occur when normal hematopoiesis is disrupted, leading to uncontrolled proliferation and a block in differentiation of progenitors of a particular lineage (myeloid or lymphoid). Although normal stem cell programs are crucial for tissue homeostasis, these can be co-opted in many cancers, including leukemia. Myeloid or lymphoid leukemias often display stem cell-like properties that not only allow proliferation and survival of leukemic blasts but also enable them to escape treatments currently employed to treat patients. In addition, some leukemias, especially in children, have a fetal stem cell profile, which may reflect the developmental origins of the disease. Aberrant fetal stem cell programs necessary for leukemia maintenance are particularly attractive therapeutic targets. Understanding how hijacked stem cell programs lead to aberrant gene expression in place and time, and drive the biology of leukemia, will help us develop the best treatment strategies for patients.

## Introduction
Stem cells perform a complex balancing act between self-renewal and differentiation throughout ontogeny. To perform these functions, stem cells proliferate rapidly and repair DNA damage. However, these “stemness” properties present a vulnerability as, if hijacked, they provide cancer cells with the pathways required for growth and survival ( ; ).

## Hematopoietic stem cells
Human hematopoiesis is a dynamic process beginning at day 18 in the yolk sac ( ), with definitive hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs) originating in the aorta–gonad–mesonephros from 4 post-conception weeks (pcw) ( ; ). HSCs subsequently migrate to fetal liver (FL), the main site of hematopoiesis until birth ( ), with contribution from fetal bone marrow (FBM) 10–12 pcw ( ; ). After birth, BM becomes the sole site of hematopoiesis.

## Fetal HSCs are molecularly and functionally distinct from postnatal HSCs
Fetal HSCs are more proliferative than their postnatal counterparts ( ; ; ; ; ) with higher self-renewal capacity ( ; ), better in vivo engraftment ( ; ), and a distinct metabolic profile ( ). Adult HSCs show a myeloid lineage bias ( ); additionally, innate lymphoid cells such as B1a B-cells are derived exclusively from embryonic/fetal HSCs ( ; ). These functional differences may be a consequence of distinct developmental gene expression programs.

## Stem cell programs in leukemia
In leukemia, stem cell programs may be inappropriately reactivated or retained and/or co-opted from fetal development. This may be a consequence of some leukemias originating in utero, especially in children ( ).
In this review, we discuss stem cell programs that are aberrantly active in the wrong cellular context (“place”) or stage of ontogeny (“time”) in pediatric leukemia and their potential applications in developing targeted therapies ( ).
 

## Stem cell programs in the wrong place

## Aberrant stem cell genes in leukemia
Leukemic blasts may exhibit stem cell properties, conferring a more aggressive phenotype. Several stem cell genes are important for leukemia biology ( ), and some key examples are discussed below.
 

## Homeobox genes
Homeobox genes form a group of transcription factors (TFs) with 235 functional genes in humans ( ).

## 
HOXA9

The largest class of homeobox genes, the HOX genes ( ), play important roles in hematopoiesis, such as development and maintenance of HSCs ( ; ). HOXA9 can reprogram tissues to a hematopoietic fate ( ; ).
Over 50% of AML cases overexpress HOXA9, which correlates with poor survival ( ; ; ). HOXA9 directly binds target genes along with PBX3/MEIS1 ( ; ), upregulating oncogenes such as FLT3, LMO2, and MYB( ; ). HOXA9 also suppresses apoptotic/differentiation factors, promoting leukemia cell survival and maintaining a more stem-like state ( ).

HOXA9 plays a key role in KMT2A-rearranged (KMT2A-r) leukemia ( ; ), mediated by binding of the KMT2A fusion protein to HOX gene promoters ( ; ) and recruitment of DOT1L H3K79 methyltransferases ( ; ). Dysregulated HOXA9 expression is also seen in NPM1c-mutated AML ( ; ).

## 
CDX2

CDX2 of the ParaHox family of homeobox genes ( ) is not normally expressed in hematopoietic cells and inhibits the hematopoietic potential of murine embryonic stem cells (ESCs) ( ).
Over 90% of AML cases overexpress CDX2 ( ; ; ), and ectopic expression of Cdx2 confers oncogenic properties to murine HSCs ( ; ). CDX2 overexpression studies suggest upregulation of HOX genes as the oncogenic mechanism ( ), although at least one alternative pathway is through direct suppression of KLF4 ( ).
Aberrant CDX2 expression is also frequently seen in ALL ( ; ) and confers a poor prognosis ( ).

## 
IRX genes
The Iroquois genes (IRX1-6) belong to the TALE group of homeobox genes. IRX1 is required for normal development of kidney and neural tissues ( ; ) but not expressed in most hematopoietic precursors ( ). IRX3 and IRX5 are not expressed in hematopoiesis.
In KMT2A-r infant ALL, IRX1 or HOXA9 expression defines two distinct subgroups ( ; ). IRX1 prevents KMT2A::AFF1 from activating HOXA9 expression; instead, expression of HOXB4 causes persistence of HSC factors ( ). IRX1+(HOXA-) KMT2A-r ALL has a poorer prognosis ( ; ).
In AML, IRX1/3/5 is aberrantly expressed ( ). In AML cell lines, expressions of IRX1 and IRX3/5 are mutually exclusive with opposing effects on GATA1/2 activity ( ).

## 
PROM1

Prominin-1 (PROM1/CD133) is a membrane pentaspan glycoprotein, identified in the mouse neuroepithelium ( ) and human HSCs ( ; ). Its function remains enigmatic; and its role in cytoskeletal organization is postulated. In solid organ malignancies, PROM1 regulates extracellular vesicle formation and release ( ; ; ).

PROM1 is expressed in many cancers ( ; ; ; ), including leukemia, where the expression is associated with poor prognosis ( ). It is a common feature of KMT2A-r leukemias and essential for survival of KMT2A::AFF1 cell lines ( ; ).

## Leukemic stem cells
In addition to inappropriate expression of stem cell genes, there may be very rare leukemic stem cells (LSCs) within a leukemia that can propagate disease in serial transplantation ( ; ; ). This concept is well-established in AML ( ), but less so in ALL ( ). AML LSCs share the key feature of self-renewal with normal HSCs alongside similarities in gene expression ( ), immunophenotype (e.g., CD133+( ), CD123+( )), and metabolism ( ).
A stem cell signature (LSC17) has been used to risk stratify adult ( ) and some pediatric AMLs ( ), where it confers a worse prognosis. A six-gene pediatric AML signature (pLSC6) ( ) was derived from a cohort of 163 pediatric AMLs ( ). The adult and pediatric signatures share five genes (DNMT3B, GPR56, CD34, FAM30A/KIAA012, and SOCS2), although with different gene weightings, and pLSC6 includes a further unique gene, SPINK2.
Single-cell RNA-seq identified LSC clusters in pediatric AML; these LSC markers require functional validation ( ). Fetal-specific genes (HMGA2) have been identified in a rare HSC-like fraction of KMT2A-r infant leukemia ( ), and IGF2BP1 maintains LSC by regulating HOXB4, MYB, and ALDH1A1 in pediatric leukemia cell lines ( ).

## Aberrant stem cell gene expression in ontogeny

## Fetal stem cell programs in pediatric leukemia
Pediatric leukemias [and solid malignancies such as neuroblastoma ( )] may exhibit properties of fetal HSCs. This may represent a fetal cell of origin or indicate reactivation of fetal programs ( ; ; ). Either way, the inappropriate expression of fetal genes is important for cancer biology. Leukemic blasts, especially LSCs ( ), show fetal-specific gene expression profiles ( ; ; ; ; ). Down syndrome (DS)-associated leukemias and juvenile myelomonocytic leukemia (JMML) are two examples resulting from perturbation of fetal HSPCs.
Trisomy 21 causes global perturbation of fetal hematopoiesis, with increased phenotypic HSCs and megakaryocyte–erythroid progenitors (MEPs), as well as a marked skew to erythropoiesis with a concomitant decrease in B-lymphopoiesis ( ; ; ). DS fetal HSPCs and stromal cells also display increased inflammatory signatures ( ). Chromosome 21 (Hsa21) stem cell genes dysregulated/overexpressed in DS include transcription factor GABPA, which affects HSC maintenance/differentiation ( ), and chromatin modifier HMGN1.
Children with DS have an increased risk of AML and ALL ( ). Mutations in the megakaryocyte–erythroid transcription factor GATA1 in fetal life lead to transient abnormal myelopoiesis (TAM) in the fetal/neonatal period ( ; ). Additional mutations, most commonly in the cohesin complex genes ( ), are required for myeloid leukemia of DS ( ). DS-ALL also likely stems from perturbed lymphopoiesis, which begins in utero, and is characterized by CRLF2/TSLPR overexpression in 50% and JAK2 mutations in 20% ( ). Key Hsa21 genes important for leukemogenesis are listed in .
JMML, an HSC-derived leukemia ( ; ), has a fetal molecular profile ( ; ). As the oncogenic hit probably occurs in fetal HSCs, developmental stem cell programs are hijacked for leukemogenesis.
Fetal oncogenes relevant to stem cell activity and implicated in pediatric leukemia act via a diverse range of mechanisms and are discussed below ( ).

## Fetal genes with transcription factor activity implicated in pediatric leukemia

SCL/TAL1 was originally identified as overexpressed in T-ALL ( ). Ablation of the gene causes embryonic death ( ), but it is dispensable for adult HSCs ( ).

LMO2 is essential for fetal hematopoiesis ( ). LMO2 expression is seen in a subset of pediatric T-ALL ( ) and B-ALL ( ). In gene therapy trials for SCID, two patients developed T-ALL through off-target activation of LMO2 ( ).

SALL4 is expressed in ESCs ( ), but downregulated postnatally with only low-level expression in HSCs ( ). It is aberrantly expressed in pediatric AML ( ) and ALL ( ; ), with overexpression conferring poor prognosis ( ; ).

## Fetal genes important for post-transcriptional regulation
The bulk of post-transcriptional control is exerted by RNA-binding proteins (RBPs). Some RBPs such as LIN28B and insulin-like growth factor 2 mRNA-binding proteins (IGF2BP1/IGF2BP3) have a fetal expression pattern, a role in stem cell biology ( ; ; ) and pediatric leukemia.

## 
LIN28B

LIN28B has wide-ranging physiological roles in fetal tissues; however, the expression after birth is limited to the placenta and testis ( ). In hematopoiesis, FL HSCs have the highest expression of LIN28B ( ), and expression in postnatal cells can reactivate fetal-like erythropoiesis ( ; ) and B-lymphopoiesis ( ). LIN28B can reprogram somatic cells to induce pluripotency ( ); however, this can be oncogenic in other contexts ( ).
The main action of LIN28B is to prevent maturation of let-7 miRNAs ( ; ). LIN28B also directly stabilizes many mRNAs in conjunction with IGF2BP1/IGF2BP3. In murine B progenitors, this includes Pax5 and Arid3a, thereby driving fetal B-lymphopoiesis ( ).

LIN28B is frequently aberrantly expressed in cancers, including leukemia ( ; ). Suppression of let-7 miRNAs by LIN28B leads to de-repression of oncogenes (MYC, RAS, MYB, and ARID3A) and epigenetic regulators, HMGA2 and CBX2 ( ).
A meta-analysis showed that 7.5% of pediatric leukemias express LIN28B ( ). Aberrant LIN28B expression defines a poor prognosis subgroup in JMML ( ), where H19, a fetal oncogene ( ), is stabilized in the presence of LIN28B ( ). AML in children <3 years has higher levels of LIN28B (and IGF2BP1/3) expression than in children >3 years ( ). Although LIN28B has predominantly been reported to have a pro-leukemic role in AML ( ), one study on a murine KMT2A::MLLT3 AML model suggests that LIN28B abrogates perinatal leukemia development ( ). Given >50% of human neonatal leukemias are of myeloid lineage, these findings seem counterintuitive, although it is possible that neonatal AML arises from LIN28B negative progenitors. Given the role of LIN28B in fetal B-lymphopoiesis, it may also be important for ALL initiation or maintenance.

## 
IGF2BP1 and IGF2BP3

IGF2BP1 and IGF2BP3 are important for fetal organogenesis and are expressed in FL HSCs, but not in adult HSCs ( ). Induction of IGF2BP3 in adult HSCs induces a fetal-type output ( ; ).
IGF2BP1 and IGF2BP3 have been linked to leukemia, as well as solid malignancies, and are often co-expressed with LIN28B ( ; ). The mechanism of action for IGF2BP3 in oncogenesis is segregation of mRNA transcripts from the cytoplasmic RNA-induced silencing complex, including the let-7 miRNA family.
IGF2BP1 is linked to pediatric AML and ETV6/RUNX1 B-ALL, while IGF2BP3 is linked to AML, KMT2A::AFF1 ALL, and BCR::ABL1 ALL ( ; ; ; ). IGF2BP1 supports an LSC phenotype in AML ( ). In AML cell lines, knockdown of IGF2BP3 leads to reduced cell proliferation in an N6-methyladenosine (m6A)-dependent fashion ( ). Depletion of the murine paralog IGF2BP3 increases the latency of leukemia in murine models of KMT2A::AFF1 AML ( ).

## Fetal genes important for epigenetic regulation

## 
HMGA1 and 2

HMGA1 and HMGA2 are fetal oncogenes affecting epigenetic regulation. The HMGA family encodes proteins with AT hooks which interact with DNA to alter the chromatin architecture. These genes have much lower expression in adult tissues than in the fetal counterparts ( ; ), and HMGA1 can promote a pluripotent state ( ). HMGA2 is expressed mainly in fetal HSC/MPP and influences both differentiation and proliferation of stem cells ( ; ; ), as well as promoting long-term in vivo reconstitution by cord blood CD34+ cells ( ).
Reactivation of HMGA1 and HMGA2 has been demonstrated in a wide range of malignancies ( ; ) including leukemia ( ). HMGA1 expression has been linked to risk of relapse in pediatric B-ALL ( ). In pediatric and adult AML, high expression of HMGA2 is linked to poor prognosis, and knockdown of the gene has induced differentiation in primary blasts ( ; ). HMGA2 induces T-ALL in a Eμ-HMGA2 transgenic mouse ( ).

## microRNAs in leukemia
Aberrant expression of microRNAs (miRNAs) specific to fetal life and stem cell compartment ( ) is implicated in pediatric leukemia ( ; ) ( ). Pediatric AML can be risk-stratified by a 24-miRNA signature ( ). Eight of these have target genes within the pLSC6 signature and includes let-7 miRNAs (known repressors of oncogenes), with lower let-7g/let-7i expression in high-risk AML. One of the pLSC6 genes (FAM30A) is an lncRNA. Signatures based on lncRNA differentiate pediatric leukemia subtypes, but do not inform prognosis ( ).

## Targeting aberrant stem cell programs in leukemia for therapy
The inappropriate expression of stem cell genes, while conferring survival advantage to leukemic cells, can also render them dependent on specific proteins or pathways, and thus vulnerable to targeted disruption. Fetal stem cell genes are the most attractive targets as they are not expressed in healthy postnatal tissues, ameliorating concerns about off-target effects. Genes expressed in leukemic and healthy postnatal stem cells present more of a challenge. However, excessive leukemic reliance on the aberrant pathway, the so-called “oncogenic addiction,” can generate a therapeutic window, whereby leukemic cells can be killed while sparing normal stem cells. Potential targeting strategies are summarized in . Specific approaches relating to stem cell genes discussed in this review are explored below.

## Small-molecule inhibitors
Many stem cell genes code for TFs or other DNA-binding proteins, considered “undruggable,” owing to their intrinsically disordered nature. Recent improvements in screening methods have identified HMGA2-binding compounds, including the antimicrobials sumarin and ciclopirox ( ; ) and MEIS1/2 inhibitors ( ).
An alternative approach employs small molecules that bind the minor groove of the TF cognate sequence. DNA binders of this type can inhibit HOXA9 ( ), with in vitro activity against HOXA9-dependent cells lines ( ). Similarly, netropsin and trabectedin demonstrate antitumor activity in HMGA2+ neoplasia. Treatment with both drugs shows a synergistic anti-proliferative effect in infant ALL cell lines ( ). Other approaches to target HMGA2 include targeting downstream pathways such as G2M transition ( ) and PI3K/Akt/mTOR ( ).
TF function can also be impaired by preventing their expression. HOXA9 transcription is dependent on DOT1L-mediated H3K79 methylation in KMT2A-r leukemia. DOT1L has been successfully targeted with small molecules, pinometostat/EPZ5676 ( ; ). Early-phase clinical trials suggest modest activity against KMT2A-r leukemia ( ). Newer DOT1L inhibitors with oral availability and improved pharmacokinetics have been developed ( ; ).
HOXA9 is also dependent on the scaffold protein menin for expression in KMT2A-r leukemias ( ) and NPM1c-mutated AML ( ). The small-molecule revumenib prevents the interaction of menin with its target proteins. Revumenib induces remission in 30% of relapsed/refractory leukemia patients ( ), although mutations in MEN1 can lead to drug resistance ( ).
Direct inhibition of CDX2 has not yet been possible; however, the observation that PPARγ signaling restores KLF4 expression offers a potential therapeutic route to partially opposing CDX2 activity. PPARγ agonists are toxic to CDX2 overexpressing leukemia cell lines in vitro ( ; ).

## Targeting RNA-binding proteins
Like DNA-binding proteins, small-molecule inhibition of RBPs is difficult, although recent high-throughput approaches have generated candidates ( ). The most promising LIN28(A/B) inhibitor is C1632, which targets LIN28B + cell lines both by disruption of LIN28B–let-7 interaction ( ; ; ) and in Ewing sarcoma by disruption of the interaction between EWS-FLI1 mRNA and LIN28B ( ). Other molecules such as LI71 bind the cold shock domain and have efficacy against LIN28B + cancer cell lines ( ).
Small-molecule inhibitors of IGF2BP1 and IGF2BP3 are at the preclinical stage. BTYNB destabilizes oncogenic transcripts by disrupting the IGF2BP1–mRNA association ( ; ; ). Combining menin inhibitors with depletion of IGF2BP3 impairs cell growth and increases differentiation of KMT2A::AFF1 leukemia ( ).
Another potential strategy to boost let-7 miRNA expression, thus inhibiting several oncogenes ( ), has yet to be applied in leukemia.

## Immune effector cell therapy
Stem cell markers as targets for immunotherapy: The cell surface marker PROM1 is a highly attractive target for immunotherapy. A CD19/CD133 tandem CAR-T ( ) and CD19/133 bispecific CAR-iNKT ( ) show efficacy in vivo against KMT2A-r cell lines. However, valid concerns about stem cell toxicity when targeting CD133 in patients have been raised ( ). Preclinical testing and early-phase trials using CD133-CAR-T in solid malignancies ( ; ; ) revealed no BM aplasia and only transient, reversible hematological toxicities. Longer-term follow-up and assessment will be required to confirm the safety of CD133 targeting. CAR-T directed against NG2 has shown promise in mobilizing leukemic blasts and rendering them more sensitive to chemotherapy in mouse models ( ). Anti-CD117 CAR-T therapy shows preclinical efficacy ( ) but also eliminates healthy HSCs, necessitating novel approaches such as terminating “safety switches” ( ). Both CAR-T ( ) and CAR-NK ( ) cells have been used to target FLT3. Anti-CD123 CAR-T therapy has been used in early-stage clinical trials, appearing safe and potentially effective ( ; ).
Immunotherapy is particularly attractive in DS-associated leukemias where conventional treatments cause significant toxicities. In DS-ALL patients, CD19-, CD22-, and TSLPR-directed immunotherapies could yield promising results ( ; ).

## Discussion
Stem cells possess unique properties allowing the expansion, self-renewal, and differentiation required for tissue homeostasis. These programs are frequently co-opted by leukemias, where they provide growth and survival advantages. Although there is renewed interest in reprogramming adult HSCs to become more “fetal-like”, the potential of fetal stem cell genes to also promote oncogenesis must be considered.
Understanding stem cell programs in leukemia, including oncofetal genes, is vital to disentangling the biology of leukemias, including treatment resistance/relapse, and identifying mechanisms vulnerable to novel targeted therapies.
