According to data from the National Health Commission released in 2022, the total number of people older than 60 years is expected to exceed 300 million in 2025 in China. Clearly, the risks and challenges posed by population aging are pressing problems that need to be addressed. Recently, clinical research on stem cells in hematopoietic system aging has been reported.[ ] In this review, we analyze the precise relationships between niche and HSC and aging by summarizing the causes of, markers of, and signalling pathways related to hematopoietic aging. We also discuss recent findings and research strategies for inducing the rejuvenation of the aging HSCs and hematopoietic niche. Identifying strategies for reversing aging or alleviating aging-related hematopoietic dysfunction will aid the development of therapeutic strategies and contribute to achieving the goal of slowing aging.

## Seed of hematopoiesis: HSCs
Hematopoiesis typically involves the development, differentiation, proliferation, and migration of HSCs, which are the seeds of bone marrow hematopoietic system.[ ] There are three main phases of the development of HSCs, including the pedigree development of HSCs in yolk sac and aorta-gonad-mesonephros (AGM) in embryonic phase, the differentiation and selection of functional HSCs in other peripheral organs including fetal liver, and the migration to bone marrow and becoming the target organ of lifelong hematopoiesis.[ ] The origin theory, lineage differentiation, and hematopoietic migration of HSCs are fully studied. Presently, the long-term expansion of HSCs and the maintenance of self-renewal are the main challenges that plague clinical transplantation. With the help of multi-omics technology and its target gene therapy, more medical gaps can be filled.

## Birthplace of hematopoiesis: Niche
Niches are local tissue microenvironments that maintain and regulate stem cells or progenitors. Hematopoietic niche, which refers to the spatial location and unique microenvironment of HSCs in bone marrow with both hematopoietic and immune cells, was collectively named in 1978.[ ] HSCs rely on their niches within the regulatory network that controls the activity and function maintenance of hematopoietic cells. HSCs develop in hematopoietic niches that is a specific microenvironment composed of maintaining, locating, and expanding HSCs in spation.[ ] Notably, the hematopoietic niche is a complex macromolecular network system composed of bone marrow endothelial cells (ECs), stromal cells, osteoblasts, adipocytes, CXCL12-abundant reticular (CAR) cells, hematopoietic cells, extracellular matrix related to bone trabeculae, and various other cells.[ , ] The maintenance of niche stability depends on the positive regulation of hypoxia-inducible factor 1α (HIF-1α) and HIF-2α, which are located on the HSC surface; therefore, the niche exhibits low levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS), making it conducive to the maintenance of biological characteristics such as self-renewal and differentiation.[ ]

## Characteristics and Harms of Hematopoietic Aging
With increasing age, various tissues, organs, and systems undergo varying degrees of functional decline. The typical characteristics of hematopoietic aging in wild-type (WT) mice include senile anemia, decreased lymphocyte numbers, and slightly increased platelet and neutrophil numbers.[ ] Correspondingly, elderly people have been found to exhibit significant changes, including increase in the adipose cells number, the platelet ratio, and clonal hematopoiesis. Aging alterations in hematopoiesis, such as clonal output delay and myeloid cell differentiation bias, are also observed in nonhuman primates (NHPs) and mice.
Declines in hematopoietic function are reflected by reduced hematopoietic cell reconstitution and bone marrow homing ability. An increasing number of changes related to the hematopoietic system, including immune aging, pedigree deviation, and a decrease in the number of bone marrow cells, have been verified to be related to aging in humans.[ ] The aging of the hematopoietic system is manifested by a decline in immune function, weakened stress responses, a reduction in the regenerative potential of HSCs, and increased susceptibility to blood diseases, including leukemia.[ ] Immature HSC accumulation and bone marrow cell differentiation bias are recognized indicators of hematopoietic aging in humans. Ultimately, aging leads to deterioration of hematopoietic immune organs throughout the body, causing multiple organ dysfunction and overall aging.

## Causes of Hematopoietic Aging
Overall, hematopoietic aging includes internal HSC and external niche aging, mainly manifesting as aging replication, DNA damage, oxidative stress, cell polarity loss, and mitochondrial dysfunction.[ ] Additionally, almost all the data obtained to date suggest that epigenetic modifications, alterations in chromatin structure, autophagy, protein homeostasis, and metabolic changes are directly involved in hematopoietic aging. This review reports findings specifically describing the causes of hematopoietic aging [Figure ]. We hope to comprehensively elucidate the mechanism of hematopoietic aging to identify a strategy for delaying aging in elderly individuals.
 

## HSCs aging
As the origin of most blood cells and immune cells, HSCs are critical for maintaining hematopoietic system homeostasis and repairing pathological hematopoietic functions in aging. In aging mice, the number of myeloid HSCs is increased slightly, but the number of platelet-biased HSCs is increased by approximately 60%, and the total number of lymphoid HSCs remains unchanged.[ ] HSC aging exhibits species diversity. For example, aged C57 mice have approximately threefold more HSCs than young mice, consistent with the increase in the number of HSCs in aging humans. However, the number of HSCs in aged BALB/c mice is decreased slightly.[ ] A balance between self-renewal and differentiation is essential for the maintenance of the hematopoietic cell pool and long-term hematopoiesis; therefore, determining whether reversal of HSC aging via rejuvenation can restore hematopoiesis in elderly individuals is important.
The aging of HSCs, like other tissues and organs, is an aging of hematopoietic system, and an effector of aging diseases. As their functions decline with age, HSCs gradually lose their self-renewal and hematopoietic capacities, which ultimately leads to systemic disorders such as senile anemia, hyperplasia, and leukemia, further accelerating whole-tissue and organ aging.[ , ] Additionally, HSC aging is the major cause of immune cell aging and the increased incidence of blood tumors in elderly individuals.[ ] Hence, HSC aging induces declines in the hematopoietic system and immune function and delays the repair of tissue and organ damage. And, we believe that reversed aging caused by HSCs can rebalance the hematopoietic system and improve the physiological functions of many tissues and organs.

## Oxidative damage is an important factor of hematopoietic aging
Oxidative stress is highly correlated with hematopoietic aging. One of the remarkable characteristics of aging is the increase in mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation.[ ] The ROS level is an indicator of hematopoietic homeostasis, and ROS are key regulators of aging; an increase in the ROS level in the niche impairs hematopoietic reconstitution aided by bone marrow stromal cells and endothelial cells (ECs).[ ] Cytokine storms rapidly increase the ROS levels in the niche, inducing caspase-1-dependent HSC death through cascade reactions initiated by the nucleotide-binding and oligomerization domain (NOD)-like receptor protein 3 (NLRP3)–caspase-1 axis, thus causing many HSCs lose their hematopoietic function.[ ] In addition, nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD), an indispensable cofactor involved in several redox reactions, has been found to participate in regulating metabolism, DNA methylation, neurodegeneration, physiological aging, and microenvironmental changes during hematopoiesis.
The aging phenotype caused by increasing ROS levels in young individuals is similar to that in elderly individuals and involves myeloid cell differentiation bias. However, ROS exert dual effects on the hematopoietic system.[ ] A low ROS level within a certain range promotes the proliferation and differentiation of HSCs and prevents HSC dysfunction; in contrast, high ROS levels directly impairs hematopoietic support of stromal cells by disturbing the physiological balance of the niche.[ ] ROS play an important role in HSC pool maintenance, blood cell mobilization, stem cell self-renewal, and hematopoietic cell differentiation. The use of antioxidants restores the vitality of aging HSCs and prolongs survival in mice, but whether these compounds can truly and effectively reverse aging-related hematopoietic dysfunction is unclear.

## DNA damage and protein imbalance accelerate hematopoietic aging
DNA damage drives the acquisition of the aging-like phenotype by HSCs. Studies have shown that upregulated p21 in HSCs promotes DNA damage and ultimately leads to hematopoietic failure.[ ] The nonhomologous end-joining (NHEJ) pathway is an important factor involved in DNA repair; KU70 is upstream of this DNA repair pathway, and its deficiency is related to aging-related remodelling of the niche and hematopoietic dysfunction.[ ] Overall, the response of HSCs to cyclin and DNA damage can determine whether cells undergo aging or regeneration.
Additionally, hematopoietic aging has been found to be associated with protein homeostasis dysregulation. For example, Kruta et al[ ] first reported that decreased heat shock factor 1 (HSF1) nuclear expression in mice is directly associated with low hematopoietic reconstitution after transplantation.In addition, HSCs secrete many matrix proteins after interleukin-6 (IL-6) and transforming growth factor β (TGF-β) accumulation, eventually leading to hematopoietic aging.[ ] Therefore, a change in protein levels in HSCs is a candidate marker of hematopoietic aging. In summary, protecting DNA integrity and protein stability can greatly reduce the incidence of hematopoietic system diseases at the cellular level.

## Genetic modifications and epigenetic alterations cause hematopoietic aging
DNA methylation and histone modification in aging are mainly involved in the regulation of HSC proliferation and differentiation.[ ] Sera et al[ ] reported that the demethylase ubiquitously transcribed tetratricopeptide repeat on chromosome X (UTX) maintains the integrity and stability of the hematopoietic system by promoting the expression of antiaging genes. Therefore, genome-wide hypomethylation and inhibitory chromatin recombination may be important causes of HSC aging and even hematopoietic aging.
HSC aging is a result of epigenetic transcriptome alterations.[ ] Aging HSCs are prone to epigenetic modification drift, which eventually leads to network remodelling, HSC pluripotency heterogeneity, lineage bias, and clonal hematopoiesis, and increases the risk of developing various types of leukemias. Notably, the transcriptional profile and epigenetic changes of HSCs in aged male mice are very similar to those of leukemia stem cells, revealing a correlation between aging and myeloid tumors.[ ] All the above results indicate that there is a significant correlation between HSC aging and abnormal genetic modification, and precise regulation of its modification sites will help to diagnose the hematological causes of aging and establish effective targeted therapy strategies.
Most members of the sirtuin family protect against hematopoietic aging. Specifically, sirtuin 1 (SIRT1), sirtuin 3 (SIRT3), sirtuin 6 (SIRT6), and sirtuin 7 (SIRT7) have been shown to exert antiaging effects on HSCs.[ , ] Additionally, SIRT1 maintains hematopoietic homeostasis through increasing the expression of antiaging genes, and knocking down SIRT1 increases the expression of p16 and p53.[ ] Findings on the role of sirtuins in aging, which mainly highlight their function in hematopoiesis, are shown in Table .
 

## Niche aging
Bone marrow is the home of the hematopoietic niche, and its aging drives hematopoietic aging. The academic term "niche aging" was coined based on bone marrow aging and microenvironment aging.[ ] Niche subsets aging significantly reduced the support and regulation of the hematopoietic niche by stem cells and indirectly induced hematopoietic aging. The aging bone marrow niche weakens the potential of MSCs to differentiate into osteoblasts and related osteoblasts, while increases the potential to differentiate into adipocytes, causing HSCs to acquire an aging-like phenotype.[ ] Moreover, support for three-lineage hematopoiesis by the hematopoietic niche is reduced in aging mice. The hematopoietic niche is essential for daily hematopoiesis; therefore, niche aging can lead to hematopoietic dysfunction in elderly individuals. Fortunately, the application of stem cells can compensate for the impaired function of the aging niche, as has been demonstrated in the field of antiaging research, and targeting hematopoietic niche is expected to successfully reverse hematopoietic aging.

## Aging-related clonal hematopoiesis
Mutated genes have gradually become a new target for the treatment of aging-related diseases. Mutations in the Tet2 and DNMT3A genes driven by the inflammatory factors interferon gamma (IFN-γ) and tumour necrosis factor alpha (TNF-α) in the niche cause gene defects in HSCs and the replacement of normal hematopoiesis with clonal hematopoiesis in the bone marrow of elderly individuals.[ ] In clonal hematopoiesis, HSCs carrying mutations produce many mature blood cells through cloning and amplification. Specifically, aging of the hematopoietic niche benefits the active cloning of mature hematopoietic cells, thereby reducing the functional diversity of the HSC pool and eventually leading to hematopoietic system deterioration and aging via myeloid hematopoiesis and ineffective hematopoiesis. Subsequently, the age-related decline in the niche composition directly causes hematopoietic aging.[ ] The aging niche inhibits the development and differentiation of HSC pedigree genes and weakens the supporting role played by stromal cells in hematopoiesis, leading to the formation of additional aging niches and ultimately to hematopoietic aging through cascade reactions.
Ho et al[ ] noted that adipocyte lineage cells impair osteogenesis and bone regeneration in the microenvironment, resulting in pathological remodelling of the hematopoietic niche, microenvironment disorders, HSC dysfunction, and aging. Interestingly, the aging niche drives the biased differentiation of bone marrow cells and megakaryocytes, accelerating the deleterious hematopoietic behaviour underlying aging. Moreover, pathological degeneration of the sympathetic nervous system is a direct driving factor of hematopoietic niche aging.[ ] The aging of the hematopoietic niche may lead to hematopoietic system aging by reducing the synergistic effects of HSCs and bone marrow stromal cells, resulting in the accumulation of aging HSCs and expansion of the hematopoietic niche pool. Briefly, hyperplasia may be a direct contributor to hematopoietic aging, and it is also the main marker for evaluating the aging of the hematopoietic niche.

## Bone marrow-biased differentiation shapes the microenvironment of aging
With increasing age, the number of stem cell groups in the body decreases, functionality of HSC is reduced, and the mutual adjustment capacity of adjacent cells is weakened. Age-dependent insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF1) and insulin-like growth factor-2 (IGF2) expression in the microenvironment decreases the potential for HSC restoration and exacerbates overall aging.[ ] An inflammatory microenvironment, which includes interleukin-1α/β (IL-1α/β), TNF-α, IFN-γ, Wnt5a, and other factors, significantly remodels the bone marrow composition, accelerating the aging of the hematopoietic niche.[ ] Aging of the bone marrow microenvironment is manifested mainly as structural disorder, an increase in the amount of bone marrow fat, especially fat cell infiltration, and bone marrow bias.[ ] Therefore, it has been hypothesized that bone marrow adipocytes drive hematopoietic aging.
Aging-related decreases in lymphoid differentiation and increases in myeloid differentiation of HSCs are a condition called bone marrow bias.[ ] Bone marrow bias is associated with age-related clonal myeloid output and epigenetic modifications in HSCs.[ ] Aging-related bone marrow contraction in the bone intima and reductions in osteopontin (OPN) secretion by the extracellular matrix affect the formation of lymphocyte aggregates, ultimately leading to a bone marrow cell-biased phenotype. Bone marrow cell bias is related to hematopoietic aging caused by increase in the secretion of proinflammatory factors and chemokines by constituent cells in the niche, and C-C motif chemokine ligand 5 (CCL5) is considered a candidate marker of the niche aging.[ ] Additionally, plasma cells in the bone marrow secrete large amounts of matrix components, thereby promoting the expression of inflammation-related genes and increasing the hematopoietic differentiation of HSCs in myelopoiesis.[ ] Overall, these findings suggest that bone marrow biased differentiation and inflammatory microenvironment jointly shape the aging hematopoietic niche.

## Molecules in hematopoietic aging
The number of HSCs in the aging hematopoietic niche is increased in a compensatory manner. Additionaly, the niche and the composition or arrangement of molecules are significantly altered, which eventually leads to hematopoietic aging. Key factors, such as stem cell factor (SCF), C-X-C motif ligand 12 (CXCL12), IL-7, and Jagged1, were found to be produced mainly by stromal cells in a paracrine manner and to support hematopoiesis.[ , ] MSCs in the central bone marrow area play a role in regulating hematopoiesis by producing the lineage-specific cytokines SCF, vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), IL-7, and vascular cell adhesion molecule 1 (VCAM1).[ , , ] Bone marrow ECs not only provide the framework of the vascular network, but also is the main sources of hematopoietic cytokines, such as CXCL12, Jagged1/2, Wnt5a, and epidermal growth factor (EGF) in the niche.[ ] Studies have found that ECs not only maintain the stability of physiological hematopoiesis but also promote recovery from hematopoietic aging. Therefore, if HSCs lose hematopoietic support function from stromal cells, multiple cells will undergo aging, ultimately leading to hematopoietic failure. Hence, age-related hematopoietic disorders of the niche may be clearly identified by the changes in abovementioned molecules.
As with the aging of other organs, the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) plays an important role in niche aging. Under physiological conditions, the molecules IL-3 and TGF-β in the niche activate Toll-like receptors on the surface of HSCs, promoting their proliferation and differentiation, and continuously promoting the anti-inflammatory functions of macrophages.[ , ] Hematopoietic dysfunction caused by inflammatory factors, such as IL-1, IL-6, and TNF-α, is characterized by HSC proliferation arrest and differentiation imbalance.[ ] Considering the multiple biological characteristics of stem cells in vivo, we speculate that attenuating the hematopoietic damage caused by the aging niche with respect to inflammation is a promising therapeutic strategy.

## Signalling Pathways in Hematopoietic Aging
Analysing the role of aging-related signalling molecules in blood diseases and constructing a complete hematopoietic aging regulation network have far-reaching research significance for aging remodelling and the treatment of hematopoietic niche aging.
The p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase (p38MAPK) pathway mediates aging-related hematopoiesis, including that of the stress-related niche and post-transplant reconstruction.[ ] Additionally, the p16, nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB), TGF-β, and Wnt signalling pathways regulate hematopoietic niche aging.[ , ] Targeting p21, p38, and mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) effectively reverses HSC dysfunction and significantly improves the hematopoietic function in aged mice.[ ] Moreover, aggregation of TNF-α in the niche mediates the extracellular regulated kinase-E26 transformation specific 1 (ERK-ETS1) pathway, leading to the binding of interleukin 27 (IL-27) and its ligand in HSCs and triggering HSC failure.[ ] IGF2 is a cytokine that is positively related to hematopoietic aging and activates osteoblast differentiation; p16Ink4a is a negative regulator that participates in the apoptosis of HSCs mediated by the mTORC2-Akt signalling pathway to balance HSC output.[ ] Notch pathway is essential to maintain the stemness of stem cells, and the increase of Notch receptor 1 (Notch1) protein expression is beneficial to the self-renewal of HSC. And the ability of multi-line colony formation depends on Notch1 and the target protein hairy and enhancer of split 1 (Hes1), which are upstream factors in the classical Wnt5a signalling.[ ] And the Wnt5a/Prox1 signalling axis is a regulator of aging HSC regeneration.[ ] The Wnt pathway inhibition is also associated with polarity loss and epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT); and targeted Wnt5a accelerates hematopoietic reconstitution of bone marrow.[ ] Hence, classical Wnt5a signalling pathways play decisive roles in maintaining hematopoietic differentiation and aging remodelling and, to some extent, maintain the homeostasis of the niche. In the process of hematopoietic aging, the secretion of stromal cell-derived factor-l (SDF1) is substantially decreased, and the level of its receptor molecule C-X-C chemokine receptor 4 (CXCR4) is age-dependent, which may change the way HSCs enter the hematopoietic niche to obstruct hematopoiesis.[ ] Additionally, the CXCR4/SDF-1 axis plays an important role in regulating the interaction between hematopoietic and niche factors, and its disruption induces aging-related HSC mobilization, ultimately leading to stem cell exhaustion.[ ] Targeting SDF1 and CXCR4 signalling molecules in bone marrow may help delay hematopoietic function decline. We speculate that the abnormal interaction of above signalling axis in the niche directly affects the number and activity of adult stem cells and ultimately causes aging hematopoiesis through the aging of HSCs. In general, hematopoietic aging involves many signal pathways, including inflammatory stimulation, oxidative stress, and metabolic interaction, but the upstream and downstream molecules in each pathway and their corresponding regulatory networks have not been reported in detail, and its molecular mechanism needs to be further clarified. The abovementioned data provide a new opportunity to dissect the complex mechanism of aging-related hematopoiesis. ## New Approaches to Hematopoietic Niche Rejuvenation
Exogenous stimuli, such as inflammation and aging, cause the hematopoietic niche to undergo aging-related remodelling; but blocking aging-related phenotypic transformation and polarity deterioration in the hematopoietic niche have been proven to markedly reverse hematopoietic aging.
Aging-related bone marrow failure affects not only the output of hematopoietic cells but also hematopoietic support from nonhematopoietic cells. Specifically, increased secretion of the chemokine regulated upon activation normal T-cell expressed and presumably secreted (RANTES) by the aging niche promotes HSC aging, but inhibition of its secretion may significantly increase the output ratio of peripheral blood T lymphocytes.[ ] Inhibition of the activity of cell division cycle 42 (CDC42) has been used to successfully block chromatin aggregation in aging HSCs; this experiment was the first to show that the vitality of HSCs can be restored by remodelling the chromatin structure with drugs.[ ] Similarly, the CDC42 activity-specific inhibitor (CASIN) significantly increases histone H4 lysine16 acetylation (H4K16Ac) levels in aging HSCs and reverses the hematopoietic aging phenotype.[ ] Targeting HIF transcription factors that participate in the ROS production pathway also significantly improves the prognosis of patients with hematopoietic system diseases by remodelling the niche composition.[ ] Furthermore, supplementation with a β3-adrenergic receptor (β3-AR) agonist increases the innervation of sympathetic nerves in the bone marrow and enhances hematopoiesis.[ ] In summary, the reconstruction strategy of targeting specific cells or chromosomes in the aging niche is expected to rejuvenate the hematopoietic niche on the molecular level. However, the poor specificity of micromolecular drugs and the potential for developing resistance to these drugs are challenges impeding the rejuvenation of the blood system.
With the development of somatic cell reprogramming, induced hematopoietic cells (iHCs) have been generated from induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) or fibroblasts.[ ]
In vitro experiments have shown that the gene expression profile and cell-like phenotype of iHCs are highly similar to those of HSCs, but iHCs cannot maintain long-term hematopoietic function. In addition, aged HSCs were reprogrammed to iPSCs and then differentiated into new HSCs to replace old HSCs in vivo by blastocyst complementation.[ ] Strategies for reprogramming aging adult stem cells into functional cells are expected to aid stem cell-mediated treatment of hematopoietic aging.
Embryonic stem cell (ESC) transplantation has greatly reduced the number of senile cells in bone marrow and improved the cell composition of the hematopoietic niche.[ ] Therefore, reprogrammed ESCs are used to treat ischemic diseases because of their unique proliferation, survival characteristics, and hematopoietic derivation. Leung et al[ ] developed a cell culture system and found that hematopoietic stem/progenitor cells (HSPCs) modified with ESC or iPSC genes produced myeloid and lymphoid hematopoietic cells with an enhanced colony-forming ability and hemoglobin expression to promote hematopoiesis in elderly individuals. If we can simulate the hematopoietic niche with nonhematopoietic adult cells and HSCs in vitro with the help of soft tissue materials, we will be able to fully analyse the mechanisms of exogenous stem cell remodelling, hematopoietic aging, and hematopoietic niche aging.
In summary, improved adult stem cells or functional hematopoietic cells play an important role in hematopoietic aging intervention or immune function reconstruction. It is speculated that endowing nonhematopoietic stem cells with hematopoietic function can overcome issues related to HSC transplantation, such as donor source and immune rejection. The application prospects of stem cells for the treatment of aging related hematopoietic diseases are broad. ## Conclusions
The balance between HSCs and aging cells in the aging niche is disrupted, which eventually leads to the progressive decline of multi-organ function and causes many age-related diseases. Significant age-related HSC remodelling occurs as a result of niche aging, but further research is needed to clarify the related pathogenic mechanism. At present, the biggest challenge in studying hematopoietic aging is the significant differences among species, as the aging phenotype is not the same even in different kinds of mice. In addition, the lack of technologies for inducing hematopoietic niche impairment in vivo or generating artificial bone marrow in vitro limits our in-depth study of hematopoietic aging-related diseases, and it is difficult to evaluate the efficacy of stem cells in reversing hematopoietic aging and restoring hematopoietic function. Fully analysing the cellular and molecular mechanism associated with hematopoietic aging and applying stem cell-mediated therapies to slow aging will help to not only enhance HSC regeneration and hematopoietic restoration in healthy individuals but also delay aging in elderly individuals. Fortunately, the current single-cell sequencing technology can help us identify the types and functional heterogeneity of blood cell populations at different ages, allowing us to track the changes associated with hematopoietic aging. It is important to clarify the compositional and spatial changes between the normal and aging hematopoietic niches.
One of the main challenges in this century is the aging of the population. Therefore, new strategies to delay aging and promote hematopoietic regeneration are urgently needed. Stem cells are important for maintaining the steady state of the body and the coordination and interaction of various systems, and their aging is the fundamental cause for the aging of the body. Thanks to the in-depth study of the molecular mechanisms of MSC and HSC aging and regeneration, stem cell therapies will be applied to combat aging-induced hematopoietic dysfunction and delay aging via the prevention or remission of age-related blood system diseases. At present, research on the treatment of hematopoietic aging focuses on micromolecular drugs and HSC transplantation. However, whether these strategies can restore the hematopoietic niche or alleviate hematopoietic niche aging has not been reported. Eliminating aging cells by reducing SASP-induced niche aging can allow the restoration of a younger niche, which may inhibit niche aging and reverse aging-related precancerous mutations. Therefore, it is necessary to identify the mechanism underlying niche aging to aid the restoration of the hematopoietic niche and reverse hematopoietic aging.
