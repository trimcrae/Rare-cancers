## Abstract
Acute leukemias of ambiguous lineage (ALAL) is a rare type of hematologic malignancies with poor outcomes. Currently, the treatment for this type of leukemia lack standardized protocols and exhibit significant heterogeneity, necessitating the exploration of novel, targeted approaches. We retrospectively analyzed the clinical characteristics, efficacy, and safety of 13 newly diagnosed Philadelphia chromosome (Ph)-negative ALAL patients between May 2022 and March 2025, who received induction therapy with the mini-CVD regimen (cyclophosphamide, vincristine, dexamethasone) combined with venetoclax and azacitidine. The cohort comprised 8 males and 5 females, with a median age of 53 years (range, 28–73 years). Among this cohort, 92.3% of patients (12/13) achieved complete remission (CR). Three patients underwent allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (allo-HSCT) during CR1, and one patient received salvage allo-HSCT following relapse. With a median follow-up of 9.0 months (range 1.2–35.4 months), the overall survival rate was 84.6% (11/13). One patient died due to primary resistance with sustained disease progression, and another succumbed to post-transplantation complications. Of the 12 patients who achieved CR, 2 (15.4%) experienced relapsed, but both attained a second CR after salvage therapy. During induction therapy, the overall incidence of infection was 76.9% (10/13), with the majority being pulmonary infections (5/10). Notably, there was no death during induction therapy. Our data demonstrated that this regimen showed a high CR rate, with manageable toxicity, offering a promising therapeutic approach for this rare and challenging leukemia subtype.
Keywords: Acute leukemia of ambiguous lineage (ALAL), Mini-CVD, Venetoclax, Azacitidine

## Introduction
Acute leukemia comprises a heterogeneous group of hematological malignancies, among which acute leukemia of ambiguous lineage (ALAL) represents a particularly challenging subset due to its biological complexity and low incidence [ ]. Despite its rarity, ALAL is associated with a poor prognosis, underscoring the critical need for optimized therapeutic strategies. Currently, the treatment approaches for this type of leukemia globally lack standardized protocols and exhibit significant heterogeneity, necessitating the exploration of novel, targeted approaches.
Previous investigations have shed light on the critical role of hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) as a potentially curative option for ALAL patients. However, its success heavily depends on achieving disease remission prior to transplantation [ , ]. This underscores a pressing challenge: the necessity of inducing primary disease remission through chemotherapy to enhance overall survival. In this context, the search for optimal chemotherapeutic regimens takes center stage in improving the prognosis of ALAL patients.
Pediatric studies have demonstrated superior outcomes with acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL)-type chemotherapy compared to acute myeloid leukemia (AML)-oriented protocols, suggesting a potential framework for ALAL treatment [ , – ]. Furthermore, venetoclax, a selective BCL-2 inhibitor, has emerged as a breakthrough therapy in both AML and ALL [ – ], with preliminary data from our center indicating promising efficacy in relapsed/refractory ALAL [ ]. Building on these insights, we developed the “mini-CVD + VA” regimen, which integrates ALL-inspired chemotherapy (cyclophosphamide, vincristine, dexamethasone) with venetoclax and azacitidine (VA)—a combination leveraging the strengths of both lymphoid and myeloid-directed therapies. We hypothesize that this dual-targeting strategy could enhance remission rates and survival while maintaining a favorable safety profile for ALAL patients.

## Materials and methods

## Patients
 We retrospectively reviewed records of 13 adult (≥ 18 years old) patients initially treated for Philadelphia chromosome(Ph)-negative ALAL with mini-CVD+VA chemotherapy regimen at the Institute of Hematology and Blood Diseases Hospital in Tianjin, China, from May 2022 and March 2025. The study was approved by our institution’s Institutional Review Board. Patients with ALAL, as defined by the 2022 WHO Classification [ ]. Data collected included age, gender, complete blood counts, karyotype, gene mutations, treatment response, time to progression, and time to death, etc. Responses were defined according to 2022 ELN for acute myeloid leukemia [ ].

## Treatment protocol
 Mini-CVD A regimen plus VA was used as induction therapy: cyclophosphamide 150 mg/m² every 12 hours on days 1-3; vincristine 2 mg on days 4 and 11; dexamethasone 10-20 mg on days 1-4 and 11-14; venetoclax, dose ramp-up: 100 mg (day 4), 200 mg (day 5), and 400 mg (days 6-12); and azacitidine 50 mg/m² on days 4-10. Consolidation therapy regimen post-induction remission: mini-CVD B-like regimen: cytarabine (0.5–1 g/m² every 12 hours, days 1–3) and venetoclax (400 mg, days 1–7), alternated with the A regimen. Transplant-eligible patients proceeded to allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (allo-HSCT). (shown in Fig. ).
 

## Statistical analysis
 Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism version 9 or R software (version 4.4.1). Continuous variables were summarized as median (range), while categorical variables were presented as counts and percentages. The overall survival(OS) was measured from day one of chemotherapy until the date of death. The recurrence free survival(RFS) was measured from the date of initial confirmed CR until the date of relapse or death.

## Results

## Patient demographics and baseline characteristics
 Thirteen patients with newly diagnosed Ph- negative ALAL between May 2022 and March 2025 were included in the study. The cohort comprised 8 males and 5 females, with a median age of 53 years (range, 28–73 years). Characteristics of the 13 patients are shown in Table . The diagnostic subtypes were as follows: Mixed-phenotype acute leukemia (MPAL)- T/myeloid 4 cases, MPAL-B/myeloid 4 cases, acute undifferentiated leukemia (AUL) 1 case, and ALAL not otherwise specified (NOS) 4 cases. Recurrent genetic abnormalities were observed in 4 patients (30.8%), including KMT2A rearrangement (2 cases), EVI1 rearrangement (1 case), and CALM-AF10 fusion (1 case). Analysis of next-generation sequencing (NGS) gene mutation results showed a mutational spectrum dominated by mutations associated with myeloid neoplasms, with RUNX1 (30.8%) and IKZF1 (23.1%) mutations being most frequent. Notably, none of the 13 patients harbored common AML-associated mutations such as FLT3-ITD or NPM1.
 

## Treatment response
 Among the 13 patients, one (patient 8) demonstrated primary resistance, another (patient 4) achieved morphological CR after two induction treatments. The remaining 11 patients achieved morphological CR after the initial induction treatment, the overall CR rate is 92.3% (12/13) (Fig.  ). Among the 12 patients who had assessable flow cytometry measurable residual disease (MRD) after the initial induction treatment, the MRD negativity rate was 41.7% (5/12). Additionally, 11 had assessable MRD after two cycles, and the cumulative negative rate after two cycles was 81.8% (9/11).
 

## Safety profile
 During induction therapy, the total incidence rate of infection was 76.9% (10/13), with the majority being pulmonary infections (5 cases). Other infections included febrile neutropenia (without a clear focus of infection), upper respiratory tract infections, skin and soft tissue infections, gastrointestinal infections, and sepsis. There was no death during induction therapy.
 The median time from discontinuation of venetoclax to the recovery of white blood cells to 1×10⁹/L was 10 days (range 0-33 days); the median time from discontinuation of venetoclax to the recovery of platelets to ≥20×10⁹/L was 0 days (range, 0-30 days), with 7 patients not requiring platelet transfusions during induction therapy.

## Survival outcomes
 As shown in Fig.2, 4 of 13 patients underwent allo-HSCT. Three received the transplant in first complete remission (CR1; patients 3, 5 and 13), and one (patient 6) received salvage allo-HSCT after relapse. After a median follow-up of 9.0 months (range 1.2- 35.4 months), the OS rate was 84.6% (11/13). Two deaths were observed: one attributed to primary refractory disease with persistent progression (patient 8) and the other to transplant-related complications (patient 13).
 Among the 12 patients who achieved CR, two (16.7%) relapsed. Patient 6 relapsed 5 months after CR1, was refractory to salvage chemotherapy, but survived following salvage allo-HSCT. Patient 7 relapsed 23 months after CR1, achieved second complete remission (CR2) with salvage therapy and remained in CR2 at the last follow-up.

## Discussion
 ALAL is a heterogeneous leukemia subgroup, and optimizing its diagnostic and treatment strategies is a clinical challenge. Per the 2022 WHO criteria, about 80% of ALAL cases meet the criteria for MPAL, marked by blasts co-expressing myeloid (e.g., MPO) and lymphoid (CD3/CD19) markers, with rare cases showing B/T/myeloid triple phenotypes. The rest are categorized as acute undifferentiated leukemia (AUL) or ALAL-NOS due to a lack of definitive lineage-specific markers. Although ALAL accounts for only 3-5% of new acute leukemia cases, its clinical outcome is significantly worse than typical AML or ALL, with a 5-year overall survival rate below 40%. This dilemma stems from two factors: firstly, the current diagnostic system relies on limited immunophenotypic markers (MPO, CD3, CD19, etc.), which cannot accurately reflect the biological nature of the disease; secondly, the low incidence has caused a lag in basic research, especially in understanding its molecular pathogenesis and therapeutic targets.
 It should be noted that the current classification system may underestimate the biological commonalities between different clinical entities. A typical example is the differentiation between T/myeloid MPAL and early T-cell precursor acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ETP-ALL). Despite differences in MPO expression being used as a diagnostic boundary, they show significant overlap in stem cell marker expression (e.g., CD34, CD117), high-frequency mutated genes (WT1, RUNX1, FLT3), and transcriptional profiles. The molecular convergence suggests that the traditional immunophenotype-based classification may not effectively guide treatment decisions, and there is an urgent need to redefine biologically relevant subgroups through multi-omics research.
 Currently, there is no unified, standardized, and effective chemotherapy regimen internationally. Building on our preliminary findings of a 100% CR rate in 6 relapsed/refractory ALAL (NOS) patients treated with venetoclax combined with azacitidine or chemotherapy [ ], we developed the novel mini-CVD+VA regimen for newly diagnosed ALAL. Compared to efficacious regimens like ALL-based hyperCVAD [ ] or hybrid CLAG-M [ ], our regimen raised the CR rate from about 70% to 92.3%, with a novel 81.8% flow cytometry MRD conversion rate after two courses. The mechanism may involve multi-level synergistic effects. For example, venetoclax may enhance tumor cells' sensitivity to the epigenetic reprogramming induced by azacitidine by inhibiting BCL-2 proteins. Additionally, low-dose cyclophosphamide (150 mg/m²) maintains lymphoid-lineage-specific killing activity while significantly reducing bone marrow suppression, a crucial advantage for middle-aged and elderly patients with poor hematopoietic reserves.
 While recent studies of regimens like FLAG-Ida [ ] have shown good efficacy in ALAL, they are associated with significantly higher treatment-related toxicity. In our cohort, infections—predominantly pulmonary—were the most frequent adverse events (76.9%), yet they were effectively managed through a stratified prophylactic strategy combining antifungal/antiviral agents with granulocyte-colony-stimulating factor. Notably, our regimen had a zero treatment-related mortality rate, outperforming traditional intensive chemotherapy. This efficient and low-toxic advantage may be more suitable for patients with organ dysfunction or the elderly.

## Conclusion
 In conclusion, we demonstrate that the mini-CVD+VA regimen for ALAL offers high remission rates and controllable toxicity. Due to the rarity of this disease, clinical trial data to guide optimal therapy are lacking, mini-CVD+VA appears to be an effective regimen, and may also be an appropriate backbone for incorporation of novel therapies for ALAL in future prospective studies.
