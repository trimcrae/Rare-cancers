## Abstract
Mesenchymal stem/stromal cells (MSCs) therapy has undoubtedly become one of the most popular treatment methods used in tissue regeneration and bioengineering in both pre-clinical and clinical trials. MSCs-based therapy has been characterized to have several benefits, mainly supporting and accelerating tissue regeneration, but at the same time it is still subjected to certain risks. The ideal MSCs population that could be used for clinical purposes should be characterized by superior homogeneity, proper safety level, and great clinical efficacy with limited or no side effects. Recently discovered, dedifferentiated fat progenitors (DFATs) could be the perfect candidates for regenerative medicine. The growing number of studies indicates DFATs superior properties compared to widely used adipose derived stem/stromal cells (ASCs). Some potentially significant differences between ASCs and DFATs have been observed in the process of isolation, their surface marker expression, proliferation ability, differentiation capacity, and expression of pluripotency-related genes. DFATs have also been shown to pose some beneficial effects in animal studies, for instance, they might contribute to bone and cartilage repair, periodontal tissue regeneration, neovascularization, skin and fat grafting, nerve tissue recovery, and intestine restoration in vivo. However, most of the published reports do not compare DFATs function directly to the well-known and used ASCs. Already found differences are inconsistent and not evaluated. Despite the growing interest in the function of DFATs in recent years, the lack of comprehensive and comparative analyses with ASCs represents a significant gap in the current literature. Therefore, it seems reasonable to organize existing data and evaluate the therapeutic potential of DFATs in comparison to the widely used ASCs. In this review, we summarized the current reports considering DFATs therapeutic properties both in vitro and in vivo. To make a clear conclusion if DFATs should be considered more beneficial, we also focused on comparing their features to the already well-investigated function of ASCs.
Keywords: Mesenchymal stem/stromal cells (MSCs), Adipose derived stem/stromal cells (ASCs), Dedifferentiated fat progenitors (DFATs), Regenerative medicine, Cell therapy, Comparative analysis

## Introduction
Human subcutaneous fat tissue is one of the safest and most readily accessible sources of multipotent mesenchymalstem/stromal cells (MSCs). The acquisition of fat tissue is carried out during routine reconstructive or plastic surgery procedures like liposuction. The isolation of adipose derived stem/stromal cells (ASCs) is relatively simple, effortless and devoid of ethical concerns. ASCs have been classified as mesenchymal stem cells according to International Stem Cell and Gene Therapy (ISCT) guidelines [ , ] and have been observed to promote injury regeneration of various tissues as confirmed by an enormous number of studies both in vitro and in vivo. The therapeutic properties of ASCs have been linked not only to their multilineage differentiation potential and their intensive proliferation in the damaged region, but also to their secretome activity [ ], immunomodulation [ ] or angiogenesis stimulation [ ]. Nowadays, ASCs are successively used in bone regeneration, cartilage repair, liver reconstruction, myocardium restoration, skin wound healing or the nervous system rebuilding [ , ]. Since the beneficial function of ASCs has been pre-clinically discovered the rapid growth of clinical studies that use ASCs-based therapies is observed [ , ]. Nevertheless, while ASCs are a highly promising candidate for tissue regeneration, their application still has several drawbacks such as the donor-dependent diversity of cells, heterogeneity of the population and the inconsistent methods for cell transport or transplantation. To improve the therapeutic outcome of stem cell-based clinical trials, we should focus on exploring new ideas in the area and among many others, we could target new alternative cell populations with even greater regenerative potential.
It is known that human adipose tissue is a heterogeneous structure, containing various subpopulations, including mature adipocytes, endothelial cells, preadipocytes, progenitor cells, and immune cells [ ]. Recent research focuses on identifying new subpopulations or precursors residing in human fat that may offer comparable or even superior benefits compared to standard used ASCs. It has been shown recently that firstly discovered by Matsumoto et al. and isolated from human fat tissue, the subpopulation of dedifferentiated fat progenitors (DFATs) might be a great competitor to ASCs in terms of their regenerative capabilities [ ]. DFATs are described to be collected from the fraction of mature adipocytes that appear in the upper fat layer after enzymatic digestion of adipose tissue. While their precise origin remains unclear, it is hypothesized that under certain conditions, they could emerge through the dedifferentiation of mature adipocytes and to some extent still retain their unique properties, but the mechanism of this process is still under investigation [ ].
As ASCs and DFATs are derived from adipose tissue, it is suggestive that they may share similar properties. However, a growing number of studies directly comparing DFATs to ASCs highlight some distinctions, particularly related to proliferation rates, multilineage differentiation potential, and gene expression profiles. Moreover, DFATs beneficial function in vivo in bone and cartilage regeneration, periodontal tissue restoration, promoting neovascularization and having supportive effects in skin, nerve, and intestinal tissue recovery have been discovered but not fully and directly compared to ASCs function.
In this review, we provide comprehensive data analysis comparing the capabilities of ASCs and DFATs in both in vitro and in vivo manner. To directly estimate the potential differences between these two populations, in our review, we especially included the publications directly comparing the ASCs and DFATs isolated from the same source. Our main aim was to revise the existing reports and conclude if DFATs show different properties than ASCs, and how these differences may affect their therapeutic potential and further use in clinical trials.

## In Vitro Studies

## The Isolation Processes
Both populations: ASCs and DFATs are derived from the same origin: adipose tissue, although their method of isolation varies. Following the digestion of adipose tissue with collagenase type I and subsequent centrifugation, three distinct layers are visibly separated in the tube. The bottom layer, or pellet, is a stromal vascular fraction (SVF) that contains a variety of components, including fibroblasts, pericytes, erythrocytes, endothelial cells, immune cells, and ASCs [ ]. The middle layer represents a washing solution, typically PBS with antibiotics, and the very top layer is mature adipocytes that are a source of a small population of DFAT cells. To obtain ASCs, the SVF fraction is directly seeded on the plastic bottles and cultured in standard conditions. The acquisition of DFAT cells is uniquely different. First time described and used by Matsumoto in 2008 ceiling culture method ended up being the most effective and the easiest way to obtain DFATs from adipose tissue [ , ]. Briefly, the method involves collecting a layer of mature adipocytes (MAs), next a certain number of MAs, usually 1 ml, is added to a flask filled with the culture medium. The bottle is next inverted, and cells are cultured upside down in standard conditions for a week. The ceiling method is based on the physical pressure of the medium that forces adherent cells that are present in the mature adipocyte layer, to attach to the upper surface of the flask. It has been confirmed that 7-days long ceiling culture allows approximately 40-50% of mature adipocytes to transform into fibroblasts-like DFATs [ ]. Other methods used to effectively isolate DFATs are floating method with the use of filtration [ ], floating culture with additional use of dish cover [ ] and mesh method [ ].
In culture DFATs and ASCs are almost identical, although in the first days after isolation DFATs present some differences in morphology. It has been observed that after 5–6 days of ceiling culture the cytoplasm of the mature adipocytes began to spread, cells are getting elongated to finally change to a fibroblast-like cell with characteristic oil droplets inside their bodies [ ]. This phenomenon is completely absent in the ASCs culture which indicates their potential differences. Matsumoto et al. showed that the oil remains presented inside the DFATs were positive for AdipoRed [ ] and Tansriratanawong et al. observed positive Oil Red O staining in cells containing lipid droplets [ ] suggesting that the obtained population indeed originates from mature adipocytes. Following the culture time and after flask inversion, Lipid droplets become fragmented into smaller ones and are consequently released by cells. Within 1–2 weeks lipid droplets disappear completely, and cells become lipid-free DFATs [ ] at this point, they become morphologically indistinguishable from ASCs. Lipid droplets are one of the most characteristic features of freshly isolated mature adipocyte-derived DFATs and have been observed by many other researchers [ , , ].

## Surface Markers Expression
One of the leading aspects distinguishing ASCs and DFATs populations is the differences in the expression pattern of specific surface markers. Most of the reports comparing ASCs and DFATs markers indicate a similar or almost identical profile of surface antigen expression [ , ]. Both populations meet the criteria defining mesenchymal stem cells, and are > 90% positive for: CD105, CD73, CD90 and < 2% negative for monocyte/macrophage markers: CD14, CD11b, lymphocyte markers: CD19, CD79-alpha and HLA-DR and hematopoietic stem cell markers such as: CD34 and CD45 [ – ]. Based on those findings, DFATs could be categorized as mesenchymal stem cells, although many reports revealed some surface marker differences compared to ASCs. For example, Sawada et al. observed slightly increased values of CD36 that is a marker of human adipocyte progenitor in DFATs compared to ASCs. They also revealed that both populations express some adhesion proteins such as CD54 (ICAM-1)-intracellular adhesion molecule, while at the same time are negative for others like: CD106 (VCAM-1)- vascular adhesion protein [ ] as also confirmed elsewhere [ ]. What is more, CD146 (MCAM)- melanoma cell adhesion molecule that is highly expressed in vascular endothelial cells has also been identified in both ASCs and DFATs [ , ] but not observed elsewhere [ ]Focusing on the differences between ASCs and DFATs, Fuji et al. revealed that DFATs increased the expression of CD140b (PDGFRB)- platelet derived growth factor receptor [ ] also found to be a pluripotent cells marker when co-expressed with CD73 [ ]. Another group, Gao et al., claimed that CD34 was positive in ASCs but negative in DFATs indicates their higher homogeneity. They also showed that both population ASCs and DFATs are similarly positive for CD31 (PECAM-1)-platelet endothelial cell adhesion molecules [ ] when others described the opposite [ , ]. Such diverse results and numerous discrepancies regarding the presence or absence of specific markers in DFATs compared to ASCs suggest that distinguishing these two populations based solely on the expression of surface markers becomes impossible. From a therapeutic application standpoint, the similarity in surface marker profiles between DFATs and ASCs poses challenges in distinguishing these cell types. Consequently, it is reasonable to investigate additional functional characteristics—such as variations in secretome composition, colony-forming unit (CFU) capacity, and differentiation potential—that may more effectively delineate these two populations.

## Proliferation Capacity
Because of the self-renewal potential of mesenchymal stem cells is considered therapeutically beneficial, it is necessary to investigate potential differences in growth rate when comparing DFATs to ASCs. In general, most of the reports showed a similar proliferation ability of DFATs and ASCs [ , , , ] with a few exceptions. For example, Tansriratanawong et al., claimed that DFATs increased their proliferation capacity compared to ASCs. Their findings indicated enhanced colony-forming ability (CFU), increased absorbance in MTT assay and shorter population doubling time (PDT) in DFATs, however, the results were not statistically significant. The same group also observed that late-passage DFATs decreased senescence compared to ASCs, demonstrating their perhaps slower aging as also shown previously [ ]Tansriratanawong et al. also described in detail the hypermethylation patterns of both ASCs and DFATs but significant differences have been observed in the age-dependent manner rather than between populations [ ]. Tansriratanawong et results regarding DFATs increased proliferation are consistent with some previous findings, for example Kono et al. group who revealed enhanced colony formation number (CFU-F) in DFATs [ ] and Huang et al. who observed that the proliferative ability of DFATs was significantly greater when entered the logarithmic growth phase after day 3rd of culture [ ]. Taken together, reports regarding DFATs growth rate are conflicting, although there are some indications confirming that under certain conditions DFATs may be characterized by better proliferation abilities than ASCs.

## Differentiation Potential
As a consequence, that both ASCs and DFATs are derived from adipose tissue, that originates from mesoderm, it is known that they can differentiate into connective tissue lineage cells, such as adipocytes, osteoblasts and chondrocytes and hence be classified as multipotent. As evidenced, most of the research confirmed the lack of differences between ASCs and DFATs multilineage differentiation potential. It has been shown that both ASCs and DFATs cultured in specific media can easily generate osteocytes, chondrocytes and adipocytes without significant differences [ , ,  ,  , , ].
However, many reports associated with osteogenic, adipogenic and chondrogenic differentiation potential of ASCs and DFATs disagree. For instance, Kishimoto et al.. data showed an enhanced osteogenic differentiation potential of DFATs cultured in osteogenic medium compared to ASCs cells, both kept under the same conditions [ ]. Similar results have been discovered by others [ , ], but some groups also reported improving in adipogenesis [ , ] or chondrogenesis differentiation capacity of DFATs [ ]. Saler et al have not observed significant variances in the differentiation function of ASCs and DFATs, but revealed differentiation- associated gene expression changes, discovering upregulation in lipid-related genes such as lipoprotein lipase (LPL) and adiponectin in DFATs and osteogenic-related genes such as: ALP and RUNX2 in ASCs [ ] similar to others [ ]. Interestingly, Gao et al.. discovered enhanced alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity in DFATs compared to ASCs, the opposite was evidenced elsewhere [ ]. Gao et al. claimed that ALP activity might be closely related to the state of undifferentiated stem cells, and that would potentially confirm the theory of DFATs’ more pluripotent features, but ALP activity is also known to be an osteoblast marker, and it may indicate higher osteogenic potential of DFATs compared to ASCs. Intriguing is also the fact that the same group discovered increased telomerase activity in DFATs, which parameter also associated with the pluripotency features [ ] as also confirmed by others [ ].
Besides the multipotency of ASCs and DFATs, their ability to differentiate into other cell types such as: endothelial, myofibroblast and neural-like cells has also been manifested. For instance, Poloni et al. demonstrated that both ASCs and DFATs were able to increase the level of endothelial lineage-specific markers, produce angiogenic factors and upregulate expression of VEGFR-2, vWF, CD133, and CD144 genes [ ]. Similarly, Jumabay et al., showed greater expression of CD31 and VEGF in DFATs than in ASCs [ ] as also confirmed elsewhere [ ]. Interestingly, the release of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) together with secretion of hepatocyte growth factor (HGF) has also been reported to be increased in DFATs compared to ASCs, but only under hypoxic conditions, suggesting their possible increased differentiation potential towards endothelial cells [ ]. Other group Tansriratanawong, et al observed that the alpha smooth muscle actin (SMA-α) marker of myofibroblast cells and pericytes is detected in DFATs, while it was completely absent in ASCs, suggesting possible enhanced myofibroblast differentiation potential of DFATs compared to ASCs [ ]. Others, as Figiel-Dabrowska et al. showed, enhanced neurogenic potential of DFATs as evidenced by the upregulated expression of neuronal marker: β-III-tubulin and astrocyte lineage marker: S100β when DFATs were cultured with healthy and injured hippocampal slices compared to ASCs. They also found elevated neuroprotection abilities and enhanced release of neurogenic factors such as: BDNF, FGF and EGF in hippocampal co-cultured DFATs compared to ASCs [ ]. Additionally, when the neuronal induction environment is applied, in particular FGF and EGF, DFATs are able to differentiate into neural-like cells and increase the expression of some neural-lineage markers such as: Nestin, SOX2 and TUJI1 [ ]. Similar results have been confirmed by Radoszkiewicz et al. who showed that biomimetic conditions may directly influence DFATs neural-differentiation potential. They showed that depending on whether DFATs are cultured in 100% human cerebrospinal fluid (hCSF) or N21 supplement, they may gain neurogenic and neuroprotective abilities, after co-culture with injured hippocampal tissue [ ].
To summarize, in terms of the multilineage potential of ASCs and DFATs there are some reports confirming DFATs greater abilities to differentiate into adipocytes, chondrocytes and osteoblasts, but most of the researchers do not identify significant differences between those two populations. Interestingly, DFATs might be potentially characterized by increased endothelial and myofibroblasts differentiation, supporting angiogenesis together with enhanced neurogenic and neuroprotective abilities than ASCs, but evidence is rather minor and more research in this area is needed. The demonstrated ability of DFATs to induce the expression of neuroectodermal markers and the secretion of neurotrophic factors suggest that this population may represent a potentially promising source of cells for the treatment of neurodegenerative diseases, especially in strategies aimed at neuroprotection of the neuronal network.

## Pluripotency Associated Genes
Pluripotency, which is defined as the ability to differentiate into any cell type and occurs as a characteristic feature of embryonic (ESCs) or induced pluripotent cells (iPSCs) in theory goes beyond the capabilities of mesenchymal stem/stromal cells. However, there is some evidence that this is one of the characteristics attributed to DFATs, which might distinguish them from ASCs.
The first group that evidenced DFATs increased pluripotency potential was Jumabay et al. The expression of transcriptional factors that play a critical role in maintaining the pluripotency state, such as: Oct 3/4, SOX2, c-Myc, Klf4 and Nanog has been discovered to be upregulated in DFATs. All the pluripotency gene levels were higher compared to ASCs, but their expression decreased time in culture [ ]. Those findings have been confirmed by others, like Figiel-Dabrowska et al., who also reported increased relative expression of genes such as: Nanog, Sox2 and Oct ¾ in DFATs [ ]. On the other hand, some researchers discovered the opposite. For example, Gao et al. indeed observed embryonic stem cell marker expression in DFATs, but the flow cytometry analysis revealed lower levels of those markers than in ASCs [ ]. Similarly, Watson et al., who showed that DFATs and ASCs expressed similar levels of embryonic pluripotency markers such as: Oct4 and SALL4, and it is the ASCs that increase expression of BMI1 and KLF4, not DFATs [ ].
A good functional indicator for pluripotency in stem cells is also their ability to trilineage differentiation, into ectoderm, mesoderm and/or endoderm. Interestingly, Jumabay et al. reported DFATs upregulation in some ectodermal markers such as: NESTIN and MAP2, which are also considered as neural markers. Based on their results, DFATs also enhanced mesodermal: Nkx2.5 and Tbx5 and endodermal markers: α-FP and GATA6 while minimal expression of those was detected in ASCs [ , ]. Others, such as Figiel-Dabrowska et al., observed an increase only in expression of neuroectoderm marker: OTX2 in DFATs compared to ASCs. [ ]. As previously described DFATs can express variety of neural lineage markers [ ], [ ], [ ] that confirms their ability to transform into neural-like phenotypes. The presence of those markers suggests DFATs might be able to differentiate into neuroectodermal-originated cells, such as neurons. Interestingly, Matsumine et.al results confirmed DFATs capability to differentiate into Schwann cells, that also originate from ectoderm [ ]. Based on the Jumabay et al results, DFATs also enhanced some endodermal markers: α-FP and GATA6 while minimal expression of those was detected in ASCs [ ]. Although regarding DFATs potential to differentiate into endoderm-originated cells, there is an interesting study performed by Hagiwara et al. They confirmed that DFATs can be easily converted into hepatocyte-like cells, but using Foxa2, Hnfa and Sall transduction. The study confirms that after induction, DFATs showed hepatocyte-characteristic morphology, expressed liver-specific markers and exhibited the ability to detoxify several compounds [ ]. Although there is no documented clear evidence that DFAT can differentiate in endoderm-lineage cells.To summarize, most of the results indicate that DFATs might possess some pluripotent rather than only multipotent abilities and may originate from ectoderm, rather than mesoderm. Although, to date, there are not many published studies demonstrating the differentiation potential of DFATs into other ectoderm or endoderm-derived cells and further investigation in this area is strongly needed. (Table. )
 

## In Vivo Studies

## Bone and Cartilage Repair
The beneficial effect of DFATs cells in vivo has been investigated in regenerative therapies, mainly in bone and cartilage tissue regeneration. When compared to the control group, which is often saline or PBS, DFATs injections or transplantations usually exhibited greater bone regeneration potential. For instance, DFATs injections have been shown to significantly inhibit the cartilage degradation in the rat model of osteoarthritis [ ], contributed to bone regeneration in rat calvaria defect models [ ] and when transplanted on gelatin sponges (GA) combined with activated platelet-rich plasma (aPRP) promoted bone formation in rat calvaria defect model [ ]. Additionally, the three-dimensional spheroid culture of DFATs has been shown to influence therapeutic properties of DFATs, as increased relative bone formation and bone mineral density in calvaria bone defects in rats has been revealed in comparison to monolayer cultured DFATs [ ]. Even though DFATs exhibited overall greater bone regeneration abilities compared to controls, there are only a few findings directly comparing DFATs and ASCs features in this area. For example, work done by Tateno et al. group, who seeded rat DFATs and ASCs onto a type I collagen scaffold made from a recombinant peptide (RCP) and transplanted cells into the rat model of bone defects in the mandible. After 8 weeks post-implantation, micro-CT images revealed superior bone regeneration and a histological analysis, significantly greater bone width in the DFATs/RCP scaffold group compared to the ASCs/RCP scaffold group [ ]. Similar results have been observed by others, such as Fujisaki et al who revealed that DFATs transplantation in mandibular defects in rats leads to increased new bone formation compared to ASCs implantation. Interestingly, such differences between ASCs and DFATs have not been observed when cells were transplanted in ovariectomy-induced animals [ ]. Another group, Takabatake et al used GFP- labeled ASCs and DFATs seeded on collagen composite scaffolds and implanted them subcutaneously into the backs of tested mice. After 4 weeks, osteoblasts lining the bone matrix and numerous osteocytes incorporated into the lacunae of the newly generated bone structure were observed in both groups. Osteocalcin- and GFP-positive cells were present in the bone tissue of both groups, but no significant differences between the ASCs and DFATs groups have been captured [ ]. Furthermore, Sawada et al. were able to isolate DFATs from femur bone marrow and compared their regenerative potential with adipose-derived DFATs, ASCs and BM-MSCs. Their results revealed that DFATs originated from bone marrow, showed greater osteogenic ability in in vitro tests. Regarding in vivo experiments, cells were mixed with peptide hydrogel (PHG) and injected into the fracture gap in the mouse femoral fracture model. These results showed that the transplantation of BM-DFATs and BM-MSCs alongside PHG resulted in increased bone mineral density (BMD) at the injection sites compared to the administration of PHG alone. Although the differences between ASCs and DFATs derived from fat tissue were not significant [ ]. To summarize, there is evidence confirming that DFATs displayed superior regenerative features in bone and cartilage regeneration in vivo and even exceed the ASCs qualities. However, to be clear that DFATs features are indeed markedly enhanced compared to standard used ASCs there is a need to explore this topic in depth.

## Periodontal Tissue Regeneration
Recent reports indicated the advantages of using DFATs in periodontal tissue regeneration. For example, GFP-positive DFATs seeded on the scaffolds and applied in alveolar bone defects in rats were present in newly formed bone, cementum and periodontal ligament and indicating their contribution to new tissue formation [ ]. A comparative study of DFATs and ASCs effect in periodontium regeneration has been performed by Akita et al. Seeding rat DFATs onto PLGA (Poly d, l-lactic-co-glycolic acid) scaffolds and transplanting them into periodontal fenestration defects in rat mandible resulted in a significantly greater augmentation in crucial tissues, including cementum and alveolar bone, in comparison to the scaffolds with ASCs [ ]. This experiment also revealed that GFP-labeled DFATs were present in the periodontal ligament adjacent to the newly formed bone and cementum. A few years later, the same group transplanted DFATs into inflammation-inducing periodontal class II furcation defects in adult miniature pigs (micro minipigs; MMPs). In this study, a noticeable improvement in attachment was evident based on probing depth (PD) and clinical attachment level (CAL) after 12 weeks of cell transplantation. Additionally, the formation of hard tissue in furcation defects of the second premolar was observed. Histological analysis further revealed the presence of newly formed cellular cementum, well-oriented periodontal ligament-like fibers, and the generation of alveolar bone. Additionally, no teratomas were identified in the internal organs of recipient MMPs [ ]. Another group, Huang et al., focused on the comparative assessment of ASCs and DFATs function in rat periodontal fenestration defect models. Both cell’ populations have been cultivated in the form of sheets that are thicker and contain a high density of cells. Moreover, cell sheets were also rich in extracellular matrix components (ECM) and expressed increased levels of osteogenic-related markers and periodontal-related markers. Micro-CT analysis of DFATs and ASCs sheets applied in periodontal intra-bony defects in rats revealed that after 6 weeks post-surgery the bone volume density (BV/TV) and Trabecular bone thickness (Tb.Th) in the defect area were increased in DFATs sheet group compared to ASCs sheet group. On the other hand, trabecular bone separation (Th.Sp) was shown to be lower in DFATs sheet group than in ASCs sheet group. However, after 8-weeks post-implantation both groups: ASCs and DFATs sheets promoted periodontal tissue regeneration compared to the control groups, indicating equivalent therapeutic potential [ ]. In conclusion, all the presented findings strongly indicate that DFATs can safely contribute to the enhancement of periodontal tissue regeneration.

## Neovascularization
Latest reports suggest that DFATs may pose the ability to differentiate into endothelial cells, enhance angiogenesis and promote neovascularization. For example, Planat-Benard et al. showed that, as referred, dedifferentiated mature human adipocytes acquire an endothelial phenotype by increasing angiographic score and cutaneous blood flow in vivo compared to control, but not to SVF-derived cells. They concluded that adipose-derived cells may contribute to vascular-like structure formation [ ]. Others, such as Jumabay et al. revealed upregulation of CD31 and VEGF expression in DFATs, compared to ASCs, and their ability to form multicellular tube structures on Matrigel, suggesting that the DFATs can give rise to endothelial cells more efficiently than ASCs. They were also able to check if DFATs could be integrated with actively formed vessels in vivo, and to do this they implanted DFATs into the infarct zone of mice after ligation of the artery. The results revealed that DFATs were easily detectable in the injury zone, differentiated into endothelial cells and potentially contributing to the new vessel formation [ ]. Some comparative studies regarding the neovascularization function of ASCs, DFATs and fibroblasts have been performed using hindlimb ischemia models of SCID mice by Watanabe et al.. After 28 days post-injection of cells, they observed significant improvement of blood flow ratio and number of vessels in the ASCs and DFATs group but only compared to the control and fibroblasts group. There were no significant differences between ASCs and DFATs groups. Interestingly, when co-cultured with endothelial cells DFATs increased the expression of some angiogenic factors, promoted endothelial tube formation and expressed pericyte markers in vitro. And, when transplanted in the Matrigel, DFATs can differentiate into pericyte-like cells in vivo [ ]. On the other hand, some reports indicating ASCs increased endothelial differentiation potential, for instance Wang et al. reported an increased number of CD31 + cells after ASCs injection into the skin liaison of mice. They also showed a greater number of capillaries and upregulated expression of VEGF in ASCs- injected animal group compared to DFATs group. Interestingly their results revealed that the relative expression of PPAR-gamma, which is known to promote adipocyte differentiation in increased together with a higher number of adipocytes in the DFATs group, suggesting their greater adipocyte differentiation potential rather than angiogenic features [ ].

## Fat Grafting and Wound Healing
DFATs and ASCs transplantation may play important roles in skin injuries, wound regeneration and skin graft revascularization. However, their functions in tissue remodeling and the scarring process have not been fully elucidated. The Wang et al. group observed that fat grafting alleviates sclerotic changes and induces the accumulation of subcutaneous and cutaneous adipocytes in both humans and mice. This research demonstrated the phenomenon of dedifferentiation and subsequent redifferentiation of adipocytes in patients with scleroderma following fat grafting procedures. Cells from the grafts appeared in the skin lesions. In addition, direct application of DFATs significantly improved dermal adipogenesis and reversed dermal fibrosis [ ]. The efficacy of DFATs and ASCs in improving the survival rate of transplanted adipose tissue was demonstrated by another group: Zhu et al.. An in vivo study using the Cell-Assisted Lipotransfer (CAL) animal model demonstrated that both cell types showed considerable promise in promoting graft survival. Specifically, DFATs showed superior abilities in promoting adipogenesis and angiogenesis, while ASCs showed stronger anti-inflammatory effects. These results indicate that both DFATs and ASCs are critical in facilitating the engraftment process of adipose tissue transplantation [ ].Some studies have also reported a beneficial role of DFATs-derived exosomes in promoting skin wound healing. Produced by 3D-cultured DFATs exosomes has been reported to not only enhance the senescent fibroblast function in vitro but also contributed to the wound healing and angiogenesis in vivo. [ ], [ ]. 

## Nerve Tissue Recovery
Since DFATs might possess potentially greater ability to differentiate into neural-lineage-like cells which has been demonstrated in vitro, the latest evidence also indicates DFATs promising features in nerve-tissue restoration in vivo. The researchers’ findings mainly indicated that two mechanisms contribute to the regeneration of injured nerve tissue: the paracrine effects of DFATs and also their capacity to differentiate. For instance, in Yamada et al. research DFATs transplanted into mouse models of cord injury promoted the functional recovery of locomotor function in animals. DFATs-induced remyelination and reduction in glial scar formation positively correlated with the changes in functional recovery of animals. DFATs transplanted to the site of injured spinal cord also expressed oligodendrocyte marker:APC, myelin marker: MBP and neural markers: Nestin,β III-tubulin, NF-H, GFAP and elevated expression of some neurotrophic factors: BDNF and GDNF [ ]. Other groups showed DFATs promoted facial nerve regeneration as an increase in axon and fiber diameter, larger myelin thickness and better results in physiologic function of nerves have been found. Matsumine et al group also observed GFP-labelled DFATs expressing S100 marker suggesting their potential differentiation into Schwann-like cells during the nerve regeneration [ ]. Others, for example Fujimaki et al., who using a similar facial nerve injury model showed that DFATs also contributed to myelinization, and whisker motion, but no differences in muscle action potential were captured [ ]. On the other hand, some results revealed that DFATs can only partially promote functional recovery of animals in the mouse focal cerebral ischemia model. The differences between DFATs group and controls were captured in sensory, perceptual, pain sensitivity and depressant behavior tests rather than learning and motor function. Moreover, any significant improvement in intact cortical surface area, volume and ratios were not observed in DFATs DFAT-treated group [ ]. Similar observation was captured by Mikrogeorgiou et al. who used DFATs transplantation in rat neonatal hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy model. Their results revealed no differences in sensorimotor function such as motor coordination and motor learning and no changes in cognitive function of DFAT-transplanted and control groups. Also, no significant changes in cell proliferation in neurogenic areas, brain volume and secretion of neurotrophic factors has been seen. Although they observed DFATs contribute to decreasing immunoactivity as confirmed to downregulate apoptotic markers, suppress microglia activation and lipid peroxidation compared to control animals [ ]. Presented results confirmed the potential contribution of DFATs transplantation in the regeneration of nerve tissue, mostly related to immune regulation, remyelination and partial functional recovery of nerves through both: the paracrine and differentiation mechanisms. Although to date, there has been no comparative study of ASC and DFAT function in this area.

## Intestine Restoration
The beneficial function of DFATs administration has also been provided in some intestinal disease models. For example, in the inflammatory bowel disease model (IBD) intraperitoneally injections of DFATs promoted colon recovery as evidenced by improved IBD-associated weight loss in mice and ameliorated clinical and histological IBD scores [ ]. The positive influence of DFATs has also been observed in the necrotizing model of enterocolitis (NEC) in which transplanted DFATs enhanced restoration of damaged intestinal tissue. The improved survival rate of animals, alleviation of abnormal expression of proteins associated with fatty acid metabolism has been discovered in DFATs DFATs-treated groups. Authors also claimed DFATs ability to reduce inflammation, as the abnormal expression of some inflammatory response-related proteins has been improved by DFATs transplantation, but interestingly the levels of IL-6, a key regulator of inflammation, increased in the DFATs-treated group compared to the control [ ]. Other groups, such Kamidaki et al., revealed positive impact of DFATs transplantation in rat model of anal sphincter injury. They showed not only restoration of proper anal pressure but also confirmed DFATs contribution to new muscle fibers formation, by discovering increased numbers of mature muscle cells when immature muscle cells were seen in the control group [ ]. Although there are some indications for DFATs beneficial function during intestinal restoration, to date, there are no studies that directly compare the ASCs and DFATs features in those conditions.

## Conclusions and Future Directions
Latest evidence indicates that DFATs can serve as a more promising model than ASCs model, possessing temporary pluripotency and considerable adaptability, making them suitable for applications in cell-based therapies (Fig.  ). Despite many reports and a still growing number of publications elucidating superior therapeutic properties of DFATs, some conclusions are still conflicting, not clear and not properly elucidated.
 In this review we summarized recent reports concerning therapeutic potential changes between DFATs and ASCs focusing on their possible superior abilities associated with proliferation, differentiation and pluripotency features in both in vitro and in vivo manner. Moreover, we particularly focused on reports that directly compare DFATs not only to control, but mainly to well-known and widely used in regenerative medicine ASCs. In our review we were able to precisely specify the equations between ASCs and DFATs and make a conclusion and outline of the direction for further research on DFATs.
The key difference between ASCs and DFATs appears early during isolation and on the first days in the cell culture. The use of the mature adipocyte layer instead of SVF during isolation is motivated by the occurrence of the dedifferentiation phenomenon in mature adipocytes. It has been shown that cells residing in human fat naturally undergo a cycle of re-differentiation in specific conditions [ ]. For example, Wang et al. observed that mammary adipocytes can fully de-differentiate into preadipocytes during lactation and then easily redifferentiate during involution. Also, already dedifferentiated adipose cells were able to lose mature adipocyte features to gain preadipocyte function [ ]. Zhang et al. described physiological adipose tissue plasticity, confirming ability to spontaneous pre-adipocyte phenotype acquisition and reversible dedifferentiation of mouse dermal adipocytes [ ]. The ceiling culture method of DFAT isolation has been observed to create certain environmental conditions that force a mature adipocyte to decide to undergo dedifferentiation. The theory behind that assumes that it is the physical pressure of the medium that creates hypoxia and forces cells to attach to the plastic surface of the bottle. Mature adipocytes subjected to ceiling culture might experience a change in cell niche that may induce their reprogramming and stimulate them to undergo dedifferentiation. The impact of both mechanical force and hypoxia conditions has been shown to drive adipocyte plasticity in vitro but many other stimuli can induce dedifferentiation, like for instance cold [ ] or osmotic pressure [ ]. In the course of ceiling culture, mature adipocytes undergo enormous morphological changes, from round fat-filled structures to fibroblast-like DFATs. Throughout the dedifferentiation process a very characteristic phenomenon of liposecretion has been captured in mature adipocytes. The oil droplets observed in cell bodies are gradually and actively released following the culture time and the rearrangement of organelles takes place [ ]. Cell body deformation, accompanied by lipid secretion finally leads to form lipid-free DFATs [ ].
Following the loss of mature adipocyte phenotype and the transition into fibroblast-like cells the switch in gene expression profile occurs in DFATs [ ]. Post-dedifferentiated cells have been shown to downregulate adipocyte markers, lipid-synthesis associated genes and upregulate in lipid-breakdown related genes [ , ]. The increase in extracellular matrix components occurs together with an elevation in remodeling genes [ ] which is expected as the huge morphology reorganization happens. The most important genetic switch in DFATs is associated with the upregulation of pluripotency related or embryonic stem cell markers, such as: Oct 3/4 or SOX2, which may potentially indicate their reprogramming ability. Expressing pluripotency markers could also be evidence that mature adipocytes reverse their phenotype to obtain developmentally younger features. Although the upregulated level of the stemness-related transcription factors occurs in DFATs, the expression declines in a time-dependent manner, in favor of the three-lineage markers growth, indicating DFATs transient flexibility [ ]. This phenomenon could be attributed to the hypoxic conditions experienced by DFATs during ceiling culture. Previous studies have shown that hypoxia might promote gaining pluripotency during de-differentiation [ ]. As shown by Rybkowska et al, low oxygen conditions together with spheroid culture of DFATs influenced on stemness-related gene expression such as: Nanog, Sox2 and Oct 3/4 increase [ ], similar results obtained by Figiel-Dabrowska et al, suggesting oxygen tension may play an important role in DFATs physiology [ ]. Interestingly,pluripotency features of DFATs were observed only in the early passages of DFATs and disappeared after transitioning to standard cultivation following bottle inversion. It is likely that the loss of temporary ceiling-culture induced hypoxia coincides with the loss of pluripotency. Therefore, maintaining hypoxia or continuous ceiling culture conditions might help preserve these advantageous properties in DFATs. Also, to maintain the pluripotency-related features of DFATs, their use in the earliest possible passage should be the priority. Following culture time DFATs were observed to acquire a more mesenchymal-like phenotype, as evidenced by their surface marker expression that is almost identical like ASCs. Based on the presented reports, it can be argued that DFATs meet the criteria classifying them as mesenchymal rather than progenitor cells. Interestingly, some reports also claimed DFATs greater homogeneity [ ] which would also be potentially more beneficial in terms of their therapeutic use. During the mesenchymal stem cell transplantation, contamination by other cell subpopulations can be a concern. To minimize cellular diversity, using high-purity DFAT cells would be a more advantageous approach. Also, at the point where ASCs and DFATs are almost indistinguishable in terms of surface markers there is a strong need to find potential protein candidates aiding in clearly distinguishing between DFATs and ASCs.
Although many genetic and phenotypic changes occur during the dedifferentiation of mature adipocytes, changes in DFATs therapeutic function are not easy to capture both in vitro and in vivo. For example, it is known that the maintenance of high proliferation in stem cells may be related to their pluripotent identity [ ]. However, as DFATs increase pluripotency markers their elevated proliferation abilities are rather negligible. It is assumed that the growth rate of both populations: ASCs and DFATs is rather similar and does not significantly differ. On the other hand, some notable variations emerge regarding DFATs’ differentiation potential compared to ASCs. First, DFATs, but not ASCs, can express early neuroectoderm markers that might potentially indicate their different origin and distance them from mesoderm-derived ASCs. Interestingly, much evidence suggests that DFATs exhibit enhanced multilineage differentiation potential in vitro, particularly in osteogenic and chondrogenic differentiation. This superior potential has been reflected in bone defects in animal models in vivo, with several studies indicating that DFATs are more effective than ASCs in enhancing bone regeneration and tissue repair following injury. In terms of endothelial differentiation potential in DFATs, in vitro findings have been only partially confirmed in vivo. No conclusive evidence has demonstrated DFATs enhance vessel formation or improve blood flow ratios in vivo, and both ASCs and DFATs exhibit similar effects in promoting new endothelium formation. Also, even though DFATs were observed to alleviate neurogenic potential in vitro, animal studies only partially confirmed their role in promoting nerve tissue recovery. Additionally, none of the in vivo findings were directly compared to the neurogenic potential of ASCs, which makes it difficult to conclude whether DFATs exceed ASCs neurogenic features.
Even though some researchers were able to observe distinct DFATs features, presented in this review conclusions are not sufficient to unambiguously clarify that DFATs are greater than ASCs in terms of therapeutic function, whether in vitro or in vivo.The studies presented in this review highlight numerous limitations and challenges, such as inconsistencies in DFATs isolation and culture methods. Although ceiling culture remains the most efficient method for DFATs isolation, different modification of it results in a noncomparable outcome. Future research should therefore focus on optimizing and standardizing existing DFATs isolation protocols. On the other hand, the introduction of advanced cell culture technologies could also be valuable, potentially enhancing DFATs therapeutical properties. It has been shown, for example, that introducing biomaterials support the microenvironment and cell to cell interactions of MSCs cultures, improving their secretory profile [ ]. Similar approaches could also be promising in terms of DFATs culture methods and should be tested in the future. Recently researchers also focused on three-dimensional (3D) culture methods, such as spheroids, which are considered to mimic the natural cellular microenvironment of the cells. It has been demonstrated that both ASCs and DFATs are capable of spontaneously forming spheroids [ ], and what is more, exosomes derived from DFATs spheroids exhibit superior properties compared to those obtained from conventional 2D cultures [ ]. In addition to methodological considerations, a critical point also involves the adipose tissue donor variability, specifically their age, weight and health status. It has been demonstrated for example, that ASCs derived from the adipose tissue of diabetic patients exhibit altered proliferative, secretory, and angiogenic capacities [ ] Interestingly, obesity of the donor might also have effect on the ASCs properties [ ]. Such factors and the inherent biological diversity among tissue donors may likewise have a substantial impact on DFATs and should also be considered when comparing existing studies. Future directions in DFATs research ought to focus on gaining a deeper understanding of the molecular mechanisms governing adipocyte dedifferentiation. As several authors have attempted to address this issue, emphasizing the involvement of several signaling pathways such as: Wnt/β-catenin, TGF-β, and Notch it is still not clear to what extend other factors such: mechanical pressure or cold exposure might contribute to the dedifferentiation process [ ]. However, the current state of knowledge regarding these mechanisms, particularly the formation of the DFAT cell population from mature adipocytes, remains incomplete. Other essential steps toward understanding DFATs physiology and derivation should include a comprehensive analysis of the epigenetic and transcriptomic landscapes of DFATs over time. Some transcriptomic data revealed interesting gene changes in DFATs [ ], [ ], [ ] explaining the changes that occur during dedifferentiation. Although identification of a specific marker candidate has not yet been conducted and should be prioritized in future research. It is imperative to perform comparative analyses of epigenetic, transcriptomic, and proteomic data across mature adipocytes, ASC populations, and DFAT cells. Such integrative screening is essential to elucidate the genuine, molecular differences among these cell types and can contribute to the identification of markers or surface factors uniquely characteristic of the DFAT cell population. The future use of DFATs as an alternative to the current use of ASCs therapy could offer certain therapeutic advantages in clinical settings, specify in neurodegenerative diseases, inflammatory bowel and soft tissue injuries. Although, to successfully translate the promising preclinical outcomes of DFATs applications into clinical settings, a greater number of studies is urgently needed. Primarily and most importantly, they must comply with Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) requirements. It is essential to evaluate the biodistribution and survival of DFATs within the organism as well as the pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic parameters. Regarding safety, a key aspect of clinical translation of DFATs involves evaluation of the purity of the cell population, their immunogenicity, genetic stability and potential toxicity. Particular attention ought to be directed, for example, to the excessive proliferation of DFATs, which may carry a potential risk of tumorigenicity. Current evidence does not allow a definitive conclusion as to whether DFATs could be safely applied in clinical studies, and a substantial number of further investigations confirming their properties and the parameters should be conducted in the future. 
In conclusion, presented in this review summary indicate that DFATs may represent a temporary precursor population, serving as an intermediary between mature adipocytes and other developmentally younger subpopulations. Additionally, DFATs may possess short-term pluripotent features, but also, they can amplify multilineage differentiation, which is reflected in the in vivo studies. Future investigations of the DFATs population should prioritize a comprehensive elucidation of the molecular mechanisms regulating dedifferentiation, alongside detailed characterization of the epigenetic and transcriptomic alterations relative to ASCs, as well as isolation method standardization and most importantly, rigorous evaluation of their safety for prospective clinical applications.
