## Introduction and importance
Synovial sarcoma is a malignant soft tissue tumor primarily affects the extremities. Its presence in the paranasal sinuses, especially within the ethmoid sinus, is exceedingly rare.

## Case presentation
We report a case of monophasic synovial sarcoma of the ethmoid sinus in a 54-year-old woman, who presented with 3 months history of left eye pain and bulging. CT with contrast, MRI, and histopathology confirmed the diagnosis. Whose management was partial surgical resection with postoperative chemotherapy and radiotherapy. Post-treatment, the patient showed a good clinical outcome and remained under regular follow-up care by the oncology team.

## Case discussion
To our knowledge, there are only six reported cases of primary synovial sarcoma in the ethmoid sinus. Its atypical presentation and unspecific features make diagnosis challenging. Partial surgical resection with postoperative chemotherapy and radiotherapy is the standard treatment. However, the prognosis remains poor, with a high risk of recurrence, necessitating thorough follow-up.

## Conclusion
This case highlights the presentation and management of a significantly rare tumor in unusual body region.

## Highlights
•Synovial sarcoma is a rare malignancy especially in paranasal sinuses such as ethmoidal sinus.
•Patient with ethmoidal mass can present with nonspecific symptoms such as eye proptosis, headache, and eye pain.
•The management of synovial sarcoma is surgical resection followed by chemoradiotherapy.

## Introduction
Synovial sarcoma is a rare malignant tumor originating from mesenchymal tissue, named for its histologic resemblance to synovial tissue. It accounts for about 1 % of all cancer cases. Males are more likely to be affected than females, and it is more common in younger individuals [ ]. While the exact causes of most sarcomas are not fully understood, several genetic mutations and environmental factors can increase the risk [ ].
There are three different histologic subtypes of synovial sarcoma: poorly differentiated, biphasic, and monophasic. The most common subtype is monophasic, which contains uniform spindle cells [ ]. Synovial sarcoma primarily arises in the soft tissues of the extremities, particularly in areas around joints, tendons, and bursae. They can also appear in the head and neck, which is less common, accounting for only about 5–10 % of cases [ , ]. Hypopharynx is the most often affected location of head and neck synovial sarcoma. Though it also originates from other locations, such as paranasal sinuses which is very rare. The occurrence of synovial sarcoma in the paranasal sinuses, especially the ethmoid sinus, is extremely rare, with only six cases reported in the literature [ , , , , ].
Here in this case report, we present a case of synovial sarcoma affecting the ethmoid sinus in a 54-year-old female who initially presented with left eye pain and swelling. Brain CT and MRI revealed a left ethmoidal soft tissue lesion, which was later confirmed as synovial sarcoma through histopathological analysis.

## Case presentation
A 54-year-old female presented to the emergency department with complaints of sudden onset left eye pain 3 months ago. The pain had been worsening over the past few days and was associated with left eye bulging, prompting her to seek medical advice. Her family also noticed episodes of intermittent squinting, which she later observed by herself. The patient denied any symptoms of nasal obstruction or epistaxis. Her medical history was unremarkable except for hypertension, diagnosed 5 years ago. She reported no personal or family history of malignancy or chronic sinusitis.
Upon examination, vital signs were within normal ranges. Physical examinations, including neurological, cardiovascular, respiratory, gastrointestinal, were unremarkable. Ophthalmologic examination showed mild limitation of movement in the left eye with mild exophthalmos (21 mm on the exophthalmometer).
A CT scan with contrast of the brain and sinuses revealed a heterogeneously enhancing left fronto-ethmoidal mass lesion (measuring 2.5 × 2 × 1.7 cm), with bone expansion and remodeling, causing lateral bowing of the lamina papyracea and mild compression of the left optic nerve ( ). MRI confirmed the same findings of a left ethmoidal mass without evidence of parenchymal extension ( ). 
Under general anesthesia, the patient underwent functional endoscopic sinus surgery (FESS) for excision of the left ethmoidal sinus mass. Intraoperatively, the mass was found to be intimately close to the optic nerve, which result in limited in surgical removal. As a result, the mass was partially resected, and tissue was sent for histopathological examination. Histopathological examination ( ) of the excisional biopsy of the mass showed multiple tissue cores with spindle cells arranged in fascicles, ovoid hyperchromatic nuclei with scant cytoplasm, and inconspicuous nucleoli. There was moderate nuclear pleomorphism and frequent mitotic figures, with tumor infiltration into bone and soft tissue structures. 
Immunohistochemical staining showed that tumor cells were diffusely positive for vimentin and B-cell lymphoma 2 (BCL2), focally for CD99, EMA, CD34, and SMA, while negative for PanCK, P63, Desmin, Myogenin and SOX10. The morphology and immunoprofile were suggestive for monophasic synovial sarcoma.
Postoperative CT scan showed a focal bulge in the lateral wall of the left ethmoidal sinus, compressing the left medial rectus muscle and left optic nerve, with underlying minimal enhancing soft tissue components suggesting residual tumor ( ). Postoperative MRI also confirmed residual disease with involvement of the left orbital floor. For staging, a CT scan of the neck, chest, abdomen, and pelvic showed unremarkable findings. 
The patient underwent three sessions of intra-arterial (IA) neoadjuvant chemotherapy, which included ifosfamide (2500 mg/m2), mesna (800 mg/m2), and doxorubicin (25 mg/m2). After completing chemotherapy, the multidisciplinary team reevaluated the case and determined that the residual tumor is unresectable. Radiotherapy was recommended with regular follow-up by the oncology team. However, the follow-up period revealed that the patient was doing well post-treatment with a good clinical outcome.

## Discussion
Synovial sarcoma is an aggressive, high-grade malignant tumor orienting from primitive mesenchymal cells, primarily occurring in the periarticular regions of the extremities. In general, it predominantly affecting young males [ ]. However, this case presents an elderly female diagnosed with synovial sarcoma in the ethmoidal sinus, an exceedingly rare case [ , ]. Although synovial sarcoma can be found in various regions of head and neck, its occurrence in the paranasal sinuses is very rare [ ]. Primary synovial sarcoma of the ethmoidal sinus is significantly rare, with only six reported cases in the English literature till date ( ), to our knowledge. 
Clinically, paranasal synovial sarcomas usually remain asymptomatic until the tumor size increases, leading to compression of the surrounding structures. Therefore, patients typically present with compressive symptoms, as seen in our case. In previous reported cases, clinical presentation was mostly similar, including epistaxis, nasal obstruction, nasal pain, headache, ear pain, and double vision. In contrast, our case presented with nonspecific symptoms, such as left eye pain and bulging, and headache, making the diagnosis more challenging. These symptoms led to a differential diagnosis that initially included conditions like orbital cellulitis, cluster headache, migraine, and vasculitis, all of which were subsequently ruled out through detailed investigations.
Radiological findings, while helpful, are not pathognomonic for synovial sarcoma and overlap with other paranasal masses such as inverted papillomas, adenocarcinomas, and hemangiomas. Therefore, histopathological examination is essential for a definitive diagnosis. Synovial sarcoma is classified into two main subtypes based on the proportion of epithelial and spindle cells and the degree of differentiation [ ]. Both subtypes are characterized by spindle-shaped cells arranged in collagenous background. In our case, histopathological examination revealed a tumor monotonous cell with scant amphophilic cytoplasm, ovoid to spindled vesicular nuclei with evenly dispersed chromatin and inconspicuous nucleoli presence. Additionally, synovial sarcomas show immunoreactivity for bcl-2, EMA, cytokeratins, and S100, as shown in our case.
Management modalities for paranasal synovial sarcoma vary in the literature, but the accepted common approach typically involves surgical excision followed by chemoradiotherapy [ ]. Despite of importance of achieving negative surgical margins, complete resection in paranasal sinus surgery can be challenging due to the proximity of surrounding essential structures. As a result, our case was managed with partial surgical resection followed by neoadjuvant IA ifosfamide-based chemotherapy regimen of three to four cycles, followed by radiation therapy.
Recurrence rate of this tumor is high, especially within the first two years [ ]. The recurrence rate depends on size, site, grade, and bone invasion. Therefore, regular follow-up with sinus endoscopy and radiological imaging is recommended to avoid miss of any recurrent lesions. Postoperative radiotherapy has proven effective in sarcoma management. In a study by Andra et al., surgical resection was performed in 81 % of cases, with negative margins achieved in only 38 %. All patients received postoperative radiotherapy, with half also undergoing chemotherapy, yielding an overall survival rate of 82 %. However, despite synovial sarcoma's chemosensitivity, overall survival remains poor for these cases [ ].
Wong et al. reported a case of ethmoidal synovial sarcoma managed with wide local excision followed by postoperative radiotherapy. However, the tumor recurred within three months, which required further endoscopic resection and radiotherapy. The patient died nine months after the diagnosis with metastasis to the brain and neck nodes [ ]. Dhiman et al. reported two cases of paranasal synovial sarcoma, one managed by surgical resection followed by chemoradiation, and the other managed solely by chemotherapy. The first patient experienced recurrence of the tumor after six-month follow-up, while the second patient died after six weeks of diagnosis [ ].

## Conclusion
This case highlights the rarity and complexity of diagnosis of primary synovial sarcoma in the ethmoid sinus, especially in an elderly female with nonspecific ophthalmic symptoms. Diagnosis required detailed imaging and histopathology due to its atypical presentation and overlapping features with other paranasal masses. Given the challenges in complete resection in paranasal locations, a multimodal approach, including partial resection, intra-arterial chemotherapy, and radiotherapy, was essential for management.
