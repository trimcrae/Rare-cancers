## Abstract
Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) exceeding 200 nucleotides participate in the physiological and pathological pathways of various cancers. VPS9D1 antisense RNA 1 (VPS9D1-AS1), situated in the q24.3 region of human chromosome 16 and known as c-Myc upregulation factor (MYU), is upregulated in multiple tumor types. This lncRNA mainly plays a cancer-promotional role by modulating target genes, adsorbing microRNAs (miRNAs), and involving in the regulation of numerous pivotal signaling pathways, such as Wnt/β-catenin, TGF-β/ISG, and PI3K/AKT. Collectively, these molecular mechanisms promote malignant phenotypes, including proliferation, invasion, migration, and epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) of cancer cells. Clinical studies have indicated that high expression levels of VPS9D1-AS1 are closely associated with tumor volume enlargement, progression in clinical staging, increased risk of metastasis, and poor prognosis. This review systematically elucidates the role, molecular mechanisms, and clinical application value of VPS9D1-AS1 in malignant tumors, emphasizing its potential as a diagnostic biomarker and therapeutic target.
Keywords: Long non-coding RNA, VPS9D1-AS1, Cancers, Molecular mechanism, Clinical application

## Introduction
Cancer is a major cause of death worldwide, including nearly 1 million deaths annually and represents a major health threat to humans and the healthcare system [ , ]. Cancer has a complicated pathophysiology due to various factors such as genetic deregulation, epigenetic alterations. and environmental influence [ – ]. Though some treatments, including surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy are available for cancer patients, the overall prognosis still fails to meet clinical expectations [ ]. Targeted therapy has become an indispensable part of cancer management and has made great advances. However, it does not produce any significant increase in the overall survival of cancer patients [ ]. As such, a better comprehension of the complex cascades mediating cancer onset and progression and pinpointing biomarkers linked to cancer development are needed with the aim of improving cancer patents’ survival rates and increasing opportunities for winning against this disease in early life.
Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) are a class of RNA transcripts with more than 200 nucleotides in length, which do not encode proteins [ ]. Once considered as the by-product of RNA polymerase II transcriptions with no biological significance, lncRNA is now developed and established for its potential vital roles in cancer [ ]. Nonetheless, with the advent of high-throughput sequencing technique and transcriptome analysis, there is a large body of evidence suggesting that lncRNA is involved in certain key biological activities such as DNA methylation, histone modification, and posttranscriptional RNA regulation [ , ]. In addition, lncRNA is also involved in the process of tumorigenesis and tumor progression through direct binding with macromolecules including DNA/RNA/protein to affect proliferation, migration, autophagy, invasion, cell cycle control, cell survival capability, drug resistance, and epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) [ ]. In the field of cancer, lncRNA has high tissue specificity and is regulated by different mechanisms, so it has a promising application prospect as a new biomarker for diagnosis and treatment in tumors.
The VPS9D1 antisense RNA 1 (VPS9D1-AS1), also known as c-Myc upregulated (MYU), is a novel lncRNA located at the q24.3 site on the positive strand of human chromosome 16. VPS9D1-AS1 (NR_036480.1) consists of a transcript with four exons and a gene length of 1753 nt (Fig.  , https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene/100128881) [ ]. Utilizing the GEPIA2 online database (http://gepia2.cancer-pku.cn/), differential expression of VPS9D1-AS1 has been observed in several cancers, including adrenocortical carcinoma (ACC), cervical squamous cell carcinoma (CESC), colon adenocarcinoma (COAD), diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBC), lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC), ovarian cystadenocarcinoma (OV), prostate adenocarcinoma (PRAD), rectal adenocarcinoma (READ), stomach adenocarcinoma (STAD), and uterine corpus endometrial carcinoma (UCEC) and uterine carcinosarcoma (UCS) (Fig.  ). The expression levels of VPS9D1-AS1 in various tumor tissues and cell lines correlate with clinicopathological features, including tumor size, degree of differentiation, TNM stage, and lymph node metastasis. Elevated expression levels of VPS9D1-AS1 can also predict poor prognosis in tumor patients, encompassing overall survival (OS), progression-free survival (PFS), and disease-free survival (DFS). This article reviews the biological functions, clinical features, and molecular mechanisms of VPS9D1-AS1, aiming to deepen our understanding of its multifaceted roles in oncology and to emphasize its potential as a key biomarker and therapeutic target.
 

## Materials and methods
A comprehensive review was conducted utilizing databases such as PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, Google Scholar, China National Knowledge Infrastructure, Weipu Data, and Wanfang Data. The search period spanned from January 2016 to August 2025. The search employed a combination of keywords, including “VPS9D1-AS1”, “VPS9D1 antisense RNA 1”, “lncRNA”, “cancer”, “c-Myc upregulated”, “MY”, “biomarker”, and “mechanism”. Emphasis was placed on original research articles, systematic reviews, and relevant narrative reviews that examine the functional role, regulatory mechanisms, and clinical significance of VPS9D1-AS1 across various cancer types. Duplicate records and literature lacking a direct connection to VPS9D1-AS1 or cancer biology were excluded.

## Hepatocellular carcinoma
Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) ranks as the fifth most prevalent cancer globally and stands as the fourth leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, exhibiting a 5-year survival rate as low as 18.1% [ ]. The malignant transformation leading to HCC is a multifaceted process influenced by a range of genetic and external environmental factors, making early detection and intervention vital for enhancing treatment outcomes. Although advances in surgical procedures as well as liver transplantation have achieved better short-term survival, the HCC prognosis is still very poor due to post-surgical recurrence and possible metastasis [ , ]. It is necessary to explore the molecular mechanisms and new therapeutic targets to establish novel and effective therapeutics for HCC.
By means of mining the microarray data in the GEO database and profiling expression of lncRNA in HCC, it was observed that compared with control tissues, the expression level of VPS9D1-AS1 increased by 7.17-fold than thate in HCC tissues. We also detected the expression of VPS9D1-AS1 in HCC tissues and adjacent tissues (180 pairs) by RT-qPCR. The results showed that the expression level of VPS9D1-AS1 in HCC tissues was 3.37 times that in adjacent normal tissues. More importantly, VPS9D1-AS1 abundance was positively correlated with tumor size and more advanced tumor stage, lymph node metastasis, and TNM stage. HCC patients with high expression of VPS9D1-AS1 displayed a worse OS. Downregulation of VPS9D1-AS1 can inhibit the proliferation and colony formation of HepG2 cells [ ]. Additionally, Fa et al. reported that VPS9D1-AS1 and SEC61 translocon alpha 1 subunit (SEC61A1) were significantly upregulated in HCC cell lines, while miR-491-5p was found to be lowly expressed in HCC. RNA pull-down and rescue experiments indicated that VPS9D1-AS1 functions as a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) by adsorbing miR-491-5p, thereby upregulating the expression of SEC61A1, which promotes proliferation, migration, and stemness in HCC [ ]. Collectively, these findings underscore the pivotal role of VPS9D1-AS1 in the early diagnosis, prognostic assessment, and treatment of HCC, suggesting its potential as a biomarker and therapeutic target.

## Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma
Esophageal cancer ranks as the ninth most common cancer and the sixth leading cause of cancer-related mortality [ ]. Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) is the predominant subtype of esophageal cancer, accounting for 80% of all esophageal cancer cases, and can develop at any location along the esophagus [ ]. Currently, treatment methods for ESCC primarily include surgical resection, complemented by radiotherapy and chemotherapy. However, the prognosis for patients remains poor, characterized by a low 5-year survival rate, frequent local relapses, and rapid lymph node metastasis. Consequently, there is an urgent need to identify new therapeutic targets to enhance the treatment efficacy of ESCC.
The significance of VPS9D1-AS1 in ESCC has garnered considerable attention. Gu et al. confirmed through RT-qPCR that the expression of VPS9D1-AS1 in ESCC tissues and cell lines was significantly upregulated, and this upregulation was closely associated with histological grade, lymph node metastasis, and TNM stage. Functional analyses demonstrated that silencing VPS9D1-AS1 could significantly inhibit the proliferation, migration, and invasion of ESCC cells [ ]. Another study reinforced these findings, indicating that VPS9D1-AS1 expression was consistently elevated in ESCC tissues, and its higher level was associated with poor prognosis in patients [ ]. Mechanistically, silencing VPS9D1-AS1 disrupts the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway by downregulating key regulatory proteins such as β-catenin and c-Myc, thereby further inhibiting cell proliferation, migration, and invasion [ ]. Following the injection of cholesterol-modified si-VPS9D1-AS1 around the tumors of nude mice for 14 days, it was observed that the tumor volume and weight in the treated mice were significantly lower than those in the si-NC group, underscoring the potential of VPS9D1-AS1 in the diagnosis and treatment of ESCC [ ].

## Colorectal cancer
Colorectal cancer (CRC) ranks third globally in terms of incidence and mortality rates [ ]. The widespread use of colonoscopy has led to satisfactory diagnostic rates; however, it remains an invasive procedure. Alarmingly, the incidence of CRC in individuals under 50 years old is rising, particularly concerning left-sided colon cancer [ ]. Studies have shown that lncRNAs play a crucial role in the occurrence and development of CRC, such as lncRNA ITGB8-AS1 and lncRNA POU6F2-AS1 [ , ]. Importantly, the role of VPS9D1-AS1 in CRC has also been confirmed.
Analysis of three independent CRC datasets (E-GEOD-31737, E-MATB-829, and the Affymetrix colon cancer dataset) revealed that VPS9D1-AS1 was significantly upregulated in CRC tissues and RT-qPCR further confirmed the elevated expression of VPS9D1-AS1 in both CRC tissues and cell lines [ ]. It demonstrated significant diagnostic ability in differentiating CRC patients from healthy subjects (AUC = 0.94), highlighting the potential of VPS9D1-AS1 as an early diagnostic biomarker for CRC [ ]. The high expression of tumor-derived transforming growth factor (TGF)-β pathway molecules and interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs) contributes to tumor immune evasion, thereby limiting the efficacy of immunotherapy in CRC patients. VPS9D1-AS1 integrates TGF-β signaling and ISG signaling to create a “VPS9D1-AS1/TGF-β/ISG” cascaded pathway, which amplifies tumor immune escape and presents a novel target for overcoming resistance to CRC immunotherapy [ ]. Huang et al. demonstrated that VPS9D1-AS1 adsorbs miR-324-5p in CRC through the ceRNA mechanism, alleviating its inhibitory effect on integrin alpha 2 (ITGA2), leading to the overexpression of ITGA2, which ultimately promotes the proliferation, migration, and invasion of CRC cells while inhibiting apoptosis [ ].
Angiogenesis, the process by which new capillaries grow from existing blood vessels, is closely linked to the growth and metastasis of CRC and many solid tumors. Studies have indicated that vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGFA) is associated with tumor invasiveness and susceptibility. High expression of VEGFA and increased tumor angiogenesis correlate closely with invasion, metastasis, and poor prognosis in CRC and other tumors [ , ]. VPS9D1-AS1 is activated by the upstream transcription factor CEBPB and subsequently activates key proteins p-phosphoinositide 3-kinase (p-PI3K) and p-protein kinase B (p-AKT) in the downstream PI3K/AKT pathway by targeting VEGFA, ultimately promoting the proliferation, migration, and angiogenesis of CRC cells [ ]. Studies have demonstrated that specific genetic polymorphisms in CRC-related lncRNA genes are linked to both the occurrence and progression of CRC [ ]. The polymorphism at the rs7206570 locus was analyzed in 500 pairs of CRC patients using Sanger sequencing. The findings indicated that CRC patients with the rs7206570 A allele exhibited a significantly lower risk of developing high-stage CRC compared to those carrying the rs7206570 G allele. Further analysis revealed that VPS9D1-AS1 and hsa-miR-361-3p were exclusively expressed in CRC tissues with the rs7206570 GG genotype. This study may provide valuable insights for preoperative staging of CRC [ ]. Overall, numerous downstream signaling pathways and targets have been identified in CRC, opening new avenues for research and treatment possibilities.

## Cervical cancer
Cervical cancer (CC) is a malignant tumor characterized by a high incidence and mortality rate among women globally, with particularly low survival rates observed in patients with metastasis. Despite advancements in screening and treatment methodologies, current therapies demonstrate limited efficacy against advanced or metastatic CC, highlighting the urgent need to identify new molecular targets to enhance patient prognosis [ ]. Shi et al. investigated the expression of VPS9D1-AS1 in CC and explored its associated molecular mechanisms. RT-qPCR analysis revealed a significant increase in VPS9D1-AS1 expression in CC tissues and cell lines. Functionally, VPS9D1-AS1 promotes the expression of stress-associated endoplasmic reticulum protein 1 (SERP1) by inhibiting miR-331-3p, thereby facilitating the growth and metastasis of CC. In vivo experiments further confirmed that the knockdown of VPS9D1-AS1 expression inhibited tumor volume and weight, suggesting the potential of targeting VPS9D1-AS1 expression as a novel therapeutic approach for CC [ ]. Kaempferol, a natural flavonoid compound derived from various fruits and vegetables, has demonstrated significant anti-tumor efficacy across a range of tumors [ ]. Kaempferol can downregulate VPS9D1-AS1 expression in the human CC cell line SiHa and upregulate miR-377-3p expression in a concentration-dependent manner. Furthermore, upregulation of VPS9D1-AS1 or downregulation of miR-377-3p markedly rescued kaempferol-mediated inhibition on SiHa cell proliferation, migration, invasion, and enhancement on apoptosis. These results reveal the correlation between VPS9D1-AS1 and miR-377-3p in the anti-tumor activities of kaempferol on CC cells, strongly suggesting that kaempferol could inhibit tumorigenesis by downregulating VPS9D1-AS1 expression as well as enhancing miR-377-3p expressions [ ]. In summary, these results verify that VPS9D1-AS1 played a crucial role in CC. Nevertheless, more clinical study should be conducted to investigate the relatively potential.

## Endometrial cancer
The morbidity and mortality of endometrial cancer (EC), which has become one of the deadliest gynecological malignancies, is increasing in the world. According to the TCGA database, the expression of VPS9D1-AS1 in EC was higher than that in adjacent tissue. and a high expression of VPS9D1-AS1 had a positive correlation with lymph node metastasis, pathological stage, and FIGO stage. Mechanically, VPS9D1-AS1 is the molecular sponge of miR-377-3p. Overexpression of miR-377-3p can reverse the proliferation, invasion and EMT of EC cells induced by VPS9D1-AS1, and it has been confirmed that SGK1 can bind to miR-377-3p [ ]. In mice silenced with VPS9D1-AS1, the increase in miR-187-3p expression led to the decrease in proliferating cell nuclear antigen (PCNA) and Ki-67 expression, reduced apoptosis, and decreased tumor volume and weight [ ]. The results indicated that VPS9D1-AS1 functions as an oncogene in EC and can serve as a potential therapeutic target. Baculoviral IAP repeat containing 5 (BIRC5) has been proven to function as a negative regulator of miRNA in various types of cancer [ ]. Chen et al. confirmed that VPS9D1-AS1, as a ceRNA, binds to miR-520-5p to induce BIRC5 expression, ultimately leading to an increase in EC progression [ ].

## Non-small cell lung cancer
Lung cancer ranks as the primary contributor to cancer-related fatalities globally, holding the second position in occurrence and the top spot in deaths [ ]. Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) makes up roughly 85% of lung cancer cases, encompassing squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), large cell carcinoma, and various other forms [ ]. At present, there is a lack of adequate biomarkers for assessing the prognosis and treatment response in NSCLC patients, leading to an unacceptably low overall 5-year survival rate, especially in metastatic situations. In recent years, the role of VPS9D1-AS1 in NSCLC has garnered increasing attention. The upregulation of VPS9D1-AS1 expression in 59 pairs of LUAD patients was confirmed through RT-qPCR and TCGA analysis [ ]. Another study also found through in situ hybridization that the expression level of VPS9D1-AS1 in the cancer tissues of SCC and LUAD was significantly higher than that in the paired normal lung tissues. Importantly, the expression of VPS9D1-AS1 in SCC tissues was significantly higher than that in LUAD tissues. Moreover, the high expression of VPS9D1-AS1 is related to lymph node metastasis of cancer. Kaplan–Meier analysis confirmed that elevated levels of VPS9D1-AS1 could predict poor prognosis in patients with SCC and LUAD [ ]. Liu et al. discovered that miR-30a-5p is involved in the tumor progression of various cancers, while kinesin family member 11 (KIF11) also plays an important regulatory role in cancer progression. Their research indicates that inhibiting VPS9D1-AS1 or upregulating miR-30a-5p can significantly suppress the proliferation, migration, and invasion of LUAD cells. This mechanism involves the competitive binding of VPS9D1-AS1 to miR-30a-5p, thereby regulating the expression of KIF11 and affecting the progression of LUAD [ ]. Therefore, VPS9D1-AS1 may serve as a prognostic biomarker and a potential therapeutic target in NSCLC, warranting further investigation.

## Prostate cancer
Prostate cancer (PCa) is one of the most frequent lethal malignancies among men worldwide, and it leads to significant burdens in public health. Despite the continuous improvement of diagnostic techniques and adjuvant treatments, the clinical prospects for patients with PCa remain poor [ ]. Elucidating its precise mechanisms and promoting the establishment of new diagnostic and therapeutic approaches are vitally important for improving the prognosis of patients suffering from PCa. Recent studies have reported that the expression of VPS9D1-AS1 is upregulated in PCa tissues and stimulates cell growth and migration in the extracellular microenvironment of PCa cells by mediating exosomal transfer [ ]. Bioinformatics analysis and mechanism studies have shown that miR-187-3p is lowly expressed in PCa cells, while VPS9D1-AS1 and fibroblast growth factor receptor-like 1 (FGFRL1) are highly expressed in PCa. The results from functional experiments including cell transfection and RT-qPCR suggest that VPS9D1-AS1, acting as a sponge for miR-187-3p, upregulated FGFRL1 expression in PCa cells to promote the development of PCa [ ]. There have been many reports that a close relationship exists between the transcription factor zinc finger e-box binding homeobox 1 (ZEB1) and cell migration and invasion in several human cancers [ ]. Wang et al. showed that ZEB1 has a binding site in the putative promoter region of VPS9D1-AS1, suggesting that ZEB1 activates transcription of VPS9D1-AS1. Furthermore, VPS9D1-AS1 promotes the proliferation, migration, and invasion as well as EMT of PCa cells by targeting myocyte enhancer factor 2D (MEF2D) via miR-4739 sponge [ ].

## Other cancers
In addition to the previously mentioned malignancies, abnormal expression of VPS9D1-AS1 is observed in numerous other tumors. In ovarian cancer (OC), VPS9D1-AS1 is found to be upregulated. Analysis of clinicopathological correlations demonstrates that the heightened expression of VPS9D1-AS1 in OC correlates significantly with FIGO stage and lymph node metastasis [ ]. The knockout of the VPS9D1-AS1 gene led to inhibited proliferation in OC cell lines SKOV 3 and A2780. This inhibitory effect is attributed to the direct interaction between VPS9D1-AS1 and miR-6827-5p within the OC cells. HMGA1 acts as a downstream target for miR-6827-5p. Reducing VPS9D1-AS1 expression boosts the levels of miR-6827. The reinstatement of HMGA1 expression counteracts the influence of VPS9D1-AS1 gene knockout on cell growth [ ]. Cyclin B2 (CCNB2), classified as a B-type cyclin subtype, exhibits high expression across various tumors, including BC, and PCa, with ccRCC showing the most pronounced difference according to the TGCA database [ ]. After controlling for variables such as age, sex, and TNM stage, CCNB2 continues to serve as an independent prognostic factor for DFS. Univariate Cox analysis has validated VPS9D1-AS1 as an independent risk factor for ccRCC, with enhanced predictive power when combined with CCNB2 [ ]. In head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC), the Kaplan–Meier curve analysis indicated that patients with elevated VPS9D1-AS1 expression experienced significantly reduced OS [ ]. Rajput et al. were the first to identify VPS9D1-AS1 as a novel lncRNA that is upregulated in gallbladder cancer (GBC), involved in hemostatic pathways, methylation processes, and apoptosis regulation, exhibiting considerable sequence similarity to viral and bacterial genomes. This suggests that cross-species horizontal gene transfer could play a crucial role in its tumor-promoting mechanisms [ ]. Furthermore, VPS9D1-AS1 is also found to be significantly expressed in bladder cancer (BLCA) [ ] and breast cancer (BRCA) [ ]. Knockdown of VPS9D1-AS1 can inhibit the proliferation, migration, and invasion of BLCA and BRCA cells. In conclusion, these studies have revealed that VPS9D1-AS1 has carcinogenic effects on various malignant tumors, but more in-depth research is needed.
Mechanisms of VPS9D1-AS1 regulation in cancer.
The roles played by VPS9D1-AS1 in cancer can be categorized into three types: ceRNA activity, protein interactions, and pathway engagement.

## Interaction with miRNAs
The ceRNA hypothesis has garnered significant attention in RNA biology research recently, uncovering novel mechanisms underlying RNA interactions [ , ]. LncRNA plays a crucial role within the ceRNA network, functioning as a molecular sponge for miRNAs to regulate gene expression [ ]. As shown in Fig.  , VPS9D1-AS1 can manage the expression of its downstream target genes through competitive interactions with miRNAs, thereby functioning as an oncogene in various human malignancies.
 Current research indicates that the SEC61 translocon subunit alpha 1 (SEC61A1) serves as an oncogene in CRC [ ]. In HCC, VPS9D1-AS1 facilitates the proliferation, migration, and stemness of HCC cells by enhancing SEC61A1 expression via sponging miR-491-5p [ ]. In LUAD, VPS9D1-AS1 interacts directly with miR-30a-5p, serving as a ceRNA that increases KIF11 expression and promotes the aggressive progression of LUAD [ ]. Research by Chen et al. revealed that the VPS9D1-AS1/miR-520a-5p/BIRC5 relationship is significantly associated with the initiation and progression of EC [ ]. Data from xenograft models suggest that the depletion of VPS9D1-AS1 can hinder EC tumor growth in vivo, primarily by acting as a sponge for miR-337-3p and modulating serum/glucocorticoid regulated kinase 1 (SGK1) expression [ ]. As a ceRNA, VPS9D1-AS1 enhances high mobility group A1 (HMGA1) expression by sponging miR-6827-5p in OC cells, potentially offering a promising target for diagnosing or prognosing OC [ ]. In CRC, the mechanism promoting tumor growth is notably linked to the facilitation of ITGA2 by miR-324-5p within the VPS9D1-AS1 sponge [ ]. VPS9D1-AS1 promotes stress-associated endoplasmic reticulum protein 1 (SERP1) expression by associating with miR-331-3p, thereby advancing the progression of CC [ ]. The elevated expression of VPS9D1-AS1 enhances the proliferation and migration of PCa cells by upregulating FGFRL1 through competitive binding with miR-187-3p, thus facilitating the aggressive progression of PCa [ ]. Furthermore, it has been established that the ZEB1/VPS9D1-AS1/miR-4739/MEF2D pathway [ ] and the VPS9D1-AS1/miR-184/c-Myc pathway [ ] significantly contribute to the advancement of PCa.

## Involvement in signaling pathways
LncRNAs play crucial roles in both the initiation and progression of cancer via various signaling pathways, indicating that modulating specific lncRNAs and their pathways might serve as viable avenues for cancer therapy [ , ]. An increasing body of evidence highlights that the alteration of the VPS9D1-AS1 signaling pathway is fundamental to several cancer types (Fig.  ). It is widely recognized that the Wnt/β-catenin pathway triggers the expression of the transcription factor c-Myc, which contributes to cell proliferation and tumor formation. Earlier research has established that VPS9D1-AS1 directly targets the lncRNA associated with the Wnt/c-Myc pathway and contributes to the tumorigenic potential of CRC cells [ ]. Reducing the levels of VPS9D1-AS1 can impair the proliferation, colony formation, migration, invasion, and cell cycle progression of ESCC cells. This effect occurs because VPS9D1-AS1 influences key components, such as β-catenin and c-Myc, within the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway [ ]. Additionally, VPS9D1-AS1 amplifies the TGF-β signaling pathway by elevating the protein expression levels of TGF-β, TGFBR1, and SMAD1/5/9 through a scaffold mechanism. Furthermore, the deletion of VPS9D1-AS1 in tumor cells leads to a downregulation of numerous ISGs, including OAS1 and IFNAR1, thus hindering the interferon signaling pathway. Also, this knockout enhances the susceptibility of tumor cells to the cytotoxic effects of T cells. Consequently, the tumor-derived VPS9D1-AS1/TGF-β/ISG signaling cascade facilitates tumor growth while promoting mechanisms of immune evasion. Therefore, this research positions VPS9D1-AS1 as a potential therapeutic target in CRC treatment [ ]. Moreover, the elevated expression of VEGFA is significantly linked to enhanced tumor angiogenesis, which correlates with increased invasion, metastasis, and unfavorable prognosis across various cancers [ ]. In CRC, VPS9D1-AS1 enhances the expression of essential proteins p-PI3K and p-AKT within the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway by interacting with VEGFA. This interaction leads to heightened vascular permeability and accelerated migration and proliferation of endothelial cells, ultimately facilitating angiogenesis in CRC [ ].
 

## Biological proteins and molecules interaction
Alongside its role as a molecular sponge for miRNA and involvement in related signaling pathways, VPS9D1-AS1 significantly contributes to the regulation of gene expression by directly interacting with particular biological proteins (Fig.  ). Findings from research suggest that lncRNA can influence the stability of mRNA by binding to RNA-binding proteins (RBP) [ , ]. Bcl-2 is an essential regulatory gene within the anti-apoptotic protein family, with its gene closely linked to its ability to inhibit apoptosis. The Bax gene, part of the apoptotic gene family, is closely related to Bcl-2, functioning not only to counteract Bcl-2's inhibitory effects but also to facilitate apoptosis [ ]. Additional experiments revealed that downregulating the expression of VPS9D1-AS1 led to an increase in Bcl-2 protein levels, while Bax protein levels significantly decreased, indicating that the elevated expression of VPS9D1-AS1 in ESCC is associated with apoptosis [ ]. In ESCC cells, following the knockdown of VPS9D1-AS1, the protein levels of Vimentin and Ki-67 were found to decrease, while E-cadherin protein levels rose, suggesting that VPS9D1-AS1 is capable of influencing the proliferation, migration, and invasion of ESCC cells [ ]. Zhou et al. noted that VPS9D1-AS1 is predominantly located in the cytoplasm, interacting with HuR to prevent cyclin-dependent kinase 4 (CDK4) degradation, which ultimately leads to the upregulation of CDK4, thereby facilitating the proliferation, malignancy, and resistance to apoptosis of HCC cells [ ].

## The potential clinical application of VPS9D1-AS1 in cancers
In oncology, identifying molecular markers is vital for cancer prevention, informing treatment decisions, and assessing prognosis. As cancer research progresses, novel treatment approaches have markedly extended patient survival rates. Nevertheless, the absence of efficient early detection methods for tumor markers results in a persistently high mortality rate for many malignant tumors. Early diagnosis, coupled with the development of evidence-based treatment plans, can yield considerable clinical advantages for those affected by cancer. LncRNAs are prevalent in both tumor tissues and bodily fluids, and their role in the regulation of tumor initiation and progression offers a foundational framework for accurate diagnosis and treatment, positioning them as promising new markers for diagnostic purposes, therapeutic interventions, and prognostic evaluations [ , ]. For instance, the FDA-approved diagnostic marker for prostate cancer, PCA3, exemplifies the clinical utility of lncRNA [ ]. Additionally, in both in vitro and in vivo studies, VPS9D1-AS1 has been shown to have a crucial role in numerous tumors, establishing it as an important therapeutic target in clinical settings.

## VPS9D1-AS1 as a potential diagnostic biomarker in cancers
Utilizing advanced technology, we detected VPS9D1-AS1 in clinical tissue samples and found that its expression was restricted in normal tissues, but markedly increased in a variety of cancer types, such as ESCC [ ], CRC [ ], EC [ ], and PCa [ ]. This suggests that VPS9D1-AS1 could serve as a dependable biomarker for cancer diagnosis. A notable rise in VPS9D1-AS1 levels was noted in tissue samples from patients with HCC, enabling distinction from healthy individuals [ ]. Liu et al. created a highly sensitive digital PCR method for measuring VPS9D1-AS1 in urine samples, which showed significant promise as an alternative diagnostic marker for PCa [ ]. The strong stability of lncRNAs in circulation, along with their resilience to enzymatic breakdown, renders them more dependable biomarkers when compared to other nucleic acids. Nonetheless, current research primarily concentrates on examining the abnormal expression of VPS9D1-AS1 at the tissue level. Future studies should pursue the exploration of VPS9D1-AS1 expression in bodily fluids (like serum, plasma, and peripheral blood cells) for malignant tumor diagnosis. Furthermore, the combination of VPS9D1-AS1 with existing cancer biomarkers holds the potential to improve the precision and specificity of cancer diagnosis.

## VPS9D1-AS1 as a therapeutic target in cancers
Due to the notable disparity in VPS9D1-AS1 expression levels between cancerous and normal tissues, knocking out VPS9D1-AS1 has the potential to hinder tumor progression and pave the way for novel therapeutic strategies. Fa et al. highlighted the function of VPS9D1-AS1 as a ceRNA, which modulates SEC61A1 expression by sequestering miR-491-5p, thus facilitating the advancement of HCC. Consequently, targeting the VPS9D1-AS1/miR-491-5p/SEC61A1 pathway could represent a viable therapeutic approach for HCC [ ]. The knockout of VPS9D1-AS1 has been shown to influence the proliferation and migration of ESCC cells and impede the growth of transplanted tumors in vivo, indicating its potential as a therapeutic target for ESCC [ ]. In xenograft models, the downregulation of VPS9D1-AS1 markedly curtailed the growth of EC tumors, with the underlying mechanism associated with VPS9D1-AS1's regulation of the miR-377-3p/SGK1 pathway [ ]. Inhibition of VPS9D1-AS1 also suppresses the proliferation, migration, and invasion of CRC and BLCA cells [ , ]. As a novel lncRNA, research on VPS9D1-AS1 is currently in the early preclinical phase. However, we remain hopeful that ongoing investigations will establish VPS9D1-AS1 as a critical therapeutic strategy in the clinical management of cancer.

## VPS9D1-AS1 as a prognostic marker in cancers
Monitoring the prognosis of cancer plays a vital role in minimizing deaths associated with the disease. In clinical settings, several factors such as tumor type, size, stage, grade, lymph node metastasis, and response to treatment all influence patient outcomes [ , ]. An elevated expression of VPS9D1-AS1 has been linked to unfavorable prognoses across various cancer forms and can function as a dependable biomarker for multiple cancers. Notably, VPS9D1-AS1 expression levels were significantly heightened in HCC tissues. This increased expression correlated with larger tumor sizes and more advanced stages of tumor, lymph node involvement, and metastasis (TNM). HCC patients exhibiting high levels of this lncRNA experienced poorer survival outcomes [ ]. Additionally, another investigation utilizing in situ hybridization demonstrated that VPS9D1-AS1 expression in the cancer tissues of SCC and LUAD was considerably higher compared to matching normal lung tissues. Importantly, SCC tissues displayed a markedly elevated expression of VPS9D1-AS1 compared to LUAD tissues. Furthermore, the increased expression of VPS9D1-AS1 is associated with lymph node metastasis in cancer cases. Prognostic evaluations revealed that the survival duration for SCC patients in the high-expression category of VPS9D1-AS1 was significantly less than that of patients in the low-expression category, suggesting that overexpression of VPS9D1-AS1 in NSCLC may be a promising biological marker for anticipating the prognosis of NSCLC [ ]. The Kaplan–Meier survival analysis also indicates that LUAD patients with elevated levels of VPS9D1-AS1 face a heightened risk of mortality, reinforcing its role as an independent prognostic factor for LUAD [ ]. In the case of CRC, patients with high VPS9D1-AS1 expression demonstrated a significantly reduced overall survival. Moreover, multivariate Cox regression analysis indicated that this was an independent prognostic factor, regardless of age, pathological stage, and TNM classification in CRC, with no interference from other clinical parameters [ , ]. Furthermore, elevated levels of VPS9D1-AS1 are associated with unfavorable outcomes for individuals suffering from metastatic osteosarcoma, as it enhances tumor advancement and suppresses the immune microenvironment [ ]. These results imply that VPS9D1-AS1 could serve as a promising prognostic biomarker across different types of cancers.

## Conclusion and future perspectives
LncRNAs are a class of RNA molecules that do not encode proteins but play crucial regulatory roles in various biological processes. Recently, VPS9D1-AS1, also known as MYU, has emerged as a significant lncRNA characterized by abnormally elevated levels in various malignant tumors, closely associated with tumor initiation and progression. Research indicates that VPS9D1-AS1 has a noteworthy promoting influence on several cancers, including HCC, ESCC, CRC, EC, NSCLC, OC, BLCA, BRCA, HNSCC, and PCa. VPS9D1-AS1 controls tumors by several mechanisms: it functions as a ceRNA to affect the miRNA expression, binds with proteins to affect signal pathways, and directly involves in regulating biological events such as cell cycle and apoptosis. In HCC, VPS9D1-AS1 upregulates CDK4 via interaction with HuR and thereby promotes cell cycle progression. It inhibits cell stability and invasion of ESCC by downregulating proteins related to Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway. In addition, it activates PI3K/AKT signals by modulating VEGFA expression in CRC and facilitates angiogenesis. These findings help to uncover the complicated and diverse functions of VPS9D1-AS1 in tumor regulation, supporting its value as an important cancer marker and therapeutic target.
The tumor-promoting effect of VPS9D1-AS1 in different carcinoma types had been identified by a number of works and the corresponding mechanisms are also well documented. Nevertheless, there are several limitations of past studies. Firstly, the studies on the role of VPS9D1-AS1 are mainly focused on certain types of cancer or single pathway; it is not sufficient for systematic exploration involving multi-disease comparison analysis. Secondly, many studies have utilized relatively small sample sizes, which hinders broad multi-center and large-sample clinical validation, thereby affecting the credibility of their clinical applicability. Furthermore, the diagnostic sensitivity, specificity, and predictive capabilities of VPS9D1-AS1 as a biomarker have not undergone rigorous evaluation in clinical trials. There is still a need for comprehensive assessments of its expression dynamics across different cancer types and stages, in addition to exploring its potential combined use with established clinical markers. Additionally, the mechanisms by which VPS9D1-AS1 influences the tumor immune microenvironment and contributes to drug resistance are not yet fully understood, particularly regarding its future applications in immunotherapy and targeted treatment strategies that require further investigation.
VPS9D1-AS1 is expressed at abnormally high levels across various cancers and is notably linked to adverse patient prognosis, indicating its potential as a biomarker. For instance, in HCC, elevated levels of VPS9D1-AS1 closely correlate with tumor size, TNM stage, and overall patient survival rates. The expression of VPS9D1-AS1 is remarkably correlated with lymph node metastasis and overall survival time in NSCLC. Meanwhile, in CRC, VPS9D1-AS1 high expression also contributes to immune escape and poor prognosis. These data imply that VPS9D1-AS1 might have potential to be a useful biomarker for early-stage cancer diagnosis, prognosis assessment, and response to therapy monitoring. In addition, the cancer cell-specific overexpression of VPS9D1-AS1 supports its potential for therapeutic target. Previous studies have attempted to suppress VPS9D1-AS1 expression by siRNA or antisense oligonucleotides, and achieved significant anti-cancer effects in animal models. These data present the basis and technical support for VPS9D1-AS1 in clinical application; however, further validation of safety and efficacy in human beings is needed.
The role of VPS9D1-AS1 in tumors needs to be investigated further, which is expected to promote the clinical utilization and development. In the first place, we need a more general analysis that combines several cancer types and multi-omics. This will allow for a systematic comparison of expression profiles and functional mechanisms represented by VPS9D1-ASI across cancer types, as well as its associations with clinical outcomes. Through integrating the genomic, transcriptomic, epigenomic, and proteomic data, a comprehensive regulatory network of VPS9D1-AS1 can be built to reveal its potential role in tumorigenesis and progression. Moreover, a prospective multi-center clinical research cohort should be established to assess the application value of VPS9D1-AS1 for early diagnosis, efficacy forecasting, and recurrence monitoring. At the same time, its diagnostic synergy with other biomarkers (CEA, CA199, PD-L1, and others) should be explored to improve the diagnosis precision. Lastly, establishing detection methods for VPS9D1-AS1 in blood, urine, or exosomes could promote its clinical utility as a liquid biopsy biomarker. It is hoped that through the use of new methods of detection which have high sensitivity and specificity (detection technology such as nanotechnology, microfluidic chip-based detection), the adaptation will make easier and more precise testing available to “patients”.
In summary, we elucidated that VPS9D1-AS1 is a novel lncRNA playing multiple complex biologic roles in distinct cancers. It regulates cell growth, apoptosis, migration, invasion, drug resistance, and EMT.VPS9D1-AS1 expression level is strongly associated with several clinicopathological parameters, suggesting that it may be a promising biomarker and therapeutic target for the treatment of cancer. Nevertheless, existing studies meet with difficulties of unidentified pathogenesis, low clinical reliability, and limited detection approaches. It is thus necessary to foster interdisciplinary cooperation and facilitative integration of basic research with clinical work. It is this goal that we hope will ultimately translate the shed lights on VPS9D1-AS1 into practical diagnostic and therapeutic tools in cancer, and thus renew patients suffering from cancer. With the continuous development of more in-depth investigations and technology, VPS9D1-AS1 may potentially serve as a promising factor in the diagnosis and treatment of cancer, contributing to precision oncology.
