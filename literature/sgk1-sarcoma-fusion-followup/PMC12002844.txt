## Highlights
•Ferroptosis is linked to multiple metabolic pathways, including iron metabolism and redox homeostasis.
•Exerkines serve as crucial mediators for optimizing cellular homeostasis.
•Exerkines regulate ferroptosis across varying intensities and disease contexts.
•Exerkines-mediated ferroptosis facilitate the design of targeted exercise interventions for disease rehabilitation.
Keywords: Ferroptosis, Exerkines, Lipid peroxidation, Iron homeostasis

## Abstract
Ferroptosis is a programmed cell death, and its mechanism involves multiple metabolic pathways, such as iron and lipid metabolism, and redox homeostasis. Exerkines are important mediators that optimize cellular homeostasis and maintain physiological health during exercise stimulation. This article comprehensively examines the mechanisms and regulatory networks for governing ferroptosis and summarizes the impact of exercise and exerkines on ferroptosis under varying load intensities and disease contexts. Notably, despite its significant efficacy and minimal side effects, the therapeutic and prognostic potential of exercise in ferroptosis-related diseases remains largely unexplored. This article, by summarizing recent progresses in the regulation of exerkines-mediated ferroptosis, could further uncover the preventive or alleviative mechanisms of some diseases upon exercise interventions, which will be beneficial to design exercise interventional strategies for alleviating disease progression through the regulation of ferroptosis.

## Introduction
Ferroptosis is a form of programmed cell death characterized by iron-dependent lipid peroxidation, which distinguishes it from classical apoptosis, necrosis, or autophagy. Its central feature involves the aberrant accumulation of intracellular iron leading to oxidation of cellular membrane lipids and subsequent impairment of cellular function. This process entails the buildup of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and disruption in antioxidant systems such as glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4). 
Although human clinical trials investigating ferroptosis are still in their nascent stages, the majority of current studies have focused primarily on in vitro experiments and animal models. Nevertheless, existing evidence strongly suggests that ferroptosis may exert a pivotal role in various human diseases , ( ). It has been proposed as a potential cell death mode for cancer therapy, especially against drug-resistant tumors. For example, specific inducers such as erastin or rat sarcoma (RAS)-selective lethal 3 (RSL3) can trigger ferroptosis, thereby impeding tumor growth. , The involvement of ferroptosis in neurodegenerative diseases such as Alzheimer's disease (AD) and Parkinson's disease (PD) has also attracted extensive interest because of its capability to induce neuronal dysfunction through iron-dependent lipid peroxidation. , Preclinical studies have elucidated the pivotal role of ferroptosis in acute kidney injury, cardiomyopathy, , and other pathological conditions. Pharmacological inhibition of ferroptosis using agents like ferrostatin-1 has demonstrated promising potential for mitigating organ damage. 
 While the development of targeted therapeutics for diseases is a logical research strategy, behavioral interventions appear to possess greater advantages in minimizing drug side effects and reducing therapeutic costs. The release and circulation of exerkines during exercise stimulation have extensive and systematic effects on the body. Among the cardiovascular benefits of exercise, exerkines promote angiogenesis, enhance blood pressure regulation, and improve endothelial function through an interaction with vascular endothelium. In adipose tissue, exercise promotes the browning of white adipose tissue and accelerates lipid metabolism by releasing exerkines. In the liver, exerkines enhance glucose metabolism and fatty acid uptake, and exercise can reduce hepatic steatosis without impacting body weight. Importantly, exercise can also alleviate disease progression through suppressing ferroptosis, as exhibited the enhanced neurological function in rats with ischemic stroke upon preconditioning exercise. Similarly, lifelong exercise reveals superior efficacy for delaying the atrophy of skeletal muscle through the mitigation of oxidative stress, inflammation, and ferroptosis during the aging process. , 
In this article, we delve into the fundamental mechanisms and regulatory networks for governing ferroptosis, outline the recently reported action mechanisms of exerkines in regulating ferroptosis, and explore the emerging therapeutic opportunities and associated challenges.

2.

## Ferroptosis
2.1.

## The mechanism of ferroptosis
Ferroptosis is a form of iron ion-mediated cell death, distinct from other types of cell death such as apoptosis and necrosis, primarily resulting in cellular demise through the disruption of the cell membrane. Within cells, iron is predominantly bound to complexes such as heme, iron–sulfur clusters, and ferritin. The minute quantity of iron that remains unbound in this manner is referred to as the labile iron pool (LIP). An increase in the LIP of a cell, especially an imbalance in iron metabolism, is a prerequisite for the occurrence of ferroptosis, and iron not only directly oxidizes phospholipids (PL) with polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) through the Fenton reaction, but also serves as an indispensable cofactor for lipid peroxidases, including lipoxygenase (Alox). Cell experiments have corroborated that the overactivation of transferrin receptor 1 (TfR1) leads to TfR1-mediated excessive iron uptake in cells, which underlies the induction of ferroptosis by cellular stress, thereby underscoring the crucial role of TfR1 in regulating this process. A portion of iron entering the cell is transported and released by divalent metal transporter 1 (DMT1) as ferrous iron (Fe2+) into the LIP, while the surplus iron is stored as trivalent iron (Fe3+) in ferritin. Ferritin, a vital iron-storing soluble protein in the human body, is composed of ferritin heavy polypeptide 1 (FTH1) and ferritin light chain (FTL). Functionally, only FTH1 can utilize oxygen to transform divalent iron ions to trivalent iron ions and release the stored iron ions as required by the body. The current literature concurs that the alteration in ferritin has a profound impact on ferroptosis. Specifically, inhibiting ferritin degradation and elevating intracellular ferritin levels can mitigate the onset of ferroptosis, which would otherwise exacerbate its progression. 
Peroxidation of membrane lipids and disruption of iron homeostasis are regarded as the primary causes of ferroptosis in cells. Phosphatidyl ethanolamine (PE) containing arachidonic acid (PE-AA) or adrenal acid (PE-AdA), as lipids specifically involved in ferroptosis, can generate lipid peroxides through autooxidation or enzymatic catalysis, which are subsequently converted to lipid peroxide radicals under the influence of Fe2+. Lipid peroxyl radicals alter the fluidity, integrity, and stability of the cell membrane, ultimately leading to ferroptosis. The current research indicates that the lipid peroxidation of the cell membrane is primarily regulated by 2 classes of genes. The genes in the first group are associated with lipid metabolism, including acyl-CoA synthetase long-chain family member 4 (ACSL4) and lysophosphatidylcholine acyltransferase 3 (LPCAT3). These genes are primarily responsible for encoding enzymes that can esterify PUFAs and integrate them into membrane PL. There are 2 oxidation forms of PL–PUFAs, including autoxidation (non-enzymatic free-radical chain reactions necessitating oxygen and Fenton reaction) and enzymatic reactions catalyzed primarily by Alox12 and Alox15, which are encoded by another class of genes controlling lipid peroxidation, Alox15. Notably, non-enzymatic peroxidation of lipids is catalyzed by redox-active metals, particularly iron.
Given that PL–PUFAs and iron overload are prerequisites for ferroptosis, oxidative stress can be considered the trigger of this process. In 2017, ferroptosis was identified as a fundamental mechanism characterized by elevated intracellular iron levels, which result in increased ROS production that is not cleared efficiently, ultimately causing lethal lipid peroxidation and cell death. Morphological alterations of mitochondria are the morphological hallmarks of ferroptosis. In cells exhibiting ferroptosis, mitochondrial enlargement and fragmentation, reduction or disappearance of cristae, and rupture of the mitochondrial outer membrane are observed. The “iron triangle” of ferroptosis, consisting of iron overload, membrane PL–PUFAs, and ROS accumulation, represents the primary focus of research endeavors exploring the mechanism of ferroptosis. The regulatory mechanisms governing the initiation or prevention of ferroptosis, to be expounded upon later, are contingent upon the aforementioned molecular processes.

2.2.

## Regulatory mechanisms underlying ferroptosis pathways
Four distinct signal pathways have been proposed to inhibit ferroptosis: GPX4 regulatory system, ferroptosis suppressor protein 1 (FSP1)–coenzyme Q (CoQ) axis, GTP cyclohydrolase 1 (GCH1)–tetrahydrobiopterin (BH4)–CoQ signal pathway, and dihydroorotate dehydrogenase (DHODH)–CoQ signal pathway ( ).
 GPX4 is an enzymatic entity that utilizes glutathione (GSH) as an electron donor to engage in oxidation reactions, thereby efficiently safeguarding liposomes and cell membrane from oxidative decomposition. GPX4 plays a pivotal role in scavenging intracellular toxic lipid hydrogen peroxide by catalyzing the reduction of GSH to glutathione disulfide (GSSG), converting the peroxide bonds of lipid peroxidation to hydroxyl groups, and neutralizing its peroxide activity. Notably, the antioxidant effect of GPX4 is substantially reliant on stable GSH levels, which are primarily controlled by the cystine/glutamate antiporter (solute carrier family 7 member 11/solute carrier family 7 member 11, SLC7A11/SLC3A2, also known as system Xc–) on the cell membrane. This antiporter is responsible for the uptake of extracellular cystine and the ejection of glutamate at a ratio of 1:1. In the system Xc–, SLC7A11 predominantly undertakes cystine transport, which is highly selective for cystine and glutamate, while another subunit, SLC3A2, primarily regulates the stability of SLC7A11 and the transport of SLC7A11 to the cell membrane. 
Apoptosis-inducing factor mitochondria associated 2 (AIFM2, also known as AMID or PRG3) has been designated as FSP1 due to its ability to inhibit ferroptosis alongside GPX4. , , , FSP1 is a flavoprotein that binds flavin adenine dinucleotide (FAD) and functions as nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NADH):CoQ10 oxidoreductase to exert its anti-ferroptosis mechanism by catalyzing CoQ10 regeneration through nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH). In the FSP1-mediated anti-ferroptosis pathway, myristoylated FSP1 is recruited to the cell membrane, where it acts as an oxidoreductase. By reducing CoQ10 to CoQ10H2, lipophilic free radicals capture antioxidants, thus generating ROS scavengers to capture antioxidants and prevent the increase of lipid peroxides. , However, it should be highlighted that unlike GPX4, anti-ferroptosis activity of FSP1 is strictly dependent on its myristoylation and localization on the cell membrane. Recent investigations into the anti-ferroptosis mechanism of FSP1 have demonstrated that in the presence of oxygen and NADPH in vitro, FSP1 can facilitate H2O2 production and the conversion of FAD to 6‑hydroxy-FAD, which directly inhibits ferroptosis. 
The GCH1–BH4–CoQ signal pathway offers protection against ferroptosis by selectively inhibiting the consumption of phospholipids containing 2 polyunsaturated fatty acid acyl groups. , In the cellular process of de novo BH4 synthesis, GCH1 serves as the initiating and rate-limiting enzyme. The administration of methotrexate (MTX)—an inhibitor of dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR), which mediates the reduction of dihydrobiopterin (BH2) to BH4—can lead to the inactivation of BH2 and render cells unable to resist RSL3-induced ferroptosis. This finding indicates that BH4 is the actual inhibitor of lipid peroxidation in this pathway. Moreover, BH4 is also observed to elevate CoQ10 levels in GCH1-overexpressing cells treated with ferroptosis inducers due to the mitigation of oxidative stress and the conversion of phenylalanine to tyrosine, which is subsequently converted to 4-OH-benzoate, a precursor of CoQ10. 
DHODH plays a critical role in resisting ferroptosis in mitochondria by catalyzing the fourth step of the pyrimidine biosynthesis pathway, the oxidation of deuterium hydrogen oxide (DHO) to orotic acid (OA), and the transferring of electrons to CoQ10 in mitochondria at the same time, which is reduced into CoQ10H2. Brequinar (BQR), a DHODH inhibitor, can selectively induce ferroptosis susceptibility in cells exhibiting low as opposed to high GPX4 expression. The subsequent knockout model of DHODH and multiple recovery experiments strongly support the notion that DHODH exerts its anti-ferroptosis effect in parallel with GPX4 by functioning within the inner mitochondrial membrane. 
The current regulatory pathways of ferroptosis can be categorized into 2 types: the classical GPX4-dominated system and the CoQ-dependent regulatory systems that produce anti-peroxidation effects through FSP1–CoQ, GCH1–BH4–CoQ, and DHODH–CoQ signal pathways. The GPX4 system is present in both the cytoplasm and mitochondria, while the FSP1 system acts on the cell membrane, the GCH1-BH4 system functions in the cytoplasm, and DHODH targets the inner mitochondrial membrane. The identification of ferroptosis-inhibiting signal pathways at distinct sites and targets enables further exploration of the specific mechanisms of ferroptosis and diseases, as well as intervention measures that can be taken at various stages of these diseases.

## Ferroptosis and exerkines
Numerous studies have demonstrated the manifold benefits of exercise on various organs and systems, including disease management and prevention. Regular physical activity enhances vascular health by improving endothelial function and reducing oxidative stress. Exercise promotes neurogenesis, reduces inflammation, and delays the neurodegenerative process. Additionally, exercise can mitigate lipid accumulation, improve renal blood flow, and prevent liver and kidney diseases. Exercise not only confers direct benefits to involved tissues and organs such as skeletal muscle and heart, but also reduces cancer risk. It is noteworthy that exercise exerts a substantial impact on iron metabolism in the body. Physical activity increases the demand of the body for iron, particularly in endurance athletes. Mild iron overload may initially enhance exercise capacity, and chronic imbalance in iron levels could impede the recovery and post-exercise function of skeletal muscle. The intricate relationship among iron metabolism in skeletal muscle, oxidative stress, and neurodegenerative disorders has been established based on the research finding that exercise diminishes iron reserves in the body and stimulates the release of myokines from skeletal muscle, thereby safeguarding neuronal tissue. 
In fact, exercise at different intensities can elicit distinct physiological responses for cellular homeostasis in the body. Generally, during low-intensity exercise, aerobic metabolism predominates for energy production, thus leading to the activation of adenosine 5′-monophosphate activated protein kinase (AMPK) and enhanced expression of peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor γ coactivator-1 α (PGC-1α), as well as the presence of nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (NFE2L2, also known as Nrf2). , , This results in the induction of antioxidant enzymes such as superoxide dismutase (SOD) and GPX, thereby augmenting cellular antioxidant capacity. Concurrently, mild-intensity exercise can reduce the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines, including tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) and interleukin-6 (IL-6), while promoting the release of anti-inflammatory cytokines, such as IL-10. Consequently, systemic inflammation is mitigated and the inflammatory state of the body is suppressed. Low-intensity exercise causes moderate physiological changes to the body, which can increase heart rate and blood flow, but will not induce cardiovascular stress. It also stimulates the parasympathetic nervous system, reduces the secretion of stress hormones such as cortisol, and improves mood and sleep quality. In addition, low-intensity exercise can optimize lipid metabolism and contribute to long-term body weight management. 
Subsequently, during moderate-intensity exercise, glycogen breakdown and lipid oxidation occur concurrently, and AMPK is further activated to augment glucose uptake and glycolysis. Moreover, partial inhibition of the mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) signal pathway commences, preferentially allocating energy towards maintaining basal metabolic functions and meeting exercise demands. During this period, Nrf2 signaling is further augmented, thus leading to enhanced antioxidant defense mechanisms. Simultaneously, there is a transient increase in the level of IL-6, which exerts metabolic regulation and pro-inflammatory effects; however, upon completion of exercise, the release of anti-inflammatory factors escalates, thereby modulating the immune system. Additionally, escalating intensity during exercise results in the release of brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) into the brain to facilitate neuronal plasticity and cognitive function. Similarly, the risk of hypertension can be reduced through regular exercise at moderate intensity by increasing cardiac output and vascular elasticity. Moderate-intensity exercise improves oxygenation capacity, thereby enhancing muscle endurance. Importantly, moderate-intensity exercise is the predominant exercise employed in current studies investigating anti-ferroptosis. , , , , For instance, treadmill exercise at a moderate intensity effectively rescued ferroptosis-induced cognitive dysfunction and traumatic brain injury in mice by inhibiting the stimulator of interferon gene (STING) signal pathway. Moreover, pretreatment with moderate-intensity exercise demonstrated significant reduction of Fe2+ accumulation and oxidative stress in doxorubicin-induced cardiotoxicity injury of mice, thereby leading to the improvement of energy metabolism and the mitigation of mitochondrial dysfunction. 
Ultimately, high-intensity exercise predominantly relies on anaerobic metabolism, resulting in rapid glycogen depletion within skeletal muscle, enhanced lactate metabolism, augmented energy expenditure, and excessive post-exercise oxygen consumption (EPOC). However, excessive generation of ROS that surpasses the antioxidant capacity of cells can cause oxidative damage to proteins, lipids, and DNA. Acute high-intensity exercise can accelerate the blood flow velocity in the body, significantly increase heart rate and blood pressure, and produce a certain load pressure on the heart. In addition, acute high-intensity exercise training can elicit colitis in mice by elevating Fe2+ and Fe3+ concentrations while suppressing the phosphorylation of Nrf2, SLC7A11, GPX4, and FTH1. Moreover, exercise-induced critical metabolites including lactate, ketone bodies, and kynurenine also can affect energy supply, neuroprotection, and cognitive functions, and optimize the recovery and repair processes of the body. Furthermore, testosterone plays a pivotal role in promoting muscle hypertrophy and augmenting muscle strength. It should be noted that excessive high-intensity exercise could temporarily suppress immune function and increase infection risk. 
Considering that various tissues and organs in the body, particularly skeletal muscle, release small molecular metabolites to regulate the physiological state of the internal environment and facilitate tissue-crossing communication during exercise, it has been proposed that peptides, nucleic acids, and metabolites released post-exercise collectively constitute what are referred to as “exerkines”. These small peptides, microRNAs, and diverse metabolites secreted by exercise stimulation are produced and discharged by the liver, skeletal muscle, adipose, and other tissues in response to alterations in the hemostasis of the body during exercise. These exerkines act on multiple organs and tissues through autocrine, paracrine, and endocrine mechanisms ( ). Numerous studies have reported the regulation of ferroptosis in different diseases by exerkines ( ).
 3.1.

## Fibronectin type III domain protein 5 (FNDC5)/irisin
Irisin is generated by the proteolytic cleavage of the N-terminal signal peptide containing FNDC5 at glutamate 112. This highly conserved and functional small molecule polypeptide is regulated by PGC-1α and secreted by exercise stimulation. As a well-recognized exerkine, irisin has been initially identified to play a significant role in promoting the browning of white adipose tissue and preventing metabolic diseases. Peripheral irisin may modulate the neuroprotective effects of exercise on synaptic plasticity and memory by entering the brain and upregulating BDNF and other neuroprotective genes in hippocampal tissues. Recently conducted studies have unveiled novel research avenues for irisin. In vivo experiments demonstrated that exercise effectively restored the decreased irisin levels in serum of postmenopausal women aged 55–65 years old. 
In vitro investigations revealed a direct binding between irisin and caveolin-1 (Cav1), wherein recruited Cav1 interacted with AMPKα to form a Cav1–AMPK complex, thereby further activating Nrf2, a downstream target of the AMPK signal pathway, thus resulting in increased transcription of heme oxygenase 1 (HMOX1) and ferroportin (FPN) to facilitate osteoblast proliferation. Similarly, FNDC5 overexpression can mitigate osteoblastic ferroptosis by suppressing the eukaryotic initiation factor 2α (eIF2α)/activating transcription factor 4 (Atf4)/CCAAT/enhancer binding protein (C/EBP) homologous protein (CHOP) signal pathway. Furthermore, a clinical analysis has revealed that serum irisin levels (12.85 ± 0.41 ng/mL, mean ± SD) in sepsis patients are significantly lower than those in healthy individuals (16.35 ± 0.58 ng/mL), and reduced serum irisin levels in patients are inversely correlated with disease severity. The administration of exogenous irisin in septic mice has demonstrated that ferroptosis is inhibited, mitochondrial function is restored, and the disease progression of septic mice is ameliorated. 
Although previous studies have demonstrated the inhibitory effect of irisin on ferroptosis during disease progression, it is evident that the anti-ferroptosis capacity of irisin discussed in the existing literature primarily arises from its direct or indirect interaction with Nrf2. In mouse models with sepsis-associated kidney injury or lung ischemia/reperfusion (I/R), irisin inhibits ferroptosis by elevating the mRNA levels of Nrf2 and heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1). , , However, this effect can be mitigated by erastin and is abolished in the presence of Nrf2-small interfering RNA (siRNA) and Nrf2–/–. In sorafenib-resistant hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) cells, irisin has also been found to counteract sorafenib-induced ferroptosis through the phosphatidylinositol 3 kinase (PI3 K)/protein kinase B (AKT)/Nrf2 signal pathway, thereby reinforcing drug resistance in HCC cells. Conversely, some studies suggest a critical role of irisin in promoting ferroptosis in cancer cells. Co-administration of irisin and erastin can lead to a further increase in intracellular free iron, lipid ROS level, and GSH depletion in human pancreatic cancer (PANC-1) cells when compared with erastin alone, and the inhibition of irisin can attenuate the ferroptosis-inducing effect of erastin in PANC-1 cells. 
The present discrepancies may be attributed to various factors such as disease types, cell types, cellular environments, and drug doses. However, if irisin indeed interacts with Nrf2 to influence ferroptosis, the overall mechanism would become significantly more intricate. Nrf2 itself is also a susceptible factor to exercise stimulation. The experiment demonstrated that exercise intervention could effectively counteract the decrease in protein levels of Nrf2, GPX4, and SLC7A11 in rats with brain I/R injury. In essence, Nrf2 governs the expression of approximately 250 genes associated with cellular homeostasis, which encompass the transcriptional regulators of SLC7A11 and GPX enzymes involved in GSH synthesis, and ferritin subunits (FTH1 and FTL). Currently, several studies have yielded promising findings regarding the impact of irisin on ferroptosis; however, the precise underlying mechanism remains unclear, and further investigation is needed to elucidate the primary-secondary relationship between irisin and Nrf2 in this process.

## Lactate
Apart from its crucial role in metabolic transition, lactate has been recognized for its significant role in cancer progression for a long time. Cancer cells accelerate their growth by increasing glucose uptake and converting substantial amounts of acetone to lactate, even in the presence of adequate oxygen. Interestingly, the Brooks team thought that due to pyruvate with a very low Michaelis constant for lactic dehydrogenase (LDH), cytosolic pyruvate tended to produce lactate in the presence or absence of oxygen. 
In vitro studies examining the influence of ferroptosis inducers on cancer cell demise have discovered that lactate concentration modulates the impact of ferroptosis inducers (RSL3 or erastin) on the generation of ROS in Hep3b cells, and the inhibition of lactate transporter monocarboxylate transporter 1 (MCT1) counteracts the intracellular ROS level. Furthermore, lactate has been reported to decrease ATP by up-regulating the intracellular AMP/ATP ratio, thereby repressing AMPK phosphorylation and elevating stearoyl-CoA desaturase (SCD1) as well as augmenting the proportion of MUFAs in the cell membrane. Unlike PUFAs, which are highly susceptible to oxidation, MUFAs have the potential to effectively inhibit ferroptosis by replacing PUFAs in the lipid membrane and reducing the accumulation of ROS. This suggests that lactate may regulate intracellular lipid metabolism in a manner that does not interfere with traditional ferroptosis regulators such as GPX4, thereby inhibiting the ferroptosis process in cancer cells. Conversely, a high lactate level induced by drugs in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) cells triggers ferroptosis through the down-regulation of GPX4 by a p38/serum and glucocorticoid regulated kinase 1 (SGK1)/neural precursor cell expressed, developmentally down-regulated 4-like (NEDD4 L)-dependent signal pathway. NEDD4 L, a protein with somatic neural precursors that is developmentally down-regulated, functions as an E3 ligase and mediates GPX4 ubiquitination, thereby playing a crucial role in lactate-mediated GPX4 regulation. , 
The understanding of lactate's identity has evolved with the advancement of research. Considering that various complex physiological responses within the human body reciprocate, we cannot assuredly assert that the presence of lactate in cancer is invariably detrimental. The emergence of lactylation also provides an opportunity for reevaluating the role of lactate in various diseases and physiological processes, thereby expanding our understanding of the interplay between exercise, lactate, and ferroptosis.

3.3.

## IL-6
Increasing evidence indicates that ferroptosis can be concurrent with inflammation, as cells can stimulate innate immune system elements through ferroptosis, contributing to the regulation of inflammatory damage, signal transduction, and cell growth. Strikingly, although inflammatory stimulation has the potential to trigger subsequent ferroptosis, it is observed that inflammatory stimulation does not induce such responses in IL-6 knockout mice. During the progression of ferroptosis, IL-6 exhibits a dual regulatory role in GPX activity. IL-6 enhances the transcription level of GPX2 while concurrently suppressing the transcription level of GPX4. Moreover, IL-6 instigates cellular oxidative stress and disrupts iron homeostasis, resulting in decreased cell viability, exacerbated lipid peroxidation, and impaired iron homeostasis. Furthermore, IL-6 can serve as a crucial link within the signal pathway to regulate ferroptosis by other factors. Elabela, a novel endogenous ligand of the apelin receptor, can execute the safeguard against angiotensin (Ang) II-induced ferroptosis in cardiac microvascular endothelial cells (CMVECs) by governing IL-6/signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3)/GPX4 signaling. These findings corroborate a regulatory role of IL-6 in ferroptosis. IL-6 participates the modulation of ferroptosis by regulating key enzymes, inducing oxidative stress, influencing iron metabolism factors, and mediating drug-induced inhibition or promotion of ferroptosis.
Suppressing IL-6 levels in various diseases holds significant clinical implications, and IL-6 produced by skeletal muscle contraction confers significant metabolic benefits by promoting lipid metabolism. As an intermediate node linking inflammatory immunity, exercise, and ferroptosis, IL-6 may act as the “thread” that connects these physiological responses.

3.4.

## Myostatin/follistatin
Myostatin, a classical regulator of skeletal muscle mass, was first discovered in 1997 as a key factor governing skeletal muscle size. It is considered a promising therapeutic target for the management of skeletal muscle wasting disorders, including muscular dystrophy, sarcopenia, and cachexia. Mice with targeted deletion of myostatin exhibited a significant augmentation in muscle mass, demonstrating an increase ranging from approximately 25 %–30 %. The health of skeletal muscle plays a crucial role in overall body well-being. Skeletal muscle dysfunction is a significant complication of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). In vivo experiments conducted on COPD mice demonstrated that myostatin downregulated the expression of GPX4, resulting in decreased levels of GSH and increased levels of Fe2+, ROS, and lipid peroxidation. The administration of exogenous myostatin or a ferroptosis inducer has been found to trigger cardiomyocyte death, whereas the administration of a myostatin blocker or the ferroptosis inhibitor UAMC-3203 can restore GPX4 expression, resulting in a notable reduction of ROS. Through modulating the muscle–liver axis, follistatin, a natural inhibitor of myostatin in skeletal muscle and a member of the transforming growth factor-β superfamily, has been reported to participate in the regulation of ferroptosis. Follistatin is secreted and enters the bloodstream following acute exercise, and its level in the liver is significantly correlated with exercise response. Follistatin-like protein 1 (FSTL1), derived from cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), has also been documented to up-regulate nuclear receptor coactivator 4 (NCOA4) expression in natural killer (NK) cells through the disco-interacting protein 2 homolog A (DIP2A)–p38 signal pathway, leading to ferroptosis of NK cells and facilitating tumor progression and immune evasion. 
The state of skeletal muscle not only influences the capacity for normal living, but also affects the health of other tissues and organs through crosstalk, demonstrating the interconnectedness of various physiological systems. Ferroptosis has been demonstrated to play a pivotal role in skeletal muscle atrophic disorders, while myostatin and follistatin serve as crucial regulators of skeletal muscle mass. However, the potential modulation of skeletal muscle atrophy through ferroptosis mechanisms by myostatin and follistatin suppression during exercise interventions remains elusive, thereby leaving ample room for further investigation.

3.5.

## Fibroblast growth factor 21 (FGF21)
FGF21, a member of the FGF family 19 (FGF19), is a secreted protein that regulates various aspects of body metabolism. Exercise-induced myogenic FGF21 has been confirmed to mitigate the accelerated aging of liver tissues caused by lipid accumulation through the stimulation of lipophagy. Additionally, FGF21 exhibits anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects by activating the Nrf2–antioxidant responsive element (ARE) signal pathway in a rat model with atherosclerosis. Interestingly, the treatment with FGF21 can up-regulate both total and phosphorylated levels of Nrf2 in hepatocytes, as well as the expression of downstream genes such as GPX4, SLC7A11, FTH1 and FTL, suggesting that FGF21 may play an upstream role in regulating ferroptosis. The induction of ferroptosis by iron overload is mediated through the overexpression of HO-1. In this context, up-regulated FGF21 stimulates ubiquitination-mediated degradation of HO-1, thereby providing protection against iron overload-induced ferroptosis in hepatocytes. This protective effect has been further validated through the application of recombinant FGF21, which effectively mitigates mitochondrial damage, liver injury, and fibrosis induced by iron overload through the inhibition of ferroptosis. 
Despite supporting evidence for the regulatory effect of FGF21 on upstream signal pathways involved in ferroptosis, further investigation is still required to elucidate the underlying mechanisms. Notably, considering lipophagy with the contribution to ferroptosis processes, an intriguing question is raised regarding whether exercise-stimulated FGF21-induced lipophagy can affect the state of ferroptosis.

3.6.

## FGF2
FGF2 is one of the earliest recognized members in the FGF family. As a ligand for FGFR1, FGFR2, FGFR3, and FGFR4, it plays a crucial positive regulatory role in bone formation and osteoblast differentiation. Skeletal muscle contraction and resistance exercise can enhance FGF2 secretion, facilitating local delivery of myogenic FGF2 to bone. Apart from its involvement in osteogenesis, FGF2 selectively up-regulates the expression of system Xc– in astrocytes, leading to enhanced cystine uptake and accelerated GSH synthesis. FGF2 has been confirmed to exert antioxidant stress and anti-ferroptosis effects in endothelial cells through the Nrf2 signal axis. Interestingly, FGF2 has also been found to induce Atf4 expression in both osteoblasts and vascular smooth muscle cells. Regarding the studies of FGF2 on ferroptosis, FGF2 not only up-regulates cystine uptake via system Xc– but also indirectly modulates the ferroptosis process by influencing Nrf2 and Atf4. The capability of FGF2 to promote cell proliferation under different cellular environments makes it an important target for cell repair or cancer inhibition, thereby highlighting its significant potential for exploring ferroptosis.

3.7.

## BDNF/cathepsin B (CTSB)
Disrupted or abnormal balance of iron levels in the brain is associated with pathological cell death observed in neurodegenerative disorders, such as PD and AD. , Postmortem brain tissue examination of PD patients has revealed mitochondrial dysfunction, iron accumulation, decreased copper content, and reduced GSH levels in relevant pathological regions. 
In vivo and in vitro studies have demonstrated that the administration of ferroptosis-specific inhibitors effectively mitigates neuronal death and memory impairment caused by amyloid β (Aβ) accumulation. BDNF, a myokine induced by exercise, exhibits predominant expression of its protein and receptor in the intracranial brain tissue at high levels. Both human and animal studies have substantiated that acute/chronic aerobic/resistance exercise can elevate circulating levels of BDNF. In the brain, astrocytes regulate iron overload in neurons by inducing cystine transport activity to maintain intracellular GSH levels while also storing significant amounts of iron. However, the functionality of astrocytes partly relies on the activation of the Nrf2 defense pathway. BDNF has been identified as a key player in circadian activation of Nrf2 in astrocytes through the truncation of tropomyosin receptor kinase B (TrkB). Although current studies have demonstrated that BDNF can freely cross the blood-brain barrier, there is still controversy regarding whether myogenic BDNF can be released into the bloodstream and exert its health effects on the brain. Nevertheless, other myokines, such as irisin and cathepsin B (CTSB), have been shown to be regulated by exercise and can be released into circulation, thereby crossing the blood-brain barrier and leading to an increase in BDNF level in the brain. 
The putative mechanism underlying CTSB-mediated cognitive enhancement involves the augmentation of BDNF and doublecortin (DCX) expression through a multifunctional protein p11-dependent signal pathway. Recently, nuclear translocation of CTSB within lysosomes has been observed in human pancreatic cancer cells, a significant molecular event implicated in organelle-specific initiation of ferroptosis. Nuclear accumulation of CTSB induces DNA damage through triggering the activation of the STING1/STING-dependent DNA sensor pathway, ultimately leading to the demise of organelles through autophagy-dependent ferroptosis. 
In fact, it is an incontrovertible fact that skeletal muscle and brain tissues can interact and communicate through small molecule substances. Given the intricate nature of the human body, it is imperative not only to acknowledge the facilitative impact of exercise on beneficial factors within the brain, but also to explore its potential involvement in eliminating excessive substances from this organ through various signal pathways.

4.

## Conclusion and prospects
Ferroptosis is an iron-dependent form of programmed cell death characterized by dysregulation of iron metabolism and peroxidation of PL–PUFAs in the cellular membrane. Intracellular molecules exert regulatory control over ferroptosis by modulating intracellular iron levels and lipid peroxidation. This process plays a pivotal role in neurodegenerative diseases, carcinogenesis, organ damage, and other pathological processes. Based on the aforementioned research status, the effects of exercise on ferroptosis may be mediated through various mechanisms, encompassing but not limited to: (a) Regulation of oxidative stress: moderate-intensity exercise can augment the antioxidant defense system and enhance the activities of antioxidant enzymes such as SOD, GPXs, and catalase in the body. This subsequently reduces oxidative stress and lipid peroxidation, potentially inhibiting the occurrence of ferroptosis. (b) Metabolic modulation: exercise at various intensities can impact energy metabolism in the body, consequently influencing cellular metabolic pathways, including fatty acid metabolism. Certain metabolites may either promote or inhibit ferroptosis. For instance, studies have indicated that moderate-intensity aerobic exercise can ameliorate oxidative stress by improving mitochondrial function ; however, high-intensity interval training might generate a substantial amount of free radicals within a short duration, thereby exerting a dual effect on ferroptosis. Regular exercise can aid in reducing pro-inflammatory cytokine levels in the body, indirectly impacting the process of ferroptosis. (c) Inflammatory response: exercise can also modulate the levels of inflammatory factors. Chronic low-grade inflammation is associated with ferroptosis, and regular exercise can indirectly impact ferroptosis processes by reducing pro-inflammatory cytokine levels in the body. (d) Iron metabolism: exercise can also impact iron absorption, transportation, and storage, which are crucial for regulating ferroptosis. Moderate-intensity exercise plays a role in maintaining iron homeostasis and preventing excessive free iron ions from contributing to lipid peroxidation. Although the current research on the relationship between exercise and ferroptosis remains limited, the impact of exercise or exerkines on crucial elements such as iron and ferritin in tissues and blood has broadened our understanding of the mechanisms underlying exercise-induced health maintenance.
Despite exercise being proven as a preventive and delaying measure for various diseases, with well-established foundations in the energy metabolism and oxidative stress fields, there remains limited studies on the interplay between exercise and ferroptosis. This limitation may be partly attributed to the disease-oriented background of current studies on ferroptosis, which primarily focus on promoting ferroptosis of cancer cells and developing corresponding precision therapies; however, many patients may lack the ability to execute physical exercise. The underlying mechanisms for the effects of exercise on the body are intricate; nevertheless, exerkines can serve as a starting point for exploring this complexity. Exerkines, as beneficial products of exercise, have potential to substitute certain advantageous effects of exercise and become targets for the development of nutrients or clinical drugs ( ).
 In addition, exercise remains a promising and cost-effective therapeutic approach for patients with unrestricted mobility, making it an essential aspect of treatment and prognosis. There are still several aspects related to ferroptosis and physical activity that warrant further discussion. Considering disease recovery, it is important to investigate potential differences between endogenous and exogenous exerkines in their regulatory effects on ferroptosis. Furthermore, exploring how aerobic and resistance exercises differentially modulate ferroptosis in various diseases can help to determine the optimal choice for promoting the prevention or rehabilitation of these diseases.
