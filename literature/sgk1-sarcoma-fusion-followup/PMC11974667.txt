Studies have shown that vascular dysfunction is closely related to the pathogenesis of Alzheimer’s disease. The middle temporal gyrus region of the brain is susceptible to pronounced impairment in Alzheimer’s disease. Identification of the molecules involved in vascular aberrance of the middle temporal gyrus would support elucidation of the mechanisms underlying Alzheimer’s disease and discovery of novel targets for intervention. We carried out single-cell transcriptomic analysis of the middle temporal gyrus in the brains of patients with Alzheimer’s disease and healthy controls, revealing obvious changes in vascular function. CellChat analysis of intercellular communication in the middle temporal gyrus showed that the number of cell interactions in this region was decreased in Alzheimer’s disease patients, with altered intercellular communication of endothelial cells and pericytes being the most prominent. Differentially expressed genes were also identified. Using the CellChat results, AUCell evaluation of the pathway activity of specific cells showed that the obvious changes in vascular function in the middle temporal gyrus in Alzheimer’s disease were directly related to changes in the vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF)A–VEGF receptor (VEGFR) 2 pathway. AUCell analysis identified subtypes of endothelial cells and pericytes directly related to VEGFA–VEGFR2 pathway activity. Two subtypes of middle temporal gyrus cells showed significant alteration in AD: endothelial cells with high expression of Erb-B2 receptor tyrosine kinase 4 (ERBB4high) and pericytes with high expression of angiopoietin-like 4 (ANGPTL4high). Finally, combining bulk RNA sequencing data and two machine learning algorithms (least absolute shrinkage and selection operator and random forest), four characteristic Alzheimer’s disease feature genes were identified: somatostatin (SST), protein tyrosine phosphatase non-receptor type 3 (PTPN3), glutinase (GL3), and tropomyosin 3 (PTM3). These genes were downregulated in the middle temporal gyrus of patients with Alzheimer’s disease and may be used to target the VEGF pathway. Alzheimer’s disease mouse models demonstrated consistent altered expression of these genes in the middle temporal gyrus. In conclusion, this study detected changes in intercellular communication between endothelial cells and pericytes in the middle temporal gyrus and identified four novel feature genes related to middle temporal gyrus and vascular functioning in patients with Alzheimer’s disease. These findings contribute to a deeper understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying Alzheimer’s disease and present novel treatment targets.

## Introduction
Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is the most prevalent neurodegenerative disorder, characterized by the presence of the so-called ATN framework, namely extracellular amyloid beta (Aβ) plaques (A), intracellular neurofibrillary tangles (NFTs) of tau (T), and neurodegeneration (N), which is often accompanied by diminished memory and cognition (Cummings, 2021; Jiang et al., 2022; Luquez et al., 2022). The etiology and mechanisms underlying AD pathogenesis have not been determined (Pang et al., 2022). Although the predominantly affected region of the brain is the hippocampus, the involvement of other regions in AD pathogenesis is attracting increasing attention.
The middle temporal gyrus (MTG) of the temporal lobe plays pivotal roles in verbal and semantic memory, as well as the integration of sensory information (Kong et al., 2015; Xu et al., 2015, 2022). Progressive deposition of amyloid plaques and hyperphosphorylation of tau are observed within the MTG (Latimer et al., 2019; Crivelli et al., 2020). One group reported that upregulation of adipocyte enhancer binding protein 1 and neuritin 1 facilitated the formation of NFTs in the MTG of AD (Piras et al., 2019). Govindpani et al. (2020) found that the mRNA expression of gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) receptor subunits within the MTG was downregulated, aggravating the symptoms of AD patients. Intercellular communication between multiple cell types, including neurons, glia, endothelial cells (ECs), and pericytes (PCs), was found to be essential to maintain normal function of the MTG (Nampei et al., 2019; Xia et al., 2023). However, few studies have addressed the role of intercellular communication of the MTG in AD pathogenesis.
In recent years, participation of cerebrovascular dysfunction in AD pathogenesis has attracted increasing attention (Zhao et al., 2021). Studies have demonstrated that damage to the blood–brain barrier (BBB) intensifies Aβ accumulation, thereby accelerating the progression of AD (Lin et al., 2021; Bellaver et al., 2023). Furthermore, it was established that vascular-related pathological alteration of specific susceptible regions can result in neurodegeneration and a gradual decline in cognitive functions of AD patients (Sierra-Marcos, 2017). Specifically, dysfunction of the neurovascular unit comprising ECs, PCs, glial cells, and neurons reportedly contributes to AD occurrence (Lochhead et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023). Notably, the MTG was found to be one such affected region in AD; however, the molecules associated with vascular function within this area require further clarification (Huang et al., 2012).
The rapid progress in sequencing technology and bioinformatics has opened an alternative window for elucidating mechanisms underlying AD pathogenesis. The CellChat algorithm infers relationships among intercellular communications by analyzing transcriptomic data from single-cell or single-nucleus RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq/snRNA-seq) (Jin et al., 2021). This approach realizes the quantification, analysis, and visualization of intercellular communication networks (Jin et al., 2021). AUCell is another widely adopted tool for evaluation of the pathway activities of cell clusters (Miao et al., 2021). To delve into the potential genes involved in a signaling pathway, two machine learning algorithms have been developed: least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) and random forest (RF) (Liu et al., 2022).
In the present study, we conducted CellChat and AUCell analyses of scRNA-seq/snRNA-seq transcriptome data from the brains of AD patients and healthy controls (HCs). We discovered profound alterations in intercellular communication associated with vascular function between ECs and PCs in the MTG. Using machine learning, we also identified four novel genes with involvement in the vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF)A and VEGF receptor (VEGFR)2 pathway in AD patients and further validated their alteration in AD mice.

## Methods

## Animals
We employed 3×Tg-AD model mice (RRID: 004807; Jackson Laboratory, Bar Harbor, ME, USA) and wild-type (WT) C57BL/6 mice (controls) (License No. SCXK (Jin) 2019-0004; Experimental Animal Center of Shanxi Medical University, Taiyuan, China) weighing 30 ± 5 g and aged 9 months (n = 10/group). To eliminate variations caused by estrus cycles (Oomen et al., 2013), only male mice were used. Mice were housed in a physiological laboratory animal room under controlled conditions comprising a temperature of 22 ± 2°C, humidity of 30%–50%, and a 12-hour dark/light cycle, and had unrestricted access to water and food prior to the experiments. All procedures involving animals were carried out in compliance with the ethical standards of Shanxi Medical University, and followed the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals (National Research Council, 2011). The Ethical Review Board of Shanxi Medical University approved this study on September 29, 2021 (Approval No. SYDL2021008).

## Single-cell dataset quality control and visualization
All analyses were carried out utilizing R Software (v4.2.3). The Seurat package (v4.0.2; https://www.satijalab.org/seurat/; Hao et al., 2021) was used for data processing, quality control (QC), and visualization of the unique features of each single-cell sample, including the numbers of detected genes and molecules as well as mitochondrial content. The GSE188545 dataset from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gds/?term=; accessed June 7, 2023) was downloaded for analysis. Clinical information for the samples is provided in . First, QC of this dataset was performed to remove low-quality cells and genes and to handle outliers and missing values (Xing et al., 2023). Specifically, cells with < 250 genes, > 6000 genes, < 200 molecules, and > 30% mitochondrial genes were filtered out. Subsequently, the single-cell data were normalized, and gene expression for each cell was standardized to ensure comparability between different cells. After QC, clustering and visualization of the dataset with resolutions of 2 and 20 dimensions, respectively, were performed using t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE). Marker genes for each cell cluster were used for annotation. We used the FindMarker function from the Seurat package to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs), which were then analyzed using the gseGO function in the clusterProfiler package (v4.7.1.003; https://bioconductor.org/packages/clusterProfiler/) to determine which molecular pathways were altered.
 

## Intercellular communication and signaling pathway analysis
To characterize intercellular communication in terms of signaling pathways, the CellChat algorithm for analyzing intercellular communication at the single-cell level (v1.6.1; https://github.com/sqjin/CellChat) was employed. Briefly, the official workflow was followed, and then the normalized dataset from Seurat was used as input for CellChat processing (Jin et al., 2021).
CellChat incorporates a ligand-receptor database (http://www.cellchat.org/) to draw inferences about intercellular communication. The CellChat algorithm was run to calculate potential interactions and pathways using “computeCommunProb” and “computeCommunProbPathway,” followed by “filterCommunication” to remove interactions with < 10 cells in each cell type. Subsequently, non-negative matrix factorization was conducted to predict communication patterns using the “identifyCommunicationPatterns” function, which aids in understanding how multiple cell clusters and signaling pathways coordinate and drive intercellular communication. The “rankSimilarity” function identified signals with significant differences by calculating the Euclidean distance. The “RankNet” function was employed to infer variances in the overall information flow across the network, but did not reflect the signal strength within specific cell populations. To address this issue, “netAnalysis_signalingRole_heatmap” was utilized. Using these methods, we quantified, visualized, and compared global intercellular communication networks and patterns under physiological and AD conditions.

## Pathway activity assessment
The AUCell package (v1.20.2; https://github.com/aertslab/AUCell) was used to evaluate the pathway activity in specific cells (Aibar et al., 2017). “AUCell_buildRankings” was employed to compute the gene expression rankings for each cell using the expression matrix and default parameters. The VEGF and VEGFA-VEGFR2 signaling-related gene sets were downloaded from the Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) website (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/index.jsp, accessed October 7, 2023; Mootha et al., 2003; Subramanian et al., 2005). These gene sets were used to assign scores to each cell. “AUCell_calcAUC” was utilized to calculate area under the curve (AUC) values for the gene sets in each cell. These AUC values were considered to represent the percentage of top-ranking genes in each pathway gene set for each cell and reflect the pathway activity scores.

## Identification of differentially expressed genes
DEGs between the AD patients and HCs were identified. Using the limma package (v3.54.2; https://bioconductor.org/packages/limma/), volcano plots were created to highlight the DEGs (Dimitrov et al., 2021). GSE5281 (accessed in June of 2023) provided the bulk RNA-seq data, from which the downloaded expression profile data were normalized. The DEG analysis of samples from the AD and HC groups was conducted using the limma package. The thresholds were set at P < 0.05 and |log 2-fold change| > 0.25. The “ggplot2” package (v3.4.2, https://ggplot2.tidyverse.org/) was used to draw volcano plots.

## Gene Ontology and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes enrichment analysis
Functional enrichment analysis methods were employed to validate the potential functions of target genes. Using the clusterProfiler package, we also conducted a functional enrichment analysis for all Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) terms (Zhu et al., 2022). GO is a widely accepted knowledgebase for associating genes with molecular functions, biological processes, and cellular components. KEGG enrichment analysis is used to study gene activity and explore advanced genomic functional information associated with such activity (Chen et al., 2023).

## Screening and validation of feature genes
The LASSO and RF machine learning algorithms were employed to select the feature genes of AD (Sun et al., 2023b). The RF technique was implemented using the randomForest package (v4.7-1.1, https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/randomForest/) while LASSO logistic regression was conducted using the glmnet package (v4.1-7, https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/glmnet/). The parameters were fine-tuned using 10-fold cross-validation to ensure optimal selection (Liu et al., 2023). The cv.glmnet function from glmnet was used for curve fitting in LASSO regression analysis. To determine which genes contributed more significantly to the predictive power of the RF model, we generated a ranking based on the MeanDecreaseGini criterion. The MeanDecreaseGini metric represented the average contribution of a feature to the reduction of impurity (i.e., increasing node purity) when it was used as a splitting variable across all trees in the RF. Featured genes were selected based on interaction analysis. The selected genes were further analyzed and validated by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves. The maximum value of the Youden’s index was calculated as follows: sensibility + specificity – 1. This helped to identify an optimal point on the ROC curve, where the corresponding threshold achieved the best balance between sensitivity and specificity. The keyword “cerebrovascular function” was imported into GeneCards (https://www.genecards.org/, accessed August 30, 2023) to obtain related genes.

## Spearman correlation analysis
The Spearman correlation analysis in R was used to assess correlations between expression of different genes. This non-parametric measure allowed us to evaluate the monotonic relationship between variables without making assumptions about the distribution of the data. To calculate correlation coefficients between pairs of genes, we used the cor.test function from the stats package (version 4.23; https://www.rdocumentation.org/packages/stats), applying the Spearman method.

## Quantitative polymerase chain reaction
Referring to Laszlovszky et al. (2020), the mice were directly decapitated under deep inhalation anesthesia with 3% isoflurane (RWD, Shenzhen, China). The MTG region of the mouse brain was dissected according to following coordinates, as described in related studies (Chen et al., 2015; Bogachouk et al., 2017; Gyllborg et al., 2020): bregma serving as the origin; antero–posterior, –3.5 mm; medio–latera, ±2.5 mm; dorso–ventral, –0.5 mm. The TRIzol-chloroform method was employed for immediate extraction of total RNA from the MTG of 9-month-old 3×Tg-AD and WT mice (Howell et al., 2021). Multiskan (Thermo Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) was used to measure the concentration and purity of the RNA. Thereafter, total RNA not subjected to any freeze-thaw cycles was reverse-transcribed into complementary DNA using a TaKaRa kit (RR047A; TaKaRa, Beijing, China). Quantitative PCR (qPCR) was performed using the SYBR Green qPCR Kit (RR820A; TaKaRa) to determine the transcription levels of target genes. Primer sequence details are provided in . The qPCR was conducted on a StepOne Plus instrument (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA) with the following protocol: primary denaturation at 95°C for 5 minutes, denaturation at 95°C for 5 seconds, annealing and extension at 60°C for 30 seconds (40 cycles), with a final melt curve analysis. Relative gene expression was calculated using the comparative cycle threshold (ΔΔCT) method (Olivares-Hernández et al., 2022).
 

## Statistical analysis
The sample size for each group of animals was set at four, which was comparable to that reported in other studies, but was not pre-determined using statistical methods (Aldous et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024). Data collection and analysis were conducted using a double-blinded approach. Unpaired two-tailed Wilcoxon rank-sum tests or unpaired two-tailed Student’s t-tests were used to compare gene expression between two groups. The level of statistical significance was set at P < 0.05. Data are presented as means ± standard deviation. GraphPad Prism 9.0.2 software (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA, www.graphpad.com) was employed for statistical data analyses.

## Results

## Annotation and visualization of single-cell datasets from the middle temporal gyrus of Alzheimer’s disease patients
Although the MTG is known to be affected by AD (Crivelli et al., 2020; Li et al., 2022), the molecular mechanism underlying its susceptibility is not fully understood. Therefore, we conducted an in-depth study of the MTG using various bioinformatics methods, including functional and pathway enrichment analyses. The online GEO database provides comprehensive bioinformatics data on human genomes in health and disease (Cheng et al., 2022). We analyzed GEO scRNA-seq dataset samples collected from the MTG of AD patients, acquiring a total of 76,120 cells that all met defined QC criteria ( A and Additional Figure 1A–C). Detailed information about these samples are provided in Additional Figure 1D. The cell clusters were annotated manually via assignment of marker genes or identification of genes with high expression levels ( ). Featured plots were created to illustrate the high expression levels of marker genes within the respective cell subpopulations ( B). Based on specific biomarkers, those cells were classified into seven subtypes: oligodendrocytes, astrocytes, excitatory neurons, inhibitory neurons, microglia, ECs, and PCs ( C). Subsequently, the Findmarker tool was employed to validate the significantly expressed genes across seven categories. For example, proteolipid protein 1 (PLP1) exhibited high expression in oligodendrocytes, while glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP) had pronounced expression in astrocytes (Additional Figure 1D), aligning with previously reported findings (Wang et al., 2022b). To determine what molecular pathways were altered in the MTG of AD patients, we employed the gseGO function for enrichment analysis of DEGs of the single-cell datasets (Additional Table 4). This revealed significant enrichment of pathways involved in blood vessel development, vascular morphogenesis, and vasculature development ( D, and Additional Tables 5–9). This is the first study to detect alteration of cerebrovascular function-related pathways in the MTG of AD patients.
 

## Alterations of intercellular communication in the middle temporal gyrus of Alzheimer’s disease patients
Intercellular communication is essential to maintain normal brain function (Dagar and Subramaniam, 2023; Si et al., 2023). However, intercellular communication of the MTG in the context of AD has seldom been investigated. We utilized CellChat to infer intercellular communication among seven types of cells in the MTG of AD patients. First, the number and strength of intercellular communication interactions were calculated using the “computeCommunProb” function. This revealed significant decreases in the number of intercellular communications in different MTG cell types in AD, with no obvious changes in the strength of intercellular communication ( A, B, and Additional Figure 2A–C). Further scrutiny of the number of intercellular communications showed that the autocrine and paracrine signals were significantly decreased in two types of cells associated with cerebrovascular functioning: ECs and PCs ( A and B).
 To further investigate which signaling pathways mediate cerebrovascular pathology within the MTG of AD patients, Euclidean distance scores were computed between each pair of shared signaling pathways. The signaling pathways involved in vascular function, such as COLLAGNE or JAM, displayed greater Euclidean distance in AD patients compared with that in HCs, implying that these pathways underwent significant changes ( C). Next, we used “RankNet” analysis to assess the information flow of signaling pathways within the MTG of AD patients. We observed downregulation of vascular function-related signaling pathways, such as VEGF, non-canonical wingless/integrated (ncWNT), and L1 cell adhesion molecule (L1CAM), in contrast to upregulation of the insulin-like growth factor (IGF) and platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF) pathways ( D). Furthermore, to elucidate how sender cells (cells serving as signal sources) drive intercellular communication, we employed the non-negative matrix factorization method for unsupervised clustering of the cell communication networks of the MTG in AD patients and HCs into different modules. We found alteration of intercellular communication patterns, and incoming and outgoing signal cooperation within seven types of cells in the MTG ( E and Additional Figure 2D–G). For example, in the AD MTG, pattern three of outgoing signals was composed of inhibitory neurons and ECs, whereas that of HCs was composed of astrocytes and microglia. Based on the strength of incoming and outgoing interactions, we further investigated which types of cells were responsible for driving the alteration of intercellular communication patterns. ECs and PCs were found to be the predominantly altered clusters of both sender and receiver cells ( F). The above findings indicated that collaborative patterns of various types of cells were altered in the MTG of AD patients
It is well-established that ligand-receptor pathways are fundamental to the intercellular communication landscape (Liu et al., 2021; Codden and Chin, 2022; Du et al., 2023). To investigate specific types of cells dominating the alteration of intercellular communication, we adopted “netAnalysis_signalingRole_heatmap” analysis. This revealed that the VEGF signaling pathway was significantly altered in PCs and ECs ( G and Additional Figure 2H and I). To clarify the autocrine/paracrine signals from various types of cells and their respective alterations in AD, we created a global communication map (Additional Figure 3), which illustrated disruptions in intercellular communication among specific types of cells, including PCs and ECs.
In summary, we found weakened intercellular communications in the MTG of AD patients, especially among ECs and PCs, which are involved in vascular development and function.

## Classification and characterization of endothelial cell subtypes pertinent to vascular function in the middle temporal gyrus of Alzheimer’s disease patients
ECs are known to work to maintain normal vascular function (Bryant et al., 2023; Sun et al., 2023a). The results described above revealed significant alterations of intercellular communication among ECs in the MTG of AD patients. To explore which signaling pathways in ECs were affected, we conducted KEGG analysis of DEGs (Additional Tables 4–9). The VEGF and AD pathways were preferentially enriched ( A and B). To determine the specific EC subpopulations involved in the VEGF signaling pathway, we categorized ECs into four subtypes using t-SNE analysis ( C and D). To further ascertain the identified genes within the aforementioned EC subpopulations, FindMarker analysis was conducted, identifying four EC subtypes: ERBB4high, CEMIPhigh, CLEDN5high, and SLC38A11high ( E and F). The AUCell package was adopted to further determine the signaling activities of these subtypes. Intriguingly, while the CLDN5high cluster exhibited enhanced VEGFA–VEGFR2 signaling activity compared with the other EC subtypes, the AUC values of VEGF signaling did not show obvious differences ( G and H). These results indicated that the CLDN5high subtype of ECs might dominate the vessel-related pathological modifications in the MTG of AD patients through a VEGFA–VEGFR2 signaling cascade.
 

## Classification and characterization of pericyte subtypes pertinent to vascular function in the middle temporal gyrus of Alzheimer’s disease patients
After validating the EC subtypes, we further investigated the parallel-screened cluster of PCs in the MTG of AD. KEGG enrichment analysis of DEGs from PCs (Additional Tables 4–9) also showed preferential enrichment of the VEGF signaling and AD pathways ( A and B). Based on t-SNE analysis, the PC subpopulations were categorized into four subtypes ( C and D) using FindMarker: ANGPTL4high, IL1RAPL2high, CUX2high, and TNChigh ( E and F). AUCell assessment of signaling pathway activity within the four subtypes of PCs showed augmented activity of the VEGFA-VEGFR2 signaling pathway in the ANGTPL4high subtype, but no significant changes in the VEGF-signaling pathway ( G and H). These findings suggested that ANGTPL4high may be the dominant subtype of PCs involved in pathologic changes in the MTG of AD patients.
 

## Integrated analysis of scRNA-seq and bulk RNA-seq data: differentially expressed genes involved in pathways in the middle temporal gyrus of Alzheimer’s disease patients
The above results revealed that intercellular communication, especially within vascular function-related pathways, was altered in the MTG of AD patients. To further investigate this observation, we combined the scRNA-seq data with bulk RNA data from the MTG of AD patients, bolstering reliability and minimizing noise and errors. Volcano plots were used to illustrate the DEGs exhibiting both upregulated and downregulated expression in the MTG of AD patients compared with that in HCs ( A, B, and Additional Table 4). GO functional enrichment and intersection analysis were then conducted on the selected DEGs (Additional Tables 5–9). Notably, vascular function-related signaling pathways, such as endothelium development (GO:0003158), vascular transport (GO:0010232), transport across BBB (GO:0150104), and BBB maintenance (GO:0035633), were inferred ( C–E), indicating cerebrovascular dysfunction in the MTG of AD patients.
 

## Featured genes associated with the vascular function of the middle temporal gyrus in Alzheimer’s disease patients
Given our findings indicating that altered vascular function in the MTG contributes to AD pathogenesis, we utilized machine learning to screen for genes with potential involvement in cerebrovascular pathology of the AD brain (Additional Table 10). Venn diagrams depicted the overlapping genes among three datasets: DEGs of RNA-seq and scRNA-seq (AD vs. HC), and genes associated with vascular function obtained from the GeneCards ( A). Totally 116 genes, brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BNDF), adhesion G protein-coupled receptor V1 (ADGRV1), adrenoceptor beta 2 (ADRB2), tropomyosin 3 (TPM3), somatostatin (SST), protein tyrosine phosphatase non-receptor type 3 (PTPN3), and glutaminase (GLS) were identified ( A). To identify the feature genes of AD, we used LASSO regression analysis to obtain 12 predictor genes with significant univariate effects ( B and C). RF algorithm screening was employed to ascertain the relationship between error rates and the quantity of classification trees, leading to the selection of 10 genes ranked in descending order of relative importance ( D and E). SST, PTPN3, GLS, and TPM3 were identified by integration analysis ( F and Additional Tables and ). Spearman correlation analysis revealed the synchronism of these four genes, which displayed high correlation in the context of AD (Additional Figure 4). We also validated the credibility of the methods adopted for screening feature genes by examining the ROC curves of the training cohort. The AUC value for the training cohort was > 0.99 ( G–J), and that for the testing cohort was > 0.69 ( K–N), both of which were higher than the criterion for acceptable validation (0.65). The above findings suggested the potential of these four independently screened genes to serve as biomarkers in the MTG of AD patients.
 

## Verification of feature gene expression through the integration of internal/external dataset analysis and biological validation
To validate the screened feature genes, we first employed the unpaired two-tailed Wilcoxon rank sum test to both the internal and external datasets. The internal dataset, comprising bulk-RNA data used for training machine learning models, showed significant reductions in the expression levels of PTM3, SST, PTPN3, and GLS in the MTG of AD patients ( A–D). Similarly, the external dataset analysis, which comprised DEGs in the MTG of AD patients and was utilized to verify the credibility of the machine learning model, also exhibited a declining tendency in the expression level of these genes ( E–H). To further validate the altered expression of these genes in the context of AD, we conducted qPCR analysis of MTG samples collected from 3×Tg-AD and WT mice. This analysis showed that the mRNA levels of TPM3, SST, PTPN3, and GLS were all downregulated in 3×Tg-AD mice compared with those in WT mice ( I–L). Taken together, our study findings confirmed that the genes TPM3, SST, PTPN3, and GLS were significantly downregulated in the MTG of the AD brain, aligning well with the bioinformatics analyses.
 

## Discussion
In the present study, we employed scRNA-seq datasets to meticulously construct a comprehensive transcriptome profile of the MTG in AD patients, revealing compromised vascular function. We also constructed machine learning models to screen for feature genes involved in regulation of vascular function, which led to the discovery of four novel related genes. These findings reveal not only the underlying mechanisms of AD pathogenesis, but also provide novel clues for the development of AD theranostics.
Our results show enrichment of vascular function-associated pathways in the MTG of AD patients. Cerebrovascular dysfunction represents a core pathological alteration that plays a crucial role in AD occurrence and progression (Alvarez-Vergara et al., 2021; Li et al., 2023). However, the exact role of vascular pathology in the MTG of the AD brain has been minimally explored. Our findings suggest that alteration of vascular function in the MTG could be an underestimated factor in the pathogenesis of AD. Govindpani et al. (2020) observed that aberrant expression of GABA signaling components in the MTG aggravated the symptoms of AD patients. Two studies using rat ischemia models reported that aberrant GABA expression accelerated the reduction of blood flow (Gnezdilova et al., 2011; Volotova et al., 2013). Myosin XVI (MYO16), one isoform of the myosin superfamily, was found to be significantly upregulated in the MTG of AD patients, where it facilitated NFT formation and Aβ accumulation (Horimoto et al., 2023). There is ever-increasing evidence that accumulation of Aβ and NFTs in AD patients is associated with underlying insufficient cerebral perfusion; the neurotoxic effects of Aβ and NFTs in turn further impair vascular function, pointing to a synergistic relationship (Alvarez-Vergara et al., 2021; Sharma et al., 2022). Integration of these studies with our findings has led us to hypothesize that changes in specific molecules and pathways in the MTG brain region intensify pathological alterations, thereby exacerbating vascular damage. Such a process would establish a positive feedback loop, hastening neurodegeneration in AD.
It is well accepted that proper intercellular communication is crucial to maintaining cerebrovascular function, with impaired intercellular communication contributing to AD pathogenesis (Enes et al., 2020; Botts et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2024). Our CellChat results demonstrate reduced intercellular communication among cells of the MTG. Among the seven cell types, ECs and PCs, both of which exhibit involvement in vascular function, were significantly reduced in the MTG of AD patients. Consistently, other groups also reported that impaired intercellular communication within either ECs or PCs expedited pathological alterations in AD, including reduced cerebral blood flow and heightened BBB permeability (Choi, 2018; Tureckova et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2024). One study utilizing the hypercapnic gas challenge method demonstrated that, compared with HCs, AD patients exhibited lower cerebrovascular reactivity scores in the MTG that correlated with the decline in cognitive function (Beaudin et al., 2022). However, the underlying mechanisms for this phenomenon remain to be further explored. We speculate that the altered intercellular communication mediated by ECs and PCs within the MTG could be a contributing factor to this decline. Furthermore, analyses of autopsied AD brains have revealed an escalation in inflammatory markers within ECs, alongside a diminished count and coverage of PCs. Dysfunction of ECs and PCs eventually disrupts BBB permeability as well as cerebral blood flow dynamics (Lindberg et al., 1988; Winkler et al., 2014; Fernandez-Klett et al., 2020; Nutma et al., 2021; Simmonds et al., 2024). Lau et al. (2020) reported the presence of specific subpopulations of ECs in the brains of AD patients. Ross et al. (2020) suggested that comparable subpopulations of PCs also existed. Despite the critical need for thorough functional investigations of these cell subtypes to advance our understanding of vascular alterations in the AD brain, such research has remained rare. The AUCell analysis in this study further confirmed that subtypes of ECs and PCs participate in the modulation of VEGF pathway activity. Specifically, ERBB4high ECs and ANGPTL4high PCs dominate the VEGFA-VEGFR2 signaling pathway, providing new insights into the functions and mechanisms of EC and PC subtypes in the MTG region of the AD brain.
Wu et al. (2017) reported that specific deletions of Erbb4 in ECs compromise the exploratory behaviors of mice. The role of ANGPTL4 in dementia was also previously reported. Chakraborty et al. (2018) demonstrated that ANGPTL4 expression in the brain promotes amyloid aggregation. ANGPTL4 was also found to modulate the endothelial barrier function of stroke-prone mice (Bouleti et al., 2013). Such results highlight the crucial role of ERBB4high and ANGPTL4high subpopulations in the regulation of brain function, suggesting their potential as novel targets for AD intervention. In the future, we plan to create mice with conditional knockouts of Erbb4 and Angptl4 in ECs and PCs to further illustrate the underlying mechanisms and roles of each gene in AD pathogenesis.
Bioinformatics analysis is crucial for identifying the fundamental genes involved in intercellular communication changes, with scRNA-seq and bulk RNA-seq representing powerful techniques for the discovery of specific disorder-related genes. Using this approach, we identified vascular function-related DEGs in the MTG of AD patients. Abnormal vascular function is the preceding event of AD occurrence (Hascup and Hascup, 2020; Li et al., 2023). Further screening of these DEGs with the LASSO and RF machine learning algorithms shortlisted four feature genes with downregulated expression in the MTG of AD patients: SST, PTPN3, GLS, and TPM3. All four of these genes inhibit the VEGFA–VEGFR2 pathway in tumors, aggravating angiogenesis and tumor growth (Bocci et al., 2007; Mehta et al., 2018; Cammalleri et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2020). To the best of our knowledge, this is the first report of the role of SST, PTPN3, GLS, and TPM3 in the MTG of AD patients. Our biological validation experiments using 3×Tg-AD mice recapitulated the machine learning results. SST, PTPN3, GLS, and TPM3 all showed significant downregulation in the MTG region in AD mice compared with that in WT mice, demonstrating trans-species conservation of the roles of these four genes in AD pathogenesis. Aligned with our findings, other studies have reported that overactivation of the VEGF pathway may lead to abnormal angiogenesis and increased vascular permeability in AD (Li et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2022a).
Given the aberrant alteration of VEGF in the AD brain, modulating the expression of VEGF or its related pathways has been adopted for the intervention of AD. Alvarez-Vergara et al. (2021) showed that sorafenib, by suppressing VEGF expression, could diminish Aβ plaques and ameliorate cognitive deficits in AD mice. Research by Jeon et al. (2020) indicated that vatalanib, by targeting the VEGF pathway, could also reduce tau protein phosphorylation in 5×FAD model mice, diminishing AD-related neurodegeneration. Interestingly, both of the EC (ERBB4high) and PC (ANGPTL4high) subtypes and all four of the AD feature genes (SST, PTPN3, GLS, TPM3) that were identified in this study are linked to the VEGF pathway. Therefore, these newly identified targets may have potential for mitigating AD-related symptoms via the VEGF pathway. Through understanding these molecular targets, we can develop more effective AD therapies.
Our research has demonstrated alterations in vascular function within the MTG region that exhibit high correlation with the identified genes. However, there remain some study limitations that should be noted. First, the scRNA-seq data were derived from a limited number of samples. Second, we used bulk RNA-seq data, which cannot provide precise cell annotations. Third, we did not test our findings in clinical human samples or validate the expression results of the four DEGs at the protein level. Comprehensive analysis of the AD feature genes identified through exploratory screening, and a more detailed investigation of how these genes (e.g., SST, PTPN3, GLS, TPM3) regulating vascular function is needed to further elucidate the underlying mechanisms of AD.
In summary, this study using bioinformatics analysis has revealed alterations in intercellular communication in the MTG of the AD brain. These alterations are enriched in the pathway that regulates vascular function. Machine learning-based screening identified four novel AD feature genes with involvement in this pathway. Our findings provide insights into the molecular mechanisms of AD pathogenesis and present novel targets for AD intervention.
