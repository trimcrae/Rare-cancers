WangChen LiuLingling ZhangJiale SunShanliang LvXinyu LiuZihao ZhouLianxin ZhuZiang NiHaiwen GuChunyan https://orcid.org/0000-0003-0228-5102YangYe Tumor relapse, fueled by cancer cell heterogeneity and an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME), continues to represent a major barrier to cure. The persistent dysfunction of both innate and adaptive immunity underscores the urgent need for novel strategies to re-establish effective anti-tumor immunity. 
Dexamethasone (DXM), a synthetic glucocorticoid, is a cornerstone of multiple myeloma (MM) therapy. Its primary mode of action is mediated through the glucocorticoid receptor (GR). Upon ligand binding, GR translocates to the nucleus to modulate the transcription of target genes, leading to the downregulation of key cytokines such as interleukin-1 (IL-1), interleukin-2 (IL-2), and interleukin-6 (IL-6). Beyond these canonical genomic actions, glucocorticoids also rapidly activate non-genomic signaling pathways involving kinases such as GSK3β and p38. Traditionally, the potent anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive properties of glucocorticoids have established them as mainstay treatments for various immune-mediated disorders. Paradoxically, in clinical practice, DXM is routinely combined with immunotherapeutic agents for both newly diagnosed and relapsed/refractory MM. This juxtaposition of its immunosuppressive reputation with its efficacy in immunotherapeutic regimens presents a compelling paradox. The specific mechanisms through which DXM and GR modulate the MM immune microenvironment remain largely elusive.
The cyclic GMP-AMP synthase/stimulator of interferon genes (cGAS-STING) pathway is a central regulator of innate immunity and anti-tumor responses. It detects cytosolic DNA arising from genomic instability and triggers interferon production. Upon recognizing aberrant self or microbial DNA, cGAS synthesizes the second messenger cGAMP, which triggers STING activation and the production of type I interferons (IFNs). The cytosolic DNA-sensing mechanism plays a vital role in cancer immunosurveillance. Tumor-derived DNA can activate the cGAS-STING pathway, triggering inflammatory responses and promoting anti-tumor immunity within the TME. However, despite the well-documented genomic instability in MM cells, the mechanisms by which these malignant cells evade STING-mediated immune surveillance remain poorly understood.
To address this critical gap, this study aims to investigate the immunomodulatory function of DXM in MM. The focus will be on its ability to activate the cGAS-STING pathway and the underlying molecular mechanism. It is hypothesized that DXM, despite its traditional role as an immunosuppressant, may actually engage a novel GR-dependent mechanism to enhance anti-tumor immunity in MM. To test this hypothesis, an integrated approach was used, including in vitro molecular assays, syngeneic murine models, single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), and functional immune co-culture systems. Our findings reveal that DXM activates cGAS-STING signaling and interferon responses in MM cells by dissociating a constitutive inhibitory complex involving GR and the phosphatase PPP1CB, thereby relieving suppression of the TBK1-IRF3 axis. This cell-intrinsic activation reshapes the TME, enhancing NK cell cytotoxicity and driving M1-like macrophage polarization, which collectively contribute to a potent anti-myeloma immune response.

## Methods

## Cell culture
Cell lines including ARP1, LP1, THP1, NK92MI, and HEK-293 were incubated at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO2. ARP1, LP1, and THP1 cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium. NK92MI cells were maintained in MEM supplemented with 10% horse serum, and HEK-293 cells were cultured in DMEM. All media were supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 100 µg/mL penicillin/streptomycin. THP1 cells were differentiated with 100 ng/mL Phorbol 12-Myristate 13-Acetate for 48 hours prior to use. Primary MM cells were isolated from bone marrow samples obtained from nine MM patients at the Jiangsu Province Hospital of Chinese Medicine using CD138+ MicroBeads (Miltenyi Biotec). The patients’ information is detailed in the . The 5TMM3VT murine myeloma cell line was kindly provided by Professor Wen Zhou (Central South University, Changsha, China).

## Plasmids and transfection
Plasmids encoding human NR3C1, its mutant/truncated form, IRF3, and PPP1CB were cloned into a tagged PLVX vector. Transient transfection was performed using Lipofectamine 2000. For knockdown experiments, NR3C1-specific siRNA (GenePharma; sequence: 5’-CCCAGGUAAAGAGACGAAUTT-3’) and PPP1CB-specific siRNA (GenePharma; sequence: 5’-GGUGGAAGAUGGAUAUGAATT-3’) were employed. Transfection efficiency was verified by Western blot.

## ScRNA-seq and data analysis
Single-cell suspensions were prepared from bone marrow cells isolated from mice treated with either phosphate buffered saline (PBS) or DXM. Due to limited cell yield, samples from 3 mice per group were pooled prior to single-cell library preparation. Cells with < 200 genes, > 2500 genes, or > 10% mitochondrial reads were excluded from analysis. To minimize batch effects, samples from both groups were processed in parallel using the same 10 × Genomics Chromium controller and library preparation kit. For data integration, we applied Seurat’s integration workflow to correct for batch effects between samples. Library preparation and sequencing were performed on the 10 × Genomics platform by Shanghai Bohao Biotechnology Corporation. Following principal component analysis (PCA), cells were clustered using a graph-based Louvain algorithm applied to a shared nearest neighbor graph. Cell types were annotated with SingleR based on known marker genes, including Cd3d, Cd4, Cd8a, Havcr2, Pdcd1, Lag3, Ctla4, Gzmm, Gzmk, Ifng, Pfn1, Il7r, Ccr7, Sell, Cd14, Tlr2, Cd86, Nos2, Cd163, Mrc1, Il10, Mmp9, Cst3, Ctsk, Slc9b2, Nkg7, Cd122, Ncr1, Cd107a, Gzmb, Actb, Cd69, Lcn2, Sdc1, Cd20, Mpo, Hbb, Cd1c, Cd10, Cd34, Cd123, and Ppbp. Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses were performed to identify biological functions and signaling pathways associated with DXM-upregulated genes.

## Quantitative real-time PCR
To quantify gene expression, quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) was performed following previously published protocols. The primer sequences used in this study are detailed in the ).

## Co-immunoprecipitation and Western blot
Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) and Western blot assays were conducted as previously described. 

## Immunofluorescence and immunohistochemical staining
Immunofluorescence (IF) and immunohistochemical (IHC) staining were performed according to previously described methods. 

## Cytotoxicity assays
Cytotoxicity was assessed by measuring lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) release using a commercial assay kit. Specific cell death was calculated relative to control samples. For antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC)/cytotoxicity assays, target cells were first coated with daratumumab (10 µg/mL, 24 hours) or pretreated with DXM (50 nM, 4 hours), and then co-cultured with NK92MI cells at the indicated effector-to-target (E:T) ratios. Cell death was quantified either by LDH release or by flow cytometry following CFSE/PI staining.

## Flow cytometry
Cells were stained with specific antibodies for 15 min at room temperature and then analyzed on a Guava easyCyteTM flow cytometer. Acquired data were processed and analyzed using FlowJo software.

## Proteomic analysis
Proteins were separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate-poly acrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE), subjected to in-gel digestion, and subsequently analyzed by liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (MS) (Lianchuan Biotech).

## Antibodies and reagents
The following antibodies were used: anti-GR (D6H2L, Cell Signaling Technology), anti-STING (13647, Cell Signaling Technology), anti-phospho-STING (Ser336) (72971, Cell Signaling Technology), anti-TBK1 (3504, Cell Signaling Technology), anti-phospho-TBK1 (Ser172) (5483, Cell Signaling Technology), anti-IRF3 (4302, Cell Signaling Technology), anti-phospho-IRF3 (Ser396) (4947, Cell Signaling Technology), anti-P65 (3987, Cell Signaling Technology), anti-phospho-P65 (Ser536) (3033, Cell Signaling Technology), anti-P38 (V121A, Promega), anti-phospho-P38 (9215, Cell Signaling Technology), anti-GSK3β (22104-1-AP, Proteintech), anti-phospho-GSK3β (AF2016, Affinity), anti-PPP1CB (10140-2-AP, Proteintech), anti-FLAG (14 793S, Cell Signaling Technology), anti-His Tag (66005-1-Ig, Proteintech), anti-HA Tag (51064-2-AP, Proteintech), Rabbit IgG (A7016, Beyotime), mouse IgG (A7028, Beyotime), goat anti-Rabbit IgG-HRP (FMS-Rb01, Fcmacs), goat anti-mouse IgG-HRP (S0002, Affinity), goat anti-mouse IgG-FITC (ab6785, Abcam), and goat anti-mouse IgG-Fluor647 (S0013, Affinity). Bortezomib and DXM were purchased from Selleck Chemicals. Salubrinal and toyocamycin were obtained from MedChemExpress.

## Animal studies
5TMM3VT cells were inoculated into 6-8-week-old male C57BL/KaLwRij or non-obese diabetic/severe combined immunodeficiency (NOD/SCID) mice via tail vein injection. Mice were randomly assigned to experimental groups using a computer-generated sequence and treated with DXM (0.1 mg/kg, three times per week), salubrinal (2.5 mg/kg, twice per week), and/or bortezomib (0.5 mg/kg, twice per week). An adoptive B-cell transfer model was established as previously described. Mice were monitored daily. Humane endpoints were defined as >20% body weight loss, hind limb paralysis, moribund appearance, or inability to reach food or water. Animals reaching any of these endpoints were immediately euthanized and counted as events in survival analysis. For the systemic MM model, disease burden was assessed by serum Ig lambda light chain levels measured weekly. Survival was defined as the time from tumor inoculation until reaching any humane endpoint as defined above. Mice that survived beyond the experimental period were censored on the final day of the study. Survival curves were generated using the Kaplan-Meier method. Micro-CT scans were performed at the time of euthanasia to assess bone lesion formation. Scans were conducted using Quantum GX, and bone lesion volume and number were quantified using Analyze V.12.0.

## Statistical analysis
Data are presented as mean ± standard error of easurement (SEM). Differences between two groups were assessed using an unpaired two-tailed Student’s t-test, whereas comparisons among more than two groups were performed by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post hoc test, using GraphPad Prism V.8 software. Survival curves were analyzed by the Kaplan-Meier method, and differences were evaluated with the log-rank test. Statistical significance is denoted as follows: n.s., not significant; *p < 0.05; **p < 0.01; ***p < 0.001; ****p < 0.0001.

## Results

## DXM activates cGAS-STING signaling and interferon responses in MM cells
Previous studies have established that bortezomib and melphalan activate the cGAS-STING pathway in MM cells ; it was unknown whether DXM exerts a similar effect. Western blot analysis revealed that DXM treatment induced dose-dependent phosphorylation of STING, TBK1, and IRF3, but not of p65 (NF-κB) ( ; ). Consistent with pathway activation, qPCR analysis demonstrated that DXM upregulated the transcription of canonical interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs), including IFNB, IFIT1, IFIT2, ISG15, and CXCL9, in MM cell lines ( ; ). IF staining confirmed that DXM stimulation promoted pronounced nuclear accumulation of IRF3, which was predominantly cytoplasmic in untreated cells ( ; ).
 Given the structural similarities among steroid hormones, we next examined whether other glucocorticoids could similarly activate cGAS-STING signaling. DXM, hydrocortisone, prednisone, and betamethasone all stimulated ISG transcription, whereas the mineralocorticoid receptor agonist deoxycorticosterone had no significant effect ( ; ). To determine whether DXM-mediated activation depends on GR, we pretreated MM cells with the GR antagonist mifepristone (MIFE). MIFE pretreatment markedly attenuated DXM-induced phosphorylation of the STING-TBK1-IRF3 axis and suppressed ISG upregulation, as confirmed by Western blot and qPCR ( ; ). Transcriptomic analysis of DXM-treated ARP1 cells revealed significant enrichment of pathways related to cytokine production, infectious disease response, and antiviral immunity in KEGG and GO analyses ( ). To further strengthen our findings, we performed Western blot (n = 3, patients #7–#9) and qPCR (n = 6, patients #1–#6) on CD138+ cells isolated from 9 MM patients (patient information provided in ). DXM treatment induced phosphorylation of STING, TBK1, and IRF3 in primary CD138+ cells, consistent with the results observed in MM cell lines ( ; ).

## DXM enhances anti-tumor immunity by potentiating NK cell function and driving M1 macrophage polarization in MM
To assess the impact of DXM on the MM TME, we performed scRNA-seq on bone marrow cells from the 5TMM3VT mouse model. DXM treatment substantially altered the composition of immune cells ( ). Although total infiltration of B, NK, and T cells was reduced ( ), the proportions of CD107hi NK cells, M1-like macrophages, and CD8+ T cells within their respective compartments increased, while the proportion of M2-like macrophages decreased ( ; ). KEGG and GO analyses of the total sample revealed enrichment in cytokine production, immune cell activation, and “Graft-versus-Host Disease” in DXM-treated mice ( ). Analysis of plasma cells from the same model showed significant enrichment of the cytosolic DNA-sensing pathway and tumor-progression-related pathways ( ). These findings suggest that DXM triggers a broad anti-MM immune response and activates cGAS-STING signaling within MM cells.
 IHC of mouse bone tissues corroborated the scRNA-seq findings, demonstrating increased infiltration of cytotoxic NK cells (NKp46+) and M1-like macrophages (CD80+), alongside decreased infiltration of plasma cells (CD138+) and M2-like macrophages (CD163+), with no significant change in CD8a+ T cells ( ). As DXM induced more pronounced shifts (> 5% change) in macrophage and NK cell subsets than in T cells, subsequent analyses focused on these populations. KEGG analysis of sorted NK cells from treated mice indicated activation of NK-cell-mediated cytotoxicity, MAPK signaling, and mTOR signaling ( ). Consistent with the above results, in a co-culture system, DXM treatment led to greater upregulation of the cytotoxic effector molecules Perforin 1, Granzyme B, and Granzyme A in NK92MI cells co-cultured with MM cells than in NK92MI cells alone ( ; ), indicating that DXM enhances NK cell activity in an MM-cell-dependent manner. DXM also upregulated the expression of MHC-I-related molecules (H2-K1, ICAM2, H2-K23, H2-D1, CD48) on MM cells, which are known to modulate NK cell recognition ( ). The increase in surface CD48 on MM cells was confirmed by qPCR and flow cytometry ( ). Functionally, while direct DXM treatment of NK cells slightly impaired their cytotoxicity, they exhibited significantly enhanced killing capacity when co-cultured with DXM-pretreated MM cells ( ; ). This enhancement was dose-dependent and observed across all E:T ratios ( ; ).
Furthermore, the co-culture system showed a substantial increase in IFN-γ and TNF-α secretion by NK cells ( ; ). To assess potential clinical synergy, we examined the effect of DXM on ADCC mediated by the anti-CD38 antibody daratumumab. DXM-pretreated MM cells were significantly more susceptible to daratumumab-mediated lysis by NK92MI cells, especially at E:T ratios (5:1 and 10:1), compared with PBS-treated controls ( ; ). We collected supernatant media from DXM-treated MM cells, as well as DXM-treated MM cells that were washed with PBS, and then co-cultured each preparation with NK cells. These transfer experiments recapitulated the enhanced NK cell cytotoxicity observed in the co-culture system containing DXM-treated MM cells, indicating that soluble factors released from DXM-treated MM cells are sufficient to mediate these anti-tumor effects ( ).
Similarly, KEGG analysis of macrophages from DXM-treated mice showed enrichment in antigen processing and presentation pathways ( ), suggesting an M2-to-M1 phenotypic shift that was validated in vitro. While DXM alone induced a modest polarization toward an M2-like state (upregulated IL-10, ARG1; downregulated CD40, NOS2), this effect was reversed in the presence of MM cells. In co-culture, DXM treatment promoted a clear M1-like polarization, characterized by downregulation of IL-10 and ARG1 and upregulation of CD40 and NOS2 ( ; ). Flow cytometry confirmed downregulation of the M2 marker CD206 and upregulation of the M1 markers CD80 and CD86 on macrophages co-cultured with DXM-treated MM cells ( , ). This polarizing effect was also recapitulated when macrophages were cultured with supernatant from DXM-treated MM cells ( ).

## DXM exerts anti-MM immunity by activating cell-intrinsic cGAS-STING signaling
To evaluate the contribution of antitumor immunity to the anti-MM activity of DXM in vivo, we used the syngeneic 5TMM3VT murine model in both immunocompetent (C57BL/KaLwRij) and immunodeficient (NOD/SCID) hosts ( ). DXM treatment (0.5 mg/kg, three times per week) suppressed 5TMM3VT tumor progression more effectively in immunocompetent mice than in immunodeficient mice ( ). DXM also significantly attenuated MM-associated osteolytic lesions in immunocompetent mice (p < 0.05) ( ).
 To directly examine whether cGAS-STING signaling underlies this immunomodulatory effect, we used an adoptive B-cell transfer model with genetically modified donor cells. MYCflox/flox·Cre+ mice were crossed with STING−/− mice to generate STING−/−·MYCflox/flox·Cre+ and MYCflox/flox·Cre+ mice ( ). Spleen cells were isolated using B220 magnetic beads and injected intravenously into immunocompetent recipients. DXM treatment significantly prolonged survival and reduced bone lesions in MYCflox/flox·Cre+ mice. In contrast, both the survival benefit and bone protection were abrogated in STING−/−·MYCflox/flox·Cre+ mice, whose survival and BMD under DXM treatment remained comparable to untreated controls ( ). These findings demonstrate that the anti-MM immune response elicited by DXM critically depends on activation of the cGAS-STING pathway within MM cells.

## DXM alleviates GR-mediated suppression of cGAS-STING signaling in MM cells by dissociating the GR-TBK1-IRF3 complex
To define the role of GR in cGAS-STING activation, we knocked down GR (NR3C1) expression in MM cells using siRNA. GR knockdown abolished DXM-induced phosphorylation of STING, TBK1, and IRF3 ( ; ), yet it alone significantly enhanced the transcription of ISGs ( ; ). These results suggest that GR functions as a constitutive repressor of the cGAS-STING pathway. Basal pathway activation is driven by endogenous cytoplasmic DNA, which can be amplified by transfecting herring-testis DNA (HT-DNA). We next tested whether GR modulates responses to cytosolic DNA. In GR-knockdown cells, HT-DNA stimulation triggered stronger phosphorylation of STING, TBK1, and IRF3, and higher ISG transcription (ISG15, CXCL9), compared with control cells ( ; ), confirming that GR inhibits cGAS-STING signaling in MM cells.
 GR regulates cellular processes through both transcriptional activity and non-genomic signaling via kinases such as p38 and GSK3β. While DXM-induced GR transcriptional activity typically peaks around 4 hours, we found that DXM rapidly upregulated ISG15 mRNA within 2 hours, whereas canonical GR target genes (FKBP5, KLF9, SGK1) remained unchanged at this early time point ( ; ). Similarly, phosphorylation of STING, TBK1, and IRF3 was detectable as early as 0.5 hours after DXM treatment, while phosphorylation of p38 and GSK3β was unaffected ( ; ). These results indicate that DXM activates the cGAS-STING pathway independently of GR-mediated transcription and without engaging these key non-genomic kinases.
Time-course analysis revealed that DXM treatment rapidly induced transcription of ISGs (interferon-stimulated genes) within 2 hours, followed by a sustained increase in STAT1 phosphorylation detectable at 4 hours ( ). As activated STAT1 is a known transcriptional activator of these same genes, including IFNB, this establishes an autocrine feedback loop that mechanistically bridges the initial transient signal to the prolonged downstream effects. 
Since Western blot analysis did not show DXM-induced upregulation of cGAS-STING pathway components, we examined whether DXM increases cytoplasmic DNA levels. PicoGreen staining revealed no significant change in cytoplasmic DNA levels upon DXM treatment ( ). We then assessed STING activation by monitoring its oligomerization and puncta formation. In unstimulated MM cells, STING already exhibited punctate structures, indicative of basal activation, and DXM did not further enhance this puncta formation ( ; ). Together, these findings suggest that DXM activates the pathway neither by elevating cytoplasmic DNA nor by promoting additional STING oligomerization, but likely acts downstream or in parallel.
We next examined GR localization and protein interactions. DXM promoted GR nuclear translocation within 0.5 hours ( ; ). Notably, Co-IP assays showed that DXM treatment reduced the physical association between GR and the key signaling components TBK1 and IRF3 ( ; ). Thus, GR constitutively suppresses cGAS-STING signaling, likely through direct interaction with TBK1 and IRF3. DXM rapidly disrupts this inhibitory complex and activates the pathway, not via classical GR-dependent transcription or kinase signaling, but through a mechanism that involves releasing TBK1 and IRF3 from GR-mediated restraint ( ).

## GR-mediated suppression of cGAS-STING signaling is executed by PPP1CB and reversed by DXM
To identify proteins involved in GR-mediated regulation of the cGAS-STING pathway, we performed Co-IP coupled with MS on endogenous IRF3 complexes in ARP1 cells ( ). High-throughput MS analysis revealed distinct changes in IRF3-interacting proteins before and after DXM treatment. Under basal conditions, IRF3 interacted with multiple regulatory proteins, including kinases, phosphatases, and E3 ubiquitin ligases. Notably, the phosphatase PPP1CB, known to bind and inactivate TBK1 and IRF3, was highly enriched in the TBK1-IRF3 complex prior to DXM stimulation. Similarly, the kinase RIOK1, an established inhibitor of TBK1-IRF3 signaling, was found to bind and inhibit TBK1 in unstimulated cells. 
 Functional assessment using specific inhibitors showed that salubrinal (a PP1/PPP1CB inhibitor), but not the RIOK1 inhibitor toyocamycin, attenuated DXM-induced cGAS-STING activation and ISG transcription ( ; ). To more specifically address the role of PPP1CB, we performed knockdown experiments using siRNA targeting PPP1CB. As shown in , and ), PPP1CB knockdown attenuated DXM-induced cGAS-STING activation and ISG15 transcription, as well as the downstream effects on NK cells and macrophages. These data indicate that the observed effects are primarily mediated by PPP1CB. Moreover, salubrinal activated cGAS-STING signaling in a dose-dependent manner, whereas toyocamycin did not ( ; ). Salubrinal treatment upregulated ISGs and promoted IRF3 nuclear translocation ( ; ). We further validated the interaction among PPP1CB, IRF3, TBK1, and GR in MM cells. DXM treatment disrupted the association of PPP1CB with TBK1 and IRF3 and stimulated IRF3 nuclear accumulation ( ; ). GR enhanced the IRF3-PPP1CB association, which was reversed by DXM ( ; ).
To map the interacting domains, we generated a series of GR truncation constructs. GR consists of an N-terminal ligand-binding domain (LBD), a central DNA-binding domain (DBD), and a C-terminal region. We found that IRF3 binds the C-terminal region of GR, while PPP1CB interacts with the DBD ( ). AlphaFold-based structural docking simulations identified key residues in GR involved in these interactions: residues E116, E118, E123, and E124 in the C-terminal region for IRF3 binding, and residues K471, R447, K446, K442, and R491 in the DBD for PPP1CB binding. Mutating these residues (E116A/E118A/E123A/E124A in the C-terminus; K471A/R447A/K446A/K442A/R491A in the DBD) significantly disrupted GR’s interaction with IRF3 and PPP1CB, respectively ( ). DXM binds to the LBD of GR, and crystallographic analysis identifies key interaction residues R611, Q570, N564, and T739 ( ). Accordingly, we generated a GR mutant with impaired DXM binding (N564A/T739A/Q570A/R611A; NR3C1-Mut-FLAG) and an LBD-truncated variant (NR3C1-C+D FLAG). In cells expressing these constructs, DXM no longer reduced the IRF3-PPP1CB interaction ( ). We expressed wild-type GR (NR3C1), a GR mutant disrupting DXM interaction (GR-LBD-mutant, NR3C1-Mut), a GR mutant disrupting IRF3 interaction (GR-C-mutant, NR3C1-C-Mut), and a GR mutant disrupting PPP1CB interaction (GR-DBD-mutant, NR3C1-DBD-Mut) in the GR-knockdown MM cells. MM cells expressing the GR-mutants disrupted DXM-induced STING-TBK1-IRF3 activation. GR-C-mutant (NR3C1-C-Mut) and the GR-DBD-mutant (NR3C1-DBD-Mut) enhanced TBK1 and IRF3 phosphorylation even in the absence of DXM, while the GR-LBD-mutant (NR3C1-Mut) showed no responses to DXM treatment ( ; ). qPCR analysis revealed that MM cells expressing the GR-mutants disrupted DXM-induced ISG15 transcription. GR-C-mutant (NR3C1-C-Mut) and the GR-DBD-mutant (NR3C1-DBD-Mut) increased basal ISG15 expression in the absence of DXM, whereas the GR-LBD-mutant (NR3C1-Mut) suppressed DXM-induced ISG upregulation ( ; ). In co-culture assays, MM cells expressing the GR-mutants disrupted DXM-induced anti-tumor effects of macrophages and NK cells in MM cells co-cultured system. MM cells expressing the GR-C-mutant (NR3C1-C-Mut) and the GR-DBD-mutant (NR3C1-DBD-Mut) enhanced NK cell cytotoxicity and M1-like macrophage polarization compared with wild-type controls, while the GR-LBD-mutant (NR3C1-Mut) attenuated these DXM-induced effects ( ).

## Pharmacological PP1 inhibition enhances anti-myeloma immunity by boosting NK cell cytotoxicity and M1 macrophage polarization
To determine the functional impact of PPP1CB-mediated suppression of the TBK1-IRF3 axis on anti-MM immunity, we evaluated the PP1 inhibitor salubrinal in NK-MM co-culture systems. Addition of salubrinal to co-cultures of NK92MI and MM cells significantly enhanced the transcription of cytotoxic effector genes (Perforin 1, Granzyme B, Granzyme A) in NK cells ( ). Salubrinal also potentiated the ADCC effect mediated by daratumumab across all E:T ratios and increased IFN-γ and TNF-α secretion in co-culture supernatants compared with the DMSO control ( ). Similarly, salubrinal treatment promoted macrophage polarization toward an M1-like phenotype. Macrophages co-cultured with MM cells and treated with salubrinal showed reduced surface expression of the M2 marker CD206, downregulated mRNA levels of IL-10 and TGF-β, and elevated surface expression of the M1 markers CD80 and CD86, along with increased transcription of CD40 and NOS2 ( ).
 Pharmacological activation of the STING pathway represents a promising strategy to overcome immunosuppression in the TME. We therefore evaluated whether PP1 inhibition alone or in combination with the proteasome inhibitor bortezomib could enhance anti-tumor efficacy in vivo. In the immunocompetent 5TMM3VT murine model, either salubrinal or bortezomib monotherapy significantly prolonged survival to a comparable extent. Strikingly, the combination of both agents yielded the most pronounced survival benefit ( ). IHC analysis of bone tissues from treated mice confirmed these immunomodulatory effects. Salubrinal treatment increased the infiltration of cytotoxic NK cells (NKp46+) and M1-like macrophages (CD80+), while reducing the presence of plasma cells (CD138+) and M2-like macrophages (CD163+) ( ).

## Discussion
In this study, we identify a pivotal role for DXM in modulating anti-myeloma immunity within the bone marrow microenvironment. Under basal conditions, GR acts as a scaffold that assembles an inhibitory complex with PPP1CB, TBK1, and IRF3, keeping TBK1-IRF3 dephosphorylated and the pathway silenced. Upon DXM binding, GR undergoes a conformational change, leading to complex dissociation and release of the brake. DXM binding to GR triggers the dissociation of both GR and the phosphatase PPP1CB from this complex, thereby relieving the suppression and activating IRF3-driven interferon signaling and downstream gene expression ( ). Importantly, pharmacological inhibition of PP1/PPP1CB with salubrinal recapitulates this immunostimulatory effect and synergizes with bortezomib to enhance anti-tumor efficacy in vivo, highlighting a novel therapeutic axis for MM.
 Cytosolic DNA sensing via the cGAS-STING pathway constitutes a fundamental innate immune mechanism that detects cellular damage and can elicit potent anti-tumor immunity. Chromosomal instability and aberrant DNA are hallmarks of MM, especially in relapsed/refractory disease ; thus, understanding how MM cells evade this surveillance is critical. Tumors employ diverse strategies to subvert cGAS-STING signaling, including degrading cytosolic DNA or cGAMP via nucleases/hydrolases and inhibiting the expression, stability, or activity of core components (eg, STING, TBK1, IRF3) through epigenetic silencing, post-translational modifications, or disruption of essential protein complexes. Our work establishes that GR contributes to this immune evasion by scaffolding an inhibitory complex that recruits PPP1CB to the TBK1-IRF3 axis. Although GR has been linked to F-actin-mediated regulation of YAP/TAZ, which can indirectly suppress cGAS-STING signaling, our time-course experiments revealed that DXM activates the cGAS-STING pathway independently of GR’s transcriptional activity or the phosphorylation of proteins such as p38 and GSK3β. Given that PP1 is a known phosphatase for IRF3, our findings position the GR-PPP1CB axis as a direct, post-translational checkpoint. Several limitations warrant consideration. First, although we show that DXM disrupts the GR-PPP1CB-TBK1-IRF3 complex and restores STING signaling, the requirement for cGAS or other DNA sensors remains to be formally tested. Second, validation in a larger cohort of primary MM samples will be necessary to generalize our findings. Importantly, although salubrinal inhibits the broader PP1 family, PPP1CB knockdown phenocopied its effects, and mutation of the GR-PPP1CB interaction interface (NR3C1-DBD-Mut) abolished DXM-induced cGAS-STING activation. These data establish PPP1CB as a key effector downstream of GR. Nevertheless, potential contributions from other PP1 catalytic subunits cannot be excluded, and whether this axis operates more broadly across other tumor types remains an important question for future investigation.
Targeting the STING pathway represents an emerging immunotherapeutic strategy, with several agonists currently in phase I/II clinical trials for solid tumors and lymphomas (eg, NCT04144140, NCT03937141). Previous studies have demonstrated that bortezomib, Alt-NHEJ inhibitors, DNA-PKcs inhibitors, and the proteasome inhibitor FHND6091 can activate cGAS-STING signaling to induce an anti-MM immune response. Consistent with these findings, we demonstrate that the PP1 inhibitor salubrinal potently activates cGAS-STING signaling in MM cells and, when combined with bortezomib, markedly enhances anti-tumor efficacy in vivo. These results not only validate PP1 as a druggable target but also provide a compelling rationale for clinical trials evaluating PP1 inhibitors as immunomodulatory adjuncts to existing MM therapies.
Our findings reshape the understanding of DXM’s immunomodulatory role in MM. While its anti-MM activity has been largely ascribed to the direct induction of immune cell apoptosis and the suppression of pro-inflammatory pathways, such as NF-κB, our data reveal a context-dependent immunostimulatory function. ScRNA-seq confirmed that DXM reduced overall immune cell infiltration, consistent with its known cytotoxic effects, and we observed no activation of the p65 (NF-κB) pathway in MM cells, suggesting its potential suppression. Nevertheless, DXM simultaneously and selectively activates a potent anti-tumor immune response, aligning with emerging evidence of pro-inflammatory glucocorticoid actions under specific conditions. Pathway analyses revealed enriched inflammatory signatures, and functional co-culture assays demonstrated that DXM, acting via MM cells, significantly enhances NK-cell cytotoxicity and drives macrophage polarization toward an M1-like phenotype. The absence of a robust T-cell response in our model underscores the complexity of the MM immune microenvironment and merits further exploration.
In summary, this study elucidates a novel mechanism through which DXM licenses anti-tumor immunity in MM by dismantling a GR-PPP1CB-mediated brake on the cGAS-STING pathway. Although DXM is routinely used in combination regimens for its direct cytotoxic effects, a deeper appreciation of its immune-modulatory properties could refine its application both as a monotherapy and in combinatorial strategies. The immunogenic activity of DXM may partly explain the improved clinical outcomes observed when it is combined with immunomodulatory drugs or monoclonal antibodies. Furthermore, our work nominates PP1 as a promising therapeutic target for activating anti-MM immunity and provides preclinical justification for evaluating PP1 inhibitors in clinical trials to amplify immune responses against myeloma.
