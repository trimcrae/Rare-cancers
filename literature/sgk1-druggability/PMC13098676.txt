## Abstract
Acute pharmacological administration of the endocrine hormone fibroblast growth factor 21 (FGF21) enhances insulin sensitivity. This acute insulin-sensitizing effect of FGF21 is mediated through direct signaling to brown adipose tissues. Since skeletal muscle is an important site of insulin-stimulated glucose intake and shares a common progenitor cell with brown adipocytes, we examined whether the beneficial effects of FGF21 administration could be enhanced by making skeletal muscle a FGF21-responsive target tissue. This was accomplished by ectopically expressing the FGF21 co-receptor, β-klotho, in skeletal muscle. Here, we demonstrate that under normal conditions, FGF21 does not enhance insulin-stimulated glucose uptake in skeletal muscle. In addition, generation of FGF21 responsiveness and direct signaling to skeletal muscle also has no effect on FGF21-mediated increases in whole-body or skeletal muscle insulin sensitivity. Instead, FGF21 uniquely signals to brown adipocytes to enhance insulin-stimulated glucose uptake. Therefore, to identify how FGF21 signals to brown adipocytes to enhance insulin sensitivity, we performed comprehensive phospho-proteomics in brown adipocytes in response to FGF21 and/or insulin. Our results indicate that FGF21 administration increases the phosphorylation of several proteins involved in the trafficking of GLUT4 in primary brown adipocytes. These results provide new insights into how FGF21 enhances insulin sensitivity.
Keywords: FGF21, beta klotho, brown adipose, skeletal muscle, insulin, sensitivity

Fibroblast growth factor 21 (FGF21) is a hepatokine that regulates glucose and lipid metabolism, and whole-body energy homeostasis ( , ). Pharmacological administration of FGF21 enhances insulin sensitivity and promotes weight loss in obese and diabetic animal models ( ). FGF21 administration has both acute and chronic effects on insulin sensitivity. The acute insulin-sensitizing effects of FGF21 start to work within minutes and reach a maximal effect by 3 hours ( , ). Chronic administration of FGF21 also markedly increases insulin sensitivity, but this results from secondary effects following decreases in body weight and reductions in peripheral lipid levels ( , ). The acute insulin-sensitizing effects of FGF21 are mediated through direct signaling to UCP1+ adipocytes ( ), with brown adipose tissues (BAT) acting as a major site of FGF21-enhanced insulin-stimulated glucose uptake ( , ). FGF21 signals to target tissues, including BAT, that express a receptor complex consisting of a traditional FGF receptor (FGFR), primarily FGFR1c, and the obligate FGF21 co-receptor β-klotho (KLB) ( ). KLB expression is required for FGF21's signaling to tissues and confers its tissue selectivity ( , ).
Multiple FGF21 analogues and mimetics have been developed which reverse liver pathology, including reductions in hepatic steatosis and fibrosis, in models of metabolic-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) ( , ). Importantly, several FGF21 analogues are currently in clinical trials to treat MASH ( ). Interestingly, some, but not all, FGF21 analogues improve markers of insulin sensitivity in individuals with type 2 diabetes ( , ). For example, in phase 2a and 2b clinical trials, efruxifermin, a bivalent FGF21-Fc fusion protein, enhanced systemic insulin sensitivity ( ). A separate FGF21 analogue significantly reduced fasting plasma insulin levels in obese, type 2 diabetic subjects, suggesting an improvement in insulin sensitivity ( ). In contrast, a different FGF21 analogue had little to no effect on insulin sensitivity ( ). As different FGF21 analogues have different structures and receptor interactions, it is unclear what accounts for the differences observed in these different studies.
Despite these advances, the mechanism by which FGF21 improves glucose homeostasis remains incompletely understood. We previously determined that adipose tissue is a key mediator of the acute, insulin-sensitizing effects of FGF21, with signaling to UCP1+ adipocytes, in particular, accounting for the acute improvements in insulin sensitivity observed following single-dose FGF21 administration ( ). While skeletal muscle is an important site of insulin action and major glucose sink ( ), KLB is not expressed in skeletal muscle ( , , ), and thus FGF21 cannot directly signal to skeletal muscle. Although skeletal muscle lacks KLB, it does express several FGFRs, including FGFR1c ( ). Therefore, since skeletal muscle and BAT share a common progenitor cell, we examined whether making skeletal muscle FGF21-responsive could enhance insulin sensitivity. To address this possibility, we generated mice which express KLB in skeletal muscle. Here we show that making skeletal muscle responsive to FGF21 provides no additional benefit in FGF21-mediated insulin sensitivity in vivo. Instead, FGF21 drives glucose disposal primarily to BAT. Importantly, using comprehensive phosphoproteomic analyses, we reveal that FGF21 increases insulin sensitivity by providing an AKT-independent alternate route to the priming and activation of common insulin targets, and GLUT4 translocation, through MAPK signaling. These data provide new insights into the molecular pathways and targets of FGF21 to enhance insulin sensitivity.

## Materials and methods

## Animals
Inducible KLB transgenic mice (KLB-MSP) ( ), KLB-Cre ( ), Acta1-Cre (Jackson Labs, stock #006149) ( ), and HiBit GLUT4 (HBG4) mice ( ) have been previously reported. To generate mice ectopically expressing KLB in skeletal muscle (KLB-SKM-TG mice), KLB-MSP female mice were crossed with Acta1-Cre male mice. All mice were maintained on a C57BL/6J genetic background. Mice were fed either chow (Teklad 2920X) or a high-fat diet (HFD) consisting of 60% kcal from fat (Research Diets D12492) for 12 weeks to induce mice to obesity. Body composition was measured using a rodent-sized NMR machine (Bruker Minispec LF50). Male mice were used for all experiments. The ages of all mice used in the studies are as indicated in figure legends. All experiments were approved by University of Iowa IACUC.

## Glucose uptake assays
For in vivo glucose uptake, 4- to 6-month-old KLB-SKM-TG were used, and wild-type littermates were used as control mice. Mice were singly housed a week prior to the in vivo radioactive glucose uptake, which was conducted as previously ( ). On the day of the uptake assay, mice were fasted for 5 hours and were transferred to a radioactive procedure room to acclimate 1 hour prior to initiating the assay. FGF21 (Novo Nordisk) or vehicle was added to insulin (Sigma)/deoxy-D-glucose, 2-[1,2-3H(N)] (PerkinElmer) mixture for a final dose of 1 mg/kg body weight (BW) of FGF21, 0.25 U/kg BW of insulin, and 1.25 uCi/g BW. After injection, mice were placed back in cages. Time 0, 15, 30, and 60 minutes blood was collected via tail bleed. Mice were sacrificed by decapitation 60 minutes post-injection. Tissues were collected and immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen.
Tissues were then processed to determine levels of radioactive phosphorylated 2-deoxyglucose (2DG). Tissues were weighed and placed in 400 mL of 1M NaOH and incubated at 85 °C for 10 minutes to homogenize. Samples were then neutralized with 400 µL of 1M HCl. Then 200 µL of homogenized samples were added to duplicate solutions of 1 mL ice-cold 7% perchloric acid or duplicate solutions of 1 mL Somogyi buffer. Somogyi buffer precipitates sugar phosphates, separating the intracellular 2DG (metabolized to 2DG-6-P) from neutral 2DG (unmodified) ( ). Somogyi buffer was prepared immediately before use by combining 500 µL 0.3N ZnSO4 with 500 µL 0.3N Ba(OH)2. Samples were spun at 13 000g for 5 minutes at 4 °C. Then 800 µL of supernatant was added to 10 mL of Bio-Safe II Scintillation fluid (RPI) and radioactivity measured on a scintillation counter in counts per minute (c.p.m.). Phospho-2DG concentration in samples was measured by converting c.p.m. measurements to disintegrations per minute (d.p.m.) based on the scintillation counter efficiency. Average d.p.m. measured from Somogyi buffer (phosphorylated 2DG) was subtracted from average d.p.m. from perchloric acid solutions (total 2DG) for each tissue and normalized to both tissue weight and plasma glucose levels.

## Insulin tolerance tests and glucose tolerance tests
For insulin tolerance tests (ITTs), mice were individually caged for at least 1 week prior to experiment. On the day of the study, mice were fasted for 5 hours and time 0 blood was collected via tail bleed. All mice were then intraperitoneally injected with insulin (0.75 U/kg BW for diet-induced obese [DIO] mice and 0.25 U/kg BW for lean mice). Tail blood was collected at 15, 30, 60, 90, and 120 minutes post-injection. Then, 1 week later, the same experimental paradigm was followed using the same cohorts of mice except all mice were administered insulin plus FGF21 (1 mg/kg) as previously ( ). For glucose tolerance tests (GTTs), mice were fasted for 5 hours and then injected intraperitoneally with glucose (1.3 g/kg BW for DIO mice and 2 g/kg BW for lean mice). Tail blood was collected at 0, 15, 30, 60, 90, and 120 minutes. Mice were maintained on the same diets throughout experiments. The same groups of mice were used to assess basal vs FGF21-stimulated insulin sensitivity and glucose tolerance.

## Plasma analysis
Blood was collected into 300K2E microvettes (Sarstedt) and spun at 3000 rpm for 30 minutes at 4 °C to separate plasma. Plasma glucose levels were measured using the glucose autokit (Wako Chemicals). Plasma FGF21 (Biovendor) and insulin (Crystal Chem) were measured using commercially available enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISAs). Plasma triglycerides and cholesterol were measured using colorimetric assays (InfinityTM, Thermo Scientific). Non-esterified fatty acids (NEFAs) were also measured using a colorimetric assay (Wako Chemicals). All measurements were performed according to the manufacturer's instructions.

## Gene expression analysis
Gene expression analysis was performed as described ( ). RNA was isolated from the indicated tissues following Trizol (Invitrogen) protocol. A total of 2 μg RNA from each sample was used to generate cDNA (High-Capacity cDNA Reverse Transcription Kit; Life Technologies), and quantitative polymerase chain reaction (QPCR) was conducted using SYBR green (Invitrogen). QPCR primer sequences are as follows: Klb from Benoit et al ( ) was used only in : 5′-GCGACAACAGCAAGACTGTG-3′, 5′- GTAACTGGCTTAGACAAGCA-3′; Klb was used in all other figures: 5′-GATGAAGAATTTCCTAAACCAGGTT-3′, 5-AACCAAACACGCGGATTTC-3′; Fgfr1c, 5′-GCCAGACAACTTGCCGTATG-3′, 5′-ATTTCCTTGTCGGTGGTATTAACT-3′; U36b4, 5′-CGTCCTCGTTGGAGTGACA-3′, 5′-CGGTGCGTCAGGGATTG-3′.
 

## Primary brown adipocyte isolation, culture, and treatment for phosphoproteomics
The intrascapular stromal vascular fraction was isolated from wild-type 4- to 6-day-old C57BL/6J pups (6-10 pups per isolation). The fat pads were cleaned of white adipose tissue, minced, and incubated in digestion buffer (DMEM high glucose, no pyruvate Sigma D5796, 2% BSA [RPI], Pen-Strep 1× [Gibco], and collagenase type II [Gibco] 1 mg/mL). The tissues were placed in a shaking incubator and digested for 30 minutes at 37 °C and 150 rpm. The cell solution was then pipetted vigorously to break up any remaining fat pads and allowed to settle for 10 minutes on ice. The infranatant was then taken from the cell solution to capture the preadipocytes and strained through a 100-μm cell strainer (Corning) and spun down for 5 minutes at 500 rpm to pellet the cells. The supernatant was then discarded, and the cells were resuspended in 1 mL red blood cell lysis buffer (NH4Cl) for 2 minutes. Then 10 mL pre-adipocyte media (DMEM Sigma D5796, 10% FBS [Gibco], Pen-Strep [Gibco], 1× NEAA [Gibco], 1× Glutamax [Gibco], and 20mM HEPES [Gibco]) was added to stop the lysing and resuspend the cells. Cells were then strained through a 40-μm cell strainer and spun down again for 5 minutes at 500 rpm. The infranatant was discarded and cells were resuspended in 10 mL pre-adipocyte media and plated onto a 10-cm2 plate for expansion. When preadipocytes reached 80% to 90% confluence, they were washed with 10 mL phosphate-buffered saline (PBS [Gibco]) and incubated with 1 mL of TrypLE Express (Gibco) to dissociate from the plate. Cells were resuspended and plated into 6-well Collagen 1 (Corning) coated plates at 2 mL per well. When cells reached confluence, the media was changed to differentiation media (DMEM, 10% FBS, 5 μg/mL insulin [Sigma], 1μM dexamethasone [Sigma], 0.5 mM IBMX [Sigma], 1nM T3 [Sigma]) and refreshed every 2 to 3 days for 5 to 7 days. After differentiation, cells were switched to maintenance medium (DMEM, 10% FBS, 1 μg/mL insulin, 1nM T3) for 2 days. Cells were then subjected to an insulin sensitization protocol previously described ( ) and treated with “vehicle” (PBS), FGF21 (1 μg/mL), insulin (100nM), or “combo” FGF21 1 μg/mL + 100nM insulin for 5 or 15 minutes. After treatment incubation, cells were washed 3× with ice-cold PBS and lysed with a buffer containing 8 M Urea, 0.1% SDS, and Complete Mini Protease Inhibitor (Roche) and PhosStop (Roche). Lysed cells were collected, run through a 28G needle 8 times to shear DNA, and then centrifuged for 10 minutes at 21 000g. The infranatant was collected and 10 μL was aliquoted for bicinchoninic acid assay (BCA) and Western blotting analysis to confirm treatments. Cell lysates with confirmed treatments were chosen over the course of 3 cell treatments and collections. The samples were then sent to Harvard's Thermo Fisher Center for Multiplexed Proteomics for “Mini-Phos” analysis.

## Sample preparation for mass spectrometry
Samples for protein analysis were prepared essentially as previously described ( , ). Proteomes were extracted using a buffer containing 200mM EPPS pH 8.5, 8M urea, 0.1% SDS and protease inhibitors. Following lysis, 200 µg of each proteome was reduced with 5mM TCEP. Cysteine residues were alkylated using 10mM iodoacetimide for 20 minutes at room temperature in the dark. Excess iodoacetimide was quenched with 10mM DTT. A buffer exchange was carried out using a modified SP3 protocol ( ). Briefly, ∼2000 µg of Cytiva SpeedBead Magnetic Carboxylate Modified Particles (65152105050250 and 4515210505250), mixed at a 1:1 ratio, were added to each sample. Then 100% ethanol was added to each sample to achieve a final ethanol concentration of at least 50%. Samples were incubated with gentle shaking for 15 minutes. Samples were washed 3 times with 80% ethanol. Protein was eluted from SP3 beads using 200 mM EPPS pH 8.5 containing Lys-C (Wako, 129-02541). Samples were digested overnight at room temperature with vigorous shaking. The next morning, trypsin (Thermo Fisher Scientific) was added to each sample and further incubated for 6 hours at 37° C. Lys-C and trypsin were used at a 1:100 protease-protein ratio. Following digestion, ∼50 µg of each sample was pooled to generate a pooled sample to be used as a bridge between 2 TMTPro 16-plex experiments. Acetonitrile was added to each sample to achieve a final concentration of ∼33%. Each sample was labeled, in the presence of SP3 beads, with ∼300 µg of TMTPro reagents (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Following confirmation of satisfactory labeling (>97%), excess TMT was quenched by addition of hydroxylamine to a final concentration of 0.3%. The full volume from each sample was pooled and acetonitrile was removed by vacuum centrifugation for 1 hour. The pooled sample was acidified, and peptides were desalted using a Sep-Pak 50 mg tC18 cartridge (Waters). Peptides were eluted in 70% acetonitrile, 1% formic acid and dried by vacuum centrifugation.

## Phosphopeptide enrichment
A phosphopeptide enrichment was performed using a High-Select Fe-NTA Phosphopeptide Enrichment Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Dried phosphopeptides were desalted by Stage-Tip and re-dissolved in 5% formic acid/5% acetonitrile for liquid chromatography with mass spectrometry (LC-MS). The flow-through from the phosphopeptide enrichment was used for total proteome profiling.

## Basic pH reversed-phase separation
TMT-labeled peptides were solubilized in 5% acetonitrile/10mM ammonium bicarbonate, pH 8.0 and ∼300 µg of the TMT-labeled peptides were separated by an Agilent 300 Extend C18 column (3.5 μm particles, 4.6 mm ID, and 250 mm in length). An Agilent 1260 binary pump coupled with a photodiode array (PDA) detector (Thermo Scientific) was used to separate the peptides. A 45-minute linear gradient from 10% to 40% acetonitrile in 10mM ammonium bicarbonate pH 8.0 (flow rate of 0.6 mL/min) separated the peptide mixtures into a total of 96 fractions (36 seconds). A total of 96 fractions were consolidated into 24 samples in a checkerboard fashion and vacuum dried to completion. Each sample was desalted via Stage Tips and re-dissolved in 5% formic acid/5% acetonitrile for LC-MS3 analysis.

## Liquid chromatography separation and triple-stage mass spectrometry
For liquid chromatography separation and triple-stage mass spectrometry (LC-MS3), proteome data were collected on an Orbitrap Eclipse mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) coupled to a Proxeon EASY-nLC 1000 LC pump (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Fractionated peptides were separated using a 120 minutes gradient at 550 nL/min on a 35-cm column (i.d. 100 μm, Accucore, 2.6 μm, 150 Å) packed in-house. MS1 data were collected in the Orbitrap (60 000 resolution; maximum injection time 50 ms; automatic gain control [AGC] 4 × 105). Charge states between 2 and 5 were required for MS2 analysis in the ion trap, and a 120 second dynamic exclusion window was used. Top 10 MS2 scans were performed in the ion trap with CID fragmentation (isolation window 0.5 Da; Turbo; normalized collision energy [NCE] 35%; maximum injection time 35 ms; AGC 1 × 104). Real-time search was used to trigger MS3 scans for quantification ( ). MS3 scans were collected in the Orbitrap using a resolution of 50 000, NCE of 55%, maximum injection time of 250 ms, and AGC of 1.25 × 105. The closeout was set at 2 peptides per protein per fraction ( ).

## Liquid chromatography separation and tandem mass spectrometry
For liquid chromatography separation and tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS2), phosphorylation data were collected on an Orbitrap Eclipse mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) coupled to a Proxeon EASY-nLC 1000 LC pump (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Fractionated peptides were separated using a 120-minute gradient at 525 nL/min on a 35 cm column (i.d. 100 μm, Accucore, 2.6 μm, 150 Å) packed in-house. A FAIMS device enabled during data acquisition with compensation voltages set as −40, −60, and −80 V for the first shot and −45 and −65 V for the second shot. MS1 data were collected in the Orbitrap (120 000 resolution; maximum injection time set to auto; AGC 4 × 105) ( ). Charge states between 2 and 5 were required for MS2 analysis, and a 120 second dynamic exclusion window was used. Cycle time was set at 1 second. MS2 scans were performed in the Orbitrap with HCD fragmentation (isolation window 0.5 Da; 50 000 resolution; NCE 36%; maximum injection time 300 ms; AGC 2 × 105).

## Data analysis
Raw files were converted to mzXML, and monoisotopic peaks were re-assigned using Monocle ( ). Searches were performed using the Comet search algorithm against a mouse database downloaded from Uniprot in May 2021. Search parameters utilized were as follows: 50 ppm precursor ion tolerance, 1.0005 fragment ion tolerance, and 0.4 fragment bin offset for MS2 scans collected in the ion trap, and 0.02 fragment ion tolerance; 0.00 fragment bin offset for MS2 scans collected in the Orbitrap. TMTpro on lysine residues and peptide N-termini (+304.2071 Da) and carbamidomethylation of cysteine residues (+57.0215 Da) were set as static modifications, while oxidation of methionine residues (+15.9949 Da) was set as a variable modification. For phosphorylated peptide analysis, +79.9663 Da was set as a variable modification on serine, threonine, and tyrosine residues.
Each run was filtered separately to 1% False Discovery Rate (FDR) on the peptide-spectrum match (PSM) level. Then proteins were filtered to the target 1% FDR level across the entire combined data set. Phosphorylation site localization was determined using the AScorePro algorithm ( ). For reporter ion quantification, a 0.003 Da window around the theoretical m/z of each reporter ion was scanned, and the most intense m/z was used. Reporter ion intensities were adjusted to correct for isotopic impurities of the different TMTpro reagents according to manufacturer specifications. Peptides were filtered to include only those with a summed signal-to-noise (S/N) ≥ 170 across all TMT channels. An extra filter of an isolation specificity (“isolation purity”) of at least 0.5 in the MS1 isolation window was applied for the phosphorylated peptide analysis. For each protein or phosphorylation site, the filtered peptide TMTpro SN values were summed to generate protein or phosphorylation site quantification values. The signal-to-noise (S/N) measurements of peptides assigned to each protein were summed (for a given protein). These values were normalized so that the sum of the signal for all proteins in each channel was equivalent thereby accounting for equal protein loading. The resulting normalization factors were used to normalize the phosphorylation sites, again account for equal protein loading.

## Bioinformatic analysis
Normalized values were utilized for comparing log fold changes between groups in PhosR ( ). Gene ontology enrichment was performed using the clusterProfiler R package ( ), analyzing proteins that met a cutoff of logFC > .5, and an adj.P value < .05. MSidDB ( ) was utilized to enrich for proteins matching signatures for insulin and glucose metabolism—to reduce clutter, only proteins that met a logFC > 1 and an adj.P value < .05 were labeled on the volcano plots. PhosphoSitePlus40 was used to query predicted kinases matching the phosphorylation sites from our data.

## Immunoblotting
For Western blotting, cells or tissues were homogenized with RIPA buffer (Sigma) supplemented with Complete Mini Protease Inhibitor and PhosStop phosphatase inhibitors. Lysates were centrifuged for 10 minutes at 21 000g at 4 °C. The infranatant was aliquoted for protein quantification by BCA (Thermo Fischer) snap-frozen, stored at −80 °C until use. Then 15 ug of protein was determined by BCA and the appropriate volume was added to 4× Laemmli buffer supplemented with 2-mercaptoethanol. Samples were then boiled at 100 °C for 10 minutes. Proteins were resolved by SDS-Page and transferred to a PVDF membrane (Bio-Rad) with the Trans-Blot Turbo Transfer System (Bio-Rad). Proteins on the membrane were labeled with No-Stain Protein Labeling Reagent (Invitrogen) for protein normalization and visualization. Membranes were then blocked with EveryBlot Blocking buffer (Bio-Rad) for 5 minutes. Primary antibodies were diluted 1:2000 in blocking buffer and membranes were incubated on a rocker overnight at 4 °C, followed by 1 hour of secondary antibody incubation at room temperature. Secondary antibodies conjugated with HRP were utilized at a 1:10 000 dilution. Proteins were visualized using Bio-Rad Clarity ECL or Thermo Scientific Super Signal on the iBright 1500. iBright analysis software was used to quantify bands and phosphorylated protein signal was normalized to total protein.

## GLUT4 translocation assays—HiBiT
Preadipocytes were isolated and expanded as previously described from HBG4+/+ pups. When preadipocytes reached near confluence on a 10-cm2 plate, cells were replated onto Phenol-Free Growth Factor-Reduced Matrigel (Corning) coated plates for differentiation in 384-well plates. Cells were differentiated and exposed to sensitization media as previously described. At 16 hours before the assay, the media was reduced to 20 µL and insulin was removed from the media. Prior to compound treatments, an equal volume of the HiBiT Extracellular Detection System (Promega) was added containing the enzyme substrate and LgBiT peptide diluted 1:100 in the supplied buffer. The resulting luminescent signal read and allowed to stabilize on FLIPR for 10 minutes per manufacturer's instructions. Compounds were then injected in 5 µL volumes via the FLIPR pipettor head. For data analysis, data was baseline corrected in Prism utilizing the first 120 seconds post-injection as a baseline for the luminescent values.

## Glucose uptake assay
Glucose Uptake-Glo (Promega) was utilized to determine glucose uptake in primary brown adipocytes according to the manufacturer's directions. In brief, differentiated adipocytes were sensitized to insulin and media was switched 1 day before the assay to no-glucose DMEM (Gibco). On the day of the assay, cells were incubated in no-glucose DMEM containing the following treatments: (vehicle-PBS, FGF21 (1 μg/mL), 100nM insulin, or FGF21 (1 μg/mL) + 100nM insulin for 1 hour. After incubation, cells were washed with PBS, then incubated in 1mM 2DG for 10 minutes. A control group of wells that received the vehicle treatment received no 2DG as a control for assay background. For fold-change analysis, the assay background was subtracted from all groups, and each replicate (4 per group) was compared against the average vehicle value.

## Results

## Generation of FGF21-responsive skeletal muscle
The expression of KLB in skeletal muscle is controversial, with some studies reporting expression in skeletal muscle ( ), while others do not ( , , ). To determine if skeletal muscle is a potential FGF21 target, we examined whether Klb is expressed in skeletal muscle by crossing Klb-Cre mice with Ai14-tdTomato reporter mice, and by QPCR for Klb in different muscles. KLB was undetectable in the quadriceps by fluorescence imaging in KLB-Cre;tdTomato mice ( ), consistent with a previous report ( ). In addition, Klb was not detected in skeletal muscle of wild-type C57BL/6 mice ( ) using the same QPCR primers as a previous study that reported expression ( ). Although we could not detect KLB expression in skeletal muscles, BAT and skeletal muscle share a common Myf5+ progenitor ( ). Because of the shared lineage between the tissues, we sought to determine if the FGF21-mediated enhancement of insulin sensitivity in BAT could be replicated in skeletal muscle artificially expressing KLB. To test if FGF21 confers metabolic benefits to skeletal muscle with KLB expression, we crossed inducible KLB transgenic mice (KLB-MSP) ( ) to skeletal muscle specific Acta1-Cre mice ( ). As expected, KLB skeletal muscle transgenic (KLB SKM-TG; KLB-MSP; Acta1-Cre) mice exhibited KLB expression in skeletal muscle when assessed by fluorescence imaging, QPCR, and Western blot analysis ( ). Importantly, while exogenous FGF21 administration to wild-type (WT) mice had no effect on phosphorylation of ERK1/2, a downstream target of FGF21 signaling, administration of FGF21 to KLB SKM-TG mice increased ERK1/2 phosphorylation in skeletal muscle without interrupting signaling to adipose tissues ( ).

## KLB expression in skeletal muscle does not confer metabolic benefits
To determine whether expressing KLB in skeletal muscle impacts basal metabolism or the physiological regulation of body weight and glucose homeostasis, we assessed body weight and other metabolic parameters in KLB SKM-TG mice. KLB SKM-TG mice showed no difference in body weight compared to WT littermates either on a chow diet or when provided a high-fat diet (HFD) for 12 weeks ( and ). Both lean and diet-induced obese KLB SKM-TG mice also showed no statistically significant differences in insulin tolerance tests (ITTs) between groups ( and ), although diet-induced obese (DIO) KLB SKM-TG mice trended to have a lowered basal starting point. To examine whether lean or DIO KLB SKM-TG exhibited differences in FGF21-mediated insulin sensitivity, we administered FGF21 with insulin to the same cohorts of mice that the ITTs were performed with. Importantly, FGF21 administration significantly increased insulin sensitivity in WT lean and DIO mice, and KLB SKM-TG mice responded similarly to FGF21 administration as WT lean and DIO mice ( and ). To more directly assess if KLB SKM-TG mice had altered skeletal muscle glucose uptake in response to FGF21 signaling, we measured radioactive glucose uptake in tissues after administration of insulin (0.25 units/kg BW) or insulin (0.25 U units/kg BW) + FGF21 (1 mg/kg), as we have done previously ( ). As expected, FGF21 administration to WT and KLB SKM-TG mice increased insulin-stimulated glucose uptake to BAT. However, FGF21 administration did not stimulate glucose uptake in skeletal muscle, and KLB SKM-TG mice also did not exhibit changes in skeletal muscle glucose uptake in response to FGF21 administration ( ). Together, these data indicate that FGF21 administration does not stimulate glucose uptake in skeletal muscle and that conferral of FGF21-responsiveness to skeletal muscle is not sufficient for FGF21-mediated enhancements of insulin sensitivity in this tissue. In addition, these data confirm that BAT is the major site of glucose disposal from FGF21-mediated enhancements in insulin sensitivity.
 

## FGF21 enhances insulin-stimulated GLUT4 translocation in brown adipocytes
Given that our in vivo data demonstrated that FGF21 enhances insulin-stimulated glucose uptake in BAT, and since GLUT4 is the primary insulin-responsive glucose transporter in adipose tissue, we next investigated whether FGF21 affects GLUT4 translocation. We utilized primary brown adipocytes (PBAs) isolated from mice which have a HiBiT-tag inserted into the first extracellular loop of endogenous GLUT4 ( ). HiBiT-tagged GLUT4 (HBG4) enables the quantification of real-time GLUT4 trafficking to the plasma membrane of cells ( ) ( ). When administered to HBG4 primary brown adipocytes, insulin treatment alone significantly increased GLUT4 translocation to the plasma membrane as expected ( ). In contrast, FGF21 treatment alone showed no difference compared to vehicle. Importantly, co-administration of FGF21 with insulin significantly increased GLUT4 translocation over insulin alone ( ). In vitro glucose uptake assays from WT primary brown adipocytes confirmed that FGF21 treatment enhanced insulin-stimulated glucose uptake in primary brown adipocytes beyond insulin alone ( ) as observed previously ( ).
 To identify a potential mechanism for FGF21-mediated enhancement of insulin sensitivity in brown adipocytes, we performed an unbiased profiling of FGF21 signaling targets in brown adipocytes using phosphoproteomics. To accomplish this, we isolated WT primary brown adipocytes and treated them with FGF21 (1 μg/mL), insulin (100nM), or FGF21 + insulin in combination before phosphoproteomic analysis. Cells were treated for 5 or 15 minutes to capture early activation and downstream propagation of signaling, with ∼9000 and ∼11 000 peptides quantified at each time point, respectively ( ). Treatment conditions for phosphoproteomics were first validated by measuring phosphorylation of AKT and ERK/MAPK. FGF21 has a weak or transient effect on AKT phosphorylation but robustly activates ERK/MAPK in primary brown adipocytes ( , quantified in Fig. S1 ( )). As expected, insulin administration increased Akt phosphorylation ( , quantified in Fig. S1 ( )). Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment for samples treated with FGF21 alone revealed changes in multiple categories including chromatin remodeling and mRNA processing ( ). Interestingly, 2 of the top 10 enriched GO categories in the FGF21 alone treated samples were “response to insulin” and “cellular response to insulin,” confirming overlap between FGF21 and insulin-mediated intracellular signaling ( ). At the 15-minute treatment timepoint, GO enrichment for FGF21-treated samples shifted toward transcriptional regulation, characterized by “Chromatin remodeling” and “Nucleocytoplasmic transport,” as well as translational regulation via terms governing mRNA spatial control, such as “RNA transport” and “RNA localization” ( ).

## FGF21 is a potent MAPK activator and weak AKT activator
When assessing the phosphoproteomic data, FGF21 was a relatively weak activator of AKT across all time points compared with insulin alone or combination treatment ( and ). Phosphorylation at AKT1 T308/AKT2 T309, which are critical sites required for full AKT kinase activation ( ), were notably absent in FGF21-treated samples. In contrast, MAPK phosphorylation was elevated in response to FGF21 ( and ), and at the 15-minute timepoint, combination treatment appeared to prolong MAPK activation ( ).
 Because gene ontology enrichment indicated that FGF21 treatment produced a signature overlapping with insulin, we next queried the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB) to identify FGF21-responsive proteins involved in insulin signaling and glucose metabolism ( ). AS160 (gene TBC1D4) emerged as a prominent candidate in both filtered datasets ( and ) due to its established role in regulating GLUT4 translocation. Under basal conditions, AS160 restrains the small GTPase RAB10 by maintaining it in its GDP-bound, inactive state ( ). Upon phosphorylation at key regulatory sites, AS160 releases RAB10, which can then be activated by guanine exchange factors ( , ). One such factor, DENND4C, was also found to be phosphorylated following FGF21 treatment ( and ). DENND4c is a key guanine exchange factor for Rab10, and DENND4c knockdown significantly reduces GLUT4 translocation ( ). As for the kinase that can regulate AS160, previous studies indicate that RSK1 (RPS6KA1) can phosphorylate AS160 at multiple sites, and RSK family members share 73% to 80% sequence homology and are major downstream targets of ERK1/2 ( , ). Consistent with this, phosphorylation of RSK2 T577, which is located within the ERK activation loop, was approximately 2.2-fold higher in FGF21-treated samples compared with vehicle at 5 minutes ( ). Together, these data support a model whereby FGF21 activates ERK1/2 signaling to phosphorylate and activate RSK2, thereby promoting downstream AS160 phosphorylation and priming GLUT4 translocation in adipocytes.
Given the increased phosphorylation of both AS160 and DENND4C, which are proteins critical for GLUT4 trafficking, in FGF21 treated primary brown adipocytes, we expanded the MSigDB query to include proteins functionally linked to GLUT4. This analysis identified the TUG (gene ASPSCR1) ( ), which anchors GLUT4 vesicles in the perinuclear region and is cleaved by USP25m upon insulin stimulation to permit translocation to the plasma membrane ( , ). Thus, FGF21 administration may prime GLUT4 trafficking and enhance insulin-stimulated GLUT4 release when insulin is present.

## FGF21 treatment activates a RSK2 to AS160 signaling pathway
In our phosphoproteomic analysis, RSK2 protein phosphorylation abundance was comparable across treatment groups, with modestly elevated log2 fold changes in the FGF21 and combination groups at the 5-minute timepoint ( ). Western blot using pan-RSK phospho-antibodies confirmed that FGF21 induced greater phosphorylation than insulin at the linker region ( , quantified in Fig. S2 ( )), a site phosphorylated by the C-terminal kinase domain following ERK1/2 activation ( ). At 15 minutes, RSK2 phosphorylation declined substantially in the FGF21 alone and insulin alone treated groups but remained elevated under combination treatment ( ), potentially contributing to the sustained downstream phosphorylation of AS160. Similarly, AS160 phosphorylation was comparable among all groups at 5 minutes ( and , immunoblot quantified in Fig. S2 ( )) but was highest in the combination group at 15 minutes ( ). Querying the PhosphoSitePlus database ( ) identified several kinases with reported phosphorylation activity at AS160 S588/S595 (human/mouse homologs), including AKT, p90RSK (RSK1), Chk1, SGK1, and AMPK (data not shown). Of these, only AKT and RSK2 were detected in our dataset, again suggesting that FGF21 enhances AS160 phosphorylation through ERK1/2-mediated activation of RSK proteins. The activation of this signaling cascade, along with downstream effects on GLUT4-trafficking proteins including TUG and DENND4C, may be responsible for FGF21-mediated enhancements in insulin sensitivity ( ).
 

## Discussion
These studies provide important insights into both the tissue targets and molecular targets of FGF21 which mediate its acute insulin-sensitizing effects. Our studies reveal that FGF21 does not signal to skeletal muscle to mediate its acute insulin-sensitizing effects. Furthermore, conferring FGF21 responsiveness to skeletal muscle does not alter endogenous insulin sensitivity or FGF21-mediated enhancement of insulin sensitivity in mice in vivo. Instead, FGF21 signals to BAT to enhance insulin-stimulated glucose uptake. Thus, although brown adipocytes and skeletal myocytes share a common progenitor cell, there is something unique about brown adipocytes which allows for an FGF21-mediated enhancement of insulin-stimulated glucose uptake that is not observed in skeletal muscle.
These studies also provide novel insights into downstream signaling pathways activated by FGF21 in brown adipocytes. Prior phosphoproteomic and transcriptomic analyses were conducted using white adipose tissue or 3T3L1 white adipocytes in response to FGF21 ( , ). However, these studies were performed prior to the discovery that brown adipose tissue is a critical target for the acute insulin sensitizing effects of FGF21 ( ). Since FGF21's acute insulin sensitizing effects are rapid, we explored cellular signaling via phosphoproteomics, as opposed to transcriptional changes. In addition, since FGF21 signals through a receptor tyrosine kinase receptor, examining signaling through phosphorylation pathways is logical but may not be an exhaustive evaluation of the mechanisms for FGF21 action. Our results indicate that MAPK-RSK induced signaling downstream of FGF21 treatment increases AS160 phosphorylation, which overlaps with insulin-stimulated AKT signaling, to provide a synergistic input converging on GLUT4 translocation to increase glucose uptake in brown adipocytes. The lack of AKT1 T308/AKT2 T309 phosphorylation following FGF21 administration is consistent with a previous study using 3T3L1 adipocytes ( ). Importantly, this may explain why FGF21 sensitizes brown adipocytes to insulin via a MAPK-RSK-AS160 signaling pathway, but FGF21 alone is not sufficient to drive GLUT4 translocation or glucose uptake. It is important to note that FGF21 production from the liver is increased during extended fasting, when insulin levels are low, which explains why FGF21 alone might not function physiologically to stimulate glucose uptake. Instead, we have proposed that FGF21 enhances insulin-stimulated glucose uptake to specific tissues during re-feeding, following an extended fast, to facilitate maximal tissue nutrient uptake ( ). Brown adipose tissue is critical for maintaining core body temperature, at least in rodents, and therefore may be the reason that FGF21 enhances insulin-stimulated glucose uptake there.
While we have implicated components of GLUT4 trafficking as targets of FGF21 in brown adipocytes, further studies are needed to evaluate the functional significance of these GLUT4 related proteins, including TUG and DENND4c, in mediating FGF21 enhancements in insulin sensitivity. However, experiments to identify the exact mechanism for FGF21-mediated enhancement of insulin sensitivity are difficult to tease apart since FGF21 signaling impinges on aspects of insulin signaling. For example, deletion of TUG or DENND4c may abolish the effects of FGF21, but they also affect insulin signaling and thus the results of these experiments are difficult to interpret.
An important observation from these experiments is that FGF21 has weak effects on AKT phosphorylation but appears to affect the activation of the critical Rab GTPase-activating protein AS160. Interestingly, previous work has identified an insulin-dependent, PI3K-independent pathway that regulates GLUT4 translocation via the activation of another small GTPase, TC10 ( ). Although TC10 was not specifically identified in our phosphoproteomic studies, given the effects of FGF21 on GLUT4 translocation and GLUT4 related proteins, future studies are needed to explore whether FGF21 signaling may also alter TC10 GTPase activity. Moreover, additional studies targeting GTPase activity at the plasma membrane are needed to determine if FGF21-induced signaling overlaps with insulin at junctions not identified by our phosphoproteomic screen.
While our phosphoproteomic studies focused specifically on primary brown adipocytes, future studies evaluating the effects of FGF21 on different adipose depots (white adipose vs brown adipose) could be useful to dissect what signaling pathways of FGF21 are unique in brown adipocytes. Finally, as both organoids and human adipocyte models become more accessible, additional studies with these models may be valuable in elucidating the complex effects of FGF21 on adipose tissues.
