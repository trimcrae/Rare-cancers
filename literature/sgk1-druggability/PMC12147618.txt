Dysregulation of this enzyme is increasingly being associated with the progression of 2 prominent types of diseases, namely viral infections, such as COVID-19, and cardiovascular disorders, such as atrial fibrillation (AF), positioning it as a potential therapeutic target. With regard to coronavirus 2019 (COVID-19), SGK1 detrimentally affects inflammatory pathways and modulates the cytokine storm, leading to lung tissue damage. Considering this dysregulation, researchers are exploring SGK1 inhibition as a potential strategy for mitigating severe COVID-19 outcomes. SGK1 also regulates pumps and ion channels, significantly affecting cardiac performance in AF. This protein is responsible for promoting fibrosis and inflammation in the cardiac tissue, making it a potential target for reducing atrial fibrillation. SGK1 inhibition offers a new avenue for therapeutic targets against both COVID-19 and AF. This review is aimed at providing a comprehensive overview of SGK1 dysregulation in both diseases, underscoring the urgent need for more preclinical and clinical trials to evaluate effective SGK1 inhibitors for patients with coexisting COVID-19 and AF.

## Key Findings

▪Under stress conditions, glucocorticoids regulate the genomic expression of serum- and glucocorticoid-inducible kinase-1 (SGK1), contributing to the cellular response.
▪SGK1 is known to regulate different ion channels, playing a crucial role in cellular homeostasis.
▪Both COVID-19 and AF are known to contain significant ionic imbalances.
▪This review highlights the intricacy between COVID-19 and atrial fibrillation and suggest a common therapeutic target to address ionic imbalances.

## Introduction
The coronavirus disease (COVID-19), caused by the severe acute respiratory syndrome-coronavirus-2 (SARS-CoV-2), had a devastating effect on health systems worldwide. Clinical data show that cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) have been the most prevalent comorbidities of COVID-19. Researchers have focused on understanding how COVID-19 might affect CVDs, especially atrial fibrillation (AF). , , Numerous studies demonstrate a clear correlation between COVID-19 and CVDs. , , COVID-19 intensifies AF through several interconnected mechanisms, with serum and glucocorticoid-regulated kinase 1 (SGK1) playing a central role. The mechanisms that are proposed in this study are thought to be speculative. The primary mechanism involves the coronavirus triggering an inflammatory response, raising cytokine levels, and altering the ionic balance, which leads to the destabilization of the electrical activity of the heart. , , The secondary mechanism involves inflammation-induced dysregulation of SGK1 affecting the ionic balance by altering the movement of sodium, potassium, and calcium across cell membranes. , , Inflammation also impairs the function of the cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator (CFTR), leading to disrupted ion transport and contributing to complications such as fluid imbalances in tissues. , , This series of responses makes the patient more likely to have palpitations, which is the initial stage of AF. Additionally, SGK1 plays the main role in phosphorylating ion channels, such as the voltage-gated sodium and calcium channel, which increases the flow of sodium and calcium. , , Dysregulated SGK1 affects the electrical activity of the heart, leading to an increased risk of arrhythmias. , , 
Beyond its role in AF, SGK1 is implicated in several other cardiovascular pathologic conditions, emphasizing its broader relevance in CVDs. For example, SGK1 contributes to cardiac hypertrophy, where its overactivation promotes pathologic growth of cardiomyocytes and fibrosis, ultimately leading to heart failure. , , In hypertension, SGK1 facilitates sodium retention in renal epithelial cells, a key factor in blood pressure regulation. SGK1 also plays a role in vascular remodeling, promoting proliferation and migration of vascular smooth muscle cells, which contributes to atherosclerosis and other vascular diseases. , , Furthermore, SGK1 influences endothelial dysfunction, a hallmark of several cardiovascular conditions, by modulating endothelial cell permeability and inflammation. , These multifaceted roles of SGK1 highlight its significance not only in the context of COVID-19-associated AF but also as a central player in the pathophysiology of various CVDs.
Considering the above-mentioned roles of SGK1, its inhibition presents a distinctive approach to stopping the cytokine storm and ionic disturbances associated with COVID-19 and AF. , , , , , Despite the promising nature of these pathways, further research is necessary to understand how SGK1 can serve as a common therapeutic target for these diseases. depicts the catalytic activity, regulation, molecular function, subcellular location, and induction of SGK1. 
Although some theoretical frameworks and preclinical studies describe how SGK1 controls inflammatory responses and ion channel function, no clinical trial on direct targeting of SGK1 in patients with COVID-19 or with both COVID-19 and AF has been conducted. , , In this review, we comprehensively explore SGK1 as a dual-target therapeutic option for individuals with AF and COVID-19. We highlight the role of SGK1 in both the diseases and describe strategies for its targeted inhibition. It is important to plan clinical studies that test the efficacy of SGK1 inhibition in preventing arrhythmias and controlling inflammation in patients with COVID-19 and AF. Such studies would not only confirm whether SGK1 inhibitors are effective but also might lead to new drug combinations for treating both the heart and lung components of COVID-19.

## Regulation of SGK1 by upstream kinases
SGK1 catalyzes the transfer of a phosphate group from adenosine triphosphatase (ATP) to serine or threonine residues on substrate proteins. , The important structural parts of SGK1 are the ATP-binding site, substrate-binding site, activation loop, and hydrophobic motif. , The phosphorylation is characterized by the binding of ATP and substrate to the catalytic domain of the kinase. , Upstream kinases, such as 3-phosphoinositide-dependent protein kinase 1 and mammalian target of rapamycin complex 2, phosphorylate SGK1 to strictly control its catalytic activity. , Other factors that influence this regulation include hormone signals and cellular stress. The ATP-binding and substrate-binding sites are both important parts of the kinase domain. The kinase domain phosphorylates serine or threonine residues on target proteins. , To achieve maximum activity, the kinase requires phosphorylation of the activation loop within this domain at specific locations, such as Thr 256 and Ser 422. , Additionally, the C-terminal part of SGK1 has a hydrophobic motif that makes the protein much more stable and improves its enzymatic activity when phosphorylated by mammalian target of rapamycin complex 2. , Although not as well characterized, the N-terminal region is involved in interactions between proteins and localization of proteins within cells. , Furthermore, SGK1 possesses numerous protein interaction domains that allow it to selectively target specific substrates and cellular compartments during its activity. Regulatory phosphorylation sites, distributed throughout the protein in response to hormonal signals and cellular stress, modulate the activity and stability of the protein. , , , The structural characteristics of SGK1 allow efficient regulation of its activity, guaranteeing the correct phosphorylation of target proteins that are involved in a wide variety of cellular activities. , As a result, SGK1 can maintain cellular homeostasis and respond to a wide range of physiologic and pathologic situations. ## Tissue-specific expression and diversity of SGK1 isoforms
SGK1 is abundantly expressed in multiple tissues, including immune cells, heart, adrenal glands, and kidneys. , Its expression is particularly prominent in the cells that control electrolytes and metabolism, which shows how important it is for keeping cellular homeostasis. Hormones such as glucocorticoids and mineralocorticoids regulate SGK1 expression, influencing its role in immune response and blood pressure regulation. , These hormones regulate SGK1 activation through a receptor-mediated mechanism that in turn activates ion channels essential for tissue function. , , , Recent findings of BioProject PRJNA280600 show a comprehensive insight of SGK1 tissue-specific expression across the human tissues. The data of this project have analyzed 20 human tissues and provide valuable insights of SGK1 expression in different organs such as adrenal gland, kidney, small intestine, heart, lung, and others. In , different tissue-specific expression of SGK1 is explained, and the dataset suggests that SGK1 plays a critical role in tissue development, especially high metabolic organs such as heart, and adrenal glands during early fetal stages. 
SGK1 is an essential kinase for various signaling pathways as well as the regulation of ion channel and transporters. These actions are essential for various cellular process such as including electrolyte balance, cell survival, and inflammation. , , The SGK1 is structurally related to protein kinases, such as PKA (protein kinase A) and exists in multiple isoforms. , These isoforms arise from alternative splicing and post-translational modification, which perform a wide range of physiologic functions across different tissues. In , Summary of SGK1 isoforms, accession number, transcript length (aa), and reported mutation are given. Through alternative splicing, 5 isoforms of SGK1 are produced, with isoform 2 being the longest (526 amino acids). , Numerous studies have been reported about the functional diversity of SGK1 isoforms, which regulate different physiologic processes among various tissues. The longest isoform 2 is quite important in the regulation of heart function and blood pressure, because it is found in the endoplasmic reticulum membrane and activates sodium channels (eg, epithelial sodium channel). In contrast, short isoforms, isoform 5, isoform 3, and isoform 4 are found in cytoplasm and the nucleus, and exhibit long half-life. , Based on the previous studies, the activation of SGK1 in various cellular compartments is essential for imparting functional specificity to SGK1 signaling pathways. , 

## SGK1-mediated regulation of ion channels and transporters
SGK1 activates a wide variety of ion channels and transporters, which are involved in cellular homeostasis, inflammation, cell proliferation, and hormone secretion regulating key physiologic processes across various tissues ( ). SGK1-sensitive ion channels, including voltage-gated sodium channels (Nav1.5), , , voltage-gated calcium channels (L-type), potassium channels (KCNQ1, KCNH2), , chloride channels (ClC-2), , and calcium-activated potassium channels (BK, KCa1.1) , are involved in the control of potassium elimination, sodium retention, and cardiac action potential. 
In addition to its role as an ion channel, SGK1 is responsible for modulating the Na+/K+ ATPase pump , CFTR, sodium-proton exchanger (NHE1), and sodium-bicarbonate co-transporter (NBC). , 
This helps to maintain normal cellular excitability, fluid balance, and ion transport throughout a variety of tissues, such as the cardiopulmonary system, the kidneys, the lungs, and the muscles. However, elevated SGK1 expression and activity contribute to the pathogenesis of various conditions, including hypertension, obesity, diabetes, thrombosis, stroke, and fibrosing diseases. , In cases of severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) infection, SGK1 can disrupt the epithelial barrier and can cause inflammation of the airways. One of the main structural elements of coronavirus virulence, the envelope (E) protein, might be involved in this process. By impairing the CFTR, which regulates nuclear factor κB (NF-κB) signaling, pathogens may disrupt transepithelial Cl− transport. , Dysregulated SGK1 represents a therapeutic target for treating interconnected diseases, which is linked to ion transport dysfunction.

## Impact of SGK1 dysregulation on CFTR channels in COVID-19
SGK1 is directly involved in the activation of various ion channels (eg, CFTR, epithelial sodium channel [ENaC], sodium channel alpha subunit [SCN5A], KCNQ1, KCNQ4), which participate in the regulation of mucosal clearance in the lungs. , , , , Many studies have reported the malfunctioning of chloride ion (Cl−) exchangers hinders proper mucosal clearance in patients with COVID-19. , 
A recent study examined the imbalances in intracellular Cl− in regulating immunologic responses during SARS-CoV-2 infection. These ionic imbalances adversely impact the immune system and ultimately weaken the immunity against SARS-CoV-2, increasing susceptibility to infection. The SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid (N) protein interacts with small mothers against decapentaplegic 3 (Smad3), a downstream effector of the transforming growth factor beta signaling pathway and affects many cellular functions. , This protein–protein interaction lowers the CFTR levels through microRNA-145 and raises the concentration of Cl− inside cells, which triggers the phosphorylation of SGK1 and subsequent inflammatory responses ( ). The study further reported a second mechanism whereby the N protein sustains high Cl− levels by depleting the secondary messenger cyclic adenosine monophosphate. This finding suggests that targeting the Cl− signaling pathway might be a novel treatment avenue for COVID-19. In this study, rolipram was used as a selective phosphodiesterase 4 (PDE4) inhibitor that lowers Cl−, which effectively lowered inflammation in the airways. The study showed how SGK1 helps the inflammatory responses triggered by the SARS-CoV-2 N protein by controlling the signaling pathways for Cl−. This knowledge enhances our understanding of targeting the Cl− signaling pathway as a novel therapeutic strategy for COVID-19. 
A similar study by Caohuy et al demonstrated that SARS-CoV-2 triggers a heightened hyper-proinflammatory immune response in the airway, contributing to sickness and mortality in patients with COVID-19. It was hypothesized that the spike (S) protein of SARS-CoV-2 interacts with CFTR and disrupts CFTR signaling, leading to the activation of NFκB signaling and an ENaC, which is responsible for sodium transport in epithelial cells. This study used SGK1 inhibitors, namely digitoxin and ouabain, to prevent the interaction between S protein and angiotensin converting enzyme 2 (ACE2). , The drugs used in this study protected the SARS-CoV-2 infection-mediated loss of CFTR. The hypothesis was tested using an organoid model, which provides an accurate representation of human airway responses. The study findings suggested that CFTR signaling inhibition caused by the S protein of SARS-CoV-2 in patients with COVID-19 resulted in symptoms, such as cystic fibrosis, characterized by persistent lung inflammation. The use of SGK1 inhibitors in this study may open avenues for novel treatment of COVID-19, notably in patients displaying airway inflammation. 
In another recent investigation, SGK1 was found to be a key player in starting the inflammatory process caused by SARS-CoV-2. The envelope (E) protein of SARS-CoV-2 was found to activate the Toll-like receptors 2 and 4 on airway epithelial cells, which begin the c-Jun N-terminal kinase signaling pathway, causing an increasing in Cl− content in airway epithelial cells. This elevation was reported to be attributable to phosphodiesterase 4D (PDE4D), which affects Cl− transport mechanisms. The elevated Cl− levels promote the phosphorylation of SGK1 and ultimately enhance inflammatory responses, contributing to the pathophysiology of airway inflammation during SARS-CoV-2 infection. Collectively, these studies suggest that SGK1 could potentially play a role in the development of new therapeutic approaches for treating AF patients with COVID-19. , 

## Lack of clinical investigations on SGK1 as a therapeutic target in COVID-19
Even though evidence for the role of SGK1 as a key player in controlling inflammation and immune functions has been accumulating, clinical studies into its potential as a therapeutic target for COVID-19 have not been undertaken. SGK1 can affect many cellular processes, such as those that cause stress reactions and inflammation, its involvement in the pathophysiology of COVID-19 remains unclear. Extensive research has been conducted on the response of the immune system to COVID-19, especially with regard to long-term COVID and heart problems; SGK1 as a possible target treatment or intervention has not been investigated. Targeting SGK1 could possibly lead to new options for treating the inflammatory and immune system problems observed in patients with COVID-19.

## Impact of SGK1 dysregulation on voltage-gated sodium (Nav1.5) channels in AF
SGK1 interacts with Nav1.5 channels, which are encoded by SCN5A. , , , These channels are essential for generating the cardiac action potential, a vital process that sustains heart rhythm and functioning. , Phosphorylation of Nav1.5 channels by SGK1 changes their function and affects sodium current kinetics. , This modulation has significant implications for the length of the action potential (APD) as well as the excitability of atrial myocytes. Researchers have linked aberrant sodium channel function, including an increase in late sodium current, contributing to the development of AF. , , 
Bezzerides et al. reported an intricate relationship between the SGK1 and Nav1.5 channels in cardiomyocytes. They demonstrated that the upregulation of SGK1 disrupts both peak and late sodium currents, which stretches out the long active potential and increases the chances of arrhythmia. , , As illustrated in , increased SGK1 activity enhances sodium influx via ENaC, hyperpolarizing the cardiac cells and subsequent arrhythmic disorders. This study used a zebrafish model (genetic long QT syndrome) to evaluate the impact of SGK1 inhibition on AF condition. The authors performed single-cell electrophysiology experiments to analyze the efficacy and kinetics of SGK1 inhibition and found that the effects of SGK1 inhibition on NaV1.5 channels aligned closely with those previously identified with continuously active SGK1. Furthermore, mutation of T1590, the specific phosphorylation site of SGK1, to alanine caused the sodium ion channel to respond differently. Apart from its regulation of ion channels such as NaV1.5, SGK1 also plays a role in controlling fibrosis and hypertrophy. These mechanisms are responsible for the structural remodeling of the atria in AF. 
A comparable study on SGK1 inhibition targeting long QT syndrome (LQTS), and preclinical findings, provided strong support for the development of SGK1 inhibitors as a prospective treatment for patients with LQTS. , The purpose of this study was to investigate the efficacy of novel selective SGK1 inhibitors in induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocyte (iPSC-CM) models of LQTS. Mexiletine (MEX)-sensitive SCN5A-P1332L iPSC-CMs were tested and compared with a CRISPR/Cas9 SCN5A-P1332L variant-corrected isogenic control. The therapeutic efficacy of SGK1-I1, a novel inhibitor, compared with that of MEX, was tested for APD at 90% repolarization (APD90) shortening in SCN5A-P1332L, SCN5A-R1623Q, KCNH2-G604S, and KCNQ1-V254M iPSC-CMs using FluoVolt (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA). , Cardiomyocytes (generated from SCN5A-P1332L iPSCs) were initially subjected to testing because of their response to MEX. They contrasted these cells with a control group that had the SCN5A-P1332L mutation fixed using the CRISPR/Cas9 technology. Next, they compared MEX and SGK1-I1 in terms of their ability to reduce APD90. Using FluoVolt, they examined this in other heart cell types, such as SCN5A-P1332L, SCN5A-R1623Q, KCNH2-G604S, and KCNQ1-V254M. APD90 was found to be much longer in the SCN5A-P1332L iPSC-CM group compared with that in the control. , , In contrast, SGK1-I1 significantly decreased APD90 levels, suggesting that, for some LQTS genotypes, SGK1-I1 outperformed MEX. The reduction of APD90 in SCN5A-R1623Q, KCNH2-G604S, and KCNQ1-V254M cells by SGK1-I1 indicated its potential for alleviation of LQTS.
Giannetti et al showed that SGK1 activation in rodent models increased the risk of heart problems, ventricular arrhythmias, and death. , This is because activation of SGK1 changes the sodium channel in the heart, which can stop heartbeats that are not working properly. In contrast, stopping SGK1 in cardiomyocytes protects against fibrosis, heart failure, and changes in sodium channels caused by hemodynamic stress. , Embark et al evaluated the effects of an isoform of SGK1 on SCN5A. They introduced cRNA expressing the human Na+ channel SCN5A into Xenopus laevis oocytes, either alone or in conjunction with cRNA encoding the wild-type kinases SGK1, the constitutively active kinase (S422D) SGK1, the inactive variant (K127NSGK1), or the wild-type SGK3. , They found that co-expression of wild-type SGK1, SGK3, and constitutively active S422DSGK1 activated SCN5A currents. Moreover, the inactive mutant (K127NSGK1) markedly reduced the currents, and the expression of SGK3 significantly altered the gating of SCN5A, necessitating a depolarization of the prepotential and hyperpolarization of the activation threshold to ensure 50% channel availability. , In addition, they reported that the mutation in consensus sequence led to the opening of the channel in opposite directions. This study showed that SGK1 is stimulated by hormones, such as glucocorticoids and mineralocorticoids, along with inflammatory mediators, affecting cardiovascular function. Furthermore, both SGK1 and SGK3 are modulated by insulin and insulin-like growth factor 1, establishing their association with cardiovascular health via hormonal mechanisms. , 
Overall, the abovementioned findings indicate that SGK1 is a crucial protein that controls Nav1.5 sodium channels and that SGK1 inhibition lowers the risk of arrhythmia and prevents LQTS. Based on these results, SGK1 can be considered a promising therapeutic target against AF and related CVDs.

## Clinical trial focusing on SGK1 as a therapeutic target in AF
SGK1 inhibition has practical implications as a potential therapeutic target against AF. A phase 1 research (NCT06507839) aiming at assessing the safety, tolerability, pharmacokinetics, and dietary interactions of THRV-1268, an inhibitor of SGK-1, is the first-in-human, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, single and multiple ascending dose study. In particular, this trial seeks to determine whether SGK1 can be a therapeutic target for AF in situations of obesity. It explores the alterations in atrial remodeling and inflammation caused by SGK1 inhibition. SGK1 activation has been reported to correlate with negative changes in cardiac cells, particularly in obese patients, thereby increasing the incidence of AF. , 

## Trial design
This single-center trial includes 2 components: single ascending dose (SAD) and multiple ascending dose (MAD) studies. The participants will be screened up to 28 days before enrollment. During the SAD phase, 5 patient cohorts will receive a single oral administration of THRV-1268, with a maximum dosage of 500 mg. In addition, 1 of the SAD groups will be used for testing the effect of food, employing a 2-period crossover design. Participants will receive THRV-1268 twice, once with food and once without food, with at least 7 days between each dose. Each cohort will include a corresponding placebo group, with 5 placebo cohorts receiving a single dose of placebo. During the MAD phase, 3 cohorts will receive THRV-1268 once daily in the morning from day 1 to day 7 to assess the effects of multiple dosing. A matched placebo group will get daily placebo doses for a period of 7 days ( ). 
The first part of the study will assess the safety, tolerability, pharmacokinetics, and dietary interactions of THRV-1268. The second part will explore how THRV-1268 might be used to treat conditions such as AF. This study focusing on healthy adult volunteers is expected to provide information relevant to the development of SGK-1 inhibitors as potential treatments for AF. The study specifications have not disclosed any articles or findings from the study. The anticipated enrollment for the study is 74 healthy volunteers, and the recruitment began on June 6, 2024. The expected primary completion date is February 1, 2025, along with the projected study completion date. Participants eligible for this study must be healthy individuals aged 18 to 60 years with a body mass index between 18.5 and 30.0. The study is open to volunteers of all genders, provided they adhere to specific contraceptive methods and meet the criteria for a physiologic postmenopausal condition or undergo surgical sterilization. Women of reproductive capability must provide consent to employ highly effective contraceptive methods or abstain from heterosexual intercourse during the study period. Men with the biological ability to reproduce must employ contraception and refrain from sperm donation during the trial and for at least 90 days after the final dose. The exclusion criteria include a history of significant medical conditions, such as cardiovascular, gastrointestinal, neurologic, or pulmonary diseases; a history of cardiac complications such as AF, pacemaker implantation, or unexplained syncope; as well as prior use of prescription medications, over-the-counter drugs, or nutritional supplements before the study. Individuals are ineligible for participation if they have smoked or used nicotine within the past 180 days, exhibit positive screening results for human immunodeficiency virus, hepatitis, or substance abuse, or have recently engaged in another clinical investigation. Furthermore, patients with abnormal laboratory results, such as creatinine clearance and liver function tests, or those who received a COVID-19 vaccination within 14 days before receiving the study medicine, are excluded. The results of this study being conducted in Canada will aid in evaluating the potential for further development of THRV-1268 for conditions such as AF. This phase 1 trial represents the need for more clinical trials to understand how SGK1 inhibitors can influence AF treatment, and the expected outcomes of this trial may provide valuable results, reinforcing the notion that SGK-1 is a promising target not only for cardiovascular conditions but also for addressing complications arising from COVID-19.

## AF risk, COVID-19 severity, and SGK1 dysregulation: Possible relationships
SGK1 deregulation is linked to hyperinflammatory responses and COVID-19 progression. Increased SGK1 activity in patients with COVID-19 may exacerbate respiratory inflammation and cytokine storm. , An understanding of the mechanisms by which SGK1 modulates inflammatory and immune responses could provide critical knowledge regarding the determinants that influence the severity of COVID-19. A correlation between dysregulation of SGK1 and the onset of AF has been identified. , , , There is a potential correlation between elevated SGK1 activity and the worsening of AF in individuals because of possible structural and electrical alterations in the atria. Investigations into the correlation between SGK1 dysregulation and the risk of AF could help develop prognostic indicators that facilitate early detection and intervention to prevent AF. Inflammatory mechanisms, including cytokine activation and immune cell infiltration, are shared features of COVID-19 and AF. , , Because of its role in controlling inflammatory pathways, SGK1 may be dysfunctional in both COVID-19 and AF because of shared mechanisms. , , , , Targeting the inflammation generated by SGK1 could be 1 of the possible therapeutic strategies for COVID-19 and AF. This could reduce the severity of the virus and slow down the progression of the condition. Fibrotic remodeling in the heart tissue is a common feature of both COVID-19–related cardiomyopathy and atrial remodeling linked to AF. There may be a link between SGK1 dysfunction, fibrotic remodeling, and the worsening of illness in both COVID-19 and AF. This is because SGK1 controls the fibrosis pathways. , Directing therapeutic efforts toward SGK1 to reduce fibrosis and remodeling may offer an approach for effectively investigating the interplay between SGK1 dysfunction, COVID-19 severity, and AF susceptibility and could facilitate the identification of shared pathophysiological pathways. This, in turn, could streamline the development of precise therapeutic approaches capable of simultaneously addressing both the disorders.

## Gaps and future perspectives
Further research is necessary to investigate the effects of SGK1 inhibitors in models of the 2 diseases, particularly in individuals with both AF and COVID-19. , Several significant diseases, including AF and the severe inflammatory reactions observed in COVID-19, are associated with SGK1. SGK1 plays roles in inflammation, fibrosis, and ion channel regulation. , Studying the roles of SGK1 in models in which these 2 conditions coexist could provide insights into how SGK1 inhibitors could potentially alleviate the complications of both the diseases. Blocking of SGK1 in these models could reveal its potential in reduce the hyperinflammation associated with COVID-19, address issues with ion channels, and alter the structure of cells that cause AF. Furthermore, there is a dearth of clinical studies that specifically focus on SGK1 in conditions of co-occurrence of AF and COVID-19. Because both diseases involve inflammation and fibrosis, clinical studies that explore SGK1 as a possible target for the treatment of these high-risk patient groups is essential. These studies could determine the effectiveness of SGK1 inhibitors, such as THRV-1268, in reducing the damage resulting from inflammation in COVID-19 and the heart issues associated with AF. This could be particularly beneficial for patients with COVID-19 who also have AF, because it could help prevent long-term heart problems. By filling in these study gaps, we might find new treatment approaches that help manage both diseases simultaneously, which would greatly improve patient outcomes.

## Discussion
The roles played by SGK1 in COVID-19 and AF is a new area of research. Several studies have shown that SGK1 dysregulation plays a big role in the worsening of these diseases. , , , In COVID-19, SGK1 alters the inflammatory pathways, such as cytokine storm, which can damage the lung tissue and cause systemic inflammation. In AF, SGK1 affects the movement of ions, which is involved in heart arrhythmogenesis and fibrosis. The finding that SGK1 is involved in both these diseases is the first step toward exploring the efficacy of SGK1 suppression in their simultaneous treatment. , Studies have shown that SGK1 plays a key role in boosting inflammatory reactions caused by the SARS-CoV-2. This kinase is induced by different cellular stress signals. It has been connected to the regulation of ion channels, such as CFTR and ENac, which play a part in controlling lung fluid and immune responses. , , , , , , For instance, a study showed that the interaction of the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein with cellular proteins such as CFTR leads to heightened inflammation through SGK1 activation. , SGK1 also helps phosphorylate different ion channels, which increases the flow of sodium and calcium and worsens inflammation in the lungs and heart. SGK1 controls ion channels that keep the heart from getting too excited and can cause arrhythmias. , Its overactivation causes more sodium to enter through ENaC, which helps change the structure of the heart. This remodeling, along with inflammation, promotes the pathogenesis of AF. , SGK1 also changes the movement of sodium and chloride ions in heart cells, which can change the electrical activity and possibly lead to arrhythmias. , , Therefore, SGK1 plays a big role in the deleterious changes, such as fibrosis, ion channel failure, and higher risk of arrhythmias, in AF. Despite the accumulating evidence linking SGK1 to COVID-19 and AF, a huge research gap exists with regard to clinical studies aimed at evaluating the inhibition of SGK1 as a treatment for these diseases. No clinical trials have directly investigated the blocking of SGK1 as a possible treatment for COVID-19. This is a very important missing piece in our knowledge about the importance of SGK1 in COVID-19 biology, especially in people who already have heart problems, such as AF. Because SGK1 plays a harmful role in both COVID-19 and AF, targeting it as part of a combination treatment could help both conditions. SGK1 inhibition could lower the inflammatory reactions in severe COVID-19 cases while also lowering the structural and functional changes in the heart in AF. Additionally, this could be especially helpful for people who developed AF because of or during a COVID-19 illness. , , SGK1 inhibition may improve results in these high-risk patients by stopping or slowing down both inflammatory and fibrotic processes. Researchers are looking at the safety and pharmacokinetics of THRV-1268, an SGK1 inhibitor, in healthy people as part of a clinical trial (NCT06507839). In future clinical trials, the efficacy of SGK1 inhibitors in patients with COVID-19 suffering from heart and breathing problems should be explored. Because long-term COVID is becoming more common and is linked to heart problems, targeting SGK1 could offer an effective treatment strategy.

## Conclusion
SGK1 is a critical modulator of interconnected pathways in COVID-19 and AF. Its diverse functions profoundly influence inflammation and heart dysfunction. In the context of COVID-19, increased SGK1 activity is associated with severe inflammatory reactions, which can lead to significant respiratory complications and systemic cytokine storm that harm the pulmonary health. In the context of AF, SGK1 alters the activity of ion channels, particularly sodium currents, and promotes the occurrence of arrhythmias by influencing the morphology and electrical dynamics of the atrial tissue. Even though SGK1 is involved in both conditions and its influence manifests in different directions, it is critical to have a thorough understanding of how it works so that it can be targeted as a novel strategy for simultaneous treatment of both these diseases. Clinical trials are needed to check the efficacy of SGK1 inhibitors in persons with both AF and COVID-19, specifically to determine whether such inhibitors can lower hyperinflammatory states while correcting ion channel abnormalities. Furthermore, researching the overlapping pathways that impacted by SGK1 may aid the discovery of biomarkers for the severity of either ailment, improving strategies for early diagnosis and therapy. Current research has many critical gaps, the most notable of which is a dearth of dedicated clinical investigations. Filling these gaps could help develop effective treatment programs that enhance patient outcomes, particularly in high-risk patients with both COVID-19 and AF. Integrative approaches could lead to personalized medicine, ensuring that therapies are not only effective but also tailored to the specific pathophysiologic profiles of individuals with these disorders. As our understanding of SGK1 function increases, it shows great promise as a crucial target in the quest to reduce the health consequences of these illnesses. This could lead to better and more comprehensive treatment strategies that can address patients’ complex demands in the period following COVID-19 and beyond.
