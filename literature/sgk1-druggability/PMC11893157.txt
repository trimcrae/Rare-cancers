## Abstract

## Background
Liver fibrosis is characterized by hepatic stellate cell (HSC) activation and collagen overproduction, but its pathogenesis remains largely unknown. This study aimed to uncover the role of neural precursor cell expressed developmentally downregulated 4-like (Nedd4L) signaling in liver fibrosis and its relationship with gut microbiota.

## Methods
Intraperitoneal injection of carbon tetrachloride (CCl4) was used to induce liver fibrosis in 8-week-old female C57BL/6J mice with Nedd4L knockout or administration of the Nedd4L protein phosphorylation inhibitor EMD638683. HSCs isolated from mice were activated with transforming growth factor-beta 1 (TGFβ1) with or without EMD638683.

## Results
An approximately 3-fold elevation in Nedd4L mRNA was observed in hepatocytes and liver tissues, and significantly higher hepatic Nedd4L phosphorylation was observed in fibrotic mice than in non-fibrotic mice. Nedd4L mRNA level in HSCs isolated from fibrotic livers and Nedd4L protein level in TGFβ1-stimulated HSCs from wild-type livers remained unchanged. In isolated HSCs, TGFβ1-induced Nedd4L phosphorylation and cell activation were suppressed with EMD638683. In CCl4-treated mice, EMD638683 alleviated liver fibrosis and induced a relative increase in fecal Bacteroides, Parabacteroides, Erysipelatoclostridium, Blautia, and Klebsiella, whereas Nedd4L deficiency predisposed mice to liver injury and liver fibrosis with a remarkable reduction in fecal Lactobacillus, Enterorhabdus, and Bacteroides.

## Conclusion
Hepatic Nedd4L signaling contributes to CCl4-induced liver fibrosis in female mice, which is associated with alterations in the gut microbiota, and Nedd4L phosphorylation is involved in TGFβ1-mediated HSC activation.

Keywords: liver fibrosis, Nedd4L, gut microbiota, hepatic stellate cell

## Introduction
Liver fibrosis (LF) is an inevitable pathological process that occurs during the progression of almost all chronic liver diseases to liver cirrhosis and cancer. Accurate estimation of the burden of LF is difficult due to the considerable variability in the incidence of chronic liver diseases in different geographical regions and the paucity of high-quality epidemiological data; however, the global burden of LF-involved chronic liver diseases is unequivocally substantial and shows an increasing trend due to the rising prevalence of unhealthy lifestyles [ ]. In addition, Man et al. [ ] recently conducted a nationwide study involving 5.7 million Chinese adults and found that 8.34% and 2.85% of the general population had significant and advanced LF, respectively; moreover, the prevalence was much higher in those with ultrasound-detected fatty liver, decreased albumin or platelet count, and hepatitis B virus persistence.
LF is the most important determinant of liver-related incapacitation and overall mortality. Clinical and fundamental studies have demonstrated that LF is reversible in the early stage [ ]. It is generally accepted that a persistent immune-inflammatory response in the liver induced by chronic stimuli promotes hepatic stellate cell (HSC) activation and extracellular matrix overproduction, which eventually disrupts the normal liver architecture and induces pseudolobule formation [ ]. Regrettably, a definitive therapeutic intervention that improves the clinical outcomes of LF, regardless of the cause, is lacking because it involves multiple systems and multi-cellular interactions. Therefore, exploring the pathogenesis of LF from the perspective of potential system-to-system and cell-to-cell interactions is warranted.
Similar to fibrosis in other organs, post-translational modifications of key signaling proteins play a decisive role in LF formation and remission, and recent studies using multi-omics integration have highlighted the role of hepatic ubiquitination in carbon tetrachloride (CCl4)-induced LF [ , ]. The fate of the ubiquitinated proteins and the biological events they mediate may vary depending on the type of E3 ubiquitin ligase involved. Neural precursor cells expressed developmentally downregulated 4-like (Nedd4L) is an E3 ubiquitin ligase that maintains cellular homeostasis by aiding membrane protein internalization and turnover by ubiquitylation [ ]. Recently, Song et al. [ ] showed that Nedd4L also mediates the degradation of tyrosine kinase receptor B (TrkB) in HSCs, thus removing the inhibitory effect of TrkB on Smad2/3 phosphorylation induced by transforming growth factor-beta 1 (TGFβ1), which is the main pro-fibrotic mediator that activates HSCs. Moreover, several studies in non-HSCs have demonstrated reduced ligase activity of phosphorylated-Nedd4L induced by serum/glucocorticoid regulated kinase 1 (SGK1) [ ]. However, whether SGK1 also mediates Nedd4L phosphorylation and its significance in HSC activation in vitro or LF development in vivo remains poorly understood.
In addition to that Nedd4L signaling (phosphorylated or non-phosphorylated) in HSCs directly affects extracellular matrix-generated biological events, it may also be involved in LF development by affecting non-HSCs and extrahepatic systems, which could be demonstrated in studies on the overall role of Nedd4L signaling in the organism. It has been revealed that gut microbiota imbalance contributes to all stages of LF [ ], and the microbiota was also found to be associated with signaling derived from the deficiency of certain E3 ubiquitin ligases in models of non-hepatic diseases [ ]. To date, no study has elucidated the role of organismal Nedd4L signaling in LF in terms of its effect on the modulation of gut microbiota.
In this study, we focused on the effect of blocking Nedd4L signaling (including removal of Nedd4L expression or inhibition of Nedd4L phosphorylation) on TGFβ1-activated primary HSCs isolated from mice and CCl4-induced LF in female mice. Furthermore, we analyzed the alterations in gut microbiota in CCl4-administered settings to reveal the possible relationship among organismal Nedd4L signaling, gut microbiota, and LF.

## Materials and methods

## Reagents and antibodies
CCl4 (#C805332) and olive oil (#O815210) were purchased from Macklin (Shanghai, China). EMD638683 (#S8824) and TGFβ1 (#HY-P7117) were purchased from Selleck (Shanghai, China) and MedChemExpress (Shanghai, China), respectively. Antibodies against Nedd4L (#A8085), phospho-Nedd4L (Ser448; #AP0843), Smad3 (#A16913), phospho-Smad3 (S423/S425; #AP1263), and Collagen I (#A21059) were obtained from ABclonal Technology Co., Ltd (Wuhan, Hubei, China). Anti-SGK1 (#12103) and phospho-SGK1 (Ser78; #5599) antibodies were purchased from Cell Signaling Technology (Shanghai, China). Zen Bioscience (Chengdu, Sichuan, China) provided glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) antibody (#390035), anti-rabbit (#511203), and anti-mouse (#511103) secondary antibodies conjugated with horseradish peroxidase (HRP). Alpha-smooth muscle actin （α-SMA） antibody (#ab7817) was purchased from Abcam (Shanghai, China). siRNA targeting the mouse Nedd4L gene (siNedd4L) and negative control siRNA (si#NC) were purchased from Tsingke Biotechnology Co., Ltd (Chongqing, China).

## LF mouse model

Nedd4L-deficient homozygous (Nedd4LKO) and corresponding wild-type C57BL/6J mice were purchased from Cyagen Biosciences, Inc. (Suzhou, Jiangsu, China). All the mice were bred and housed in ventilated cages in the same housing unit [room temperature (25 ± 2)°C; humidity, (50 ± 5)%; light/dark, 12 h/12 h] throughout the experiment. Female offspring aged 6–8 weeks and weighing (20 ± 2) g was used for the experiments. CCl4 diluted in olive oil (v/v, 1:4) was injected intraperitoneally (1 mL/kg) twice a week for 6–8 weeks to establish the LF mouse model, as required, and the corresponding control mice were injected intraperitoneally with an equal volume of olive oil. For the pharmacological inhibition of SGK1 activity by EMD638683 in the LF mouse model, EMD638683 (20 mg/kg) or its solvent (dimethyl sulfoxide; #D806645; Macklin) was injected intraperitoneally at weeks 1, 3, and 6 during the CCl4 injection. All experimental animal protocols were approved by the Ethics Committee of Chongqing University Cancer Hospital (CZLS2023147-A).

## Isolation of hepatocytes and HSCs
Female mice from the LF or control group that received 8 weeks of CCl4 or olive oil injections were used to isolate the hepatocytes and HSCs (six mice per group). The method for mouse HSC isolation has been described elsewhere [ , ]. Briefly, mice anesthetized with chloral hydrate were sequentially perfused in situ with Gey’s balanced salt solution (GBSS) containing 0.05% Pronase E (#1074330001; Sigma-Aldrich, St Louis, MO, USA) or 0.05% Collagenase D (#11088858001; Roche, Basel, Switzerland) through the portal vein via a pump. This was followed by excision of liver tissues and subsequent digestion in GBSS containing 0.02% Pronase E, 0.05% Collagenase D, and 0.02 mg/mL DNAse I (#2270A; Takara, Beijing, China) for 25–35 min at 37°C. Next, the solution was filtered through cotton gauze and centrifuged for 7 min at 450 ×g. Finally, density-gradient centrifugation was performed to obtain HSC cell suspension according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The protocol for mouse hepatocyte isolation has been described elsewhere and is widely used in studies on liver physiology [ ]. It involves perfusion, collagenase-mediated dissociation, and hepatocyte separation by density-gradient centrifugation.

## Cell culture and related interventions
Isolated murine HSCs were cultured in Roswell Park Memorial Institute (RPMI)-1640 medium (#11875119; Gibco, Waltham, MA, USA) containing 20% fetal bovine serum (#A5669801; Gibco) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin solution (100 ×; #PB180120; Procell, Wuhan, Hubei, China). To mimic fibrosis by inducing HSC activation, various doses (1, 2, and 4 ng/mL) of TGFβ1 (#HY-P7117; MedChemExpress) were administered for 72 h. In addition, EMD638683 at 1 μM was given 6 h prior to TGFβ1 treatment to suppress Nedd4L phosphorylation, and siNedd4L was transfected by Lipofectamine3000 reagent (#L3000075; Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) 24 h prior to TGFβ1 treatment to reduce Nedd4L expression. Methyl thiazolyl tetrazolium (MTT) Kit (#C0009M; Beyotime, Beijing, China) was used to assess the cell proliferation and cytotoxic effects of TGFβ1 and EMD638683 on primary murine HSCs.

## Histology and immunohistochemistry
Liver sections were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (#P885233; Macklin) for 24 h and embedded in paraffin (#P815701; Macklin). To observe the morphology and infiltration of inflammatory cells, de-paraffinized and rehydrated liver slices (4 μm thick) were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (HE; #DH0020; Leagene, Beijing, China). To observe collagen deposition, Sirius red staining (#DC0041; Leagene) and Masson staining (#DC0032; Leagene) were used, which colored collagen red and blue, respectively [ ]. In addition, immunohistochemical staining was performed. Briefly, 2% horse serum-blocked liver slices (6 μm thick) were incubated with anti-α-SMA (1:500) for 16 h at 4°C and then washed. Subsequently, the slices were incubated with the secondary antibody for 1 h at room temperature and visualized using a diaminobenzidine (#PW0072; Leagene) substrate under a Leica DMRE microscope.

## Western blot analysis
Fresh liver tissue was immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80°C until use. The frozen tissue was ground and lysed using strong radio immunoprecipitation assay （RIPA） lysis solution (#P0013B; Beyotime), whereas HSCs were lysed using medium-strength RIPA lysis solution (#P0013C; Beyotime). Total proteins were extracted by centrifugation of the lysates at 12,000 ×g for 15 min at 4°C. Protein concentrations were determined using a bicinchoninic acid kit (#P0009; Beyotime). Next, 40 μg protein samples from tissues and 20 μg protein samples from HSCs were subjected to 10% sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and transferred to a polyvinylidene fluoride membrane (#IPVH09120; Millipore, Bedford, MA, USA), followed by immunoblotting with primary antibodies (1:1000) and anti-GAPDH antibodies (loading control, 1:2000). Finally, the target bands were visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescence detection kit (#WBKLS; Millipore). Each immunoblot assay was performed at least thrice, and one of them is shown.

## Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction
Total RNA was extracted from frozen liver tissues, isolated hepatocytes, and HSCs by phenol-chloroform RNA extraction using the TRIzol reagent (#15596018CN; Invitrogen). Reverse transcription of cDNA was performed using the HiScript II 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (#R211-01; Vazyme, Nanjing, Jiangsu, China). Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) was performed using AceQ qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix (#Q121-02; Vazyme). Amplification of both mouse Nedd4L and mouse β-actin mRNA was carried out under the following conditions (20 μL reaction system): 95°C for 5 min, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 10 s, and 60°C for 30 s. The following primers were used: mouse Nedd4L (sense: ACTCAGCAGGGAGGGAGAAT, anti-sense: GTCCTTCTTGGCGAGGTCAA) and mouse β-actin (sense: CCCCTGAACCCTAAGGCCA, anti-sense: CGGAGTCCATCACAATGCCT). 2−ΔΔCT was used for quantifying the relative expression of Nedd4L mRNA. The experiment was repeated thrice.

## Hepatic RNA and fecal bacterial DNA sequencing analysis
Fresh livers from fibrotic and wild-type mice were collected and immediately stored at −80°C. Total RNA from the fresh livers were extracted using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen). Nanodrop ND-1000 Spectrophotometer (Nucliber) was used to determine the quality and integrity of the isolated RNA. The Ion Total RNA-Seq Kit V2 (Life Technologies, Seattle, WA, USA) was used to prepare a sequencing library for each RNA sample. The cDNA libraries were sequenced using an Illumina HiSeq2000 (Majorbio Biotechnology Company, Shanghai, China). Analysis of the transcriptome data [raw data deposited in the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) Sequence Read Archive (SRA), Accession Number: PRJNA1159401] was consistent with that reported by Zhang et al. [ ]. In addition, feces collected from the small intestine were used for 16S rDNA sequencing, and the sequencing and analysis procedures were same as described previous report [ ]. The raw reads were deposited in the NCBI SRA database (Accession Number: PRJNA1152530).

## Biochemical analysis
Serum alanine transaminase (ALT) and aspartate transaminase (AST) levels were detected using commercial kits (#C009-2–1 and #C010-2–1; Nanjing Jiancheng, Nanjing, Jiangsu, China) at 510 nm using an enzyme labeler. Serum TGFβ1 was measured by an ELISA kit (#E-EL-0028c; Elabscience Biotechnology, Wuhan, Hubei, China).

## Statistical analyses
Bioinformatic analyses of the sequencing data have been described previously. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and Student’s t-test was used to compare them between the groups. A P-value < 0.05 (two-sided) was considered as the cutoff for statistical significance.

## Results

## Effect of Nedd4L suppression on primary HSC activation in vitro
To test whether cellular Nedd4L signaling is involved in HSC activation, primary HSCs isolated from adult wide-type female mice were used in experiments in vitro involving the application of TGFβ1, the classical pro-fibrotic factor. MTT assay showed that TGFβ1 slightly promoted cell proliferation in a dose-dependent manner ( ). TGFβ1 induces collagen I production and Smad3 phosphorylation (the upstream key event in collagen I production) in a dose-dependent manner. Meanwhile, the protein level of total Nedd4L (the ligase that mediates Smad3 ubiquitination and degradation) remained unchanged, but increasing phosphorylation of Nedd4L and activation of its upstream kinase SGK1 were observed in TGFβ1-treated cells in a dose-dependent manner ( ). EMD638683, a specific SGK1 inhibitor with no significant cytotoxicity to isolated HSCs when the dose below 2 μM ( ), inhibits the phosphorylation of Nedd4L and Smad3, thus suppressing collagen I production ( ). In contrast to the control, the phosphorylation of Smad3 and collagen I production were significantly higher in primary HSCs with siRNA-mediated knockdown of Nedd4L expression under EMD638683 challenge.
 

## Alterations in hepatic Nedd4L mRNA or protein levels in CCl4-induced LF
Compared with the mock-treated C57BL/6 mice, whole liver transcriptome RNA sequencing analysis of documented differentially expressed genes (DEGs) revealed that a total of 1,158 DEGs were significantly upregulated and 1,635 were remarkably downregulated in CCl4-induced fibrotic mice ( ). Among the upregulated DEGs, the ratio of Nedd4L mRNA in the LF group to the mock group was 2.65 ( ). In validation settings using qPCR with specific primers, hepatic Nedd4L mRNA was also found to be elevated by 2.99 times in the CCl4-administered group than the mock group ( ). Further, murine hepatocytes and HSCs were isolated separately and total RNA was extracted. qPCR showed that Nedd4L mRNA was 3.32-fold elevated in hepatocytes but was comparable in HSCs from fibrotic livers compared with those from normal livers ( ). Finally, we extracted total proteins from six murine livers and isolated hepatocytes from each group. Western blotting results demonstrated that the protein levels of Nedd4L in the whole liver or isolated hepatocytes were slightly upregulated, but the ratio of phosphorylated to total Nedd4L protein was significantly higher in fibrotic livers than in non-fibrotic livers ( ).
 

## Defective Nedd4L phosphorylation impairs LF formation
To confirm the effect of Nedd4L phosphorylation inhibition on CCl4-induced liver inflammation and LF, EMD638683 and its solvent were applied to CCl4-induced LF in adult female C57BL/6 mice. Compared with the LF or LF + S groups, weight gain at weeks 4, 6, and 8 was slightly higher in the LF + EMD638683 group ( ), whereas no significant difference in the ratio of the liver wight to body weight of mice at week 8 was observed among the three groups ( ). Between LF and LF + S groups at week 8, similar protein levels of pro-fibrotic factor (serum TGFβ1) and liver injury indicators (serum ALT or AST) were found. However, compared with the LF + S group, administration of EMD638683 significantly reduced the serum levels of TGFβ1 (16.94 ± 2.04 vs 10.94 ± 2.08 ng/mL, P < 0.001), ALT (122.17 ± 12.35 vs 68.77 ± 13.14 U/L, P < 0.001), and AST (236.59 ± 22.37 vs 138.76 ± 22.35 U/L, P < 0.001) in . Further analysis by Western blotting showed that phosphorylation of Nedd4L protein was inhibited by EMD638683-mediated suppression of SGK1 activity ( ). Lastly, HE staining results showed a similar degree of inflammatory cell infiltration and liver tissue damage, such as apparent structural abnormality of liver lobules and extensive necrosis of liver cells in the LF and LF + S groups; however, administration of EMD638683 significantly improved these pathological changes in liver tissues ( ). To confirm the effect of EMD638683 on LF, the expression of HSC activation marker α-SMA was accessed by immunohistochemical staining, and collagen deposition was visualized by Sirius red staining and Masson staining. Compared with the LF and LF + S groups, there was almost no collagen accumulation and no α-SMA expression in the liver tissues from the LF + EMD638683 group ( ).
 

## 
Nedd4L knockout mice display LF-prone phenotype compared with the wild-type mice
To better understand the effects of Nedd4L signaling on LF progression, the Nedd4L gene was knocked out in female C57BL/6 mice. On performing 3-month weight monitoring in adult mice without CCl4 challenge, it was found that the wild-type and Nedd4L-deficient mice had comparable weight gain from 8 to 20 weeks and a similar ratio of liver weight to body weight at week 20 ( ). Six weeks after CCl4 injection, it was found to decrease weight gain and the ratio of liver to body weight in wild-type mice compared with these without CCl4 injection, and greater declines were observed in Nedd4L-deficient mice ( ). Compared with the CCl4-administered wild-type mice, Nedd4L deficiency did not influence the weight gain and ratio of liver to body weight, but remarkably increased serum TGFβ1 (15.41 ± 2.65 vs 21.01 ± 3.58 ng/mL, P < 0.05), ALT (82.47 ± 11.38 vs 125.25 ± 17.67 U/L, P < 0.001), and AST levels (136.19 ± 25.67 vs 219.74 ± 22.26 U/L, P < 0.001) in . Additionally, hepatic SGK1 protein and its phosphorylation activity did not change upon Nedd4L deficiency ( ). Moreover, Nedd4L-deficient mice had significant liver histopathological changes, including more severe necrosis and inflammatory cell infiltration, increased collagen accumulation in the liver as visualized by Sirius red and Masson staining, and higher α-SMA expression in the liver as visualized by immunohistochemical staining compared with the wild-type ones ( ).
 

## Alterations in gut microbiota in fibrotic mice with Nedd4L signaling blockade
To uncover the potential mechanism of Nedd4L signaling in CCl4-induced LF in vivo, gut microbiota from various groups were profiled. At the operational taxonomic unit (OTU) level, the wild-type, wild-type or Nedd4L-deficient mice with CCl4 administration had comparable values for the ACE index, Shannon diversity index, and Shannon evenness, indicating unchanged bacterial community richness, alpha-diversity, and evenness (P >0.05 for all variables, ). The distinguished clusters presented on principal coordinates analysis demonstrate significant differences in beta-diversity among the three groups (R = 0.753, P = 0.001, ). The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used to analyze the differences in the relative abundance of the top 20 fecal bacteria at the genus level among the three groups. The results showed that compared with solvent treatment, CCl4 administration caused a significant increase in Enterorhabdus (0.73 ± 0.15 vs 2.32 ± 0.95, P < 0.01) and a remarkable reduction in Bacteroides (1.59 ± 0.91 vs 0.54 ± 0.26, P < 0.05) in wild-type mice. Compared with the wild-type mice, Nedd4L deficiency caused a significant increase in Acetatifactor (0.52 ± 0.22 vs 5.41 ± 2.57, P < 0.05), Blautia (0.15 ± 0.18 vs 6.01 ± 6.17, P < 0.05), Desulfovibrio (0.15 ± 0.14 vs 3.09 ± 1.18, P < 0.01), and Faecalibaculum (0.05 ± 0.04 vs 4.24 ± 2.25, P < 0.001), along with a remarkable reduction in Lactobacillus (45.86 ± 3.05 vs 1.63 ± 1.32, P < 0.001), Enterorhabdus (2.32 ± 0.95 vs 0.59 ± 0.55, P < 0.01), and Bacteroides (0.54 ± 0.26 vs 0.11 ± 0.07, P < 0.05) in CCl4-injected mice ( ).
 Similar to the results shown in , no differences in bacterial community richness, alpha-diversity, and evenness (P > 0.05 for all variables, ), and significant differences in beta-diversity (R = 0.326, P = 0.006, ) were observed between CCl4-treated mice given EMD638683 and its solvent. Compared with the solvent group, the genera Bacteroides (0.57 ± 0.31 vs 7.10 ± 1.34, P < 0.01), Parabacteroides (0.18 ± 0.15 vs 3.51 ± 2.22, P < 0.05), Erysipelatoclostridium (0.27 ± 0.31 vs 2.73 ± 2.71, P < 0.05), Blautia (0.25 ± 0.34 vs 1.80 ± 1.86, P < 0.05), and Klebsiella (0.005 ± 0.008 vs 0.96 ± 1.82, P < 0.05) were relatively increased, while Dubosiella (2.99 ± 1.46 vs 0.64 ± 0.51, P < 0.01), Enterorhabdus (2.85 ± 2.46 vs 0.44 ± 0.26, P < 0.01), Desulfovibrio (1.09 ± 0.52 vs 0.41 ± 0.47, P < 0.05), and Candidatus_Saccharimonas (1.07 ± 1.11 vs 0.11 ± 0.18, P < 0.05) were relatively decreased in the EMD638683 group.

## Discussion
Data from the Global Burden of Disease and World Health Organization show that chronic liver disease and LF-induced cirrhosis cause approximately 1 million deaths per year, and the burden is increasing, primarily due to the rising incidence of alcoholic hepatitis and non-alcoholic steatohepatitis [ , ]. Therefore, it is important to develop strategies to curb LF progression. However, factors such as the diversity of etiologies and drug delivery barriers impede the development and application of an effective anti-fibrogenic agent in clinical settings, which is largely attributable to an incomplete understanding of the multifaceted nature of LF pathogenesis [ ].
Probing protein ubiquitination is an emerging and important method to unravel LF pathophysiology and discover potential therapeutic targets. Song et al. [ ] recently demonstrated the pro-fibrotic property of Nedd4L protein via promoting TrkB ubiquitination and degradation in primary murine HSCs. Unlike other E3 ubiquitin ligases, the ubiquitin ligase activity of Nedd4L is negatively regulated by SGK1 activity. To further verify this property of Nedd4L in the present study, we first explored the role of intracellular Nedd4L signaling in TGFβ1-mediated activation of HSCs isolated from wild-type mice. shows that increasing TGFβ1 exposure only increased the phosphorylation of Nedd4L, along with upstream SGK1 and downstream Smad3 rather than affecting their expressions. This finding suggests that activated SGK1 incapacitates Nedd4L by mediating its phosphorylation, which then blocks the proteasomal degradation of phosphorylated-Smad3 and ultimately contributes to collagen production [ ]. We confirmed this hypothesis by inhibiting SGK1 activity through the application of EMD638683 ( ). Downregulation of Nedd4L expression with siRNA failed to alleviate TGFβ1-mediated Smad3 phosphorylation and collagen production under EMD638683 challenge, while the Nedd4L knockout group without EMD638683 exposure showed lightly higher level of phosphorylated-Smad3 and collagen I also demonstrated the anti-fibrotic property of Nedd4L in HSCs ( and ).
To clarify alterations in intra-hepatic Nedd4L signaling, transcriptomic analysis and validation experiments were conducted. Compared with the control group, hepatic Nedd4L mRNA was approximately 3-fold elevated in CCl4-induced LF in whole liver transcriptomes and validation experiments by qPCR. Furthermore, cellular separation experiments confirmed that the elevation of Nedd4L mRNA and protein occurred mainly in hepatocytes, but not in HSCs ( ). E3 ligases in non-HSCs (more than those in hepatocytes) may also contribute to LF via various mechanisms. For example, Xu et al. [ ] showed that Casitas B-lineage lymphoma (Cbl)-b or c-Cbl deletion in dendritic cells (DCs) can lead to spontaneous LF by regulating multiple properties of conventional type I DCs with CD103 positivity. Notably, tissue- or cell-specific Nedd4L expression during fibrogenesis may differ significantly owing to the organ microenvironment in specific disease states. Reduced Nedd4L transcription and protein levels in lung tissues and lung fibroblasts isolated from patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis or experimental pulmonary fibrosis have been reported [ , ], while significantly upregulated Nedd4L protein levels in the renal tubules of mice with tubulointerstitial fibrosis in the context of diabetic kidney disease were also observed [ ]. Unlike these studies, we focused not only on the expression of Nedd4L (upregulation), but also on its phosphorylation level (upregulation), as it influences the ubiquitin ligase activity of the Nedd4L protein.
Furthermore, information on other E3 ubiquitin ligases, including Synoviolin, Tripartite motif-containing protein (TRIM) 23, TRIM26, and Smad ubiquitin regulatory factor 2 (Smurf2), was also evaluated in the transcriptomic data. Although comparable hepatic mRNA levels were observed between the LF and control groups in this study (data not shown), other reports have confirmed that they are associated with HSC activation or LF development via diverse mechanisms. Synoviolin and TRIM 23 act as pro-fibrotic modulators in TGFβ1-activated HSCs and CCl4-administered liver, respectively [ , ]. TRIM26 was found to promote HSC ferroptosis to mitigate CCl4-induced LF via mediating the ubiquitination of solute carrier family-7 member-11 [ ]. With biological effects similar to TRIM26, Smurf2 attenuated CCl4- or bile duct ligation-induced LF via facilitating phosphodiesterase 4B degradation, thus promoting miR-132-mediated inhibition of the expression of connective tissue growth factor [ ].
In addition, in vivo experiments showed similar findings to those from the in vitro LF model, i.e. EMD638683, the inhibitor of SGK1 activation significantly delayed serial pathological changes in CCl4-induced murine LF, including attenuation of hepatic injury and inflammation, lower expression of pro-fibrotic TGFβ1 and intra-hepatic α-SMA, and impaired collagen deposition ( ). In contrast, Nedd4L knockout in the whole organism aggravated the degree of hepatic injury and LF ( ). Collectively, these results suggest that in the whole organism, upregulation of Nedd4L expression or phosphorylation has an effect not only in HSCs but also in the early stages of liver injury, which initiates HSC activation. Similar to our observations, it was reported that Nedd4L deficiency in mice could also result in pulmonary and renal fibrosis, which is associated with increased epithelial sodium channel activity that is regulated by SGK1 [ , ]. Results from are of particular interest as the action of SGK1 in LF induced by different etiologies remains largely unrevealed except that it contributes to the regulation of hepatic gluconeogenesis, hepatic insulin resistance, and liver regeneration [ ]. In human liver tissues with chronic infection due to hepatitis B or C virus, Fillon et al. [ ] found excessive expression of the SGK1 protein in Kupffer cells and inflammatory cells localized within fibrous septum formations, suggesting its pro-fibrotic effect. However, Huang et al. [ ] claimed that the activation of Wnt/β-catenin signaling blunts Foxo3A-induced apoptosis in hepatocytes by maintaining SGK1 expression and activity, thereby attenuating the progression of alcoholic liver disease to LF in a rat model, which suggests the anti-fibrotic effect of functional kinase activity of SGK1 in alcohol-exposed liver. In our study, the role of SGK1 kinase activity in LF was specifically investigated, and it was confirmed that it has a pro-fibrotic effect in primary murine HSCs by regulating the phosphorylation of downstream Nedd4L.
New advances in systems biology have suggested that organismal Nedd4L signaling may be involved in LF by influencing extrahepatic systems, and elucidating system-to-system interactions is critical to understanding the underlying pathogenesis of LF progression or remission in the whole organism. The gut microbiota of patients and murine models of chronic liver disease have been extensively characterized. However, inconsistent conclusions have been reported regarding the changes in the abundance of fecal microbiota and microbial diversity. For example, compared with the control, CCl4 injection upregulates microbial α-diversity while downregulates the abundance of fecal genera Firmicutes and Bacteroides [ ]. In our study, similar α-diversity and a remarkable decrease in the genera Bacteroides and Desulfovibrio, along with a significant increase in Enterorhabdus and slight increase in Lactobacillus, were observed in the CCl4-administered group ( ), which is partly consistent with the findings of previous reports [ ]. It is well known that the functions and relevant influencing factors of gut microbiota are diverse and may be influenced by E3 ligases. For example, TRIM31 knockout increases fecal Prevotellaceae and Veillonellaceae and thus makes C57BL/6J mice susceptible to impaired glucose metabolism [ ]. Deficiency of the E3 ligase Itch aggravates dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-induced colitis by increasing fecal B. vulgatus [ ]. Based on CCl4-induced murine LF, we found that inhibition of Nedd4L phosphorylation caused a relative increase in fecal Bacteroides, Parabacteroides, Erysipelatoclostridium, Blautia, and Klebsiella, whereas Nedd4L ablation caused a remarkable reduction in fecal Lactobacillus, Enterorhabdus, and Bacteroides ( ). It has been reported that gut Lactobacillus and Bacteroides are essential for the homeostasis of the type I interferon (IFNI) response in non-LF diseases and blocking endogenous IFNI signaling promotes bile duct ligation-induced LF [ ]; thus, it is hypothesized that the decrease in gut Lactobacillus and Bacteroides in mice with Nedd4L ablation may aggravate CCl4-induced LF by impairing organismal IFNI homeostasis. Therefore, gut dysbiosis varies with the blockade of the specific organismal E3 ubiquitin ligase in different disease models.
This study has at least three limitations. Firstly, the validation of Nedd4L mediating Smad3 ubiquitination and subsequent degradation in cultured isolated murine HSCs could not be performed. Secondly, we did not perform in vivo experiments with the knockout of the Nedd4L gene in hepatocytes or HSCs. Lastly, the direct causal relationships among altered gut microﬂora, Nedd4L signaling blocking, and CCL4 or other etiologies induced LF progression need to be further elucidated by other methods, such as fecal microbiota transplantation and supplementation or deletion of specific bacteria.

## Conclusions
Altogether, this study demonstrates that hepatic Nedd4L signaling contributes to CCl4-induced liver damage and fibrosis in mice, which is associated with alterations in the gut microbiota, and Nedd4L phosphorylation is involved in TGFβ1-mediated activation of isolated murine HSCs.
