## ABSTRACT

## Aim
Exosome-like nanoparticles mediate intercellular communication and regulate gene expression. In this study, we isolated and purified exosome-like nanoparticles from Salvia miltiorrhiza (SM-ELNs), a traditional Chinese medicinal herb, and investigated their therapeutic effects on diabetic cardiomyopathy (DCM).

## Materials & methods
To investigate the effect of SM-ELNs on DCM, we established a mouse model via HFD/STZ treatment. Cardiac function was assessed by echocardiography. Cardiac hypertrophy was assessed by measuring the heart weight/body weight ratio and HE staining, while myocardial fibrosis was evaluated using Masson’s trichrome staining. The role of SM-ELNs on NLRP3 inflammasome inhibition and macrophage pyroptosis were evaluated both in vivo and in vitro. The interaction between NEDD4 and SGK1 was analyzed by Co-IP and ubiquitination assays.

## Results
SM-ELNs treatment alleviated cardiac function and histopathological changes in DCM mice. Moreover, SM-ELNs suppressed NLRP3 inflammasome activation and subsequent macrophage pyroptosis in both in vivo and in vitro models. Mechanistically, NEDD4 facilitated the ubiquitination and degradation of SGK1 in macrophages. Both NEDD4 depletion and SGK1 addition could counteract the SM-ELNs-induced suppression of NLRP3 inflammasome-triggered macrophage pyroptosis in LPS/ATP-treated RAW264.7 cells.

## Conclusion
Our study provides the first evidence that SM-ELNs inhibit NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated macrophage pyroptosis in DCM by modulating the NEDD4/SGK1 axis.

NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis is a form of inflammatory cell death that contributes to the pathogenesis of DCM [ ]. We further examined whether the cardioprotective effects of SM-ELNs in DCM were mediated by NLRP3 inflammasome inhibition. Upon activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome, downstream proteins such as IL-1β, IL-18, and GSDMD were cleaved to their active forms, which triggered pyroptosis [ ]. As illustrated in , the upregulated mRNA expression of NLRP3, IL-1β, IL-18, and GSDMD in DCM mice was significantly attenuated by SM-ELNs treatment. Moreover, treatment with SM-ELNs distinctly suppressed NLRP3 protein level and the cleavage of IL-1β, IL-18, and GSDMD in DCM mice ( ). Further, immunofluorescence analysis indicated that SM-ELNs treatment distinctly decreased the expression of GSDMD and ASC in macrophages in the heart tissues of mice ( ). Notably, using western blotting, we quantified F4/80 protein expression as a macrophage marker in myocardial tissues from three experimental groups (sham, DCM, and DCM+SM-ELNs). Western blot analysis revealed significantly elevated F4/80 protein levels in DCM mice compared to sham controls, which were partially attenuated by SM-ELNs treatment (Figure S2H). The findings indicated that SM-ELNs inhibited NLRP3 inflammasome-regulated macrophage pyroptosis in DCM mice.

3.3.

## SM-ELNs ameliorated oxidative stress in DCM model mice
Previous studies indicated that oxidative stress activates the NLRP3 inflammasome, which affects the progression of various diabetes-related diseases [ ]. To evaluate the antioxidative effect of SM-ELNs in DCM model mice, we measured MDA levels, SOD activity, and GSH levels in myocardial tissues. As shown in Figure S3A, MDA levels were significantly elevated in the DCM group but decreased after SM-ELNs treatment. Moreover, the impaired SOD activity and GSH levels in DCM mice were significantly rescued by SM-ELNs administration (Figure S3B and C). Collectively, these findings demonstrated that SM-ELNs treatment mitigated oxidative stress in a murine model of DCM.

3.4.

## SM-ELNs inhibited NLRP3 inflammasome-induced macrophage pyroptosis in vitro
To investigate the effect of SM-ELNs on macrophages in vitro, the non-cytotoxic dose of SM-ELNs for RAW264.7 cells was measured using the CCK-8 method. As displayed in , SM-ELNs exhibited nearly no cytotoxic effects on RAW264.7 cells at or below 100 μg/mL. Moreover, CCK-8 assays showed that LPS decreased RAW264.7 cell viability, while SM-ELNs restored it dose-dependently ( ). SM-ELNs at 100 μg/mL showed the most significant improvement in cell viability and were therefore selected for further experiments. RAW264.7 cells were first subjected to SM-ELNs pretreatment. To activate the NLRP3 inflammasome, RAW264.7 cells were primed with LPS (200 ng/mL, 6 h) followed by ATP (2 mm, 1 h) stimulation. As illustrated in , SM-ELNs abolished the LPS/ATP-induced upregulation of NLRP3, IL-1β, IL-18, and GSDMD in RAW264.7 cells. Moreover, SM-ELNs effectively suppressed NLRP3 protein level and the cleavage of IL-1β, IL-18, and GSDMD in LPS/ATP-irritated RAW264.7 cells ( ). Furthermore, flow cytometry further confirmed that SM-ELNs suppressed pyroptosis in RAW264.7 cells ( ). These results revealed that SM-ELNs inhibited NLRP3 inflammasome activation and macrophage pyroptosis in vitro.
 

3.5.

## SM-ELNs restrained macrophage pyroptosis via regulating NEDD4
Prior studies demonstrated that NEDD4 ameliorates myocardial reperfusion injury through suppression of macrophage pyroptosis [ ]. Therefore, we investigated whether SM-ELNs protect against DCM by modulating NEDD4-dependent macrophage pyroptosis. As demonstrated in , NEDD4 expression was enhanced in SM-ELNs-treated RAW264.7 cells. Then, NEDD4 expression was assessed in the DCM model in vitro, and the results demonstrated that LPS decreased NEDD4 expression, while SM-ELNs restored it ( ). These results suggested that NEDD4 May be implicated in the progression of DCM and may hinder its development. Next, we knocked out NEDD4 in LPS/ATP-triggered RAW264.7 cells to investigate the effect of SM-ELNs on macrophage pyroptosis through the regulation of NEDD4. The transfection efficiency was confirmed by RT-qPCR and western blot ( ). As demonstrated in , loss of NEDD4 abolished the effect of SM-ELNs on reduced expression of NLRP3, IL-1β, IL-18, and GSDMD expressions in LPS/ATP-irritated RAW264.7 cells. Moreover, depletion of NEDD4 reversed the inhibitory effect of SM-ELNs on macrophage pyroptosis in LPS/ATP-treated RAW264.7 cells ( ). These findings suggested that SM-ELNs inhibited NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated macrophage pyroptosis through the regulation of NEDD4.
 

3.6.

## NEDD4 facilitated the ubiquitination and degradation of SGK1 in macrophage
To explore the downstream proteins of NEDD4 involved in inhibiting macrophage pyroptosis, we first analyzed the BIOGRID database (https://thebiogrid.org/) and found that SGK1 interacts with NEDD4 ( ). Meanwhile, the UbiBrowser database (http://ubibrowser.bio-it.cn/ubibrowser_v3/) revealed that NEDD4 May be implicated in SGK1 ubiquitination ( ). Therefore, we hypothesized that the effect of NEDD4 on macrophage pyroptosis may be achieved through the regulation of SGK1 level by ubiquitination. To prove our hypothesis, we first overexpressed NEDD4 in macrophages and detected transfection efficiency by RT-qPCR and western blot assays ( ). SGK1 mRNA and protein levels were assessed in macrophage overexpressing NEDD4. The results showed that overexpression of NEDD4 did not alter SGK1 mRNA levels but significantly reduced SGK1 protein levels, suggesting that NEDD4 May regulate the post-transcriptional modification of SGK1 ( ). Co-IP experiments demonstrated that NEDD4 interacted with SGK1 ( ). In addition, CHX chase assays demonstrated that NEDD4 promoted SGK1 degradation ( ). When MG132 suppressed proteasome activity, NEDD4 failed to decline SGK1 protein expression ( ). As indicated in , NEDD4 promoted the ubiquitination of SGK1, thereby reducing its levels. These results revealed that NEDD4 interacted with SGK1 and promoted its ubiquitination.
 

3.7.

## SM-ELNs inhibited macrophage pyroptosis via regulating NEDD4/SGK1 axis
Previous studies have indicated that SGK1 expression is up-regulated in DOX-treated cardiomyocytes, resulting in increased cardiomyocyte apoptosis and myocardial injury [ ]. In addition, inhibition of SGK1 blocked NLRP3 inflammasome activation [ ] and inhibited the pyroptosis of macrophages [ ]. Based on this, we hypothesized that SM-ELNs inhibit macrophage pyroptosis via regulating SGK1 by upregulating NEDD4. We first overexpressed SGK1 in RAW264.7 cells ( ). As shown in , SM-ELNs decreased SGK1 expression in LPS/ATP-triggered RAW264.7 cells, a change that was reversed by the addition of SGK1. Moreover, overexpression of SGK1 reversed SM-ELNs-induced inhibition of macrophage pyroptosis in LPS/ATP-induced RAW264.7 cells, as indicated by elevated levels of pyroptosis-related proteins and an increased pyroptosis rate ( ). These findings indicated the effect of SM-ELNs on NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated macrophage pyroptosis through the regulation of the NEDD4/SGK1 axis.
 

3.8.

## SM-ELNs inhibited PM pyroptosis via modulating NEDD4/SGK1 axis
At concentrations of 100 μg/mL or lower, SM-ELNs exhibited negligible cytotoxicity against PMs, as illustrated in Figure S4A. Moreover, CCK-8 assay revealed that LPS treatment markedly reduced PM cell viability, whereas SM-ELNs enhanced viability in a concentration-dependent manner (Figure S4B). Since 100 μg/mL SM-ELNs exerted the most pronounced effect, this concentration was selected for subsequent experiments. To validate the role of SN-ELNs in modulating NLRP3-driven pyroptosis via the NEDD4/SGK1 pathway in PMs, we first treated PMs with SN-ELNs, followed by LPS priming (4 h) and ATP challenge (5 mm, 1 h). As shown in Figure S4C, LPS/ATP stimulation upregulated NLRP3, IL-1β, IL-18, and GSDMD levels in PMs, an effect that was reversed by SM-ELNs. Moreover, SM-ELNs effectively downregulated NLRP3 protein level and the cleavage of IL-1β, IL-18, and GSDMD in LPS/ATP-irritated PMs (Figure S4D). Flow cytometry analysis further corroborated the inhibitory effect of SM-ELNs on pyroptosis in PMs ( ). Furthermore, SM-ELNs treatment reversed the LPS/ATP-induced decrease in NEDD4 and increase in SGK1 protein levels in PMs ( ). These findings demonstrated that SM-ELNs modulated NLRP3-mediated pyroptosis through the NEDD4/SGK1 axis in PMs.

4.

## Discussion
Despite the increasing incidence and mortality of DCM, our understanding of its underlying mechanisms and effective treatments is still limited. In this study, we demonstrated the occurrence of macrophage pyroptosis and its key role in DCM. In addition, we found that SM-ELNs significantly mitigated the progression of DCM by inhibiting NLRP3 inflammasome-induced macrophage pyroptosis both in vivo and in vitro. Mechanistically, we found that SM-ELNs exerted their protective effects on DCM by upregulating NEDD4, which decreased the level of SGK1 and thereby inhibited NLRP3 inflammasome-triggered macrophage pyroptosis.
As a traditional Chinese medicine, Salvia miltiorrhiza (SM) has been used to prevent tissue damage and organ dysfunction caused by cardiovascular diseases [ ]. Luo et al. indicated that Sal B, derived from SM, has been confirmed to ameliorate DCM via deubiquitinating Smad7 [ ]. Hu et al. revealed that Tanshinone IIA, extracted from SM, suppresses ferroptosis and apoptosis through VDAC1 and has a protective effect on myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury [ ]. Moreover, SM and its extracts have shown therapeutic effects on diabetes-related diseases [ , ]. Based on the benefits of SM extract for the treatment of cardiovascular diseases and diabetes-related diseases, we hypothesized that SM-ELNs may be a potential candidate for DCM therapy. A previous study indicated that SM-ELNs significantly improved EF% and FS% in MI/R mice [ ]. Similar to previous findings, our results also found that SM-ELNs increased EF% and FS% and decreased LVPW.s and LVPW.d in DCM mice. Moreover, SM-ELNs alleviated HFD/STZ-induced cardiac tissue abnormalities and myocardial fibrosis. Importantly, we found the inhibitory effect of SM-ELNs on NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated macrophage pyroptosis both in vivo and in vitro.
To further investigate the potential mechanism by which SM-ELNs regulate macrophage pyroptosis in DCM, we turned our attention to NEDD4. The NEDD4 family, a group of E3 ubiquitin ligases belonging to the HECT class, recognizes various substrate proteins – such as ACE2, SF3A2, TrkA, and PPAR-γ—via its WW domain and subsequently catalyzes their ubiquitination. This process plays a role in various cardiovascular diseases, including hypertension [ ], myocardial infarction [ ], heart failure [ ], and atherosclerosis [ ]. Moreover, Sun et al. reported that NEDD4 ameliorates myocardial reperfusion injury by inhibiting macrophage pyroptosis [ ]. Consistent with previous research, we observed that NEDD4 knockdown enhanced NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated macrophage pyroptosis in LPS/ATP-stimulated RAW264.7 cells. Mechanistically, NEDD4 interacted with SGK1 and facilitated its ubiquitination.
Serum and glucocorticoid-induced kinase 1 (SGK1) is a serine/threonine kinase that belongs to the AGC kinase family [ ]. A previous study reported that SGK1 promotes DOX-induced cardiac inflammation and apoptosis by enhancing NF-κB activity [ ]. Moreover, SGK1 inhibitors, such as herbacetin, can effectively alleviate cardiac hypertrophy [ ]. These data indicated that inhibition of SGK1 May be an effective therapeutic strategy to provide cardiac protection. Besides, the SGK1 inhibitor (EMD638683) inhibited NLRP3 inflammasome activation in cardiac tissues [ ]. Notably, SGK1 facilitated macrophage pyroptosis, thereby exacerbating the progression of rheumatoid arthritis [ ]. Here, we confirmed that SGK1 levels were elevated in LPS/ATP-stimulated RAW264.7 cells, suggesting a facilitating role of SGK1 in DCM. SGK1 reversed the inhibition of macrophage pyroptosis by SM-ELNs in LPS/ATP-treated cells. Our study demonstrates that SN-ELNs inhibit NLRP3-driven pyroptosis and implicates the NEDD4/SGK1 signaling axis in this process. However, the exact molecular cargo within SN-ELNs that mediates these effects remains to be fully characterized. Identifying the active components, such as proteins, RNAs, or small molecules (e.g., Tanshinone IIA or its analogs), would provide deeper mechanistic insights into the immune-regulatory properties of SN-ELNs. Future studies should focus on comprehensive profiling of SN-ELNs’ molecular composition to elucidate the key mediators of pyroptosis inhibition and their potential therapeutic applications.
In summary, our study demonstrates that SM-ELNs effectively ameliorate DCM by inhibiting NLRP3 inflammasome-triggered macrophage pyroptosis through modulation of the NEDD4/SGK1 axis. These findings not only elucidate a novel mechanistic pathway but also underscore the translational potential of SM-ELNs as a promising therapeutic strategy for the prevention and treatment of DCM.

5.

## Conclusion
Collectively, our study unveils a novel mechanistic pathway through which SM-ELNs ameliorate DCM by targeting the NEDD4/SGK1 axis to suppress NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated macrophage pyroptosis. These findings significantly advance our understanding of DCM pathogenesis while providing compelling evidence for SM-ELNs as a mechanistically grounded therapeutic strategy.
