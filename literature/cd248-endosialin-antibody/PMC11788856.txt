•The 30–50 % of patients with advanced breast cancer develop brain metastasis, causing severe damage to their life quality.
•The alteration of macrophage subclusters was identified in the central nerve system after breast cancer metastasis, especially Arg1+ macrophages and Ly6c+ macrophages.
•The metastatic cancer cells re-shaped the TME cellular interactions for immune evasion and nutrition supply.
Keywords: Breast cancer, Brain metastasis, Single cell RNA-seq, Tumor microenvironment

## Abstract
Breast cancer is one of the most common cancers worldwide, 30–50 % of patients with advanced breast cancer develop brain metastasis, causing severe damage to their life quality. Due to the existence of the blood-brain barrier (BBB), brain lesions were recognized to be a unique microenvironment with limited infiltration of circulating immune cells and drugs. However, emerging studies reported the immunology of the brain tumor microenvironment (TME) and indicated the potential of immunotherapy against brain metastases. Therefore, it is of great value to comprehensively investigate the TME and identify the pro-tumoral mechanisms facilitating brain metastases and the crucial molecules involved in this process. In this research, we re-analyzed public data on three brain surgical specimens of breast cancer metastases and identified the immunosuppressive roles of macrophages in the metastatic TME. Then, we conducted the first single-cell RNA sequencing on a murine model of breast cancer brain metastasis. In the brain TME, immune cells showed prominent heterogeneity, especially the mononuclear phagocyte system (MPS). We identified the alteration of macrophage subclusters in the central nerve system (CNS) after breast cancer invasion and found that metastatic cancer cells re-shaped the TME cellular interactions for immune evasion and nutrition supply. Finally, this research could serve as a reference for further analysis of new therapies against brain metastatic lesions.

## Introduction
Breast cancer has risen to become the most common cancer worldwide. Among 4.3 % of Chinese breast cancer patients, the brain is the first metastatic lesion to be detected, and the median survival after brain metastasis is less than two years [ ]. Brain metastasis causes an increase in intracranial pressure and damage to brain parenchymal injury. For a long time, the brain tissue has been regarded to be immune privileged. Thus, treatment targeting breast cancer brain metastasis was mainly based on local therapy including surgery and radiotherapy. However, little was known about the detailed molecular and cellular mechanisms underlying brain metastasis. Moreover, in the process of cancer development, the microenvironment undergoes immune initiation, immune activation, immune exhaustion and immune domestication to facilitate the colonization and proliferation of cancer cells [ , ]. In consideration of the biological functions of various immune components, the TME is pro-inflammatory and anti-tumoral, as well as immunosuppressive and pro-tumoral. This balance occurs in both cell clusters and the interactions of heterogeneous components. Moreover, emerging evidence suggested that circulating immune cells and drugs were able to cross the BBB to perform a therapeutic function. Clinical trials were established to investigate the value of biological agents (NCT04965064, NCT03933982, NCT05357417, NCT03807765, and NCT04334330). Therefore, it is of great value to thoroughly investigate the microenvironment of brain metastases.
Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) is a high-throughput technique to study the transcriptome profile of a certain tissue. Applied in the field of cancer research, scRNA-seq can not only portray the characteristics of single cells, but also identify heterogeneous members in the TME, categorize the biological significance, infer cellular development and differentiation, and analyze the cellular interactions among cell types [ , ]. In this research, we started with public scRNA-seq data on surgical specimens of breast cancer brain metastases. To extend our investigation on TME cells, we established a murine model and performed scRNA-seq and the subsequent bioinformatic analysis. We found the dual aspects of immune cells, filtered out transcriptome features of crucial immune cell subclusters, and found that metastatic cancer cells re-shaped the TME by interacting with immune cells and stromal tissues.

## Materials and methods

## Murine model of breast cancer brain metastasis
Animal studies were performed with the approval of the ethics committee of the affiliated wuxi No.2 hospital of nanjing medical university (2022-Y-226). Six-week mice were randomly assigned for all the experiments. The right common carotid artery of pentobarbital-anesthetized mice was catheterized and the right external carotid artery was ligated with surgical silk. The right common carotid artery catheter was connected to a syringe to perfusion 200ul 0.9 % NaCl with or without 1*105 4T1 cells suspension into the right cardiac ventricle. At the end of the experiment, mice were humanely killed, and their brains were collected to prepare for single-cell suspension or fixed in 4 % formaldehyde for histopathology evaluation.

## Immunofluorescence staining
The immunofluorescence staining was performed to explore the protein expression of Arg1, Ly6c, F4/80 and CD68. The mouse tissues or human tissues were firstly collected for deparaffinization and rehydration while they were conducted by antigen retrieval in the following. The 3 % bovine serum albumin was used to block mouse tissues or human tissues for one hour while the antibodies were applied to incubate tissues at 4 °C overnight. Subsequently, the secondary antibodies were added into tissues for one hour at 37  °C while tissues were counterstained with DAPI in the following. Finally, the inverted microscope was applied to record images. The relevant antibodies were shown as follows: anti-Arg1 antibody (Servicebio, GB11285), anti-Ly6c antibody (Servicebio, GB115601), anti-F4/80 antibody (Servicebio, GB113373) and anti-CD68 antibody (Servicebio, GB113150).

## Hematoxylin and eosin staining
The 4 % paraformaldehyde was used to immerse tissues for four hours while tissues were transferred to 70 % ethanol in the following. The tissues were performed into individual lobes to place in processing cassettes. A serial alcohol gradient was utilized to dehydrate tissues while tissues were embedded in paraffin wax blocks in the following. Before immunostaining, the xylene was used to dewax tissues while decreasing concentrations of ethanol was conducted to rehydrate tissues. Then, the hematoxylin and eosin staining were performed in tissues, which were furtherly dehydrated via increasing concentrations of ethanol and xylene.

## Tissue dissociation and preparation for single cell sequencing
GEXSCOPE™ Tissue Preservation Solution (Singleron) was used to store fresh tissue. The specimens were washed twice with Hanks balanced salt solution and cut into 1–2 mm pieces. Tissue pieces were then digested for 15 min with 2 ml of GEXSCOPE™ Tissue dissociation solution (Singleron Biotechnologies, Nanjing, China) at 37 °C in a 15 ml centrifuge tube with uninterrupted agitation. After digestion, samples were filtered using 40-μm nylon sterile strainers and centrifuged at 350 g for 5 min. Then, the supernatant solution was removed and the sediment was resuspended in 1 ml of PBS (HyClone).

## Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNAseq)
Single-cell suspensions with 1 × 105 cells/ml in PBS (HyClone) were prepared. The suspensions were then loaded onto microfluidic devices and scRNA-seq libraries were constructed according to the Singleron GEXSCOPETM protocol using the GEXSCOPE™ Single-Cell RNA Library Kit (Singleron,1110011). After quality checks, individual libraries were diluted to 4 nM and pooled for sequencing on an Illumina NovaSeq with 150 bp paired end reads.

## Single cell RNA-seq data processing and quality control
Sequencing outputs were demultiplexed to convert BCL files to FASTQ format using bcl2fastq(illumina), sequencing data were processed using the CeleScope pipeline (https://gitee.com/singleron-rd/celescopeSingleron). Adapters and poly A tails were trimmed (fastp V1 Ref) before aligning read two to GRCm38 using Ensemble v.92 gene annotation (STAR v2.6.1a and featureCounts 1.6.2). Reads with the same cell barcode, UMI, and gene were grouped together to calculate the number of UMIs per gene per cell. The UMI count tables of each cellular barcode were used for further analysis.
We use 2 samples for single-cell RNA-seq data. The gene expression matrix was then processed and analyzed by Seurat v3.1.2 package. To reduce the influence derived from contamination and doublet in following analysis, DecontX was used to estimate and remove contamination. Cells were filtered by gene counts below 200 and the top 2 % gene counts and the top 2 % UMI counts. Cells with over 20 % mitochondrial content were removed.

## Dimensionality reduction, clustering, cell-type labeling and visualization
Seurat v3.1.2 was used for dimensionality reduction, clustering and visualization. For each sample dataset, we used the filtered expression matrix to identify cell subsets. The filtered gene expression matrix was normalized using Seurat's NormalizeData function, in which the number of UMIs of each gene was divided by the sum of the total UMIs per cell, multiplied by 10,000, and then transformed to logscale (ln (UMI-per-10000+1)). After data normalizing, highly variable genes were identified and used for the following Principal component analysis (PCA). Harmony v0.1 be use to integrate samples and performed downstream analysis. Subsequently, clustering with 30 principal components and resolution 1.2 was performed by graph-based clustering and visualized using t-Distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (t-SNE) or Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) with Seurat functions RunTSNE and RunUMAP. For the clustering of Microglial cells and Macrophages, the top 30 PCs were selected with a resolution parameter equal to 0.5. Cell types were annotated by SynEcoSys database (Singleron Biotechnologies).
The method returned cell clusters which were then visualized on UMAP space using the DimPlot command. Cell-type-specific canonical gene markers were used to label clusters differentially expressing those markers. To accurately label individual clusters, the Wilcox test was performed to find differentially expressed genes for each cluster. We used the FindAllMarkers function in Seurat to get a list of differentially expressed genes for each cluster. Gene expression was visualized using FeaturePlot, DoHeatMap, and VlnPlot functions from Seuratv3.1.2.

## Differential gene expression analysis and enrichment analysis
Augur (R package to prioritize cell types involved in the response to an experimental perturbation within high-dimensional single-cell data) was performed cells differences between Treatment and Contorl tissues.Differentially expressed genes in a given cell type compared with all other cell types were determined with the FindAllMarkers function from the Seurat package (Wilcoxon rank sum test, p values adjusted for multiple testing using the Bonferroni correction). For computing differentially expressed genes, all genes were probed provided they were expressed in at least 10 % of cells in either of the two populations compared and the expression difference on a natural log scale was at least 0.25. Differentially expressed genes were filtered by |fold change| > 0.25 and FDR < 0.05. For the differential expressed genes of different cell types, genes with p value <0.05 and |logFC| > 0.25 were selected for enrichment analysis. The up-regulation genes and down-regulation genes were analysis separately. The GO and KEGG enrichment analysis were performed by clusterProfiler (3.16.1).The score of specific gene set (by hallmarkgene sets) was generated by UCell (UCell is an R package for evaluating gene signatures in single-cell datasets).

## Single-cell analysis of human breast cancer brain metastases
All steps in the clustering analysis were performed using Seurat v3.1.2. 3 same as Mouses. GSM5645891, GSM5645908, GSM5645892, GSM5645893 have been deposited in the GEO database under accession code GSE186344. Harmony v0.1 be use to integrate samples and performed downstream analysis. Subsequently, clustering with 30 principal components and resolution 0.8 was performed by graph-based clustering and visualized using t-Distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (t-SNE) or Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) with Seurat functions RunTSNE and RunUMAP. For the clustering of Macrophages, the top 30 PCs were selected with a resolution parameter equal to 0.3. Cell types were annotated by SynEcoSys database (Singleron Biotechnologies).

## Single-cell copy-number variation (CNV) evaluation
The CNV evaluation of each cell was conducted by infercnv R package (version 1.4.0; https://github.com/broadinstitute/inferCNV/wiki) . The CNVs of Cancer cells were calculated and the Pericytes cells MSC-like cells and Endothelials cells were applied as the reference. The inferCNV analysis was performed with parameters including “denoise”, default hidden Markov model (HMM) settings, and a value of 0.1 for “cutoff”.

## Gene regulon analysis
Regulon activity scores (RASs) of enriched transcription factors in each single cell were acquired utilizing pySCENIC software based on UMI matrix. The pySCENIC (1.10.0) was used with its command line implementation. pyscenic grn commandwas used with grnboos2 method and default options and a fixed seed to derive co-expression modules between transcription factors and potential targets. The RAS matrices were then submitted to Seurat to investigate the regulatory networks underlying stromal cells. AUCell scores (regulon activities) in each cell were computed with pycenic aucell command (default options). To be noted that number of target genes was highly variable from a regulon to another.

## RNA velocity analysis
RNA velocity is a powerful concept which enables the inference of dynamical cell state changes from seemingly static single-cell RNA sequencing data. The spliced and unspliced reads were counted using the velocyto.py package (v0.17.17) from aligned bam files generated. and fed into thescVelo package (v0.2.2) to calculate RNA velocity values for each gene of each cell. The resulting RNA velocity vector was embedded into the UMAP space.

## Cell-cell interaction analysis
To resolve the cellular communications between different cell clusters of samples, CellphoneDB was implemented to detect the cell interactions on the basis of normalized UMI from Treatment and Contorl, respectively. Significant cell interactions (P value < 0.05 and mean>1.0) were used for further analysis. Cytoscape was used to visualize and analyze network graphs of Cell interaction.

## Statistical analysis and plots
Wilcoxon test were conducted by R language to compare the difference between two samples. P values < 0.05 were considered to be significant. R language was applied to plot heatmaps (by pheatmap package), box plots, bar plots, etc. (by ggplot2 package).

## Results

## Immunosuppressive macrophages in brain metastatic microenvironment of surgical specimens
To better elucidate the transcriptomic characteristics and biological functions of the brain TME, we downloaded and analyzed a scRNA-seq data on three surgical specimens of breast cancer brain metastasis ( A, B). Monocyte/macrophage took the majority of cellular components in the brain TME with high expression of genes including SPP1, APOE, CD74, S100A9, S100A8, CCL4, and IL1B ( C, top 10 DEGs of macrophage in human brain samples). SPP+ macrophages were reported to be an adverse prognostic factor of glioma by supporting angiogenesis to sustain glioma cell survival [ ]. Previous research also reported that SPP+ macrophages help establish an immune-excluded microenvironment via macrophage-fibroblast interaction to reduce immunological recognition, T cell infiltration, and the therapeutic effects of immunotherapy [ , ]. A bioinformatic algorithm established based on large-scale transcriptomic and proteomic data demonstrated that a tumor-specific macrophage subcluster expressing APOE, C1Q and TREM2 was associated with post-surgical recurrence of renal cancer, suggesting that APOE+ macrophages could serve as a predictor to further intervention [ ]. APOE encodes apolipoprotein E (ApoE) as a member of cancer cell metabolism and paracrine substance in the TME. Research on pancreatic cancer demonstrated that by secreting ApoE, TAM interacted with cancer cells via lipoprotein receptor, thus facilitating cancer cells to express chemokines such as CXCL1 and CXCL5, and re-modeling the immunosuppressive microenvironment with less CD8+T infiltration [ ]. CD74 is a membrane protein closely related to antigen presentation and has been recognized as the receptor for MIF to exert immune evasion of cancer cells [ , ]. Studies on lymphoma showed indicated that MIF-CD74 activated NF-κB signaling pathway to support cell proliferation [ ]. Moreover, blockade of MIF-CD74 signaling on antigen-presenting cells including macrophages showed anti-tumoral effects in metastatic melanoma, and this finding was in accordance with ours that CD74+ macrophages could be a potential target for metastatic cancers [ ]. S100A9 and S100A8 influenced macrophage phenotypes and S100A8/S100A9 heterodimer took part in modulating phagocytosis of macrophages under bacterial infection [ ]. Monocyte-derived macrophages expressing CCL4 can interact with cancer cells, therefore, inducing the pro-tumoral STAT signaling pathway and facilitating EMT and stemness in vitro [ ]. Besides, interleukin 1-β (encoded by IL1B) and CCL4 promoted monocyte-endothelial adhesion and increased monocytes infiltrating into the tumor sites [ ].
 Innate immunocytes were clustered into three subclusters including two macrophage subclusters (Macrophage1 and Macrophage2) and a dendritic cell cluster ( D). We further conducted enrichment analysis on DEGs of these subclusters ( E-G). Macrophage1 consisted of the majority of innate immunocytes, had top DEGs of APOE, APOC1, and C1QB, and was most enriched in antigen presentation-related pathways and neutrophil activation. Macrophage2 had top DEGs including S100A8, S100A9, and IL1B, and was most enriched in bacterial infection-related pathways and inflammation. The dendritic cell had top DEGs including FCER1A, CXCR4, and AREG, and was most enriched in mRNA and protein processing, indicating an intense synthesis and activated biological function.

## An overview on brain microenvironment with breast cancer metastasis
Bioinformatic analysis of public data of breast cancer brain metastasis demonstrated transcriptome profiles of TME cells and indicated the importance of macrophages in the anti-tumoral immunity in the brain. Yet under clinical practice, the surgical specimens were mostly composed of tumor tissue and lacking in glial cells and other cellular members in the TME. Therefore, we established a murine model to simulate the pathology of breast cancer brain metastasis.
The brain is one of the common sites of breast cancer metastasis, especially triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC). It was reported that 30 % of patients with metastatic TNBC develop brain metastasis [ , ]. To infer the effects of metastatic breast cancer cells on brain microenvironment, a murine model of TNBC brain metastasis was established ( A), and brain tissues were obtained for scRNA-seq from the experimental group and the control group ( B). After quality control, we identified a total of 10,304 cells in the experimental group where 4T1 cells metastasized to the brain, 9,365 cells in the control group, and used uniform manifold approximation and projection for dimensional reduction and visualization ( C-D). Glial cells made up the majority of normal brain tissue with oligodendrocytes and microglial cells took the greatest parts ( E). In the experimental group, macrophages accounted for about half among all cellular components in metastatic tumor-microenvironment which was over 47 % increase compared with normal, however, oligodendrocytes and microglial cells had 18.42 % and 9.43 % decrease in cell proportion, separately ( F). Violin plot demonstrated the differentiated expressed genes (DEGs) of all 15 cell clusters ( G). In the brain metastases, we also detected that the proportion of mural cells reduced in brain metastases compared with the control group. Mural cells are critical components of the blood-brain barrier, stabilizing the homeostasis of brain microenvironment, and are characterized by marker genes including PDGFRB, RGS5 and CD248 [ ].
 

## Transcriptional profiling of immune cells in brain metastases
Next, we dissected the single-cell transcriptomic characteristics of cellular components in the two brain tissue samples, identified the DEGs and analyzed the enriched biological functions of brains with and without breast cancer metastasis.
Macrophage derived from the brain with breast cancer metastasis highly expressed genes including CX3CR1, ARSB, S100A8, PRDX5 and ATP1A2 ( A). It was reported that in macrophages S100A8 and PRDX5 induced pro-inflammatory features of macrophages [ , ]. Proliferative markers such as ATP1B2 and ATP1A2 were also highly expressed by macrophages in brain metastatic tumor-microenvironment [ ]. With breast cancer metastasis, microglial in the brain microenvironment highly expressed genes including CD74, SAA3, CCL5, and SPP1. It was reported that SAA3 regulated microglial activation [ ], CCL5 promoted neuroinflammation [ ] and SSP1 was secreted by proliferative microglia [ ], suggesting that in the brain with breast cancer metastasis, neuroinflammation-related functions were upregulated compared with the normal brain tissue. Oligodendrocytes are a group of neuroglia that provides support and isolation to axons, and the cell proportion of this cluster underwent a decline after breast cancer cells metastasized to the brains. Compared with the control group, oligodendrocytes from the brain tumor-microenvironment had higher expression of B2M, H2-D1 H2T23, Serpina3, PSMB8, and C4B ( B). Interestingly, previous reports on Alzheimer's disease suggested that Serpina3n+C4b+ oligodendrocytes facilitated amyloid beta accumulation in the brain of murine models [ ], and PSMB8+ oligodendrocytes were reported in multiple sclerosis brain lesion as well [ ]. Dendritic cells (DCs) are important antigen-presenting cells that function as vital bridges between innate and adaptive immunity. Once recognized, DCs take up antigens and presented the neoantigen to CD4+ and CD8+ T cells. On DC cells in the TNBC tumor microenvironment, we detected a high expression of genes not only showed activated immune recognition (including CD209a, Clec4b1, TRF), but also genes that demonstrated active metabolism (such as PKM) ( C). Notably, the immunosuppressive MIF-CD74 signaling pathway was also highly expressed in DCs derived from TME of metastatic TNBC. Notably, similar immunosuppressive markers were also detected in T cells from the brain TME ( D). Besides the MIF-CD74 signaling pathway and PKM which was significantly upregulated by T cells in the brain, other genes related to immune recognition and response were also highly expressed. For example, IFITM3 is a potent protein that strengthened the response of T cells to various pathogens including neoantigen, and the IFITM family also served as regulators of Th1/Th2 polarization during tumor progression [ , ]. SPP1 was also reported to mitigate T cell activation and influence macrophage polarization, which are vital factors for cancer cell immigration.
 Pathway enrichment analysis further revealed on gene-set basis that in the tumor-microenvironment, macrophages were more enriched in pathways including response to interferon-gamma, cellular response to interferon-gamma, ATP metabolic process, and antigen processing and presentation; microglia were more enriched in pathways associated with ribosome biological function and response to interferon-gamma; oligodendrocytes were more enriched in neuroinflammation-related pathways such as response to interferon-beta, antigen processing and presentation, and response to interferon-gamma; DCs were enriched in antigen processing and presentation via MHCII and immune cell proliferation; T cell were enriched in gene translation and cell adhesion in the matrix ( E-G).
Collectively, the transcriptomic characteristics demonstrated the dual effects of TME cells in metastatic sites of the brain. With tumor cell infiltration, TME cells reacted with a stronger immune response, while at the same time, metastatic cancer cells also exerted immunosuppression to the microenvironment. This seemingly paradoxical observation presented the tumor-immune conflict and tumor-immune escape.

## Transcriptional profiling of stromal cells in brain metastases
Fibroblasts made up less than 2 % of cellular components in this murine model of TNBC brain metastasis, yet their transcriptomic characteristics and biological functions shaped the immunosuppressive TME as well. Reports demonstrated that cancer-associated fibroblasts increase the permeability of blood-brain barrier and facilitate metastatic breast cancer cells with better abilities of proliferation and migration [ ]. The top DEGs of the brain metastasis group included LY6A which assists neurotransmission of the nerve system [ ], S100A6 which is associated with cytoskeleton organization [ ], LOX which stimulates collagen synthesis [ ] ( A). Notably, galectin-1, encoded by LGALS1, induces fibroblast to proliferate and synthesize collagen in the connective tissue [ ], while for gastric cancer cells, this protein contributes to EMT via TGF-β1/ Smad pathway, provokes the migration of tumor cells, and is associated with poor prognosis [ ]. The mechanism may be related to microvesicles uptake [ ]. Transcriptomics demonstrated that for the microenvironment with TNBC metastasis, the synthetic function of fibroblasts was stimulated and could facilitate metastasis for TNBC cells ( B).
 To fuel the intense proliferation and metabolic activity, tumor tissues are highly vascular, demonstrating the importance of endothelial cells [ ]. Compared to the TME of the control brain tissues, in the brain tissues with cancer cell metastasis, endothelial cells are more enriched in pathways associated with angiogenesis, protein and amino acids transport and response to hypoxia ( C). Interestingly, endothelial cells derived from brain metastasis have positive regulation of ERK1 and ERK2 cascade. ERK-MAPK signaling is a hallmark of increased angiogenesis and tumor growth, and inhibition to this pathway results in cell death of endothelial cells as well as the retraction of sprouting tubules [ ]. This observation provided potential opportunities for the application and working mechanism of ERK signaling inhibitors.

## Heterogeneity of mononeuclear phagocyte system (MPS) in TNBC immune microenvironment
MPS cells, represented by microglial cells and macrophage, are a major component of innate immunity against antigens. Circulating monocytes are recruited to the TME and make up the main origin of mature macrophages in the tumor microenvironment [ ]. Besides decreased phagocytosis, tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) undergo functional transformation to become immunosuppressive. Classically, macrophages are divided into subpopulations based on phenotypes and anatomical locations, such as Kuffer cells (liver), osteoclast (bone) and microglia (brain). Depending on different physiological and pathological conditions, macrophages take part in immune surveillance, autoimmunity, inflammation, tumorigenesis and fibrosis. In the process of tumorigenesis, macrophages are mainly classified into M1 type which activates tumor-killing mechanisms and M2 type which suppresses tumor-specific immune response and accelerates disease progression [ ].
To further investigate the phenotypic and functional characteristics of macrophages in the TME, we analyzed the transcriptomic profiling of macrophages in the brain tissues with and without TNBC metastasis. Among all macrophages, we identified seven subclusters ( A). Notably, the Siglech+ microglial cells accounted for the majority of MPS subclusters in normal mice CNS, yet none was detected in brain tissues with 4T1 tumor metastasis ( B). For the central nervous system (CNS), microglial cells physiologically present in the parenchyma, macrophages residue in the boundary sites, and monocytes infiltrate upon the damage of blood-brain barrier [ ]. Siglech is a biomarker of CNS microglia, distinguishing this MPS subcluster from infiltrating monocytes and CNS-associated macrophages, whether that residue in the peripheral regions or circulating monocytes infiltrated into the metastatic tumor lesions [ ]. After cancer cell metastasis, the heterogeneity of MPS in the TME has been altered, from a steady state to a more active state, with a significant increased proportion of Arg+ macrophages and decreased proportion of Siglech+ microglial cells ( C). This observation also corresponded with a previous report purposing the microglial replacement with peripheral macrophages in pathological in CNS disorders [ ]. Arg+ macrophage has the highest proportion in the brain tissue with 4T1 metastasis. Arg (also known as Abl2) encodes tyrosine kinase whose activation facilitates cytoskeleton remodeling and foreign antigen engulfment during antiviral response [ , ]. Besides, Arg+ macrophages also have high expression of ACP5, Lyz2, and Cfp, suggesting improved glioma growth [ ] ( D). Another MPS subcluster enriched in metastatic TME is the MKi67+ microglial. Gene set enrichment analysis revealed significant cell cycle progress, and MYC activation in this subcluster ( E-F).
 

## Identifying specific gene sets of MPS in breast cancer brain metastasis
To better understand the phenotypes of MPS cells in the brain TME, we analyzed the transcriptomic characteristics on the level of gene sets. By unsupervised bioinformatics algorithm, we identified ten gene set modules ( A). Module1 was most enriched in pathways related to cell migration, gliogenesis and angiogenesis. Module2 was most enriched in pathways related to antigen processing and presentation, and adaptive immunity. Module3 was most enriched in pathways related to metabolic processes. Module4 was most enriched in inflammation and defense response. Module5 was related to tumor necrosis factor and coagulation process. Module6 was most enriched in cell division-related pathways such as chromosome segregation and organelle fission. Module7 was most related to the activity and functions of ribosomes. Module8 was also related to antigen presentation, but different from Module2, this module is more associated with peptide antigen. Module9 was enriched in reactive oxygen species (ROS) metabolic process and smooth muscle cell proliferation. The enrichment of Module10 was obscure, ranging from neuron death to B cell-mediated immunity.
 To infer the underlying mechanisms of altered macrophages, we analyzed the phenotypes of MPS cells based on the expression of both transcription factors and targeted genes, and the result showed that MPS cells of different origins are quite different in transcriptional regulation ( B). Transcription factors and targeted genes highly enriched in microenvironment without metastasis include Maf, Etv1, and Sox4 ( C-E). Maf as a transcriptional factor could be repressed by IFN-γ and is a regulator during macrophage polarization [ ]. Etv1 was reported to be a determinant to the dual effects of FGF/TGF-β signaling pathway in cancer-associated fibroblasts [ ]. For macrophages, Sox4 transcripts NF-κB pathway and was correlated with lymph node metastasis and poor overall survival [ ]. Macrophages in the brain metastatic TME had the significant expression of transcription factors including Klf5, Stat2. Fosl1, and Fosl2 ( F-H). Notably, Klf5 is a regulator of hematopoietic cells and promotes the output and circulation of myeloid-derived immune cells [ ]. Stat2 assisted immune response based on type I immunocompatible antigen [ ]. The Fos family was reported to take part in regulating cellular transformation and osteogenesis [ ].

## Metastatic cancer cells remodeled cellular interactions in brain TME
To further demonstrate how cancer cells change the microenvironment inside the brain tissue, we used CellPhoneDB software to generate a cell-cell interaction network and inferred the cellular and molecular mechanisms by which cancer cells re-shape the brain microenvironment and promote the colonization of metastatic cancer cells ( A-B).
 Macrophages and microglia showed good plasticity to adapt their phenotypes to the local microenvironment, and cancer cells mainly interact with the MPS cells in the brain TME ( C-D). Cytokines play important roles in functional alterations such as macrophage polarization. Generally, IL1 is a regulator of the innate immune response and chronic inflammation. Recent studies revealed its role in facilitating the immunosuppressive microenvironment during tumorigenesis and progression [ ]. Particularly, we identified the IL1R-IL1-β pair between metastatic breast cancer cells and the largest macrophage subcluster, Arg+ macrophages. This observation was consistent with previous report that IL-1β was a driver of tumor progression by inducing neoangiogenesis, evoking anti-apoptotic signaling pathways and recruiting cancer-associated fibroblasts [ ]. The expression of colony-stimulating factors 3 (CSF3) by metastatic breast cancer cells was identified, and this is the first report indicating that after breaking the blood-brain barrier, cancer cells induced the M2 polarization of macrophages which facilitates an immune-tolerating microenvironment for tumor proliferation. This finding was in accordance with a previous study on TNBC cell lines that CSF secreted by cancer cells is positively associated with CD163+ macrophages and poor overall survival [ ]. Besides the paracrine cytokines, we also figured out ligand-receptor pairs that modulated the immunosuppressive TME. Previous research showed that myeloid-derived cells in brain metastasis had downregulation of CX3CR1 [ ], yet our single-cell RNA sequencing demonstrated the existence of this cellular interaction and its potential role in activating angiogenic macrophages that fuel cancer cell survival [ ]. On the other hand, antitumor immune response was also identified in the metastatic sites. We found that cancer cells release ANXA1 to bind a pattern recognition receptor on the surface of macrophages [ ], thereby promoting antigen presentation after cancer cell invasion.
Glial cells played important roles in the structural and molecular stability of brain tissues. Metastatic 4T1 cells secreted SPP1, which maintained the survival of glial and stromal cells [ ]. Moreover, we first identified that cancer cells interact with microglial and macrophages via SPP1-PTGER4 (also known for EP4) and SPP1-CD44 pairs in brain metastasis. Previous research reported that these interactions triggered M2 polarization of macrophages in hepatocellular carcinoma and exerted neuroprotective effects in the brain [ , ].
For the interaction between cancer cells and endothelial cells, we identified significant expression of the pro-angiogenic VEGFβ-FLT1 pair [ ]. FLT1 was reported to accelerate endothelial proliferation in liver regeneration [ ], and our research first revealed this cellular interaction in breast cancer brain metastasis based on single-cell transcriptome profiling. In addition to promoting angiogenesis of the TME, we also identified that breast cancer brain metastasis strengthened the structural stability to support its proliferation (COL4A1-a1b1 complex). Integrins are transmembrane proteins located on the surface of metastatic cancer cells, and its binding to endothelial-derived collagens stabilized the organization and vascularization of the TME [ ]. CD74 is a component of class II major histocompatibility complex and its interaction with APP was reported in the field of Parkinson's disease [ ], a study on liver cancer also reported the existence of this pair and its potential role in tumor progression [ ], while this is the first time that metastatic cancer cells interact with endothelial cells via CD74-APP pair in the brain TME.

## The infiltrating of Arg1+ macrophages and Ly6c+ macrophages was enhanced in brain microenvironment remodeling by breast cancer metastasis
As to animal model, we acquired mice brains with or without breast cancer metastasis, serving as treatment group and control group respectively. Meanwhile, based on surgical specimens, we acquired human peri-tumor brain tissues and human brain tissues with breast cancer metastasis. Furtherly, we applied the hematoxylin-eosin staining and immunofluorescence analysis to present the infiltrating of Arg1+ macrophages and Ly6c+ macrophages in brain microenvironment remodeling by breast cancer metastasis. According to the brain microenvironment in both mice and human, the infiltrating of Arg1+ macrophages and Ly6c+ macrophages were remarkably increased by the remodeling role of breast cancer metastasis ( A-D).
 

## Discussion
Breast cancer has risen to become the most common cancer worldwide, 30–50 % of patients with advanced breast cancer develop brain metastasis [ ]. In China, 4.3 % of patients suffered from brain metastasis at their first diagnosis of metastatic breast cancer, and the median survival after brain metastasis is less than two years [ ]. Brain metastasis exhibits a series of symptoms by increasing intracranial pressure and damaging brain parenchymal injury, causing severe damage to life quality.
Due to the existence of the blood-brain barrier, most drugs cannot reach the target lesion, not to mention sustain effective concentration in the cerebrospinal fluid. Besides surgery and radiotherapy, conventional treatment to brain metastasis mainly aims to dehydrate and prevent epilepsy. Notably, in the LANDSCAPE trial, lapatinib plus capecitabine showed promising efficacy as first-line treatment of brain metastases of HER2+ cases [ ]. Later, neratinib and pyrotinib came out and showed therapeutic effects for HER2+ breast cancer brain metastasis as well. However, on the whole view, breast cancer brain metastasis is a severe and thorny problem. In addition, BBB established an immunological unique TME composed of CNS-native cells and bone-marrow-derived cells. Therefore, it is an urgent need to simulate breast cancer brain metastasis and comprehensively investigate the TME to identify the pro-tumoral cell cluster facilitating brain metastases and the crucial molecules involved in this process.
In this research, we re-analyzed public data on three brain surgical specimens of breast cancer metastases and identified the immunosuppressive roles of macrophages in the metastatic TME. To better investigate the microenvironment of metastatic breast cancer in the brain, we established a murine model to simulate breast cancer brain metastasis. Based on single-cell RNA sequencing, we identified macrophages and microglial cells upregulated in pro-inflammatory features, dendritic cells upregulated in antigen presentation and active metabolism, and T cells with increased immunosuppression and decreased activation. For stromal cells, fibroblasts and endothelial cells were more active in synthesis and migration to facilitate angiogenesis of the TME. In the brain TME, immune cells showed prominent heterogeneity, especially the mononuclear phagocyte system. We identified a CNS-specific macrophage subcluster highly expressing Siglech. However, after breast cancer cell infiltration, this macrophage subcluster decreased, while Arg+ macrophage and MKI67+ macrophages increased. This observation demonstrated the alteration and replacement of macrophage subclusters after breast cancer cells invading into the brain tissue. As a consequence, macrophages were more active in remodeling the TME. In addition, according to immunofluorescence analysis, the infiltrating of Arg1+ macrophages and Ly6c+ macrophages were remarkably increased in brain TMEby the remodeling role of breast cancer metastasis. Recently, some relevant researches have been reported. According to Jung K, the elevated ARG1 level of primary monocytes-derived macrophages could serve as a biomarker for predicting higher grades of erythema, moist desquamation and CTC grade in early breast cancer [ ]. As to Spallanzani RG, exposure to medroxyprogesterone acetate in mice bearing breast tumors could influence myeloid-derived suppressor cells to restrain tumor immunosurveillance, including CD11b(+)Ly6G(+)Ly6C(int) and CD11b(+)Ly6G(-)Ly6C(high) cells [ ].
Moreover, metastatic cancer cells re-shaped the cellular interactions in the microenvironment to exert immune evasion and to fuel nutrition supply. Various research has paid attention to breast cancer brain metastasis in an attempt to figure out new biomarkers to target the disease progression [ , ], yet few conducted a thorough and comprehensive analysis of the characteristics of the brain microenvironment that facilitated this progress. Previous research on brain metastases of breast cancer was mainly based on statistical analysis in a cohort study. Olivier Graesslin previously studied a large cohort of 362 patients with brain metastatic breast cancer and found that HER2+ subtype was most correlated with brain metastasis than the hormone receptor-positive subtype and triple-negative breast cancer [ ]. In the field of mechanistic research, the immune microenvironment of metastasis undergoes an alteration from an anti-metastatic state to immunosuppressive phenotypes, suggesting the balance between anti- and pro-tumoral states during brain metastasis [ ]. It has been known that the brain microenvironment is different from that of other organs, since BBB hindered the crossing of macromolecular substances and immune cells, the brain tissue is recognized as “immune-privileged”. However, emerging evidence suggested the potential of immunotherapy against brain tumors [ ]. It was reported that microglia and tumor-infiltrating myeloid-derived cells had significant expression of arginase, therefore depleting arginine in the TME and limiting the biological activities of effector cells [ ]. Moreover, the cellular interactions via paracrine, ligand-receptor recognition and even gap junctions also took part in the process of brain metastasis [ ]. These reports were in accordance with our findings that myeloid-derived immune cells presented both inflammatory and immunosuppressive roles in brain metastases, and the cancer-TME interactions facilitated immune tolerance of breast cancer brain metastasis.
However, there are still some limitations in this study. On the one hand, the number of animal models and human tissues was limited with the tendency of data deviation, which should be furtherly expanded. On the other hand, this study was conducted via single-cell sequencing, which was verified by hematoxylin-eosin staining and immunofluorescence analysis. The exploration of underlying mechanism was lacked while experiments in vivo and in vitro were needed for further verification.

## Conclusions
To sum, this research portraited the transcriptome profiles of breast cancer brain metastasis, reporting the balance of activation and suppression on single-cell transcriptome level, and indicating the potential to treat brain metastasis by targeting TME cells.
