## Background
The trans-differentiation of vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) into macrophage-like phenotypes contributes substantially to the advancement of atherosclerotic lesions. However, it remains uncertain whether this trans-differentiation is involved in the early pathogenesis of human coronary atherosclerosis. Given that lipid deposition is a pathological hallmark of early-stage atheroma and that single-cell evidence implicates lipid-processing signatures in VSMC trans-differentiation, it was hypothesized that lipid infiltration critically triggers this process during the early stages of coronary atherosclerosis.

## Methods
Clinical information and lipid profiles were collected from 38 heart transplant recipients. Coronary artery specimens were obtained from their explanted hearts and classified as initial lesions, fatty streaks, or advanced lesions. Immunohistochemical (IHC) staining and immunofluorescence (IF) analyses were performed on the tissue samples to assess lipid infiltration, VSMC phenotype, and trans-differentiation.

## Results
Lipid infiltration and VSMC phenotype switching were observed at the initial lesion stage. IHC and semi-quantitative analysis showed that with increasing lipid infiltration, the densities of foam cells, fatty acid binding protein 4 (FABP4)+ SMCs, CD248+ cells, and CD68+ cells rose significantly, correlating with lesion severity. Moreover, the density of FABP4+ SMCs was positively associated with intimal thickness as well as the densities of CD248+ cells, foam cells, and CD68+ cells.

## Conclusions
Lipid infiltration begins in the early stages of human coronary artery atherosclerotic lesions and may promote trans-differentiation of intimal SMCs into macrophage-like cells, as indicated by expression of the macrophage-associated protein FABP4. These findings provide novel insight into early atherogenesis and may help identify potential targets for timely prevention and intervention in cardiovascular disease.

## Introduction
Coronary atherosclerosis is the culmination of intimal thickening, extracellular lipid deposition, and macrophage accumulation in the arteries, which leads to luminal narrowing and restricted blood flow [ , ]. Plasma-derived lipids and infiltrating macrophages contribute to the progression of early lesions, such as diffuse intimal thickening (DIT) and fatty streaks, to more advanced stages of atherosclerosis, including pathologic intimal thickening (PIT) and plaque formation [ ]. In the normal coronary artery intima, resident macrophages are scarce [ ]. However, vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs), like macrophages of monocytic origin, are also capable of expressing scavenger receptors and transforming into foam cells upon lipoprotein stimulation. In human coronary atherosclerosis, up to 50% of foam cells and about 40% of CD68-positive cells have been shown to derive from smooth muscle cells via phenotypic modulation during mid-to-late lesion development [ , ]. VSMC-derived macrophage-like cells exhibit significant functional differences compared to those derived from monocytes, potentially contributing to the extensive formation of foam cells in the subendothelial layer [ – ].
VSMCs undergo trans-differentiation and express macrophage markers in response to lipid exposure [ , ]. Rong et al. observed downregulation of VSMC antigens and upregulation of macrophage markers (CD68 and Mac-2) after lipid loading in murine aortic smooth muscle cells in vitro, confirming that lipid loading can induce the trans-differentiation of VSMCs into macrophage-like cells [ ]. Wu et al. found that in an animal model, mice fed a high-fat diet exhibited significant trans-differentiation of arterial VSMCs into macrophage-like cells, supporting the role of lipid load in inducing this phenotype switch [ ]. As the primary cell type, the increase in smooth muscle cell (SMC) number and phenotype switching induced by lipid loading should be considered fundamental aspects of foam cell formation and atherosclerosis. In addition to cholesterol uptake, foam cell formation depends on the cells’ ability to release excess cholesterol.
Fatty acid-binding protein 4 (FABP4) is a crucial protein involved in lipid metabolism and immune responses and is primarily found in adipocytes and macrophages. Under normal conditions, VSMCs do not express FABP4. However, single-cell analysis of human coronary arteries by Hu et al. revealed higher levels of FABP4-positive SMCs in plaques compared to healthy tissue [ ].
The mechanisms underlying the progression of early coronary lesions to mid- and late-stage atherosclerotic lesions remain incompletely understood. As the primary cell type in early lesions, the phenotypic switching of SMCs is likely a crucial factor [ ]. Due to the lack of reliable animal models for early lesions, research on these lesions is presently confined to cytological and histological studies of human tissues. Pathological studies have shown that in atherosclerotic lesions, SMCs make up 90–95% of the cellular population. Accounting for nearly 50% of advanced plaques, these cells and their secretory products are implicated in driving both DIT progression and the development of atherosclerotic lesions [ , ]. While VSMCs exhibit phenotypic plasticity, it remains unclear whether this trans-differentiation occurs during the early stages of atherosclerosis, such as DIT and fatty streaks. This study aimed to test the hypothesis that lipid infiltration in early coronary lesions contributes to the phenotypic modulation of VSMCs into macrophage-like cells, potentially involving lipid-handling molecules such as FABP4. Through the application of lipid-specific staining and immunohistochemistry on human coronary artery specimens, the findings provide new evidence for cellular transitions occurring during early human atherogenesis.

## Methods

## Samples and population
Ethical approval was granted by the Ethics Committee of Guangdong Provincial People’s Hospital, and all procedures complied with the Declaration of Helsinki. Written consent was obtained from all participants or their legal guardians. Coronary artery specimens were analyzed from 38 patients who underwent heart transplantation due to idiopathic dilated cardiomyopathy and had histologically confirmed intimal thickening or plaque formation, without evidence of advanced obstructive changes. None of the patients had a history of vasculitis. The patients, aged 6 to 64 years (mean age 43.3 ± 16.8 years), included 31 males and seven females. Specimens were collected from the failing hearts of these patients between September 2020 and June 2023. Coronary tissue samples, each 1 cm in length, were collected from the left anterior descending, left circumflex, and right coronary arteries at 1 cm distal to the respective ostia. The basic and clinical information of these patients are summarized in Table  .

 

## Histological and immunohistochemical examination
The specimens underwent fixation in 10% neutral buffered formalin, followed by paraffin embedding and serial sectioning at 3–5 μm beginning from the proximal end. Histologic sections were then cut at 5 μm, mounted on glass slides, and stained with hematoxylin-eosin (H&E), Masson’s trichrome, and elastica van Gieson (EVG) for visualization.
Immunohistochemistry was performed using specific antibodies: anti-CD68 (ab303565, Abcam, Shanghai, China) for macrophage identification; anti-alpha smooth muscle actin (α-SMA, ab7817, Abcam, Shanghai, China) for smooth muscle cell identification; anti-smoothelin (ab8969, Abcam, Shanghai, China) to characterize the contractile state of SMCs; and anti-apolipoprotein B (apoB, ab20737, Abcam, Shanghai, China) to assess lipid load and foam cells. Additionally, anti-CD248 (60170-1-Ig, also known as TEM1 or endosialin, Proteintech, Wuhan, China) was used to examine phenotypic modulation of VSMCs contributing to coronary atherosclerosis. At the same time, anti-FABP4 (ab13979, Abcam, Shanghai, China) was employed to detect FABP4 expression. After washing, sections were incubated with species-appropriate HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (goat anti-mouse IgG-HRP or goat anti-rabbit IgG-HRP, depending on the host species of the primary antibody) for 1 hour at room temperature. Immunoreactivity was visualized using 3,3’-diaminobenzidine (DAB) as the chromogen, and nuclei were counterstained with hematoxylin.

## Immunofluorescence (IF) staining
Frozen human coronary artery samples were embedded in OCT compound and stored at − 80℃ until sectioning. Serial cryosections (5 µm thick) were cut using a cryostat and mounted on glass slides. After air drying and fixation with cold acetone for 10 minutes, sections were blocked with 5% normal donkey serum containing 0.3% Triton X-100 for 1 hour at room temperature. Primary antibodies against α-SMA, FABP4, CD68, ApoB, oxidized low-density lipoprotein receptor 1 (LOX-1, 11837-1-AP, Proteintech, Wuhan, China), low-density lipoprotein-related protein 1 (LRP1, 26106-1-AP, Proteintech, Wuhan, China), CD36 (85127-6-RR, Proteintech, Wuhan, China), CD31 (GB11063-3-100, Servicebio, Wuhan, China), and VE-cadherin (GB14013-50, Servicebio, Wuhan, China) were applied overnight at 4℃. Fluorescent secondary antibodies were used for detection. Nuclei were counterstained with 4’,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI), and slides were mounted with antifade reagent. For neutral lipid staining, adjacent cryosections were incubated with Nile Red solution (1 µg/mL in 75% glycerol/PBS) for 15–30 min at room temperature in the dark, followed by brief PBS rinsing and nuclear counterstaining with DAPI. Slides were mounted with antifade reagent and imaged immediately. Imaging was performed using a Leica fluorescence microscope, and co-localization was assessed with ImageJ software (https://imagej.nih.gov/ij/).

## Pathological diagnostic criteria and semi-quantitative assessment
The pathological diagnostic criteria for coronary lesions followed the Virmani classification and used the American Heart Association (AHA) classification as a reference [ ]. Histologically, initial lesions were defined as the normal accumulation of SMCs in the intima without obvious lipid or macrophage foam cells. Fatty streaks were characterized by luminal accumulation of foam cells without a necrotic core or fibrous cap. Advanced lesions were characterized by a well-defined necrotic core, often accompanied by extensive calcification and a thick fibrous cap, and they could include cholesterol crystals, inflammatory infiltration, and signs of previous plaque rupture or hemorrhage.
To characterize the lesions, intimal and medial thickness were measured, and the intima/media (I/M) ratio was calculated. To elucidate the characteristics of intimal cells, the density of macrophage-like cells (CD68+), foam cells, CD248+ cells, and FABP4+ SMCs (FABP4+ and α-SMA+ double-positive cells) was determined through immunohistochemical semi-quantification. For each of the 38 coronary artery specimens, three serial paraffin sections were obtained from the proximal segment of the same anatomical site. Immunohistochemical staining was performed on each section. Semi-quantitative analysis was performed on three high-power fields (400×) per section, chosen from areas with representative intimal lesions. The number of positively stained cells was manually counted using ImageJ software, and the average density (cells/mm2) was calculated across the three sections for each marker per patient. In addition, correlation analysis was performed to evaluate the relationships between FABP4+ SMC density, lesion severity, and the densities of other cell types.

## Statistical analysis
Data are presented as mean ± SD. Categorical variables were presented as counts and percentages, and continuous variables as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median with interquartile ranges (IQR), depending on data distribution. Chi-squared or Fisher’s exact tests were used for categorical comparisons, and Student’s t-test or Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables, based on the distribution of the data. For histological and immunohistochemical semi-quantitative analyses, normally distributed data were evaluated with t-tests, and non-normally distributed data with Mann–Whitney U tests. Correlation analysis was conducted using Pearson or Spearman correlation coefficients, as appropriate. A P-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results

## Basic information and histological classification
To investigate the effect of lipid infiltration on the phenotypic switch of human coronary SMCs, as well as changes in FABP4 expression, a thorough analysis of histological staining results obtained from coronary artery tissue excised from 38 heart transplant recipients (31 males, 7 females; average age 43.3 ± 16.8 years) was conducted (Table  ). The tissue sections were categorized into three groups based on their histological staining characteristics: initial lesions (n = 24), fatty streaks (n = 42), and advanced lesions (n = 48). Both initial lesions and fatty streaks were categorized as early-stage lesions following the AHA classification criteria [ ]. The tissue sections underwent staining procedures utilizing H&E, EVG, and Masson’s trichrome methods (Fig.  ). Initial lesions primarily exhibited diffuse intimal thickening devoid of lipid infiltration, with preserved elastic layers. Fatty streaks were characterized by lipid accumulation and foam cell formation within the vessel intima. Advanced lesions represented the most severe type, displaying plaque formation, complete disruption of the elastic fiber layer, intimal necrotic core formation, and extensive collagen deposition. Examination of the intima-media boundary under H&E staining magnification revealed few round or oval cells in initial lesions, some containing lipid droplets in the cytoplasm, while medial cells maintained a spindle-shaped morphology, indicative of the contractile SMC phenotype. In the fatty streak group, numerous round or oval cells were observed in the intima and media, with a loss of the spindle-shaped morphology characteristic of SMCs, although spindle-shaped cells were still present in the outer layer. In advanced lesions, the boundary between the intima and media was indistinct, with significant intimal thickening. The area showed extensive infiltration of inflammatory cells and the formation of lipid vacuoles. The intima-media contained numerous round or oval cells with few spindle-shaped cells, along with abundant fibrin and collagen deposition.

 

## Cellular composition of the tunica intima and SMCs phenotypes
Immunohistochemical staining (IHC) for smooth muscle cell antigen (α-SMA), contractile SMC antigen (smoothelin), CD248 (a transmembrane receptor characterized as an activation marker of cells including VSMCs, fibroblasts, and pericytes etc.), and the macrophage antigen (CD68) was performed on coronary artery specimens (Fig.  [A1–4, B1–4, and C1–4]). The results revealed abundant expression of α-SMA in the intima and media of coronary arteries at different pathological stages, suggesting that SMCs are the predominant cell type throughout lesion progression from initial to advanced stages. In initial lesions, α-SMA expression was localized near the middle layer of the intima, accompanied by some CD248-positive cells. In the intima of fatty streaks and advanced lesions, the number of CD248-positive cells appeared to increase notably, indicative of an upsurge in secretory SMCs. Morphologically aberrant cells expressing contractile antigens were noted at the intima-media boundary in the fatty streak group. In advanced lesions, there was a marked decrease in expression of contractile antigens in the intima, with minimal expression near the media, where the normal smooth muscle cell layer had been disrupted. Initial lesions showed no evident CD68-positive cells in the intima, while fatty streaks and advanced lesions exhibited substantial CD68-positive staining.

 

## Lipid load induces SMCs trans-differentiation into macrophage-like cells
Compared to initial lesions, a marked increase in FABP4-positive SMCs was observed within the intima of fatty streaks, with these cells generally exhibiting normal nuclear morphology (Fig.  : A5, B5, and C5). In advanced lesions, their number further increased, indicating a progressive accumulation with disease severity. Anti-ApoB immunostaining revealed the deposition of apolipoprotein B-containing lipoproteins, suggesting extracellular lipid infiltration (Fig.  : A6, B6, and C6). Furthermore, comparison of FABP4 and ApoB staining patterns revealed a consistent spatial distribution, suggesting a close association between FABP4-positive SMCs, lipoprotein deposition, and lipid accumulation. Semi-quantitative analysis revealed a progressive increase in the density of CD248-positive cells (Fig.  D), CD68-positive cells (Fig.  E), foam cells (Fig.  F), and FABP4-positive SMCs (Fig.  G) with advancing lesion severity, with statistically significant differences observed between stages.
To confirm the identity of FABP4-positive SMCs, co-immunofluorescence staining for α-SMA and FABP4 was performed (Fig.  [A1–3, B1–3, and C1–3]). Corresponding semi-quantitative analyses revealed a decreasing trend in α-SMA expression and a concomitant increase in FABP4 levels with advancing lesion severity (Fig.  : D&E).

 

Nile Red staining detected intracellular neutral lipids and cholesterol esters, reflecting cellular lipid accumulation and foam cell formation (Fig.  : A1-3). Anti-apoB and Nile Red staining of lesions at various stages demonstrated a significant increase in lipid load within the intima-media with escalating lesion severity. To further explore the cellular context of lipid uptake, an additional immunofluorescence double staining of ApoB with CD68 and FABP4 in lesions of varying severity was performed. These analyses demonstrated partial co-localization of ApoB with CD68-positive macrophage-lineage foam cells and with FABP4-positive smooth muscle cells, particularly in fatty streaks and advanced lesions, indicating that both cell types are involved in the incorporation of ApoB-containing lipoproteins (Fig.  ). Furthermore, to address the involvement of other lipid-handling receptors, co-staining of ApoB with LOX-1 (Fig.  : B1–3), LRP1 (Fig.  : C1–3), CD36 (Fig.  : D1–3), CD68 (Fig.  : E1–3), and FABP4 (Fig.  : F1–3) was conducted. In the early stages, ApoB predominantly co-localized with LOX-1, LRP1, and CD36 within the endothelial layer. As ApoB infiltration progressed, these co-localization signals extended into the intima–media junction and even the medial layer. In contrast, the co-localization of ApoB with CD68 and FABP4 became prominent only in the advanced stage (Fig.  : G1-3). These markers showed overlapping distribution with ApoB in intimal regions enriched with foam cells and phenotypically modulated SMCs, suggesting a multifactorial lipid uptake mechanism. Co-localization of ApoB with endothelial markers CD31 (Fig.  : H1-3) and VE-cadherin (Fig.  : I1-3) was observed in the early stage of atherosclerotic lesions, with signal intensity and distribution gradually extending from the endothelial layer into deeper regions of the vessel wall as lesion severity increased.

 

To investigate the relationship between FABP4+ SMCs and lesion severity, as well as SMC trans-differentiation, a correlation analysis was conducted between the density of FABP4+ SMCs and intimal thickness, CD248+ cell density, foam cell density, and CD68+ cell density. The results revealed a strong positive correlation of FABP4+ SMC density with intimal thickness (r = 0.76, P < 0.001), CD248+ cell density (r = 0.86, P < 0.001), foam cell density (r = 0.89, P < 0.001), and CD68+ cell density (r = 0.86, P < 0.001) (Fig.  ).

 

## Discussion
Mechanistic studies and drug interventions for atherosclerosis have predominantly relied on animal models such as mice, rats, pigs, and rabbits, established through high-fat, high-sugar diets supplemented with arterial endothelial balloon injury, gene engineering, or plaque transplantation [ , ]. However, these lesions, primarily resulting from acute intimal injury combined with rapid growth and lipid accumulation at lesion sites, markedly differ from the pathophysiological process of human atherosclerosis, thereby creating a gap in reliable animal models for early lesion research. Previous studies on early atherosclerotic lesions by the Milei and Nakashima teams have heavily depended on human coronary artery samples [ , , ]. Nevertheless, due to significant challenges in obtaining human samples, particularly coronary arteries, research progress on early human coronary artery lesions remains sluggish. To further investigate the mechanisms underlying the occurrence and development of early human coronary artery intimal lesions, rare human coronary artery samples representing various degrees of lesions were utilized for analysis.
Histological, immunohistochemical, and immunofluorescence staining analyses were performed on coronary artery specimens obtained from 38 heart transplant recipients, confirming the occurrence of lipid infiltration in the early stages of atherosclerotic lesions, which progressively worsens with lesion development. From initial to fatty streak lesions, there was a progressive increase in intimal thickness, a significant augmentation in the number of SMCs at the intima-media junction, and a loss of normal spindle-shaped morphology, leading to irregular shapes. As the lesions advanced, the intima exhibited plaque formation, fibrosis, and necrosis, accompanied by disrupted elastic fibers and collagen accumulation.
Lipid accumulation within the vascular wall was evaluated using ApoB immunostaining and Nile Red staining. Immunostaining for ApoB identifies lipoproteins bearing apolipoprotein B, mainly low-density lipoproteins (LDL), reflecting the presence of extracellular atherogenic lipids. Nile Red staining identifies intracellular neutral lipids and cholesterol esters, indicative of lipid droplet formation and foam cell development. Both ApoB and Nile Red staining revealed a progressive increase in lipid deposition correlating with lesion severity, consistent with findings reported by Nakagawa et al. [ ]. Immunofluorescence co-localization showed that the trans-differentiation of smooth muscle cells into macrophage-like cells was accompanied by progressive lipid infiltration. ApoB showed partial co-localization with CD68 and FABP4 in the intima, supporting the presence of both extracellular and intracellular lipid accumulation within foam cells and SMC-derived populations. In addition to FABP4, ApoB also co-localized with LOX-1, CD36, and LRP1—key receptors mediating the uptake of modified lipoproteins—further validating the active involvement of these cells in lipid handling. With lesion progression, the expression levels of ApoB, CD68, FABP4, CD248, LOX-1, CD36, and LRP1 all increased markedly in the intima, consistent with enhanced lipid uptake and the phenotypic modulation of multiple intimal cell types.
Morphological and quantitative changes were observed in intimal SMCs at very early stages of coronary artery lesions. The selective upregulation of CD248 in activated, non-contractile VSMCs during atherosclerosis supports its use as a marker of VSMC phenotypic modulation in early coronary lesions [ ]. Staining for α-SMA, smoothelin, and CD248 revealed that SMCs had undergone phenotype switching, leading to the loss of their normal cellular morphology and basic contractile function. Previous studies have suggested that upon phenotypic modulation, SMCs may secrete characteristic proteins or express receptors to execute corresponding cellular functions, thereby promoting atherosclerotic progression [ , ]. Pan et al. proposed that atherosclerosis shares characteristics with neoplastic diseases, largely driven by SMCs. These SMC-derived cells exhibit tumor-like behaviors, including genetic instability, bypass of senescence, uncontrolled proliferation, apoptosis resistance, and enhanced invasiveness [ ]. Under conditions of cholesterol enrichment and inflammatory cues, VSMCs actively proliferate and invade the intimal layer, with a subset undergoing trans-differentiation into macrophage-like cells, promoting plaque development [ ]. These studies are often conducted at the cellular and animal model levels, lacking human histological evidence and results on early lesions. The association between the phenotypic switching of intimal SMCs in early coronary artery lesions and the abundance of CD68-positive cells in mid-to-late-stage lesions remains unclear.
The macrophage-mediated inflammatory response is a key factor in atherosclerotic plaque formation. Anti-CD68 staining of lesions at different degrees revealed almost no CD68-positive cells in initial lesions, while fatty streaks and advanced lesions, particularly around atherosclerotic plaques, exhibited abundant CD68-positive cells. This staining pattern is consistent with the AHA classification of atherosclerotic lesions. Single-cell analysis has revealed that during atherosclerosis, SMCs typically switch into an intermediate cell state with multipotency, capable of differentiating into macrophage-like or fibrocartilage-like cells, while lacking CD68 expression in early differentiation stages [ ]. These findings demonstrate that in the initial lesions, there were no evident CD68-positive cells in the intima. However, the observed morphological changes and altered expression of SMC-specific antigens may indicate a potential trans-differentiation of SMCs, as CD68 positivity appeared in these cells during lesion maturation.
FABPs constitute a superfamily of intracellular lipid-binding proteins that are essential for the uptake, intracellular transport, and metabolism of long-chain fatty acids across diverse tissues. Among these, adipocyte-type FABP, also referred to as FABP4 or aP2 (adipocyte protein 2), is predominantly expressed in adipocytes and macrophages, where it markedly enhances the cell’s lipid-binding capacity [ – ]. Increased circulating levels of FABP4 have been observed in clinical conditions such as insulin resistance, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, and atherosclerosis [ – ]. Normal SMCs do not express FABP4 [ ]. The staining results show that in the early stages of lesions, FABP4-expressing SMCs are present in the intima, and as lesions progress, the number of FABP4+ SMCs, foam cells, and the intimal lipid burden significantly increases. Specifically, it was revealed that FABP4 is expressed not only in traditional macrophage-lineage foam cells but also in smooth muscle cells exhibiting trans-differentiation toward a macrophage-like state.
FABP4 is a functional protein of macrophages, whose increased expression can significantly enhance cellular lipid-binding capacity, leading to intracellular cholesterol accumulation and foam cell formation. It is believed that in very early stages, intimal SMCs begin to undergo phenotypic switching, with some cells partially trans-differentiated into macrophage-like phenotypes. Although not expressing CD68, these cells express FABP4, promoting lipid deposition.
It was discovered that FABP4+ SMCs appear in human coronary arteries during early atherosclerotic lesions, and their numbers increase significantly with lesion progression. Correlation analysis showed a strong positive association between FABP4-positive SMC density and lesion severity, as reflected by intimal thickness, as well as with markers of SMC phenotype modulation, including CD248-positive, foam, and CD68-positive cell densities. The results indicate that FABP4+ SMCs represent a previously unrecognized transitional phenotype, capable of lipid uptake and foam cell transformation in early atherosclerosis. These cells likely contribute to the formation of lipid-rich lesions and may serve as both markers and mediators of early disease progression. Combined with the results of Rong et al.‘s in vitro cell study [ ], it can be inferred that lipid-induced SMC phenotype switching is an important factor in the progression of early lesions. Studies have found that macrophage-like cells derived from SMCs exhibit significant functional differences from macrophages; these cells are more prone to foam cell formation after lipid ingestion and cannot be cleared normally through autophagy or apoptosis. Such differentiated cells are increasingly valued in the process of atherosclerosis development, forming a new research focus.

## Strengths and limitations
This study focuses on early-stage human coronary atherosclerotic lesions, providing direct histological and immunohistochemical evidence of lipid infiltration and VSMC phenotypic modulation. The use of lipid-specific staining (Oil Red O and anti-ApoB) and immunolabeling reveals the presence of lipid deposits in early lesions, coinciding with changes in VSMC marker expression. The observed co-localization of α-SMA and FABP4 suggests a potential link between lipid accumulation and VSMC trans-differentiation into macrophage-like cells.
However, this study is based on observational analysis of human coronary artery samples and does not allow direct investigation of underlying mechanisms. The potential roles of FABP4 in VSMC proliferation, migration, and signaling require further experimental validation. Moreover, while the derived data suggest an association between lipid infiltration and VSMC phenotypic modulation, a direct causal relationship cannot be established. Inflammatory, oxidative, or metabolic factors may also contribute. Future studies using in vitro models and pathway inhibition will be needed to clarify the mechanisms involved.

## Conclusion
Lipid infiltration occurs early in human coronary atherosclerosis and may promote intimal SMC trans-differentiation into macrophage-like cells, as indicated by FABP4 expression. Targeting FABP4 or lipid-handling pathways might offer new avenues for early diagnosis, prevention, and treatment. Further functional studies are needed to explore their clinical applications.
