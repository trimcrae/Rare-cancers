## Abstract
Pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors (PanNETs) are rare malignancies of the pancreas, but their incidence is steadily increasing. Standard therapy with antiangiogenic inhibitors, including sunitinib, has shown clinical benefit for advanced PanNETs; however, its long-term effectiveness is limited due to the development of resistance. In this study, we demonstrate that targeting the CD93-IGFBP7 axis enhances the efficacy of sunitinib by normalizing tumor vasculature in PanNETs. Both CD93 and its ligand IGFBP7 are enriched in the tumor microenvironment (TME) of PanNETs. Disrupting the CD93/IGFBP7 interaction with monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) in RIP1-Tag2 mice normalizes tumor vasculature to inhibit tumor progression and metastasis. Combining anti-CD93 mAb and sunitinib synergistically slows tumor growth and improves survival. Mechanistically, CD93 blockade mitigates sunitinib-induced tumor hypoxia and invasiveness, preventing the upregulation of proangiogenic factors. Critically, anti-CD93 mAb treatment prolongs survival in RIP1-Tag2 mice with late-stage, sunitinib-resistant PanNETs. Our results support CD93 blockade as a promising therapeutic approach for advanced PanNETs.
Keywords: CD93 blockade, IGFBP7, vascular normalization, pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor, sunitinib, antiangiogenic, tumor hypoxia

## Graphical abstract
 
The CD93/IGFBP7 axis is enriched in pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors (PanNETs) and blockade of this pathway with monoclonal antibodies normalizes tumor vasculature to inhibit cancer progression and metastasis. In addition, CD93 blockade treats advanced PanNETs that are otherwise unresponsive to sunitinib by inducing hypoxia and preventing the induction of proangiogenic factors.

## Introduction
Pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors (PanNETs), also known as islet cell tumors, are a rare but increasingly prevalent form of pancreatic cancer, accounting for 1%–2% of pancreatic neoplasms. , As PanNETs are highly vascularized tumors, antiangiogenic agents such as sunitinib have emerged as Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved therapies for patients with advanced PanNETs. , , However, the overall survival benefit from these therapies is limited, and the efficacy of these therapies remains modest. Rapid progression and tumor recurrence after initial responses to vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) inhibitors are well documented, likely due to preexisting or induced compensatory proangiogenic signaling. , , , Consistent with this, mechanistic studies in preclinical PanNET models show that prolonged VEGF inhibition promotes tumor invasiveness and metastasis, which is often driven by exacerbated intratumoral hypoxia resulting from excessive pruning of the tumor microvasculature. , Thus, there is a critical need for more effective therapies for advanced PanNET.
The CD93 pathway is a recently identified surface interaction that is enriched in the tumor microenvironment (TME) and contributes to tumor vascular dysfunction in solid cancers. CD93 is a member of group 14 of the C-type lectin-like domain (CTLD) superfamily and is predominantly expressed on endothelial cells and is involved in tumor angiogenesis and vascular dysfunction. , , The ligand insulin-like growth factor binding protein 7 (IGFBP7) is a secreted protein that is broadly expressed in cancer stromal cells. In addition to functioning as a CD93 ligand facilitating tumor angiogenesis and invasiveness, IGFBP7 has been reported to suppress tumor cell proliferation. , Blockade of the CD93/IGFBP7 interaction normalizes tumor vasculature to promote a favorable TME, thereby slowing tumor progression in a series of implanted mouse tumor models. Unlike VEGF inhibitors that trim tumor microvessels to cause hypoxia in the TME, blockade with anti-CD93 monoclonal antibody (mAb) improves tumor vascular maturation and function to maintain normoxic conditions within the TME. , 
We found that PanNET is a unique tumor type in which cancer cells constitutively produce IGFBP7. We hypothesized that targeting the TME-enriched CD93/IGFBP7 pathway would normalize PanNET vasculature, delay resistance to sunitinib—a VEGF-targeting therapy commonly used in advanced PanNET— and effectively treat advanced PanNETs that have developed resistance to sunitinib. Using an RIP1-Tag2 mouse model, a genetically engineered model of PanNET, , we evaluated the therapeutic potential of CD93 blockade.

## Results

## The CD93-IGFBP7 axis is highly expressed in the TME of PanNETs
We examined the expression of the CD93/IGFBP7 axis in human PanNET tissue samples from the University of Colorado Cancer Center. IGFBP7 expression was significantly higher in tumor vasculature than in adjacent normal pancreas in which islet cells are the main source of IGFBP7 ( A). Besides tumor microvessels, cancer cells in PanNET, which presumably derive from islet cells, constitutively expressed IGFBP7, though at different expression levels ( A and A). Expression of cancer cell-derived IGFBP7 was associated with PanNET stage and differentiation, with lower IGFBP7 levels observed in larger, poorly differentiated, and highly proliferative late-stage tumors ( B and B). Like we previously reported, CD93 expression was profoundly upregulated in blood vessels within PanNET tumors, in comparison with normal microvessels in adjacent normal tissues ( C).
 We confirmed the enrichment of the CD93/IGFBP7 pathway in islet tumors from RIP1-Tag2 mice, a genetically engineered mouse model of PanNET. These mice start to develop insulinoma at the age of 8 weeks old. In pancreas from wild-type mice, IGFBP7 was exclusively expressed by islet cells. However, both microvessels and islet-transformed cancer cells within Rip1-Tag2 tumors were highly positive for IGFPB7 ( C). The levels of cancerous IGFBP7 in Rip1-Tag2 tumors tended to be affected by tumor size and the progression of tumors ( D and D), similar to what we have observed in human PanNET. CD93 was highly expressed by tumor-associated blood vessels, while its expression was negligible in those outside of the tumors ( E).

## Blockade of the CD93/IGFBP7 interaction normalizes tumor vasculature and slows tumor progression in RIP1-Tag2 mice
The enrichment of CD93 and its ligand IGFBP7 in PanNET led us to investigate the impact of the CD93 pathway using the RIP1-Tag2 mice. We treated RIP1-Tag2 mice with a mouse CD93 neutralizing mAb (clone 7C10) at the age of 10 weeks ( A), when the mice had developed small encapsulated tumors. We measured serum glucose to monitor tumor progression in RIP1-Tag2 mice. RIP1-Tag2 mice without treatment exhibited a gradual decline in serum glucose levels below 50 mg/dL, consistent with insulinoma progression. In contrast, mice treated with anti-CD93 initially showed a spike in blood glucose levels following antibody administration, which then remained above 60 mg/dL throughout the observation period ( B). Median survival for the control group was 14.4 weeks compared with 16.6 weeks for the group treated with anti-CD93 mAb ( C). To further investigate the therapeutic effects of CD93 blockade, we analyzed pancreatic tissues from RIP1-Tag2 mice after 2 weeks of anti-CD93 treatment. Tumor burden was significantly reduced by anti-CD93 treatment, as evaluated by both tumor count and tumor-covered area in the pancreas ( D). Immunofluorescent staining of tumor vasculature for CD31 and alpha-smooth muscle actin (αSMA) indicated that anti-CD93 significantly improved pericyte coverage ( E). To assess tumor dissemination, we stained SV40 T antigen (Tag) to enumerate cancer cells in peripancreatic lymph nodes (LNs). The numbers of macroscopically visible tumor nodules in the anti-CD93 treated group was reduced to less than 40% of the control group ( F).
 The high abundance of CD93 ligand IGFBP7 in RIP1-Tag2 tumors prompted us to test whether blockade of IGFBP7 could similarly inhibit tumor progression in RIP1-Tag2 mice. We observed a comparable antitumor effect as targeting CD93 when we treated RIP1-Tag2 mice at 10 weeks old with the IGFBP7 mAb (clone 2C6), which we previously reported, and blocks the IGFBP7/CD93 interaction. As shown in G and 2H, anti-IGFBP7 was able to extend the median survival of RIP1-Tag2 mice over a week, from 12.8 weeks to 14.0 weeks. Consistently, anti-IGFBP7 mAb therapy led to a modest improvement in pericyte coverage ( ). Together, our findings demonstrate that targeting either CD93 or its ligand IGFBP7 abrogates tumor progression and prolongs survival in RIP1-Tag2 mice.

## CD93 blockade synergistically inhibits RIP1-Tag2 tumor progression with sunitinib
Sunitinib is an FDA-approved tyrosine kinase inhibitor used in the treatment of advanced PanNET and its antitumor effect for PanNET has been extensively investigated in RIP1-Tag2 mice. , To evaluate the combinatory therapeutic potential, we compared anti-CD93 and sunitinib individually and in combination in RIP1-Tag2 mice. We initiated the treatments of anti-CD93 and sunitinib in 12-week-old RIP1-Tag2 mice, when mice harbored a substantial tumor burden in the pancreas ( A). Without therapy, control RIP1-Tag2 mice quickly succumbed to hypoglycemia, and all mice died from tumor progression within 16 days ( B and 3C). Both anti-CD93 and sunitinib were able to maintain serum glucose levels above 50 mg/dL, and more than 50% of mice in both groups survived over 30 days upon therapy ( C). Consistently, examination of RIP1-Tag2 pancreas after 10 days of therapy revealed that both anti-CD93 and sunitinib significantly reduced tumor burden; the combination of these two agents was able to further shrink tumors in the pancreas ( D), as assessed by tumor area ( E) and tumoral counts ( F).
 Although anti-CD93 mAb and sunitinib exhibited similar antitumor effect in RIP1-Tag2 mice, they had a distinct impact on tumor vasculature ( G). The treatment of anti-CD93 did not affect vessel density within tumors while microvessels in sunitinib-treated tumors became visibly sparse ( H). Both anti-CD93 mAb and sunitinib promoted tumor vascular maturation, as assessed by pericyte coverage of blood vessels within the tumors ( I). Interestingly, the inclusion of anti-CD93 protected blood vessels within tumors from trimming by sunitinib ( H). As a result, tumors in the combination group were abundant with mature microvessels that were well covered with αSMA+ pericytes ( I).

## CD93 blockade reduces sunitinib-induced invasiveness in RIP1-Tag2 tumors
Targeting tumor vasculature has a direct impact on tumor hypoxia. We stained tumor tissues for glucose transporter 1 (GLUT1) to assess tumor hypoxia. , RIP1-Tag2 tumors treated with sunitinib alone became hypoxic ( A), as revealed by their strong expression of GLUT1. In contrast, CD93 blockade itself improved tumor oxygenation, and co-administration of anti-CD93 alleviated the intratumoral hypoxia induced by sunitinib treatment ( A). This effect was further supported by quantitative RT-PCR analysis of tumor tissues, which showed reduced expression of hypoxia-associated genes HIF-1α and GLUT1 ( B). As previously described, many pro-angiogenetic factors, including at least angiopoietin-2 (ANG2), matrix metalloproteinase-9 (MMP9), and hepatocyte growth factor (HGF), were induced in RIP1-Tag2 tumors under the treatment of sunitinib ( B and ). Their protein expression changes in tumor tissues were further validated by immunofluorescent staining ( C). CD93 mAb alone had no impact on the expression of these pro-angiogenic factors. Moreover, the inclusion of anti-CD93 significantly lowered the expressions of these molecules induced by sunitinib ( B, 4C, and ). Thus, CD93 blockade inhibits the expressions of pro-angiogenetic factors caused by sunitinib-induced hypoxia.
 Aggravated intratumoral hypoxia by antiangiogenic agents often associates with tumor invasiveness and metastasis. , The differential impact on tumor hypoxia and induction of proangiogenic factors between anti-CD93 and sunitinib would suggest their different outcomes on metastasis. First, we evaluated the morphology and invasiveness of tumors in RIP1-Tag2 pancreas, based on the categorization being described previously ( ). RIP1-Tag2 tumors under sunitinib treatment were smaller but more irregularly shaped and more invasive than control tumors, consistent with previous publications. , Anti-CD93 alone had no impact on tumor local invasiveness; however, its inclusion was able to alleviate tumor invasiveness caused by sunitinib ( D). To assess local metastasis, we stained peripancreatic LNs of RIP1-Tag2 mice for SV40 Tag. Compared with control, RIP1-Tag2 mice treated with CD93 mAb had fewer SV40 Tag-positive cancer cells within the LNs, whereas mice treated with sunitinib showed a marked increase in metastatic tumor cells. Consistently, the addition of anti-CD93 mAb was able to reduce local metastasis caused by sunitinib ( E).

## CD93 blockade extends the survival of RIP1-Tag2 mice that are resistant to sunitinib
Sunitinib therapy in PanNET eventually leads to resistance in clinic, , which has been well documented in RIP1-Tag2 mice. , , , , RIP1-Tag2 mice under sunitinib treatment experienced increases in serum glucose levels in the first week but declined during the following week ( B). After 2 weeks of treatment, blood glucose in most RIP1-Tag2 mice was lower than 50 mg/dL, suggesting that tumors progressed and became unresponsive upon 2 weeks of sunitinib treatment. Notably, we found that IGFBP7 expression in tumor cells was significantly upregulated in response to sunitinib, while tumor vascular CD93 expression remained unchanged ( ). These observations led us to investigate whether the inclusion of anti-CD93 could rescue RIP1-Tag2 mice from sunitinib resistance ( A). After 2 weeks of sunitinib administration, we randomly divided sunitinib-resistant RIP1-Tag2 mice into two groups: one continued with sunitinib while the other group received anti-CD93 together with sunitinib. In mice treated with sunitinib alone, serum glucose levels continued to decline, and all mice died within 22 days. In contrast, the inclusion of anti-CD93 together with sunitinib increased and sustained serum glucose ( A). As a result, the medium survival of the group receiving anti-CD93 and sunitinib reached 23 days, in contrast to only 9 days for the sunitinib alone group ( B).
 We performed immunohistochemistry staining on RIP1-Tag2 tumor tissues after 10 days of sunitinib treatment to evaluate the features associated with resistance. The inclusion of anti-CD93 was able to inhibit the progression of tumors that were otherwise resistant to sunitinib ( C). Microvessels from sunitinib-resistant RIP1-Tag2 tumors appeared generally spotted and scarce. RIP1-Tag2 mice treated with sunitinib alone lose a substantial degree of tumor microvessels leading to exacerbated intratumoral hypoxia ( D and 5E). Remarkably, the inclusion of anti-CD93 mAb together with sunitinib was able to retain microvessel density within the tumors ( D). As a result, tumors under the combination of anti-CD93 and sunitinib became less hypoxic than those under sunitinib alone ( E). In alignment with this, the combinatory treatment was able to reduce tumor invasiveness ( F), resulting in fewer metastatic cancer cells in pancreatic LNs ( G).

## Discussion
Pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors (PanNETs) remain difficult to treat, in part because current antiangiogenic therapies like sunitinib often lead to hypoxia-induced tumor invasiveness and therapeutic resistance. Here, using the spontaneous RIP1-Tag2 PanNET model, we demonstrate that targeting the CD93-IGFBP7 axis improves tumor vascular normalization, alleviates sunitinib-induced hypoxia, and reduces local metastasis, supporting CD93 blockade as a promising therapeutic strategy for advanced PanNET. Mechanistically, CD93 blockade preserves vessel density and promotes vascular maturity by enhancing pericyte coverage, contrasting with sunitinib’s vessel-pruning effects that exacerbate hypoxia. The combination of anti-CD93 with sunitinib maintains vascular function, alleviates intratumoral hypoxia, and downregulates hypoxia-induced proangiogenic factors, ultimately reducing tumor invasiveness and local lymph node metastasis.
Treatment with anti-CD93 mAb in RIP1-Tag2 mice shows antitumor effects comparable to sunitinib; however, these two agents have distinct impacts on tumor vasculature and the TME. Both therapies promote tumor vascular maturation, but only sunitinib exhibits a clear antiangiogenic effect by trimming immature microvessels in RIP1-Tag2 tumors. In contrast, anti-CD93 primarily improves tumor vascular function and alleviates hypoxia. When combined with sunitinib, anti-CD93 mAb reduces the hypoxia induced by sunitinib treatment. Moreover, unlike prolonged sunitinib treatment, which upregulates proangiogenic factors, anti-CD93 blockade prevents the induction of such factors. Interestingly, anti-CD93 mAb also protected tumor microvessels from the pruning effects of sunitinib, likely by promoting their maturation, thereby preserving vascular integrity. As a result, CD93 blockade reverses sunitinib-induced tumor hypoxia and cancer invasiveness in RIP1-Tag2 tumors.
Anti-angiogenetic therapy in PanNET has shown only modest efficacy because of the development of acquired drug resistance. Our study advocates that CD93 blockade offers a potentially more efficacious therapeutic approach for advanced PanNET. Unlike conventional antiangiogenic agents, namely tyrosine kinase inhibitors, CD93 blockade appears to facilitate a favorable tumor-slowing effect without the undesirable consequences of intratumoral hypoxia caused by vascular trimming. Moreover, anti-CD93 therapy synergistically improves sunitinib therapy in PanNET, underscoring the complementary features of these two pathways in tumor vasculature. The addition of anti-CD93 mAb promotes the survival of otherwise sunitinib-resistant PanNET and reduces tumor invasiveness and lymph node metastasis promoted by sunitinib. Notably, the CD93-IGFBP7 pathway is uniquely enriched in the PanNET TME—both tumor cells and tumor-associated stroma cells constitutively secrete the ligand IGFBP7.
Several questions remain beyond the scope of our current study. Most PanNET-related mortality results from liver metastasis. Our study in RIP1-Tag2 mice was limited to peripancreatic metastasis, as this model rarely develops liver metastasis due to high mortality from hypoglycemia after 12 weeks. While we have demonstrated the involvement of the CD93/IGFBP7 interaction in vascular abnormality of PanNETs, the impact of MMRN2, the other known ligand for CD93, , has not been investigated. We found that anti-IGFBP7 monotherapy was less effective than CD93 blockade, which would support a role of MMRN2 in CD93-promoted PanNET progression. However, these experiments were performed in independent male and female cohorts, which may contribute to slight variations in survival curves. Besides CD93, MMRN2 interacts with multiple members of the group XIV C-type lectin receptor family, including CD248 and CLEC14A, , making it challenging to isolate the specific contribution of the MMRN2/CD93 interaction. Adding to this complexity, unlike other solid cancers, cancer cells from PanNET secrete the ligand IGFBP7. As a result, the distinct roles of cancer cell-derived vs. stromal or vascular sources of IGFBP7 in PanNET remain unclear. Finally, advanced PanNET tends to downregulate cancerous IGFBP7, which might desensitize their responsiveness to CD93 blockade. However, the abundance of stromal IGFBP7 and MMRN2 as the second CD93 ligand suggests that CD93 blockade remains a viable approach in late-staged PanNET.
In conclusion, our results support the CD93/IGFBP7 axis as a mechanistically distinct and clinically actionable target for vascular normalization in PanNETs. By improving vascular function without inducing hypoxia, CD93 blockade offers a complementary approach to traditional antiangiogenic therapy. Agents that interfere with the CD93/IGFBP7 pathway should be explored in combination with VEGF/VEGFR inhibitors to treat advanced PanNET.

## Materials and methods

## Mice and reagents
RIP1-Tag2 mice in black B6 background were rederived with cryopreserve sperm obtained from the National Cancer Institute Mouse Repository. RIP1-Tag2 mice were maintained by breeding male hemizygote with wild-type C57BL/6J females (the Jackson Laboratory, #000664). Monoclonal antibodies, including anti-mouse CD93 (clone 7C10) and anti-mouse IGFBP7 (clone 2C6), were produced from hybridomas that were generated in the laboratory. Purified antibodies were administered intraperitoneally at 300 μg/injection. Sunitinib malate (Catalog #HY-10255, MedChemExpress) was dissolved in DMSO and administered via oral gavage at a daily dose of 50 μg.

## Therapeutic procedures
Ten-week-old RIP1-Tag2 mice were used for two-arm studies (males for control vs. anti-CD93 7C10 or females for anti-IGFBP7 2C6). Purified antibodies were administered intraperitoneally twice a week for the first 2 weeks and then once a week for the rest of the therapeutic period. Tumor tissues were collected at 14 weeks old (after four treatments) for H&E or immunofluorescence staining. In the four-arm study (control, anti-CD93, sunitinib, and the combination of anti-CD93 plus sunitinib), 12-week-old RIP1-Tag2 mice were used with same schedule for anti-CD93 treatment. Sunitinib was given orally at a daily dose of 50 μg. Tumor tissues for staining were collected 10 days after treatment started. For the therapy of acquired resistance to sunitinib, 11-week-old RIP1-Tag2 mice under about 2 weeks of sunitinib treatment were used when blood glucose just dropped below 50 mg/dL.
Mice were randomized into two groups: one continued with sunitinib treatment and the other group received sunitinib plus anti-CD93. Tumor tissues for downstream analysis were collected 10 days after randomization. The progression of RIP1-Tag2 PanNET and sunitinib resistance were monitored by blood glucose levels daily. For the survival studies, all treatment remained continuously until the death of the mice. All animal protocols and procedures conducted were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of the University of Colorado Anschutz Medical Campus (Protocol #00132).

## Human PanNET tissues
Pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor tissues and adjacent normal pancreatic tissues were obtained from patients undergoing surgical procedures at the University of Colorado Hospital. This study was conducted under an institutional review board (IRB)-approved protocol, with informed consent provided by all participants. Additional human cancer specimens were procured through the Tissue Biobanking and Histology Shared Resource at the University of Colorado Cancer Center (UCCC). All human subject studies conducted conform to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. All specimens were reviewed by two independent investigators to determine the IGFBP7 H scores, Ki-67 index, tumor invasiveness, and the local lymph node involvement according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) 8th staging system for PanNET. Patients’ clinical characteristics are summarized in .

## Immunofluorescent staining
Tissue samples were rapidly frozen in optimal cutting temperature (OCT) compound, and 8-μm-thick frozen sections were prepared. Sections were air-dried, fixed in acetone at −20°C, and either processed immediately or stored at −80°C for future use. Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) sections and hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining were prepared by the CU AMC Histology Core. FFPE sections underwent rehydration and antigen retrieval using Tris-EDTA buffer (pH 10.0) or citric acid (pH 6.0) at 110°C for 15 min prior to staining.
For immunofluorescent staining, tissues were blocked with 2.5% goat or donkey serum, followed by overnight incubation with primary antibodies at 4°C. Secondary antibodies were applied for 1 h at room temperature, followed by DAPI counterstaining for 10 min. Stained tissues were cleared, mounted, and imaged using a Zeiss Axio Observer microscope. Images were analyzed using SlideBook software (Version 6.0.23). The antibodies used are detailed: mouse CD31 (FFPE tissue, R&D Systems, AF3628; frozen tissue, BioLegend, clone 390), mouse alpha-smooth muscle actin mAb (1A4, eBioscience, Cat # 50-9760-82), mouse CD93 (Invitrogen, PA5-81345), mouse IGFBP7 (Cell Signaling, 47744S), human CD93 (R&D Systems, AF3279), human IGFBP7 (Sino Biological, 13100-R115), SV40 (Santa Cruz, sc-147), mouse Glut-1 (Cell Signaling, #73015), mouse ANG2 (abcam, ab8452), mouse MMP9 (Cell Signaling, #24317), and mouse HGF (R&D Systems, AF2207).
H&E sections for RIP1-Tag2 whole pancreas were assessed for PanNET invasiveness by counting noninvasive (IC0, tumor was confined with the capsule), microinvasive tumors (IC1, tumor protrudes the capsule), and invasive carcinomas (IC2, normal acinar cells trap into tumor) tumors.

## Quantitative PCR
To assess gene expression changes in response to treatment, we harvested RIP1-Tag2 tumors and stored them at −80°C before RNA isolation. Total RNA was extracted using the QIAGEN RNeasy Mini Kit (Cat#74104) and cDNA synthesis was performed with the QIAGEN QuantiNova Reverse Transcription Kit (Cat#205411). Quantitative PCR (qPCR) was carried out using PowerUp SYBR Green Master Mix (Applied Biosystems, A25741) on a QuantStudio 3 instrument (Applied Biosystems). Primer sequences are listed in .

## Statistical analysis
All data analysis and visualization were performed using GraphPad Prism software (Version 9.5.0). Statistical significance, including mean ± SEM, was determined using methods described in the figure legends. A p value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.
