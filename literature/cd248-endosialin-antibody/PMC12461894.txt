## Abstract

## Background:
This study aims to investigate the expression profile, molecular mechanisms, and biological functions of Connective Tissue Growth Factor (CTGF) in bladder cancer (BLCA).

## Methods:
For accurate CTGF mRNA expression assessment, 1728 samples were collected. Additionally, for uncovering potential signaling pathways, differentially co-expressed CTGF genes were employed. For exploring CTGF’s effects, its impact on immune microenvironment and drug sensitivity was studied.

## Results:
CTGF mRNA was significantly underexpressed in BLCA (standardized mean difference [SMD] = −1.06, 95% CI: −1.89–0.23), and this downregulation was confirmed at the protein level (p < 0.0001). CTGF was mainly involved in immune microenvironment-related pathways and biological processes (BPs) associated with stromal remodeling and extracellular matrix dynamics. Moreover, A statistically significant correlation was identified between the expression levels of CTGF and the infiltration degrees of variety of immune cells. Notably, CTGF expression was positively correlated with sensitivity to EGFR inhibitors (e.g., Afatinib) and negatively correlated with resistance to PARP inhibitors (e.g., Olaparib).

## Conclusions:
This study elucidated the low expression of CTGF in BLCA. CTGF may promotes tumor progression by remodeling the immune microenvironment and extracellular matrix. Additionally, its expression is positively correlated with sensitivity to EGFR inhibitors and negatively correlated with resistance to PARP inhibitors.

Keywords: Gene expression, CTGF, molecular mechanism, immune microenvironment, drug sensitivity

## PLAIN LANGUAGE SUMMARY
Bladder cancer (BLCA) is a common type of cancer, and despite the availability of multiple treatment approaches, its recurrence rate remains high. This study investigated a molecule named Connective Tissue Growth Factor (CTGF) to explore its potential as a biomarker or therapeutic target for BLCA treatment. Databases, such as TCGA, GEO, and HPA were utilized to examine the levels of CTGF in BLCA tissues. Additionally, computational tools were employed to investigate how CTGF regulates the progression of BLCA, immune infiltration in cancer tissues, and drug efficacy. Our results indicated that CTGF are significantly lower in BLCA tissues than in normal tissues. Mechanistic analysis suggested that CTGF and its related genes are involved in processes, such as motility, invasion, and regulation of the tumor microenvironment. Furthermore, there were notable differences in the types of immune cell infiltration in BLCA tissues with varying CTGF expression levels, suggesting that CTGF is involved in the regulation between stromal control and immune response. Finally, drug sensitivity analysis revealed that CTGF expression was positively correlated with sensitivity to EGFR inhibitors (e.g., Afatinib) and negatively correlated with resistance to PARP inhibitors (e.g., Olaparib). Overall, CTGF may be a valuable biomarker or target for BLCA treatment, but further research is needed to fully understand its clinical application.

## ARTICLE HIGHLIGHTS

Unlike most solid tumors, in BLCA, we found that CTGF is significantly downregulated at both mRNA transcription and protein levels, and high CTGF expression is associated with poor patient prognosis.
It was found that significant differences in CTGF expression were observed across different patient’s ages, genders, pathological stages, tumor sizes (T), and lymph node metastasis (N).
There are significant differences in the infiltration levels of CD4+ T cells, M0/M1/M2 macrophages, B cells, and other immune cells in BLCA tissues with varying CTGF expression levels.
CTGF expression levels are positively correlated with sensitivity to EGFR inhibitors (e.g., Afatinib) and negatively correlated with sensitivity to PARP inhibitors (e.g., Olaparib).
These findings suggest that CTGF is associated with immune infiltration and drug therapy in BLCA, strongly supporting CTGF as a promising potential therapeutic target.

## Introduction
According to recent cancer statistics, bladder cancer (BLCA) ranks as the ninth most prevalent form of cancer globally, with an estimated 613,791 new cases and 220,349 deaths occurring annually. This accounts for approximately 3.1% of all new cancer diagnoses and 2.3% of cancer-related mortalities [ ]. The predominant histological subtype of BLCA is urothelial carcinoma. Notably, the 5-year survival rate for BLCA patients stands at around 72%–80% [ ], reflecting the variable clinical outcomes associated with this malignancy. However, a significant challenge in BLCA management is its high recurrence rate, with approximately 30% of patients experiencing disease relapse within a median interval of 12 months following cystectomy [ ]. This propensity for recurrence underscores the need for enhanced surveillance strategies and improved therapeutic interventions. Furthermore, the absence of specific diagnostic protocols often results in late-stage diagnoses, particularly in patients with advanced BLCA, who are at heightened risk for distant metastasis. A growing body of empirical evidence indicates that angiogenesis exerts a pivotal influence on the survival and progression of BLCA [ ].
In recent years, there has been a surge in research exploring the role of molecular markers in the diagnosis and treatment guidance of BLCA. For instance, tumors with infiltrating immune cells expressing PD-L1 have demonstrated better remission rates following treatment [ ]. PD-L1-targeted drugs, such as immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), have been approved for the treatment of locally advanced muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC) or metastatic BLCA. Additionally, targeted specific molecular characteristics include FGFR3 mutations and HER2 amplification, which provide potential avenues for precision treatment [ , ]. These findings underscore the pivotal role of molecular markers in guiding treatment decisions for BLCA patients. By identifying patients who are most likely to benefit from specific therapies, molecular markers may ultimately contribute to improved clinical outcomes and quality of life. However, to fully realize this potential, it is imperative to undertake more profound investigations into the pathogenesis and progression mechanisms of BLCA. Such research endeavors are essential for uncovering potential diagnosis and therapeutic targets specific to BLCA, thereby enhancing treatment efficacy and patient outcomes.
Connective Tissue Growth Factor (CTGF), commonly known as CCN2, is a highly adaptable signaling regulator that belongs to the secreted CCN protein family. This multifunctional molecule plays an instrumental role in numerous biological processes (BPs), including but not limited to wound healing, inflammatory reactions, cell adhesion, chemotaxis, apoptosis, angiogenesis, lymphangiogenesis, and tissue fibrosis. By modulating a spectrum of biological activities, such as cell proliferation, migration, invasion, drug resistance, and epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT), CTGF significantly contributes to the initiation, progression, and metastatic potential of tumor, thereby positioning itself as a central figure in the intricate landscape of tumor biology [ ]. Therefore, the role of CTGF in tumor pathogenesis and progression has attracted widespread attention from the scientific community. A large number of studies have elucidated the dysregulation of CTGF in tumors or its involvement in tumor-related mechanisms. In terms of clinical significance research, immunohistochemical staining techniques and cell line experiments have provided compelling evidence that the expression levels of both CTGF protein and mRNA are elevated in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) cases. Furthermore, this upregulation has been correlated with an unfavorable prognosis in affected patients [ ]. Moreover, CTGF plays a complex role in cancer progression. Knockdown of CTGF reduces TNBC cell proliferation, migration, and invasiveness in vitro [ ], and decreases lung and lymph node metastasis rates while prolonging survival in a 4T1 mouse breast cancer model [ ]. In colorectal cancer, CTGF is significantly upregulated and promotes the malignant progression of the tumor [ ]. In hepatocellular carcinoma, CTGF protein and mRNA levels are significantly upregulated [ ]. However, CTGF has also been identified as an endogenous metastasis inhibitor, suppressing lung adenocarcinoma metastasis by enhancing HIF-1α degradation [ ] and inhibiting human oral squamous cell carcinoma proliferation and tumor growth in vivo upon overexpression [ ].
In BLCA research, functional enrichment analysis showed CTGF was significantly enriched in recurrent BLCA, with immunohistochemistry confirming its upregulation [ ]. However, this study has predominantly focused on recurrent cases, leaving the expression profile in primary BLCA largely unexplored. Moreover, the precise mechanisms by which CTGF exerts its effects remain to be elucidated. Additionally, researchers have demonstrated that knocking down CTGF can inhibit the migration and invasion of BLCA cells, as evidenced by wound healing and Transwell experiments [ ]. However, this study was limited to cell line models and did not investigate the expression levels of CTGF. Moreover, several studies have indicated that both CTGF protein and mRNA levels are upregulated in BLCA cell lines and tissues [ , ]. Nonetheless, these investigations primarily focused on CTGF as a target gene within the Hippo-YAP pathway and did not delve into the underlying mechanisms associated with its function. Regarding its angiogenic function, CTGF has been implicated in tumor angiogenesis [ ]. However, the precise mechanisms through which CTGF contributes to angiogenesis in BLCA remain obscure. Importantly, in the study by Ferro et al., they discovered that factors such as lymphovascular invasion serve as independent predictive indicators of BLCA’s response to BCG therapy [ ]. This further supports the significant impact of angiogenesis on the treatment outcomes of BLCA patients. As such, the expression patterns and molecular underpinnings of CTGF in BLCA are not yet fully elucidated. Further valuable research is essential to uncover the specific mechanisms at play.
We hypothesize that CTGF is involved in the mechanisms related to the occurrence and progression of BLCA. This study is designed to conduct a thorough investigation of CTGF protein and mRNA expression profiles in BLCA tissues, while also exploring the intricate relationships between CTGF and the immune microenvironment of BLCA, as well as its interactions with therapeutic interventions. Through the execution of these in-depth and all-encompassing analyses, we aspire to gain a deeper insight into the exact mechanistic pathways by which CTGF impacts the development and advancement of BLCA, along with the associated molecular changes. Furthermore, we seek to clearly elucidate the critical roles that CTGF plays in these complex processes.

## Materials and methods
2.1.

## Data collection and processing for CTGF mRNA expression analysis
In this study, CTGF mRNA expression data were sourced from several comprehensive databases, namely Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO), ArrayExpress, and The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA). To ensure data consistency and comparability, all mRNA expression datasets were normalized using the log2(x + 1) transformation and the “limma” package prior to analysis. In order to mitigate batch effects, we combined independent datasets that were generated on the same platform into a single, unified dataset.

2.2.

## Assessing the clinical significance of CTGF mRNA expression
To effectively illustrate the distribution patterns and fluctuations of CTGF mRNA expression across various sample groups, violin plots were meticulously constructed utilizing R software version 4.4.1 (The University of Auckland). Subsequently, t-tests were employed to rigorously evaluate the statistical significance of any observed disparities in expression levels. In order to assess the efficacy of CTGF mRNA in distinguishing between BLCA tissues and control bladder tissues (ROC) curves were plotted for all datasets. To enhance the reliability of the findings, a comprehensive analysis was conducted on 937 BLCA samples and 188 control bladder samples sourced from various public databases. The standardized mean difference (SMD) was calculated using STATA software version 17.0 (College Station, TX) to quantify the magnitude of differences in CTGF expression levels. Additionally, the summary receiver operating characteristic (SROC) curve was employed to provide a comprehensive assessment of CTGF’s discriminative capacity. In addition, clinicopathological data were collected from the TCGA database. The Kaplan–Meier (KM) survival curve was plotted using GraphPad Prism version 8.0 (GraphPad Software Inc., La Jolla, CA) to evaluate the impact of CTGF expression on the survival prognosis of BLCA patients. Furthermore, the influence of CTGF mRNA expression levels on various clinical parameters, including age, gender, pathology stage, tumor size (T), lymph node involvement (N), and distant metastasis (M) was assessed. This analysis aims to provide a more in-depth and comprehensive comprehension of CTGF’s significance within the clinical context of BLCA.

2.3.

## Evaluation of CTGF protein expression via immunohistochemistry
This study received formal approval from the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Guangxi Medical University, ensuring compliance with ethical standards and guidelines for human subject research (Approval No.: NO2023-S05701). Three tissue microarrays (BLC481, BLC1021, and BLC1501) were utilized for immunohistochemical staining to assess CTGF protein expression levels. These microarrays encompassed samples from 137 cases of BLCA to 33 cases of non-cancerous bladder tissue, with the latter sourced from adjacent cancerous or inflammatory bladder tissues. The results of the immunohistochemical staining were independently evaluated by two experienced pathologists. The scoring was based on both the intensity of the staining and the percentage of positive cells. The scoring criteria were as follows: a) staining intensity: 0 (no staining), 1 (weak staining), 2 (moderate staining), 3 (strong staining); b) percentage of positive cells: 0 (<5%), 1 (5%–25%), 2 (26%–50%), 3 (51%–75%), and 4 (76%–100%). The total staining score = staining intensity × percentage of positive cells. Subsequently, GraphPad Prism version 8.0 software was employed to conduct t-tests, comparing expression levels between groups and visualizing differences in protein expression between cancerous and non-cancerous tissues via violin plots and ROC curves. Furthermore, external immunohistochemical data for BLCA and normal bladder tissues treated with the CAB005123 antibody were obtained via the HPA database. Our research was meticulously carried out in full compliance with the fundamental principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki.

2.4.

## Potential signaling pathways of CTGF in BLCA
The co-expressed genes of CTGF were identified by calculating the Pearson correlation coefficient (r). Genes with a Pearson correlation coefficient |r| > 0.3 and p < 0.05 were considered as co-expressed with CTGF, and the gene met this condition in at least 5 datasets, it was considered to co-express with CTGF. In addition, to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs), the SMD was calculated for each of the included datasets. with high-expression genes having SMD > 0, p < 0.05, and low-expression genes having SMD < 0, p < 0.05. The intersection of DEGs and co-expressed genes was defined as CTGF’s differentially co-expressed genes. To gain a more in-depth understanding of the potential molecular mechanisms through which CTGF operates in BLCA, pathway enrichment analysis was carried out based on differentially co-expressed genes. The “clusterProfiler” package [ ] was employed to conduct Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) and Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analyses.

2.5.

## Epigenetic regulatory mechanisms of CTGF
In an effort to conduct an in-depth exploration of the potential regulatory mechanisms that orchestrate the expression of CTGF, the transcription factors (TFs) associated with the upstream transcription start site (TSS) of CTGF were predicted through the Cistrome Data Browser database [ ]. Subsequently, this prediction was validated using chromatin immunoprecipitation sequencing (ChIP-seq).

2.6.

## Analysis of immune microenvironment
With the TCGA dataset, the “IOBR” software package [ ] was employed to conduct immune-related analyses. The CIBERSORT algorithm was utilized to estimate the abundance of immune cell infiltration. Boxplots were constructed to illustrate differences in immune infiltration between BLCA and normal bladder samples, as well as between high and low CTGF expression groups within BLCA. Subsequently, in order to comprehensively evaluate the associations between the expression levels of CTGF and the infiltration of 22 distinct types of immune cells, Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient, which was computed using the “corrplot” package, was employed. The degree of association was visualized using bubble plots and scatterplots.

2.7.

## Analysis of drug sensitivity
The relationship between CTGF expression and the sensitivity of drugs in BLCA patients was predicted using the “oncoPredict” package [ ]. Based on the median value of CTGF gene expression from TCGA, patients were categorized into two distinct groups: the high-expression group and the low-expression group. The differences in half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) between the two groups were analyzed, and the association between CTGF expression levels and drug sensitivity was evaluated through Pearson correlation analysis. Our analysis relied on drug sensitivity data that were extracted from the Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (GDSC) project, specifically version 2.0 (Sanger Institute, UK).

2.8.

## Statistical analysis
R version 4.4.1, Stata version 7.0, and GraphPad Prism version 8.0 were employed to conduct all statistical analyses in this study. Unless otherwise specified, p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. In the context of comparing two groups of quantitative data, when the data conformed to a normal distribution, t-tests were employed for the subsequent analysis. Otherwise, Wilcoxon tests were employed. Chi-square Q tests and I2 statistics were used to determine the calculation model for mRNA expression level SMD. If I2 > 50%, significant heterogeneity was present, and a random effects model was used to calculate SMD. Otherwise, a fixed effects model was applied.

3.

## Results
3.1.

## Comprehensive analysis of CTGF mRNA expression
Based on the principles of evidence-based medicine, 24 mRNA high-throughput datasets were consolidated into 14 newly formed cohorts ( ), with a combined total of 1351 BLCA samples and 207 control bladder samples being integrated to comprehensively assess CTGF mRNA expression. Compared to control bladder samples, six cohorts showed notably downregulated expression of CTGF mRNA (Figure S1). Moreover, ROC curves indicated that CTGF mRNA possessed robust discriminative power for BLCA samples (Figure S2). The comprehensive analysis revealed significant heterogeneity (I2 > 50%, p < 0.05), prompting the use of a random effects model to calculate SMD. As shown in , CTGF mRNA expression levels were significantly reduced when compared to control bladder samples (SMD = −1.06, 95% CI: −1.89 − 0.23). Additionally, SROC curve was plotted to visualize the discriminative ability of CTGF mRNA expression levels between BLCA and control bladder tissues ( ), with results indicating strong discriminative power (AUC = 0.92, 95% CI: 0.90 − 0.94; sensitivity = 0.88, 95% CI: 0.74 ∼ 0.95; specificity = 0.86, 95% CI: 0.74 ∼ 0.93).
 
3.2.

## Clinical significance of CTGF mRNA expression in BLCA
Given the potential clinical implications of understanding the factors influencing CTGF expression in BLCA, KM curves were plotted based on data from the TCGA database. The results revealed a significant difference in overall survival (OS) rates between patients with high and low CTGF expression. Specifically, patients in the high CTGF expression had significantly poorer OS compared to those in the low expression (p < 0.05; ). Subsequently, we conducted an analysis to explore the relationship between CTGF mRNA expression and a multitude of clinical characteristics of BLCA patients. It was found that significant differences in CTGF expression were observed across different patient’s ages, genders, pathological stages, tumor sizes (T), and lymph node metastasis (N) (p < 0.05; ).
 
3.3.

## Internal and external data analysis of CTGF protein levels in BLCA tissue

Figure 2(E,F) reveals that, when compared to 33 control tissue samples, the immunohistochemical score of CTGF protein levels in 137 BLCA tissue samples was notably lower (p < 0.0001). Furthermore, it was demonstrated that CTGF protein exhibited strong discriminative capacity between BLCA and control tissues (AUC = 0.968, 95% CI: 0.9312 − 1.000). This finding strongly implies notable disparities in CTGF protein expression levels between the internal BLCA tissues and the control tissues. Additionally, external CTGF expression in tissue was obtained through HPA, interestingly revealing that CTGF protein was undetectable or weakly expressed in external BLCA tissue, while weak expression was detected in normal bladder tissue ( ). These results may indicate the presence of different subgroups or subtypes of BLCA tissues with varying CTGF protein expression levels, possibly reflecting the heterogeneity of BLCA cells.
 
3.4.

## Analysis of potential enrichment pathway
A comprehensive analysis in this study led to the identification of 905 genes displaying substantial differential co-expression patterns in relation to CTGF (Figure S3), providing a reliable data foundation for subsequent functional analysis. Based on KEGG pathway annotation analysis, these genes showed significant pathway enrichment, mainly clustering in core pathways, such as cytoskeleton in muscle cells, focal adhesion, dilated cardiomyopathy, and cGMP − PKG signaling pathway ( ). These pathways are closely associated with extracellular matrix remodeling, mechanical stress response, and cardiomyopathy processes. GO functional annotation further revealed multi-level biological characteristics ( ): at the BP, these genes were mainly involved in processes, such as extracellular matrix organization, extracellular structure organization, and external encapsulating structure organization; cellular component (CC) analysis showed that gene products were primarily localized in structural microdomains, such as the basement membrane, platelet alpha granule, and collagen-containing extracellular matrix; in terms of molecular function (MF), enriched terms included conferring tensile strength, extracellular matrix structural constituent, and collagen binding.
 
3.5.

## Epigenetic regulatory mechanisms of CTGF expression
Next, an in-depth investigation into the epigenetic regulatory mechanisms of CTGF expression was carried out. By utilizing the Cistrome Data Browser database, the top four TFs with the highest scores – RAD21, FOSL1, FOXA1, and SMC1A – were identified. Subsequently, the potential for these TFs to bind to the upstream TSS of CTGF and thereby regulate its expression was explored. ChIP-seq was then conducted on these TFs, and their binding sites upstream TSS of the CTGF were validated ( ). These results suggest that CTGF expression is likely to be regulated by these TFs.

3.6.

## Analysis of the correlation between CTGF expression levels and the immune microenvironment
Based on the TCGA dataset, significant differences in eight immune cell subsets were found between cancerous and control tissue samples ( ). Compared to control bladder samples, BLCA samples exhibited significantly higher levels of immune infiltration by resting NK cells, activated dendritic cells, M1 macrophages, and M0 macrophages. Conversely, infiltration levels of γδ T cells, resting mast cells, naïve B cells, and resting memory CD4+ T cells were significantly lower in BLCA samples. Furthermore, we compared immune cell infiltration levels in tissues with different CTGF mRNA expression levels. As shown in , in the high-expression group, the infiltration levels of naïve B cells, resting memory CD4+ T cells, activated memory CD4+ T cells, M0 macrophages, M1 macrophages, M2 macrophages, and resting mast cells were significantly higher than in the low-expression group, whereas the infiltration levels of memory B cells, monocytes, resting dendritic cells, and activated dendritic cells showed the opposite trend. Additionally, the CIBERSORT algorithm was employed to derive immune cell characteristics and explore the correlation between CTGF expression and immune cell infiltration in BLCA. demonstrated that the infiltration levels of M2 macrophages, M1 macrophages, naïve B cells, and activated memory CD4+ T cells were positively correlated with CTGF expression, while several immune cells, including follicular helper T cells, memory B cells, naïve CD4+ T cells, and activated dendritic cells, showed significant negative correlations with CTGF expression. Scatter plots (Figure S4) more intuitively display the correlation results between CTGF expression and individual immune cell subsets. These findings reveal marked differences in immune cell infiltration between BLCA and control bladder samples, highlighting significant associations between the levels of immune cell infiltration and CTGF expression.
 
3.7.

## Relationship between CTGF expression and drug sensitivity
Next, the importance of CTGF in BLCA drug therapy was investigated, and its potential correlation with drug sensitivity in BLCA patients was explored. Significant differences in the sensitivity of 16 drugs were observed between the high-expression and low-expression groups. Decreased sensitivity to 14 drugs, including Dasatinib and Olaparib, was associated with high expression, while increased sensitivity to Afatinib and Erlotinib was noted ( ). Further analysis using Pearson’s test revealed that CTGF expression was negatively correlated with sensitivity to 13 drugs, including Dasatinib and Olaparib, while a positive correlation was observed with Afatinib (Figure S5). The present findings are in alignment with the outcomes of the differential expression analysis. Based on these consistent results, it is postulated that the drugs under investigation may hold significant potential in exerting therapeutic effects for the treatment of BLCA.
 

4.

## Discussion
This study amalgamated an extensive array of sample resources from 24 distinct datasets across the globe, encompassing 1351 BLCA samples and 207 control bladder samples, to perform a thorough and in-depth analysis. This vast repository of data not only bolstered the statistical rigor of the study but also furnished substantial data support for unraveling the intricate molecular mechanisms underlying BLCA. First, in terms of expression, CTGF was found to be significantly downregulated in BLCA tissue compared to control bladder tissue. This finding contrasts sharply with previous reports in other tumor types, revealing CTGF as a potential key regulator in the development of BLCA and offering a new perspective for molecular subtyping and therapeutic target selection in BLCA. Second, with respect to mechanisms, epigenomic methods were employed to identify potential regulatory mechanisms governing CTGF expression. Third, the relationship between CTGF expression and immune microenvironment, potential signaling pathways, and drug sensitivity in patients with BLCA were concurrently investigated. Notably, the subgroup with downregulated CTGF expression exhibited significant differences in drug sensitivity, indicating that this molecule may act as a biomarker for forecasting treatment response. Consequently, it furnishes a vital molecular underpinning for the formulation of personalized treatment strategies tailored to BLCA.
CTGF, a highly versatile secretory protein exhibiting a wide array of regulatory functions, plays a pivotal role in several critical pathological processes, including the modulation of fibrosis, the stimulation of cell proliferation, the induction of resistance mechanisms in tumor treatment, the promotion of tumor angiogenesis, and the augmentation of invasive and metastatic potential [ ]. Existing studies have shown that abnormal upregulation of CTGF in various solid tumors, including breast cancer, colorectal cancer, and hepatocellular carcinoma, is significantly associated with tumor malignancy progression and poor clinical prognosis [ ]. Notably, research on human oral squamous cell carcinoma presents a unique phenotype – both in vitro and in vivo experiments have confirmed that overexpression of CTGF can inhibit cancer cell proliferation and tumor growth [ ], indicating significant tumor type-specificity in the biological effects of this molecule. In the realm of BLCA research, prior studies have revealed intriguing yet contradictory findings. Specifically, one research group observed a marked upregulation of CTGF in recurrent BLCA specimens [ ]. However, this investigation was confined to recurrent cases and did not incorporate primary cancer samples as controls, thereby limiting the generalizability of its conclusions. Conversely, another study demonstrated that exosomal miR-217 intervention could elicit elevated CTGF expression in BLCA cell lines, namely T24 and 5637 [17]. Nevertheless, the applicability of these findings, particularly with respect to the representativeness of the cellular model employed and their potential clinical relevance, remains to be rigorously validated. Furthermore, immunohistochemical techniques were employed in a study that reported heightened CTGF expression in BLCA [ ]. However, this investigation was constrained by a limited sample size, with only 57 cases included, and it focused solely on measuring protein levels. Consequently, the broader representativeness of these findings still remains to be fully elucidated. In contrast, leveraging a multicenter, large-sample clinical cohort, our study meticulously unveiled the heterogeneous expression profile of CTGF in BLCA tissue. Contrary to conventional wisdom, CTGF exhibited a marked tendency toward low expression in BLCA specimens. This distinctive expression paradigm not only holds significance for mechanistic investigations but also presents promising prospects for clinical translation.
From a translational medicine perspective, the low-expression pattern of CTGF in BLCA may harbor the potential for developing novel diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets. Through ROC and SROC analyses, we found that CTGF expression levels demonstrated strong discriminative power in distinguishing BLCA from control bladder tissue (AUC = 0.92, 95% CI: 0.90–0.94). Moreover, CTGF expression significantly varied among different BLCA patients in terms of age, gender, and tumor stage. Additionally, significant differences in OS were observed between patients with different levels of CTGF expression. This indicates its potential as a biomarker for BLCA diagnosis or prognostic assessment. However, despite the rigorous statistical validation of this hypothesis in our study, the actual application of CTGF in clinical diagnosis still requires further validation, including larger-scale clinical cohort studies and the establishment of standardized detection methods for biomarkers.
Second, in terms of mechanistic research, this study utilized epigenomic methods to reveal the potential regulatory mechanisms of CTGF expression. ChIP-seq validation identified binding sites upstream of the CTGF TSS for TFs such as RAD21, FOSL1, FOXA1, and SMC1A, suggesting that these TFs may be involved in regulating CTGF expression. This finding not only uncovers the complexity of CTGF expression regulation but also provides new targets for further investigating the role of CTGF in tumor progression mechanisms.
With the aim of gaining a more in-depth understanding of the biological functions that CTGF exerts in BLCA, our study conducted an integrative multi-omics analysis, revealing the complex molecular network of CTGF in BLCA microenvironment regulation. KEGG pathway enrichment results showed that differentially co-expressed genes of CTGF significantly clustered in pathways, such as cytoskeleton in muscle cells, focal adhesion, and cGMP − PKG signaling pathway. These pathways play dual roles in BLCA progression: on one hand, dynamic changes in cytoskeleton remodeling and focal adhesion may lead to changes in the cellular microenvironment, thereby enhancing the migratory and invasive capabilities of cancer cells [ , ]; on the other hand, the cGMP-PKG signaling pathway cascade is considered an endogenous apoptotic pathway in many cancer types, especially in malignant tumors, such as cervical cancer, and gastric cancer [ , ]. These studies suggest that CTGF dysregulation may lead to abnormal activation of the cGMP-PKG pathway, thereby regulating tumor vascular permeability or immune cell infiltration to indirectly promote a malignant phenotype. Notably, the enrichment of dilated cardiomyopathy-related pathways indicates that CTGF-mediated extracellular matrix remodeling may not only be a characteristic of the local tumor microenvironment but also potentially influence systemic metabolic homeostasis through circulating factors, providing new clues for understanding the multi-organ comorbidities in patients with BLCA. GO functional annotation further elucidates the molecular basis of CTGF’s role in tumors: during BLCA stromal remodeling, CTGF dysregulation may promote tumor progression by regulating extracellular matrix remodeling and collagen cross-linking. Research from prior studies supports this notion. A team found that under CD248+ cancer-associated fibroblast conditions, the Hippo pathway is abnormally activated, leading to increased expression of CTGF. This, in turn, creates a collagen type I-rich environment linked to extracellular matrix stiffness, which promotes non-small cell lung cancer metastasis [ ]; other studies have shown that CTGF dysregulation may lead to epithelial-mesenchymal transition, activation of CAFs, and the formation of a fibrotic environment, thereby promoting tumor angiogenesis, invasion, and metastasis [ ]. Collectively, these analyses further support the notion that CTGF-mediated stromal remodeling may be a key driver of the invasive phenotype in BLCA.
Regarding the TME, for the first time, this study revealed the profound association between CTGF expression and the dynamic distribution of the immune microenvironment in BLCA. The TME, a complex system comprising the extracellular matrix, stromal cells, and immune cells, has been proven to have a decisive impact on cancer metastasis, prognosis, and therapy outcomes [ , ]. In the field of BLCA research, the complex and crucial interplay between the TME and the malignant progression, alongside the recurrence of the disease, has garnered considerable attention [ ]. Notably, the significant association between PD-L1 positivity and higher survival rates in metastatic urothelial carcinoma patients is a game-changing discovery, emphasizing the critical role of TME in shaping treatment outcomes [ ]. Additionally, in a review article by Iacovino et al., the effectiveness of ICIs in treating metastatic urothelial carcinoma patients who are ineligible for platinum-based chemotherapy was discussed. Their investigation revealed that metastatic BLCA patients treated with anti-PD-L1 drugs (Atezolizumab) achieved a favorable pathological complete response rate (29%), underscoring the significance of immunotherapy for patients with metastatic BLCA [ ]. Our study conducted a comprehensive analysis of the immune infiltration landscape in both BLCA tissues and normal tissues. Our findings unveiled a significant correlation between CTGF expression and the dynamic changes in the immune microenvironment. Specifically, within cancerous tissues, we observed a notable increase in the proportions of resting NK cells, activated dendritic cells, M1 macrophages, and M0 macrophages. Conversely, there was a marked decrease in the proportions of γδ T cells, resting mast cells, naïve B cells, and resting memory CD4+ T cells. Further analysis showed that CTGF expression levels were positively correlated with the infiltration of naïve B cells, resting memory CD4+ T cells, and M2 macrophages, and also reshaped the immune microenvironment by regulating the distribution of activated dendritic cells. These findings not only corroborate the clinical significance of previously reported immune cell infiltration characteristics (such as the enrichment of M0 macrophages in high-risk BLCA [ ], but also suggest that CTGF may act as an immune regulatory hub, influencing BLCA treatment responses by modulating immune cell differentiation and chemotaxis.
Finally, our study found a significant association between CTGF expression and drug sensitivity in BLCA. Notably, the multifunctionality of CTGF extends beyond immune regulation. Previous studies have shown that this molecule plays a key role in paclitaxel resistance in ovarian cancer [ ], chemotherapy tolerance in breast cancer [ ], 5-FU resistance in colorectal cancer [ ], and cisplatin resistance in osteosarcoma [ ]. This study further expands our understanding by revealing a significant association between CTGF expression and drug sensitivity in BLCA. Specifically, patients with elevated CTGF expression displayed heightened resistance to 14 different drugs, encompassing Dasatinib and Olaparib. Conversely, an anomalous increase in sensitivity was observed toward Afatinib and Erlotinib in this patient subgroup. Previous research has shown that the human monoclonal antibody against of CTGF, pamrevlumab, combined with gemcitabine and nab-paclitaxel chemotherapy, demonstrated a synergistic inhibitory effect on pancreatic cancer growth [ ]. This finding not only indicates that CTGF may be an important potential biomarker for tumors but also suggests that different CTGF expression subgroups require optimized drug selection strategies. Particularly noteworthy is the positive correlation between Afatinib and CTGF, implying that specific drugs may rely on CTGF-mediated molecular mechanisms to exert their therapeutic effects, with the exact mechanisms warranting further in-depth investigation. In the realm of future clinical translational research, there lies an opportunity to further substantiate the dual significance of CTGF – both as a prognostic biomarker for predicting disease outcomes and as a promising therapeutic target. By delving deeper into this area, researchers can explore innovative combined targeting approaches aimed at augmenting the overall efficacy of BLCA treatment regimens.

5.

## Conclusions
This study, utilizing multi-omics analysis and multi-center sample evaluations, unveiled the low-expression pattern of CTGF in BLCA. It was indicated that CTGF promotes tumor progression through the remodeling of the immune microenvironment and extracellular matrix. Moreover, it was discovered that CTGF expression is positively correlated with sensitivity to EGFR inhibitors (e.g., Afatinib) and negatively correlated with resistance to PARP inhibitors (e.g., Olaparib). Although the potential for clinical translation is substantial, there exists a necessity for the establishment of standardized detection systems and the exploration of combined treatment strategies. Future research endeavors will be directed toward the development of in vivo detection techniques for CTGF and the investigation of the mechanisms underlying immune combination therapies, with the aim of providing novel decision-making foundations for precision medicine in BLCA.
