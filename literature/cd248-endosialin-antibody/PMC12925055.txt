Transcription factors (TFs) involve in colorectal cancer (CRC). However, the function and mechanism of VAX2 in the development of CRC remain barely known. In the present study, we observed that transcription factor VAX2 is frequently upregulated in CRC internal sample set and external tissue microarray and cell lines. High VAX2 expression is observably correlated with tumor invasiveness and AJCC stage of tumors in CRC. Furthermore, decreased expression of VAX2 functionally inhibits the proliferation and facilitates the apoptosis of CRC cells and vice versa. Mechanistically, VAX2 involves in WNT/beta-catenin signaling through facilitating the nucleus accumulation of beta-catenin and VAX2 specifically binds to the promoter and trigger the transcription of SERPINE1. SERPINE1 overexpression significantly reverses the suppression of malignant behavior and nucleus accumulation of beta-catenin induced by VAX2 knockdown in vitro. In vivo, both the knockdown of SERPINE1 and the oral administration of its inhibitor Tiplaxtinin consistently attenuated the VAX2-enhanced proliferative capacity. Consistent with analysis of TCGA-CRC, positive correlation can be detected between VAX2 and SERPINE1 in fresh CRC samples. Thus, VAX2-SERPINE1 axis participate in CRC progression and work as a potential target against CRC.

## Introduction
As the third most common cancer worldwide with an estimation of 1926,118, colorectal cancer (CRC) leads the second cause of death with an estimation of 903,859 in 2022 [ ]. Despite significant advancements in diagnosis and treatment, the survival of CRC patients was still not optimistic [ ]. Hence, investigating and elucidating the molecular patterns underlying CRC tumorigenesis is crucial.
As a member of homeobox (HOX) family, ventral anterior homeobox 2 (VAX2) is documented as an oncogenic transcription factor (TF) in multiple cancers [ , ]. For instance, VAX2 is elevated in gastric cancer (GC) compared to normal tissues, and mediates GC metastasis through VAX2-LINC01189-hnRNPF axis [ ]. Moreover, VAX2 is upregulated in papillary thyroid carcinoma and contributes to malignant behaviors of papillary thyroid carcinoma cells [ ]. However, the biological roles and molecular mechanisms of VAX2 underlying CRC remain unknown.
Serpin family E member 1 (SERPINE1), also known as plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 (PAI-1), is a key component of plasminogen activator proteolytic system and is frequently upregulated in CRC [ , ], where its high expression correlates with advanced clinicopathologic features and poor patient survival [ , ]. Beyond its classical role in regulating plasminogen activation and fibrinolysis, SERPINE1 modulates tumor microenvironment by promoting angiogenesis or mediating chemotherapy resistance in CRC [ , , ]. Moreover, SERPINE1 promotes CRC progression by enhancing cell proliferation, invasion and metastasis [ , , ]. For instance, SERPINE1 bound to and promoted the nuclear translocation of p65, activating the NF-κB signaling and promoting migration in CRC [ ]. Furthermore, SERPINE1 induced MMP1 expression to increase CRC metastasis [ ]. Additionally, SERPINE1 is also reported to involve in WNT signaling [ , , , ]. For example, SERPINE1 triggered by CD248 involves in WNT signaling to promote tumor growth in lung cancer [ ]. Besides, SERPINE1 epigenetically repress DKK1 to increase beta-catenin accumulation for WNT signaling activation [ ].
Therapeutic targeting of SERPINE1 shows promise. SERPINE1 inhibitor, Tiplaxtinin, repress tumor growth in vitro and in vivo in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma [ ]. Additionally, Tiplaxtinin inhibited angiogenesis and tumor growth in bladder cancer [ ]. Besides, targeting SERPINE1 by Tiplaxtinin treatment synergizes with anti-PD-L1 immune checkpoint blockade therapy in a syngeneic murine model of melanoma as well [ ]. Previous studies indicated that Tiplaxtinin regulated tumor growth in CRC [ , ].However, the involvement of VAX2 on the effect of Tiplaxtinin on CRC proliferation is unknown.
This study examined VAX2 expression and its association with clinicopathologic features and outcomes in CRC. Enhanced VAX2 accelerated, while knockdown of VAX2 impeded cell proliferation in CRC. Moreover, a molecular mechanism by which VAX2 transcriptionally activated SERPINE1, promoted CRC cell proliferation and inhibited cell apoptosis was validated. Thus, our study provides information on VAX2 and its mechanism in driving CRC progression, which will aid in the identification of potential therapeutic targets for the treatment of patients with CRC.

## Materials and methods

## Cell lines, antibodies and reagents
Normal colonic epithelial cell line FHC, human CRC cell lines (RKO, SW1116, HCT116 and SW620) and human GC cell lines (AGS) were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, USA; Catalog Numbers: FHC CRL-1831, RKO CRL-2577, SW1116 CCL-233, HCT116 CCL-247, SW620 CCL-227, AGS CRL-1739). Human CRC cell lines (LoVo, SW480, Caco2 and HT-29) and human GC cell lines (HGC-27) were obtained from the Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CBCAS, China; Catalog Numbers: LoVo TCHu82, SW480 TCHu172, Caco-2 SCSP-5027, HT-29 SCSP-5032, HGC-27 TCHu 22). All cell lines were authenticated by short tandem repeat (STR) profiling and routinely tested for mycoplasma contamination using a PCR-based detection kit (CA1081, Solarbio, China). Mycoplasma testing was performed every month and prior to key experiments. Cells are maintained in RPMI-1640 medium (Procell, China) or DMEM (Procell, China) supplemented with 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS; Biological Industries, Israel), penicillin (100 µg/mL) and streptomycin (100 µg/mL) (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) and cultured at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere comprising 5 % CO2. Tiplaxtinin (S7922) was obtained from MedChemExpress, USA.

## Clinical samples
Seventy-one pairs of CRC and their matched para-cancer tissues (>5 cm from the margin of the tumor) were collected during surgeries from patients without chemotherapy, radiotherapy, or molecular-targeted therapy approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Nanfang Hospital, Southern Medical University, Guangzhou, China (NFEC-2017–026). Each sample was attached to a confirmed pathological diagnosis. The human samples were collected with patients’ informed consent and in accordance with the ethical standards of the Medical Ethics Committee of Nanfang Hospital, Southern Medical University. All ethical regulations relevant to human research participants were followed.

## Tissue microarrays (TMA) and immunohistochemistry (IHC)
CRC tissue microarrays (HColA180Su10) were purchased from Shanghai Outdo Biotech Co Ltd. The tissue microarray analysis included 99 CRC samples and 79 corresponding adjacent normal tissue samples. The tissue microarray was stained for VAX2. Paraffin-embedded tissue blocks were cut into 4 μ m sections and then transferred to glass slides. In short, the slides were deparaffinized with xylene, rehydrated with decreasing concentrations of ethanol, subjected to microwave retrieval with a citrate buffer, immersed in three percent hydrogen peroxide and then incubated with the primary antibodies at 4 °C for overnight, followed by incubation with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated anti-rabbit or anti-mouse secondary antibody (ZSGB-BIO, China) at room temperature for 2 h. The expression levels of VAX2, SERPINE1, PCNA, beta-catenin, p65 and MMP1 were then visualized using freshly prepared 3, 3′-diaminobenzidine (DAB) and counterstained with hematoxylin. The concentration of antibodies used in our study was listed in Supplementary Table S1.

## RNA extraction and quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) analysis
Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (AG21101; Accurate Biology, China) according to the protocol of manufacturer and quantified using a Nanodrop 2000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The cDNA was generated from total RNA (500 ng) with reverse transcription kit (AG11705; Accurate Biology, China), and used as the template for qRT-PCR analysis. TUBB were used as internal control. qRT-PCR was then performed using SYBR Green PCR Kit (AG11702; Accurate Biology, China). Roche LightCycler 480 system is used for run and the primers used are listed in Supplementary Table 2.

## Western blotting
We first extracted total protein using RIPA buffer and quantified using a BCA kit (23,227; Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). In a nutshell, we resolved the protein samples with SDS-PAGE on 8 to 12 % polyacrylamide gels and transferred them to nitrocellulose membranes (ISEQ00010; Merck Millipore, USA). The membranes were blocked with 5 % skim milk and then probed with primary antibodies for overnight and species-specific secondary antibodies for 1 h. After repeatedly washing with Tris-buffered saline with Tween® 20 (TBST), the membranes were visualized by chemiluminescence (36208ES76; YEASEN, China). TUBB was used as an internal control in our experiment. ImageJ software version 1.53 was utilized for quantifying the results. The concentration of antibodies used in our study was listed in Supplementary Table S1.

## Colony formation
In the colony formation assay, transfected cells were seeded in 12-well microplates and then cultured for 14 days. Colonies were fixed with 4 % paraformaldehyde for 10 min, stained with crystal violet (G1062; Solarbio, China) for 10 min, washed repeatedly with water and counted manually.

## 5-ethynyl-20-deoxyuridine (EdU) staining assay
Cells seeded on 6-well plates for EdU staining experiment (C10310, Ribobio, China) were incubated with 50 μM EdU for 2 h, followed by staining of Apollo for additional 10 min. Following repeated PBS washes, cells were analyzed by flow cytometry. Briefly, cells were first gated on FSC-A/SSC-A to exclude debris, then on FSC—H/FSC-A to select singlets, and finally analyzed within the singlet population using a single channel. The EdU positive rate was accessed as the ratio of EdU-positive cells to all cells in each test by flow cytometry.

## Detection of apoptotic cells by flow cytometry
Cells seeded on 6-well plates after being transfected for 48 h were stained with Annexin V-YSFluor™ 647/PI Apoptosis Detection Kit (YeaSen, Shanghai, China) for 15 min, and then apoptotic cells were detected by flow cytometry. Following repeated PBS washes, cells were analyzed by flow cytometry. Briefly, cells were first gated on FSC-A/SSC-A to exclude debris, then on FSC—H/FSC-A to select singlets, and finally analyzed within the singlet population using a two-parameter Annexin V vs. PI plot to distinguish different apoptotic stages.

## Transwell migration assay
In vitro migration assays were carried out with 8-μm-pore Transwell chambers (353,097, BD Biosciences, USA). The chambers were put into a 24-well plate with 500 μL medium containing 10 % FBS while CRC cells resuspended in 200 μL serum-free medium were seeded slowly into the upper chamber. After incubation for 24 to 72 h, the non-migrated cells in the upper chambers were rubbed away gently by cotton swab. Then, the chambers were washed repeatedly with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), fixed in 4 % paraformaldehyde for 10 min, and stained with crystal violet solution. The cells were counted in three random fields under a microscope. The migration rates in each group were calculated by standardizing control group to 100 % migration.

## Data source
RNA-seq of patients with CRC in the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) were obtained from the Genomic Data Commons (GDC), consisting of 624 CRC tissues and 51 normal tissues. Differential gene analysis was carried out with DEseq package. Survival analysis was performed in GEPIA2(http://gepia2.cancer-pku.cn/#index) database. Phenotypes of CRC patients in TCGA were obtained from UCSC Xene (https://xena.ucsc.edu/). Patients with sufficient clinical information of age, gender, TNM stage, T stage, N stage, M stage were included for further univariate and multivariate cox regression analysis. A simplified linear combination approach was employed to integrate the prognostic information from both continuous variables VAX2 and SERPINE1 in TCGA-CRC. Specifically, we first standardized the expression values of VAX2 and SERPINE1 (Z-score normalization). We then performed univariate Cox regression for each gene to obtain their respective regression coefficients (β). The combined risk score for each patient was calculated as: Risk Score = (β_VAX2 × VAX2_z) + (β_SERPINE1 × SERPINE1_z). Patients were subsequently dichotomized into High-Risk and Low-Risk groups based on the median of this score.

## Plasmid construction, siRNA and lentivirus packaging
Human VAX2 sequence was amplified and subcloned into pcDNA3.1 (Kidan Biosciences, China). Human SERPINE1 plasmid was provided by Ruibiotech, China. Ablation of VAX2 and SERPINE1 were performed by transfection with siRNA duplex oligos, which were synthesized by GenePharma, China. We transfected cells with plasmids or siRNA duplex oligos using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen Life Technologies, USA) following the instructions of manufacturer. SERPINE1-shRNA and VAX2-overexpression (pSLENti-EF-1-EGFP-F2A-Puro-CMV-VAX2–3×flag-WPRE) lentivirus and their corresponding controls were provided by HanYi and Obio Technology, China, respectively. The transfection was conducted following the manufacturer’s procedure. The efficiency of all transfection experiments was initially evaluated by qRT-PCR and further validated by western blotting. Transfection was considered effective and proceeded to subsequent assays only when the following criteria were met: in knockdown experiments, target gene mRNA levels were reduced to <50 % (0.5-fold) relative to the negative control; in overexpression experiments, mRNA levels were increased to >200 % (2-fold) compared to the control. The sequences of the siRNAs used are listed in Supplementary Table 3.

## Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP)
For Co-IP assays, cells were washed with PBS repeatedly and harvested with cell lysis buffer for 30 min at 4 °C. And then cell extract was incubated with anti-LEF1, anti-VAX2, anti-β-catenin or IgG at 4 °C for overnight, followed by addition of Protein A/G agarose beads and incubation for 2 h at 4 °C. The immunoprecipitated proteins were eluted with SDS-PAGE loading buffer by boiling for 10 min followed by western blotting.

## Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP)
ChIP was performed in CRC cell lines HCT116 and SW480 and gastric cancer cell lines AGS following the protocol of manufacturer. Briefly, cells were cross-linked with 1 % formaldehyde, sonicated and incubated with anti-VAX2 antibody or IgG (as a negative control) at 4 °C for overnight. Then, complexes were washed with low and high salt buffers, and DNA was extracted, precipitated and purified. Conventional PCR was used to detect DNA template enrichment with primers specific for predictive binding site in SERPINE1 promoter. The primers used are listed in Supplementary Table 2.

## Dual luciferase assay
SERPINE1 promoter (WT) and its mutagenesis (MUT) was constructed into pGL3 plasmid by Kidan Biosciences. The reporter constructs pTOPFLASH and pFOPFLASH, in which the luciferase gene was driven by the wild-type or dominant negative mutant consensus 3 × T cell factor/lymphoid enhancer factor binding element (TBE), respectively, were kindly provided by Professor Weimei Tang of Southern medical university, China [ ]. Then, cells were co-transfected with siVAXp or siNC or VAX2 or Vector and pTOPFLASH or pFOPFLASH or WT or MUT pGL3 dual luciferase plasmids for 48 h. Firefly luciferase activity normalized to Renilla luciferase activity was measured. The results are shown as the relative luciferase activity of promotor to corresponding control within the same experiment, which is set to 1. A minimum of three independent transfections were carried out for each experimental group.

## Animal models
The animal schemes in our study were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Nanfang Hospital (IACUC-LAC-20,221,219-003). To evaluate the in vivo tumorigenic effects, BALB/c nude mice (nu/nu, aged 4–6 weeks) from Guangdong Medical Laboratory Animal Center, China were randomly assigned into five groups (n = 6 per group), with the allocation being determined by a sequence of random numbers that were computer-generated. 5 × 106 cells were injected subcutaneously into the right flank of nude mice. After two weeks of injection, Tiplaxtinin (5mg/kg) diluted in 100 μL corn oil was administered via oral gavage every day for 3 weeks. Tumor volumes were evaluated using a formula V=π /6 × L × W2 (L is the length and W is the width). The weight of the tumors was recorded and the successful transfection was validated with IHC. The proliferation index was assessed by the ratio of PCNA-positive cells to the total number of cells in slides. Throughout the experiment, the investigators responsible for measuring tumor size and performing immunohistochemistry were blinded to the group identity of the mice until the experiment was completed.

## Statistical analysis
All statistical analysis were performed using SPSS 25.0 software (SPSS Inc., USA) and GraphPad Prism 8 (GraphPad Software, USA). Unless otherwise stated, all experiments were performed in triplicate. For normally distributed continuous variables, Student's t-test or one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used where appropriate, whereas for other comparisons between qualitative variables, the Mann-Whitney U test or Wilcoxon signed-rank test was applied. Survival rates were calculated using Kaplan-Meier curves, and the log-rank test was used to analyze the significant differences in the survival time. Correlations were assessed using Pearson’s correlation. All data are presented as mean ± Standard Deviation (SD). Differences were considered significant (two-tailed) at **, p < 0.05; ***, p < 0.01; ****, p < 0.001.

## Results

## VAX2 is upregulated and correlates with malignant progression in CRC
To investigate VAX2 expression in CRC, we downloaded TCGA-CRC (containing 624 CRC tissues and 51 normal tissues) RNA-seq datasets and found that VAX2 is upregulated in tumors compared with adjacent normal tissues (Supplementary Table. 4). Furthermore, Kaplan–Meier survival analysis indicated that high VAX2 expression correlated with decreased Overall Survival (OS, Supplementary Fig. 1A) and Disease-Free Survival (DFS, Supplementary Fig. 1B) in TCGA-CRC.
To confirm VAX2 expression in CRC, we first assayed VAX2 expression in the normal colon epithelial cell line, FHC, and in CRC-derived cell lines (SW1116, SW620, LoVo, HCT116, SW480, Caco2, HT29, and RKO). Compared to FHC, VAX2 was relatively upregulated in all CRC cell lines except RKO, as determined by western blotting ( A). Next, we examined VAX2 expression in 13 pairs collected CRC and corresponding normal tissues (Sample set 1). The result revealed that VAX2 is frequently upregulated in CRC compared with normal tissues by western blotting ( B). Similarly, immunohistochemistry (IHC) demonstrated that VAX2 is upregulated in 9 out 13 CRC compared with their counterparts ( C and Supplementary Fig. 1C). 
To further validate VAX2 expression in CRC, we performed IHC with Tissue microarray (TMA, containing 99 CRC samples and 79 corresponding adjacent normal tissue samples) and found that VAX2 is significantly upregulated in CRC using either paired- or un-paired non-parametric tests ( D– ). Moreover, VAX2 expression was positively correlated with tumor invasion (T2–3 vs. T4, p = 0.0188; G) and the TNM stage (AJCC) (I-II vs. III-IV, p = 0.0398; H) but gender, age, tumor size or lymph node metastasis (Supplementary Table.5). Similar to above TCGA-based survival analysis (Supplementary Fig. 1A), high VAX2 expression was related to worse OS in TMA as well ( I).
To summary, above findings strongly hinted that VAX2 is upregulated and correlates with malignant progression in CRC.

## VAX2 facilitates CRC cell malignant behavior in vitro
Considering VAX2 expression was relatively lower in LoVo cells and higher in HCT116 and SW480 cells ( A), we established VAX2 overexpression in LoVo cells, and knockdown in HCT116 and SW480 cells, confirmed by western blotting and qRT-PCR ( A1–2 and Supplementary A-B). 
To test the function of VAX2 in CRC cell behavior, we carried out gain-of-function and loss-of-function assays in vitro, respectively. The results showed that cell proliferation increased following VAX2 overexpression in LoVo ( B1 and C1), while decreased VAX2 expression attenuated the proliferative ability in both HCT116 and SW480 cells, as determined by colony formation and EdU assays ( B2 and C2). Moreover, enhanced VAX2 expression inhibited cell apoptosis in LoVo ( D1). Conversely, VAX2 knockdown led to an increased apoptosis rate in HCT116 and SW480 ( D2). Besides, transwell assays indicated that high VAX2 expression facilitated cell migration in LoVo ( E1), and vice versa in HCT116 and SW480 ( E2).
Taken together, our results revealed that VAX2 facilitated CRC cell malignant behavior in vitro.

## VAX2 involves in WNT/beta-catenin signaling by promoting beta-catenin nucleus accumulation
To investigate the molecular mechanisms of VAX2 on CRC cell malignant behavior, we performed RNA-seq of VAX2-siRNAp or Scr-siRNA-transfected HCT116 cells (Supplementary Table 6) and conducted enrichment analyses. Both Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG, A) and Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA, B) indicated that VAX2 involves in WNT/beta-catenin signaling. 
Since nucleus accumulation of beta-catenin is the key to WNT/beta-catenin signaling activation [ , , , , , , , , ], we performed protein isolation and found that VAX2 knockdown led to a significantly decreased beta-catenin nucleus accumulation in HCT116 ( C). Furthermore, immunofluorescence (IF) demonstrated that decreased VAX2 expression inhibited beta-catenin nucleus accumulation in HCT116 and SW480 ( D). To determine whether VAX2 directly binds to β-catenin, we performed reciprocal co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) experiments. Neither anti-VAX2 nor anti-β-catenin antibody pulldown yielded evidence of a direct interaction in HCT116 and SW480 (Supplementary Fig. 3A). Given this finding, we conclude that VAX2 modulates WNT/β-catenin signaling via an indirect regulatory mechanism. Considering that GSK3β is a critical and central kinase responsible for β-catenin phosphorylation and degradation [ , , , ], we performed western blotting and found that VAX2 knockdown increased p-β-catenin (Ser33/37/Thr41) levels but decreased p-GSK-3β (Ser9), suggesting that VAX2 regulates GSK-3β-mediated β-catenin phosphorylation (Supplementary Fig. 3B).
Previous studies revealed that beta-catenin binds to TCF/LEF-1 to involve in WNT/beta-catenin signaling [ , , , , , , , ]. beta-catenin-TCF/LEF-1 complex synergistically triggers the transcription of downstream target genes such as MMP7, cyclin D1 and c-jun et al. [ , , , , ]. Thus, we performed co-immunoprecipitation (co-IP) assay and found that VAX2 knockdown weakened the binding between beta-catenin and LEF1 ( E). To test the effects of VAX2 knockdown on beta-catenin/TCF/LEF-1 complex targets, we performed qRT-PCR and revealed that MMP7, cyclin D1 and c-jun are significantly downregulated in VAX2-knockdown HCT116 and SW480 compared to their corresponding controls ( F). Consistently, the TOP/FOP Flash reporter assays confirmed that VAX2 knockdown weakened β‐catenin‐dependent luciferase activity ( G).
In summary, above results demonstrated that VAX2 involves in WNT/beta-catenin signaling by promoting beta-catenin nucleus accumulation in CRC cells.

## VAX2 transcriptionally activates SERPINE1 in gastrointestinal cancers cells
As a transcriptional factor, VAX2 can regulate downstream protein-coding genes or non-coding genes. Thus, we performed RNA-seq ( A) and found that 110 genes were significantly downregulated while 112 genes were upregulated above the threshold level (Log2[Fold Change] > 1 or Log2[Fold Change] < −1, Padj<0.05, Supplementary Table 6). We then focused on the downregulated genes since their potential use as early diagnostic markers or intervention targets. Among those, we identified SERPINE1 was markedly downregulated with maximum absolute Log2(Fold Change) and Log2(Pvalue) ( B). Then, we performed qRT-PCR and validated that SERPINE1 expression was significantly downregulated in VAX2 knockdown HCT116 and SW480 cells ( C). Similarly, SERPINE1 was found to be upregulated in VAX2-overexpression LoVo cells ( D). Former research indicated that VAX2 acted as an oncogene in gastric cancer (GC) as well [ ]. Thus, we wondered whether VAX2 overexpression can also increase SERPINE1 abundance in GC cell lines. The results showed that VAX2 overexpression elevated SERPINE1 expression in AGS and HGC-27 cell lines as well (Fig.4E1–2), indicating a potentially conserved oncogenic mechanism for VAX2 across gastrointestinal cancers. Additionally, we performed survival analysis and found that, similar to the results of VAX2 (Supplementary Fig. 1A-B), high SERPINE1 abundance was correlated with worse overall survival and disease-free survival (Supplementary Fig.4D-E). 
To ensure whether VAX2 transcriptionally trigger SERPINE1, we investigated the human SERPINE1 gene promoter (2-kb: −1500 ∼ +500 bp) sequence region (SERPINE1 promotor, SERPINE1p) and identified three probable VAX2-binding sites with >92 % identity to the consensus sequences with JASPAR database (http://jaspar.genereg.net/) ( F), which were located at –1172 to –1167 bp (site 1) and –1152 to –1147 bp (site 2) and –1146 to –1141 bp (site 3). Then, we performed chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) with anti-VAX2 antibody or IgG. The distance between predictive site 1 and site 2/site 3 is 15 bp. The qPCR primer cannot be designed using online software (primer3 and primer3plus) because the predictive sites are in close proximity and the intervening sequence has very low GC content. Thus, we combined VAX2 binding sites to design primers for PCR. PCR amplification showed a clear band in VAX2 group compared to IgG group in CRC cell lines HCT116 and SW480 and GC cell line AGS ( G), which was further validated by ChIP-qPCR (Supplementary Fig. 4A-C). Furthermore, we cloned SERPINE1p (Wildtype, WT) and three site-directed mutagenesis (Mutation, MUT) into luciferase reporter plasmid, respectively ( H). The results showed that decreased VAX2 expression led to a declined SERPINE1p luciferase activity in HCT116 and SW480 ( I) while VAX2 overexpression elevated SERPINE1p luciferase activity in AGS ( J). More importantly, site-directed mutagenesis indicated that site 1-directed mutagenesis but the others is critical for VAX2-induced SERPINE1 transcription ( I– ). These results strongly demonstrated that SERPINE1 is a direct transactivating target of VAX2.
In summary, VAX2 can transcriptionally trigger SERPINE1 expression in gastrointestinal cancers cells.

## VAX2 mediates CRC proliferation by transactivating SERPINE1 expression in vitro
To investigate whether SERPINE1 involves in VAX2-mediated CRC cells proliferation, we strengthened SERPINE1 expression in VAX2-knockdown HCT116 and SW480 cells (Supplementary Fig. 4A-B). The results indicated that enhanced SERPINE1 expression reversed the suppression of CRC cells proliferation induced by knockdown of VAX2 as tested by colony formation and EdU assays ( A& ). Furthermore, SERPINE1 overexpression lessened the apoptosis rate lifted by VAX2 knockdown in CRC cells ( C& Supplementary Fig. 5C-D). 
Former researches indicated that SERPINE1 participated in WNT/beta-catenin signaling [ , , , ]. Hence, we wondered whether SERPINE1 functions in VAX2-mediated WNT/beta-catenin signaling. We first performed Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) based on TCGA-CRC separated by median of SERPINE1 and validated an association between SERPINE1 and WNT/β-catenin signaling using BEST database (https://rookieutopia.hiplot.com.cn/, Supplementary Figure 5E). Furthermore, we performed western blotting and found that SERPINE1 overexpression reduced p-β-catenin (Ser33/37/Thr41) levels and elevated p-GSK-3β (Ser9) levels (Supplementary Fig. 5F). To delve deeper, we performed Co-IP assays and revealed that VAX2 knockdown weakened the binding between beta-catenin and LEF1. However, enhanced SERPINE1 abundance can partially reversed above effect ( D). To further verify whether SERPINE1 participates in VAX2-mediated WNT/beta-catenin signaling in CRC, TOP/FOP Flash reporter assays were conducted and the results indicated that VAX2 knockdown significantly weakened β‐catenin‐dependent luciferase activity, which was partially reversed by SERPINE1 overexpression in HCT116 and SW480 cells (Supplementary Fig. 5 G). Consistently, qRT-PCR showed that enhanced SERPINE1 expression led to an increased expression of beta-catenin-TCF/LEF-1 target genes reduced by VAX2 knockdown in HCT116 and SW480 cells ( E).
Taken together, we confirmed that VAX2 mediates CRC proliferation and apoptosis by transcriptionally triggering SERPINE1 expression in vitro and that SERPINE1 involved in VAX2-mediated WNT/beta-catenin signaling.

## SERPINE1 participates in VAX2-mediated CRC cells proliferation in vivo
To demonstrate the impact of SERPINE1 on VAX2-mediated proliferation in vivo, we introduced LoVo cells into nude mice to construct subcutaneous tumor model ( A). The results showed that tumor volumes and tumor weights in Lv-VAX2+Lv-Scr-shRNA group (group 2) were significantly bigger and heavier than those in Lv-Vector+Lv-Scr-shRNA (group 1), while Lv-VAX2+Lv-SERPINE1-shRNA (group 3) yielded smaller tumor volumes and slighter tumor weights than Lv-VAX2+Lv-Scr-shRNA (group 2, B– ). Consistently, orally administration of SERPINE1 inhibitor, Tiplaxtinin (group 5), reverted the promotion effects of VAX2 overexpression (group 4) in LoVo cell proliferation ( B– ). 
We next performed H&E staining to validate subcutaneous tumor tissues in each group ( D). Subsequently, IHC was carried out to validate the transfection efficiency of VAX2-overexpression and SERPINE1-knockdown ( E). Furthermore, we performed IHC assays and found that cell proliferation (PCNA) markedly heightened in Lv-VAX2+Lv-Scr-shRNA group (group 2) compared to Lv-Vector+Lv-Scr-shRNA (group 1), whereas SERPINE1 knockdown (group 3) or oral administration of Tiplaxtinin (group 5) weakened the promoting effects of VAX2 overexpression (group 2&4, F). To delve deeper, we performed IHC to investigate whether SERPINE1 involved in VAX2-mediated activation of WNT/beta-catenin signaling in vivo. The results indicated that enhanced VAX2 expression (group 2&4) in LoVo led to a lifted nucleus accumulation of beta-catenin, which could be counteracted by decreased SERPINE1 expression (group 3) or Tiplaxtinin treatment (group 5, G). Moreover, IHC indicated VAX2 overexpression (group 2&4) promoted p65 nuclear translocation and elevated MMP1 expression, while SERPINE1 knockdown (group 3) or Tiplaxtinin treatment (group 5) substantially attenuated above effects ( H), which is consistent with previous studies [ , ].
All in all, these data demonstrated that SERPINE1 participates in VAX2-mediated CRC cells proliferation and WNT/beta-catenin signaling activation in vivo.

## VAX2 is correlated with SERPINE1 in human CRC tissues
To further validate the relationship between VAX2 and SERPINE1, we performed qRT-PCR in 71 pairs collected CRC and matched normal specimens. The results indicated that VAX2 was upregulated in 59 out of 71 CRC tissues ( A) while SERPINE1 was upregulated in 55 out of 71 ( B). Furthermore, both VAX2 and SERPINE1 were significantly upregulated in CRC compared to normal tissues ( C– ). In addition, a positive correlation was detected between VAX2 and SERPINE1 in these 71 pairs CRC specimens ( E). Similarly, pearson analysis showed that a posistive relationship was observed in TCGA-CRC as well ( F). To determine whether VAX2 or and SERPINE1 are independent prognostic factors, we first performed univariate and multivariate Cox regression on TMA and TCGA-CRC. VAX2 retained independent prognostic significance across all analyses in both TMA and TCGA-CRC (Supplementary Fig. 7A-D). For SERPINE1, it was a significant prognostic factor in the TCGA-CRC univariate analysis but not in the subsequent multivariate analysis (Supplementary Fig. 7A-B); this may be explained by its role as a key downstream mediator of VAX2, which is an independent prognostic indicator in CRC. Based on above results, we constructed a combined VAX2-SERPINE1 risk score using a linear combination approach based on TCGA-CRC. Kaplan-Meier analysis confirmed that patients in the High-Risk group had a significantly shorter overall survival compared to the Low-Risk group (Supplementary Fig. 7E).

## Discussion
In this study, we investigated VAX2 expression in internal sample set and external tissue microarray via qRT-PCR and IHC and confirmed VAX2 was upregulated and positively correlated with worse clinicopathologic features and prognosis in colorectal cancer (CRC). Ectopic expression of VAX2 influenced cell proliferation and apoptosis in CRC. Mechanistically, VAX2 participated in WNT/beta-catenin signaling through facilitating nucleus accumulation of beta-catenin. RNA-seq, qRT-PCR, ChIP and luciferace assays validated that VAX2 transcriptionally trigger SERPINE1 expression through binding to −1171–1166 bp upstream the transcription start site of SERPINE1. Moreover, SERPINE1 involved in VAX2-induced activation of WNT/beta-catenin signaling and cell proliferation and apoptosis in vitro and in vivo. Additionally, VAX2 positively associated with SERPINE1 in our CRC sample set in accordance with the analysis of TCGA-CRC. These results suggest that VAX2 functions as a potential oncogene. As a member of homeobox (HOX) family, ventral anterior homeobox 2 (VAX2) is located in chromosome 2p13.3 [ ]. VAX2 was identified to be specifically expressed in the developing vertebrate eye at the very beginning and was named because of the high degree of sequence similarity to VAX1 [ ]. Recent studies indicate that VAX2 participate in the development of malignant tumors [ , ]. It was reported that VAX2 was upregulated in cancer tissues compared to normal controls and associated with worse outcome in papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) by Bei Guo et al. [ ]. VAX2 knockdown inhibited cell proliferation and migration and led to a decreased phosphorylated(p)-ERK and p-MEK abundance in PTC. In addition, VAX2 was frequently increased in gastric cancer (GC) tissues and cell lines compared to their corresponding controls [ ]. High VAX2 expression positively correlated to advancement of tumors in GC. VAX2 contributed to GC progression through VAX2-LINC01189-hnRNPF axis. In our recent study, we revealed that VAX2 was elevated in CRC compared to normal tissues in internal sample set and external tissue microarray through qRT-PCR and IHC. Besides, high VAX2 expression is relative to worse clinicopathologic features (tumor invasion and AJCC stage) and outcomes (overall survival and disease-free survival) in our TMA and public TCGA-CRC cohort. Furthermore, etopic VAX2 modulated proliferation and apoptosis in CRC, detected by colony formation, EdU and apoptosis assays. Additionally, VAX2 involved in WNT/beta-catenin signaling through facilitating nucleus accumulation of beta-catenin. These data indicated that VAX2 plays an oncogenic role and promotes the development and progression of CRC.
Serpin family E member 1 (SERPINE1) encodes a member of the serine proteinase inhibitor (serpin) superfamily [ ]. SERPINE1 is the principal inhibitor of tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) and urokinase (uPA), and hence is an inhibitor of fibrinolysis [ ]. SERPINE1 also functions as a component of innate antiviral immunity. Previous studies hinted that SERPINE1 involved in the progression of multiple cancers, including CRC [ , , ]. For example, Gianluigi Mazzoccoli et al. reported that SERPINE1 was upregulated in CRC and correlated to grading and microsatellite instability [ ]. In addition, Mi-So Park et al. reported that high SERPINE1 abundance was associated with worse clinical outcome and knockdown SERPINE1 decreased cell proliferation and migration in CRC [ ]. Furthermore, SERPINE1 activated AKT signaling and contributed to radioresistance and aggressive phenotypes in breast cancer [ ]. Additionally, SERPINE1 is known to participate in WNT/beta-catenin signaling. For instance, SERPINE1 involved in CD248-mediated WNT signaling to promote angiogenesis and tumor growth in lung cancer [ ]. Moreover, SERPINE1 participated in cigarette-smoking-induced activation of WNT/beta-catenin signaling [ ]. What’s more, SERPINE1 regulated AT2 proliferation and differentiation via the WNT/beta-catenin cascades [ ]. In our study, we reveal that high SERPINE1 expression was correlated to worse overall survival and that SERPINE1 was transcriptionally activated by VAX2 in gastrointestinal cancers. SERPINE1 involved in VAX2-mediated activation of WNT/beta-catenin cascade by facilitating nucleus accumulation of beta-catenin and transcriptionally activation of the downstream targets of beta-catenin in vitro and in vivo. These results suggest that SERPINE1 is essential for VAX2-mediated malignant behaviors in CRC cells.
However, there are some limitations of the methods used in our study. Firstly, although the binding of VAX2 to the SERPINE1 promoter in gastrointestinal cancer cell lines has been demonstrated in our study, employing primary cells or patient-derived organoids in future studies would be valuable to strengthen the clinical relevance. Secondly, the current in vivo evidence is based on subcutaneous xenograft models, which, while informative, cannot fully recapitulate the native tumor microenvironment of the colon. Thus, future work employing orthotopic mouse models would be valuable to verify the pathological significance of the VAX2-SERPINE1 axis under more physiologically relevant conditions. Furthermore, while our study elucidates the downstream oncogenic axis of VAX2, the drivers of its frequent overexpression in CRC remain elusive. Upstream mechanisms could be implicated at multiple levels. Speculatively, at the genomic level, amplification of the 2p13.3 locus containing VAX2 could be a direct driver. At the epigenetic level, dysregulation such as promoter hypomethylation might also contribute to its transcriptional activation.
In conclusion, our results suggest that VAX2 is upregulated in CRC and promotes malignant phenotypes. VAX2 transcriptionally trigger SERPINE1 in CRC to promote the nucleus accumulation of beta-catenin, thus involving in WNT/beta-catenin signaling. VAX2 expression correlates positively with SERPINE1 in CRC. Thus, our study reveals a novel VAX2-related mechanism underlying the proliferation and apoptosis of CRC cells and identifies a promising prognostic biomarker for CRC.
