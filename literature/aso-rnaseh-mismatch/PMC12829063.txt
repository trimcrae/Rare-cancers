## Background
Myocardial infarction (MI) is a severe cardiovascular event associated with various molecular alterations. This study aimed to explore the intricate relationship between mepivacaine, a local anesthetic, and MI by identifying and characterizing overlapping genes through a comprehensive analysis.

## Methods
Differential expression analysis was performed using the GSE19339 data set to identify overlapping genes between MI and mepivacaine, and functional enrichment analysis as well as expression verification was performed. Rat cardiomyocyte H9c2 cells were divided into four groups: control, a model group (mepivacaine), treatment (si-FXYD2 + mepivacaine) and PI3K inhibitor group (si-FXYD2 + mepivacaine + LY294002). In vitro analysis of the effects of FXYD2 regulation on the viability, of Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) release, Reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation, PI3K/AKT signaling pathway activation, oxidative stress, apoptosis, mitochondrial respiratory chain activity, and endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress of mepivacaine-treated H9c2 cells.

## Results
Eight genes were identified linking MI to mepivacaine, with FXYD2 notably upregulated in MI samples. In vitro experiments confirmed that FXYD2 knockdown in H9c2 cells significantly alleviated the mepivacaine-induced decrease in cell viability and oxidative stress, as well as the increase in apoptosis (p < 0.05). Knocking down FXYD2 inhibits mepivacaine-induced mitochondrial damage, effectively alleviates endoplasmic reticulum stress under mepivacaine treatment, and reduces the expression of stress markers (GRP78, CHOP). These cellular protections are mediated through the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, whose activation is modulated by FXYD2 expression and mepivacaine treatment (p < 0.05).

## Conclusions
Knockdown of FXYD2 plays a key role in reducing mepivacaine-induced cardiomyocyte injury by modulating oxidative stress, apoptosis, mitochondrial function, and ER stress, mediated through the PI3K/AKT signaling cascade.

## Introduction
Myocardial infarction (MI), mostly brought about by blockage in the coronary arteries, causes impaired myocardial blood flow, leading to ischemia and cell death [ ]. Despite a significant decline in the incidence and mortality rates of acute MI in recent years, it remains a major global cause of death [ ]. Heart failure after MI, alongside the resultant impairment in quality of life and diminished survival rates, exhibits a significant inter-relation [ ]. Furthermore, smoking, obesity, and sedentary lifestyles are widely acknowledged as pivotal factors precipitating MI [ ]. Early MI diagnosis, utilizing methods, such as electrocardiography, blood biochemical markers, echocardiography, and computed tomography scanning, facilitates timely intervention and improves patient prognosis [ , ]. The immediate interventions of MI, including aspirin (an antiplatelet agent), nitroglycerin (to alleviate chest pain), and oxygen supplementation, along with analgesics, such as morphine or pethidine, aim to alleviate symptoms [ , ]. Long-term strategies focus on pharmacotherapy, cardiac rehabilitation programs, and surgical interventions for recurrence prevention and cardiac recovery [ ]. However, some studies suggest that prolonged or misuse of certain drugs, including certain local anesthetics, may increase cardiovascular disease risk, including MI [ ]. Researchers delved into the molecular mechanisms and genetic factors underlying MI occurrence, aiming to identify novel therapeutic targets and personalized treatment approaches [ ]. Therefore, gaining a deeper understanding of MI pathogenesis and exploring novel therapeutic drugs is crucial for enhancing our understanding of this disease.
Numerous studies have demonstrated that certain local anesthetics can alleviate and treat cardiac-related diseases, such as arrhythmias and MI [ , ]. Mepivacaine, an amino amide-linked potent local anesthetic, finds application in infiltration and regional anesthesia for both adult and pediatric patients [ , ]. Toxicity arising from mepivacaine primarily impacts the central nervous and cardiovascular systems [ ]. The incidence of perioperative cardiac arrest is notably high in trauma patients undergoing emergency surgical anesthesia, with correlations to patient factors, such as advanced age, cardiopulmonary comorbidities, alcohol history, elevated ASA physical status, preoperative shock with hemodynamic instability, and surgical site involvement [ – ]. Prolonged use of local anesthetics for perioperative pain control is constrained by potential cardiac toxicity, including arrhythmias and contractile suppression, possibly leading to cardiac arrest [ ]. Studies have indicated that mepivacaine can significantly reduce transmembrane Ca2+ transport by blocking Na+ channels and enhancing the reverse mode activity of the sodium–calcium exchanger [ , ]. Concerns about anesthesia risks have been raised following reports by Martínez MA and colleagues, highlighting patient deaths during elective surgery attributed to excessive local anesthetic doses [ ]. Froehle M et al. proposed that neutralizing free radicals and reducing oxidative stress could alleviate the cardiac toxicity induced by local anesthetics [ ]. Therefore, investigating the relationship between mepivacaine and MI becomes pivotal.
The FXYD motif is located close to the N-terminus of FXYD proteins, which are single-span membrane proteins [ ]. These proteins bind to the α subunit and modify Na, K-ATPase (NKA) transport characteristics according to tissue and subtype [ ]. To create and maintain resting membrane potentials in neurons, a transmembrane protein pump called Na, K-ATPase is needed to actively transport of three sodium ions out of the cell and two potassium ions into the cell. The study of Derre A et al. demonstrated that FXYD2–LASO–Gapmer administration almost completely alleviated pain symptoms without significant side effects, providing long-term relief for patients with chronic pain [ ]. The research of Wang F et al. revealed that FXYD2 negatively regulates NKA activity, contributing to inflammation-induced persistent pain hypersensitivity [ ]. In addition, Cellini A et al. found that mice with overexpression of the α2 subtype of Na+/K+-ATPase in the myocardium exhibited prevention of functional deterioration and adverse cardiac remodeling by minimizing β-adrenergic desensitization and improving calcium handling in cardiac cells [ ]. Consequently, cardiac arrest following MI may be associated with impaired NKA function and ion imbalance. Given the potential impact of FXYD2 in modulating cardiac function, it is crucial to explore whether there is a regulatory interaction between FXYD2, mepivacaine, and myocardial cells to deeply understand the specific mechanisms influencing myocardial infarction.
The purpose of this investigation was to discover the complex mechanisms of mepivacaine-induced cardiotoxicity, focusing on the role of the FXYD2 gene, a regulator of ion transport in cardiomyocytes. Given the relevance of FXYD2 in cardiac function and its potential interaction with mepivacaine, we sought to elucidate the impacts of FXYD2 regulation on cardiomyocyte viability, apoptosis, and oxidative stress. Furthermore, by elucidating the interaction between FXYD2 activity, mepivacaine induction, and the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, we aimed to investigate the mechanisms underlying the role of FXYD2 in mepivacaine-induced cardiotoxicity.

## Materials and methods

## Integrative analysis of the GSE19339 data set and mepivacaine-related genes
To investigate potential targets associated with myocardial infarction (MI), we selected the GSE19339 data set from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gds/). This data set comprises samples from 4 MI patients and 4 healthy individuals. The “Limma” package in R was used to carry out the differential expression analysis. Genes with fold change (FC) > 1.3 were considered upregulated, while those with FC < 0.7 were considered downregulated, with significance established at p < 0.05. To explore genes associated with mepivacaine, data for 29 genes were acquired from the PubChem database (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) (Supplementary Table 1). Subsequently, a bioinformatics platform ((https://bioinformatics.psb.ugent.be/webtools/Venn/) was utilized for conducting a comparison analysis between up-regulated differentially expressed genes (DEGs), down-regulated DEGs, and mepivacaine-related genes, aiming to identify mepivacaine-related genes in MI.

## Enrichment analysis and expression validation of eight mepivacaine-related genes in MI
Functional enrichment analysis was performed using Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways and Gene Ontology (GO) terms for Biological Processes (BP), Cellular Components (CC), and Molecular Functions (MF) using the Database for Annotation, Visualization, and Integrated Discovery (DAVID) (https://david.ncifcrf.gov/tools.jsp). The expression profiles in the GSE19339 data set were then analyzed to distinguish the expression degrees of eight overlapping genes in samples from the normal group and the MI group, and the “ggplot2” package of R program was utilized for data processing and visualization.

## Cell culture, transfection, and treatment
Rat myocardial cell line H9c2 was provided by Cell Bank (Shanghai, China). Cultivation involved the use of Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM, 11,965,092, Invitrogen, USA), supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Cat No. A5669701, Thermo Fisher scientific, USA), and kept in a humidified environment with 5% CO2 at 37 °C. For transfection, H9c2 cells were seeded in 24-well plates at a density of 2 × 105 cells per well and allowed to grow overnight to reach approximately 70–80% confluence. Transfection procedures adhered to the manufacturer's guidelines, employing Lipofectamine 2000 transfection reagent (Invitrogen, Shanghai, China). Specifically, specific small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) aiming for FXYD2 (si-FXYD2-1 and si-FXYD2-2) or negative control siRNA (si-NC) were transfected into H9c2 cells. Standardizing the protocol, transfections were conducted in 24-well plates. Further experiments were conducted 48 h post-transfection.
To explore the impacts of mepivacaine (Cat No. HY-B0517, MedChemExpress, USA) on H9c2 cells, cells were treated with varying measures of mepivacaine (0, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 1, 1.2, 1.4, and 1.6 mM) for various durations (0, 12, 24, and 36 h). This allowed for the determination of optimal treatment concentrations and durations. In addition, to further elucidate the impact of FXYD2 and mepivacaine on the PI3K/AKT pathway, H9c2 cells transfected by FXYD2 knockdown plasmids and treated with 1 mM mepivacaine were subjected to subsequent experiments with the addition of 10 μM of the 4002) for 24 h.

## Cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) assay
The CCK-8 test (CK04, Dojindo, China) was used to measure the vitality of the cells. After treatment, a density of 5 × 103 cells per well was used to seed H9c2 cells in 96-well plates. Afterward, each well-received 10 μl of CCK-8 reagent added to it. The plates were then cultured for 4 h at 37 °C with 5% CO2 in a cell culture incubator. Following the incubation period, a microplate reader (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) was used to detect absorbance at 450 nm. To verify that the results could be repeated and are accurate, each experiment was carried out three times.

## Gene expression analysis in H9c2 cells through quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)
To examine the expression of genes in H9c2 cells, the RNeasy kit (Cat. No. 74106, Qiagen, Germany) was used to extract total RNA. cDNA synthesis was performed with the QuantiTect SYBR Green PCR kit (Cat. No. 205313, Qiagen, Germany). Subsequently, SYBR Green PCR Master Mix (Cat. No. 4312704, Applied Biosystems, USA) was utilized for qRT-PCR on the StepOnePlus Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, USA). Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) acted as an internal reference for the standardization of the data. The primer sequences used for amplification are listed in Table  . Gene expression analysis was conducted using the 2−ΔΔCT method.
 

## Western blotting (WB)
H9c2 cells were decomposed using RIPA buffer (pH 8.0), and the BCA protein assay kit (CWBIO, China) was applied to quantify the isolated proteins. Proteins were separated on an SDS–PAGE gel and then transferred to a polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) (Cat No. YA1701, Solarbio, Beijing, China) membrane. At 4 °C for an hour, the membrane was sealed with PBST containing 5% BSA. Overnight incubation with primary antibodies was conducted at a dilution of 1:1000 for FXYD2 (Cat No. 11198-1-AP), GAPDH (Cat No. 60004-1-Ig), cleaved-PARP (Cat No. 60555–1-Ig), cleaved-caspase3 (Cat No. 68773-1-Ig), p-AKT (Ser473, Cat No. 66444-1-Ig), AKT (Cat No. 10176-2-AP), p-PI3K p85 α (Cat No. 60225-1-Ig), PI3K (Cat No. 67071-1-Ig), GRP78 (Cat No. 11587-1-AP), and CHOP (Cat No. 15204-1-AP) (all from Proteintech, Wuhan, China). After the primary antibody was incubated, membranes were exposed to an hour-long treatment with HRP-conjugated goat anti-rabbit secondary antibody (1:2000; Cat No. SA00001-2, Proteintech). The Amersham Pharmacia Biotech ECL system was used to visualize protein bands, and protein band quantification was performed using ImageJ software from the National Institutes of Health.

## Flow cytometry
In this study, we employed flow cytometry (ACEA NovoCyte, Hangzhou, China) to analyze apoptosis in H9c2 cells. Initially, H9c2 cells were cultivated for 24 h after being seeded in 24-well plates at a density of 1 × 104 cells per well. Following trypsin–EDTA dissociation and PBS wash, apoptosis analysis was conducted by incubating cells for 15 min at room temperature with 5 μl of Annexin V-FITC and 5 μl of PI solution, followed by measurement of Annexin V and PI staining using FACSCalibur instrument (BD Biosciences) and data analysis using FlowJo software.

## Assessing reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) release in H9c2 cells
The ROS (Cat No. S0033S) and LDH (Cat No. C0016) cytotoxicity test kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) was used to examine the generation of ROS and the release of LDH in H9c2 cells. H9c2 cells were plated in 96-well cell culture plates at a density of 1 × 104 cells per well. For ROS measurement, cells were incubated with 10 μM DCFH–DA at 37 °C for 30 min in the dark according to the manufacturer’s instructions, followed by washing with serum-free medium to remove excess probe. Intracellular ROS levels were quantified using a fluorescence microplate reader (BioTek Instruments) at an excitation wavelength of 488 nm and an emission wavelength of 525 nm. For LDH measurement, cell culture supernatants were collected, and LDH release was assessed following the manufacturer’s protocol. Absorbance was measured at 490 nm using a microplate reader (BioTek Instruments) to determine LDH activity as an indicator of cytotoxicity.

## Measurement of oxidative stress biomarkers in H9c2 cells
A 96-well cell culture plate was used to seed H9c2 cells at a density of 1 × 104 cells per well. Cell lysates were obtained by centrifuging the cells at 10,000 × g for 5 min after they had been washed twice with PBS, lysed in cell lysis buffer. The granules of cells were sonicated and restored to suspension in the ice-cold buffer for determination of malondialdehyde (MDA, Cat No. S0131S, Beyotime, Shanghai, China) content as well as the activities of glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px, Cat No. S0056, Beyotime, Shanghai, China), catalase (CAT, Cat No. MAK531, Sigma-Aldrich, USA), and superoxide dismutase (SOD, Cat No. S0101S, Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Measurements were carried out by the manufacturer's instructions using the appropriate commercial assay kits (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China).

## JC-1 assays
JC-1 was detected using a mitochondrial membrane potential detection kit (Cat No. #M8650, Beijing Solarbio Science&Technology Co., Ltd). The cells were pre-seeded in glass-bottom cell culture dishes. After the cells were completely attached to the wall, the laser confocal detection (Carl Zeiss AG, ZEISS LSM 880) was performed according to the instructions. Under normal conditions, the mitochondria were in the multimolecular state and emitted red fluorescence (excitation wavelength 585 nm, emission wavelength 590 nm). When apoptosis occurred, JC-1 was in a monomeric state and produced green fluorescence (excitation wavelength 514 nm, emission wavelength 529 nm).

## Determination of mitochondrial respiratory chain complex activities
Quantitative determination kits from Solarbio (Beijing, China) were used to measure the activities of the mitochondrial respiratory chain complex I–III. Complex I (NADH–ubiquinone oxidoreductase) activity (Cat No. BC0515, Solarbio, Beijing, China) was assessed by monitoring the reduction of NADH, indicated by the decrease in absorbance at 340 nm. The activity of Complex II (succinate dehydrogenase, Cat No. BC3230, Solarbio, Beijing, China) was measured by monitoring the variations in absorbance of 2,6-dichlorophenolindophenol (DCPIP) at 600 nm. Variations in the absorbance value of cytochrome C at 550 nm were observed for complex III (cytochrome c reductase) activity (Cat No. BC3240, Solarbio, Beijing, China).

## Measurement of cellular adenosine triphosphate (ATP) content
The cellular ATP amount was assessed using the ATP Assay Kit (Cat No. S0026, Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Following respective treatments, H9c2 cells were taken out and put through an ice-cold ATP lysis buffer lysate. The resulting lysates were centrifuged at 12,000 × g for 10 min at 4 °C, and the supernatants were collected. Subsequently, the ATP detection buffer included in the kit was combined with 10 μL of the supernatant. Luminescence indicative of ATP content was determined by a microplate reader (BioTek Instruments).

## Statistical analysis
The R software package (version 3.4.1) was utilized for comprehensive data set analysis. Student's t test was employed to evaluate intergroup variations, and the results were presented as mean ± SD. Tukey's post-hoc test, coupled with analysis of variance (ANOVA), was applied to recognize variations across many groups. For statistical significance, a threshold of p < 0.05 was determined. All experiments were performed with at least three biological replicates.

## Results

## Interaction analysis reveals eight overlapping genes between mepivacaine and MI
Samples from the GSE19339 data set, which was obtained from the GEO database, showed 2396 upregulated DEGs and 1230 downregulated DEGs during differential expression analysis (Fig.  A). Further intersection analysis identified eight overlapping genes between GSE19339-up-regulated DEGs, down-regulated DEGs and mepivacaine-related genes, namely, ATP1B1, ATP1B3, CACNA2D2, CACNB1, CHRNA4, FXYD2, HTR1A, and POMC (Fig.  B). 

## Functional enrichment and expression analysis of eight mepivacaine-related genes in MI
Subsequent functional enrichment analysis of these overlapping genes unveiled their significant involvement in GO term analysis, such as “Sodium Ion Export Across Plasma Membrane,” “ Intracellular Potassium Ion Homeostasis,” and “ Intracellular Sodium Ion Homeostasis,” as evident from BP. In terms of CC, the overlapping genes were enriched in terms, such as “Cation-Transporting ATPase Complex,” “Sodium: Potassium-Exchanging ATPase Complex,” and “Voltage-Gated Calcium Channel Complex.” Moreover, MF analysis revealed enrichment in “Voltage-Gated Monoatomic Cation Channel Activity,” “ATPase Binding,” and “Ligand-Gated Channel Activity,” among others (Fig.  C). In the KEGG pathways, the majority of overlapping genes were enriched in pathways, such as “Cardiac muscle contraction,” “cAMP signaling pathway,” and “Carbohydrate digestion and absorption” (Fig.  D). Analysis of expression was carried out on eight overlapping genes in the normal group and MI group in the GSE19339 data set (Fig.  E). The findings demonstrated that in contrast to the control group, ATP1B1, ATP1B3, CACNB1, CHRNA4, FXYD2 and HTR1A were considerably more expressed in the MI group, while CACNA2D2 was less expressed in the MI group. Apart from this, the expression difference of POMC between the two groups was not significant. The study identified FXYD2, a gene closely associated with cardiac function, as a central focus given that overlapping genes have been shown to be involved in key pathways related to intracellular and extracellular ion balance, specifically sodium and potassium ions. This decision highlights the critical role of FXYD2 in the mechanisms of heart disease in the context of our findings.

## Impact of FXYD2 knockdown on mepivacaine-induced cell viability in H9c2 cells
To elucidate the effect of mepivacaine on H9c2 cells, we first assessed the survival of H9c2 cells exposed to various mepivacaine concentrations (0, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 1, 1.2, 1.4, and 1.6 mM) using the CCK-8 assay. Our findings showed the viability of cells steadily reduced as the concentration of mepivacaine increased (Fig.  A). Subsequently, we evaluated the capacity of H9c2 cells exposed to 1 mM mepivacaine at various times (0, 12, 24, and 36 h). The findings revealed that cell viability declined progressively with prolonged treatment time (Fig.  B). This suggests that mepivacaine exerts an inhibitory effect on myocardial cell viability, with the inhibitory effect reaching half at 1 mM concentration and 24-h treatment, prompting the selection of these conditions for subsequent cell treatment. Confirmation of effective knockdown of FXYD2 in H9c2 cells was achieved through qRT-PCR and WB analysis (Fig.  C–E), with si-FXYD2-1 demonstrating superior knockdown efficiency. Therefore, si-FXYD2-1 was chosen for further experiments as the knockdown plasmid targeting FXYD2. The impact of combined treatment with 1 mM mepivacaine and FXYD2 knockdown on H9c2 cell viability was evaluated with the CCK-8 test. The findings showed that in contrast to the control group, FXYD2 knockdown significantly enhanced cell viability and rescued the suppressed cell viability induced by 1 mM mepivacaine treatment (Fig.  F). Additional experiments examining FXYD2 expression revealed that mepivacaine treatment significantly upregulated FXYD2 expression, while FXYD2 knockdown reduced its expression compared with the mepivacaine-only group (Figs. G and H), indicating the modulatory effect of FXYD2 under mepivacaine exposure. 

## Knockdown of FXYD2 alleviates mepivacaine-induced ROS and PI3K/AKT pathway inactivation in H9c2 cells
To further investigate the interplay between FXYD2, the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, and mepivacaine, we assessed the viability of H9c2 cells using the CCK8 assay after si-FXYD2-1 transfection and treatment with mepivacaine or the PI3K inhibitor (LY294002). As depicted in Fig.  A, knocking down FXYD2 reversed the mepivacaine-induced reduction in H9c2 cell viability, while this reversal was resisted upon the inclusion of LY294002 to block the AKT/PI3K signaling cascade. Subsequently, we examined the levels of intracellular ROS and LDH in H9c2 cells handled with si-FXYD2-1, mepivacaine, or LY294002. Compared to the control group, FXYD2 knockdown led to a reduction in intracellular ROS and LDH levels, while mepivacaine increased ROS and LDH levels. Interestingly, the introduction of si-FXYD2−1 mitigated mepivacaine-induced ROS and LDH production, and this attenuation was reversed upon the addition of LY294002 (Fig.  B, ). Finally, WB analysis was performed to evaluate FXYD2, PI3K and AKT protein levels and their phosphorylation levels in H9c2 cells treated with si-FXYD2−1, mepivacaine, or LY294002. The results showed that FXYD2 knockdown activated the PI3K/AKT pathway, as indicated by elevated levels of p-PI3K and p-AKT. In contrast, mepivacaine treatment upregulated FXYD2 protein expression, thereby suppressing pathway activation and reducing phosphorylation. Moreover, the addition of LY294002 markedly inhibited PI3K/AKT signaling, underscoring its role in modulating the cellular responses to FXYD2 knockdown and mepivacaine exposure in H9c2 cells. Collectively, these findings highlight the regulatory interplay between FXYD2 and mepivacaine within the PI3K/AKT signaling cascade.

## FXYD2 modulates altered apoptosis and oxidative stress in mepivacaine-treated H9c2 cells via the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway
Flow cytometry analysis was employed to assess apoptosis in H9c2 cells after si-FXYD2−1transfection and treatment with mepivacaine or the PI3K inhibitor LY294002. In comparison with the control group, FXYD2 knockdown considerably decreased apoptosis, while the addition of mepivacaine markedly increased apoptotic rates. Notably, FXYD2 knockdown inhibited mepivacaine-induced apoptosis, and this inhibition was reversed upon the addition of LY294002 (Fig.  A, ). Subsequent WB analysis confirmed these findings by revealing alterations in the levels of cleaved caspase3 and cleaved PARP proteins in H9c2 cells treated with si-FXYD2−1, mepivacaine, or LY294002 (Fig.  C, ). MDA assay further supported these observations, demonstrating that knocking down FXYD2 inhibited the mepivacaine-induced increase in MDA levels in H9c2 cells. However, this inhibition was resisted upon the inclusion of LY294002 to inhibit the PI3K/AKT signaling cascade (Fig.  E). Finally, we investigated the functions of SOD, CAT, and GSH-Px in H9c2 cells treated with si-FXYD2−1, mepivacaine, or LY294002. FXYD2 knockdown reversed the mepivacaine-induced decrease in CAT, SOD, and GSH-Px activities, while this reversal was resisted upon the inclusion of LY294002 to inhibit the PI3K/AKT signaling cascade (Fig.  F–H). 

## FXYD2 regulates mitochondrial respiratory chain activity and endoplasmic reticulum stress in mepivacaine-treated H9c2 cells
To further investigate the potential relationship between FXYD2 and mitochondria, mitochondrial membrane potential was assessed using the JC-1 fluorescent probe, which measures depolarization according to the red/green fluorescence ratio. Compared with the control group, mepivacaine treatment induced significant mitochondrial depolarization. Knockdown of FXYD2 attenuated this depolarization, whereas the addition of LY294002 reversed the protective effect of FXYD2 knockdown (Fig.  A). Mitochondrial respiratory chain activity, a pivotal process in cellular energy production involving complexes I, II, and III, was assessed in H9c2 cells after si-FXYD2−1 transfection and treatment with mepivacaine or the PI3K inhibitor LY294002. In contrast to the group under control, FXYD2 knockdown notably increased the activity of mitochondrial respiratory chain complexes I, II, and III. Conversely, the addition of mepivacaine markedly reduced the activities of these complexes. Furthermore, FXYD2 knockdown reversed the mepivacaine-induced decrease in the actions of mitochondrial respiratory chain complexes I, II, and III, while this reversal was resisted upon the inclusion of LY294002 to inhibit the PI3K/AKT pathway (Fig.  B). The impact of FXYD2 modification was confirmed by measuring the amounts of ATP in H9c2 cells using an ATP assay kit. FXYD2 knockdown significantly increased ATP content in H9c2 cells, indicating upregulation of cellular energy reserves. However, treatment with mepivacaine alone or in combination with si-FXYD2−1 significantly reduced ATP levels. On this basis, lower levels were observed when the PI3K inhibitor LY294002 was further added to the combination therapy. This finding was consistent with the observed changes in mitochondrial respiratory chain activity (Fig.  C). Finally, the mRNA and protein levels of GRP78 and CHOP, proteins related to endoplasmic reticulum stress in H9c2 cells were evaluated by qRT-PCR and WB after si-FXYD2−1 transfection and treatment with mepivacaine or LY294002. Knockdown FXYD2 led to a notable decrease in the level of GRP78 and CHOP in comparison with the control group. Conversely, these endoplasmic reticulum stress-related proteins were more highly expressed when mepivacaine was added. FXYD2 knockdown inhibited the mepivacaine-induced increase in GRP78 and CHOP expression, and this inhibition was reversed upon the addition of LY294002 (Fig.  D–G). These findings suggest a role for FXYD2 in modulating endoplasmic reticulum stress and mitochondrial function in H9c2 cells treated with mepivacaine. 

## Schematic diagram of the mechanism of action of FXYD2 on mepivacaine-induced cardiomyocyte injury
Mepivacaine exposure upregulated FXYD2 expression, which further activated the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, leading to oxidative damage. Subsequently, an exacerbation of oxidative stress was noted, contributing to mitochondrial dysfunction and damage. The study further uncovered a feedback loop, wherein oxidative stress exacerbated ER stress, and both mitochondrial impairment and ER stress synergistically promoted apoptotic cell death (Fig.  ). 

## Discussion
MI is the most common cause of cardiogenic shock and seriously threatens human life and health. With the continuous deepening of the understanding of anesthetic drugs, the key role of various anesthetic drugs in the treatment of myocardial infarction has received widespread attention. As classic inhibitors of neuronal sodium channels, local anesthetics can block peripheral rapid voltage-gated sodium channels on neuronal axons and show notably elevated activity in the cardiovascular and central nervous systems. A study by Landoni G et al. compared complete intravenous anesthesia with the use of volatile anesthetics during elective coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) surgery in 5400 patients and indicated no discernible difference in 1-year mortality between the two groups [ ]. Furthermore, a study by Lotz C et al. showed that sevoflurane anesthesia had a protective function on cardiac ischemia/reperfusion (I/R) injury in rabbits compared with propofol, and this protective effect was attributed to mitochondrial function of preservation [ ]. Pegel PS et al. provide a historical review and a modern viewpoint on the possible use of volatile anesthetics for myocardial preparation in cardiac anesthesia practice are presented [ ]. Furthermore, the study by Smit M et al. highlights the importance of type 2 myocardial oxygen supply and demand mismatch as a key pathophysiological factor in perioperative MI, providing anesthesia providers with insights into predicting and mitigating triggers, ultimately reducing the incidence of such events [ ].
Mepivacaine is a local anesthetic well-known for its rapid onset and long duration, and is widely used clinically for its nerve conduction blocking effect. In this study, analysis of MI-related DEGs in the GSE19339 data set and exploration of functional enrichment related to mepivacaine revealed significant enrichment of overlapping genes in terms of terms and pathways, such as sodium:potassium exchange ATPase complexes, calcium channel activity, cAMP signaling pathway, and myocardial contraction. Studies exploring the relationship between Na +/K + -ATPase (NKA) and MI found that NKA-α2 expression increased in the heart after MI, improving cardiac function and resuscitation by reducing calcium desensitization and enhancing β-adrenergic reactivity [ ]. In addition, another study highlighted the importance of calcium ions in maintaining cardiomyocyte excitation–contraction coupling and its part in the onset of cardiac illness [ ]. Especially in myocardial ischemia/reperfusion (MI/R) injury, excess calcium may exacerbate MI/R injury. Furthermore, research has indicated that the cAMP signaling pathway is involved in regulating the adaptation of cardiomyocytes to various external stimuli [ ]. Although MI leads to cardiomyocyte death and impaired cardiac function, long-term activation of the cAMP signaling pathway can compensate for this loss to a certain extent. By analyzing overlapping gene expression, this study found that FXYD2 is highly expressed in MI patients, suggesting a potential association between FXYD2 and MI pathogenesis.
The PI3K–AKT signaling pathway constitutes an essential intracellular signaling cascade and regulates various cellular biological processes. Research has demonstrated that this pathway participates in the pathophysiology of various heart diseases, not only regulating cardiomyocyte survival, angiogenesis, and inflammatory responses, but also participating in myocardial injury induced by substances, such as H2O2, mercury, and lipopolysaccharide [ ]. Research by Feng Q et al. demonstrated that the PTEN inhibitor BPV enhances cardiac function, reduces apoptosis, and activates the PI3K/Akt/VEGF pathway in myocardial infarction models [ ]. Similarly, Zhao X et al. reported that lncRNA MIAT decreases myocardial fibrosis and inflammation in heart failure (HF) by activating the PI3K/Akt pathway, improving HF conditions [ ]. FXYD2, crucial in MI research for its role in Na, K-ATPase transport, impacts various bodily functions. Studies highlight its association with disease prognosis and immune regulation, potentially through pathways, such as TGF-β–SMAD2/3 and PI3K–AKT–mTOR [ ]. The study of Arystarkhova E et al. demonstrates the effects of FXYD2 on sodium balance, blood pressure, glycemic control, and β-cell mass via the PI3K–Akt pathway, highlighting its potential to treat hypertension and diabetes [ ]. While there is not received much attention on the function of FXYD2 in MI, our experiments explored this aspect. In vitro experiments revealed that knocking down FXYD2 rescued mepivacaine-induced cell death in H9c2 cells, mitigating oxidative stress induced by mepivacaine and reversing the suppression of the PI3K/AKT pathway. The rescue effect was reversed upon the addition of a PI3K inhibitor (LY294002), providing insights into the potential interplay between FXYD2, mepivacaine, and MI.
In cardiovascular disease, oxidative stress impacts myocardial infarction (MI) and atherosclerosis, notably through apoptosis regulators, such as PARP and caspase-3. Wang Q et al. discovered that atorvastatin-induced tolerogenic dendritic cells (tDC) alleviate myocardial cell apoptosis post-infarction by inhibiting TLR-4/NF-κB activation, contributing to improved cardiac remodeling after MI [ ]. MDA, CAT, GSH-Px, and SOD activities are key indicators for assessing oxidative stress and antioxidant defense capabilities. Oxidative stress plays a crucial role in cardiovascular pathologies, including myocardial infarction and atherosclerosis, prompting research into its effects on myocardial apoptosis for potential therapies. Huang L et al. showed that GPR19 inhibition could mitigate ischemia–reperfusion injury by reducing apoptosis and fibrosis via miR-324-5p and Mtfr1, thereby lessening oxidative stress [ ]. Abdelzaher WY et al. discovered that DAP reduced isoproterenol-induced myocardial damage through the NRF2/HO-1 and TLR4/TNF-α pathways, curbing oxidative stress and inflammation [ ]. Poznyak AV et al. underscored oxidative stress's role in atherosclerosis progression, involving enzymes, such as NADPH oxidase and xanthine oxidase, emphasizing the significance of managing oxidative mechanisms in the treatment of cardiovascular disease [ ]. In the research by Hu M et al., the evaluation of these activities revealed that in polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), abnormal expression of FXYD2-associated genes during pregnancy, induced by high androgen levels and insulin resistance, increases the risk of miscarriage [ ]. This may occur through mechanisms involving uterine functional defects, mitochondria-mediated oxidative stress, inflammation, and gene expression dysregulation. Our experiments delved into the assessment of cellular apoptosis and oxidative stress to elucidate the role of FXYD2. The findings suggested that knockdown FXYD2 could trigger the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, inhibiting mepivacaine-induced apoptosis and oxidative stress in H9c2 cells. In Fig.  C, we noted basal caspase-3 and PARP cleavage in the control group despite a low apoptosis rate by Annexin V/PI staining. We speculate that this may reflect the higher sensitivity of caspase/PARP cleavage as early apoptotic markers, whereas Annexin V/PI detects later stages, thus explaining the discrepancy.
Mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation provides 90% of the required ATP in the heart. A study has indicated that activation of the PI3K–AKT–Gsk-3β pathway and mitochondrial apoptosis are associated with hypoxia/reoxygenation-induced myocardial cell damage [ ]. Empagliflozin has been demonstrated to lessen inflammation and oxidative stress in heart failure patients, enhancing the NO–sGC–cGMP cascade [ ]. According to the research of Cai S. et al., loss of mitochondrial complex I protein in macrophages leads to mitochondrial dysfunction, resulting in increased inflammation and reduced repair after AMI [ ]. Interventions targeting mitochondrial ROS production can intervene and improve this condition. Research by Ramachandra CJA et al. emphasized the critical role of cardiac mitochondria in cell survival and death during AMI, highlighting the importance of preventing mitochondrial dysfunction caused by AMI as a therapeutic strategy for cardiac protection [ ]. Extensive research indicates an interaction between mitochondrial damage and ER stress [ ]. GRP78 and CHOP are proteins associated with cellular stress responses, ER stress, and cell survival. When GRP78 binds to unfolded proteins, it releases IRE1α, PERK, and ATF6. These proteins further regulate signaling pathways in ER stress response, with CHOP acting as an effector protein involved in determining cell survival and apoptosis. Ruan Y et al. comprehensively discussed ER stress induced by I/R injury, emphasizing that this stress leads to myocardial damage by altering ER function and activating signaling pathways [ ]. Our findings reveal that FXYD2 knockdown enhances mitochondrial and ER functions, reducing mepivacaine-induced damage. This is supported by the prevention of PI3K/AKT signaling pathway activation, suggesting the potential of FXYD2 in cardioprotective strategies by modulating mitochondrial and ER stress responses.
Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, although our data suggested that ROS may inhibit the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, we did not perform validation experiments with ROS inhibitors, such as 2-MPG, which restricts the mechanistic certainty of this conclusion. Second, our assessment of mitochondrial dysfunction was limited to complexes I–III, while complex IV, an essential component of the respiratory chain, was not examined. Third, the identification of differentially expressed genes relied solely on the GSE19339 data set with a small sample size (four MI patients and four healthy controls), which may reduce the generalizability of the findings. Fourth, the cardioprotective role of FXYD2 was inferred mainly from in vitro models and correlative analyses, without evidence from animal models or clinical validation. Finally, causal involvement of the PI3K/AKT pathway was inferred based on pharmacologic inhibition only; additional gain-of-function or rescue experiments, such as FXYD2 overexpression, are warranted to further substantiate the mechanistic conclusions. Future studies incorporating larger data sets, animal models, clinical validation, and complementary mechanistic approaches will be necessary to overcome these limitations and strengthen the translational relevance of our findings.

## Conclusion
Our research elucidates the critical function of FXYD2 in attenuating mepivacaine-induced cardiotoxicity. Through comprehensive analysis, we identified the critical role of FXYD2 downregulation in activating the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, thereby reducing apoptosis and oxidative stress in cardiomyocytes after mepivacaine treatment. Furthermore, the effects of FXYD2 extend to protecting mitochondrial function and alleviating endoplasmic reticulum stress, establishing a protective mechanism against the harmful effects of mepivacaine. These findings highlight the potential of targeting FXYD2 for therapeutic intervention in mepivacaine-induced cardiac dysfunction, providing new insights into the prevention and treatment of myocardial injury.
