This retrospective population-based research examined the effect of the COVID-19 pandemic on the prevalence of influenza-like illness (ILI) cases and influenza positive cases in Sichuan Province. Interrupted time series analysis (ITS) was conducted using data gathered from surveillance hospitals throughout 21 cities, including information on patients diagnosed with ILI and positive nucleic acid testing. Subsequent to the implementation of NPIs, there was a notable temporary reduction in both the incidence of ILI cases and confirmed influenza cases, decreasing by 94.7% and 98.0%, respectively (P<0.001). The weekly trend in influenza-positive cases was notable (+4.1%/week, P=0.001). Following the execution of the second-phase intervention, the fluctuations in the transient changes of ILI patients and influenza-positive cases varied; nonetheless, both subsequently exhibited a significant declining trend (P<0.001). Temperature had a substantial negative correlation with both indicators (P<0.001), resulting in a reduction of instances by 13.0% and 11.5%, respectively; humidity demonstrated no statistically significant impact on either. Our data demonstrate that NPIs significantly reduce two influenza outcome indicators in the short term; nevertheless, they do not effectuate a sustainable alteration in the long-term increasing trend of influenza. Through the examination of the dynamic effects of NPIs on influenza transmission, we provide policymakers with an enhanced evaluative instrument, a decision-making framework, and theoretical backing, while also contemplating the prospective advantages and hazards of NPIs for additional respiratory infectious diseases in the future.

## Introduction
The COVID-19 pandemic started in early 2020 and is an infectious illness caused by SARS-CoV-2 . Its extremely infectious and pathogenic nature swiftly instigated a global public health catastrophe, resulting in the rapid infection of millions worldwide . The World Health Organization (WHO) designated COVID-19 as a pandemic in 2020. In the absence of a developed vaccine, WHO has implemented a variety of NPIs and preventative strategies to mitigate the transmission of the virus, which include mask-wearing, regular ventilation, hand cleanliness, and adherence to social distancing protocols . Since January 2020, the Chinese government has implemented NPIs to curb the transmission of COVID-19, including stringent measures such as social distancing, movement restrictions, limitations on group dining, school closures, extensive screening, and lockdowns . Numerous researches have shown that these NPIs are essential for decreasing transmission rates, sustaining healthcare systems, and safeguarding public health , , . These measures resulted in a rapid reduction in COVID-19 cases in China . NPIs have shown efficacy in several countries in curbing the transmission of respiratory infectious illnesses, including influenza – .
Influenza is a highly transmissible respiratory illness caused by RNA influenza viruses. Influenza epidemics may impact millions globally annually, resulting in around 500,000 fatalities , . Influenza affects individuals of all ages and, similar to COVID-19, may be transmitted by airborne droplets, such as those released while sneezing or coughing . The majority of those infected with influenza have minor symptoms, including fever, headache, and cough, and do not require specialized medicine. Nonetheless, the considerable genetic variability of influenza viruses makes treatment of influenza a formidable objective . Influenza prevention is achievable with vaccination prior to the onset of the annual flu season .
NPIs do not entirely mitigate influenza and COVID-19 infections – . Certain investigations indicate the presence of viral interference between the two viruses. In the event that an individual contracts COVID-19 followed by influenza, the viral load of the influenza may be reduced – . The methods and consequences of this reciprocal interference are challenging to anticipate, since the two viruses use diverse techniques to circumvent interferon activation and its related signaling pathways .
Until early 2023, Sichuan Province adhered rigorously to China’s NPIs strategy, which included enhanced screening, patient isolation, and management of imported cases. Nonetheless, given both illnesses are airborne respiratory infections, despite the use of stringent preventive, control, and intervention measures, the transmission of influenza and the SARS-CoV-2 has not been entirely curtailed . This motivated us to investigate the enduring effects of NPIs on influenza throughout the COVID-19 pandemic and after to their cessation, rather than concentrating just on the lockdown phase. This study seeks to investigate the immediate and enduring impacts of NPIs on influenza transmission. We utilized time-series data on ILI cases and laboratory-confirmed influenza cases in Sichuan Province for Interrupted time series analysis (ITSA). Utilizing Bernal’s impact model framework, we hypothesize that the effectiveness of NPIs not just the immediate rate of influenza transmission but may also modify transmission patterns. Following a decrease in population immunity, both the trajectory and magnitude of influenza transmission may alter. Prolonged effects may emerge as a resurgence in transmission patterns or deferred consequences . Our objective is to furnish data support and reference metrics for the prevention and management of influenza and other respiratory infectious diseases in the future, to mitigate public health emergencies and assure the seamless functioning of the healthcare system.

## Methods

## Data sources and management
ITSA is a method for evaluating the effects of human interventions or significant alterations in the external environment on time series data, frequently employed to analyze health policies . We used two-stage ITSA to assess the impact of non-pharmaceutical intervention policies on influenza epidemic trends through a piecewise regression model.
This study used a retrospective research approach and was carried out in Sichuan Province, China, from January 1, 2017, to December 31, 2024. Sichuan Province is situated in southwestern China. The region comprises 21 cities, spans an area of 480,000 square kilometers, and has a permanent population of 83.72 million . The participants were categorized into three groups before to and during the execution of NPIs during the COVID-19 pandemic: (1) Baseline period prior to intervention (before the implementation of NPIs): week 1 of 2017 to week 4 of 2020; (2) Initial period post-intervention (during the implementation of NPIs): week 5 of 2020 to week 5 of 2023; (3) Subsequent period post-intervention (following the cessation of NPIs): week 6 of 2023 to week 52 of 2024.

## Variables
The outcome variables included the count of ILI cases and the count of laboratory-confirmed influenza cases. ILI cases were identified by healthcare professionals in accordance with the National Influenza Surveillance Technical Guidelines (2017 edition), encompassing data that satisfied the clinical diagnostic criteria for influenza-like illness, specifically fever (temperature ≥ 38℃) accompanied by symptoms such as cough or sore throat. Laboratory-confirmed influenza cases pertain to instances that tested positive for the influenza virus via real-time fluorescence reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR). The case data were sourced from the influenza network surveillance data inside the China Disease Control and Prevention Information System. In 21 cities within Sichuan Province, a minimum of one hospital per city was designated as a regular influenza monitoring facility, amounting to a total of 30 hospitals. The data included in this investigation were gathered from fever clinics, internal medicine clinics, pediatric clinics, and their respective emergency departments within these surveillance hospitals. The obtained samples mostly consisted of nose and pharyngeal swabs, along with respiratory lavage fluid, tracheal extracts, and sputum. Weekly, the surveillance hospital gathered 10–40 specimens of ILI (averaging 20 per week) and promptly sent them to the laboratory for nucleic acid testing of the influenza virus. The staff documented the name, gender, age, location, collecting hospital, specimen type, and real-time polymerase chain reaction test results in the China Disease Control and Prevention Information System. This research included all surveillance data gathered by influenza monitoring hospitals in Sichuan Province throughout eight full epidemiological cycles, totaling 416 weeks. All influenza monitoring hospitals and labs performed tests continuously throughout the year, with no data omissions.
This study encompassed other variables such as the pre-intervention time trend, the instant level change at the first intervention, the post-intervention trend alteration, the immediate level change at the second intervention, and the subsequent post-intervention trend change. Level change denotes the instantaneous alteration in the number of cases at a particular moment before and after the intervention, usually indicating the direct effect of the intervention, which means an intervention may markedly increase/decrease the instances at a certain time point. Slope change, conversely, examines the alteration in trend prior to and following the intervention, whether the intervention modified the rate of growth in case numbers. We utilized temperature and humidity as environmental covariates, applying weekly average data to account for the potential influence of meteorological conditions on case incidence. Meteorological data were sourced from the Sichuan Provincial Meteorological Information Center (https://data.cma.cn/).

## Study design and statistical analysis
This study employed a negative binomial regression model, within the generalized linear model (GLM) framework, to analyze the impact of interventions on ILI cases and positive cases, due to the count data outcome indicators exhibiting significant dispersion (variance exceeding the mean). ACF (Autocorrelation Function)/PACF (Partial Autocorrelation Function) analysis was employed to identify autocorrelation in the residuals of the outcome indicator; if autocorrelation was detected, Newey-West robust standard errors were utilized for correction. The model’s fit was assessed utilizing pseudo R² and bias. A Z-test was employed to assess the statistical significance of the regression coefficients (α = 0.05), and 95% confidence intervals were computed to evaluate estimation precision. The IRR (Incidence Rate Ratio) and its 95% confidence interval (95% CI) were derived from the exponential regression coefficients (exp(β)) and transformed into percentage change ((IRR-1)×100%) to effectively illustrate the intervention’s relative effect on the occurrence of outcome indicators.

## Results
Table  displays the pertinent data for the 416 weeks encompassed in the study period, during which 2,707,386 occurrences of influenza-like illness were reported and 40,340 influenza-positive cases were identified. The observation durations prior to intervention, during post-intervention phase 1, and during post-intervention phase 2 were 160 weeks, 161 weeks, and 95 weeks, respectively. For ILI cases, the minimum counts in the three stages were 5, 78, and 384, while the maximum counts were 14,907, 58,769, and 111,008, respectively. The mean ± standard deviation were 1,515.29 ± 3,103.88, 1,788.32 ± 5,290.92, and 16,284.43 ± 25,197.95, respectively; the median (interquartile range) were 157.0 (43.5–823.75), 255.0 (173.0–1,252.0), and 3,646.0 (1,480.0–23,460.0), respectively. The count of positive instances diminished from a mean of 117.52 ± 119.16 prior to the intervention to 54.16 ± 87.85 following the adoption of NPIs, subsequently rising to 134.93 ± 156.03 after the second intervention. 
Significant autocorrelation was identified in the residuals of the outcome indicators by ACF/PACF analysis, with 6 lag orders for the ILI case and 12 lag orders for the Positive case (Figs.  and ). Consequently, we employed Heteroskedasticity and Autocorrelation Consistent (HAC) robust standard errors (maxlags = 12) for correction to guarantee the reliability of statistical inference. The model fit indices indicated that the Pseudo R² for the optimized model of the Positive example was 0.83 (p < 0.001), while the Pseudo R² for the optimal model of the ILI instance was 0.96 (p < 0.001), both aligning with the fitting characteristics of count time series data and demonstrating substantial resilience.

 

 

Figure  ; Table  illustrated the interrupted time-series analysis on the effect of NPIs on weekly influenza-like cases. The baseline incidence of ILI cases (β₀) was 6.56 (95% CI: 2.90–10.22, P = 0.000); the pre-intervention trend (β₁) was 0.03 (95% CI: 0.02–0.04, P = 0.000), resulting in an incidence rate ratio of 1.03 and a relative weekly increase of 3.0%. The immediate change in level (β₂) following the initial intervention was − 2.94 (95% CI: -3.84 to -2.04, P = 0.000), with an IRR of 0.05, indicating an instantaneous reduction of 95.0%. The trend alteration following intervention 1 (β₃) was 0.00 (P = 0.880), and the immediate level change post the second intervention (β₄) was 1.01 (P = 0.230), neither of which reached statistical significance. The trend modification subsequent to intervention 2 (β₅) was − 0.04 (95% CI: -0.06 to -0.02, P = 0.000), indicating a relative weekly reduction of 4.0%. Among the covariates, the coefficient for weekly average temperature was − 0.14 (95% CI: -0.19 to -0.09, P = 0.000), with an IRR of 0.87, signifying that for each 1 °C rise in temperature, ILI cases diminished by 13.0%; the coefficient for relative humidity was − 0.01 (P = 0.710).

The baseline level of positive cases (β₀) was 5.43 (P = 0.008); the pre-intervention trend (β₁) was 0.00 (P = 0.875). The immediate change in level following the initial intervention (β₂) was − 3.92 (P = 0.000), with an IRR of 0.02, indicating an immediate reduction of 98.0%. The trend alteration following intervention 1 (β₃) was 0.04 (95% CI: 0.02–0.06, P = 0.001), indicating a weekly relative increase of 4.0%. The immediate change in level (β₄) following the second intervention was − 1.09 (P = 0.380). The trend change (β₅) following intervention 2 was − 0.05 (P = 0.000), indicating a weekly IRR was 0.95 post-intervention, which signifies a relative decline of 5.0% each week. Among the covariates, the weekly average temperature coefficient was − 0.12 (P = 0.000), with IRR of 0.89, signifying that for each 1 °C rise in temperature, the number of positive cases diminished by 11.5%; the relative humidity coefficient was 0.01 (P = 0.670), which lacked statistical significance.

 

 

## Discussion
This study utilized a two-stage interrupted time series analysis alongside a negative binomial regression model to systematically assess the effects of two treatments on ILI and positive instances, while adjusting for temperature, humidity, and residual autocorrelation. The findings indicated that the two interventions produced distinct impacts on the two case categories, however both exhibited statistical significance in critical effect dimensions.
Our findings indicate that non-pharmaceutical measures can rapidly mitigate the transmission of the influenza virus in the short term. Following the installation of NPIs, there was a notable immediate reduction in both ILI cases and positive instances. We referenced analogous ITS studies from other regions, including the research by Yi and Chen , , which evaluated the influence of COVID-19 control measures on influenza transmission. The ITS approach was employed, and a notable decline in the influenza positive rate was found following the adoption of COVID-19 control measures, aligning with the immediate lowering impact attributed to NPIs in this study. The research conducted by Korsun, in Bulgaria indicated by ITS that influenza activity was curtailed during the outbreak, but subsequently surged following the cessation of measures . our reflects the increase in influenza-like illnesses following the removal of NPIs in our study, underscoring the widespread effects of NPIs and affirming their significant inhibitory influence, including social distancing, mask usage, and venue regulation, on the transmission of respiratory viruses . These procedures, by minimizing close contact and diminishing the danger of viral exposure, effectively sever the human-to-human transmission chain of the influenza virus, resulting in a precipitous decline in case numbers within a few timeframes. This affirms the prompt inhibitory impact of physical isolation and behavioral measures (such as mask usage and limiting social interactions) on viral transmission , .Influenza epidemics are affected by various causes, such as compromised immunity and viral alterations. This indicates that the transmission chain of influenza cannot be entirely disrupted, nor can the inherent upward trajectory of long-term influenza epidemics be altered , .Although non-pharmaceutical interventions can effectively reduce influenza transmission in the short term, prolonged application may result in the accumulation of “immune debt,” obstructing the natural development of herd immunity, which is evidenced by a resurgence in influenza-like illness and influenza-positive cases , .The underlying cause may be the population’s prolonged lack of exposure to the virus, leading to inadequate antibody levels, which subsequently precipitated a rapid resurgence of the influenza epidemic , .In our investigation, following the elimination of non-pharmacological therapies, ILI data escalated to 2.73 times the baseline. Although the positive data did not indicate a substantial instant increase, taking into account the bounce prior to the intervention, the aggregate data continues to encounter prospective growth pressures. This outcome might indicate that NPIs diminish immunological imprinting by lowering population exposure, allowing the virus to swiftly reestablish transmission chains in populations with minimal immune defenses upon the cessation of these measures. Prolonged non-pharmaceutical interventions may merely postpone rather than eradicate the peak of infectious disease outbreaks , . Nonetheless, the conclusive validation of this mechanism necessitates additional investigation in forthcoming studies that integrate serological surveillance and viral genotyping. Following the second phase of interventions, the trend in influenza transmission exhibited a marked shift. Weekly trends in both influenza-like illness and laboratory-confirmed influenza-positive cases continued to decline at rates of approximately 4% and 5% per week respectively. This outcome indicates that influenza activity did not immediately revert to pre-intervention baseline levels following the lifting of NPIs. Multiple factors may explain this downward trend. Firstly, stringent NPIs may have suppressed influenza transmission chains within communities to extremely low levels, necessitating considerable time for the virus to re-establish effective transmission networks following contact restrictions . Secondly, personal protective behaviors adopted during NPIs implementation—such as voluntary mask-wearing, enhanced hand hygiene, and self-isolation upon illness—became habitual. These practices were voluntarily maintained by some individuals after NPIs withdrawal, forming a persistent societal protective barrier . Moreover, the interventions may have altered the competitive dynamics among different respiratory viruses, thereby creating conditions conducive to the spread of other viruses and consequently suppressing the transmission of influenza viruses , . Naturally, such artificially suppressed epidemic levels may delay the accumulation of natural immunity within the population, potentially increasing the size of susceptible cohorts in future epidemic seasons. Long-term implications warrant further monitoring.
This study demonstrates the varied effects of NPIs on two categories of influenza surveillance indicators, offering a novel viewpoint for enhancing future infectious disease management methods. The inhibitory impact of non-pharmaceutical interventions on the positive influenza cases surpassed that on the ILI cases. This may result from alterations in population healthcare behavior and the distribution of testing resources during the execution of NPIs. For instance, stringent control measures may incentivize suspected cases to seek testing more proactively, hence enhancing the detection sensitivity of positive cases. The diagnosis of ILI cases depends on clinical judgment. If home isolation results in a reduction of patients attending medical facilities, certain mild ILI cases may be excluded from monitoring, hence diminishing the perceived efficacy of NPIs on ILI to a degree. The identification of positive cases depends mostly on established laboratory protocols and is less influenced by variations in healthcare practices, hence more effectively representing the suppressive impact of interventions on viral transmission .The cancellation of non-pharmaceutical therapies aligned with the onset of the cold season, thereby intensifying the effects and leading to a brief influenza outbreak. Nonetheless, the eventual attainment of herd immunity resulted in a reduction of influenza transmission efficacy, precipitating a fundamental turnaround of the epidemic. Both indices had a prolonged decrease, aligning with the natural epidemiological pattern of decline , .
Research indicates that the influenza A virus often co-infects with the SARS-CoV-2 virus, exhibiting an overall frequency of 2.45%, with a greater co-infection incidence seen in the elderly compared to adults . Research has shown competitive transmission between the SARS-CoV-2 virus and the influenza virus . Viral interference is the occurrence wherein one virus competitively obstructs the reproduction or growth of another virus inside a shared host cell , . Still, current research exhibits significant shortcomings in analyzing the competitive action mechanisms between the two viruses, particularly in critical areas such as receptor binding site competition (ACE2 versus sialic acid), humoral immune cross-reactivity, host cell resource competition, and regulation of the interferon signaling pathway , . Studie suggests that SARS-CoV-2 viral infection may disrupt the development and replication of live attenuated influenza vaccines (LAIV) in the host respiratory mucosa. Despite the very low influenza vaccine coverage in Sichuan Province, this interference may be accomplished by eliciting non-specific immune responses. These interaction pathways may affect the overall evolution of influenza pandemics, ultimately compromising the vaccine’s ability to generate local immunity and leading to decreased vaccination efficiency . Nevertheless, our investigation necessitates additional molecular data to confirm the ecological niche competition among viruses.
NPIs serve as an effective mitigation method for emerging infectious illnesses, safeguarding those without immunity to novel pathogens during the first phases prior to vaccine availability , . However, in the long term, this may not alter the overall trajectory of the pandemic . This study examines the influence of non-infectious respiratory diseases on influenza transmission to provide intervention strategies in future public health crises. The major objective of Non-Pharmaceutical Interventions (NPI) was originally to mitigate the development of COVID-19; however, the transmission dynamics of influenza, a prevalent respiratory infectious disease, can also be substantially affected by NPI. Through the examination of both the immediate and enduring impacts of non-pharmaceutical intervention (NPI) on influenza, we aim to furnish policymakers with empirical evidence concerning the timing, duration, potential outcomes, and enhancement of NPI strategies in response to analogous public health emergencies or other infectious diseases in the future, thereby facilitating the formulation of more scientifically robust and rational intervention policies.

## Limitation
Although Interrupted Time Series Analysis (ITSA) is extensively utilized to evaluate the efficacy of public health initiatives, this study presents certain drawbacks. The model fit for the two outcome measures differed; the ILI case count model outperformed the influenza positive case count model. We must evaluate parameters including testing capacity, sampling tactics, population immunity levels, and test reagent sensitivity, which substantially affect the incidence of influenza positive cases. These covariates were excluded from the model; hence, the model accounts for a relatively minor percentage of the data variation. The unequal spatial distribution of the population in Sichuan Province may potentially affect the results due to inter-regional population migration. Third, our data source exclusively comprises patients who underwent influenza virus nucleic acid testing at monitoring hospitals, hence introducing selection bias. Ultimately, during the execution of non-pharmaceutical intervention plans, certain patients exhibiting moderate symptoms refrained from seeking medical care; hence, we may have neglected some influenza-positive individuals who conducted self-antigen tests. Consequently, forthcoming research ought to amalgamate multi-center ITS data and take into account variables such as population mobility and vaccination to enhance tactics for the prevention and control of respiratory infectious diseases.

## Conclusion
Our data indicate that human interventions can temporarily modify the course of respiratory infectious disease epidemics; nonetheless, the long-term trend of viral transmission remains unchanged. Non-pharmaceutical interventions during the COVID-19 pandemic served as a natural experiment for influenza control, demonstrating that the transmission of this respiratory infectious disease can be concurrently influenced by various factors, including policy measures (short-term), immune defenses (long-term), and temperature. Our findings elucidate the efficacy of NPIs in mitigating influenza transmission, offering empirical evidence and strategic guidance for future coordinated prevention and control efforts regarding influenza and other respiratory infectious illnesses.
