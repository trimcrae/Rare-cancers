## Abstract
The spread of COVID-19 hit both the Japanese travel market and the foreign travel market to Japan, forcing travel-related businesses to shrink, temporarily suspend operations, or close down their businesses. However, Japanese people's desire to travel after the slowdown of COVID-19 remains and the desire of people from Asia, Europe, the United States, and Australia to travel to Japan is also high. The Japanese Government provides loans and other support measures to enable travel-related business operators to survive through the COVID-19 crisis. They advance measures to increase the benefit of accommodation facilities and tourism areas in preparation for the recovery period of travel demand. Although the situation is unpredictable as the mutation of the virus continues, under the assumption that the vaccination will progress worldwide and travel restrictions will be relaxed in the near future, this study examines the path and issues for the recovery of Japanese travel demand and foreign travel demand for overseas visitors to Japan.
Keywords: Travel and tourism, Travel market, Desire to travel, Japanese government initiatives, Travel demand recovery

The global travel market has been severely hit by the spread of COVID-19. This paper analyzes the impact of COVID-19 on the Japanese travel market and the travel market of overseas visitors to Japan, as well as the Japanese government's support for the travel industry, and discusses the direction of recovery in travel demand.
1.

## Impact of COVID-19 on the travel market
1.1.

## Number of foreign tourists worldwide 
Although the number of foreign tourists to Japan declined in 2009 due to the global financial crisis, it has increased every year since and was estimated to reach 1.47 billion in 2019 [ ]. However, in 2020, when travel restrictions were implemented globally due to COVID-19, the number dropped sharply to 390 million (down 73% from the previous year) ( 
). The global tourism market shrank significantly in 2020, and The World Tourism Organization of the United Nations (UNWTO) is estimated to lose US$1.3 trillion, more than 11 times the decline caused by the global financial crisis in 2009, thereby jeopardizing 120 million jobs in the tourism sector [ ]. In addition, the number of foreign tourists in January 2021 fell by 87% from the same month of the previous year, and a continuation of this trend could see the number of foreign tourists in the first quarter of 2021 decline by 85% (260 million people) compared to the first quarter of 2019 before the COVID-19 crisis [ ].
 
1.2.

## Japanese travel market
As of 2019, the Japanese travel market had been on an increasing trend, with Japanese domestic travel (overnight and day trips) having a stable market of ¥20–22 trillion along with an increasing number of foreign visitors to Japan. However, in 2020, the total was ¥11 trillion, down 61% from ¥27.9 trillion in the previous year ( 
).
 1.2.1.

## Foreign visitors to Japan
The number of foreign tourists visiting Japan was growing until 2019, but it decreased to 4,116,000 in 2020 ( 
). Although a decrease was seen due to SARS (severe acute respiratory syndrome) in 2003, the swine flu pandemic in 2009, and the Great East Japan Earthquake in 2011, the impact of COVID-19 is unique, and it has been a major blow to tourism-related businesses that depend on foreign visitors to Japan. Travel demand literally disappeared because travel goods could not be inventoried.
 Consumption by foreign visitors to Japan in 2020 is estimated at ¥0.7 trillion down 85% from the previous year ( ). Comparing the consumption of ¥4.5 trillion for foreign tourists visiting Japan in 2018 with the export value by product in the same year, following the ¥12.3 trillion for automobiles (finished products), consumption by foreign travelers visiting Japan was ranked second with ¥4.5 trillion, ¥4.2 trillion for electronic components such as semiconductors, and ¥4.0 trillion for automobile parts, which was the second largest group [ , ]. Most of the consumption, driven by foreign visitors to Japan referred to as foreign demand, was lost.

1.2.2.

## Travel by Japanese nationals
The total number of Japanese domestic tourists in 2020 was 293.41 million (down 50.0% from the previous year), of which 160.7 million were overnight trips (down 48.4% from the previous year) and 132.71 million were day trips (down 51.8% from the previous year) [ ]. In addition, in 2020, overnight travel consumption stood at ¥7772.3 billion (down 54.7% from the previous year), day trip consumption was ¥2201.5 billion (down 53.9% from the previous year), and Japanese domestic travel consumption was ¥9973.8 billion (down 54.5% from the previous year) ( ).
The number of Japanese departing from Japan reached a record high of 20,080,000 in 2019, but in 2020 it stood at 3,174,000 (down 84.2% from the previous year) [ ].

1.3.

## Impact on accommodations and travel
1.3.1.

## Impact on the accommodations industry
The room occupancy rate at accommodation facilities in 2020 was 34.6%, down significantly from 62.7% in 2019 [ ]. Comparing 2019 and 2020, the total number of overnight guests at accommodation facilities decreased by 40%–60% in all regional blocks. In particular, there was a significant decrease of 57.0% in Okinawa, which was a hub for long-distance travelers from Tokyo and other areas in Japan, and a decrease of 61% in Kinki, which was heavily dependent on foreign tourists [ ]. According to Tokyo Shoko Research, Ltd., the number of bankruptcies in the accommodation industry in 2020 stood at 118 (up 57.3% from the previous year), and 55 such cases represented bankruptcies due to the spread of COVID-19 [ ]. The accommodation industry is the gateway to a region's acquisition of travel consumption. Due to its strong ability to spread travel consumption within the region [ ], the regional economy suffers direct damages such as the decrease in travel spending and the increase in the number of bankruptcies in the accommodation industry. Further, it also suffers indirect damage such as the decrease in the possibility that travel spending will widely enrich the regional economy through the accommodation industry.2

1.3.2.

## Impact on travel agencies
The number of bankruptcies in the travel industry in 2020 was 26 (up 4.0% from the previous year), of which seven were due to the spread of COVID-19 [ ]. Although the number of bankruptcies is less affected by the COVID-19 pandemic than by the accommodation industry, travel agencies have embarked on structural reforms that aim to make significant additions. JTB, the largest company, expects a record deficit of ¥100 billion in the fiscal year ending March 2021, its largest ever. Hence, JTB will reduce its capital from ¥2.3 billion to ¥100 million to reclassify itself as a small- and medium-sized enterprise, reduce tax burden, and secure funds to compensate for the expected huge losses [ ]. KNT-CT Holdings, which owns Kinki Nippon Tourist and Club Tourism, expects insolvency of over ¥9.6 billion in the fiscal year ending March 2021 [ ]. In addition to strengthening club tourism, the company has begun fundamental reforms by consolidating its Kinki Nippon Tourist business, reducing personnel, and closing a large number of stores. Overseas travel of the Japanese people has been restricted, and the domestic travel market is also restrained from traveling across prefectures. The market environment continues to be harsh for travel companies, which have a structure in which profits increase as customers are sent over long distances. However, the repayment of loans to support the financing of COVID-19 is approaching, and there is concern that travel agencies whose financing exceeds the limits of their organizational strength will go on a spree of “giving up bankruptcy.”

2.

## Trend in travel demand
2.1.

## Intention to travel after COVID-19 has converged
2.1.1.

## Intention to travel overseas in Asia, Europe, the United States, and Australia
In December 2020, the Development Bank of Japan and the Japan Travel Bureau Foundation (JTBF) conducted the “Intention Survey of Foreign Travelers coming from Asia, Europe, the United States, and Australia to Japan” among residents in Asia, Europe, the United States, and Australia who have experienced overseas travel. In response to the question, “When the COVID-19 pandemic converges, and the situation returns to normal, do you wish to travel overseas again?” The proportion of people who answered “Yes” or “I would say yes” among Asian, European, American, and Australian residents is more than 80%, and their intention to travel overseas after the COVID-19 convergence is high ( 
). Japan has been chosen as the most desired travel destination by Asian, European, American, and Australian residents ( 
).
 
2.1.2.

## Japanese people's intention to travel domestically
According to a questionnaire survey conducted by JTBF from May 2020, the percentage of people who answered "I want to travel more than ever" or "I want to travel as much as I did before" after the convergence of COVID-19 was 60%–70%. In particular, according to the results of the October 2020 survey and the January 2021 survey, the percentage of people who answered "I want to travel more than ever" is increasing ( 
). 
By age group, younger generations in their teens to their 30s have the highest travel intentions [ ].
 

2.2.

## New trends in travel
According to the results of JTBF’s expanded analysis of two regular surveys ("Travel Awareness Survey" and "Travel Field Survey"), the following differences were found in the implementation of Japanese domestic travel in August 2019 before COVID-19 and August 2020 during the COVID-19 pandemic.
Travel within regional blocks 
has increased significantly. For Hokkaido, where the percentage of travel within the regional block was high at 53.8% as of the August 2019 survey, nearly 100% responded that travel within Hokkaido took place as of the August 2020 survey. Similarly, in Tohoku it changed from 53.0% to 75.0%; in Kyushu/Okinawa, it changed from 58.8% to 81.8%; and in Kanto, which was a large-scale customer market nationwide, it changed from 25.9% to 51.6%; in Chubu, it changed from 37.4% to 72.4%; in Kinki, it changed from 29.8% to 44.9%; and in Chugoku-Shikoku it changed from 32.2% to 57.5% [ ]. Micro tourism 
has become mainstream amid the self-restraint of traveling across prefectural borders.
Regarding activities at travel destinations, the number of visits to nature, scenic spots and hot springs, which ranked first and second in the August 2019 survey, increased, and the number of walks around the city and visits to historical and cultural attractions decreased. The number of two-three night stays has decreased, and the number of one-night stays has increased. Among the accommodation facilities used, resort hotels, business hotels, and city hotels usage declined, while the use of inns increased, with small-scale inns accounting for 10.2% to 18.1% of total micro-tourism travel. As for companions, the number of couples and families increased, while the number of solo travelers and trips with friends and acquaintances, which had been increasing before COVID-19, decreased [ ].
Private car demand has greatly increased as the main means of transportation to travel destinations, with the usage rate of 45.3% in the August 2019 survey growing to 69.7% in the August 2020 survey. Rental car usage also increased from 1.6% to 2.9%, indicating that using vehicles where you can travel with companions only is preferred. However, the usage of trains, airplanes, and buses decreased [ ].
As of August 2020, travel styles that were greatly affected by infection prevention measures, that is, family trips with a small number of people, avoiding contact with the general public, traveling to nature-rich areas and hot spring areas by private car, and enjoying private room stays at small accommodation facilities have become mainstream. It is said that eradication of a virus which repeatedly mutates is difficult, and it is thought that infection prevention measures will continue even after the spread of the infection has converged, so this travel tendency will continue and take root in the future. In particular, for trips aimed at enjoying the outdoors and nature, in addition to staying in private rooms with baths which are expensive, the use of cheap campsites is also increasing 
. Various forms are being developed, and the options for enjoyment are expected to increase and become firmly established.

3.

## Government efforts post COVID-19
 From the "Policy Plan for Preventing the Spread of the Infection and Recovering Tourism Demand" (December 3, 2020) of the Tourism Strategy Execution Committee, projects that will serve as material for examining the recovery of travel demand are considered
3.1.

## Go to travel project
The “Go To Travel Project,” which began on July 22, aims to restore travel demand, stimulate local travel spending, and popularize and establish a safe and secure travel style 
while complying with COVID-19 restrictions. 
The project has been suspended since the announcement of a uniform national suspension on December 14
The ratio of users of this project to the number of Japanese domestic overnight travelers in August, the month following its initiation, exceeded 50%. When it became available to the citizens of Tokyo from October 1, the same ratio reached more than 70% in November [ ]. The number of users checking out between July 22 and December 28 was at least 87.81 million overnight stays, 
the average discount support per person was ¥4,649, and the total amount of support was at least ¥539.9 billion (discount of accommodation and travel costs ¥408.2 billion and use of regional common coupons ¥131.7 billion), which was announced by the Japan Tourism Agency [ ]
The Kanko Keizai Shimbun, one of Japan's tourism industry newspapers, dated November 23, 2020 reported that "The number of users of the project infected with COVID-19 was 148 by November 15 (according to data from the Japan Tourism Agency). The total number of users of the project from July 22 to October 31, when the project began, was at least 39.76 million, and the percentage of infected persons was about 1 in 270,000." On December 5, Kyodo News reported that "246 of Go To Travel users have been confirmed to be infected." In addition, both the analysis results, that this project caused the spread of infectious diseases and that no causal relationship was found, have been presented by the medical field, and the authenticity is yet to be confirmed
Although there are discussions on the significance and outcomes of the project, the number of users increased immediately after its launch, boosting the number of Japanese domestic overnight travelers. The number of Japanese domestic overnight travelers in November 2020 reached 34,067,000, recovering to 83.9% of the same period the previous year, and the occupancy rate of all accommodation facilities reached the highest level of 46.2% since the Emergency Declaration was issued to seven prefectures on April 7 [ ]. Considering that travel intentions are not declining, people will react sensitively when it becomes possible to travel, and travel demand will return quickly

3.2.

## Promotion of workcation
With telecommuting being promoted, some people are beginning to practice so-called “workcation.” Companies that have embraced workcation include Japan Airlines Co., Ltd., Unilever Japan Corporation, Nomura Research Institute, Microsoft Japan Co., Ltd., Salesforce.Com, Inc., Lancers, Inc., Cybozu, Inc., and LIFULL Co., Ltd. The government is promoting it as a contributor to work style reform and travel demand equalization, but it has not spread widely because of labor management issues and the need to ensure educational opportunities for children. However, it is likely to spread to a certain extent if companies become more aware of the fact that workcation is effective as a work style from the perspective of BCP (Business Continuity Plan), and that it contributes to improving employee productivity and extending life expectancy and quality of life

3.3.

## Revitalization of tourist regions
Amid sluggish travel demand, accommodation facilities, tourism facilities, and travel operators are forced to reduce their business scale, take a leave from work, and close down. This contributes to the structural reform of the tourism industry in response to changes in the market. However, since it is a labor-intensive service industry, if the service provision system is sufficient to meet recovery needs of travel demand, that is, if employees who maintain skills are not secured, demand will stay concentrated on tourism areas and resources that have supported a certain level of the system, and the risk of the spread of infectious diseases will increase. There is a concern that when people become more dissatisfied with the congestion and actual services in response to high expectations for travel, it will hinder the subsequent expansion of travel demand
To support the revitalization of hotels, inns, and tourist districts, the government secured ¥55 billion in the third supplementary budget for fiscal 2020. This subsidy program can be used to improve local tourist facilities (accommodation, food and drink, souvenirs, etc.) so that a small number of individual visitors can stay at travel destinations without making contact with an unspecified number of people. They can remove abandoned houses, which are increasing due to COVID-19 catastrophes, and improve the street landscape if they impair the attractiveness of tourist areas. The attractiveness of public facilities (facilities in national parks, cultural facilities, etc.) can be improved by introducing private-sector vitality to public facilities (facilities in national parks, cultural facilities, etc.). They can also promote demonstration projects in collaboration with transportation and tourism companies. The application is based on the premise that a tourist center revitalization plan, agreed upon by the region, has been formulated, and a part of this plan will be promoted with the assistance of the government during the fiscal year
Among Japan's tourist resources, support is also being provided for Japan's internationally competitive snow resorts, adventure tours, 
castles, shrines and temples, and ancient rural houses as accommodation facilities

3.4.

## Initiatives for MICE 
MICE, where large numbers of people gather, has also been hit hard. According to the Japan National Tourism Organization, 2589 international conferences 
were held in Japan in 2019 and 28 in 2020 [ ]. International conferences were held online in 2020, but the 14th UN Congress on Crime Prevention and Criminal Justice 
(Kyoto Congress) was held at the National Kyoto International Conference Center on March 7–March 12, 2021. This was Japan's first large-scale, hybrid-type international conference, with approximately 5600 registered participants from 152 countries worldwide. According to the UN announcement, approximately 4200 of the participants were online and approximately 160 representatives from 13 countries and UN staff members visited Japan. All participants in Kyoto were obliged to take PCR tests, and foreign participants stayed at dedicated hotels and venues and were forbidden to go out. At the venue, contact such as handshakes was prohibited, and a doctor was stationed. Because of these measures, as of March 12 this year, participants are reported to have had no infectious diseases [ ]
In light of these experiences and trends in other countries, the Japan Tourism Agency has begun to support Japan's international conferences and other organizations to acquire accreditation with “GBAC STAR,” 
an international accreditation system, so that the visualization of measures against infectious diseases and the content of measures can be compared and discussed by conference organizers
Regarding the way international conferences are held, both the merits of online meetings, which allows people to participate easily without incurring travel costs, and the merits of real meetings, such as building relationships of trust and unexpected discoveries and stimuli, are recognized. For the time being, it is thought that the hybrid-type hosting will be the mainstream, and it will be difficult for foreign tourists visiting MICE in Japan to return to the pre COVID-19 state in the near future. However, because important business negotiations will certainly be made face-to-face with a small number of people, movement will probably be initiated with business jet users

4.

## Trend of travel demand recovery
4.1.

## Difficulty in prediction
The ferocity of COVID-19 differs from the event risk experienced by Japan's travel and tourism fields. In recent years, there have been many natural disasters, such as the Great East Japan Earthquake, the Kumamoto Earthquake, and torrential rains. Although the damage to the affected areas was large, the remitting customer market, which is the source of travel demand in metropolitan areas, was good. Regarding infectious diseases, the 2009 influenza pandemic 
was the most recent, and although many school trips aimed at Kansai were canceled or postponed, 
the decrease in the total number of overnight guests in each month of 2009 was within a few percent of the same month of the previous year, and the year-on-year decrease in 2010 turned to an increase of about 20% [ ]
Suppose the remitting customer market exists on a fixed basis. In that case, the relationship between the tourist area and remitting customer market, where travel demand has declined, will be arranged as close-distance, medium-distance, and long-distance. Further, measures will be taken to promote tourist traffic in this order, considering the convenience of traffic access and travel experience, such as travel intentions and repeat travelers. This approach is based on the empirical rule that it is necessary to work and improve the environment to encourage people to come from afar, based on the characteristic of travel demand in which people can easily come if it is nearby
Tourism recovery in Tohoku after the Great East Japan Earthquake first occurred in the area where there were no problems with transportation access, in the order of Tohoku residents who participated in disaster area support tours and volunteer tours, and travelers from Kanto onward. The disaster area support tours and volunteer tours were “triggers” to encourage people by providing a legitimate reason when they think, “I want to go to Tohoku, but could I?” The first trips were to tsunami-affected areas in groups for specific purposes, such as sightseeing and educational trips. Subsequently, travelers from Taiwan, where most tourists in Tohoku came from even before the Great Earthquake, used charter flights and low-cost carriers linking Sendai and Hanamaki airports to Taipei and returned to Tohoku. Travelers from Taiwan returned early after the Kumamoto Earthquake, and in addition to being close in terms of distance, psychology, and traffic access, they were also accustomed to earthquakes. Though Fukushima Prefecture and the surrounding areas still face difficulties, a strategy focusing on specific targets was adopted because motorcycle riders came in after the earthquake [ ]
The basic policy, even during COVID-19, is to restore Japanese travel demand in the following order: (1) local customers who are close and can obtain information easily and regular customers who are mentally close and actively trying to obtain information, (2) medium distances, (3) long distances, (4) and inbound tourism from countries and regions to which both sides can travel. However, what is certain is that it is difficult to identify areas that correspond to medium or long distances by traveling only within the neighboring areas. Even if areas and regions that could be connected are identified, the spread of infectious diseases may recur due to mutation strains resulting in travel restrictions. Therefore, COVID-19 makes the future uncertain and unstable for the recovery of travel demand

4.2.

## Proposal toward a path to recovery
On the assumption that vaccination progresses in Japan and infectious disease countermeasures will be gradually strengthened, I describe my proposal for the recovery of travel demand. Though I should be presenting my argument with a persuasive basis, please allow me to include my own experiences in the content that I have presented so far
4.2.1.

## Proposal framework
To recover travel after an event risk becomes a crisis and actual damage occurs. It is necessary to have the following five factors in the order of short, medium, and long-distance: (1) desire to travel; (2) whether the business operators and regions can provide the elements that tourists hold important during the recovery period of travel demand; (3) possibility to move around; (4) sufficient supply of services by travel-related businesses and if local residents are willing to accept travelers; and (5) presence of a “trigger” that will jumpstart the recovery of travel demand
For (1), existing surveys have confirmed that the desire of people in Japan and overseas to travel has not declined. In particular, it is worth noting that Japan ranks first among Asia, Europe, the United States, and Australia in terms of desire to travel after the infectious disease has converged
In (2), post COVID-19 trends can be seen, such as avoiding congestion and contact with an unspecified number of people, enjoying the outdoors and nature, and staying in private rooms at accommodation facilities. Many tourist areas are developing travel content based on these trends and convey various attractions through videos, SNS, and other media. In addition, it is expected that government support will help accommodation facilities and tourist attractions strengthen their response to individual travel
(3) is the factor most strongly influenced by COVID-19. In Japan, transportation is maintained even though the number of railways, buses, airplanes, and vessels is reduced, and although transportation by private cars and rental cars is carried out without any trouble, some foreign countries have imposed movement restrictions. It is important to acquire herd immunity from vaccination to alleviate this, and to get there, a mechanism that allows movement without a quarantine period (by presenting evidence of vaccination and non-infection) is needed. Urgent efforts are being made toward the introduction of “vaccine passports,” but they are expected to be introduced in a digital format using mobile devices to shorten the confirmation time at airports and other locations
(4) There are two concerns about the acceptance system of travel-related businesses and regions. First is that downsizing, business holidays, and closures are increasing. The accommodation industry, where productivity is low and internal reserves are insufficient, and tourism facilities, which have high fixed costs, have reached the limit of their organizational strength. In addition, repayment of their support loans is also imminent. Even if the summer vacation season is difficult, recovery in travel demand is expected from the autumn travel season. Second is the awareness of local residents about the acceptance of tourists. There are regions where there is a strong awareness that travelers from outside the region are the source of infection, and it is necessary to consider local sentiment when accepting Japanese group guests and foreign tourists
(5) The Go To Travel project played a role as a “trigger” to start the recovery of travel demand, but the current system design seems insufficient, perhaps due to media reports on the same. However, there is a need for a campaign to stimulate demand. Given the growing motivation to travel, I think it is sufficient to let people know which areas they can travel to safely, rather than incentivizing discounts. With regard to foreign visitors to Japan, the Tokyo Olympic Games was expected to be the first step in attracting foreign visitors to Japan, but its effect is unclear at present. An alternative visit to the Japan campaign is needed

4.2.2.

## Scenario for recovery of travel demand
4.2.2.1.

## Travel of Japanese people
Micro tourism has already been put into practice, and domestic medium and long distance travel is the first step in recovering demand. I expect that many people choose tourist destinations that match the trends in (2) as their travel destinations; however, some opt for standard tourist destinations such as Kyoto. Visiting family members, relatives, and friends whom they could not meet in person due to COVID-19 will also be an important travel purpose
Regarding overseas travel by Japanese people, it is possible that they will start returning to areas familiar to Japanese nationals and those that employ sufficient countermeasures against infectious diseases, such as Hawaii

4.2.2.2.

## Foreign visitors to Japan
Since there is a high level of awareness of visiting Japan, it is necessary to clarify the loosening of travel restrictions, intention to accept foreign tourists in tourist areas, and the “triggers” that will replace the Tokyo Olympics. Travel restrictions will depend largely on the trend of vaccination. If inoculation in Japan progresses, it is thought that the awareness of the local residents toward travelers will change. Regarding routes for visiting Japan, in addition to utilizing international airports in the metropolitan area, it is necessary to consider linking areas that have succeeded in controlling infection, such as Taipei and Tohoku, by applying a “travel bubble” that allows them to come and go without quarantine. In 2017, 27 regional airports were certified as inbound tourist support airports, and it is possible to utilize these. For “triggers,” the Japan National Tourism Organization is required to conduct campaigns that are not inferior to those of the top countries; details are listed in . Since many countries and regions are making diligent preparations to disseminate information and improve the environment for accepting inbound tourists as soon as possible, international competition is expected to become more intense than pre COVID-19 in the early stages of the recovery of overseas travel demand
According to a survey of expert committee members 
conducted by UNWTO (data collected by January 2021), 50% of respondents expected international tourism to show signs of recovery in 2022, while 43% of respondents expected it to return to the 2019 pre-pandemic level in 2023, and 41% expected recovery to return to that level after 2024 [ ]. The International Air Transport Association (IATA) also expects international travel demand to exceed 2019 levels in 2023, taking into account global vaccination status [ ]. This is one year ahead of the forecast released by IATA in July 2020, and international travel demand seems to be moving faster when the environment is in place

4.3.

## Challenges
4.3.1.

## Control of the demand recovery process
If many people want to visit famous tourist destinations because it is their first trip in a long time, the concern is that a situation such as the pre COVID-19 over-tourism will occur. In Japan, the percentage of paid vacation used to fund weekday travel is stagnant, and the vacation and holiday period that people devote to travel tends to be concentrated. In areas where travelers are likely to be concentrated post COVID-19, it is necessary to devise ways to control the process of recovering travel demand to the extent possible, by disseminating information in advance to diversify the interests of travelers and providing congestion information during travel. This control makes it possible to prevent pre COVID-19 over-tourism from recurring

4.3.2.

## Evolution of group tours
While the emphasis is on avoiding the 3Cs, there are opinions that group tours handled by travel operators and bus operators will end. However, it is precisely during this market change that it is necessary to return to that essence of group tours and consider the possibility of their survival. Group tours, or package tours as they are called, can be booked simultaneously as “Ago Ashi Makura,” 
with chosen service providers whose quality is guaranteed by the travel agency. Measures against infectious diseases are also important in the post COVID-19 era, and it is thought that there is a need for high-value package tours in which travel agencies thoroughly manage safety and security, despite their high fees. By being able to choose a variety of travel styles, the possibility of enjoying travel, even as you become older, increases

4.3.3.

## Inheritance of experience
At the beginning of COVID-19, few people in the travel and tourism industry could discuss their experiences with the swine flu pandemic and the Great East Japan Earthquake. In particular, many people, active in the inbound tourism business, entered the market after 2013, when the number of foreign visitors to Japan rose, and many business owners had no experience encountering event risks. In other words, the situation was that most people did not know about the past, as experiences were neither recorded nor inherited. In the initial stage of risk response, it was important for managers to have a firm mindset to “somehow overcome” and pave the way gradually while flexibly taking various measures. However, some managers were stunned, and some administrative staff repeatedly did the unexpected. Many natural disasters occurred in Japan, and infectious diseases are expected to recur regularly. I think that the knowledge, experience, and lessons learned from COVID-19 should be analyzed, shared, and inherited for the sake of the future

5.

## Conclusion
Although COVID-19 is a risk that should not be manifested, many people recognize travel as a valuable and enriching part of their life despite falling under the category of nonessential and nonurgent. During the recovery of travel demand, I hope that various travel experiences supporting this perception will be realized, and that more people will incorporate travel into their lifestyles
COVID-19 also provided the travel and tourism sector an opportunity to become aware of its various vulnerabilities. To overcome this, some local governments are working to create sustainable tourism areas with the aim of revitalization from a bigger perspective, rather than simply returning to its pre COVID-19 status. I hope that travel demand will recover to allow residents and travelers to build good relationships without imposing a burden on nature and cultural resources
