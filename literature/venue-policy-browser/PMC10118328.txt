## Background:
Flood cases in so many parts of Nigeria, especially in Anambra state and the accruing psychological and health related problems, affecting members of families and society necessitated this study. In view to assist and help affected individuals who specialized in farming therapeutically, this study investigated the effect of rational emotive family health therapy (REFHT) on flood-induced anxiety disorders among farmers in Anambra state Nigeria. This study was guided with 3 null hypotheses.

## Methods:
This is a group randomized control study, conducted in Anambra state Nigeria. The sample size was 120 registered cassava farmers in Anambra State Nigeria. A measure of anxiety disorders was used to assess the baseline data of the condition before the treatment, immediately after treatment, and after 3 weeks. The treatment was a self-developed REFHT manual that lasted for 12 sessions, 60 minutes per session with aim of reducing anxiety disorders among farmers who are victims of flood. The manual was delivered by counseling psychologists with orientation in rational emotive behavior and family therapies. Data collected were analyzed using descriptive (mean, standard deviation, chi-square, and percentage) and inferential (Partial Eta Squared, and Analysis of Covariance) statistical tools.

## Results:
The result revealed no significant gender difference was observed among the study participants’ socioeconomic, working status, and loan status. REFHT was significantly effective in reducing flood-induced anxiety disorders among farmers. There was no significant moderating effect of gender on flood-induced anxiety disorders among farmers. There was no significant interaction effect of gender and REFHT treatment on anxiety disorders among farmers.

## Conclusion:
REFHT was significantly effective in reducing flood-induced anxiety disorders among farmers. Based on the findings, this study recommended among others that rational-emotive therapists and family health professionals should liaise with community leaders to provide career counseling services to farmers and establishment of community-based rational emotive institute for each state in Nigeria.

Keywords: farmers, flood-induced anxiety disorders, Nigeria, rational emotive family health therapy, REBT

## 1. Introduction
Agriculture is an important source of income that every country needs across the globe. This is because constant supply of agricultural products such as staple food (maize, yam, cassava, coconut, and rice), vegetable, and meat is needed to feed 7 billion people in the world. Failure to do so would result in starvation and may lead to death. Drastic food insecurity leads to nutritional disasters which would cause variability to the human population and social economy of a nation. Food security is threatened by climate change which affects agricultural production.
One of the major consequences of climate change is the fluctuation in the rainfall regime which increases the probability of occurrence of extreme events in the form of excessive rainfalls in certain areas and drought in others. The occurrence of excessive rainfall in a particular geographical location could lead to flooding that may not only affect human activities like education, business, and banking, among others but also affect agricultural activities.[ ]
Flooding as an environmental factor affecting food security has not been extensively studied in South-eastern Nigeria. As a natural disaster, people including farmers have recounted horrible life experiences. Nigerian farmers have reported negative experiences of flood disaster as it affects their lives.[ ] Given the painful disruption of life routine, the farmers are vulnerable to several psychological disturbances. Each time there is flood; farmers become emotionally unstable.[ ] These experiences continuously reoccur. Once there is a heavy rain, crops and livestock are likely to be damaged by the flood following after. As a result, the farmers become highly anxious and tensed about destruction of property, destruction of farmland, loss of high cost of farm products.[ ]
The occurrence of flood in Anambra State Nigeria is generally recognized as it has damaged high value crops such as yams, maize, cassava, lives, and properties like houses and among others.[ ] Anambra state is one of the states in South Eastern Nigeria that is well known to be agrarian and susceptible to flood. Because of that, most farmers have been reported to be flood victim in one occasion or the other.[ ] Empirical study conducted in Anambra state showed that about 48% of household were victims of flood. Out of the 48% in 8 communities, Igbariam and Ossomala communities were more vulnerable to flood-related cases compared to other places in Anambra state.[ ] Increasingly, the percentage of affected family continued to rise in the year 2020 as nearly 73% of household in Igbariam community were kept hungry.[ ] Another community that has experienced chronic effect of flood is Oguta community with 22% of its households having poor quality of family health as a result of food insecurity.[ ] Farmers in Anambra state have witnessed several heavy rainfalls which have caused chronic pains to the affected farmers. In 2022, flood started about 2 months ago (August–September) in Anambra, with more homes being inundated every day and more victims moved into internally displaced persons camps.[ , ] Anambra East Local Government Area’s 2 internally displaced persons camps were recently visited, and the level of suffering the majority of the experiences of victims was evident.[ ] Numerous people were seen residing in a school-owned hall in Umuoba Anambra state.[ ]
Anxiety disorder has been conceptualized by scholars from different orientations or field of study. Anxiety disorder is a public mental disorder that produces frequent fear of someone or social situations.[ ] Operationally, anxiety disorder is developmental mental health and emotional problems, characterized by fear, worry, and intense panic that could be triggered by environmental and natural disasters that threatens farmers. Possibly, individuals with experiences of flood could be prone to aggressive situation triggered by natural disaster-related situations. People with experiences of flood disaster, perhaps, farmers are irritated, infuriated, and anxious of how to survive, where to go, what to do among others.[ ] Most flood victims are susceptible to psychological disturbances which anxiety is one of them.[ ] It is worrisome that anxiety associated with people appears to be farmer-domain-specific compared to non-farmers. This means that worry, fear, distorted feeling, and grief are more prevalent in farmers with experiences of flood. Symptoms of anxiety disorders and depression are always higher in farmers compared to non-farmers.[ ] In line with the above empirical reports, farmers with experience of flood are usually more vulnerable to other severe psychological symptoms and their emotional conditions compared to other populations.
Relating anxiety and belief system has been established by past studies. For instance, Scores on the irrational belief measure were found to be associated with state anxiety scores and were found to predict the amount of increase in state anxiety experienced in the more stressful situation.[ ] Irrational beliefs among adult individuals, who appear to be in good health digger anxiety symptoms, and through the elevated stress profile, were linked to an increased risk of developing health problems.[ ] This finding advances psychological research in supporting and canvasing for primary prevention of mental health disorders.[ ] Also, evidence showed that negative feelings and thoughts are associated with anxiety symptoms.[ ] Irrational views play a disruptive role in the formation of psychological symptom, and increase the impact of catastrophizing beliefs.[ ]
A recent call for immediate psychological therapies for Nigerian farmers experiencing anxiety was made.[ ] This is because farmers endure painful events at a high rate and because these painful experiences are often accompanied by psychological issues like worry. The rational emotive family health therapy (REFHT) is one such intervention. This family-based rational emotive behavior therapy (REBT) approach was created by Ede et al[ ] to challenge the cognitive bias, behavioral misreading of potential scenarios, and negative emotional response that are thought to be the root of family welfare issues. Later, in 2021, the strategy was modified to use the family health-model of REBT.[ ] The fundamental tenet of REFHT is that if parents have unreasonable expectations of their family’s circumstances, they are likely to be ineffective in solving the issue. The proponent made the argument that the family members’ worldviews govern how well families function. According to Ede et al,[ ] the cognitive, behavioral, and emotional interpretations of the event are what are to blame for the psychological problems that the household is going through rather than the existence of a problem. In this study, the farmers’ interpretation of the occurrence is a flood and the harm it did. The belief system can be “I am dead and finished,” “I must commit suicide,” or “I feel like dying and I wonder if my neighbors aren’t to blame for this.” According to the creators of the family health-model of REBT, parents who have such a negative perception may be more prone to psychological symptoms like stress and a poor quality of family life. As a result, the farmers may develop clinical and non-clinical issues that call for therapy. Family health, well-being, and quality of life may be badly impacted if farmers and family members are perceived negatively.[ ] They contend that incorrect interpretations take place when there is a discrepancy between the cognitive representation of the event and its actual occurrence, which is consistent with the REBT philosophy. People in such a mindset will interpret reality incorrectly, which could result in unfavorable beliefs. When farmers reject circumstances they cannot handle, occurrences are interpreted incorrectly. Due to the psychological effects of the flood, some of the farmers may be agitated, hostile, and gloomy. As a result, the family tends to withdraw from society, which can lead to feelings of sadness, anxiety, irritability, and self-pity, among other things.[ , ]
Given the presence of anxiety disorders in farmers in Anambra state, which has been attributed to flooding events as seen in past cross-sectional and other quantitative studies.[ , , ] and the need for psychological intervention,[ ] it has become pertinent to investigate the effect of REFHT on flood-induced anxiety disorder among farmers in Anambra state Nigeria.

## 1.1. Research hypotheses
The following null research hypotheses are formulated and were tested at 0.05 level significance:
Ho1: There is no significant difference between anxiety disorder scores of participants intreatment group and control group on the effect of REFHT on anxiety disorder
Ho2: There will be no significant difference between anxiety disorder scores of participants intreatment group and control group on the effect of REFHT on anxiety disorder at follow-up stage
Ho3 there is no significant moderating effect of gender on anxiety disorder among flood victims
Ho4 There is no significant interaction effect of the groups and gender on anxiety disorder in flood victims (farmers)

## 2. Methodology

## 2.1. Design of the study
This study adopted a group-randomized control design. A randomized control design is method that permit randomization of subjects into experimental and comparison controls.[ ] A randomized control study increases the likelihood of equivalence within the intervention and comparison groups, noting potential participants’ characteristics that could be threats to the experimental variables in which the researchers is interested.[ ] Given the definition, the present study adopted a group randomized controlled trail since it allowed the researchers to systematically assigned or allocated participants into their respective groups at different level. Hence, the design become good enough for this present study.

## 2.2. Area of the study
The location of this study was Anambra State Nigeria. Anambra state is one of the states in South-East Nigeria. It is bounded with Enugu state at the east axis, Imo and River states to the south, Delta State at western axis, and northern part is bounded with Kogi State. Anambra State is one of the Igbo speaking area in Nigeria, dense with traders, farmers, craftsmen, and academics. Evidential literature demonstrated that a good number of Anambrans are farmers.[ ]

## 2.3. Sample and sampling techniques
The sample size in this study was 120 registered cassava farmers (parents) in Anambra State Nigeria. The sample size was chosen using multi-stage sampling techniques. Firstly, a purposive sampling technique was used to 2 riverine local government areas in Anambra state. Reason for choosing these categories of farmers is anchored on the fact that cassava is tuber crop that do not need much water. It is not water friendly and affected when there is excess water compared to other crops.
Secondly, a simple random sampling technique was used to select 4 farmers’ cooperatives in those two (2) local government areas from the Agriculture zones. Using this technique, the researchers crafted 2 cutout cards that was tagged with YES and NO. The researchers put the cutout cards in bowl where each cassava farmer from all the riverine local governments was asked to pick one card. Indicating that once a person picks a card with YES, the local government area where the person came from was automatically selected for the study. This technique was used to select 2 local government areas. The 2 local government areas were selected because an equal opportunity was given to all the locations that have similar characteristics.
Thirdly, a systematic sampling technique was used to select 7 registered farmers from the 6 farmers’ cooperative societies in first local government area while 4 registered farmers would be selected from the 20 farmers’ cooperative societies in the second local government area. The estimated value (122) was statistically subjected to Gpower sample estimation method. This statistical method demonstrated if the sample size is adequate or not. For more information or details concerning the procedure for randomization of the participants into treatment group and control condition (comparison group), kindly see procedure section.
In addition the 122 potential participants were screened using the anxiety disorders scale indicating if the flood victims have high level of anxiety disorders. The essence is to ascertain the baseline data/information. They were also checked using the following inclusion and exclusion criteria;
Must be screened with the outcome/dependent measure (Screen for Adult Anxiety Related Disorders)
Must be a registered farmer
Must be a resident in sampled areas in Anambra State Nigeria
Must be above 18 years
Must give oral consent and willing to participate
Must have recorded experience of flood as a victim
Must be a parent
Exclusion criteria include
Presence of psychosis
Receiving treatment from psychotherapists
Severe illness due to flood experience
Less than 18 years
Farmers without the above inclusion criteria were excluded and called not eligible. Those that were eligible were assigned to groups, that is, REFHT group and waitlist group by the researchers and research assistant team. This process was facilitated by principle of sequence allocation method.[ ]

## 2.4. Instrument for data collection
Screen for Adult Anxiety Related Disorders was developed by Angulo et al[ ] and was adapted for the present study. The aim of the instrument is to measure adult anxiety related disorders. The instrument has total of 43 items and it has 4 dimensions such as panic disorder or significant somatic symptoms which contains 17 items, second generalized anxiety disorder which contains 13 items, separation anxiety disorder contains 7 items and Social Phobia disorder which contains 7 items. The scales have 3 response options which include: Not True, sometimes true, and very true. The response options were weighed at 1 to 3, indicating the higher score shows higher experience of adult anxiety related disorder. The reliability of the instrument was determined by using Cronbach’s Alpha method for measuring its internal consistency. The value of internal consistency was 0.619.

## 2.5. Treatment manual

## 2.5.1. Rational emotive family healthy treatment manual on anxiety disorders.
The treatment is based on REFHT manual for anxiety disorder.[ , ] The principles of rational emotive family healthy programme were adapted during the development of this manual. The REFHT manual is a psychoeducational therapy that aimed at exploring REBT approaches by using it to alter irrational beliefs associated with anxiety disorder which reduced level of stress among flood victims. The treatment guidelines were arranged based on time, content, weeks/session, objectives, activities, and techniques/strategies. Each of this classification has unit of activities under it. The manual was a 12-session intervention programme that lasted for 8 weeks. Each session begin with a specific objectives and content and lasted for 60 minutes. Sample of rational emotive family healthy therapy utilized was cognitive alliance, biofeedback technique relaxation technique Practice exercise, Problem-solving technique, Practice exercise, Cognitive disputation Mood monitoring technique Practice exercise, Mood monitoring and Relapse prevention Cognitive disputation and emotional disputation (see Table ).
 

## 2.5.2. Therapists and research assistants and treatment integrity.
The intervention was delivered by 2 therapists, all university employees who had been certified to practice as counselors after appropriate training, including counseling. The role of the therapists is to implement the REFHT manual as expected. They are female and male counselors who have practiced counseling for over 6 years.
The research assistants were 2 postgraduate students, one from Guidance and Counselling, Department of Educational Foundations, Faculty of Education and other from Department of Agricultural Science Education, Faculty of Vocational and Technical Education, both from University of Nigeria, Nsukka. The research assistants served as external assessors who monitored and observe the implementations of treatment sessions, making reference to the treatment manual to ensure that the therapists are following the steps. They assisted the researchers during the recruitment process and randomization of the participants to treatment group and waitlist control group. They also assisted in gathering information through the instrument during pretest, posttest, and during follow up test.

## 2.6. Treatment procedure

## 2.6.1. Ethical adherence and considerations.
As required by American Psychological Association and Helsinki Declaration, the researchers obtained a written permission from the Head, Department of Agricultural Science Education, Faculty of Vocational and Technical Education University of Nigeria Nsukka to conduct this research. The researchers sought oral approval/consent from the potential participants. The prospective participants were assured of respect for their privacy, observation of confidentiality and that there were no adverse risks that were accrued from the treatment.

## 2.6.2. Pretest.
The pretest helped the researchers to identify and screen the prospective participants for eligibility. This was done shortly after the sampling process as elaborated in sample and sampling techniques section. The pretreatment stage was facilitated with help of 2 research assistants. As the 122 potential participants were screened using the screening tools and criteria for inclusion and exclusion, 120 participants were qualified to be participate in this study. Other 2 people were not included because one was receiving medical treatment elsewhere and the second person willingly decline. This process was facilitated by principle of sequence allocation method.[ ] and this is illustrated in the Figure .
 

## 2.6.3. Treatment phase.
The REFHT is a 12-session treatment manual that aimed at reducing anxiety disorders among farmers who are victims of flood in last 2 months. The participants were scheduled for first session. The treatment process was based on the REFHT model package developed by the present researchers. The REFHT aimed at helping the participants reduce their anxiety disorders. The intervention lasted for 12 sessions, 60 minutes per session. Sessions were held face to face interactions twice a week for 6 consecutive weeks, giving 12 sessions.
The first session focused on introduction of the therapists (Counsellors), the participants, and external assessors. They introduced themselves as a way of knowing each other name, profession, state of origin, etc. The therapists highlighted rule and regulations for coordination of the activities. After the introduction, the research assistants exposed the participants to the purpose and relevance of the group interaction in resolving psychosocial problems. The participants were advised by the therapists to respect confidentiality and privacy of each participant.
Immediately after the first session, next was the second and third session (week two and three) which focused on the meaning of anxiety disorders, causes and consequences and how it can develop into something negative (irrational belief). At the end of the sessions, home exercise was given to the participants. Sessions 4 addressed the participants’ flood experiences, how they feel each time there is a heavy rain. Session five concentrated on assessment of the feelings, identification of irrational beliefs associated with flood-induced anxiety disorders. The therapists helped the participants to explore how the negative thoughts which they will develop as a result of anxiety disorders develop. There was use of example to demonstrate true situation to the participants.
Sixth session focused on acting event (A) and beliefs system (B) principles of REBT. The therapist explained to the participant the meaning of “A” and “B” according to Ellis. The “A” principle means “activating event” and “B” beliefs system; more discussions were done by the therapist. This stage helped the therapist to identify other irrational beliefs that lead to anxiety disorders. Seventh session continued to address philosophies of the farmers with anxiety disorders with flood. The therapists used that to relate to “C” principle of REBT. The “C” means the consequences of the beliefs or philosophies. The participants were exposed to how some illogical philosophies of life could generate to negative consequences. Eight sessions focused on teaching them how to accept situations they cannot change. Drawing reference to the flood experiences that the damages caused by the flood should be accepted with good faith. The participants were exposed to the “D” principle. The “D” means disputation and how to dispute irrational beliefs into rational beliefs. The process of disputation was practiced during the said session. For the ninth session, the therapist focused on evaluation of events and how to recognize accurate/rational beliefs. This helped the participants to engage in relapse prevention techniques. Tenth session focuses on real life practice. Thereafter, the final session commenced to finalize the sessions. This was on twelfth session and that was termination day/due date. At this point, the researchers appreciated and thanked the participants for their commitment and cooperation. Remind the group that even though the group experience has ended, confidentiality was maintained.

## 2.6.4. Post-test.
Immediately after the termination, there was a post-test. Prior to the completion and filling the instruments, researchers invited the participants in waitlist control group to the same treatment venue for the posttest. As all the participants come together, a posttest was administered to those in treatment and waitlist control groups using the dependent measure.

## 2.7. Method of data analysis
To test the hypotheses, the study used ANCOVA and Partial Eta Squared. Essentially, Analysis of Covariance (ANCOVA) used to evaluate whether the means of a dependent variable are equal across levels of a categorical independent variable often called treatment while statistically controlling for the effects of other continuous variables that are not of primary interest called covariate. The posttest and follow-up sum of mean scores were used as the dependent variables while the pretest sum of mean scores were used as covariates. The results of the hypotheses were based on the significant level of 0.05. All these were conducted using SPSS version 28 (IBM Corp, Armonk, NY).

The outputs of both studies are marvelous as rational-emotive techniques have proven to be advantageous to the different population sampled in the 2 studies. The present study is In line with Aneke et al[ ] who showed that REBT intervention was efficacious for changing teachers’ irrational beliefs to better ones. The outcome of both studies are excellent as rational-emotive techniques have proven to be profitable in reducing irrational belief to rational belief in different population sampled in the 2 studies. This finding is in line with Grove et al[ ] who showed statistically significant decreases in anxiety symptoms with medium, small-to-medium, and small effect sizes, respectively. The present study is in line with Obiweluozo et al[ ] who indicated that significant difference in levels of examination anxiety was also found among students in the experimental and control groups, in favors of students in the experimental group. The present study agrees with Ngwoke et al[ ] who showed that cognitive restructuring significantly reduced test anxiety of low-achieving students. The present study is in view with Grove et al[ ] who showed significant decreases were also found in irrational belief scores (small-to-medium effect size). Mondin et al[ ] showed that anxiety disorders were associated with psychiatric problems in parents. The present study is in agreement with Ezegbe et al[ ] cognitive-behavioral intervention was a successful intervention which decreased the symptoms of anxiety in social science education students who participated in the study. This is shocking as the reviewed study was a foreign study showing that REBT interventions are great treatment approaches for anxiety irrespective of location and population. Given that male and female farmers suffer flood-induced anxiety disorders, the implication is that farmers who are ensuring to provide food for the nation are at health risks. This suggests for intervention across path of country that are flood victim. It implies that if more intervention is not given to male and female famers who have anxiety disorder this may bring down family health, economy income, lack of education food insecurity and insecurity.
It has shown REFHT has no interaction effect of gender and treatment on flood-induced anxiety disorder of male farmers than their female counterparts. There was no significant interaction effect of gender and treatments on flood-induced anxiety disorder among farmers. Therefore, it implies that the effectiveness of REFHT intervention helps to reduce anxiety level male and female farmers. It is a clear indication that farmers irrespective of gender could be assisted with REFHT when experiencing psychological problem such as flood-induced anxiety disorders. The implication extends to reduction of possible socioeconomic and psychological moderators and mediators of flood-induced anxiety disorder in farmers. It also implies that if this intervention is not applied it may threaten the wellbeing of the farmers’ family health.

## 5. Conclusion
The rampaging flood incidences in Nigeria and how resident farmers were affected in recent flooding motivated this study to investigate the effect of REFHT on flood-induced anxiety disorder among farmers. Based on the results obtained, this study concluded REFHT was an effective intervention for reduction of the anxiety disorder among farmers Anambra State. There was a significant effect of rational emotive family healthy therapy on flood-induced anxiety disorder among farmers in Anambra state, Nigeria. Gender does not have moderating effect on flood-induced anxiety disorder among farmers. There was no significant moderating effect of gender on flood-induced anxiety disorder among farmers. REFHT proved to be more effective in reducing the flood-induced anxiety disorder of male farmers than their female counterparts. There was no significant interaction effect of gender and treatments on flood-induced anxiety disorder among farmers.

## 5.1. Recommendations
Based on the findings of this study, the following recommendations were suggested for policymakers, and practitioners:
It is recommended that rational-emotive therapists and family health professionals should liaise with community leaders to provide career counseling services to farmers. The service will expose them more on potential impacts of REFHT. The expectation is to give them a preventive measure that could ameliorate the high prevalence of flood-induced post-traumatic and anxiety disorders.
It is recommended that famers should increase their acceptability level of REFHT to sustain the psychological treatment outcomes. This is because if the farmers have adequate psychological dispositions for REFHT, they would build internal resilience and persistent ability to address flood-related health and psychological problems. Building the resilience may be beneficial in sustaining relapse prevention. Once the farmers could prevent relapse or avoid reversing back to their irrational beliefs, they would have sorted out their to 50%, especially when a professional is unavoidable.
There should policy initiative to present recommendations for establishment of community-based rational emotive institute for each state in Nigeria where flood has deteriorated the citizens’ livelihood. This is recommended with the intention to have therapeutic centers that will be able to give immediate attention to victims of flood in the future. The institutionalization of community-based rational emotive family therapy can only be possible if government is committed to it.
Therefore, it is recommended that state government should ensure that deploy qualified psychologists, counselors, and other related experts to schools and institutes where the farmers will be reached.
Government should have educational policy that will embrace the practice of rational emotive behavior therapy at every local government council.
The present researchers recommended inclusive interventions, aiming to address flood-induced psychological problems. By making inclusive, male and female farmers will be included.

## 5.2. Limitations of the findings
As in other quantitative studies, the present study has methodological flaws. One of the major limitations of this study is the inability of the researchers to measure irrational beliefs of the farmers. Using a measure of irrational beliefs would have assisted to researchers to the specific beliefs for example, awfulizing, demandingness, etc. identifying this, would have required a specific rational-emotive techniques to deal with. Also, the sample size may not be adequate to represent entire farmer populations either in Anambra and Nigeria at large. Besides these, this study fails to analysis data according to the dimensions of the measurement tools (instruments). If that was done, it would have given though breakdown of the problems and extent of improvements due to the REFHT intervention. Another major limitation of this study is that the instrument seemed not have mechanisms of change. Given these methodological weaknesses, the researchers, therefore, suggest that subsequent researchers should incorporate these things that are missing here to the future studies. Based on the limitations, further studies should focus on moderators and effect of REFHT on anxiety disorder in farmers with flood experiences. Also, it is important if subsequent studies address the mediators and impact of REFHT on anxiety disorder in farmers with flood experiences. Advancing the benefits of the same intervention, future researchers could investigate the effect of REFHT on anxiety disorder in other disasters victims.
