## Abstract
Globally, approximately 148.1 million children are affected by stunting, significantly threatening their health and cognitive development. This nationwide cross-sectional study uniquely focused on sports-related factors, including family sports-related spending, duration of physical activity, and consistent participation in sports. Using data involving 5,017 children under 5 years old from 471 counties across 31 provinces in China, significant socioeconomic, regional, and behavioral correlates of stunting were identified. Stunting prevalence differed notably by gender and geographical division, with boys having significantly higher odds of stunting compared to girls (OR = 1.539; 95% CI: 1.238–1.913; p < 0.001), and children in East China having significantly lower odds compared to those in Northwest China (OR = 0.566; 95% CI: 0.398–0.804; p = 0.002). Non-enrolled children exhibited significantly increased odds of stunting (OR = 2.266; 95% CI: 1.360–3.775; p = 0.002). Increased sports-related family spending was associated with lower odds of child stunting (OR = 0.730; 95% CI: 0.580–0.919; p = 0.007). Furthermore, children with insufficient daily physical activity (0–15 min/day) were more likely to experience stunting compared to those exercising over 60 min per day (OR = 1.413; 95% CI: 1.030–1.937; p = 0.032). Long-term consistent participation in sports (participation duration over 3 months) significantly reduced stunting risk (< 1 month vs. >3 months, OR = 1.432; 95%CI: 1.023–2.006, p= 0.036). Screen time and sleep duration showed no significant associations with childhood stunting in this study (p > 0.05). Thus, we recommend increasing family sports-related investments and encouraging regular and long-term participation in physical activities, especially targeting low-income families, to support child growth and reduce the risk of stunting.

## Introduction
Childhood stunting is not only a significant risk factor for childhood morbidity and mortality, but it is also closely associated with social, economic, and political conditions, particularly prevalent in developing countries [ ] According to data from the United Nations Children’s Fund (UNICEF) and the World Health Organization (WHO), approximately 148.1 million (22.3%) of children under five years of age were affected by stunting in 2022 [ ]. Despite some progress made over the past decade in improving maternal and infant nutrition, the global challenge of stunting persists. This issue not only poses long-term threats to individual health and cognitive development [ ], but also has profound implications for future educational and occupational outcomes [ ]. Therefore, addressing childhood stunting is crucial for improving the quality of life and developmental prospects of children.
The importance of physical activity for the health and development of young children has been widely recognized in recent years [ , ]. Moderate physical exercise not only enhances children’s muscle strength and promotes overall physical health, but also significantly improves children’s height growth and reduces stunting risk through specific physiological mechanisms—such as increasing bone density, stimulating growth hormone secretion, and enhancing the absorption of nutrients [ , ]. In addition, some studies have indicated that engaging in moderate physical activity during early childhood benefits not only physical growth, but also positively influences neurological development, cognitive abilities, and emotional stability, thereby indirectly reducing the risk of childhood stunting [ ].
Family factors play a decisive role in shaping children’s habits and competencies during early childhood [ ]. Economic conditions have been proven to significantly influence children’s risk of stunting [ , ]. Additionally, supportive and proactive parenting attitudes can provide children with robust psychological support, helping them to develop healthy behavioral patterns [ ]. Especially, parents’ positive attitudes and financial support regarding children’s sports participation reflect the family’s prioritization of fostering children’s physical fitness, which helps to enhance children’s level of sports participation and improve their physical condition [ , ].
Existing research has examined various influencing factors of childhood stunting, but most studies narrowly focus on single dimensions such as nutrition or healthcare [ ]. In particular, the association between sports-related factors—such as physical activity participation, parental attitudes toward children’s sports involvement, and parental economic investment in sports—and child stunting remains under-researched within the context of China [ , ]. Although several international studies have begun to explore the relationship between physical activity and child development [ ], systematic nationwide evidence from China—a country with rapid socioeconomic development and significant regional disparities—remains notably scarce regarding how sports-related factors specifically influence childhood stunting. Moreover, regional differences in lifestyles and sports habits, as well as substantial disparities in families’ attitudes and financial investments towards children’s sports activities, have made the precise mechanisms underlying these associations unclear.
Therefore, the present study specifically focuses on sports-related factors—including parental financial investment and attitudes toward children’s sports participation, as well as children’s own sports-related habits (exercise duration and regularity)—through an in-depth investigation of a large nationwide sample covering 31 provinces in China. Our research aims to fill the current knowledge gap in China and to provide targeted empirical evidence that can inform public health policy-making.

## Methods

## Survey methodology
The data for this study are derived from the 2020 China National Fitness Activity Status Survey, which is conducted every five years in mainland China to assess the level of physical exercise participation among residents. To ensure the representativeness of the sample, the survey employed a multi-stage stratified random sampling method, utilizing proportionate systematic probability sampling to select samples from all 31 provinces (autonomous regions and municipalities) across the nation.
The specific survey process consisted of three stages. The first stage involved county-level sampling, where 10 to 20 counties (county-level cities/districts/banner) were randomly selected from each province (region, municipality) using probability sampling proportional to the local population size, considering regional economic development and geographical differences. The second stage constructed a sampling frame for all village (resident) committees under the selected counties (county-level cities/districts/banner) based on data from the Sixth National Population Census. From this framework, 13 villages (resident committees) were randomly selected, proportionate to the urban-rural population ratio within the county. The third stage involved resident sampling. In the selected villages (committees), two eligible subjects aged 3 to 6 were randomly selected from the survey participants. In total, 5,760 village (resident) committees were sampled from 471 counties (county-level cities/districts/banner) across the nation. The final valid analytical sample for children under 5 years old was 5,017, representing a validity rate (valid analytical samples / returned questionnaires) of 99.5% and an overall effective response rate (valid analytical samples / distributed questionnaires) of 87.1%.

## Dependent variables
In this study, childhood stunting was treated as the dependent variable. To assess chronic malnutrition status, the Height-for-Age Index (H/A) was employed as an indicator. Based on the height and age of each child, we calculated the H/A index using the World Health Organization (WHO) growth standards from 2006, which serves as an important measure of stunting.
According to WHO standards [ ], the normal range for the H/A index is defined as ± 1 standard deviation (SD) from the mean. A child is deemed normal if the H/A index is greater than or equal to -2 SD, classified as “0”; if the H/A index is below − 2 SD, the child is considered to have moderate stunting, classified as “1”; and if it is below − 3 SD, the child is regarded as having severe stunting, classified as “2.”

## Independent variables
Multiple independent variables were collected and analyzed to evaluate their impact on children’s growth and development (Appendix Table ). The sociodemographic variables included Gender (Male, Female), Urban/Rural classification (Urban, Rural), Geographical Division (North China, Northeast China, East China, Central South China, Southwest China, Northwest China), and Ethnicity (Han Chinese, Minority Ethnicity).
Family and economic factors encompassed whether the parents live with the child (Yes, No), the Father’s Education and Mother’s Education (Primary School and Below, Junior High, High School or Vocational, University and Above), Annual Household Income, and whether there are any Sports-Related Spending (Yes, No).
Health and behavioral characteristics included whether the child has any congenital conditions or adolescent illnesses that affect/limit physical activity (No, Yes), the Childcare Situation (Not Enrolled, Kindergarten, Preschool Class), whether the child has any biological siblings (No, Has one biological sibling, Has two or more biological siblings), and the Child’s primary caregiver in the past year (Parents, Grandparents).
The analysis also considered the average daily duration of sports activities (0–15 min, 16–30 min, 31–60 min, Over 60 min), whether parents enroll their children in sports-focused training programs/classes (No, Considering Inaction, Occasionally, Ongoing), and the weekly duration of the child’s sports training programs/classes (0–60 min, 61–90 min, Over 90 min). Other variables included the number of sports the child has participated in (None, 1, 2, 3, 4 or More), the duration of the child’s consistent participation in a single sport (Less than 1 month, 1 ~ 2 month, 2 ~ 3 month, More than 3 month), screen time on weekends (None, 0–30 min, 31–60 min, 61–90 min, 91–120 min, 121–180 min, 181 min or More), and the average daily sleep duration (Less than 8 h, 8 to 9 h, 9 to 10 h, 10 to 11 h, More than 11 h).
Family sports-related consumption refers to the monetary expenditures incurred by families over the past year specifically for children’s participation in sports activities, including the purchase of sports equipment and related merchandise, venue rental fees, tickets or fees for online viewing of sports events, registration fees for sports training courses, and expenses related to sports tourism.

## Statistical methods
In this study, we utilized SPSS 26.0 for data analysis and conducted chi-square tests to initially examine the associations between categorical independent variables (such as gender, urban/rural classification, and ethnicity) and childhood stunting (normal, moderate, and severe stunting). During this process, groups with expected frequencies below the threshold were handled by merging categories. We employed ordered logistic regression due to the ordinal nature of the dependent variable (normal, moderate, severe stunting). Unlike multinomial models, ordered logistic regression explicitly incorporates information on the sequential ordering of outcomes, thus improving interpretability and statistical power. In contrast to linear regression, an ordered logistic model does not assume equal intervals between outcome levels. The proportional odds assumption for ordered logistic regression was tested and satisfied (p > 0.05). Results are reported at significance levels of 1% and 5%.

## Results
A Chi-square test was conducted to compare demographic characteristics (including gender, urban-rural residence, and regional distribution) between the non-respondents and excluded questionnaires (n = 743) and the final analytical sample (n = 5017). No significant differences were found (p > 0.05), suggesting that there was no substantial non-response bias in this study. At the data analysis stage, listwise deletion was applied to handle a small number of questionnaires (n = 27, representing 0.5% of the returned questionnaires) with missing values on critical variables. Given the very low proportion of deleted cases, this approach was unlikely to have substantially affected the analytical outcomes.
We used chi-square tests (Table  ) and ordered logistic regression analysis (Table  ) to examine the multiple factors influencing childhood stunting. The ordered logistic regression model was statistically significant (χ²=168.386, p = 0.000).
 

## Demographic factors
Gender has had a significant impact on childhood stunting. The chi-square test (Χ²=16.778, p < 0.001, Crammer’s V = 0.058) indicated a statistically significant relationship between gender and stunting; however, the magnitude of this association was limited, suggesting only a weak practical significance. The proportions of male children with moderate and severe stunting (6.09% and 3.41%) were higher than those of female children (4.72% and 1.85%). Logistic regression analysis further confirmed this, showing a significantly increased risk of stunting in males (OR = 1.539, p < 0.001).
The chi-square test results (Χ²=8.688, p = 0.013, Cramer’s V = 0.042) initially indicated differences in stunting between urban and rural children. A higher proportion of rural children (3.56%) experienced severe stunting compared to urban children (2.21%). However, the urban-rural classification was not significant after adjusting for potential confounders in the logistic regression analysis (p = 0.941), suggesting that the statistical significance observed from the chi-square test may have arisen due to confounding factors such as socioeconomic status, parental education, and sports-related participation. Therefore, our final interpretation and conclusion rely on the adjusted multivariable logistic regression results rather than the chi-square findings alone.
There were significant differences in childhood stunting among children in different regions, as shown by the chi-square test (Χ²=34.877, p < 0.001, Crammer’s V = 0.059). The East China region had the highest proportion of normal development at 94.44%, while the Northwest China region had the lowest at 88.66%. Ordered logistic regression analysis indicated that the risk of stunting was significantly lower in East China compared to Northwest China (OR = 0.566, 95% CI: 0.398–0.806, p = 0.002).
Ethnicity approached significance with the chi-square test showing (Χ²=4.761, p = 0.092, Crammer’s V = 0.052), suggesting a potential influence; however, this was not confirmed in the logistic regression analysis.

## Family and household characteristics
Childcare Situation: Childcare Situation strongly influenced childhood stunting, with a chi-square result (Χ²=50.722, p < 0.001, Crammer’s V = 0.071). The normal development proportion among non-enrolled children (85.25%) was significantly lower than that among those in kindergarten (92.94%). Logistic regression results similarly indicated a significantly increased risk for non-enrolled children (OR = 2.266, p = 0.002).
Number of Biological Siblings: The chi-square test indicated non-significance (Χ²=6.936, p = 0.139, Crammer’s V = 0.026), and logistic regression also did not find a significant impact.
Primary Caregiver: The chi-square test showed no influence on stunting (Χ²=1.693, p = 0.429, Crammer’s V = 0.022), and logistic regression analysis also did not display significance.
Cohabitation with Parents: The chi-square result (Χ²=1.689, p = 0.430, Crammer’s V = 0.018) indicated no significant impact on childhood stunting.
Parental Education: The chi-square test revealed a significant association between parental education levels and childhood stunting. The chi-square statistic for father’s education level was (Χ²=28.78, p < 0.001) and for mother’s education level was (Χ²=31.115, p < 0.001), with Crammer’s V values of 0.056 and 0.057, respectively, indicating a significant relationship between lower parental education and higher rates of stunting in children. In further ordered logistic regression analysis, the statistical significance of individual education levels weakened when controlling for complex interactions and other potential confounding factors. For instance, the relationship between fathers with primary or lower education levels and children’s stunting risk showed lower significance in multivariable analysis (p = 0.970). Similarly, the impact of mothers with primary or lower education was not highly significant (p = 0.184). These results suggest that while educational level has an important effect in univariate analysis (chi-square test), other factors may have more significant impacts within the overall model framework.
Annual Household Income: In this study, household economic status was identified as a key influence on childhood stunting. The chi-square test (Χ²=32.741, p < 0.001, Crammer’s V = 0.057) showed a significant association between average annual household income and stunting rates. This association indicates that lower household income is linked to higher rates of stunting. Ordered logistic regression analysis further quantified the impact of economic level, revealing that children from households with an annual income of 20,000 yuan or less had 2.58 times higher odds (OR = 2.581) of experiencing stunting compared to those from households earning over 150,000 yuan annually. The significance reached statistical meaning (p < 0.001), indicating that even when controlling for other socioeconomic factors and demographic characteristics, income remained an independent risk factor.

## Sports-related and lifestyle factors
Presence of Disease Affecting Physical Activity: The chi-square test result (Χ²=3.252, p = 0.197, Crammer’s V = 0.025) showed no significance in this study.
Sports-Related Spending: The chi-square test indicated significance (Χ²=29.602, p < 0.001, Crammer’s V = 0.077). In logistic regression, sports-related spending was associated with a lower risk of stunting (OR = 0.730, p = 0.007).
Average Daily Duration of Sports Activities: The chi-square test showed a significant association between sports activity duration and childhood stunting (Χ²=17.833, p = 0.007, Crammer’s V = 0.042). Children with insufficient weekly physical activity (0–15 min) had 1.41 times higher odds (OR = 1.413, p = 0.032) of experiencing stunting compared to those who engaged in physical activity for 60 min daily. Ordered logistic regression further explored this relationship, revealing that the less the exercise time, the higher the risk of stunting.
Enrollment in Sports-Focused Training Programs/Classes: Chi-square tests indicated that different enrollment statuses influenced childhood stunting (Χ²=19.453, p = 0.003, Crammer’s V = 0.044), with moderate and severe stunting rates being highest among children not enrolled in any classes, slightly lower among those who were interested but did not enroll, and lowest among those who occasionally or continuously enrolled.
Weekly Duration of the Child’s Sports Training Programs/Classes: Chi-square tests supported the benefits of longer participation in sports training (Χ²=16.789, p = 0.002, Crammer’s V = 0.055), with fewer children showing stunting among those participating for longer periods weekly. However, in logistic regression, neither of these factors emerged as independent influencing factors.
Number of Sports Participated In: This factor showed a significant relationship with childhood stunting (Χ²=17.743, p = 0.023, Crammer’s V = 0.042), with lower stunting rates observed in children participating in more sports activities. This was not identified as an independent influencing factor in logistic regression.
Duration of Consistent Participation in Sports: Chi-square tests indicated that long-term consistent participation in sports significantly reduced the risk of stunting (Χ²=31.171, p < 0.001, Crammer’s V = 0.056). Logistic regression analysis confirmed that those with more than 3 months of consistent participation experienced a significant reduction in the risk of stunting compared to those with less than one month.
Screen Time and Sleep: Analyses based on chi-square tests did not show a significant association between screen time on school days, weekend screen time, and average daily sleep (including naps) with children’s growth and stunting.

## Discussion
This study explored the impact of various factors on childhood stunting, revealing the significant roles of sports participation, sports consumption, and children’s exercise habits, as well as basic demographic information such as gender, geographical region, enrollment status, household economic conditions, and parental education level.
In this research, gender has a significant influence on childhood stunting. The proportion of male children experiencing moderate to severe stunting is significantly higher than that of female children. This difference may stem from biological mechanisms, as male children are often more sensitive to adverse environmental factors during their growth and development [ ]. Moreover, societal expectations associated with gender roles may also influence the prioritization of nutrition and health care for boys and girls [ ]. This phenomenon has been corroborated by multiple studies, which found that the prevalence of stunting among boys was significantly higher than that among girls (40% vs. 36%) across 16 countries, with a rough risk ratio of 1.16, indicating that boys in low socioeconomic families are more susceptible to health inequalities [ ]. Although variables such as gender, urban-rural classification, geographic region, household income, sports-related spending, and duration of physical activity showed statistical significance, the Cramer’s V values were consistently low (< 0.1). This indicates that the associations between these variables and childhood stunting were relatively weak, suggesting limited practical significance. Therefore, the practical implications of these statistical associations should be interpreted cautiously.
Regional factors also show a significant effect on childhood stunting. Children in East China have the highest proportion of normal development, while those in Northwest China exhibit the lowest. Such marked regional disparities may reflect differences in economic conditions, resource distribution, and public health services. The higher incidence of stunting in Northwest China compared to East China may be related to local cultural practices and inadequate health facilities [ ]. A study conducted in China also indicates that as many as 39.7% of children in impoverished regions experience developmental delays, closely linked to economic factors such as lack of toys, caregiver depression, insufficient learning activities, and low family income [ ]. Children from families with an annual income below 20,000 yuan have 2.58 times higher odds to experience stunting compared to their higher-income counterparts, suggesting that economic hardships may restrict families’ access to nutritious foods and quality healthcare service.
Furthermore, parental education levels are highly correlated with children’s developmental status. Although univariate analyses indicate that children of parents with lower educational attainment are more likely to experience stunting, this effect weakens in multivariate models, suggesting that the interplay of various factors affects development in complex environments. Research in many countries has shown that poor family economic conditions are an independent risk factor for childhood stunting [ , , ]. Low-income families often struggle to provide sufficient nutritious foods and quality healthcare services, which adversely affects children’s nutritional status and healthy development [ ].
Enrollment status is also an important factor affecting childhood stunting. The proportion of normal development among non-enrolled children is significantly lower than that of children enrolled in kindergartens, highlighting the critical role of early education and supervision in children’s healthy development. Most studies indicate a positive association between childcare attendance and children’s developmental performance on standardized measures [ ]. However, some research emphasizes [ ] that attending kindergarten or daycare might increase the risk of malnutrition, with these children more likely to experience underweight and stunting compared to those cared for at home, especially within the age group of 24 to 60 months, which aligns precisely with this study’s scope. Our findings markedly differ from those of prior studies, a divergence that may be attributed to considerable differences in kindergarten quality between China and other regions. In recent years, kindergartens in China have been subject to strict national education and health regulations, emphasizing high-quality standards in nutritional meals, healthcare services, and hygiene supervision. These conditions substantially differ from childcare environments seen in previous studies conducted in resource-limited areas or economically disadvantaged countries.
Our study found that regular participation in physical activity significantly reduced children’s risk of stunting. The underlying mechanisms for this association may include stimulation of growth hormone secretion [ ], which directly promotes bone and muscle growth, as well as the improvement of gastrointestinal function through regular exercise [ ], thereby enhancing nutrient absorption and utilization, improving overall nutritional status, and consequently reducing stunting likelihood [ ].
A significant association exists between time spent on sports activities and childhood stunting. Children with insufficient sports activity (average weekly activity time of 0–15 min) are more prone to stunting compared to those who engage in sports activity for 60 min daily. This finding supports existing literature, which suggests that active investment and participation in sports can enhance children’s sports development. Current research indicates that more active children generally exhibit higher peak bone mass compared to less active peers [ ]. These physiological changes not only stimulate the development of bodily systems but may also enhance cognitive function and emotional stability, further supporting holistic development.
Therefore, encouraging regular sports activity among children is a necessary measure for preventing stunting and promoting overall health. Children who engage in a greater variety of sports exhibit lower stunting rates, although logical regression does not identify this as an independent factor. However, sustained participation in sports for more than three months significantly reduces the risk of stunting and serves as an independent protective factor. Compared to diverse sports activities, the consistent engagement in sports activity is more crucial in fostering healthy habits and reducing stunting risks.
Opportunities for play facilitate the development of fundamental motor skills such as crawling, standing, walking, running, and jumping. Without these opportunities, habitual sports activity may be jeopardized, potentially leading to reduced energy expenditure (and possible weight gain) [ ]. In this study, sports consumption significantly reduced the risk of stunting in young children, independent of annual family income as an economic factor, emphasizing the need for parents to invest in sports activities or courses to provide better sports environments and opportunities. Chi-square tests also indicate that when parents are willing to enroll their children in sports training classes, the children’s stunting probability is lower, and if they continue enrollment, the probability further decreases. Conversely, children whose parents are hesitant or unwilling to enroll them face higher stunting probabilities, underscoring the importance of parental attitudes toward financial investment in sports activities.
While many studies have explored these factors, the novelty of this study lies in its approach by focusing on early childhood sports participation, considering previously underexplored aspects such as family support for sports activities (attitudes and economic factors) and the quality and quantity of children’s own participation in sports. This multidimensional analysis offers a more comprehensive perspective on how sports promote healthy development in early childhood, revealing impacts of sports activities not widely explored in previous research. Additionally, this study is based on a nationwide government survey, covering almost the entire mainland, with scientifically weighted sampling and high-quality data collection and supervision, providing more reliable insights into unique influence patterns within the Chinese context. However, this study also has limitations, as its cross-sectional design, while offering valuable insights into related factors, cannot establish causality. Thus, the observed associations need further validation in future studies to confirm how these factors directly affect stunting. For a deeper understanding of causality, future research should consider a longitudinal design to observe how changes in influencing factors over time affect children’s long-term development. Although the overall response rate was relatively high, potential non-response bias still deserves consideration. Non-respondents may systematically differ from respondents in terms of socioeconomic background, family educational status, residential location, or health status, potentially influencing the representativeness and generalizability of our study findings.

## Conclusions
In this nationwide cross-sectional study, it was found that the risk of childhood stunting is influenced by gender, family economic status, enrollment status, and geographical division. Boys exhibit a higher rate of stunting compared to girls, and children in East China face a lower risk than those in Northwest China. Family support for sports activities, encompassing both attitudinal and material support, along with children’s participation in sports, serve as important protective factors against childhood stunting. Children who actively engage in sports activities have a significantly lower risk of stunting compared to those with insufficient physical exercise. In comparison to a variety of sports activities, the duration of consistent participation in a single sports discipline more effectively reduces the risk of childhood stunting.
We recommend that the government provide financial support for children’s sports activities and facility construction in economically underdeveloped regions such as Northwest China. Additionally, efforts should be made to encourage kindergartens to integrate appropriate regular physical activities into their daily curricula and to strengthen parental awareness regarding the importance of physical exercise. Despite the strengths of this study—including broadly representative regional coverage and a large national sample—our cross-sectional design limits the validity of causal inference. Future longitudinal or prospective cohort studies are therefore urgently needed to explore the complex interactions among physical activity participation, nutritional intake, genetic factors, and notably socioeconomic determinants, thereby clarifying causal relationships and assessing long-term effects more definitively.
