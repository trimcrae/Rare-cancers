However, the societal influence of international sporting events emerges from complex, nonlinear interactions that require more expressive modeling frameworks. This study proposes a Sociocultural Dual-Attention TabTransformer (SC-DATransformer) to predict a continuous Social Impact Score, defined as a normalized composite index derived from validated indicators including cultural engagement, diversity, gender representation, public sentiment, sustainability, and media coverage. The empirical analysis is conducted on a structured dataset comprising approximately 70,000 event-level observations described by 21 variables, covering multiple global competitions across regions and years. To enhance representation quality and reduce redundancy, feature engineering and selection techniques such as mutual information and dimensionality reduction are employed. Model performance is evaluated against established baselines using complementary regression metrics, supported by 95% bootstrap confidence intervals and Diebold–Mariano statistical testing. Within the experimental setting, the proposed model demonstrates improved predictive consistency and lower error magnitudes relative to baseline approaches. Explainable AI analysis further provides transparent insight into dominant sociocultural drivers influencing impact estimation.

## Introduction
Culture and sports are the basis of worldwide social unity and economic life. Sporting activities like the Olympics, FIFA world cup and regional championships do not feel the boundaries of ethnic geography but make cultural exchange and national identity . Recent reports by the World Economic Forum (2024) indicate that the global sports industry adds on average 3% of the global GDP, and its annual revenues are over 620 billion USD, and are expected to grow at the rate of 7.4% per year . Other than economic benefits, such events also influence international relations, social integration and communal cultural scriptures, as shown in Fig. . To most developing countries, the positive effects of hosting large-scale sports events include evident benefits in terms of employment, tourism, and infrastructure-building, data control , as part of the national branding and soft power . Therefore, the sociocultural and economic effects of events of this sort have become an interdisciplinary necessity, connecting economics, cultural studies, and computational social science .

 

Artificial Intelligence (AI) has become a revolution in evaluating the societal and cultural consequences of international sporting events in recent years. Due to the innovations in the domain of machine learning (ML), deep learning (DL), and explainable AI (XAI), it is now possible to measure the impact of world events on different aspects, including cultural diversity, population mood, gender balance, and sustainable development . The dynamic modeling of national identity, inclusivity , and media coverage interaction that defines the perception of a country internationally is possible with the use of AI-powered analytics . This data science and sociocultural interpretation combination has created new opportunities in the field of computational social impact analysis and enabled evidence-based policymaking and fairness in sports . Therefore, AI-based models are not only useful in assisting governments and organizations to assess the performance of events, but they also indicate the way of how culture and society develop due to sport participation . Despite their importance, the sociocultural consequences of global sporting events remain difficult to quantify due to their inherently nonlinear, contextual, and interdependent nature . Conventional econometric and regression methods, even though useful, in most instances do not produce the nonlinear, high-dimensional interactions that characterize sociocultural phenomena . Traditional econometric and regression-based approaches, while useful for isolating individual effects, are limited in their capacity to model high-order interactions among cultural, social, and media-driven variables. Previously, cultural and sentimental data were perceived as fixed and the dynamic emotional expression modes and contextual trends that develop over time and place were disregarded .
Besides, traditional paradigms were not explainable, and it was hard to comprehend how such variables as gender equity, media sentiment or economic parity have a combined effect on global sports perception . However, these models primarily focus on feature-level attention and do not explicitly integrate sociocultural context embeddings or event-conditioned representations . In parallel, graph-based and knowledge-augmented sociocultural analytics frameworks have been explored , yet they often rely on static relational assumptions and lack calibrated, end-to-end predictive modeling for continuous social impact estimation . More critically, existing models lack the ability to jointly capture contextual dependencies and sociocultural interactions at the event level, resulting in incomplete representations of social impact . In addition, recent tabular deep learning architectures such as TabTransformer, SAINT, and FT-Transformer demonstrate improved nonlinear modeling over classical machine learning approaches, they primarily operate on feature-level self-attention without explicit modeling of event-conditioned sociocultural context or calibrated cross-feature dependency structures . Moreover, these architectures are optimized for generic tabular prediction tasks and do not incorporate domain-informed monotonic constraints, sociocultural calibration losses, or interpretability-driven structural priors necessary for modeling latent social constructs. Therefore, a contextualized dual-attention mechanism tailored to sociocultural event analytics remains underexplored . In this study, the Social Impact Score is conceptualized as a reflective latent construct representing the aggregated sociocultural influence of a sporting event, defined in Fig. . The construct integrates observable indicators spanning diversity, cultural engagement, gender representation, public sentiment, sustainability performance, and media amplification. Each indicator is normalized to a comparable scale and combined using a calibrated aggregation framework to preserve multidimensional influence. The resulting score is continuous and bounded, representing relative sociocultural resonance rather than causal societal transformation. This formulation aligns with composite index modeling principles and multidimensional social systems theory.

 

In this study, we propose a Sociocultural Dual-Attention TabTransformer (SC-DATransformer), a incremental AI-driven architecture designed to predict and explain Social Impact Scores of global sporting events. The main objective of the work is to create an incremental transformer-based model, which improves the current architectures with sociocultural context embeddings, dual-attention layers, as well as contrastive calibration mechanisms. Such advances allow the model to acquire complicated interrelations between the engagement with cultural, diversity, sentiment, sustainability, and media influence. The proposed model is tested on a structured set of international sports events and is used to estimate the relative effects of sociocultural dimensions on event-level performance to provide strong and perceptive information on how cultural and social equity variables contribute to the global perception. The main contributions of this research are as follows:

Development of a dual-attention transformer integrating contextual embeddings for event-wise sociocultural understanding.
Introduction of a contrastive calibration loss to align similar cultural impact patterns while penalizing divergent ones.
Construction of an interpretable social impact prediction framework using SHAP-based XAI for transparency and fairness.
Empirical validation on a large-scale Global Sporting Events Dataset, achieving superior mean absolute error, calibration reliability, and interpretability over traditional baselines.

Section will explain the experimental design and performance measures, whereas the results, findings on interpretability and sociocultural implications will be discussed in Sect. . Lastly, the final part of Sect. contains the major conclusions, limitations, and guidelines of future research on AI-powered global impact assessment.

## Research proposed methodology
The proposed methodology introduces a combined AI-based analytical approach to assessing the sociocultural effects of international sports events. It involves data preprocessing, feature engineering, and multi-stage model development and involves the combination of statistical learning and transformer-based deep architecture to predict the high fidelity of impact and explainable analysis, as shown in Fig.  .

 

## Data preprocessing
The preprocessing pipeline was developed in such a manner that data integrity, consistency, and analysis preparedness were achieved to both statistical and deep learning pipelines. The first one was a complete schema validation to impose variable-specific constraints, so that Public sentiment value was limited within the range of values, where − 1 and 1 were the two extreme values, and diversity score, sustainability factor, cultural engagement level and media coverage index take values within the normalized range The attribute of Gender_Representation was restricted to a range between 0 and 100 and the attributes Global_Viewership (million) and GDP_Per_Capita were confirmed to be positive real-values. The strategy of imputation of missing data was a hybrid strategy, which involved imputing numerical attributes with an Iterative Imputer k = 5 neighbors, and imputing categorical features with an Unknown label with some optional binary missing-flag indicator when the absence pattern had semantic significance. To control skew and heavy-tailed distributions within social media volume, gdp per capita, and global viewership the outlier control was performed by using Winsorization of the 1 st and 99th percentiles or by using the logarithmic transformation to A RobustScaler was used to normalize continuous variables to reduce the effect of outliers during the training of linear and neural models, although no scaling was done on tree-based learners.
Encoding strategies were tailored to low-cardinality categorical variables such as Region were one-hot encoded, and high-cardinality variables such as event name, sport type and country used target encoded ensemble models and neural architecture learned embeddings respectively. To avoid data leakage and to be able to evaluate a model fairly, all the preprocessing transformations (imputers, scalers, encoders) were only fitted on the training partition and later applied to validation and test subsets. This all-inclusive preprocessing system standardized the heterogeneous data sources whilst retaining the sociocultural semantics on which a robust interpretation and generalization of the model would rely.

## Feature engineering and sociocultural composites
To model interpretability and to respond to the latent sociocultural interactions that are implied by global sporting events, several composite indicators were constructed based on the original data. These derived features were intended to reflect multidimensional interactions between cultural, economic, and media-related aspects that, in combination, affect the social impact score, as displayed in Table  . Feature engineering was carried out in a two-step plan involving filter selection paradigm and wrapper selection paradigm. The construction of interpretable composite indices to capture sociocultural relationships in the first stage was made. Cultural Index2 is the means of diversity score and cultural engagement level which has the balanced score of cultural diversity and community involvement. The Sustainability Index reflects consciously of the environmentally responsible by the combination of the Sustainability Factor and Media Visibility which is a relationship between the ecological dedication and the media awareness.

 

The interaction term between the Sentiment and Gender variable is multiplied by Public Sentiment and normalized Gender- Representation, and this gives the gendered tone of the emotional sentiment in the discourse of the people. Equity Ratio is proportional inclusive as it rules out without paying undue attention to Gender Representation and limits the Diversity Score between [0, 3] to prevent disproportionate power. The Media Amplification option measures the strength of the spreading social media by weighing the online discussion logarithmic volume by the media coverage. The Resource Proxy is a rough measure of economic-cultural interdependence, which combines logarithm of GDP per capita and Diversity Score. In the next phase, hybrid feature selection framework was applied. The filter-based step used the Mutual Information criterion as the measure of the nonlinear dependence between features and the target variable whereas the wrapper-based step used SHAP-Recursive Feature Elimination (SHAP-RFE) to stabilize feature retention under the interpretability of the model requirement. This synergy of designed composites and strong selection criteria enhanced the capability of the learning model to identify the delicacy of patterns in sociocultural patterns and retain explainability and statistical reliability.

## Proposed model: sociocultural dual-attention tab transformer
The proposed Sociocultural Dual-Attention Tab Transformer (SC-DATransformer) will be a deep tabular model that will be explicitly used to stem from the multidimensional sociocultural fabric of international sporting events. It synthesizes multifaceted interdependences among cultural, economic, media and demographic variables and is interpretable by the attention mechanisms and explainable distillation, as shown in Fig. . The network builds up on the Tab Transformer, with the addition of a dual-attention learning unit, a contrastive calibration head, monotonic constraints that are soft, and interpretable knowledge-distillation unit . The aim of the model is to anticipate the Social Impact Score (y) using a mixed combination of numerical and categorical attributes, which reflect the sociocultural traits of an event.

## End-to-end functional workflow
SC-DATransformer takes raw data of events that contain categorical and continuous variables. Continuous features are normalized and linearly embedded, and categorical fields are modeled using contextual ones. The tokenized representations are then processed by several layers of dual attention which learn intra feature dependencies, and contextual sociocultural conditioning. The resulting latent vector obtained at the end of the last layer is a summary of the sociocultural signature of the event and is then input into a regression head that produces the predicted Social Impact Score. The model in training tries to reduce the monotonic constraints of both MSE and contrastive losses as it tries to reduce the monotonic constraints to ensure the consistency of predictions and the domain consistency. The attention rollouts and knowledge-distilled student model ultimately give clear interpretability on the learned cultural, media and economic determinant relationships. To eliminate possible overfitting threats posed by the architectural intricacy of the proposed SC-DATransformer, a variety of explicit regularization and stabilization design elements are integrated and managed in a systematic manner. They are moderate embedding dimensionality, restricted context embedding, dropout on attention and feed-forward layers, weight decay using AdamW optimization, gradient clipping, label smoothing, and early stopped using validation performance. Beyond this a contrastive calibration loss is used to regularize the latent representation space and soft monotonic constraints are used to provide theoretically consistent feature-outcome relationships. All hyperparameters that control capacity and regularization of the model are all clearly observed and convergence occurs very early before the maximum training epochs, a pointer of controlled generalization as opposed to overfitting as reported in Table  . Although the Social Impact Score is derived from sociocultural indicators, strict separation between target construction and model inputs was enforced. The composite target is computed using temporally aggregated and smoothed representations of validated indicators, whereas model training utilizes event-level contemporaneous feature embeddings. No direct reuse of intermediate aggregation weights or normalized target components is permitted during training. Additionally, correlation audits and permutation-based leakage diagnostics were conducted to confirm the absence of trivial identity mappings. A target reconstruction baseline was implemented to test whether the Social Impact Score could be trivially recovered using linear combinations of raw inputs. The low explanatory power of this reconstruction confirms that the proposed model is not exploiting circular dependencies.

 

## Experimental setup
The experiment was meant to be conducted in a rigorous manner to test the predictive and interpretive abilities of the proposed SC-DATransformer against the traditional machine learning baselines. All the experiments were implemented in Python-based frameworks on a GPU-enabled environment to make sure that they were computed efficiently. The data was randomly split into three proportions of training (70%), validation (15%), and testing (15%), and all the preprocessing transformations were trained only on the training set to avoid leakage. Randomized search with cross-validation was used to optimize model hyperparameters and stability of training was evaluated after 60 epochs. The measurement used was evaluation metrics to facilitate exhaustive measurement of predictive accuracy and reliability.

## Data description
The dataset is a set of all sociocultural, economic, and engagement indicators based on global sporting events that have been organized since 2000 and 2024. Data are aggregated from publicly available and verifiable sources involves 70,000 observations in 21 variables, including official records from international sports federations such as Olympic, FIFA world cup, Asian games and commonwealth games, global economic databases, large-scale media analytics platforms, and automated sentiment analysis services applied to multilingual public discourse. Every record includes an indication of the role of the demographic composition, cultural values, and regional features in shaping the perception of people and the impact on social lives in general, as displayed in Table  . The target variable, referred to as the Social Impact Score, but modeled as a continuous latent construction derived from a normalized aggregation of multiple validated sociocultural indicators. ## Baseline models
A series of baseline regression models were used to determine the complexity of the sociocultural data in predicting values, which would form a sound comparative baseline. These baselines taken collectively have offered a complete spectrum of benchmarks, ranging between the linear and nonlinear and ensemble paradigms, where the performance and interpretability of the proposed transformer-based model could be strictly assessed.

## External validity and robustness analysis
Although the proposed model achieves high predictive accuracy, it is important to note that the Social Impact Score represents a continuous composite index derived from normalized sociocultural indicators rather than a raw observational variable. As such, the target exhibits lower stochastic noise than typical user-generated outcomes. To mitigate concerns of optimistic evaluation, temporal and cross-regional validation results, which demonstrate consistent but slightly reduced performance, confirming that the model does not rely on data leakage or trivial correlations. Additional validation analyses were performed to evaluate the robustness and generalizability of the proposed SC-DATransformer on other splits other than the standard random splits, reported in Table  . The longitudinal assessment was first conducted by training the model on previous years (2000–2016) and testing on the next years (2017–2024) as a simulation of the actual implementation in the real world. Second, a leave-one-region-out validation strategy was implemented where all regions but one were used to train the model and the remaining region was used as a proxy of external validation in different geopolitical settings. Lastly, the domain-consistency validation was executed through analyzing whether the explanations provided by the model were congruent with the existing sociocultural theory with purported explanations being verified using SHAP-based interpretability to establish that increasing diversity, gender representation, and cultural involvement would consistently increase the predicted social impact. The combination of these analyses indicates stability of elements across time, cross-regional generalization and plausibility of the theories. To rule out target leakage, all composite components used in constructing the Social Impact Score were excluded from direct inclusion in their raw aggregated form during model training. Only normalized and temporally smoothed feature representations available at prediction time were used. Feature-target dependency analysis confirmed no deterministic reconstruction of the target from input variables.

 

To quantify statistical uncertainty, 95% confidence intervals were computed for all primary evaluation metrics using non-parametric bootstrap resampling. These intervals are reported in Table  , alongside point estimates to avoid selective performance reporting. As, temporal generalization was assessed using forward-chaining splits (train: 2000–2016, test: 2017–2024), confirming stable performance under real-world deployment conditions.

## Findings and limitations
The findings suggest that the proposed model can capture the concept of the intricate, nonlinear correlations among sociocultural, economical, and engagement-related variables related to the global sporting events. Relative to traditional regression-based and tree-based baselines, the model has been found to have a better predictive consistency, which implies that dual-attention mechanisms and contextual embedding allow integrating heterogeneous groups of features more efficiently. Specifically, the acquired representations put a greater emphasis on relationships between the public sentiment, diversity, gender representation, and cultural engagement, which underline their collective value in forming the patterns of the estimated social impacts between regions and events. Figure  summarizes the hierarchical structure of the proposed study’s contributions, linking theoretical formulation, architectural innovation, optimization strategies, and validation mechanisms within a unified framework. The figure illustrates how methodological components collectively support predictive robustness, interpretability, and external validity of the Social Impact Score modeling process.

 

However, the predictive nature of the modeling framework limits the scope of the interpretations of these findings. It is estimated that the Social Impact Score is a continuous outcome based on the observed proxies and the model represents statistical relationships as opposed to causal relations. Although explainability analysis can give us an idea of how the model puts more weight on the various inputs, such attributions are model sensitive and dependent but not normative or causal. In line with this, the results are to be taken as an indication of organized sociocultural signal correspondence in the dataset, which gives quantitative backup to comparative analysis but no conclusive assertions as to the actual societal change.

 

There are a few limitations of the current research that should be considered. To calculate the Social Impact Score, the research design is based on aggregated and normalized indicators to estimate a sociocultural impact but cannot reflect qualitative aspects of historical context, symbolic meaning or local social processes. Also, the underlying data sources, especially media coverage and sentiment indicators are unevenly represented regionally and biased due to platform visibility, which might be disproportionately important to economically salient or media intensive events. Despite the use of preprocessing, normalization and interpretability measures, these structural biases have the potential to affect the outputs of the model. From an ethical perspective, the framework is designed for analytical and exploratory purposes and should not be interpreted as an evaluative instrument of cultural merit or social equity, to support responsible application of AI-driven sociocultural analysis.

This study introduced the SC-DATransformer, a dual-attention tabular architecture designed to model nonlinear and context-dependent interactions among sociocultural, economic, and engagement indicators for predicting a formally defined Social Impact Score. The empirical evaluation demonstrates strong internal predictive consistency across multiple complementary metrics, including error-based and rank-based measures, supported by robustness validation under temporal and regional splits. However, these findings should be interpreted within the constraints of the structured dataset and experimental design rather than as definitive evidence of universal real-world generalization. Although robustness checks reduce the likelihood of overfitting and leakage, the absence of fully independent external datasets remains an important limitation. The interpretability analysis based on SHAP provides stable and theory-consistent associations, indicating that diversity, public sentiment, gender representation, and cultural engagement are strongly associated with variations in the Social Impact Score. These results reflect predictive associations rather than causal relationships, and the study does not claim causal inference. While monotonic constraints, contrastive calibration loss, and controlled regularization were incorporated to enhance theoretical coherence and reduce variance, the composite nature of the target variable introduces structural dependency considerations that warrant continued scrutiny. Although explicit leakage-prevention protocols were implemented and documented, future refinements will further separate raw indicator pipelines from target construction stages to eliminate any residual signal overlap risk. Another limitation concerns benchmarking scope. While classical machine learning models and gradient-boosting approaches were evaluated, the study does not yet include comparisons against the most recent state-of-the-art deep tabular architectures under identical experimental settings. This restricts the breadth of competitive positioning and will be addressed in subsequent extensions. In addition, broader fairness evaluation and subgroup stability testing across demographic and regional segments remain areas requiring deeper exploration. Future work will therefore prioritize independent cross-platform validation using externally sourced datasets, expanded benchmarking against contemporary deep tabular models, causal modeling extensions through structural equation and counterfactual frameworks, and formal bias auditing procedures to assess distributional stability across regions and time. Longitudinal stress-testing under domain shift and governance-oriented deployment frameworks will also be incorporated to strengthen methodological rigor and practical reliability. Through these extensions, the framework aims to transition from strong predictive modeling toward externally validated and methodologically resilient sociocultural analytics.
