## Abstract

## Abstract

## Objectives
 Inconsistent condom use (ICU) during anal sexual activity among men who have sex with men (MSM) can result in infection with HIV and other sexually transmitted infections (STIs). This study aimed to provide a scientific foundation for developing effective HIV prevention and control strategies for this population by analysing the prevalence of ICU and associated factors during anal sexual encounters among MSM recruited online.

## Design
A cross-sectional online survey was performed.

## Setting and participants
MSM were recruited online through a non-governmental organisation in May 2024 and completed a questionnaire survey in China.

## Primary and secondary outcome measures
Based on whether the participants used condoms during anal sex with male partners, they were divided into two groups (ICU and non-ICU). Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were used to analyse the factors associated with ICU.

## Results
A total of 604 participants were analysed, of whom 400 (66.2%) reported ICU during anal sexual activity with homosexual partners. Multivariate logistic regression analysis indicated that participants who engaged in behaviours such as drinking alcohol, using drugs or taking performance-enhancing medications were more likely to have ICU during encounters with homosexual partners (adjusted OR (aOR) 2.93; 95% CI 1.64 to 5.47). Conversely, participants who had one-night stands (aOR 0.57; 95% CI 0.39 to 0.84), those with a self-efficacy score of 9 for condom use (aOR 0.47; 95% CI 0.29 to 0.77) and those who underwent HIV testing in the past year (aOR 0.39; 95% CI 0.21 to 0.68) were less likely to report ICU.

## Conclusions
A high proportion of participants reported ICU during sexual activity with homosexual partners. There is a need for education and awareness campaigns targeting MSM to promote consistent condom use during sexual activity, expand HIV testing coverage and discourage alcohol intake, drug use or use of enhancement medications during sexual encounters with homosexual partners to reduce the risk of contracting HIV and other STIs.

## STRENGTHS AND LIMITATIONS OF THIS STUDY
This study used online recruitment via a non-governmental organisation, enabling convenient access to the specific men who have sex with men (MSM) population in China.
Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were applied to rigorously identify factors associated with inconsistent condom use.
The cross-sectional design of this study prevents the establishment of causal relationships between the identified factors and inconsistent condom use.
Reliance on self-reported questionnaire data may introduce recall bias or social desirability bias among participants.
Recruitment from a single non-governmental organisation and a specific time point (May 2024) may limit the generalisability of the results to the broader MSM population in China.

## Introduction
 AIDS continues to be a significant public health challenge globally and profoundly affects the quality of life of individuals infected with HIV. Over the past decade, the incidence of new AIDS cases worldwide has gradually declined, largely due to the promotion of antiretroviral therapy and preventive strategies. However, HIV transmission remains a serious issue among high-risk populations, including men who have sex with men (MSM), people who inject drugs and commercial sex workers. Despite ongoing improvements in the accessibility of antiviral medications, HIV-related discrimination and stigma persist, adversely impacting medical care, treatment adherence, willingness to adhere to medication and the overall health of infected individuals. AIDS is a critical problem in China. Many individuals continue to hold misconceptions about AIDS, particularly adolescents and residents of certain rural areas where knowledge of HIV transmission is limited. Furthermore, the uneven distribution of social support and medical resources for some HIV-positive individuals hinders timely and effective treatment. Thus, health education tailored to various populations, promoting social inclusion, reducing stigma and increasing investment in AIDS prevention and control are essential strategies in the ongoing efforts against the AIDS epidemic.
The HIV infection rate among MSM is significantly higher than that among the general population. The increasing popularity of social media and online dating platforms has led to an increasing number of MSM using the internet for socialisation and dating. Although the internet provides convenient communication channels, it also increases, to some extent, the incidence of risky behaviours. Moreover, the HIV infection rate among younger MSM is particularly alarming, as this demographic often lacks adequate knowledge regarding HIV treatment and prevention measures. High-risk behaviours, such as inconsistent condom use (ICU) and having multiple partners, are significant contributors to the rapid spread of HIV in this population. Although the internet affords MSM more opportunities for connection and dating, its anonymity can lead to complacency, thereby increasing the likelihood of engaging in risky behaviours. Additionally, societal stigma and discrimination create barriers that hinder some MSM from seeking support and medical assistance. In certain cultural contexts, MSM may experience heightened social pressure and psychological burdens, which can discourage them from undergoing HIV testing or accessing preventive measures out of fear of discrimination or rejection.
Through social media and online platforms, MSM have increased access to sexual partners and can conveniently communicate their emotional and sexual needs. However, this convenience also introduces potential risks, particularly concerning the ICU during anal sexual activities. While condom use effectively reduces the risk of HIV and other sexually transmitted infections (STIs), many MSM choose not to use condoms consistently during anal sex, which increases the likelihood of contracting HIV or other STIs. Presently, research on the factors associated with ICU among MSM recruited online is limited. This study aimed to address this research gap by investigating the characteristics of MSM and analysing their social interaction patterns and sexual behaviour traits. Through this cross-sectional study, we aimed to gain deeper insights into the prevalence of condom use adherence while engaging in sexual activities among MSM recruited online and the associated factors. These findings will enhance our understanding of partner selection behaviours within the MSM community and provide scientific evidence for developing more effective health interventions and HIV prevention strategies.

## Materials and methods

## Research design and participants
This cross-sectional survey study was conducted by a non-governmental organisation (NGO) named Ma'anqianxun health information consultation center in Zhejiang province in May 2024 by recruiting MSM online and administering a questionnaire. The NGO is based in Zhejiang Province that focuses on community health, with an emphasis on health promotion for sexual minorities—particularly MSM. The organisation does not directly provide HIV antiviral treatment services. Instead, its core activities include disseminating health education materials, conducting antidiscrimination advocacy and offering psychological support and health-related counselling services to relevant communities. Recruitment advertisements were primarily distributed through online communities and platforms operated by the NGO. The advertisements clearly outlined the research topic (‘MSM health-related behaviour survey’), participation criteria (men aged 18–60 who have had sexual activity with other men) and the principles of anonymity. MSM refers to having engaged in sexual activities between males, including masturbation, oral sex and anal sex. 
The questionnaire was set up on the Wenjuanxing platform, allowing each WeChat ID to complete it once. Effective quality control measures were implemented through questionnaire restrictions, including mandatory questions, logic jumps and response range limitations, to obtain valid questionnaires that met the recruitment criteria. The survey staff received standardised training for using a uniform questionnaire for data collection. Before the survey, the investigators explained the purpose, significance, methodology and privacy protection policy to the participants; this information was included at the beginning of the questionnaire. Participants were informed that the survey aimed to develop prevention strategies for HIV and STIs among MSM, that the survey would be anonymous, and that only group data, not personal data, would be analysed.
This study used internet recruitment rather than random sampling; thereby, we employed a non-probability sampling method akin to snowball sampling. To determine the sample size, the following calculation method was applied: using data from behavioural monitoring obtained in repeated surveys, we derived the formula n=400×Q/P. Here, P represents the estimated proportion of the relevant behaviour occurring during the survey; specifically, it is based on the proportion of MSM who reported unprotected anal intercourse, estimated between 28.7% and 53.0% according to related surveys. , In this study, we used P=47.5%, Q=1 P. Given these parameters, the study necessitates a minimum of 442 participants recruited via the internet. A total of 626 MSM were enrolled, of which 604 completed the survey after providing informed consent, while 22 were excluded because of refusal to participate.
The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) aged 18 years or older, (2) recruited online and (3) willing to participate in the survey after providing informed consent.
The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) individuals under 18 years of age, (2) recruited offline or at fixed locations, (3) refusal to participate after providing informed consent and (4) individuals who self-reported having mental disorders or consciousness impairments.

## Survey content
Our questionnaire design was mainly based on the Chinese MSM sentinel monitoring questionnaire and the college student survey questionnaire , (see ). After conducting a presurvey and improving the questionnaire variable settings, we conducted a formal questionnaire survey. The survey questionnaire mainly included general sociodemographic characteristics (age, marital status, place of residence, educational level, average monthly income and family relationships), sexual behaviour-related characteristics (sexual attitudes, use of condoms during sexual activities, current regular sexual partner status, whether sexual activities occurred in the last 6 months and whether there was any alcohol or drug use during homosexual activities), whether the participants accessed HIV prevention services in the past year, whether they had undergone HIV testing in the previous year, self-efficacy regarding condom use and knowledge of the HIV infection status of homosexual partners.

## Definitions of relevant indicators
ICU refers to participants engaging in anal sexual intercourse with homosexual partners in the past 6 months without using a condom every time or not using a condom throughout the entire sexual activity. Commercial sexual behaviour refers to sexual activities that involve financial transactions such as keeping a mistress, solicitation or prostitution. A harmonious family refers to a positive dynamic wherein members communicate effectively, offer mutual emotional support, navigate challenges collectively and experience minimal conflict. HIV prevention services refer to whether the participants received HIV-related materials, condom distribution, counselling services and training sessions in the past year. One-night stand refers to temporary sexual encounters between MSM, characterised by a single sexual relationship that occurs voluntarily based on physiological impulses, sensory attraction or immediate needs among MSM who do not know each other, without emotional commitment, follow-up contact or transactions involving money or goods. 
Self-efficacy in condom use was measured using a scale developed by Hanna et al that includes three questions: (1) before engaging in sexual activity, do you feel confident discussing condom use with your sexual partner? (2) If your partner refuses to use a condom or does not have one, do you feel uncertain about not engaging in sexual activity? (3) Do you feel confident in preparing condoms in advance before engaging in sexual activity? Each of the three questions has five response options: very confident, fairly confident, somewhat confident, not confident and very unconfident, with scores of 3, 2, 1, 0 and −1, respectively. The total score was divided into three categories: 9 points, 5–8 points and ≤4. The Cronbach’s alpha coefficient for this measurement was 0.816.

## Quality control
A questionnaire platform was established to allow each participant with a WeChat account to complete the survey only once. Effective quality control was implemented through various restrictions in the questionnaire, such as mandatory questions, logical skips and limitations on responses, to obtain valid questionnaires that met the recruitment criteria. The investigators underwent unified training before the survey using a standardised questionnaire. Before the survey, the investigator explained the purpose, significance, methodology and privacy protection policies related to the study to the participants, and this information was included at the beginning of the questionnaire. Participants were informed that the purpose of the survey was to formulate HIV and STIs prevention strategies for MSM and that the survey was anonymous and analysed only aggregate data without using personal data.

## Patient and public involvement
None.

## Statistical analysis
Statistical analyses were performed using R V.4.4.1 software. Continuous data are expressed as mean±SD. Categorical data are expressed as counts or rates and were analysed using the χ2 test. Participants were divided into two groups based on whether they adhered to condom use during anal intercourse with homosexual partners (1=ICU, 0=non ICU). Variables with p<0.2 in univariate regression analysis were included as independent variables in the multivariate logistic regression model using the Enter method. Statistical significance was set at p<0.05. A nomogram performed by R software was constructed to visually predict the probability of having sex with a male partner after drinking, integrating five key variables. For each variable, points are assigned based on the presence (‘Yes’) or absence (‘No’) or specific assignment (‘9 scores’, ‘5–8 scores’, ‘4 or below scores’) of the characteristic. These points are summed to get the total points, which then correlate to the predicted probability.

## Results

## General demographic characteristics
A total of 604 participants were recruited for this study, with an average age of (28.0±6.4) years, a minimum age of 19 years and a maximum age of 53 years. Individuals aged 19–25 years accounted for 40.2% (243/604) of the participants. Furthermore, 93.1% (562/604) of participants were unmarried or divorced, 38.6% (233/604) were rural residents, 58.6% (354/604) had an average monthly income of 6000 CNY or less and 22.5% (136/604) had a high school education or lower. Those reporting harmonious family relationships comprised 54.8% (331/604), and students comprised 20.5% (124/604) of the sample. presents the general demographic characteristics of the participants.
 

## Analysis of the associated factors
In this study, 400 participants (66.2%, 400/604) reported not adhering to condom use during homosexual activities. Variables with p<0.2 in the univariate analysis ( ) included marital status, family relationships, whether they believed that using recreational drugs like Rush Popper could increase the risk of HIV infection, whether they had accessed HIV prevention services in the past year, the role they assumed in homosexual activities, whether they currently had a regular homosexual partner, the primary venue/method for finding male sexual partners (internet/dating apps), whether they had engaged in drinking, drug use or use of performance-enhancing drugs during homosexual activities, whether they had experienced one-night stands with homosexual partners, whether they had sexual activity with opposite-sex partners in the past 6 months, self-efficacy in condom use measurement, whether they wanted to know about their homosexual partner’s HIV infection status, whether they were concerned about being infected with HIV through their homosexual partner, whether they believed condoms were effective in preventing HIV transmission among homosexual partners and whether they had undergone HIV testing in the past year.
 These variables were included in the multivariate logistic regression analysis. The results showed ( ) that participants who engaged in drinking, drug use or use of performance-enhancing drugs during homosexual activities had a 193% increased likelihood of ICU (adjusted OR (aOR) 2.93; 95% CI 1.64 to 5.47). Participants who had indulged in a one-night stand had a 43% decreased likelihood of ICU during homosexual activities (aOR 0.57; 95% CI 0.39 to 0.84). Participants with a self-efficacy measurement score of 9 had a 53% lower likelihood of ICU during homosexual activities (aOR 0.47; 95% CI 0.29 to 0.77). Participants who had undergone HIV testing in the past year had a 61% decreased likelihood of ICU during homosexual activities (aOR 0.39; 95% CI 0.21 to 0.68). ## Discussion
According to the results of this study, 66.23% of the participants who engaged in anal intercourse reported ICU, highlighting the significant risk of HIV and STIs among these participants. On certain social and dating platforms, the MSM community may prioritise immediate sexual gratification over long-term partner relationships. The scarcity of sexual education resources has led to inadequate awareness of the importance of condom use among MSM, directly impacting their risk perception and heightening their susceptibility to HIV infection. Furthermore, some MSM may experience social pressure during their interactions on these platforms, leading to the perception that not using condoms could yield greater recognition and acceptance within specific MSM circles, thereby facilitating unprotected sexual behaviours.
Alcohol consumption and drug use frequently result in a decline in judgement and self-control and significantly increase the risk of HIV infection. The findings of this study indicate that MSM who engage in activities such as drinking, drug use or using aphrodisiacs during homosexual encounters have a 193% increased likelihood of not consistently using condoms. Under the influence of alcohol or drugs, MSM are more susceptible to impulsive behaviour, disregarding the safe choices they would typically make while sober, such as condom usage. Furthermore, the use of these substances may heighten sexual desire and diminish awareness of potential risks, thereby increasing the likelihood of engaging in unsafe sexual practices. Additionally, aphrodisiac drugs can affect sexual decision-making. These substances may intensify physiological sexual desire, encouraging individuals to participate in more sexual encounters while simultaneously providing a sense of psychological relaxation and confidence, leading to reduced condom use during sexual activities. This mindset may promote unprotected sex and increase the risk of HIV and other STIs. Therefore, when developing health intervention strategies aimed at MSM, it is essential to consider how these behaviours influence risk decision-making and promote relevant education and support services within the MSM community, while advocating for reduced alcohol consumption and avoidance of drug use to decrease the incidence of unsafe sexual behaviour.
One-night stands typically involve sexual encounters with strangers or short-term partners. In these situations, individuals are more likely to emphasise the importance of using condoms during their initial sexual experience to mitigate the risk of HIV and other STIs. The nature of one-night stands encourages participants to take protective measures before establishing a foundation of trust or undergoing HIV testing following an encounter, thereby increasing awareness of safe sexual practices. The findings of this study suggest that MSM who engage in one-night stands are more likely to consistently use condoms during sexual encounters with men. The transient nature of one-night stands significantly restricts the individuals’ understanding of their partners, making them more inclined to adopt protective measures to reduce potential health risks. This behaviour may arise from general vigilance regarding STIs, particularly during one-night stands, where both partners lack knowledge of each other’s health status, positioning condom use as a self-protective strategy. However, it is worth noting that the differences in condom use between one-night stands and long-term partner relationships illustrate complex behavioural dynamics. While one-night stands seem to encourage condom use to some extent, individuals in long-term relationships may opt to decrease their level of protection because of mutual trust or familiarity with their partner’s health status, which can lead to the opposite outcome. Therefore, sexual education interventions aimed at MSM should concurrently address the characteristics of both one-night stands and long-term partner relationships to comprehensively enhance awareness of safe sexual behaviour and effectively reduce the risk of HIV and other STIs.
Consistent condom use can effectively prevent the transmission of STIs, and MSM who do not consistently use condoms face a higher risk of HIV or STIs. MSM with higher self-efficacy regarding condom use are more likely to believe that they can effectively use condoms, and this confidence can directly translate into actual behaviour. The results of this study revealed that MSM with a self-efficacy score of 9 for condom use are more likely to consistently use condoms during sexual encounters with other men, emphasising the importance of self-efficacy in HIV prevention. For some individuals, condom use may involve emotional and social factors; high self-efficacy can help them overcome the pressure that may arise in intimate relationships, as well as negative perceptions or confusion regarding condom use. Generally, individuals with strong self-efficacy are more proactive in seeking information related to sexual health, such as correct condom usage, risk of STIs and personal health choices. MSM with high self-efficacy scores are more likely to consistently use condoms during sexual encounters, and this finding provides valuable insights for improving HIV prevention strategies. Education and support can play a crucial role in promoting safe sexual behaviours by enhancing the self-efficacy of MSMs. Therefore, relevant health education initiatives should focus on building individuals’ confidence and capabilities to effectively reduce the risk of HIV transmission.
Undergoing HIV testing enhances individuals’ awareness of their health status, leading to more responsible and safer attitudes in sexual behaviour and decision-making. The results of this study indicate that MSM who have been tested for HIV in the past year are more likely to consistently use condoms during sexual encounters, emphasising the critical role of HIV testing in promoting safe sexual practices. HIV testing enables MSM to promptly understand their HIV status, reducing ignorance and misconceptions about infection risk. When individuals know that they are HIV negative, they typically feel safer and more confident, making them more inclined to adhere to condom use during sexual activity. HIV testing not only serves as a tool for individuals to ascertain their health status but also reinforces social norms regarding safe behaviours. As more individuals are tested and share their results, this behaviour can create a ripple effect within the community, encouraging others to take similar actions. In the MSM community, the widespread uptake of HIV testing fosters an environment of mutual concern and support, thereby promoting condom use. Furthermore, many individuals who undergo HIV testing may receive relevant health education and counselling services, equipping them with a deeper understanding of how to reduce the infection risk and appropriately use protective measures. To effectively reduce the risk of HIV transmission, public health policies should actively promote regular HIV testing and provide accessible testing services and related health education to the MSM community, thereby cultivating a supportive community environment that enhances individuals’ awareness of safe sexual practices.
This study had several limitations. First, it employed a cross-sectional design that relies on self-reported data from participants, which may have introduced information bias and inaccuracies. Respondents may selectively conceal their true circumstances because of social expectations, feelings of shame or concerns about privacy, leading to biased estimates of condom use. Furthermore, the sampling method used in this study depended solely on online recruitment, which may not represent the entire MSM population and lacks comprehensiveness, particularly among individuals who are less likely to use the internet, affecting the generalisability of the study findings. Additionally, a cross-sectional design cannot establish causal relationships; it can only demonstrate correlations between variables. For instance, although this study identified factors associated with ICU, it cannot definitively ascertain whether these factors cause non-use or whether individuals who do not use condoms are more likely to exhibit these factors. Future research should incorporate temporal factors, as longitudinal studies may more effectively clarify the dynamic changes in the sexual behaviour and decision-making regarding safe practices among MSM, as well as show how the influencing factors evolve over time.

## Conclusions
Participants who engaged in behaviours such as drinking, drug use or using aphrodisiacs during homosexual encounters were more likely to not use condoms consistently. However, those who participated in one-night stands, had a self-efficacy score of 9 for condom use and had undergone HIV testing in the past year were more likely to consistently use condoms. Consequently, it is necessary to enhance educational outreach targeting MSM; promote consistent condom use during sexual activities; increase the coverage of HIV testing and minimise behaviours such as drinking, drug use and use of aphrodisiacs during homosexual encounters. Additionally, efforts should focus on reducing the prevalence of casual sexual encounters (one-night stands) and strengthening sexual health education to lower the risk of infections, including HIV and other STIs.
