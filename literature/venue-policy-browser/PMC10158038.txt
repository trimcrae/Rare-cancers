## Abstract

## Objective
The aim of this study is to assess the trends in industry payments to radiologists and the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic, including trends in different categories of payments.

## Methods
The Open Payments Database from CMS was accessed and analyzed for the period from January 1, 2016, to December 31, 2021. Payments were grouped into six categories: consulting fees, education, gifts, research, speaker fees, and royalties or ownership. The total number, value, and types of industry payments to radiologists were subsequently determined and compared pre- and postpandemic from 2016 to 2021.

## Results
The total number of industry payments and the number of radiologists receiving these payments dropped by 50% and 32%, respectively, between 2019 and 2020, with only partial recovery in 2021. However, the mean payment value and total payment value increased by 177% and 37%, respectively, between 2019 and 2020. Gifts and speaker fees experienced the largest decreases between 2019 and 2020 (54% and 63%, respectively). Research and education grants were also disrupted, with the number of payments decreasing by 37% and 36% and payment value decreasing by 37% and 25%, respectively. However, royalty or ownership increased during the first year of the pandemic (8% for number of payments and 345% for value of payments).

## Conclusions
There was significant decline in overall industry payments coinciding with the COVID-19 pandemic, with biggest declines in gifts and speaker fees. The impact on the different categories of payments and recovery in the last 2 years has been heterogeneous.

Key Words: COVID-19, industry, open payments, pandemic, radiology

## Introduction
Physician-industry relationships are ubiquitous and simultaneously drive innovation and technological progress, while raising concerns about potential conflicts of interests. To increase the transparency of these relationships, Congress passed the Physician Payments Sunshine Act in 2010 as part of the Affordable Care Act, which required drug, device, and medical supply manufacturers to report payments over $10 made to health care providers [ ]. Data are tracked by CMS and published annually in the Open Payments database. Past studies have examined the frequency and magnitude of these financial interactions between radiologists and industry [ , , ]. However, the effect of the COVID-19 pandemic on these relationships is not well understood. The aim of this study is to assess the trends in industry payments to radiologists and the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic, including trends in different categories of payments.

## Methods
Data from the CMS Open Payments Database were analyzed from January 1, 2016, to December 31, 2021. Physicians were included in the study if they had “Allopathic & Osteopathic Physicians/Radiology” in their specialty code, which included all radiology subspecialties such as vascular and interventional radiology, diagnostic radiology, and radiology oncology.
Payments were grouped into six categories: consulting fees, education (education and compensation for services related to a continuing medical education program), gifts (food and beverages, gifts, entertainment, and charity contributions), research (grants and research payments from the Open Payments Database research payments data), speaker fees (travel and lodging, honoraria, and compensation for services as a speaker at a venue), and royalties or ownership (royalty, license, or current or prospective ownership or investment). Payments relating to debt forgiveness and medical supplies were only available starting with 2021 and were grouped together into one category [ ].
Physician demographics including gender and primary location of practice were collected from the CMS National Plan and Provider Enumeration System National Provider Identifier database.
The total number, value, and types of industry payments to radiologists were subsequently determined and qualitatively compared pre- and postpandemic from 2016 to 2021. In addition, a multivariable regression model was constructed to examine the association between physician characteristics and total aggregated industry payment. Physician characteristics evaluated in this model included gender, region of practice (Midwest, Northeast, South, or West), and radiological subspecialty. Statistical analysis was performed using R version 3.6.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Institutional review board approval was not required as only publicly available data were used in the analysis and no patients were included.

## Results
From 2016 to 2021, a total of 30,695 radiologists received industry payments. More than three-quarters of these recipients were men and almost 70% were diagnostic radiologists ( 
). Although constituting more than 20% of the radiologists receiving payments, in terms of value, women received around 10% of industry payments in each year from 2016 to 2021 with no significant change over time (e-only ). The median payment value in radiology was $28 (interquartile range: 16-135) and the corresponding median payment value overall for all physicians was $16 (interquartile range: 12-24)( ).
 The COVID-19 pandemic coincided with a sharp decrease in industry payments to radiologists in March 2020 that lingered until 2021 ( 
). In the 4 years before the pandemic, the total number of payments increased from 107,309 to 117,362 between 2016 and 2019. However, this dropped by 50% to 58,274 from 2019 to 2020 and only partially rebounded to 78,292 in 2021. For all physicians, the total number of payments fell by 45%, and the total value of payments decreased by 32%. The total number of radiologists receiving industry payments increased from 14,787 to 15,170 between 2016 and 2019. However, this fell by 32% to 10,323 from 2019 to 2020 and only reached 11,949 in 2021. Interestingly, although the total number of payments and number of radiologists receiving payments decreased during the pandemic, the value of payments increased, with the mean payment value and total payment value increasing by 177% and 37%, respectively, since 2019 and peaking at $1,607 and $93,640,669, respectively, in 2020 ( ).
 These patterns largely held across the different radiological subspecialties ( 
). The decrease in the total number of payments was observed among all subspecialties (e-only ) but was more pronounced in radiation oncology (decrease of 15,291 payments or 58% between 2019 and 2020) and diagnostic radiology (decrease of 34,143 payments or 53%) than vascular and interventional radiology (decrease of 9,654 payments or 36%). The increase in total payment value was observed in both diagnostic radiology (increase of $26,239,841 or 58% between 2019 and 2020) and vascular and interventional radiology (increase of $4,408,268 or 34%), but the opposite was observed in radiation oncology (decrease of $5,246,894 or 55%).

## Categories of Payments
There were observed differences in the type of industry payments made to radiologists before and during the pandemic ( 
). The onset of the pandemic decreased the total number of payments, but this varied by payment type ( 
). Gifts and speaker fees experienced the largest absolute drop in numbers, decreasing by 47,818 and 11,723 payments, respectively, between 2019 and 2020 (or 54% and 63%). This corresponded with a decrease in total payment value of 61% and 60%, respectively ( 
). Research and education grants were also disrupted, with the number of payments decreasing by 579 and 337 (or 37% and 36%) and payment value decreasing by 37% and 25%, respectively. Interestingly, number of payments for consulting fees and royalty or ownership increased during the first year of the pandemic, rising by 1,356 and 13 payments, respectively (or 19% and 8%). The value of payments for consulting fees and royalty or ownership also increased, rising 3% and 345%, respectively. Similar trends were seen for all physicians overall, in which the value of royalty or ownership payments increased from 25% in 2019 to 39% of all payments in 2020, with a corresponding drop in speaker fees.
 From 2020 to 2021, gifts experienced the largest percent increase in number of payments and total value of payments ( 
), rising by 18,399 payments (45% increase) and $1,017,667 in value (62% increase). During the same period, the number of royalty or ownership payments increased from 186 to 237 (27% increase) but the total payment value decreased from $56,347,942 to $21,138,070 (63% decrease). The number and total value of research payments decreased by 159 (16%) and $370,403 (6%), respectively. Although the number of physicians receiving research payments decreased from 270 in 2020 to 151 in 2021 ( 
), the median total payment value increased from $3,655 in 2020 to $8,295 in 2021, a 127% increase (e-only ). During the same time, the number and total value of payments related to consulting fees increased from 8,349 to 9,490 (14%) and from $20,415,877 to $25,787,743 (26%), respectively.
 

## Association Between Physician Characteristics and Aggregated Industry Payment Value
After adjusting for other physician characteristics, male radiologists received a significantly larger value of industry payments than female radiologists (+$3,002 [95% confidence interval {CI}: 953-5,050]; P = .004) ( 
). Furthermore, radiologists in the Northeast received a larger value of industry payments than the radiologists in the Midwest (+$3,575 [95% CI: 1,105-6,045]; P = .005) and South (+$5,224 [95% CI: 2,999-7,449]; P < .001). The trends in regional variation in industry payments to radiologists over time are presented in e-only . In addition, radiation oncologists received a lower value of industry payments than their diagnostic radiologist (−$2,548 [95% CI: −4,559 to −537]; P = 0.04) and interventional radiologist colleagues (−$2,532 [95% CI: −4,937 to −128]; P = 0.04). Furthermore, when comparing aggregated annual payments in prepandemic years (2016-2019) with the pandemic years (2020-2021), industry payments to radiologists were higher in the pandemic years (+$2,450 [95% CI: 660-4,239]; P = .007). The mean annual aggregated payment per physician increased for diagnostic radiology during pandemic years (2020-2021), but it declined for radiation oncologists ( 
).
 

## Discussion
The COVID-19 pandemic significantly impacted all facets of radiology practice nationwide in the United States [ ]. This study revealed that the number of industry payments to radiologists decreased substantially during the first year of the pandemic and only partially rebounded in 2021. This pattern held across all subspecialties and was largely driven by the decreases in gifts (eg, food, entertainment) and speaker fees that were cut as in-person events were canceled. These findings provide an important update to the literature and reverse the trends outlined in past studies, which identified an increase in industry payments over time [ , ]. Although the number of payments and radiologists receiving payments decreased, the mean and total value of payments increased. This may be attributed again to the changing composition of payments, in that the number of consulting fees and royalty or ownership payments increased. Past studies have found the average value of consulting and royalty payments to be substantially higher than gift and speaker payments [ ]. Therefore, the increase in these payment types (as radiologists were paid to share their expertise during the public health crisis) may explain the overall growth in payment value.
More broadly, this study revealed that the COVID-19 pandemic significantly altered the number, value, and types of industry payments to radiologists. These changes persisted a year into the pandemic, suggesting possible lasting effects on physician-industry relationships. These findings have broad significance. As stated in an earlier study, if high-value industry payments largely reflect gratuitous interactions with industry (eg, gifts), this would raise concerns about quid pro quo relationships and conflicts of interests that lead to negative outcomes for health care spending and resource utilization [ ]. By contrast, if the transfers predominantly reflect innovation or market-rate payments for strategic consulting, this would raise less concern and could arguably drive advancements in imaging technologies and patient care [ ]. However, research payments also decreased between 2019 and 2020 by approximately 37%, both for number and total value of payments, with further decrease between 2020 and 2021 (16% and 6%, respectively). From 2020 to 2021, the number of physicians who received payments decreased by 44% but the median value of total research payments to each physician increased 127%, suggesting a greater concentration of industry research funding to a smaller number of physicians. Education grants showed similar decrease between 2019 and 2020, with only slight increase between 2020 and 2021.
Gifts and speaker fees experienced large declines between 2019 and 2020 (54% and 63%, respectively). However, gifts experienced a significant rebound between 2020 and 2021 (45% increase in number and 62% increase in value of payments). Speaker fees showed very little change between 2020 and 2021 (7% increase in number but 2% decrease in value of payments). It remains to be seen whether these patterns will persist.
There is significant gender disparity in industry payments with female radiologists receiving around 10% of industry payments by value, and this trend has not changed significantly in 6 years. This disparity was previously demonstrated in female interventional radiology physicians, who received 1% of total payments in 2018 despite constituting 13% of physicians [ ].
This study has limitations. The findings rely on accurate reporting from industry, which may underestimate financial relationships in situations of noncompliance. Additionally, payments to academic institutions or organizations (rather than individual physicians) are not captured in this study, which excludes a potential source of industry influence. Strengths of this study include its novel longitudinal analysis, which highlights important changes during the COVID-19 pandemic.

## Take-Home Points

▪There was significant increase in industry payment across all categories from 2016 to 2019.
▪A big drop was seen in the number of industry payments in 2020, most significantly in speaker fees and gifts.
▪The recovery in the number and value of industry payments since 2020 has been inconsistent across different categories.
