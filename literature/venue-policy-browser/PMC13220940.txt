## Abstract 
Beginning in 2014, the American Thoracic Society (ATS) launched the Building Education to Advance Research (BEAR) Cage competition, with the first in-person competition held in 2015. Organized and run by the ATS Drug/Device Discovery and Development Committee, the BEAR Cage competition is structured as a “ pitch competition” where early career investigators submit streamlined proposals for out-of-the-box and potentially transformative ideas, and the 3 top proposals are invited to attend the ATS International Conference to pitch their ideas and compete for cash prizes. Over the first 10 years of its existence, the BEAR Cage has not only grown as a venue for some of the most exciting and creative ideas at the ATS conference, it has also shown itself to be associated with future success, with BEAR Cage finalists emerging as leaders across the pulmonary, critical care, and sleep medicine enterprise. In this Perspective, we provide an overview of the ATS BEAR Cage’s first 10 years and offer thoughts on the continued importance of the BEAR Cage as a place for innovation, competition, and fun to coexist.

## Background
The American Thoracic Society (ATS) has several committees that are tasked with overseeing high-priority, cross-cutting topic areas of interest to ATS members. One of those committees, the Drug/Device Discovery and Development (DDDD) Committee (see https://www.thoracic.org/members/committees/committeeDetail.php?id=a0I40000001BLJnEAO for details), “provides a forum within the Society that focuses on identifying and instilling principles of translational and clinical science specific to the discovery and development of new drugs and devices relevant to the American Thoracic Society mission” and “ takes a multifaceted and collaborative approach to supporting the development of innovative therapies for those with respiratory diseases, critical illness and sleep disorders.” The DDDD Committee brings together diverse perspectives from research, clinical medicine, academia, industry/private sector, government/policy, and other sectors to advance the translation of the most exciting and novel ideas into drugs and devices poised to help people. Current membership of the DDDD Committee can be found on the ATS website (https://www.thoracic.org/members/committees/committeeDetail.php?%20id=a0I40000001BLJnEAO).
Beginning in 2014, the DDDD Committee conceived of and created a pitch competition-style event, the Building Education to Advance Research (BEAR) Cage competition (see landing page at: https://site.thoracic.org/conference/program/early-career-professionals/bear-cage), to serve the committee’s charge and goals in a fun and less conventional way. Early career ATS members were invited to submit short proposals for out-of-the-box ideas in the pulmonary, critical care, and sleep medicine space. The proposals needed to relate to drugs or devices relevant to the pulmonary, critical care, and sleep medicine space, to be truly innovative, to include a plan for how they might develop the idea, and to provide a vision for how the idea might be a game-changer if completely successful. The DDDD Committee members evaluated the eligible submitted proposals on these criteria and selected 3 finalists who were invited to compete in a live competition event at the ATS International Conference. Each finalist made a short pitch for their proposed work, and then answered questions from a panel of appointed judges as well as an open audience of ATS attendees. The judges deliberated and chose the winner and runners-up on site, with cash prizes awarded to each finalist.
The BEAR Cage competition has been held annually during the ATS International Conference each year since 2015. The prize for the winner has increased from $5000 to $10 000 at present, with each runner-up receiving $2500. Fundamentally, the core elements have stayed the same—early career ATS members bringing their most exciting ideas to compete for cash prizes in a format that encourages creativity, rigor, and a spirit of fun. A few aspects of these core elements warrant specific comment. The first is that the cash prizes are given directly to the finalists to reward their ingenuity and creativity. These are not grants, they are prizes; the winners can use their winnings however they see fit. The second point to note is that excitement, innovation, and possibility carry the day. BEAR Cage reviewers, judges, and finalists are aware that there are several options for traditional funding and that a distinct focus of the BEAR Cage is to encourage creative approaches that could improve the health of our patients in the near term. Finally, the BEAR Cage offers 2 important differences to the usual scientific ideation process: (1) the BEAR Cage can be a great forum for ideas that do not fit with an investigator’s “ main” research program, and (2) successful entries must address practical questions of how to make their ideas actionable, in keeping with the original evaluation criteria noted above.

## A decade of BEAR Cage finalists
The BEAR Cage presentations over the past decade ( ) exemplify both the strength and diversity of the competition, showcasing cutting-edge solutions, inventions, and approaches that shape pulmonary medicine and respiratory health. Many proposals introduce novel approaches to diagnosis and treatment of complex pulmonary conditions, ranging from precision-targeted therapies to groundbreaking imaging techniques. The breadth of topics spans from early disease detection to interventions addressing chronic and acute respiratory conditions, emphasizing a commitment to both scientific advancement and real-world clinical impact. A general publicly available large language model (ChatGPT 5.0 free version, August 18, 2025) was given the prompt “ Please extract the main themes represented in the following project titles:” followed by a direct cut-and-paste of the project titles from . The main themes extracted and summarized were clustered “ around respiratory diseases (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, acute respiratory distress syndrome, interstitial lung disease, tuberculosis, pulmonary arterial hypertension, aspiration) with major emphases on diagnostics and imaging, novel therapeutics and devices, precision/personalized medicine, infection and immune responses, and mobile/community health interventions,” suggesting that BEAR Cage finalists have been consistently advancing not only the goals of the competition and the DDDD Committee but also the broader goals of cutting-edge research and development advancing pulmonary, critical care, and sleep science and medicine that are of utmost important to the ATS.
 

## Where are they now?
The BEAR Cage program has served as a springboard for numerous researchers and scientist-leaders, with participants collectively attracting over $108 million in research funding, as documented in the National Institutes of Health (NIH) RePORTER database. Among those who participated more than 5 years ago, two-thirds have secured over $5 million in funding, and 25% have surpassed $15 million in research grants ( ). Similarly, the total funding-years, a measure of both funding success and funding consistency, has exhibited an exponentially modeled increase as a function of years since BEAR Cage participation ( ). These trajectories suggest that participation in the BEAR Cage may serve as a pivotal launching point, providing participants with both recognition and motivation to pursue impactful advancements in respiratory health. The trajectory of funding acquisition among finalists follows an impressive upward trend, mirroring the rapid rise in the number of indexed publications ( ). Additionally, illustrates the diverse and successful career paths of past finalists and winners, with many going on to roles in academia and government, to establish startups, and to continue to drive groundbreaking advancements in the field. Though, of course, causality cannot be inferred, these correlative relationships do suggest that the ATS has been able to identify and support future leaders in pulmonary, critical care, and sleep medicine and research for an investment of less than $150 000 over a decade.
 From its inception, the BEAR Cage had the support of early career ATS members who embraced its themes of novelty and free-spirited ideation. Multiple BEAR Cage finalists have commented that their experience competing in the BEAR Cage solidified their interest in pursuing a career in research, encouraged them to think big and bold and to push the limits of the possible, promoted a spirit of cross-functional collaboration, and ultimately led them to additional funding opportunities and thriving research careers. Testimonials from former BEAR Cage finalists underscore the impact participating in the competition has had on their career journey:
“ The BEAR Cage award was pivotal in launching my career. When I was a fellow and not sure if I would continue down this path, the BEAR Cage program showed me it was possible and super rewarding. Now, I’m a tenure track professor with a lab funded by multiple R01s and companies, and we spin out our own companies. All that is thanks to BEAR Cage!” —Dr. Jake Brenner, University of Pennsylvania (BEAR Cage 2016)
“ Participating in the BEAR Cage provided me the exposure to a diverse group of inventors, researchers, and clinicians for constructive feedback. It forced me to consider potential obstacles, alternative solutions, and further innovation surrounding a clinical problem that affects many of the patients I want to care for. Despite the ‘shark tank’ setting, I found it to be much more exciting than intimidating—and a fun way of discussing our project.” —Dr. Marco Almeda, Riley Hospital for Children and Indiana University Associated Hospitals, BEAR Cage 2023

## Future directions
In summary, we celebrate the remarkable success that the BEAR Cage competition has brought to its participants. With finalists collectively securing over $108 million in research funding, publishing extensively in high-impact journals, and moving into key leadership positions (eg, members of pulmonary and critical care medicine division leadership teams, members of ATS leadership, members of NIH institute/center leadership teams) across multiple sectors of the biomedical research and medical enterprise, the competition has proven to be a powerful catalyst for professional growth. BEAR Cage fosters a culture of bold thinking rewarded with early success, encouraging participants to pursue their most ambitious, even unconventional, ideas while embracing interdisciplinary collaboration to drive meaningful progress. As we look to the future, we hope this competition continues to inspire the next generation of innovators to push the boundaries of discovery, translate novel concepts into real-world solutions, and ultimately improve outcomes for clinicians and patients alike. The kind of bold thinking, creative approaches to design and funding, interdisciplinary team building (typically required to conceive a winning proposal), and practical solutioning the BEAR Cage seeks to support is more important in 2025 than ever before and is vital to ensuring a healthy and vibrant pulmonary, critical care, and sleep research enterprise now and in the future.
