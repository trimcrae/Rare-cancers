## Abstract

## Objective:
This study aimed to analyze research trends, cooperative networks, and emerging topics in Digital Management in University Sport (DMUS) literature published between 2001 and 2024. Unlike previous descriptive bibliometric studies, this research explicitly addresses critical knowledge gaps by posing 2 guiding questions: who are the leading contributors to DMUS research at the author, institutional, and national levels, and how are international collaborations structured? how has DMUS research evolved thematically, and what emerging hotspots and frontiers can be identified? By answering these questions, the study seeks to provide both theoretical and practical contributions to the field.

## Methods:
A bibliometric analysis was conducted using data from the Web of Science Core Collection, SCOPUS, and EBSCOhost databases, encompassing 1161 articles. A 2-step screening process was applied to ensure relevance and rigor, and publications were analyzed across multiple languages (English, Spanish, Chinese, Portuguese, German, and Russian). VOSviewer (v1.6.20) and CiteSpace (v6.3.R1) were used with documented parameter settings to ensure methodological transparency and reproducibility.

## Results:
Publication Trends: Annual publications have consistently increased, categorized into 3 phases: initial (2001–2013), early development (2014–2018), and rapid growth (2019–2024). Cooperation Networks: Countries/Regions: The United States leads in publication volume, with strong collaborations observed among major publishing nations; Institutions: La Trobe University is the most prolific, followed by institutions in Australia, the UK, and South Korea; Authors: The top 5 authors contributed 17.73% of total publications. Co-occurrence Networks: Key research areas include early studies on reliability and social media, digital tools for health management, and responses to global challenges like COVID-19. Recent trends (2022–2024) emphasize quality of life improvements through digitalized sports programs.

## Conclusion:
This study highlights the evolution and growing importance of DMUS research. From a theoretical perspective, it conceptualizes the trajectory of DMUS from exploratory adoption studies to evaluative and policy-oriented research. From a practical perspective, it provides evidence-based guidance for policymakers and university administrators to strengthen digital infrastructure, foster international collaboration, and implement digitalized sports programs that enhance student health and well-being. Future research should address underrepresented regions and employ longitudinal approaches to evaluate the sustained effects of digital sports management strategies.

Keywords: bibliometric analysis, digital management, university sport

## 1. Introduction
In recent years, the rapid advancement of information and communication technologies has ushered in what is commonly referred to as the digital age or Industry 4.0. These technological innovations are profoundly transforming various aspects of daily life, alongside reshaping the dynamics of social and economic interactions. Scholars have frequently described this phenomenon as the Fourth Industrial Revolution,[ ] characterized by the pivotal influence of digitalization and emerging technologies, a change boosted by the critical role of digitalization and new technologies. Furthermore, digital progress holds significant relevance to the sustainable development goals outlined in the United Nations 2030 Agenda. To mitigate the adverse impacts of digital exclusion, countries, institutions, and organizations are urged to take active measures to bridge the digital divide.[ ]
There are a lot of researches about the digital management on higher education especially in sports field. The 5g digital technology integration of wireless sensor networks with pyroelectric infrared sensors have been identified as a key innovation in the realm of sports training and management. This technology not only enhances the detection and positioning of athletes during training but also provides a robust framework for data collection and analysis, which is crucial for the digital management of sports in universities.[ ] In the realm of collegiate sports management, the integration of digital technologies, particularly wearable devices, has been transformative. The application of wearable technology in monitoring training tolerances and athlete performance, provides valuable insights into the digital management of sports at the collegiate level. It is also instrumental in advancing the discourse on digital management strategies in university sports, emphasizing the role of big data and wearables in enhancing athletic performance and health.[ ] In the exploration of digital management within university sports, a pivotal study highlights the transformative role of the Internet in shaping college physical education. The research underscores how digital platforms can significantly enhance students’ engagement and autonomous learning in physical activities, and provides a substantive basis for the growing importance of digital management strategies in enhancing the quality and reach of sports education within higher education institutions.[ ] In college sports management, the digital transformation has introduced innovative approaches to physical education delivery, as evidenced by a recent study evaluating the effectiveness of a computer-based physical education platform during the COVID-19 pandemic. These such platforms are quite effective, with high percentages of students reporting improved focus and understanding of learning materials, underscoring the potential of digital tools in shaping the future of sports education management.[ ] The potential of AI-driven technologies to revolutionize the management and evaluation processes within higher education sports, aligning with the evolving landscape of digital management strategies, the system can provide a more comprehensive and objective basis for evaluating student and teacher performance, thereby enhancing the overall effectiveness of sports education management.[ ] The role of multimedia technology in facilitating interactive learning and providing a rich array of educational resources, which are pivotal for the digital transformation of sports education in higher education institutions. In addition, the integration of network multimedia platforms can lead to improved student engagement and outcomes in physical education, aligning with the objectives of digital management in university sports.[ ] By presenting a comprehensive approach to enhancing physical fitness and mental well-being among college students by leveraging deep learning technology and artificial intelligence, it highlights the importance of an intelligent sports management system in improving the efficiency of sports venue management and the overall quality of sports activities for college students, which is a critical aspect of digital management in higher education sports. Meanwhile, this contributes valuable insights into the development of digital strategies that can potentially transform sports education and mental health support in universities.[ ] With enhancing DMUSs, a pivotal study presents a comprehensive approach to integrating mobile technology into physical education. It delves into the design of a sports curriculum app that aligns with contemporary university needs, offering a new paradigm for organizing and managing sports activities among college students. As well as it reveals the potential of mobile applications to revolutionize traditional sports education by providing a platform that facilitates independent student exercise under scientific and systematic guidance, thereby enhancing the overall effectiveness and engagement in physical education within higher education institutions.[ ]
However, there has yet to be a systematic review of Digital Management on university sport (DMUS). Consequently, undertaking a thorough and systematic evaluation of the existing research landscape in the field of DMUS is essential.
By conducting quantitative analyses of existing literature, it becomes possible to ascertain the current state of research and forecast future directions within a given field.[ ] Utilizing mathematical models alongside network algorithms facilitates the identification of influential authors, institutions, and journals while also spotlighting emerging research topics in specific areas.[ ] Furthermore, bibliometric tools equipped with visualization capabilities enable the exploration of collaborative networks and thematic clusters within particular domains.[ ] Consequently, digital management analysis has found extensive application in conducting in-depth studies and discerning developmental patterns in various fields, including sports medicine[ ] and mental health.[ ]
Yet, existing studies remain largely descriptive and fragmented, often limited to publication counts or isolated case analyses, and fail to provide a systematic overview of DMUS. This leaves critical knowledge gaps, such as the lack of longitudinal mapping of research evolution, insufficient attention to cross-cultural adoption and collaboration patterns, and limited guidance for policy and governance in university sports digitalization. This study addresses these gaps by applying a temporal–thematic bibliometric framework to uncover both structural (authors, institutions, countries) and thematic (keyword bursts, research frontiers) dimensions of DMUS. Theoretically, it contributes by conceptualizing the trajectory of DMUS as a distinct research domain, moving from exploratory adoption studies to evidence-based and policy-oriented scholarship. Practically, it provides insights for policymakers, administrators, and educators on strengthening digital infrastructure, fostering collaboration, and implementing evidence-based strategies for digital management in higher education sports.
To accomplish this objective, 1161 articles were sourced from the Web of Science (WOS), SCOPUS, and EBSCOhost databases and analyzed bibliometrically to offer a comprehensive and dynamic synthesis of DMUS-related research spanning the early 21st century (2001–2024). The second section of this paper elaborates on the data sources and methodological framework utilized in the study. The third section presents a multi-faceted analysis of results, covering publication trends, geographical and institutional contributions, author productivity, citation patterns, keyword clustering, and emergent terms. The fourth section discusses the findings, highlighting both future research prospects and limitations in the field. Finally, the conclusion synthesizes the key insights of the study. Accordingly, this research addresses 2 guiding questions: who are the leading contributors to DMUS research at the author, national, and institutional levels, and how are international collaborative networks structured? how has DMUS research evolved thematically, and what are the emerging hotspots and frontiers? These questions ensure that the study not only describes publication patterns but also identifies research discontinuities, thematic shifts, and practical implications for the digital transformation of university sports.
 

## 2. Materials and methods

## 2.1. Data collection
This study employed a meta-analysis to systematically examine the relevant literatures. Given their status as comprehensive and scientifically robust resources containing highly influential journals,[ ] the WOS, Scopus, and EBSCOhost databases were utilized for the analysis. Initially, the WOS Core Collection was chosen, encompassing the Science Citation Index Expanded (SCI-EXPANDED), Social Sciences Citation Index, Arts & Humanities Citation Index (A&HCI), and Emerging Sources Citation Index. Then it offers comprehensive and extensive datasets spanning a long period, making it an excellent resource for scientific research. Its sophisticated search functionalities enable precise retrieval of necessary data. Furthermore, the global recognition and widespread use of WoS, SCOPUS and EBSCOhost as a reputable indexing service lend credibility and representativeness to research findings, ensuring that outcomes are both authoritative and persuasive.[ – ] The search terms for this study included “University sport” and “Digital management,” with the timeframe restricted to January 1, 2001, to November 25, 2024. The initial search yielded 1250 papers. Recognizing that while English is the dominant academic language, contributions in other languages are also significant, publications in English, Spanish, Chinese, Portuguese, German, and Russian were included. Only articles and review papers were retained. A 2-step screening process was applied: (1) title/abstract screening to ensure relevance to DMUS, and (2) full-text examination to exclude studies outside the higher education sports context. After screening, 1161 articles were finalized for analysis (Supplemental File 1–5, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q980).

## 2.2. Research methodology
To conduct quantitative analysis and visually represent the acquired papers, over 30 freely available tools have been created for bibliometric mapping.[ ] Among these, VOSviewer (www.vosviewer.com), created by Waltman et al in 2020, is one of the most widely utilized.[ , ] This software generates maps derived from network data and enables their visualization. It includes 5 primary modules: co-occurrence, coauthor, bibliographic citation, co-citation, and coupling.[ ] Additionally, this research employed the visualization software CiteSpace[ , ] for analyzing DMUS-related literature. Accordingly, CiteSpace 6.3.R1 (64-bit) and VOSviewer version 1.6.20 were utilized for the visual analyses (Figs. and ).
 For reproducibility, the following parameter settings were applied:
VOSviewer (v1.6.20): fractional counting method; minimum occurrence threshold of 5 for co-authorship and keyword co-occurrence; resolution parameter = 1.0; clustering algorithm = LinLog/modularity.
CiteSpace (v6.3.R1, 64-bit): time slicing from 2001–2024 (1-year per slice); node selection by g-index (k = 25); pruning via Pathfinder and Minimum Spanning Tree; betweenness centrality calculation for node importance; burst detection for emerging keywords and references.
The combined use of VOSviewer and CiteSpace enabled both macro-level (publication trends, collaboration networks) and micro-level (topic evolution, citation bursts) analysis, ensuring methodological transparency and analytical rigor.

## 3. Results and discussion

## 3.1. Trends of publication
Trends of publication serve as quantitative measures of academic activity and reflect the level of scholarly interest in a specific area.[ ] Figure illustrates the annual publication patterns related to DMUS, which reveal a consistent upward trajectory over the study period. This progression can be divided into 3 distinct phases. During the start-up phase (2001–2013), the DMUS field received limited attention from the international academic community, as annual publications remained below ten, resulting in a cumulative total of 44 papers. In the early development stage (2014–2018), research activity increased gradually, with annual publications surpassing ten and reaching a cumulative total of 113 papers. The rapid development period (2019–2024) marked a significant acceleration, with annual publications exceeding 40 and showing an almost linear increase. Over this period, a total of 1004 papers were published, signifying that research on DMUS has entered a phase of dynamic growth and has started to make influential contributions to the field.

## 3.6.3. Keyword emergence analysis
Keyword emergence refers to a rapid increase in the frequency of a term over a short timeframe, which could reveal research hotspots and highlight recent advances in DMUS. In this research, keywords were analyzed to uncover significant themes in research on DMUS. Bibliometric keyword analysis plays a crucial role in identifying disciplinary directions and supporting the development of scientific strategies. The evolution of key topics and areas was examined using the authors’ keywords. To explore the focal points of DMUS research, an emergence analysis of keywords was conducted for studies published between 2014 and 2023. By setting the γ value to 0.7, 20 emergent keywords were identified (Fig. ). The research hotspots in 2014 to 2019 concentrated on the research areas for DMUS, the keywords are adolescents, life, reliability, social media, knowledge, and guidelines. The keywords appeared for 4 years. The 2020 to 2022 hotspots of research concentrated on an emphasis on the impact of digital tools and strategies on addressing mental health challenges like depression and disorders, particularly in the context of fitness and well-being during the COVID-19 pandemic. Efficacy and validation highlight the focus on evaluating the effectiveness of online interventions and digital marketing approaches in promoting health and fitness. The moderating role keyword suggests a deeper exploration of factors influencing the outcomes of these digital interventions (keywords: disorders, online, efficacy, moderating role, digital marketing, depression, validation, covid-19 pandemic and fitness). From 2022 to 2024, the keywords were quality, program, quality in life, and people, and the research focused on evaluating the effectiveness of physical education programs in enhancing participants’ overall quality of life. The focus on people highlights the human-centered approach, aiming to understand how digitalized management and program design impact individuals’ well-being. Additionally, the term quality reflects efforts to improve the standards and outcomes of these programs through innovative and digital solutions.
 

## 4. Discussion

## 4.1. Prospects
This study provides a synthesized overview of DMUS research trends by linking quantitative bibliometric findings with qualitative thematic interpretation, highlighting both the historical evolution and emerging priorities in the field. The steady increase in publication volume, along with the identified thematic clusters, suggests that DMUS research is entering a stage of methodological diversification and application expansion. The continued focus on digital tools for health management, especially in the context of physical activity, mental health, and the COVID-19 pandemic, suggests that future research will likely delve deeper into the integration of advanced technologies such as mobile health applications, virtual reality, and AI-driven health assessments. Additionally, the increasing emphasis on user acceptance and the impact of digital interventions on mental well-being will be critical in shaping how digital solutions are adopted in university sports programs.
Furthermore, the transition toward evaluating the effectiveness of digitalized sports programs on life quality and well-being indicates a shift toward more holistic, human-centered approaches. Future research will likely explore new ways to personalize digital interventions, enhance the usability of digital platforms, and measure their long-term impact on student-athletes’ performance and mental health. As digital transformation continues to redefine the landscape of university sports management, interdisciplinary collaboration between technology, health, and education sectors will be essential in driving innovation and addressing the diverse challenges faced by modern sports programs.
Future studies should employ longitudinal designs to assess the sustained effects of digital sports interventions, explore cross-cultural adoption models, and integrate digital management strategies into institutional sports policies. Such directions can bridge current implementation gaps and promote equitable access to digital resources in higher education sports. Interdisciplinary collaboration between technology, health, and education sectors will be essential in driving innovation.[ – ]

## 4.2. Limitations
This study is limited by its reliance on the WOS Core Collection, Scopus and EBSCOhost, which may not encompass all relevant articles in the field, potentially excluding studies from other databases or those published in non-English, non-Spanish, non-Chinese, non-Portuguese, non-German and non-Russian languages. Additionally, the dataset is restricted to publications between 2001–2024, which may omit early foundational studies and post-2024 emerging trends. The use of citation-based metrics for evaluating research impact may also favor highly cited works, potentially overlooking valuable but less frequently referenced studies. Lastly, the study’s scope does not address in-depth qualitative aspects of the research in DMUS.

## 4.3. Theoretical and Practical Implications
These conclusions are consistent with recent systematic reviews and empirical studies on digital transformation in university sports, which highlight technology’s dual role in enhancing performance and driving institutional change. The findings also provide actionable guidance for policymakers and administrators, emphasizing evidence-based strategies to enhance digital adoption, user engagement, and long-term program sustainability.

## 5. Conclusion
This study utilizes data from the WOS Core Collection, SCOPUS, and EBSCOhost to conduct a visualization and analysis of 1161 articles on DMUS published between 2001 and 2024, employing the tools CiteSpace and VOSviewer. The investigation reveals trends in publication volume and the features of cooperative networks across 4 dimensions: publication trends, research institutions, geographic regions, and individual authors. Additionally, this study examines the co-occurrence network through 3 lenses: citation analysis, keyword emergence, keyword clustering, to comprehensively assess the trends of development, hotspots of research, and the prospective domain.
The primary findings are summarized as follows:
Publication Trends: The annual number of DMUS-related publications has shown consistent growth, with distinct phases of initial (2001–2013), early development (2014–2018), and rapid growth (2019–2024).
Cooperation Networks: The United States, England, Australia, and China lead in publication volume, with La Trobe University and other institutions playing significant roles. Author collaboration remains relatively concentrated among prolific contributors.
Co-occurrence Networks: Five thematic areas emerged, including reliability and social media, digital tools and health management, health and global challenges, COVID-19 adaptation, and digital acceptance. From 2022–2024, keywords such as “quality,” “program,” and “quality in life” reflect a shift toward evaluating the effectiveness of digitalized university sports programs in improving well-being.
From a theoretical perspective, this research bridges a gap by systematically mapping DMUS scholarship over 2 decades, revealing a transition from descriptive adoption studies to evaluative and policy-oriented analyses. From a practical standpoint, the findings provide actionable insights for university administrators and policymakers to foster international collaboration, invest in digital infrastructure, and implement digitalized sports programs that enhance student health and well-being. Future studies should address the current underrepresentation of developing countries and the scarcity of longitudinal evaluation studies.
