## ABSTRACT

## Background:
Tobacco use is a leading cause of preventable diseases, including oral cancer and periodontal disease. Dental settings offer a unique opportunity for tobacco cessation interventions due to the frequent patient–dentist interactions.

## Materials and Methods:
A randomized controlled trial was conducted across 20 dental clinics. Participants included 400 adult tobacco users who were randomly assigned to either an intervention group (n = 200) or a control group (n = 200). The intervention group received a comprehensive tobacco cessation program consisting of behavioral counseling, nicotine replacement therapy, and follow-up sessions over 6 months. The control group received standard care, which included general advice on quitting tobacco. Data on tobacco use, quit rates, and oral health status were collected at baseline, 3 months, and 6 months. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 24.0, and significance was set at P < 0.05.

## Results:
At 6 months, the intervention group showed a significantly higher quit rate (45%) compared to the control group (20%) (P < 0.001). Additionally, participants in the intervention group exhibited significant improvements in oral health parameters, including reductions in periodontal pocket depths (a mean reduction of 2.5 mm) and improvements in gingival health scores. Satisfaction with the program was high among participants in the intervention group, with 85% reporting they would recommend the program to others.

## Conclusion:
The tobacco cessation program implemented in dental settings significantly increased quit rates and improved oral health outcomes compared to standard care. These findings suggest that integrating tobacco cessation programs into routine dental practice can effectively reduce tobacco use and enhance oral health.

## INTRODUCTION
Tobacco use is a major public health concern globally, contributing to a wide range of preventable diseases, including cardiovascular disease, respiratory disease, and various forms of cancer, notably oral cancer.[ ] The World Health Organization (WHO) reports that tobacco use is responsible for over 8 million deaths annually, with a significant portion of these attributable to second-hand smoke exposure.[ ] In the context of oral health, tobacco use is a well-established risk factor for periodontal disease, leukoplakia, and malignancies of the oral cavity.[ ]
Dental professionals are uniquely positioned to contribute to tobacco cessation efforts due to the regular and ongoing contact they have with patients. This interaction provides an opportunity to identify tobacco users and offer timely and relevant cessation interventions.[ ] Dental settings have been recognized as an ideal venue for tobacco cessation programs because dentists can observe the deleterious effects of tobacco on oral health firsthand, thereby providing a compelling reason for patients to quit.[ ] Previous studies have demonstrated the potential benefits of integrating tobacco cessation programs into dental practice, highlighting improvements in both quit rates and oral health outcomes.[ , ]
Despite the recognized potential, the implementation of comprehensive tobacco cessation programs in dental settings remains limited. Barriers such as lack of training, time constraints, and insufficient reimbursement have been cited as significant obstacles.[ ]

## MATERIALS AND METHODS

## Study design and participants
This randomized controlled trial aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of a comprehensive tobacco cessation program in dental settings. Conducted over 6 months, the study involved 20 dental clinics and included 400 adult tobacco users aged 18 years and older. Participants were recruited from patients visiting these clinics and met the inclusion criteria of current tobacco use, willingness to participate in a cessation program, and the ability to provide informed consent. Exclusion criteria included pregnancy, current enrolment in another cessation program, or use of cessation medication at baseline.

## Randomization and intervention
Participants were randomly assigned to either the intervention group (n = 200) or the control group (n = 200) using a computer-generated randomization sequence, ensuring equal representation from each clinic. The intervention group received a comprehensive tobacco cessation program comprising behavioral counseling, nicotine replacement therapy (NRT), and follow-up sessions. Behavioral counseling sessions were led by trained dental professionals and focused on motivation to quit, coping strategies, and relapse prevention, conducted at baseline, 1 month, 3 months, and 6 months. Participants were provided with NRT (patches, gum, or lozenges) based on preference and clinical suitability, with dosages adjusted as needed. Follow-up phone calls at 2 weeks, 1 month, and 4 months supplemented these sessions, offering additional support.

## Control group and data collection
The control group received standard care, consisting of brief advice on quitting tobacco during routine dental visits without structured counseling or NRT. Data collection occurred at baseline, 3 months, and 6 months.

## Statistical analysis and ethical considerations
Data analysis was performed using SPSS version 24.0.

## RESULTS

## Participant characteristics
A total of 400 participants were enrolled in the study, with 200 in the intervention group and 200 in the control group. The baseline characteristics of the participants, including age, gender, and tobacco use history, were comparable between the two groups.

## Quit rates
At 6 months, the intervention group exhibited a significantly higher quit rate compared to the control group [ ].
 

## Oral health outcomes
Participants in the intervention group showed significant improvements in oral health parameters compared to those in the control group. The mean reduction in periodontal pocket depths was greater in the intervention group, and gingival health scores improved markedly [ ].
 

## Participant satisfaction
Satisfaction with the program was high among participants in the intervention group. A substantial majority reported that they found the program helpful and would recommend it to others [ ].
 The results indicate that the comprehensive tobacco cessation program implemented in dental settings significantly increased quit rates and improved oral health outcomes compared to standard care. The high levels of participant satisfaction further support the feasibility and acceptability of such programs in dental practice.

## DISCUSSION
One of the key strengths of this study is the integration of tobacco cessation interventions into routine dental practice. Dental professionals, due to their regular interactions with patients and ability to observe the oral health consequences of tobacco use, are in a unique position to deliver effective cessation programs.[ ] The significant improvements in oral health parameters, including the reduction in periodontal pocket depths and improvements in gingival health scores, further emphasize the potential benefits of such programs. These findings align with previous research demonstrating the positive impact of tobacco cessation on periodontal health.[ ]
Participant satisfaction with the program was notably high, with 85% of the intervention group reporting that they found the program helpful and would recommend it to others. This level of satisfaction suggests that patients are receptive to receiving cessation support in dental settings. Previous studies have also reported high acceptability of tobacco cessation interventions delivered by dental professionals.[ ] The structured approach of combining behavioral counseling with NRT likely contributed to the high quit rates and participant satisfaction observed in this study. Behavioral counseling addresses the psychological and social aspects of tobacco addiction, while NRT helps manage withdrawal symptoms, providing a comprehensive support system for individuals attempting to quit.[ ]
The barriers to implementing comprehensive tobacco cessation programs in dental settings, such as lack of training and time constraints, must also be addressed. Training dental professionals in tobacco cessation techniques and incorporating these programs into routine practice could enhance the reach and impact of such interventions.[ ] Moreover, policy changes that provide adequate reimbursement for cessation services in dental settings could further support the integration of these programs.[ ]

## CONCLUSION
In conclusion, this study provides robust evidence supporting the effectiveness of a comprehensive tobacco cessation program in dental settings.
