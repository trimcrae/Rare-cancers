## Abstract
ABCD-ReproNim is a research educational course that provides training in responsible and reproducible analyses of data from the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) Study. ABCD-ReproNim was established in 2020 and structured as a collaborative partnership between ABCD investigators and ReproNim, a NIBIB-funded P41 center for reproducible neuroimaging whose vision is to help researchers achieve more reproducible data analysis workflows and outcomes. With over 1.1 K registered students and > 18 K YouTube views, ABCD-ReproNim has been well-received by the community and is providing ABCD data training while promoting skill development in reproducible analyses that support efficient, re-executable design and FAIR practices. Participants first receive didactic training across a semester-long online course that includes lectures, readings, and data exercises. We use an innovative curricular approach that is grounded in modern, transformational methods of STEM pedagogy, which includes philosophies and features drawn from active learning, inverted classrooms, and hack weeks. At the completion of the course, participants contribute to team-based, collaborative data analysis projects during an in-person hackathon at the host institution, Florida International University, in Miami, Florida. Through this research educational program, we provide (i) a comprehensive understanding of the ABCD dataset; (ii) instructional techniques that enhance the validity and reproducibility of research methods; (iii) support of interdisciplinary, team-based collaborations; and (iv) dissemination of the course, project materials, and research findings. Our overall goal is to contribute to the generation of a cadre of investigators that are well trained in the techniques that support responsible, reproducible, and valid analyses of ABCD data.
Keywords: ABCD, ABCD-ReproNim, ReproNim, Research educational course, Hackathon

## Introduction
Today’s biomedical research enterprise includes multiple population-based health initiatives of increasing scale and scope, leading to an unprecedented era of data acquisition, distribution, and utilization. Large volumes of complex, shared data are being generated by consortium-based studies, such as the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development℠ (ABCD) Study ( ), which is the largest, long-term study of brain development and child health in the United States. The ABCD Study® is a ten-year, longitudinal study that employs multidisciplinary protocols to collect brain, behavioral, genomic, and socioenvironmental data. The availability of such large, complex data has given rise to the need for enhanced education and training programs that not only provide instruction about ABCD data (e.g., measures, formats, sources), but also about principles and strategies for responsible ( , , ), reproducible ( , , , ), and valid ( ) data analyses.
An increasing body of evidence points to reproducibility issues in biomedical or life sciences ( , ). Lack of reproducibility has now been described across several scientific domains and for years concerns have been raised in the scientific community. Here, we use the term “reproducible research” to broadly refer to research that is sufficiently rigorous and transparent, allowing for independent verification, such that results can be reproduced (i.e., same analysis, same data) and/or replicated (i.e., same analysis, different data) ( ). ReproNim, a Center for Reproducible Neuroimaging Computation, has developed a curriculum that gives researchers the information, tools, and practices to perform repeatable and efficient research, as well as a map of where to find the resources for deeper practical training. Acknowledging that “Everything Matters” ( ) when it comes to the conduct of neurobiological research, the ReproNim vision is to help neuroimaging researchers to: (i) find and share data in a FAIR fashion ( , ), (ii) comprehensively describe their data and analysis workflows in a replicable fashion, and (iii) manage their computational resource options so that the outcomes of their research are more reproducible. Achieving these goals requires both technical development of readily accessible, user-friendly computational services that can be efficiently integrated into current research practices, and broad educational outreach about reproducibility to the community at large, including developers as well as applied researchers, across basic sciences and clinical disciplines.
ReproNim tools and services are intended to support a wide range of users with varied backgrounds, from applied users with minimal or extensive experience in image analysis, physics, and computer science to technology developers and software engineers. Reproducible analytics, a topic not taught in traditional university curricula, requires absorbing a significant amount of technology-oriented content. Hackathons, which are a natural venue for learning about, sharing, and building these tools, are increasingly well-attended around neuroimaging and other domain-specific conferences. documented how “Brainhacks” ( ) have grown from small gatherings to a global standard for training early-career researchers in reproducible science. However, these events, by their design, tend to attract individuals who are already highly engaged with computational tools and familiar with many existing reproducible workflows. As a result, the participants who could benefit most from introductory training are often the ones least likely to self-select into these events. Moreover, individuals with minimal data science or computational experience may find a few days of hackathon-related training to be insufficient. Additionally, there is a separate but equally important imperative in biomedical research and training, to utilize proper data analysis techniques in the face of existing sources of bias in observational data (e.g., due to selection, confounding variables, measurement error, model misspecification, and measurement non-invariance). Such problems, while well known in the epidemiological literature, are not widely recognized by researchers in other fields ( ) and can lead to invalid analyses.
In addition to reproducible and valid data analyses, analyses must also be responsible ( , , ). When large, complex datasets are openly shared and available for download, data analysis projects can proceed quickly, compared to when a project includes both data collection and analysis. However, responsible data analyses require careful planning, familiarity with data acquisition protocols, and a deep understanding of the data’s inherent limitations. In secondary data analyses, research questions must be appropriately tailored to the available data. This is especially critical when examining socioenvironmental factors, such as those related to the social determinants of health, which include non-medical conditions (i.e., where people are born, grow, live, and age) that shape daily life, influence health outcomes, and drive inequities ( ). The demographically diverse ABCD dataset ( ) includes many socioenvironmental variables that are associated with the social determinants of health, including family income and education, employment, housing, and neighborhood characteristics ( , ). Researchers working with data from diverse populations must thoughtfully consider the responsible use of variables related to race, ethnicity, gender, and sex ( , ). All of these socioenvironmental variables influence data missingness, sampling variability, and generalizability ( , , ); thus, statistical analyses should be designed with them in mind. Moreover, prior to analysis or interpretation, researchers must fully consider the psychological, social, economic, and potentially harmful impacts their research could have on individuals, communities, and society, in line with the principles of respect for persons, beneficence, and justice ( ). Importantly, despite the critical need for researchers to consider analytic design in the context of socioenvironmental factors, few resources currently provide hands-on training in responsible analyses.

## Program rationale
Our team’s prior training experiences have taught us that mastery of conceptual knowledge and computational technologies is a challenging task for students to complete in a one- to two-week immersive hackathon or workshop. Learning, consolidation, and skill development does not optimally occur over a short, intensive period of time. As instructors, we know it is common to spend a significant portion of a hack week on didactic instruction. Given all this, we established the ABCD-ReproNim Educational Program (www.abcd-repronim.org) in 2020.

3.

## ABCD-ReproNim
ABCD-ReproNim is based on modern active learning educational approaches ( ) and includes the use of inverted classroom practices, collaborative learning projects, and a hack week model ( ). Using the “flipped classroom” approach ( ), students first receive didactic instruction across a semester-long online course (e.g., lectures, readings) that allows the instructional process to occur over many weeks and with a curated set of teaching objectives. Flipped classrooms have been shown to yield gains in knowledge and skills acquisition compared to lecture-based classrooms ( , ), as well as improve self-efficacy and motivation ( , ). In the ABCD-ReproNim program, each week’s lectures are taught by invited instructors who are leading experts in the relevant specialty domain, ensuring that students receive current, field-informed instruction that directly supports the goals of both the ABCD Study and ReproNim. Data exercises are assigned to allow students to gain hands-on experience; students complete quizzes at the end of each exercise to ensure that the learning objectives are met. At the completion of the online course, students self-organize into small groups and collaboratively work on data analysis or resource development projects. This work culminates in a hackathon, where students conduct collaborative data projects, applying the skills they learned and working towards completion of ABCD project activities.

4.

## Program
ABCD-ReproNim offered its first online course from October 2020 through February 2021 (inclusive of a two-month winter break), while the second course offering was from January through March 2022. Additionally, as part of the 2022 course offering, a supplemental five-week mini-course on artificial intelligence and machine learning (AI/ML) was added to the curriculum. At the completion of the online course, participants took part in virtual hackathons in March 2020 and April 2022, respectively.

5.

## Cohorts
During the first offering (2020) of the ABCD-ReproNim course, a total of 744 students registered, including 37 undergraduates, 306 graduate and/or medical students, 152 postdoctoral researchers, 137 staff scientists, 74 assistant professors, and 27 associate/full professors. Registrants were asked to provide demographic information (e.g., age, country of residence, gender, race/ethnicity), as well as information on their educational background (e.g., career stage, academic discipline/field of study) and data science skills (e.g., familiarity with: Python, scripting, working with large datasets, working with the ABCD dataset). Given the high interest in ABCD-ReproNim, we were unable to provide the full student experience to all registrants and sought to carefully balance the finite nature of our time and resources with our desire to make ABCD-related reproducibility training accessible to as many researchers as possible. Of the 744 students who registered, 100 were selected as “enrolled students” with full course access and the remaining 644 were designated as “observer students” with more limited support. Student selection is a critical component to the overall success of any training program, and we recognized that review of student applications may be highly susceptible to implicit bias. With the intention of reducing bias as much as possible, we used a lottery-based selection process to select the 100 enrolled students; this process was weighted to achieve a representative distribution of the frequency of applicants’ self-reported computational and data science skills while promoting selection of students from underrepresented groups.
Following didactic instruction, a total of 54 enrolled students completed 15 collaborative ABCD data analysis projects during the hackathon. Over the course of the program, we observed a substantial drop-off from the initial number of registered students to the number of students who completed the course materials and contributed to data analysis projects. As a result, we opted to make no differentiation between enrolled and observer students during our second course offering (2022) and instead allow students to determine the level of participation that was appropriate for them based on their interest in the program and availability of time to dedicate to course materials. A total of 316 students registered, including 15 undergraduates, 150 graduate and/or medical students, 63 postdoctoral researchers, 48 staff scientists, 22 assistant professors, and 7 associate/full professors. Following didactic instruction, a total of 35 students completed 6 collaborative ABCD data analysis projects. A complete list of projects from both cohorts can be found on our GitHub repository at https://github.com/ABCD-ReproNim. As shown in , students represented a diverse group in terms of race/ethnicity, gender, underrepresentation in STEM, and coding experience (e.g., scripting, Python 3). We believe that the high numbers witnessed in 2020 were due to extremely strong interest in virtual training programs during the COVID−19 pandemic, as well as the novelty of interest in the ABCD Study.
 We recruited a team of teaching assistants (TAs) to support ABCD-ReproNim students and contribute to program success. Similar to students, TA applicants were asked to provide demographic data and information on their educational background and data science skills. They also were asked to provide a description of their teaching experience and an NIH-formatted biosketch. TAs were selected based on their experience with reproducible analytics and/or their experience working with ABCD data and received a $5000 stipend for their participation in the program. Their responsibilities included: contributing to lecture review and quality control, creating data exercises, mentoring students during office hours, participating in ABCD-ReproNim administrative meetings, and moderating the live weekly Q&A sessions with instructors. In addition, TAs contributed to collaborative project activities, joining individual project teams related to their research areas of expertise and providing one-on-one mentoring to students. Six TAs participated in the 2020 program; in 2022, five TAs contributed to the main program and two TAs to the AI/ML mini-course. All TAs were either graduate students or postdoctoral fellows.

## Student feedback
Overall, the ABCD-ReproNim Program was well received by the student cohorts. We administered a post-program survey to gather information across multiple domains on student perceptions of teaching (SPOTs). Although they are subjective measures of an educational experience, SPOTs offer insight into student satisfaction and can be useful as formative tools for improving teaching quality and student engagement ( , ). Results of this survey are shown in . We asked students how satisfied they were with: Q1) how much they learned; Q2) the amount of progress their teams made during project week; Q3) how reproducible their code was ; Q4) how much support they received from the ABCD-ReproNim team; Q5) the JupyterHub and other computational resources; Q6) the professional connections and friendships they made. provides a representative sample of the written feedback we received from students. We were pleased with the strong, positive responses regarding knowledge gained and personnel/computational support; meanwhile, we gained important, critical feedback about setting reasonable expectations for progress and code and how to prepare for data projects that will guide our future implementation of ABCD-ReproNim.

## Changes in knowledge and skills
Overall, students performed well on the data exercise quizzes, scoring an average of 73.88% among the 2020 enrolled cohort, 71.18% among the 2020 observer cohort, and 87.19% among the 2022 cohort. As the same data exercises were administered to all cohorts, the reason for the performance increase from 2020 to 2022 is unclear but may include greater familiarity with ABCD data or stronger motivation for success among the 2022 students.

## Long-term outcomes
Finally, we surveyed students from both cohorts in 2023 (i.e., 1–3 years follow-up) to assess the long-term outcomes of the program. Of the 43 students who responded, we learned that a total of 28 peer-reviewed articles or preprints were published as a result of ABCD-ReproNim training and activities. Of the 43 respondents, 26% indicated that they remained engaged in sustained collaborations or other activities as a result of their participation in ABCD-ReproNim (some nearly 3 years after the program!). These collaborations/activities took several forms: continued progress on collaborative ABCD manuscript drafts or resubmissions, engagement with ABCD-ReproNim materials with their own students (i.e., student-becomes-teacher model), collaborations extending to other projects and datasets, and participation in peer-based career support networks. One student responded, “I joined a lab where 2 of the graduate students had taken the ReproNim course. This was super useful, as we were able to collaborate using a common vocabulary and similar tools that had been introduced in the course! This helped us amplify the material of the course and share it with other lab members!”. Finally, of the 43 respondents, 12% indicated that they had submitted an NIH grant proposal (e.g., F, K, or R applications) involving ABCD data analysis. Overall, these data indicate that the 2020/2022 ABCD-ReproNim programs had a strong and sustained impact on the research career development of our students.

## Program renewal
Since 2020, ABCD-ReproNim has served a total of 1170 students and our YouTube course videos have a total of 18,450 views (as of January 2026). Over a 90-day period, this included 918 views, 254 unique viewers, and 12,894 impressions (October 17, 2025 to January 14, 2026). Funding for the ABCD-ReproNim Educational Program was renewed in late 2024, to include new offerings that will further develop, refine, and extend this innovative training through 2027. As before, using an inverted classroom design, we offer an online course to deliver didactic instruction via video-recorded lectures, with additional readings assigned to enhance and reinforce online lessons. Existing course content has been updated and new content has been added, based on student feedback and the evolving nature of the ABCD Study. Also as before, ABCD-ReproNim Students complete weekly data exercises with quizzes, which allow our team to evaluate progress and determine if any students need additional support or assistance. Once students have ample time to learn and master the proposed data analytic skills, they have the opportunity to put those skills to use during a four-day hackathon hosted by Florida International University in Miami, Florida. Hackathon projects may include data analyses to identify, model, or predict adolescent development in the ABCD dataset or may include tool development projects to optimize, integrate, or visualize ABCD data analyses. These projects may lead to publications or software contributions back to the greater community.
As in previous course offerings, the 2025–2027 ABCD-ReproNim Educational Program is designed to equip students to:
•Describe best practices in reproducible and valid analyses and explain why they are important.
•Describe best practices in responsible analyses and explain why they are important.
•Compare different data types and resources in the ABCD dataset.
•Compare different ReproNim computational tools and services.
•Demonstrate the ability to access and analyze ABCD data.
•Demonstrate the ability to access and use open source software.
•Contribute to one or more collaborative ABCD-ReproNim data projects.
These seven learning outcomes are designed to yield a comprehensive training program in ABCD protocols and measures, integrated with instruction on ReproNim’s best practices for reproducible and open science.

## Online course structure and support
The online course is free with open enrollment. An unlimited number of students may access course materials and complete the data exercises to allow for wide dissemination of course content. Our experience with the 2020/2022 ABCD-ReproNim offerings taught us that while many students were interested in our live meetings (e.g., webcast Q&As), the demands of their responsibilities to graduate programs or postdoctoral fellowships or time zone differences often caused scheduling challenges. Given their pre-existing commitments, it can be difficult for students to set aside sufficient time and effort needed to complete a full semester’s worth of ABCD-ReproNim activities. With both previous cohorts, we observed large numbers of initial registrations, with the size diminishing across the semester, resulting in a smaller, core number of students maintaining engagement through the end of the collaborative project activities. A similar trend has been noted across many massive open online courses and is not unique to the ABCD-ReproNim Program ( ). Moving forward, we have structured our new program in ways that best fit this model of adaptive engagement. We wish to provide as much flexibility as possible to all students; thus, our 2025 online course is asynchronous and students are welcome to determine what level of participation is appropriate for them. While synchronous learning experiences may foster greater engagement and real-time feedback, asynchronous offers flexibility, deeper reflection, and suitability for higher-order thinking ( , , ). We anticipate that, as in previous offerings, some students may complete the full course while others may only sample a lecture or two. We recognize that students are best able to determine what level of interaction with ABCD-ReproNim is appropriate for them, based on identifying a suitable balance between their ongoing responsibilities and their training needs.

## ABCD-ReproNim Fellows
To assist in the implementation of the 2025–2027 educational program, we expanded our Teaching Assistants program and plan to recruit 10–15 ABCD-ReproNim Fellows. Fellows have experience working with ABCD data (measured by self-report and/or a publication history involving ABCD data analysis) and provide support to students enrolled in the program; they also receive a $5000 stipend and travel funds to attend the hackathon. To foster the development of meaningful connections, ABCD-ReproNim Fellows host Zoom office hours multiple times each week to allow students to drop in and ask questions. Students and fellows conduct collaborative data analysis projects during the hackathon; we will share their resultant publications and they will be encouraged to present their findings to the ABCD Consortium to further promote their accomplishments.

12.

## Online course schedule
At the time of this article’s publication, the online course includes 15 weeks of content. Each week’s materials include two 45-minute lectures per week, along with a supplemental reading list and data exercise. For all lectures, a supplemental reading list of related journal articles and other materials is provided by each instructor and distributed to the students. Each week includes a data exercise designed to review and reinforce course content while also developing practical skills in reproducible neuroimaging analyses. The ABCD-ReproNim Fellows develop and implement the data exercises that are assigned each week.

13.

## Weeks 1–11
Online content during the first portion of the course consists of a pair of lectures: one focused on the ABCD Study and the other on ReproNim. ABCD lectures emphasize understanding different data sources and types available in the ABCD dataset. ReproNim lectures emphasize data tools, resources, and strategies for reproducible analytics.

14.

## Weeks 12–15
During the final three weeks of the course, we transition away from lectures that treat ABCD and ReproNim as individual topics and deliver more integrative content. These “Special Topics” lectures focus on data analytics for large, complex datasets that have been frequently requested by our previous students (e.g., longitudinal analyses, considerations for socioenvironmental variables).

15.

## Course platforms
YouTube. Once recorded, lectures are uploaded to the ABCD-ReproNim YouTube Channel and linked to the course website. As of January 2026, we currently have a library of 71 videos, which collectively have a total of 18,450 views. Our most popular lectures are the Week 1 Introduction to the ABCD Study (2.1 K views) and Week 2 ABCD Data Access and DEAP (1.8 K views).
Slack. To foster interactions among students, fellows, and program faculty, we use an ABCD-ReproNim Slack workspace. Anyone is welcome to join. Slack serves as a dedicated online social collaboration hub, which is key to promoting social networks, reinforcing lessons, clarifying concepts among each other, and seeking additional support from fellows and program faculty. Private channels are created for data project discussions; confirmation of authorized access to ABCD data is verified prior to adding members to these private channels.
Gather.Town. We use ABCD-ReproNim Gather.Town, which is a digital space to host synchronous meetings, such as project launch, discussions, office hours, and presentations. Gather.Town is useful both for informal discussions about the development and progress of data analysis projects, as well as for formal presentations of data analysis results. We encourage students to interact and self-organize into collaborative teams in preparation for a productive start to the hackathon.
NowIKnowMyABCD. We also encourage students to post their questions on the NowIKnowMyABCD ( ) forum. ABCD-ReproNim Fellows are charged with monitoring boards and answering questions. Some responses may be generated by the fellows, whereas others may be pushed to ABCD Consortium members for their deep knowledge and expertise. Public posting of Q&As will be encouraged to promote greater dissemination of responses (i.e., we prefer generating answers that can be searched online by others and not hidden behind private emails or other direct messages). We ensure that messages do not contain PGUIDs or other unauthorized distribution of ABCD data.

## Computational considerations
Our prior training experience has also shown us that computational demands and access to computational resources are often a limiting factor for project teams during hack weeks, especially for those working with large datasets. With this in mind, and similar to our previous course offerings, ABCD-ReproNim provides access to a JupyterHub. This online Hub allows students with access to ABCD data to use a common computational platform for performing course-related data exercises and participating in the hackathon. In addition to our JupyterHub, we will also encourage participants to employ the use of state-of-the-art reproducible and scalable workflows (e.g., BIDS App Bootstrap; BABS ( )).

18.

## Professional and personal development opportunities
Students in the ABCD-ReproNim Educational Program: (i) learn how to access ABCD data, (ii) receive training on best practices for responsible data use and strategies and tools to support reproducible outcomes, (iii) are given access to a shared computational platform for conducting data analyses, (iv) receive individualized guidance on integrating reproducibility tools into their research methods, and (v) generate diverse, professional connections centered around interdisciplinary, team-based ABCD data projects that can be turned into publishable manuscripts. As students learn to conduct reproducible data analyses, they also gain experience in community interactions and peer-based research collaborations. They gain real-world experience in the challenges of navigating academic research, problem-solving, leadership, and time management. ABCD-ReproNim provides robust analytic training, as well as substantial enrichment and professional development opportunities. As program leaders, we know that running this course is very time- and labor-intensive. However, it is incredibly rewarding to contribute to the development of early career scientists. Their desire to learn and acquire practical skills is strong, their interactions are positive, and their enthusiasm is infectious. captures a brief but representative Slack conversation that was observed during the original 2020 hackathon. Overall, the ABCD-ReproNim experience complements and enhances traditional research training. By creating a stimulating and challenging environment that emphasizes collaborations and team-based productivity, ABCD-ReproNim fulfills a unique role in fostering the development of an innovative, talented, and cooperative scientific workforce.
 
19.

## Conclusions
By coupling reproducibility and responsibility training with ABCD data, the ABCD-ReproNim Educational Program provides support for a new form of an inverted classroom for workshops and hackathons. Here, students engage in best practices for learning about responsible ABCD data use while applying strategies and tools that support reproducible and valid outcomes. By participating in a semester-long online course, this training program fills the gap between a typical NIH T32 program and an immersive summer school. The increased number of hours spent on training through instructor- and assignment-based pedagogy provide a strong foundation for participants before engaging in a collaborative analysis experience that is focused on innovating around ABCD data. Overall, our goal is to enhance workforce training to meet biomedical, behavioral, and clinical research needs in the responsible and reproducible use of ABCD data.
