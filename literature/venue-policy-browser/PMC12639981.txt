## Background
The school physical education program is an important factor in improving students’ physical fitness. In China, secondary school students’ physical fitness is facing a lot of problems, and in order to improve students’ physical fitness, the quality of the implementation of the physical education program by the school should be ensured, so it is necessary to investigate the current status of the implementation of the physical education program by junior high school schools in China, and to analyze the dimensions in which schools with high levels of implementation are better than others, so as to provide a reference for other levels of implementation to better improve the physical fitness of students.

## Methods
A questionnaire was used to assess the physical education implementation levels of 47 schools in the city, covering 10 dimensions. ANOVA was used to compare the differences in the dimensions among schools of different levels, and multiple linear regression analysis was used to explore the influence of each dimension on the implementation level.

## Results
The implementation level of physical education in junior high schools in the city was characterized by a distribution with more in the middle and less at the ends, and the high-level schools were significantly better than the low-level schools in several dimensions. Regression analysis showed that only campus physical activities (β = 0.058, t = 2.616, p = 0.013) and teacher team (β = 0.080, t = 3.331, p = 0.002) had a significant positive predictive effect on the implementation level.

## Conclusion
campus physical activities and teacher team development are the key players in improving the implementation level of physical education. It is recommended that education departments prioritize the strengthening of these two aspects, while paying attention to inter-school differences and promoting the balanced development of regional physical education.

## Background
There is a relationship between the implementation of school physical education programs and students’ physical health, and school physical education programs are important factors in improving students’ physical fitness and promoting their overall development. Several studies have shown that content-rich, well-planned, and goal-oriented physical education program implementation helps to improve students’ physical fitness and develop good exercise habits and health awareness [ , ].
The implementation of physical education programs in schools usually refers to planned, sequential, and standardized physical activities developed by the government for students. The implementation of these programs is often carried out at the school level and aim to promote students’ physical fitness, motor skills, social adaptability, mental health, and lifelong physical activity awareness through various physical activities. This series of activities not only includes classroom teaching content and methods, but also covers multiple dimensions such as teacher allocation, teaching facilities, curriculum objectives, and assessment standards [ – ].
Each of these dimensions plays a unique role in promoting students’ physical, mental, and social well-being. A study has pointed out that high-quality physical education courses are correlated with students’ cardiorespiratory fitness. Physical activities with sufficient frequency and intensity can improve students’ cardiorespiratory fitness and reduce obesity rates [ ]. Teacher quality is also crucial. Studies have shown that the professional competence of physical education teachers has a significant impact on students’ physical development and health, and that professionally competent physical education teachers are more effective than generalist teachers in helping children improve their physical fitness [ ]. In addition, adequate facilities and equipment access are positively correlated with students’ level of physical activity during school hours [ ]. These findings indicate that the effectiveness of school physical education programs depends on the coordinated implementation of their core components.
In China, secondary school students are facing problems such as unhealthy lifestyles, poor dietary habits, lack of physical activity, and obesity, which are seriously affecting their physical and mental health development [ – ]. In order to improve this situation, China’s Ministry of Education 2022 issued a new curriculum indicator, in which the diversity of students’ physical education program content and the frequency of the program have been increased, with the goal of comprehensively improving the quality of physical education in schools and ensuring that students’ physical fitness and health steadily improve [ ].
However, previous studies have found that Chinese schools still face many challenges in the actual implementation of government-mandated physical education programs, such as inadequate curriculum implementation, insufficient teaching resources, and a lack of extracurricular activities. These problems lead to differences in implementation effectiveness among different schools under a unified policy framework, which in turn causes inequality in students’ access to physical education. The government and schools should take effective measures to narrow the implementation gap and provide fair and high-quality physical education for all students [ , ].
Although previous studies have focused on the differences in the effectiveness of physical education program implementation among schools and proposed corresponding recommendations for improvement, there has been relatively little empirical research on whether there are differences in implementation effectiveness among different schools after the implementation of China’s new curriculum reform policy and what these differences are. Such issues still require continued attention. Therefore, in order to investigate the current situation in schools, enable schools to achieve good results in the implementation of physical education programs, reduce the differences between schools, and enable schools to better promote the physical development of students, this study will systematically evaluate the implementation level of physical education programs from various dimensions of schools. It will not only explore the specific situation of the implementation of physical education programs in Chinese junior high schools, but also analyze the causes of implementation differences and provide schools with practical recommendations for improvement.
In addition, most studies have used teacher interviews or student questionnaires to address this issue, but lack quantitative evaluation tools that are constructed on a systematic basis from the perspective of the school as a whole. Therefore, this study will also attempt to promote a quantitative and comparable measure of the implementation level of physical education programs from the perspective of the school as a whole. Based on this, this study took Huaibei City, China, as the study site, and used a combination of questionnaire and fieldwork to systematically assess the implementation level of physical education programs in middle schools in Huaibei City, China, focusing on the key dimensions of organization and management, education and teaching, conditions and safeguards, students’ physical fitness, and supervision and evaluation.
Specific objectives include: (1) To assess the current status of the implementation level of physical education programs in different schools of middle schools in Huaibei City, China, from the ten dimensions of the questionnaire; (2) To explore the key dimensions affecting the current implementation level of middle school schools in Huaibei City, China, from the ten assessment dimensions of the questionnaire; (3) To analyze the main determinants of the implementation level of middle school physical education programs in middle schools in Huaibei City, China, from the key dimensions that are basically related to the implementation level of schools; (4) Provide scientific evidence for policymakers and school administrators to optimize resource allocation and intervention strategies and improve the efficiency of program implementation. This study helps fill the gap in research on the implementation of physical education programs and promotes the transition from good planning to good implementation, which has important theoretical value and practical significance.

## Methods

## Participant
The research site chosen for this study is Huaibei City, China, because it is located in the middle of China’s northern and southern regions, with both southern and northern climatic characteristics [ ]. In terms of the city’s economic development, Huaibei city has a GDP per capita of US$9,604 and the median GDP per capita of all Chinese cities is US$9,179, both of which are close to China’s GDP per capita of US$12,614.06 [ , ], so that its economic development can be a good representation of most of China’s cities at the middle level of development. Although the sample is limited to one city, its geographic and economic attributes are still representative and can provide a reference basis for the implementation of physical education in central China and cities of similar economic level. Information on middle school schools in the city was obtained from the local education department, with a total of four districts in the city, and schools in each district were randomly selected at a rate of 30% [ ], resulting in 47 schools being selected as representatives for the questionnaire evaluation.

## Instrument
To collect data on the implementation status of physical education programs in the sampled schools, this study employed a questionnaire with good reliability and validity [ ]. The questionnaire was developed based on the content of “Annex 1: Evaluation Index System for Physical Education Work in Primary and Secondary Schools” from the Evaluation Methods for Physical Education Work in Primary and Secondary Schools, issued by the Ministry of Education of the People’s Republic of China (in Chinese: 《中小学体育工作评估方法》第 1 附录) [ ]. The annex is an evaluation form used by administrative assessors to rate the extent to which schools implement national physical education policies. It adopts a 100-point scoring system and classifies schools into four levels based on their scores: Excellent (≥ 90 points), Good (75–89 points), Pass (60–74 points), and Fail (≤ 59 points), detailed information can be found in additional file 2. The questionnaire used in this study remains highly consistent with the original evaluation form in terms of content dimensions but adopts a self-reported Likert 5-point scale format. It consists of 10 dimensions and 38 items in total, with each item scored from 0 to 4 points, resulting in a total score range of 0 to 152. The full questionnaire is provided in additional file 1.
To meet the needs of this study and enhance the comparability of the results, we applied a linear transformation method to convert the national classification standards from the original evaluation form into a grading system suitable for this study [ ]. Linear transformation is a commonly used method in educational assessment to maintain score interpretability across different scoring systems [ ]. The final total score of the questionnaire will be categorized as High, Moderate or Low to reflect the level of implementation in the school. The High, Moderate, and Low categories were designed to align with the ‘Excellent’ (≥ 90 points), ‘Good’ (75–89 points), and ‘Fail/Pass’ (≤ 74 points) ratings of the original 100-point government framework. A linear transformation was applied to map the total questionnaire score (0–152) onto this 0–100 scale, resulting in the thresholds of 137 (for High) and 113 (for Low).

## Theoretical framework
This study is theoretically grounded in the CIPP evaluation model (Context, Input, Process, and Product), originally developed by Stufflebeam in the 1970s and subsequently refined over time [ ]. The CIPP model serves as a comprehensive and systematic framework for evaluating educational programs by identifying their strengths, weaknesses, and effectiveness. It has been widely used in both educational and policy evaluation contexts [ , ].
Given that the purpose of this study is to evaluate the implementation level of physical education programs at the school level, the CIPP model provides a structural framework that is consistent with the research objectives. The ten dimensions included in the questionnaire used in this study closely correspond to the four components of the CIPP model.
The details are as follows: Context includes “strengthening management” and “leadership attention.” These two dimensions mainly reflect the policy environment and support background provided by the school at the institutional, organizational, and leadership levels for physical education projects, which are the prerequisites and external conditions for project implementation.
Input includes “teacher team” and “venue equipment and funds,” which represent the human and material resources invested by the school in the project and are the basic guarantees for its smooth implementation.
Process covers “curriculum teaching,” “campus physical activities,” “organizing tests for all students,” and “supervision.” These contents involve the implementation of teaching plans, the implementation of daily sports activities, the organization of tests, and process supervision, reflecting the specific operations and management of the project during its implementation.
Product includes “test results” and “test evaluation,” which mainly reflect the achievement of students’ physical fitness standards and the school’s analysis and feedback on the results. These are key indicators for measuring the effectiveness and output of the project.

## Procedure
From each of the 47 schools sampled, we invited one physical education teacher from each school who was most familiar with the situation of physical education in the school to score the questionnaires, and a total of 47 people completed the questionnaires, including 38 males (35 ± 3.4 years old) and 9 females (31 ± 4.2 years old). All raters were trained on the uniform scoring criteria before completing the responses to improve the consistency and reliability of the data. Considering that most of these physical education teachers were either the main person in charge of physical education in their schools or had core responsibilities such as curriculum scheduling, organization of extracurricular activities and implementation of programs in their schools, they were able to provide a more comprehensive picture of the actual implementation of the physical education program in their schools.
To further guarantee the authenticity and representativeness of the data, we asked each interviewed teacher to provide brief supporting materials (e.g., curriculum, activity photos, activity records, etc.) when submitting the questionnaire. In addition, we randomly selected 10% of the sample schools to carry out field verification, comparing and validating the questionnaire contents through field observation and interviews with other relevant personnel (e.g. school leaders, other PE teachers). The results showed that the agreement between the scoring results and the actual situation reached 92%, indicating that the questionnaire data had a high degree of reliability and representativeness on the whole.
The questionnaire was distributed by e-mail, with the physical education teachers’ e-mail addresses and contact information provided by the local education department or a willing partner in the school district. The survey began at the start of the school year in the spring of 2025, and respondents were asked to base their answers on the actual school year of 2024. All processes were reviewed and approved by the UPM Ethics Committee under approval number JKEUPM-2024-511 Written informed consent was not required as respondents only reported on policies and practices at their school and did not provide any identifying information other than their individual school position, age, and gender.

## Data analysis methods
Based on the questionnaire data from the 47 sample schools in Huaibei City, after classifying the schools into high, moderate, and low implementation levels based on the scoring criteria, we first counted the number and percentage of each of the 47 schools in each of the three different levels of implementation, as well as calculating the total mean scores and standard deviations of the schools in different levels of implementation, which were used to characterize the approximate overall distribution of the level of implementation of physical education in junior high schools in Huaibei City.
To derive between-group differences in each dimension between schools with different levels of implementation in Huaibei City at high, moderate, and low levels of implementation, a one-way ANOVA was conducted for each of the 10 dimensions, with implementation level grouping as the independent variable and scores for each dimension as the dependent variable. To cope with multiple comparisons across multiple dimensions, a Bonferroni correction was used to adjust the significance threshold to α = 0.005 (i.e., 0.05 divided by 10), and the results were recognized as statistically significant only when the p value was less than 0.005. And for dimensions showing a significant main effect in the ANOVA (with Bonferroni-corrected p-values less than 0.005), post hoc pairwise comparisons were performed using Tukey’s Honestly Significant Difference (HSD) test to control for family error rates. The significance level for all post hoc tests was set at α = 0.005.
To further examine the core drivers of implementation levels, an initial screening was conducted using Spearman’s correlation analysis to explore potential key factors influencing implementation levels, and only those factors that showed a significant and strong positive correlation with the total implementation score were included in the next step of the multiple linear regression analysis. Multiple regression models were then used to quantify the independent contribution of each factor after controlling for confounding factors. All statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics version 26.0.

## Results

## Overall distribution
In this study, 47 schools were surveyed and the final scores of the schools were categorized into high, moderate and low implementation levels based on the scoring criteria. As shown in Table  , the overall trend shows that the city has a high number of moderate implementation level schools and a relatively low number of high and low implementation level schools. The average score for schools at the high implementation level was 138.727, or 23.4%; the average score for schools at the moderate implementation level was 128.154, or 55.3%; and the average score for schools at the low implementation level was 103.3, or 21.3%. The average score for all 47 schools was 125.34, which is at the moderate implementation level.

 

## Differences in dimensions
Table  demonstrates the means and standard deviations of the 47 schools in the different levels of implementation level groups on each dimension, as well as the results of the one-way ANOVA. After Bonferroni correction (α = 0.005), curriculum instruction, physical activity on campus, teaching staff, field equipment and funding, and test results showed statistically significant differences between groups (p < 0.005), whereas strengthened management, leadership attention, organization of testing for all students, and test evaluation and monitoring did not reach the adjusted threshold (p > 0.005). 
In order to determine specific differences in the presence of significance dimensions across implementation levels, post hoc pairwise comparisons were conducted using the Tukey HSD test, with significance levels assessed at the Bonferroni-corrected alpha value of 0.005. Table  Tukey HSD test showed that overall high implementation level schools outperformed moderate and low implementation level schools in general on the dimensions of curriculum instruction, physical activity on campus, teaching staff, field equipment and funding, and test results. 
Specifically, high (MD = 6.764) and moderate (MD = 5.169) implementation level schools outperformed low implementation level schools in the curriculum teaching (P < 0.005), but there was no significant difference between high and moderate implementation levels (P = 0.126). For the campus physical activities, the high (MD = 8.964) and moderate (MD = 6.215) groups were significantly better than the low group (p < 0.005), but there was no significant difference between the high and moderate groups (p = 0.009). For the teacher team, the high implementation level group was significantly better than the moderate and low implementation level groups, and the moderate implementation level group was better than the low implementation level group (MD = 6.492, p < 0.005). In the venue equipment and funds, both the high (MD = 4.845) and moderate (MD = 3.838) groups were significantly better than the low group (P < 0.005), but there was no significant difference between the high and moderate groups (P = 0.540). In the test results, the moderate group was significantly better than the low implementation level group (MD = 1.446, P = 0.002), but there was no significant difference between the high and moderate-low groups.
Based on the interpretation of the CIPP model, these significant differences reflect the key roles of the “Input” and “Process” components in the differentiation of school PE implementation levels. High-quality teachers and richer activity schedules (Process), as well as more adequate resource inputs (Input), provided a strong guarantee for the effectiveness of implementation.

## Correlation analysis
To further test the core drivers influencing the level of implementation, an initial screening was conducted using Spearman correlation analysis to explore potential key factors influencing the level of implementation. Table  presents the results of the Spearman correlation analysis of the total score of the school’s implementation level with each of the dimensional variables respectively. The results of the analysis showed that all of the eight items among the total score and the strengthening management, curriculum teaching, campus physical activities, teacher team, venue equipment and funds, Organizing test for all students, test results and supervision showed significance with the correlation coefficient values of 0.631, 0.843, 0.745, 0.846, 0.705, 0.433, 0.601, 0.681, which implied that there is a positive correlation between the total score and these eight dimensions, but the Organizing test for all students dimension is relatively weak with the total score of school implementation level. Meanwhile, the total score showed no significance between the 2 items of leadership attention and test evaluation, and the value of the correlation coefficient is close to 0, which means that there is no correlation between the total score and these 2 dimensions. Therefore, the three dimensions of leadership attention, organizing test for all students, and test evaluation were not included in the next regression analysis. 
Although this analysis provided direction for the initial screening, the correlations themselves were not sufficient to explain the causal mechanism between the variables. Therefore, whether these stronger positive correlations still have independent predictive power after controlling for other variables will be further explored in the next step of regression modeling.

## Regression analysis
Multiple linear regression analyses were conducted in order to explore in depth the main factors affecting the level of physical education program implementation, with the level of physical education program implementation grade as the dependent variable, and the significantly related dimensions of strengthening management, leadership attention, curriculum teaching, campus physical activities, teacher team, venue equipment and funds, test results, and supervision as the independent variables. Table  demonstrates the fit of the regression model and the regression coefficients.
The predictive model for physical education program implementation level grades developed through linear regression analysis was: physical education program implementation level grades = −1.824 + 0.058 × [campus physical activities] + 0.080 × [teacher team]. The model had high explanatory power (R² = 0.746), indicating that the seven predictive dimensions included collectively explained 74.6% of the variance in physical education program implementation level grades. The overall test of the model showed a significant effect (F = 16.407, p < 0.001), confirming that at least one of the dimensions was significantly associated with implementation level grades. The multicollinearity test against the model showed that the variance inflation factor (VIF) for all variables was less than 5, indicating that the model does not suffer from serious multicollinearity problems. In addition, the Durbin-Watson statistic (D-W value) is 2.155, indicating that there is no significant autocorrelation of the model residuals and the sample data are independent of each other. Taken together, the model satisfies the basic assumption conditions of linear regression.
According to Table  , campus physical activities (β = 0.058, t = 2.616, p = 0.013) and teacher team (β = 0.080, t = 3.331, p = 0.002) were the variables with a significant positive effect; strengthening management (β = 0.027, p = 0.569), curriculum teaching (β = 0.003, p = 0.944), venue equipment and funds (β = 0.012, p = 0.639), test results (β = 0.034, p = 0.623) and supervision (β = 0.042, p = 0.440) were five dimensions that did not reach statistical significance. This result indicates that only two dimensions, campus physical activities and teacher team, showed significant positive predictive effect (p < 0.05) on the level of physical education program implementation rating, controlling for other variables, while the regression coefficients of the remaining variables did not reach statistical significance (p > 0.05).
From the perspective of the CIPP model, the composition and competency of the teacher team represent one of the most critical elements within the “Input” component. The quality of this input directly affects the organization of physical education curricula, classroom effectiveness, and student engagement, thereby influencing the implementation strength of the “Process” dimension. Meanwhile, the dimension of campus physical activities, which reflect the extensiveness of curriculum implementation and the breadth of student participation, serve as a key indicator within the “Process” component and provide a direct representation of implementation outcomes. These findings are not only statistically significant but also reveal core mechanisms in the actual operation of physical education, particularly the role of students、teachers and physical activities in institutional implementation.

 

## Discussion
This study investigated the implementation level of physical education programs in 47 junior high schools in the city by means of a questionnaire, and explored the characteristics of the overall distribution of the level of physical education in junior high schools in the city, the characteristics of the differences in the dimensions of schools at different implementation levels, and the mechanisms by which the different dimensions affect the implementation level of the schools. The study divided the schools into three implementation level groups: high, moderate, and low based on the total score of the questionnaire, and the results showed that the approximate overall distribution of the implementation level of physical education in junior high schools in the city showed a distribution pattern with more in the middle and fewer at the ends. This distribution characteristic indicates that most schools (55.3%) are at the moderate implementation level, and that the city has made some progress in advancing its physical education program, so that most schools have a certain foundation for the implementation of physical education; the number of schools at high and low implementation levels each accounted for 23.4% and 21.3%, which indicates that there are obvious differences between schools in the city region, there is an uneven development phenomenon, and that the number of schools with high quality implementation levels is relatively low compared to the number of schools with moderate implementation levels. The number of schools with a high level of implementation is relatively small compared to the number of schools with a moderate level of implementation, and the overall level needs to be further improved.
Based on an analysis using the four dimensions of the CIPP evaluation model, the most significant differences in the implementation of physical education programs in junior high schools in Huaibei city were observed at the Input and Process dimensions. Specifically, the results of the one-way ANOVA and post hoc comparisons indicated that schools with high and moderate levels of implementation scored significantly higher than those with low levels in five areas: curriculum teaching, campus physical activities, teacher team, venue equipment and funds, and test results. Additionally, schools with a high level of implementation slightly outperformed those with a moderate level, reflecting the advantages of well-implemented programs in terms of resource support and instructional execution. In contrast, no significant differences were found among the groups in dimensions such as strengthening management, leadership attention, organizing test for all students, test evaluation, and supervision. This suggests that although low-implementation schools may actively respond to institutional requirements or government policies, gaps remain in translating these policies into effective school-based practice [ ]. These findings also indirectly highlight a broader issue in China’s education reform: a strong emphasis on policy formulation, but insufficient attention to implementation outcomes.
Further regression analyses showed that, controlling for other variables, only two dimensions, campus physical activity and teacher team, had a significant positive predictive effect on the level of physical education program implementation. This result suggests that even if a school has a good institutional and resource base, the lack of an active physical activity organization and a team of highly qualified teachers will not have the desired effect on the implementation of the program. This finding is similar to a study that showed that encouraging students to be active in school-based sports was significantly positively associated with adolescent physical activity, and that adolescents who participated regularly in a variety of organized sports activities were positively associated with their physical health [ ]. Another study on professional teacher literacy found similarly to the findings of this paper, that teachers’ professional background and quality of instruction were the core variables affecting the effectiveness of physical education programs [ ].
Based on the above analysis, it is recommended that the government and schools focus their resources on strengthening the development of teacher team and organizing campus physical activities to improve the effectiveness of physical education program implementation. Educational authorities should develop specific policies to enhance the professional development pathways for physical education teachers, increase the attractiveness of teaching positions, and promote diverse and regular extracurricular physical activities at all levels of schools. Additionally, the CIPP model should be used for a systematic evaluation of schools’ performance at each stage of the “environmental input – process execution – output results” framework, in order to design personalized improvement plans and promote balanced educational resource allocation across regions.
This study reflects the current status of physical education program implementation in some Chinese junior high schools, though it is important to note that data was collected exclusively from Huaibei City. Consequently, the findings should not be overgeneralized to the national level. Nevertheless, the research on Huaibei City holds significant value, as its geographical location and socioeconomic characteristics represent typical features of both northern and southern China. This makes the findings particularly relevant for understanding the challenges and opportunities faced by medium-sized cities in similar contexts.

## Strengths and limitations
This article introduces the CIPP model into the evaluation study of physical education programs in junior high schools and corresponds to the content of the actual research questionnaire, providing a structured analytical framework for program implementation evaluation in the field of physical education in junior high schools in China. In addition, this article conducted field research in 47 junior high schools in Huaibei City, China, to assess the implementation level of physical education programs in junior high schools in the region after the implementation of the new policy from a multidimensional perspective, and found that there are significant differences in Input, Process, and Output, which fills in the gaps of relevant empirical studies and provides data support and policy implications for the development of more targeted education policies in the future.
However, this study still has some limitations. First, the sample is limited to one city, which may limit the generalizability of the study’s findings. Second, despite efforts to ensure validity, the use of self-report measures may introduce subjective bias. Third, although several significant predictors were identified in this study, these results were based on cross-sectional data and therefore causality between variables cannot be concluded. Future research should conduct data collection in a wider area to improve the representativeness and applicability of the findings, and longitudinal designs or experimental methods could be used to further validate the causal mechanisms between those significant predictors and implementation levels.

## Conclusion
Based on questionnaire data from 47 middle schools in Huaibei City, China, this study explored the overall distributional characteristics of physical education program implementation levels in junior high schools in the city, the differences in scores on the dimensions, and their relationships with implementation level grades. The results showed that the implementation level of physical education in junior high schools in the region did differ significantly on key dimensions and was overall at a moderate implementation level, indicating that there are still real challenges to promote the implementation of high-quality physical education. Campus physical activity and teacher team may be the key dimensions that differentiate schools’ implementation levels, and it is recommended that these dimensions be prioritized in the next policy interventions and resource inputs in order to further improve the overall implementation quality. Moreover, future studies may further expand the sample to cover different regional levels to enhance the external validity and national applicability of the findings.
