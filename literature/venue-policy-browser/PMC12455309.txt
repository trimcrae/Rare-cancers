World Health Organization Collaborating Centre for Infectious Disease Epidemiology and Control, School of Public Health, Li Ka Shing Faculty of Medicine, The University of Hong Kong, Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, China , ]. Hong Kong (HK) contained the first 4 coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) epidemics (January 2020 to April 2021) through continued public health and social measures (PHSMs), including travel controls, entrance restrictions, inbound traveler testing, mandated quarantine, school and restaurant closures, social distancing, and contact isolation [ ]. However, in early 2022, HK experienced a severe outbreak (the fifth wave) from January 2022 to the end of April 2022, which was triggered by a superspreading event [ ]. The fifth wave (predominated by Omicron BA.2), which recorded >10 000 daily cases per million during the peak, quickly overwhelmed the local healthcare system given limited prior population immunity from vaccination or infection [ ]. Reactive PHSMs were reinstated by April 2022 [ ]. Meanwhile, genomic surveillance detected rising imported BA.4/BA.5 cases [ ]. Following the emergence of Omicron BA.5 and subsequent relaxation of PHSMs, HK experienced a second larger wave that peaked in late September 2022, with BA.4/BA.5 accounting for >85% of subsampled cases [ ].
Given the substantial immune escape of Omicron variant [ ] and administration of vaccines, population immunity induced by both infection and vaccination in HK may shift dramatically. Therefore, to understand how population immunity and PHSMs shaped the observed dynamics and the potential impact of immune evasion across Omicron subvariants by age in HK, we constructed an age-structured multistrain model and fitted this model based on epidemiological data streams including age-specific polymerase chain reaction (PCR)–confirmed cases, vaccination, and Omicron subvariant proportion from subsamples, using a Bayesian synthesis approach. We estimated the relative change in transmissibility and ascertainment proportion of cases for Omicron BA.2 and BA.5 waves, the impact of PHSMs on the transmission, and the contribution of natural infection and vaccination for age-specific population immunity against infection over time in HK.

## METHODS

## Data Sources and Processing
We calibrated the model using multiple epidemiological data streams, including daily reverse-transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR)–confirmed COVID-19 cases (aggregated weekly by age), vaccination doses administered, and Omicron subvariant proportions from subsampled genomic surveillance. Daily RT-PCR–confirmed cases and vaccination data were sourced from the Centre for Health Protection (CHP) of the Department of Health (DH) in HK, with vaccination records stratified by age and vaccine type (BNT162b2 messenger RNA [mRNA] and CoronaVac inactivated) to account for differential effectiveness. From late April 2022, the DH Public Health Laboratory Services Branch has implemented whole genome sequencing (WGS) analysis to closely monitor the transmission of Omicron subvariants, initially focusing on identifying emerging variants in imported cases [ ]. Subsequently, daily updates on the proportion of each identified subvariant among a subsample of local cases for verification testing were released by the DH. However, the use of WGS in its analysis has not been explicitly mentioned since 20 June 2022 [ ]. Additionally, from a cohort study by the University of Hong Kong, we obtained COVID-19 point-prevalence data using self-reported rapid antigen test (RAT) results from 10 000 daily random sampled participants irrespective of symptom status and exposure history across all 18 districts in HK [ ]. We aimed to use estimates of time-varying infection prevalence detected by RAT from this prospective cohort to externally validate our model-derived infection prevalence estimates.

## Age-Structured Multivariant Model
We developed an age-structured, multivariant susceptible-exposed-infectious-recovered (SEIR) model of COVID-19 transmission stratified by vaccination status and 3 age groups, namely children and adolescents (0–19 years), adults (20–60 years), and elderly adults (>60 years), aligning with age categorization of HK vaccination data ( ). Within each age group, populations were categorized into 5 vaccination statuses: unvaccinated, protected by 1 dose, 2 doses, waned 2-dose, or 3 doses. The time-varying transmission rate was constructed as a piece-wise linear function estimated every 14 days, determined by model comparisons using deviance information criteria ( ). We then computed the time-varying instantaneous reproductive number without the effect of immunity ( ), which is only driven by the effects of PHSMs and instantaneous transmissibility of subvariants, representing the average number of secondary cases generated by a primary infection among a fully susceptible population at time t, and effective reproductive number that accounted for population immunity ( ) ( ). The former allows us to disentangle the intrinsic and extrinsic factors that impact transmission. To investigate the impact of the Omicron BA.5 subvariant (imported in late April 2022), the model was further stratified to accommodate 3 infection histories: primary BA.2, primary BA.5, or BA.5 reinfection following prior BA.2 infection. We assumed that BA.5 infection conferred full protection against BA.2, while prior BA.2 infection provided partial protection against BA.5 [ ].
The model follows a SEIR framework. Susceptible individuals (S) transition to the exposed compartment (E) upon infection and become infectious (I) before recovering (R) after a variant-specific infectious period. The rollout of the vaccination is informed by daily age-specific administration data, with eligibility extended to susceptible and recovered individuals, consistent with CHP's guidelines on COVID-19 vaccination [ ]. Age-specific vaccine effectiveness (VE) was calculated as a weighted average of mRNA and inactivated VEs, proportional to their daily administered doses per age group (Supplementary Figures 1 and 2). Ascertainment proportion by RT-PCR for each age group during the fifth wave was assumed to increase exponentially from an estimated initial value to a peak proportion, and remain thereafter until the end of the fifth wave. We then incorporated different relative ascertainment proportions to reflect shifts in testing capacity for 3 age groups during the sixth wave relative to the fifth wave. Full model equations and details are provided in the . Fixed and estimated parameters are listed in Supplementary Tables 1, 2 and 5.

## Model Inference
We fitted the model to age- and variant-specific weekly RT-PCR–confirmed cases (from 31 January 2022 to 25 September 2022) to capture the co-circulation of Omicron BA.2 and BA.5. Self-reported RAT cases were excluded due to lower sensitivity and reliability, compared to PCR [ , ]. Our analyses were truncated until 25 September 2022, when BA.2 and BA.5 were no longer dominant amid multiple emerging variants [ ]. We inferred the seeding date of BA.5 (defined as the start day of BA.5 transmission in our model) and relative transmissibility of Omicron BA.5 over the BA.2 subvariant. In the model, we seed BA.5-infected cases at a rate of 2 per million of the total population over 7 days to incorporate the initial transmission of BA.5 ( ). The relative transmissibility of BA.5, which represents transmission advantage of BA.5 over BA.2, was modeled as a constant factor capturing relative change in the time-varying transmission rate ( ).
The model was fitted using a Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) framework, performed with Metropolis-Hastings algorithm. We performed 5 chains with each running 200 000 iterations, with the first 140 000 as burn-in using random initial parameter values. Convergence was assessed by visually checking trace plots (Supplementary Figure 3) and calculating the Gelman-Rubin statistic and effective sample sizes across all estimated parameters. Sensitivity analyses tested assumptions on key parameters (Supplementary Tables 3 and 4) including VE, duration of immunity, and cross-protection between 2 subvariants ( ). All analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.4.1). Inference under MCMC was conducted with R package “lazymcmc” [ ]. Model evaluation was performed using R packages “coda” [ ] and “posterior.”

## Risk of Infection and Population Immunity Dynamics
We estimated cumulative infections by subvariant for each age group based on the inferred transmission dynamics. The impact of tightened PHSMs was evaluated by the relative decrease in provided that PHSMs were in place during early February and mid-April 2022. We also evaluated the contribution of PHSM relaxation on transmission by relative increase in after the 2-stage relaxation through April and 19 May 2022 to the end of the fifth wave ( ). Further, the variant-specific population immunity against infection (defined as the percentage of population who are immunized and protected against infection of a specific subvariant at time t) was computed by 3 types of immune status [ ], including (1) vaccination-induced immunity, (2) infection-induced immunity, and (3) hybrid immunity (both vaccination- and infection-induced immunity). The overall population immunity was then calculated as the weighted average of variant-specific immunity by the prevalence of each variant ( ).

## RESULTS

## COVID-19 Omicron Outbreaks in Hong Kong
Hong Kong experienced a major outbreak beginning in January 2022, triggered by a BA.2-associated superspreading event [ ]. While multiple variants of concerns including Delta, Omicron BA.1, and BA.2 were detected initially, BA.2.2 became dominant and drove the fifth wave until May 2022 [ ]. With the implementation of PHSMs and rising vaccine uptake in January and February, reported cases decreased to an average of 200 cases per day. In total, there were 162 000 cases per million persons confirmed in the fifth wave during January and May 2022 in HK.
On 19 April and 19 May 2022, respectively, the HK government implemented a 2-stage relaxation including school reopenings, extended restaurant “dine-in” services, and resumed public venue operations [ ]. Meanwhile, the DH public health laboratory consistently identified BA.4 and BA.5 subvariants from sampled imported cases during May 2022 and included local cases afterward. A subsequent BA.5-dominant outbreak peaked on 1 September 2022, with 3091 daily cases detected by RT-PCR. By late September, BA.5 was supplanted by co-circulating subvariants (BA.2.75 and BQ.1) amid evolving viral diversity [ , ].

## Transmission Dynamics of Omicron Outbreaks in Hong Kong
The model-expected COVID-19 reported case numbers matched the observed number throughout 2 waves in 2022 in HK ( C). Given the low vaccination coverage in early February 2022, with only 64% and 13% of the population having received primary doses and a booster dose, respectively ( D), the fifth wave infected 3.5 million (95% credible interval [CrI], 3–4.1 million) people as of late May 2022 by BA.2, corresponding to 47% (95% CrI, 41%–54%) of the HK population. The subsequent BA.5-dominant outbreak infected another 2.4 million (95% CrI, 1.9–3.0 million) people, corresponding to 33% (95% CrI, 26%–40%) of the total population. Among these, 30% (95% CrI, 25%–36%) were infected by the BA.5 subvariant, 13% (95% CrI, 9%–16%) were BA.5 reinfections after BA.2 infection, and the remaining 57% (95% CrI, 52%–64%) were BA.2 infected ( E and F, ).
 Model-estimated infection prevalence aligned with independent estimates from a prospective random cohort. We used the RAT detection probability after infection [ , ] to compute daily prevalence of SARS-CoV-2 RAT positivity ( G). The RAT-estimated daily prevalence peaked in early March 2022 with 7% (95% confidence interval (CI), 5%–11%) infection prevalence, which was consistent with our model estimate of 7% (95% CrI, 5%–9%). In contrast, during the BA.5 wave, a much lower peak RAT-estimated prevalence was 1.5% (95% CI, .6%–2.9%) in early September, in line with our model estimate of 1.8% (95% CrI, 1.2%–2.7%).
Reactive PHSMs—including quarantine mandates (8 February 2022), private gathering limits, vaccine pass (10 February 2022), and suspension of flights from high-risk regions (11 February 2022)—lowered the by 67% (95% CrI, 52%–78%) ( H) from 8 February to 19 April 2022. Subsequently, in the absence of population immunity, rebounded to a similar level observed in early February to 3.4 (95% CrI, 2.8–4.1) by the end of May, driven by the enhanced transmissibility of BA.5 subvariant and the 2-stage relaxation (19 April and 19 May 2022).

## Age-Specific Pattern of Transmission for Omicron BA.2 and BA.5 Subvariants
We inferred age-stratified infection dynamics for BA.2 and BA.5. The model accurately captured the epidemic trajectories across age groups ( ). Overall, during the BA.2-dominant fifth wave, 23% (95% CrI, 19%–27%) and 25% (95% CrI, 21%–30%) of all infections were ascertained by RT-PCR for adults and elderly adults. In contrast, we found a much lower ascertainment proportion of 10% (95% CrI, 8%–13%) by RT-PCR for children and adolescents. In addition, we estimated that the relative ascertainment proportions of RT-PCR for the sixth wave starting from 25 May 2022, compared to the maximum proportions during the fifth wave, were 0.25 (95% CrI, .18–.30), 0.24 (95% CrI, .17–.29), and 0.18 (95% CrI, .12–.23) for the 3 age groups, corresponding to a 75%–82% decrease in PCR ascertainment proportion during the sixth wave. This is consistent with the observation that during the sixth wave, a much smaller proportion of reported cases was attributed to PCR (35%) compared to 63% during the previous fifth wave [ , ].
Cumulative BA.2 infections in the fifth wave were highest in children and adolescents (54% [95% CrI, 49%–60%]), compared to 46% (95% CrI, 42%–54%) and 42% (95% CrI, 38%–47%) for adults and elderly adults, respectively. In contrast, during the sixth wave, cumulative infections of BA.5 were lower but more uniform: 16% (95% CrI, 12%–20%), 15% (95% CrI, 11%–20%), and 13% (95% CrI, 9%–18%) across the same age groups. We estimated transmissibility of BA.5 to be 25% (95% CrI, 15%–36%) higher than BA.2, consistent with its observed dominance.

## Changes in Population Immunity Against BA.2 and BA.5 infections
Hong Kong initiated the COVID-19 population vaccination program in February 2021. By the beginning of the fifth wave, only 64% of the population had received primary doses ( ). Prior to the sixth wave (late May 2022), primary vaccination uptake reached at 86%, with 54% of the population having received the booster dose. Accounting for people ever infected and immunity waning, we estimated that 15% of the HK population was protected against BA.2 infection at the onset, primarily through vaccination alone ( ). Population immunity against BA.2 infection rapidly rose from 8% (95% CrI, 5%–12%), 18% (95% CrI, 14%–23%), and 12% (95% CrI, 9%–16%) on 31 January 2022, to 60% (95% CrI, 55%–65%), 57% (95% CrI, 51%–62%), and 49% (95% CrI, 43%–55%) on 31 May 2022, across age groups from children and adolescents to elderly adults, respectively. Hybrid immunity (vaccination and infection) was the dominant contributor across all ages. Following the emergence of BA.5 in late May 2022, population immunity against BA.5 infection was 15%, 18%, 14%, and 13% lower than against BA.2 infection for all ages together, children and adolescents, adults, and elderly adults, respectively. Population immunity from hybrid immunity and infection gradually increased from June to September 2022, but remained below the May 2022 peak.
 Sensitivity analyses showed comparable trajectories in immunity and infection curves across parameter assumptions. However, cumulative infections and population immunity estimates varied, particularly under the pessimistic assumption, with a shorter duration of immunity, lower VE, and cross-immunity. For example, under the pessimistic assumption, population immunity against BA.5 infection declined by 28% relative to BA.2, versus <15% under baseline or optimistic assumptions ( and ).

## DISCUSSION
Using territory-wide epidemiological data and a multipathogen SEIR model, we analyzed the age-stratified transmission patterns of Omicron BA.2 and BA.5 in HK from January to September 2022. Our model reconstructed the COVID-19 immune landscape by sources of vaccination, natural infection, and hybrid immunity, and quantified the impact of PHSMs, transmission advantage of BA.5, and age-specific population immunity. Model-expected infection prevalence aligned with independent cohort-based prevalence estimates [ , ], supporting model validity.
Unlike most of the countries or regions with widespread transmission in 2020–2021, HK suppressed community transmission of SARS-CoV-2 until 2022, with <0.2% cumulative infections by early 2022 [ ]. In early 2022, the BA.2-driven fifth wave infected 47% of the population, disproportionately affecting children and adolescents aged 0–19 years (∼60% infected) due to a lower vaccination coverage prior to the fifth wave (< 40%). Despite rising immunity in the younger age group, cumulative infections were similar across age groups in the sixth wave, likely due to the reopening of schools [ ].
Subsequently, despite a dramatic increase in population vaccination uptake and a large proportion of the population that had been immunized with natural infection, another BA.5-dominant wave infected 35% of the HK population during June and September 2022, with nearly 30% of BA.5 cases being reinfected after BA.2. We found an increased transmissibility of BA.5 compared to the BA.2 subvariant, consistent with previous reports [ , ]. In addition, studies that involved individuals with different immune histories found that there are substantial differences in neutralizing antibody titers for the BA.1, BA.2, and BA.5 subvariants [ , ], indicating a strong immune evasion potential of BA.5. Our estimates were consistent with previous analyses adapting different mechanisms. For example, Lau et al carried out a statistical inference based on city-wide sewage and individual viral load from serosurveys, and estimated that overall 25% of infections during the fifth wave were detected by RT-PCR, resulting in a cumulative infection of 45% in HK [ ].
We estimated the variant-specific time-varying effective reproductive number ( ) and population immunity against infection. These estimates indicated rapid-evolving transmission dynamics that affected by PHSMs, increasing population immunity and virus evolution. At the beginning of our modeling (late January 2022), we identified a relatively higher of 3.3 for the Omicron BA.2 subvariant. Previous reviews found the pooled effective reproductive number of SARS-CoV-2 Omicron BA.1/BA.2 ranged between 2.4 and 5.1 [ , ]. Another phylodynamic analysis conducted in HK estimated an effective reproductive number of 2.7 (95% prediction interval, 1.8–3.7) at the end of January 2022 [ ], supplementing our estimates. We also found that , the time-varying effective reproductive number without population immunity, would have surged to levels observed back in early February 2022. This increase was driven by both the increased transmissibility of BA.5 subvariant and the 2-stage relaxation of PHSMs subsequently implemented in April and May 2022. In contrast, given over 80% primary vaccine and 55% booster coverage, together with 47% previous infection, the remained 1.5 at the beginning of the sixth wave. These findings highlighted the critical role of PHSMs in suppressing transmission when immunity is low [ , ]. The BA.5 surge in a highly vaccinated population further highlights how immune-evading variants exploit reduced cross-protection from prior infections [ ], suggesting that high vaccine-induced immunity alone may inadvertently favor immune-evading variants during co-circulation.
Our modeling analysis has several limitations. First, the estimation of population did not account for individual-level heterogeneity in neutralizing antibody, which may influence the durability and magnitude of protection against reinfection [ , ]. Second, while genomic surveillance indicated that BA.2.2 dominated the fifth wave (>90%), minor other sublineages including BA.2.10 and BA.1 were also present [ ]. We modeled BA.2 as a single entity due to low prevalence of other sublineages, potentially overestimating BA.2-specific cumulative infections. Third, assumptions regarding the crude cross-protection between Omicron BA.2 and BA.5 in unvaccinated individuals were informed by sensitivity analyses instead of empirical data, though our baseline assumption of 60%–90% protection in vaccinated individuals aligns with previous evidence [ ]. In addition, we assumed that cross-protection, initial VE, and rates of waning of immunity are same for all age groups. We acknowledge that this is a strong simplifying assumption, as there is evidence suggesting that vaccine-induced immunity is lower and wanes more quickly for adults aged >65 years than for younger age groups [ , ]. The lack of direct measures on immunity waning for each age group and subvariant limits our assumptions to account for varying waning patterns across these factors. While we do not model age- or subvariant-specific waning rates, we conducted sensitivity analyses using shorter and longer overall durations of immunity to evaluate their effects on the model estimates. Fourth, we did not consider individuals who were immunized with heterologous booster dose (ie, the booster dose vaccine is different from the primary doses) [ ], changes in the recommendation of the duration between doses [ ], or potential of prior infections [ ], and thus the booster VE was derived by assuming that the primary vaccine type was the same type as the booster. Therefore, our estimates on vaccination-induced immunity may be slightly underestimated according to a study [ ] that indicated a higher VE for 3 doses of BNT162b2 compared to a 2-dose CoronaVac plus BNT162b2 regimen. Further, we acknowledge that the weighted average approach used to compute VE in our model may mask differential risks in subpopulations. While this approach was adopted to balance simplicity with realism, aligning with methods employed in previous studies [ , ], we emphasize it was also necessary to avoid prohibitive computational complexity. As a sensitivity analysis, we refit the model assuming a reasonable but substantially lower VE for the elderly age group and found marginal effects on our key estimates ( ). Finally, while there were different PHSMs implemented in HK, we only considered their collective effects in our models and did not model their individual effects, and we did not have detailed epidemiolocal data such as the place of infections.
In conclusion, we demonstrate that Omicron BA.5 has enhanced transmissibility and significant immune evasion capabilities. Our findings reveal a dynamic shift in transmission control factors throughout the epidemic: PHSMs were crucial early in the outbreak when population immunity was minimal, while acquired immunity became the dominant constraint later, keeping effective transmission low despite PHSM relaxation. This transition from PHSM-dependent to immunity-dependent control was driven by substantial accumulated protection from both infections and vaccination during the fifth wave. With hybrid immunity now predominating in the HK population, it will likely continue to be the primary moderator of transmission, though immune-evasive variants remain a concern. These findings underscore the dynamic interplay between population immunity, PHSMs, and variant characteristics in shaping epidemic trajectories.
