## Abstract
To address the structural imbalances and sustainability challenges faced by the sports industry in its high-quality development transformation, and to overcome the shortcomings of existing research in dynamic modeling, multi-objective optimization, and quantitative solutions, this paper constructs an intelligent optimization framework integrating deep learning (DL) and genetic algorithms (GA). This framework uses a one-dimensional convolutional neural network to extract deep features and predict trends from high-dimensional industry data, and employs a genetic algorithm as the optimization engine to find the Pareto optimal solution that synergistically improves economic, social, and environmental benefits. Based on industry statistics from 2010 to 2022 and provincial panel data from 2018 to 2020, the study reveals the core characteristics of the sports industry’s transformation towards service orientation and verifies the impact of the pandemic, which resulted in a 7.2% and 4.6% decrease in total industry output and added value in 2020 compared to 2019, respectively. After adaptation and validation with data from the United States and Germany, the model’s cross-regional prediction error is less than 6%, and the innovation-driven path can increase the proportion of sports services to 68.5% by 2025. This paper breaks through the traditional static description paradigm and provides an intelligent decision-making tool that combines theoretical depth and practical value, offering a new paradigm and empirical support for the precise optimization of industrial structure and cross-regional application.

## Introduction
Global trends demonstrate that upon reaching sufficient economic maturity, nations frequently position their sports sector as an economic pillar. As an emerging industry, sports consistently generates substantial employment opportunities while catalyzing growth in auxiliary enterprises and delivering broad societal impact. Although China’s sports industry exhibits promising development trajectories, its foundational capacity remains underdeveloped. This context renders critical the dual imperative of comprehensively mapping current sports event ecosystems and implementing evidence-based structural optimizations.
While existing research has provided detailed descriptive analyses of the scale, growth rate, and macroeconomic policy environment of China’s sports industry, significant gaps remain in dynamic modeling and structural optimization. Traditional econometric methods often struggle to capture the complex nonlinear feedback and emergent characteristics within industrial systems, while isolated AI application research lacks deep coupling with sustainable development theory. Therefore, this paper aims to bridge this research gap by constructing an intelligent analysis framework that deeply integrates deep learning and genetic algorithms to answer the following core question: How can machine learning techniques be used to dynamically optimize the internal structure of the sports industry to synergistically maximize economic, social, and ecological benefits? The motivation for this research stems from the urgent need for interdisciplinary integration : embedding the predictive and optimization capabilities of artificial intelligence into the robust framework of industrial economics and sustainable development theory, thereby driving the transformation of sports industry research from a “static description” to a “dynamic simulation and intelligent decision-making” paradigm. To address this deficiency, this research aims to construct an intelligent analysis framework integrating deep learning and genetic algorithms to deeply deconstruct the structure of China’s sports industry, simulate its dynamic evolution, and explore optimization paths. Deep learning models, especially convolutional neural networks (CNNs), excel at automatically extracting deep features and spatially correlated patterns from high-dimensional unstructured industrial data. Genetic algorithms (GAs), with their powerful global search and multi-objective optimization capabilities, can effectively solve complex combinatorial optimization problems in industrial restructuring. The organic combination of these two approaches provides a novel methodological perspective for addressing the technical bottlenecks in the optimization of the sports industry structure.
However, as far as the structure of the sports industry is concerned, due to the lack of relevant experience accumulation, there is also a lack of macro evaluation of its current situation. Based on this, this paper will sort out the relevant theories on how to optimize the structure of my country’s sports industry from the overall level and reasonably and completely evaluate the real situation of Chengdu’s sports industry structure combined with the actual situation. In addition, the relevant influencing factors and future development will be analyzed and its shortcomings will be discussed. Then it will give corresponding solutions to optimize its structure so as to lay a theoretical foundation for the government to implement the relevant strategies of sports development.
In summary, artificial intelligence (AI) has demonstrated enormous potential in industrial modeling and economic forecasting, while research on the sustainable development of the sports industry increasingly emphasizes systematicness and synergy. However, existing literature suffers from two key limitations: first, AI applications are mostly concentrated in manufacturing or finance, with few studies systematically applying it to the complex structural optimization of the sports service industry; second, sustainability research in the sports industry remains primarily qualitative analysis and macro-indicator evaluation, lacking computational models capable of integrating multi-dimensional data, simulating dynamic evolution, and providing quantitative optimization solutions. This paper addresses this challenge by innovatively integrating the feature learning capabilities of deep learning with the global optimization capabilities of genetic algorithms to construct an intelligent decision support system specifically for the sustainable structural configuration of the sports industry. This not only expands the application boundaries of AI in the service economy but also provides a novel methodological tool for the refined and intelligent governance of the sports industry.

## Related work
Recent scholarly discourse has explored the integration of deep learning (DL) and genetic algorithms (GAs) across diverse domains. Matthew implemented stochastic gradient descent alongside dropout regularization to optimize deep architectures, targeting minimization of out-of-sample prediction error (MSE) for spatiotemporal modeling. This framework successfully captured abrupt traffic flow discontinuities and generated classification rules for forecasting short-term futures prices using order book depth data .Rui provided a comprehensive review of DL applications in machine health monitoring, analyzing Autoencoders and derivatives, Restricted Boltzmann Machines (including Deep Belief and Boltzmann Networks), Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs). Experimental validation confirmed the efficacy of these approaches . Yi advanced spatial-temporal forecasting for shared bicycle systems using convolutional LSTM (conv-LSTM) networks, predicting bike availability, distribution uniformity, and usage patterns at specific daily intervals. Results demonstrated conv-LSTM’s superior capability in modeling spatiotemporal correlations compared to standard LSTM .Bentkamp rigorously formalized mathematical proofs of DL expressiveness via Isabelle/HOL, addressing HOL type system limitations through proof generalization. This effort yielded reusable formal libraries for matrix rank, Borel measures, multivariate polynomials, and tensor analysis . Wang introduced a novel stealth black-box attack model leveraging DL for non-intrusive load monitoring via smart meter data, exposing critical vulnerabilities in machine learning systems within smart grids and highlighting significant risks to energy analytics and decision-making .In economic modeling, Dawid applied genetic algorithms to simulate learning behaviors of boundedly rational agents, comparing two cobweb model variants: one involving production quantity decisions alone, and another incorporating market entry/exit choices prior to quantity determination. Experimental outcomes revealed markedly distinct learning dynamics between these scenarios . Iqbal optimized load dispatch using GAs with real-time pricing signals (RTP) to enhance grid stability and energy efficiency. By categorizing appliances based on energy demands, operational constraints, and duty cycles, the approach reduced total energy costs and peak-to-average ratios, establishing GA as an effective solution for residential load scheduling .Although deep learning and genetic algorithms have shown great potential in many fields, research on their application to the optimization of the sports industry structure is still lacking. Existing literature on the discussion of the sports industry structure focuses on traditional input-output analysis or decomposition based on added value. Although these methods can characterize static associations, they are difficult to deal with complex nonlinear dynamics and multi-objective optimization problems in the industrial structure. In recent years, some scholars have tried to use machine learning for sports economic forecasting, but their focus is on macro-output value rather than deep deconstruction and path planning of micro-structures.
In summary, despite the fruitful achievements of deep learning and genetic algorithms in their respective fields, research on their collaborative application to optimize the structure of the sports industry remains elusive. Existing literature either focuses on macro-level descriptions of industrial policy, lacking empirical support driven by micro-data; or is limited to the application of a single technology, failing to establish a complete analytical chain from feature identification to dynamic prediction to structural optimization. In particular, there is a lack of systematic exploration of the deep integration of artificial intelligence with sports economics and sustainable development theory. This study, conducted within this context, seeks to bridge this gap in technological application by constructing a CNN-GA fusion model. Furthermore, it aims to provide a decision-making support tool with both theoretical depth and practical value for the high-quality, sustainable development of the sports industry. Of course, this study also has limitations, such as the model’s high dependence on data quality and the need to verify its cross-regional applicability.

## Deep learning and genetic algorithms
The methodology can be divided into three stages: First, 12 basic indicators derived from the “China Sports Industry Statistical Yearbook” are standardized and preprocessed. Second, the preprocessed data is input into a one-dimensional convolutional neural network (1D-CNN), which utilizes its local perception and weight sharing characteristics to automatically extract deep temporal features of the industry’s operational status and make high-precision predictions of future development trends. Finally, the CNN’s prediction output is used as the input to the fitness function to drive a genetic algorithm (GA) to iteratively search within a predefined structural configuration space. GA continuously generates and evaluates new industry combination schemes by simulating the biological evolution process of “selection, crossover, and mutation,” ultimately converging to a Pareto optimal solution set that maximizes comprehensive sustainable development benefits.

## Deep learning
Deep learning fundamentally emulates the layered abstraction of the mammalian neocortex, progressively deriving representations from raw input data (images, videos, audio, text) through self-supervised mechanisms to accomplish target objectives. As a prominent artificial intelligence subfield contrasting with shallow learning, this paradigm shift builds upon earlier neural networks using Backpropagation (BP) algorithms. Shallow models apply statistical methods to identify feature patterns from extensive training datasets for predictive tasks, yet their single hidden layer imposes critical limitations: (1)Difficulty optimizing parameters for complex problems.(2)Inadequate performance with constrained samples or computational resources.(3)Poor generalization capabilities.These constraints led to diminished relevance.Deep architectures, typically containing five or more hidden layers, overcome such limitations by learning increasingly sophisticated feature hierarchies. Each layer computes linear combinations of input features, with connection weights determining feature significance in these transformations. Model capacity scales exponentially with network depth , markedly enhancing recognition precision. Figure illustrates the associated computational workflow.

The efficacy of these operations critically determines overall model performance . Unlike traditional feature extraction, deep learning simplifies this process: data merely requires transformation into model-compatible formats, whereupon iterative training automatically performs both feature derivation and generalization.Model evaluation necessitates task-specific metrics; appropriate selection enables scientifically valid assessment and optimization. Fundamental deep learning architectures include Deep Belief Networks (DBN), Stacked Autoencoders (SAE), Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN). CNNs constitute a feedforward architecture employing convolutional operations and deep layered structures to learn grid-based features (e.g., pixels, audio) through convolutional layers. By progressively combining localized feature representations into higher-level abstractions, CNNs effectively process spatial hierarchies and are widely applied in computer vision and natural language processing domains.

## Characteristics of convolutional neural networks
When a neural network processes an image, its input is often high-dimensional. A traditional neural network connects the neurons in the next layer to all the neurons in the previous layer. Such a connection method is usually called full connection, in which the number of parameters in the network will increase exponentially with the deepening of the network width and depth. In this way, when actually training the network model, the requirements for computer hardware resources and computing power are relatively high, and the training is relatively slow. Therefore, in response to this phenomenon, the convolutional neural network adopts the method of connecting each neuron to some neurons in the previous layer, which is called local connection. This is actually the size of the neuron mapped to the input image matrix space .
Convolutional layers implement local connectivity by restricting each neuron’s inputs to a spatially constrained region of the preceding layer, forming localized receptive fields. As illustrated in Fig. , neurons in layer m with receptive field size 3 connect exclusively to three corresponding neurons in layer m-1, while layer m + 1 maintains similar localized connectivity. This biologically-inspired architecture emulates visual cortex organization where neurons respond selectively to specific subregions of the visual field. The constrained connectivity significantly reduces parameter counts while enabling localized feature detection. Crucially, hierarchical stacking progressively expands receptive fields relative to the input layer, with each neuron remaining invariant to stimuli beyond its designated region. Through successive layers, this mechanism evolves from local feature extraction to global pattern recognition, transforming initial spatial filtering into comprehensive hierarchical representations while maintaining computational efficiency.
 Compared with the feedforward neural network, the convolutional neural network has the characteristics of weight sharing in addition to the advantages of local connection. The schematic diagram of weight sharing is shown in Fig. . Assuming that there are 5 neurons in the m-1th layer, convolve the features of the m-1th layer to obtain the output feature map of the mth layer with 3 units in total. Although each neural unit in the mth layer is connected with 3 neurons in the m-1th layer, the weight parameters of the same set of convolution operations are the same. After weight sharing, the 9 parameters can be reduced to 3, and the same arrows in the figure are the same weight parameters.
 Convolutional neural networks achieve significant computational efficiency through parameter sharing across network layers. This architectural principle enables multiple neurons to utilize identical convolution kernels when processing distinct spatial regions within input data. The resulting weight sharing mechanism fundamentally reduces both learnable parameters and computational load during training.Mathematically, this approach ensures that specific feature detectors (convolution kernels) are systematically applied across all spatial positions. Consequently, CNNs develop translation-equivariant representations - detecting identical features irrespective of their positional coordinates within the input space. This property confers critical translation invariance: recognition capabilities remain consistent despite spatial shifts in input patterns, while feature extraction robustness persists under affine transformations.

## Genetic algorithms
American psychologists quoted the evolutionary laws of organisms in the random search algorithm (that is, the population that is suitable for a certain condition can continue to survive and live; the population that is not suitable for the specific condition will be eliminated), and this algorithm is called genetic algorithm. In the traditional genetic algorithm, genes are represented by the parameters of the problem, chromosomes are represented by the solution of the problem, and different chromosomes can form a group. According to the principle of biological evolution, the fittest survives and the unfit is eliminated, this group can produce the best offspring group, and the optimal solution to the problem is this best offspring group. The genetic algorithm has the following characteristics. When searching the entire population, the search speed is relatively fast and the search time is relatively short. It can be automatically obtained in the search space. In theory, there are no restrictions on the derivation and continuity of functions. It does not require building complex search rules.

## Key elements of genetic algorithms

## Chromosome decoding and encoding
The initial phase in adapting problems for genetic algorithms involves transitioning from the universal problem space to an algorithmically tractable search space through ​encoding​ - a critical preparatory step. ​Decoding​ constitutes the inverse transformation, converting solutions back from the search space to the original problem domain. Encoding methodology substantially governs population evolution efficiency and convergence behavior . Contemporary approaches to chromosome representation include: General genetic algorithms use binary coding, that is, individual binary symbols {0,1} composed of 0 and 1. Groups of individuals consisting of other genotypes are also available as a binary coded string. Its length is closely related to the accuracy of solving the problem.

## Fitness function design
The fitness function can reflect the quality of individuals in the population. This is also the main basis for selecting parental individuals. In the process of problem solving, it must first establish a fitness function according to the requirements of the problem. It then evaluates it and draws the probability of selecting an individual for the next step. Since the value of the objective function can be positive or negative, the relationship between it and the fitness function is not unique. Therefore, in order to maximize the objective function, the direction of the objective function should be consistent with the direction of the fitness value change.

## Genetic manipulation design
Genetic algorithms and genetic operations of biological genes are roughly the same. It’s all randomly selected in a certain way. The task of genetic manipulation is to realize the evolution process of survival of the fittest and elimination of the unfit by evaluating the fitness function and then performing specific operations. It generally includes operations such as selection, crossover, and mutation. Selection refers to the selection of individual copies with high fitness according to the selection probability. Crossover means that two randomly paired chromosomes exchange part of their genes according to the crossover probability to generate two new chromosomes. Mutation refers to changing the loci in the chromosome with a certain probability to generate a new population.

## Genetic algorithm parameter settings
The termination condition, that is, the maximum value of the evolutionary algebra, generally ranges from 100 to 500. The number of populations, generally ranging from 20 to 100. The crossover probability is used to determine the probability of the application of the crossover operator, and its value is generally set to 0.40 ~ 0.99. The mutation probability is used to determine the probability of the mutation operator application, and its value is generally set to 0.001 ~ 0.1.

## Basic steps of genetic algorithm
Figure shows the basic steps of the genetic algorithm. It first randomly generates the initial population, and then calculates the individual fitness F of the population. It then selects excellent individuals X and Y according to F, and then performs crossover and mutation operations on X and Y. It finally judges whether it is the same as the termination condition. If it is the same then it ends, otherwise continue to the second step. But for many complex practical problems, it is necessary to analyze the problem to be solved in detail firstly, model and analyze the problem to be solved, set the constraints and solve the goal and so on. The genetic algorithm is just an algorithm framework. For specific problems, it has to design a specific genetic operator on the basis of the original genetic operation. For example, the selection operator that retains the optimal chromosome in the population, etc. It improves the global optimization performance and convergence speed of the algorithm by designing corresponding local search operators.
 To effectively apply the aforementioned theoretical model to the analysis of China’s sports industry structure, this study developed a rigorous methodological process. First, the dataset was derived from the 2010–2022 “China Sports Industry Statistical Yearbook,” published by the National Bureau of Statistics and the General Administration of Sport. It covers 12 core indicators, including annual output value, employment, number of enterprises, and R&D investment, for 28 sub-sectors within the three broad categories of sports manufacturing, sports services, and sporting goods sales. The timeframe was chosen to cover the three key stages before, during, and after the pandemic to capture the dynamic evolution of the industry structure. Second, a CNN model was used to process this multidimensional time series data. Using a one-dimensional convolutional kernel, it automatically learns the spatial correlations and temporal dependencies between indicators across various industries, generating high-order feature vectors that serve as a deep representation of the industry’s structural state. Based on this, this paper formalizes the industrial structure optimization problem as a multi-objective optimization problem, with the objective functions including maximizing total industry output, minimizing the regional development imbalance index, and maximizing the green and low-carbon transition index. A genetic algorithm (GA) was introduced to solve this problem, with its chromosome encoding representing the resource allocation weight of each sub-sector in the future industrial structure .The GA population size was set to 100, the crossover probability to 0.8, and the mutation probability to 0.05. These parameters were fine-tuned on the validation set using grid search and cross-validation to ensure model convergence and generalization. Finally, the robustness of the model was tested using bootstrap resampling and Monte Carlo simulation to assess the confidence intervals of the prediction results.
The datasets from 2010 to 2022 were split in a standard 7:2:1 ratio during the CNN training process: 70% of the samples were used for model training to learn the spatiotemporal correlation features of the sports industry structure, industry dynamics, and dependencies between indicators; 20% of the samples served as a validation set to fine-tune the CNN’s hyperparameters, such as convolutional kernel parameters and learning rate, while also assisting in optimizing parameters such as population size and crossover probability of the genetic algorithm; and the remaining 10% of the samples served as a test set to evaluate the CNN’s prediction accuracy.

## Discussion
The significant changes in the internal structure of the sports industry in 2020 were closely linked to the macroeconomic environment and specific policy interventions. Specifically, the decline in the added value of the sports venue service industry can be directly attributed to the restrictions on large gatherings implemented to combat the COVID-19 pandemic, which temporarily disrupted venue operations. Conversely, the counter-cyclical growth of fitness and leisure sports activities was driven by the national health authorities’ advocacy of personal fitness and the promotion of home fitness models, demonstrating the critical role of policy in guiding consumer habits and fostering industry resilience. Therefore, these changes are not isolated fluctuations in figures, but rather the result of a combination of external economic shocks and industrial policies.
This study validates the status of the sports service industry as a core engine and provides concrete solutions for industrial restructuring through quantitative simulation, responding to and expanding upon the assertion of the servitization trend in the sports industry. Based on the CNN-GA model, the study clearly elucidates that innovation-driven development is the best path to achieve high-quality development, providing a scientific basis for policymakers. Governments can use the industry weights derived from the model to precisely guide high-potential areas; enterprises can optimize their industrial chain positioning and investment strategies. However, the model relies on the accuracy of official statistical data, does not fully consider micro-data such as consumer behavior, and its cross-border applicability needs further verification. In the future, social media data can be integrated and extended to countries along the Belt and Road Initiative to construct a global sports industry analysis model. To test the model’s cross-cultural applicability and robustness, this paper uses sports-related industry data released by the U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis from 2015 to 2020 to conduct a validation test on the CNN-GA framework. Although there are differences in the sports industry classification systems of China and the United States, after mapping the data from various countries to the 12 core dimensions defined in this model, it is found that the model can effectively capture the common characteristics of their structural evolution. The model successfully replicated the steady increase in the service sector’s share in Germany during this period (an average of + 1.8% per year) and made a reasonable prediction of the industrial contraction in the United States in 2020 due to the pandemic (error rate < 5.0%). This demonstrates that the framework has certain potential for cross-border application after appropriate data adaptation.

## Conclusion
This study aims to fill the core knowledge gaps in existing literature on sports industry structure optimization, namely, the limitations of methodology and the inadequacy of predictive capabilities. The study innovatively constructs and validates an intelligent analysis framework integrating Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) and Genetic Algorithms (GA). This framework not only successfully analyzes the inherent interrelationships and dynamic evolution of China’s sports industry structure, but also provides three quantifiable high-quality sustainable development paths through multi-objective optimization simulation, verifying the superiority of the innovation-driven approach. This paper successfully constructs an intelligent structural optimization framework for the sustainable development of China’s sports industry by integrating deep learning and genetic algorithms. The core scientific contribution of this framework lies in its first systematic application of AI’s predictive and optimization capabilities to the complex system modeling of the sports service industry, providing a computationally achievable and verifiable new paradigm for solving the problem of internal structural imbalances within the industry. At the practical level, this model provides governments and industry associations with a dynamic and precise decision support tool, effectively assisting them in scientifically formulating resource allocation and industry support policies under multiple objective constraints, and promoting the sports industry towards a new stage of high-quality and sustainable development.
