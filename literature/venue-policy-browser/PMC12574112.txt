## Background
A series of intervention strategies have been successively implemented to prevent HIV among men who have sex with men (MSM) in Shenzhen. This study aimed to examine HIV yield and assess the impact of different intervention strategies on HIV prevention in Shenzhen.

## Methods
A serial cross-sectional study was conducted in Shenzhen from 2009 to 2021 among MSM. The temporal trends of HIV yield and HIV risk behaviours were assessed using the Joinpoint Regression model. The effectiveness of the various interventions was evaluated using interrupted time series (ITS) analysis. Multilevel logistic regression was employed to evaluate the changes in the HIV epidemic and HIV risk behaviours across different periods of intervention.

## Results
The overall HIV yield was 9.8% among MSM in Shenzhen from 2009 to 2021. The HIV yield decreased rapidly after the comprehensive implementation of venue-based differentiated behavioural interventions, from 14.74% in 2013 to 10.69% in 2016 (SPC=-3.80%, 95%CI: -7.10 to-0.40). Since the implementation of immediate cART and PEP/PrEP promotion, the HIV yield further decreased from 9.51% in 2017 to 4.08% in 2021 (SPC=-9.50%, 95%CI: -12.80 to -6.20%). The results of the ITS models revealed that, compared with an increase of 0.57% per quarter in HIV yield during the preintervention period, a significant decrease of 0.28% per quarter was observed after the implementation of venue-based differentiated behavioural interventions. During the implementation of immediate combined antiretroviral treatment and pre- and postexposure prophylaxis promotion, the trend of HIV yield did not change significantly. HIV risk behaviours such as unprotected anal intercourse, drug abuse, and having sex with both men and women all showed a downwards trend.

## Conclusions
The yield of HIV among MSM in Shenzhen has declined since 2012 and was lower than the national average from 2020. Although this is the era of pharmacological interventions, adherence to precisely tailored behavioural interventions for controlling HIV yield in MSM has continued to yield significant benefits.

## Introduction
 Human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection is a global public health challenge, with an estimated 39 million people living with HIV/AIDS (PLWH) by the end of 2022. In China, the number of PLWH had reached approximately 1.22 million by the end of 2022, with 107,000 newly reported cases that year. Among these new infections, 23.0% were attributed to male-to-male sexual transmission, and 72.0% were attributed to heterosexual transmission [ ]. Furthermore, 78% of newly reported cases in 2022 were among men, whereas another study suggested that 14.3% of men self-reporting heterosexual transmission had engaged in sexual contact with men who have sex with men (MSM) [ , ]. Furthermore, the prevalence of HIV among MSM in China increased from 1.4% in 2005 to 8.0% in 2015 [ ]. Therefore, curbing male-to-male sexual transmission remains the primary task and a major challenge in controlling the spread of HIV in China. Although the increasing trend in HIV prevalence among MSM has been contained in recent years, it is still greater than that reported in other high-risk populations.
Expanded HIV testing and condom promotion are the most important behavioural interventions with significant effects on HIV prevention in MSM. A meta-analysis confirmed that voluntary HIV counselling and testing have protective effects on sexual risk behaviours [ ]. Another meta-analysis suggested a significant increase in condom use among newly diagnosed HIV-infected individuals after receiving HIV testing services, which could be effective in reducing HIV transmission [ ]. However, a previous study suggested that behavioural interventions would become less effective in containing HIV in MSM after a long period of implementation [ ]. Therefore, additional intervention strategies are needed to augment combination strategies for curbing the HIV epidemic among MSM. The key to HIV infection control and prevention is disrupting the transmission of the virus from the source of infection. Since 2016, the Chinese government has implemented immediate combined antiretroviral treatment (cART) to suppress the viral load of PLWH to achieve the Undetectable = Untransmittable (U = U) [ ], which indicates that the virus cannot be transmitted to others. However, findings regarding whether effective HIV therapy and the consequent decrease in “community viral load” reduce the incidence of HIV have been mixed across studies. Expanded access to HIV testing and publicly funded cART, within the context of province-wide treatment as a prevention strategy, has resulted in a decline in new HIV diagnoses among MSM in British Columbia [ ]. However, the results of this strategy have not been as significant in Africa, where the incidence of HIV remains high despite increasing rates of HIV treatment in the region [ ].
In addition, the WHO has recommended that postexposure prophylaxis (PEP) and preexposure prophylaxis (PrEP) should be incorporated into prevention programs along with HIV testing, condom use, and the screening and treatment of sexually transmitted infections (STIs). Providing effective PEP/PrEP services for high-incidence groups is a critical measure to reduce HIV transmission [ ]. As of July 2022, 84 countries had approved PrEP for HIV prevention. The number of new HIV diagnoses among MSM decreased in some countries after PrEP use was introduced, reflecting the importance of approving PrEP use worldwide [ ]. Previous cost-effectiveness studies have suggested that promoting PEP/PrEP among MSM is a highly effective intervention strategy in China [ , ]. However, other studies have indicated that PEP/PrEP may cause side effects and risk compensation, which refers to an inverse increase in STIs incidence due to behavioural disinhibition [ , ]. Therefore, we should be more cautious when re-evaluating the effect of PEP/PrEP promotion. Furthermore, a previous study reported significant declines in condom use and PrEP adherence among MSM in China [ ], and a U.S. study revealed that approximately 52.6% of PrEP users discontinued their use during the COVID-19 pandemic [ ]. The COVID-19 pandemic has significantly impacted the promotion strategy of PEP/PrEP, not only by acting as a major obstacle but also by diverting crucial public health resources away from HIV prevention and control efforts [ ]. Consequently, this shift has profoundly affected the implementation of HIV prevention-related health services.
As a megacity located in southern China, Shenzhen is one of the most economically developed cities in China. Moreover, Shenzhen is a young and vibrant city, attracting many young migrants from all over the country each year. Consequently, to some extent, the HIV epidemic trend observed in Shenzhen may be representative of and indicative of trends among MSM in China [ ]. In Shenzhen, where approximately 80% of MSM are migrants, this population demonstrates limited awareness of and access to HIV-related care and prevention services due to relatively lower educational levels and sexual health literacy [ ], which makes them less accessible through conventional interventions and thereby diminishes intervention effectiveness. Surveillance data has demonstrated that the proportion of MSM among newly reported HIV cases increased nearly threefold over the past decade in Shenzhen, from 27.6% in 2009 to 65.4% in 2021. Since 2012, the Shenzhen Center for Disease Control and Prevention (SZCDC) has implemented venue-based differentiated behavioural interventions by collaborating with the operators of all MSM venues in Shenzhen [ ]. The Chinese government introduced an immediate cART policy regardless of the CD4 + T-cell count in 2016, whereby all PLWH became eligible to receive free cART upon diagnosis. Subsequently, PEP/PrEP access in China expanded in 2019. Although the average utilization rate of PEP/PrEP among MSM in China remains less than 1% [ ], the rate in Shenzhen has already reached 8–10%, far exceeding the national average. These three intervention strategies currently constitute the primary HIV prevention strategies for MSM citywide in Shenzhen. Despite the significant attention given to HIV prevention and intervention among MSM over the past decade, little information exists regarding the effectiveness of combined HIV interventions in reducing the HIV epidemic among MSM. Therefore, this study aimed to describe trends in HIV yield and HIV-related risk behaviours among MSM in Shenzhen from 2009 to 9 to 2021 and to assess the potential associations between these three key intervention strategies and HIV yield.

## Methods

## Data collection
This study was designed as a serial cross-sectional study conducted in Shenzhen from 2009 to 2021 among MSM. The participants were recruited via time‒location sampling (TLS) and respondent-driven sampling (RDS). The details of the application of TLS and RDS for MSM sampling have been described previously [ ]. The eligibility criteria for study enrolment included the following: (1) being biologically male; (2) being aged 16–60 years; (3) having reported anal or oral sex with a male partner in the past; and (4) not being a money boy.
All potentially eligible subjects were invited to participate in the survey and were required to complete an anonymous computer-assisted questionnaire as well as take an HIV test. The questionnaire collected information on sociodemographic and HIV-related risk behaviours. The validity of the questionnaire used in this study has been confirmed previously [ ]. After completing a questionnaire, each participant was required to provide a blood sample. All blood samples were sent to a laboratory for HIV testing. Each participant was identified using mobile number and fingerprint ID to avoid duplicate investigations within a year. Informed consent was obtained from all participants.

## Definition and measure

## HIV yield
 . Since previously diagnosed HIV-positive MSM were underrepresented in this study, most participants were either HIV-negative or testing-naïve. Cross-referencing with the Chinese National Notifiable Disease Reporting System revealed that 86% of the HIV-positive patients were newly diagnosed. This high proportion of new diagnoses suggests that the HIV yield closely reflects the HIV incidence, making it a reliable metric for assessing the effectiveness of intervention.

## Unprotected anal intercourse (UAI)
UAI was defined as having never or sometimes used condoms in anal intercourse with a male in the past 6 months.

## Men who have sex with men and women (MSMW)
MSMW was defined as the respondents having sex with males and females.

## Drug abuse
The questionnaire also assessed respondents’ drug abuse behaviour in the past, which involved 9 types of drugs (heroin, marijuana, cocaine, methamphetamine, ketamine, lysergic acid diethylamide, MDMA, flunitrazepam, and cough drops (abuse)).

## STIs
For each item in a list of 8 sexually transmitted diseases (syphilis, condyloma acuminatum, genital herpes, gonorrhoea, genital chlamydia trachomatis, urethritis, pubic lice, hepatitis C), participants were asked if they had ever been diagnosed in the past 12 months. A dichotomous variable was calculated to represent infection of at least one STI in the past 12 months (“yes” or “no”).

## HIV testing
The participants were asked whether they had ever been tested for HIV. Those who had been tested were then asked if they had been tested in the past 12 months.

## Multiple anal sexual partners
The number of sexual partners ≥ 6 in the past 6 months.

## Intervention strategy and implementation time
Intervention-1 (venue-based differentiated behavioural interventions (VBDB interventions)): VBDB interventions represent a differentiated intervention strategy tailored to MSM venues. This strategy consists of three elements. First, the CDC promoted a CDC-MSM venue joint marketing model, which supported MSM venue promotion and trained venue operators to disseminate HIV knowledge, promote condoms, and expand HIV testing services [ ]. Second, to reduce the yield of HIV-risk behaviours among MSM in venues, the CDC conducted environmental epidemiological surveys of MSM venues and modified venue-specific factors contributing to HIV-risk behaviours among MSM (e.g., room layout, lighting conditions, etc.), and provided different lubricant and condoms service packages on the basis of venue characteristics and customer preferences to improve condom accessibility in venues. Third, the CDC conducted epidemiological surveys among venue clients and developed targeted interventions tailored to the demographic and behavioural characteristics of MSM frequenting different venues. In brief, by collaborating with venue operators, the SZCDC provides individualized public health intervention strategies on the basis of the venue environment and the HIV risk behavioural profiles of the customers. The strategy was initially piloted in selected venues in 2012 and then progressively extended to all MSM dating venues citywide in Shenzhen since 2013.
Intervention-2 (Immediate cART): China entered the era of immediate cART, regardless of the CD4 + T-cell count, in 2016. In Shenzhen, immediate cART was formally implemented in October 2016.
Intervention-3 (PEP/PrEP promotion plus the COVID-19 era): PEP/PrEP was promoted in 2019. Moreover, in the early years of the 2020 s, the COVID-19 pandemic significantly disrupted HIV prevention and control efforts.

## Statistical analysis
Descriptive statistics and chi-square tests were used to compare sociodemographic and behavioural data across all participants. The semiannual HIV yield and HIV-related risk behaviours (January–June is the first half of a year, whereas July –December is the second half) were calculated. The joinpoint regression program (version 5.0) was employed to assess the temporal trends in HIV yield and HIV risk behaviours from 2009 to 2021. The Bayesian information criterion (BIC) was applied to select the optimal model in joinpoint regression. The semiannual percentage change (SPC) represents the estimated semiannual percentage change in the outcomes of the interest half-year, assuming a constant rate of change relative to the previous period.
Interrupted time series (ITS) analysis was employed to evaluate the effectiveness of population-level health interventions implemented at a clearly defined time point. The HIV yield was calculated per quarter (3 months), with the average annual yield used to fill in missing data for specific quarters (first quarters of 2010 and 2015). The study period (2009–2021) was divided into four segments: preintervention (from the second quarter of 2009–2012), intervention-1 (from 2013 to the third quarter of 2016), intervention-2 (from the fourth quarter of 2016 to − 2019) and intervention-3 (from 2020 to 2021). All analyses were performed using R (version 4.3.0).
The following model was established for ITS analysis:
Yt = β0 + β1*time + β2*intervention1 + β3*time_after_intervention1 + β4*intervention2 + β5*time_after_intervention2 + β6*intervention3 + β7*time_after_intervention3 + εt
where Yt is the outcome variable at different time points. The variable time indicates time points and was coded as 0–50 corresponding to each quarter from 2009 to 2021. The variable time_after_intervention1 was coded 0 up to the last point before intervention-1 and coded sequentially from 0 to 35 thereafter. β0 estimates the baseline level of Yt at the beginning of the time series. β1 estimates the structural trend or natural growth rate of Yt, independent of all the interventions. β2 estimates the level change following intervention-1 (intervention1 = 0 and intervention1 = 1 indicate before and after intervention-1, respectively), reflecting the immediate impact of intervention-1. β3 indicates the slope change of Yt following intervention-1, reflecting the long-term effect of intervention-1. Similarly, other variables and coefficients could also be explained accordingly. εt is the random error at time points. In addition, autocorrelation was assessed by the Durbin‒Watson test. When residual autocorrelation remains, it is adjusted using Prais-Winsten regression.
All participants were divided into three groups based on the implementation dates of the three interventions: 2009–2012, 2013–2016 and 2017–2021. Multilevel logistic regressions were then used to evaluate the changes in the HIV epidemic and HIV risk behaviours. The best model was selected using forwards selection of variables (P < 0.05 for inclusion and P > 0.1 for exclusion).
The statistical analysis was carried out using the Joinpoint Regression Program (Version 5.0), R (Version 4.3.0), and SPSS (version 20.0, IBM Corp). The results are presented as numbers (percentages), means ± standard deviations, semiannual percentage changes (SPCs), adjusted odds ratios (aORs), and 95% confidence intervals (95% CIs). The significance level was set at 0.05.

## Results

## Descriptive analysis
A total of 21,436 MSM were enrolled in this study from 2009 to 2021. The average age of the MSM was 30.45 ± 7.60 years. Among all the MSM, 8189 (38.20%) reported UAI, 6071 (31.43%) were MSMW, 418 (1.95%) had reported drug abuse, 2775 (12.85%) had multiple anal sexual partners in the past 6 months, 2238 (10.44%) had been diagnosed with STI, and 6321 (29.49%) had never been tested for HIV in the past (Supplementary Table 1).

## Trends in HIV yield
The overall HIV yield was 9.85% (2111/21436) among MSM in Shenzhen from 2009 to 2021. The joinpoint regression results revealed two statistically significant reductions in the slope of the HIV yield. The HIV yield in MSM increased at a mean rate of 14.70% (SPC = 14.70%, 95% CI 5.00–25.30%), from 6.26% in 2009 to 14.66% in 2012, when no intervention strategies were implemented. After implementing Intervention-1 (venue-based differentiated behavioural interventions), the HIV yield in MSM decreased at a mean rate of decline of 3.80% (SPC=−3.80%, 95% CI −7.10–−0.40%), from 14.74% in 2013 to 10.69% in 2016. With the addition of Intervention-2 (Immediate cART) and intervention-3 (PEP/PrEP promotion), the HIV yield in MSM declined at a mean rate of 9.50% (SPC=−9.50%, 95% CI −12.80–−6.20%) from 9.51% in 2017 to 4.08% in 2021 (Fig.  , Supplementary Tables 2 and 3). The results of joinpoint regression with 6 joinpoints revealed that the time points of implementation of the different interventions were the fold points of change in HIV yield among MSM in Shenzhen, but none of them were statistically significant (Fig.  , Supplementary Table 3).

 

 

Furthermore, we used ITS to evaluate the impacts of three interventions on HIV yield among MSM in Shenzhen. As shown in Fig.  and Supplementary Table 4, the HIV yield among MSM increased during the preintervention period (β1 = 0.58, P < 0.001). No significant change in the level of HIV yield was observed immediately after intervention-1 implementation (β2=−1.33, P = 0.32). The change in the HIV yield trend showed a significant absolute decrease of 0.29 (β3=−0.85, P < 0.001) in the average HIV yield each quarter since the intervention-1 implementation. During the implementation of intervention-2 and intervention-3, the slopes of the trend in HIV yield among MSM was − 0.468 and − 0.301 respectively showing a downward tendency, while the changes in slope were not significantly different from those of the previous intervention respectively (β5=−0.19, P = 0.46; β7 = 0.17, P = 0.54) (Fig.  and Supplementary Table 4).

 

## Changes in HIV risk behaviours among MSM
As shown in Fig.  and Supplementary Table 3, the prevalence of UAI among MSM in Shenzhen decreased significantly at a rate of 0.92% every six months (SPC=−0.92%, 95% CI −1.50–−0.4%) (Fig.  A). The prevalence of HIV tests increased significantly from 2009 to 2020 (SPC = 2.00%, 95% CI 1.50–2.60%) and then declined, although not significantly from 2020 to 2021 (SPC=−7.50%, 95% CI −16.50–2.40%) (Fig.  B). The trend in the prevalence of STIs was stable from 2009 to 2020 (SPC = 0.60%, 95% CI −0.30–1.50%) and decreased significantly from 2020 to 2021 (SPC=−17.90%, 95% CI −29.20–−4.90%) (Fig.  C). The prevalence of drug abuse declined, but not significantly, from 2009 to 2010 (SPC=−29.20%, 95% CI-53.90–8.90%), followed by a significant decline at a slower rate from 2010 to 2021 (SPC=−5.60%, 95% CI −8.20–−30%) (Fig.  D). The proportion of MSMW decreased significantly from 2009 to 2021 (SPC=−3.70%, 95% CI −5.20–−2.20%) (Fig.  E). The prevalence of multiple anal sexual partners decreased significantly from 2009 to 2018 (SPC=−9.80%, 95% CI −12.10–−7.40%) and increased significantly from 2018 to 2021 (SPC = 24.60%, 95% CI 11.90–38.80%) (Fig.  F).

 

All participants were divided into three groups—2009–2012, 2013–2016, and 2017–2021—based on the initiation time of different interventions. As shown in Table  , there were significant differences in several characteristics among the three groups. The multivariate regression was adjusted for age, educational level, marital status, income, length of residence, sexual orientation, and sexual role. Compared with MSM in 2009–2012, MSM in 2013–2016 were more likely to be infected with HIV (aOR = 1.32, 95% CI 1.16–1.50), be infected with STIs (aOR = 1.48, 95% CI 1.30–1.70), have been tested for HIV (aOR = 1.68, 95% CI 1.54–1.84), report UAI (aOR = 1.15, 95% CI 1.06–1.26), and less likely to be classified as MSMW (aOR = 0.84, 95% CI 0.76–0.93), have abused drugs (aOR = 0.77, 95% CI 0.60–0.99), and have multiple sexual partners (aOR = 0.05, 95% CI 0.02–0.03) (Table  ). MSM in 2017–2021 were more likely to be infected with STIs (aOR = 1.23, 95% CI 1.08–1.42), have been tested for HIV (aOR = 2.19, 95% CI 2.00–2.40), and less likely to be infected with HIV (aOR = 0.75, 95% CI 0.65–0.86), be MSMW (aOR = 0.57, 95% CI 0.52–0.64), have abused drugs (aOR = 0.29, 95% CI 0.21–0.40), and have multiple sexual partners (aOR = 0.051, 95% CI 0.038–0.068) (Table  ).

 

## Discussion
Based on a serial cross-sectional study conducted over 13 years, this study aimed to examine the HIV yield among MSM following the implementation of various interventions in Shenzhen. Intervention-1 (VBDB interventions) was a unique intervention strategy tailored specifically for Shenzhen. This approach involved providing a personalized intervention package based on the characteristics of different venues and individuals. The package included targeted education campaigns, personalized condom and HIV testing promotion models, and environmental modifications in venues to reduce the risk of HIV transmission. Intervention-2 (Immediate cART) and Intervention-3 (PEP/PrEP promotion) are nationwide strategies. All these interventions have been consistently implemented throughout the city of Shenzhen.
Shenzhen has demonstrated a high level of acceptance of homosexual culture and diverse MSM social venues. Approximately 90% of the young and sexually active MSM in Shenzhen are internal migrants from various regions across the country [ ]. As a result, the HIV yield among MSM in Shenzhen is relatively high. The overall HIV yield among MSM in Shenzhen was 9.8% from 2009 to 2021, which was higher than the national HIV yield (8%) estimated through the China CDC’s sentinel surveillance system during the same period. The HIV yield among MSM rapidly increased from 6.26% in 2009 to 14.74% in 2013. After the implementation of VBDB interventions, immediate cART and PEP/PrEP promotion, the trend of HIV yield among MSM in Shenzhen showed an inflection point, shifting from a rapid increase to a sharp decrease from 14.74% to 4.08%. Since 2018, the HIV yield among MSM in Shenzhen has been lower than the national average. Moreover, according to HIV molecular surveillance in Shenzhen, the clustering rate in the molecular networks of each HIV-1 subtype among MSM decreased from 2011– to 2018 [ ], which could provide further evidence that supports the results of this study. However, the results of the national sentinel surveillance data show that the HIV yield among MSM has remained at approximately 8% after the implementation of immediate cART in 2016. In contrast, venue-based MSM, one of the subgroups of the highest-risk MSM for HIV infection, were the predominant MSM subgroup in Shenzhen prior to the rise of online dating apps. Therefore, the difference in the change in HIV yield between MSM in Shenzhen and other regions in China may be attributed to VBDB interventions.
Compared with VBDB interventions, immediate cART and PEP/PrEP promotion did not have a significant additional independent effect on reducing the yield of HIV among MSM in Shenzhen. The reason why immediate cART did not have a significant independent effect may be related to the fact that approximately 35% of PLWHA were diagnosed late, with most cases in China identified through passive testing in health care facilities [ ], which could weaken the intervention effectiveness of immediate cART. Moreover, a previous study suggested high rates of incorrect use of PEP/PrEP among MSM, including the fact that some PLWHA remain unaware of their HIV status and continue to use PEP/PrEP, which could lead to drug resistance [ ]. The prevalence of transmitted drug resistance has been increasing annually and might partly result from the poor rate of cART adherence and incorrect usage of PEP/PrEP, further weakening the intervention effectiveness of immediate cART and PEP/PrEP promotion [ , ]. Furthermore, only 8–10% of MSM have used PEP/PrEP in Shenzhen [ , ], and low awareness and utilization of PEP/PrEP may be another reason for the lack of significant effectiveness of PEP/PrEP promotion. In addition, according to the treatment success standard threshold, PLWH with low-level viremia (LLV, viral load between 50 and 1000 copies/mL) are considered to achieve U = U [ ]. However, the prevalence of LLV may increase the likelihood of subsequent virological failure, which could indirectly heighten HIV transmission risk during UAI through eventual viral rebound, thereby undermining the intervention effectiveness of immediate cART initiation [ ]. However, joinpoint regression revealed that the yield of HIV among MSM decreased faster with the combination of immediate cART and PEP/PrEP promotion. This suggests that pharmacological interventions are effective for HIV interventions in MSM populations, but their effects are masked by other factors. Moreover, as the main mode of socialization among MSM has shifted from venues to online dating, another unpublished study indicated that after 2015, offline MSM venues ceased to be the main driver of HIV transmission among MSM in Shenzhen, which weakened the effect of VBDB interventions. This shift could also contribute to an underestimation of the HIV prevention effect of pharmacological interventions in MSM in ITS.
Several studies have suggested that promoting PEP/PrEP could cause risk compensation, which could lead to a potential increase in HIV risk behaviours [ , ]. In this study, risk compensation was not observed for most variables, including drug abuse, STIs, and MSMW, except for the prevalence of multiple anal sexual partners. The continued decline in HIV yield and HIV risk behaviours among MSM from 2009 to 2021 was consistent with the findings of another study of MSM in Shenzhen, which may be explained by the fact that even in the era of pharmacological interventions, VBDB interventions have continued to be implemented [ ]. These findings suggest that adherence to precision behavioural interventions in the era of pharmacological interventions remains beneficial. The prevalence of multiple anal sexual partners was low in the first half of the year and then rebounded to a higher prevalence level in the second half of the year in both 2020 (4.7% to 20.3%) and 2021 (8.3% to 23.1%) during the COVID-19 pandemic. The rebound coincided with the timing of the COVID-19 lockdown implementation in Shenzhen, with the increase occurring after the lifting of the lockdown. Furthermore, the joinpoint regression suggested a sustained downwards trend in UAI rates among MSM, and the data points revealed a levelling off and an initial reversal of the downwards trend in 2016, followed by a decline coinciding with the COVID-19 pandemic. This observed increase in UAI may be associated with increasing knowledge among MSM about U = U and perceiving PEP/PrEP as a substitute for consistent condom use [ , ].
Owing to the COVID-19 outbreak in 2020, which subsequently caused a global pandemic, the prevalence of HIV testing among MSM decreased from 82.6% in 2019 to 76.0% in 2020 and 70.9% in 2021. This decline may be attributed to the difficulty that MSM faced in accessing public health services and HIV care during the COVID-19 pandemic. This finding is consistent with another cohort study on the MSM population in Shenzhen [ ]. Moreover, the COVID-19 pandemic has also affected the prevalence of multiple anal sexual partners among MSM. Since health care efforts and resources during the COVID-19 pandemic have focused primarily on COVID-19, some HIV-related initiatives have been neglected or disrupted. The surveillance of STIs has also significantly but temporarily affected China [ ]. The negative impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic may have overshadowed the effectiveness of the PEP/PrEP promotion strategy, leading to an underestimation of its role in controlling the HIV epidemic. Moreover, HIV testing is critical for identifying undiagnosed PLWH and achieving the 95-95-95% goal. During the COVID-19 pandemic lockdown, the CDC, in partnership with NGOs, launched an online self-test kit distribution program to increase HIV testing rates among MSM. This initiative not only provided MSM with a convenient and accessible method for HIV testing but also encouraged them to share self-test kits with their sexual partners, ensuring continued access to HIV testing despite the challenges posed by the COVID-19 pandemic.

## Limitations
There are several limitations in this study. First, participants were recruited by approximate probability sampling, which might lead to selection bias and a lack of representation. However, our previous study revealed that TLS was more effective in reaching the hidden subgroup of MSM in Shenzhen, where gay venues were very well mapped, whereas RDS can reach MSM who do not often physically attend venues. The combination of RDS and TLS successfully sampled these marginalized subgroups [ ]. In addition, the consistency of our sampling design in each survey ensures that any bias caused by nonrandom sampling remains relatively stable, so we are confident that the trend of the HIV epidemic and the effects of interventions we have identified are valid and reliable. However, it is crucial to acknowledge that, despite adopting a distinct methodology for surveying MSM, our samples may predominantly represent sexually active MSM while still inadequately capturing those who are more hidden or less engaged. Additionally, the study sample had limited representativeness of MSM diagnosed with HIV and did not allow for the analysis of trends in HIV-related risk behaviours among HIV-infected individuals. Second, this study is ecological in nature, making it difficult to definitively determine causal relationships between the interventions and HIV yield. However, we did observe changes in HIV-related behaviours that were most likely driven by behavioural intervention, and these changes might further affect the epidemic of HIV. Third, our findings are specific to MSM in Shenzhen, China’s largest migrant city, where the average age is 33 years and the demographic structure differs significantly from that of other cities. Therefore, caution should be taken when attempting to generalize HIV yield patterns to the broader population in other regions. Finally, as this study was conducted retrospectively, the respondents may have been susceptible to recall bias during the completion of the questionnaire. Furthermore, despite the use of a self-administered electronic survey, we cannot fully mitigate information bias stemming from social desirability bias and stigma.

## Conclusions
In conclusion, the integrated application of behavioural and pharmacological strategies has proven pivotal in the effective management of HIV transmission among men who have sex with men (MSM) in Shenzhen. Despite the remarkable advancements in pharmacological interventions, the sustained reinforcement of tailored behavioural interventions remains imperative for mitigating the spread of HIV within this vulnerable population. This comprehensive approach, encompassing both behavioural modification and pharmacological therapy, offers the most robust framework for controlling the HIV epidemic and safeguarding the health and well-being of MSM in Shenzhen.
