## Abstract

## Background
24-hour movement behaviors integrating physical activity (PA), sedentary behavior (SB), and sleep are important components influencing adults’ health. Canadian 24-hour movement guidelines for adults were launched in 2020. However, there is little evidence on the prevalence and correlates of guideline compliance and its associations with health outcomes among Chinese adults. The purposes of this study were to investigate the prevalence and correlates of meeting 24-hour movement guidelines and examine its association with physical fitness and depressive symptoms among Chinese adults.

## Methods
A total of 7059 adults (45.73 ± 14.56 years, age range: 20–79 years, 52% female) were recruited by stratified cluster random sampling from health surveillance of Hubei, China, between 25-Jul and 19-Nov 2020 during the COVID-19 pandemic. Participants completed a self-reported questionnaire including movement behaviors (PA, SB, and sleep), depressive symptoms, and demographic information. In addition, eight objectively measured physical fitness tests (body mass index, waist-hip ratio, body fat percentage, vital capacity, handgrip strength, flexibility, balance, and choice reaction time) were conducted. SPSS 28.0 was used to perform Generalized Linear Mixed Models analysis to examine the correlates of movement guidelines and its association with fitness and depressive symptoms.

## Results
25.54% of participants met all three movement guidelines, while 48.62% met only two, 23.10% met one, and 2.75% met none. Participants who were older adults (OR = 2.24; P = .017), unmarried (OR = 0.21; P < .001), and living near PA facilities (OR = 1.58; P = .017) were more likely to meet all three movement guidelines. Meeting more movement recommendations was associated with less likelihood of depressive symptoms (OR = 0.41; P < .001), while no significant associations were observed between meeting 24-hour movement guidelines and physical fitness indicators (P > .05).

## Conclusions
The findings of this study indicate the need to promote adherence to all movement guidelines among Chinese adults. Future promoting strategies to meet movement guidelines should emphasize the demographic differences of the targeting population, especially age, marital status, and accessibility of PA facilities. Future interventions should be applied to affirm the effects of compliance with overall 24-hour movement guidelines to mitigate depressive symptoms among adults.

## Introduction
24-hour movement behaviors, including physical activity (PA), sedentary behavior (SB), and sleep, play a crucial role in promoting both physical and mental well-being [ – ]. Physical inactivity, prolonged sedentary behavior, and sleep disorders are common risk factors linked to non-communicable diseases (NCDs), encompassing metabolic disorders (e.g., diabetes and cardiovascular disease) and mental health conditions (e.g., depression and dementia). These factors significantly contribute to the public health burden through increased healthcare costs [ – ]. Physical inactivity, sedentary behavior, and sleep disorders are also risk factors for low levels of physical fitness (e.g., obesity, cardiorespiratory fragility, muscular strength, and balance) [ – ]. Research focusing on the health impacts of physical activity, sitting less, and sufficient sleep has surged, strongly recommending them for improving physical and mental health among adults.
However, adults have shown a high global prevalence of physical inactivity (27%) [ ] and sleep disorders (29%) [ ]. The device-based measured sitting time among adults was 8.3 h/day [ ]. Furthermore, the pattern of inactive lifestyles among adults, characterized by prolonged sedentary behavior and disturbed sleep, has caused a series of physical problems (e.g., deterioration in physical fitness and weight status) [ , ] in combination with mental problems (e.g., depression) [ – ]. In addition to the benefits of the recommended individual behaviors, integrated movement patterns within a 24-hour period may have spillover effects on overall health [ , – ]. National-level surveillance (2007–2013) found that Canadian adults adhering to all three movement guidelines had more favorable aerobic fitness, BMI, waist circumference, triglyceride, insulin, C-reactive protein, and serum glucose levels [ ]. Therefore, promoting 24-hour movement guidelines among adults to improve health outcomes and relieve economic costs post-pandemic is an important public health agenda.
The Canadian 24-hour Movement Guidelines for adult populations (≥ 18 years) promote that adults should have at least 150 accumulated minutes of MVPA per week, limit sedentary time to 8 h per day (no more than 3 h of recreational screen time), sleep 7–9 h (18–64 years)/ 7–8 h (≥ 65 years) per day with consistent bed and wake-up times [ ]. The adults’ 24-hour movement guidelines have increased research interest in the integrated effect of 24-hour movement behaviors on health among adult populations [ , ]. However, most existing studies identified the association between meeting 24-hour movement guidelines with health outcomes among children, adolescents [ – ], and adults in Western countries [ ]. In China, only a few studies examined the effects of 24-hour movement guidelines on mental health (e.g., depression, stress, anxiety) among university students [ – ] and caregivers of preschoolers [ ]. Moreover, one study investigated the associations between movement behaviors and young adults’ psychological features [ ], and one study on older adults’ fitness, depressive symptoms, and loneliness [ ]. However, these studies did not address the association of movement guidelines with comprehensive health outcomes among a broad adult population, particularly Chinese adults.
In addition, evidence revealed that 24-hour movement behaviors could be correlated with demographic characteristics (e.g., age, gender, education, marital status, residence, economic status, health condition, and community facilities) [ – ]. In particular, men were more likely to meet the combined guidelines than women [ , ], and adults with higher education levels were correlated with meeting all guidelines [ ]. Married individuals were more likely to meet al.l the guidelines [ ]. Also, studies indicated older age, living in the city or an area with a higher economic level, and unemployment were associated with unfavorable 24-hour movement behaviors [ , ]. Adults without chronic disease were more likely to meet al.l guidelines [ ]. Perceived neighborhood facilities’ availability had effects on intention to movement behaviors [ ]. In contrast to existing research, there is a lack of evidence on Chinese adults. Understanding these correlates about the adherence to 24-hour movement guidelines is crucial for illuminating population disparities and cultural contexts in future policies promoting 24-hour movement guidelines.
Taken together, the current study aimed to (1) investigate the prevalence of meeting 24-hour movement guidelines among adults after the withdrawal of the pandemic lockdown by a provincial sample in China; (2) examine the demographic correlates of meeting 24-hour movement guidelines; (3) identify the association between meeting 24-hour movement guidelines and physical fitness and depressive symptoms.

## Methods

## Study design and participants
This study constituted part of the 5th National Fitness Surveillance conducted in the Hubei province of China. With the cross-sectional surveillance study design, a stratified cluster random sampling method was used to select samples involving four steps. Firstly, all municipalities in Hubei province were recruited for surveillance, and then three districts were randomly selected from each municipality. Next, three towns and three villages were randomly selected from each district. Participants were recruited from the residents of different communities in each selected town and village. The eligibilities of participants were (1) adults aged 20–79 years old; (2) adequate language skills, being capable of reading and writing in Chinese; (3) without exercise contraindications (e.g., physical disabilities). Invited participants received physical activity risk screening by a registered medical practitioner using the physical activity readiness questionnaire (PAR-Q screening) of the Chinese version [ ]. If the participants interested in participation were out of the age range, could not comprehend Chinese, and were not competent in physical activity. This study followed the strengthening of the reporting of observational studies in epidemiology ( ) statement guidelines [ ].

## Procedures and quality control
The surveillance in this study was approved and supported by the General Administration of Sport in China and the Hubei Institute of Sport Science. Ethics approval was from these two institutes before study implementation (CISS-2019-01-31; HISS-2019-03-01). The work manual and declaration of surveillance were released [ ]. Data were collected after the lockdown was withdrawn in Hubei province (from July 25, 2020, to November 19, 2020). Although quarantine at home and transportation system suspension were withdrawn, the group gatherings and public area use were still suspended conditionally. To control the quality of assessments, data collection was conducted by a physical fitness surveillance team consisting of 15–20 certificated assessors in each municipality in a stadium. Each assessor was trained by a qualified supervisor and passed a competency test before conducting the assessment for participants. Assessors were required to follow the assessment protocol [ ].
All recruited participants signed written informed consent at first and then received the screening procedures. Eligible participants were required to complete the measurements, including the written questionnaire and physical fitness tests. A total of 32,080 participants were invited and received 27,826 responses (86.7% response rate). Through screening invalid values and outliers of all variables, 7,059 participants (45.73 ± 14.56 years, 52% females; ) were included in the statistical analyses.

## Measurements

## 24-hour movement behaviors
Physical activity (PA) and sedentary behavior (SB) were measured by the Chinese version of the International Physical Activity Questionnaire (IPAQ-C long form), which showed good reliability (ICC ranging from 0.74 to 0.95 for walking, moderate, vigorous, and total PA) among Chinese adults [ ]. The questions involved sedentary time, walking (light PA), moderate PA (MPA), and vigorous PA (VPA) in transportation, household, work, and exercise on weekdays and weekends. MVPA was defined as the sum of MPA and VPA.
Screen time was measured by questions: “On weekdays, how many days were spent watching the screen and how many minutes on an average day?” and “On weekends, how many days were spent watching the screen and how many minutes on an average day.” The following equation calculated the average screen time per day: (weekdays * mins + weekend days * mins)/ (weekdays + weekend days).
Sleep duration was evaluated using a self-reported question adapted from a previous study [ ]. The participants were asked: “During a week, how much time do you sleep on average per day, including the nap time at midday?”.
The meeting criteria of 24-hour movement guidelines for adults (aged 18–64 years; 65 years and above) included three movement behaviors: (1) PA time: meeting MVPA ≥ 150 min per week; (2) Sedentary time: meeting sedentary time ≤ 8 h and meanwhile screen time ≤ 3 h per day; (3) Sleep time: meeting the sleep duration of 7 to 9 h per day for adults aged ≤ 64 years; meeting the sleep duration of 7 to 8 h per day for adults aged ≥ 65 years [ ]. Participants were categorized as 0 = meeting none of movement behaviors, 1 = meeting one recommendation of movement behaviors, 2 = meeting two recommendations of movement behaviors, and 3 = meeting all three recommendations of movement behaviors.

## Physical fitness
According to physical fitness identified by the American College of Sports Medicine [ ], two categories of physical fitness were measured including (1) health-related physical fitness: body composition (body fat rate, body mass index, waist-hip ratio), cardiorespiratory endurance (vital capacity), muscular strength (handgrip), joint flexibility (sit and reach); (2) skill-related physical fitness: balance (one-leg standing with eye-closed) and reaction time (choice reaction time). The measurement of physical fitness followed the protocol of the National Physical Fitness Surveillance of China for adults [ , ], and it was an ongoing consecutive provincial surveillance among Chinese adults every 5 years. All the measures of physical fitness used Jianmin equipment produced by Xin Dong Hua Teng Sports Equipment Co. LTD, Beijing. China. The equipment has been used in previous Chinese national physical fitness surveillance [ , ]. The best outcomes of two trials for each indicator were recorded as the final measure. The cut-off points of body fat rate, BMI, and waist-hip ratio were based on the Asian and age-gender-specific criteria, respectively [ , ]. The scores of vital capacities, handgrip strength, flexibility, balance, and choice reaction time were evaluated according to the norm of Chinese physical fitness surveillance [ , ]. The evaluation was categorized into five points ranging between 1 (very poor), 2 (poor), 3 (pass), 4 (good) and 5 (very good). Then, the five ratings were re-coded into two categories for statistical analysis. A score < 3 points was coded as “below average”, while a score ≥ 3 points was coded as “meet and above average.”

## Health-related physical fitness

## Body mass index (BMI, kg/m2)
BMI is calculated by objectively measuring height (cm) and weight (kg). Both measures were assessed using calibrated weighing scales and a stadiometer (Jianming GMCS-SGJ3 and GMCS-RCS3, Beijing, China). The error range of the stadiometer and weighing scale were ± 0.2% and ± 0.1 kg, respectively. Based on Asian adults’ cut-offs, participants were categorized into non-overweight or non-obese (BMI < 23), overweight or obese (≥ 23) [ ].

## Waist-hip ratio (WHR)
Waist (cm) and hip circumference (cm) measurements referred to the assessment protocol [ ]. Both were objectively measured using an instrument (Jianmin GMCS-WD3, Beijing, China). The error range of the ruler was ± 0.1 cm. Participants were categorized into non-central obesity (WHR < 0.9 for males; WHR < 0.8 for females) and central obesity (WHR ≥ 0.9 for males; WHR ≥ 0.8 for females) [ ].

## Body fat percentage (BFP %)
BFP was directly measured by bioelectrical impedance (Jianmin GMCS-TZL3, Beijing, China). Bioimpedance accuracy is ± 3% at 1-500 KHZ test frequency point, and the accuracy is ± 6% at 1000 kHz test frequency. Participants were categorized into non-obese (BFP < 25% for males; BFP < 35% for females) and obese (WHR ≥ 25% for males; BFP ≥ 35% for females) [ ].

## Vital capacity (VC, ml)
VC was measured using a spirometer (Jianmin GMCS-FHL, Beijing, China). The participants were instructed to take maximum inspiration and blow it into the exhaled mouthpiece. The volume of exhaled breath was measured as vital capacity. The error range of the spirometer was ± 2.5%.

## Handgrip strength (HGS, kg)
Handgrip strength was measured by a mechanical dynamometer (Jianmin GMCS-WCS3, Beijing, China). The dynamometer reading in kilograms was determined by the tension generated in a steel spring. The distance between handles could be adjusted regarding hand size to make the instrument individual specific. The error range of the dynamometer was ± 0.3 kg.

## Flexibility (cm)
Flexibility was measured by a sit-and-reach test using an instrument (Jianmin GMCS-TQQ3, Beijing, China). Participants were instructed to sit on the ground with straight legs against a standard box and push it forward as far as possible. The distance score (cm) was recorded in the extreme reach position. The error range of the instrument was ± 0.2 cm.

## Skill-related physical fitness

## Balance (second)
Balance was assessed by the duration (second) of single-leg standing with eyes closed using an instrument (Jianmin GMCS-DJZL3, Beijing, China). Participants were required to stand on an electronic pad for as long as possible until the other leg stepped. If the participants’ standing reached the maximum duration of the norm, participants were asked to finish this test. The error range of the instrument was ± 0.1s.

## Choice reaction time (second)
Choice reaction time was measured by a traditional test plate (Jianmin GMCS-FYS, Beijing, China). Participants were instructed to rest one hand on the reset bottom of the plate to start the stimulus. They were asked to respond as quickly as possible by pressing a bottom-appearing visual stimulus with the middle finger. The times (second) of completing the task were recorded. The error range of the instrument was ± 0.01s.

## Depressive symptoms
Depressive symptoms was assessed using the Chinese version of PHQ-9 which includes 9 items with a 4-point Likert scale from “Not at all = 0” to “Nearly every day = 3”, and the total score can range from 0 to 27 [ ]. This scale has been widely used with good internal constancy reliability (Cronbach’s alpha = 0.86) and validity (area under the curve of the ROC = 0.92) in Chinese adults [ ]. Depressive symptoms score between 0 and 4 was coded as “no depressive symptoms,” and above 4 was coded as “yes with depressive symptoms” [ ].

## Demographic correlates
All participants needed to report the demographic information on a written questionnaire, including age, gender, residence (urban or rural area), education (college or above; middle school or high school; primary school or below), occupation (employed; retired; unemployed), marital status (divorce or widow; married; unmarried), living arrangement (with families; with partner; alone or other), chronic diseases (yes or no), and physical activity facility/venue within 15-minute walk or ride from participant’s home (yes or no).

## Data analyses
Data analyses were performed using SPSS 28.0. MVPA time/week, sedentary, and screen time/day falling outside of ± 3 SD were regarded as invalid and excluded from the final analysis [ , ]. Values of sleep duration/day below 3 h or above 16 h were excluded from subsequent analyses [ ]. Also, the cases with missing values for physical activity or, sedentary time or screen time, or sleep duration were removed. Descriptive analyses were used to present the characteristics of the samples meeting 24-hour movement guidelines with mean, standard deviation, and percentage.
For the primary analysis, generalized linear mixed models (GLMM) were applied to examine (1) the association of demographic correlates (independent variables) with meeting 24-hour movement guidelines (dependent variables coded as categorical variables with four levels including meeting none, meeting one of movement recommendations, meeting two of movement recommendations and meeting all three movement recommendations) by using multinomial logistic regression analysis, and (2) the association of meeting 24-hour movement guidelines (independent variables) with physical fitness and depressive symptoms health outcomes (dependent variables coded as categorical variables with two levels) by using binary logistic regression analysis. Moreover, all the significant demographic variables (age group, marital status, community feature) were controlled in the adjusted models when examining the association between meeting 24-hour movement guidelines and health outcomes. The results of GLMM were presented as adjusted odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence interval CIs, and statistical significance was set up as p < .05 (two-tailed).

## Results

## Characteristics of participants
A total of 7,059 participants were analyzed in this study (see Fig.  ). Approximately 90% of the included samples were aged 20–64 (45.73 ± 14.56 years), and 52% were female. The mean values of health-related fitness were 23.93 ± 3.12 for BMI, 0.88 ± 0.07 for waist-hip ratio, 26.15 ± 6.43% for body fat rate, 2624.45 ± 963.99 ml for vital capacity, 32.98 ± 11.18 kg for handgrip, and 7.18 ± 8.82 cm for flexibility. The mean values of skill-related physical fitness were 22.00 ± 27.26s for balance and 0.68 ± 0.75s for reaction time. In addition, the average score of depressive symptoms was 1.81 ± 2.71, and 12.9% of participants were identified with depressive symptoms. More sample characteristics are presented in Table  .
 

## Prevalence of meeting 24-hour movement guidelines
The prevalence of meeting 24-hour movement guidelines in different combinations is presented in Fig.  . The percentage meeting all three movement guidelines was only 25.54%. Moreover, 74.89% of adults met at least sleep, 68.85% at least MVPA, and 53.22% at least sedentary behavior.
 

## Correlates of meeting 24-hour movement guidelines
Demographics correlate with meeting 24-hour movement guidelines presented in Table  . Compared with participants who were under 65 years old, those aged 65 years and above had higher odds of meeting all three (OR, 2.24; CI, 1.15, 4.35; P = .017) or two behavior recommendations (OR, 2.10; CI, 1.09, 4.04; P = .026) of 24-hour movement guidelines. Compared with unmarried participants, participants who were divorced or widowed (OR, 0.21; CI, 0.09, 0.49; P < .001) and married (OR, 0.44; CI, 0.22, 0.90; P = .024) had lower odds of meeting three or two or one 24-hour movement guidelines. Participants who reported physical activity facilities in the community (OR, 1.58; CI, 1.09, 2.30; P = .017) had higher odds of meeting all three 24-hour movement guidelines than those without physical activity facilities.
 

## Associations between meeting 24-hour movement guidelines and health outcomes
The associations of meeting 24-hour movement guidelines with physical fitness and depressive symptoms are presented in Table  , controlling the significant demographic correlates shown in Table  (age group, marital status, community feature). Meeting 24-hour movement guidelines was not significantly associated with physical fitness including BMI, waist-hip ratio, body fat rate, vital capacity, hand grip strength, sit and reach flexibility, the balance of one-leg standing with eye closed, and choice reaction time in this study (all P > .05). Whereas meeting any single guideline (OR, 0.62; CI, 0.40, 0.96; P = .03), any two guidelines (OR, 0.51; CI, 0.33, 0.77; P = .002), and all three guidelines (OR, 0.41; CI, 0.26, 0.64; P < .001) of 24-hour movement guidelines were significantly associated with a lower odd of depressive symptoms.
 

## Discussion

## Principal findings
The present study provides evidence on the prevalence and correlates of meeting 24-hour movement guidelines and its association with physical fitness and depressive symptoms among Chinese adults. It was found that only 25.54% of the study sample met all three movement guidelines, while 74.89%, 68.85%, and 53.33% met the health guidelines for sleep, MVPA, and sedentary behavior, respectively. Age, marital status, and physical activity facilities were salient correlates of meeting 24-hour movement guidelines. The likelihood of low depressive symptoms levels increased with meeting more movement guidelines. While meeting 24-hour movement guidelines was not significantly associated with physical fitness indicators.

## Prevalence of meeting movement guidelines
When comparing the findings of this study with previous studies, the study time frame should be considered. The data was collected a few months after the COVID-19 pandemic lockdown was lifted. Restrictive use of public physical activity facilities, changes in daily routines, and mandatory quarantine were still influential factors. The prevalence of meeting all movement guidelines among Chinese adults in this study was 25.54%, similar among Slovenian adults (25%) [ ] and Thai adults (21.3%) [ ]. It was lower than those studies targeting Chinese university students (27%) [ , ]. The difference may be attributed to sample characteristics. University students (ages 18–26) unsurprisingly exhibited a higher prevalence of all movement guidelines compared to other adults due to their greater motivation and ability for physical activity, as well as the supportive campus environment [ , ]. In comparison, the prevalence of meeting all guidelines was higher than that among Chinese caregivers of preschoolers (15.1%), Canadian adults (7.1%), and Latin American adults (1.6%) [ , , ]. Given that caregivers of preschoolers experienced a greater negative impact on their 24-hour movement behaviors during the pandemic than general adults [ ]. The school closures forced caregivers to allocate more time to caring for preschoolers, resulting in increased sitting time and fewer hours of sleep. The evident lower prevalence among Canadian and Latine American adults was understandable, given the data from past decades [ , ]. Fewer measures were enforced to improve meeting 24-hour movement behaviors in the past decades compared to recent years. Besides, diverse measurements may result in discrepant prevalence in different studies. This study used a self-reported questionnaire assessing physical activity and sedentary behavior, which might overestimate prevalence due to social desirability and recall bias. Behavioral data from Canada and Latin America were objectively measured using an accelerometer. The prevalence of meeting sleep recommendations in this study (74.89%) was higher than that for Korean adults (48.6%) and Thai adults (56.4%) [ , ]. The underlying reason may be reopening after lockdown or working from home in this study was associated with earlier sleep timing [ ]. However, we found that only around half of the adults (53.22%) met sedentary behavior guidelines, showing a lower adherence to sedentary behavior guidelines compared with previous studies [ , ]. The difference might be attributable to that this study applied the recommendation of sedentary behavior, not only including meeting sitting time but also screen time [ ]. Meanwhile, the pandemic elicited more sitting indoors or on transportation. The study implies low adherence to 24-hour movement guidelines among Chinese adults. It suggests the imperative of effective measures and policies to promote movement guidelines.

## Correlates of meeting movement guidelines
The study found that adults aged 65 and above were more likely to meet two and all three movement recommendations than adults aged 20–64. The findings have coincided with a previous study reporting that Chinese adults ≥ 65 years old spent less screen time and more physical activity time [ ]. However, the result was inconsistent with a study targeting Canadian adults; the age group of 18–65 years was closely associated with meeting the integrated guidelines [ ]. In terms of marital status, adults who were married or previously married (divorced or widow) were found to be less likely to meet any combination of guidelines compared to those who were unmarried, which is contrary to the previous study in Thailand [ , ]. The mixed results indicate more studies to explore whether age and married status are facilitators or barriers to meeting movement guidelines. In addition, this study found that meeting all movement recommendations was positively correlated with nearby physical activity facilities. Proximity to physical activity facilities increases the likelihood that people will use them to sit less and move more. This finding is consistent with prior research demonstrating significant positive associations between physical activity and the physical environment [ , ]. Nevertheless, this study did not find that gender, rural or urban area, economic status, education, occupation, living situation, or chronic diseases significantly correlated with meeting 24-hour movement guidelines. In other studies, being male adults, having higher education, and not having chronic disease were significantly relevant to movement recommendations [ , ], while those living in urban areas at a high economic level and unemployed were shown to have the lowest likelihood of meeting 24-hour movement guidelines [ ]. These findings imply that future strategies for promoting movement guidelines should consider demographic differences of age, marital status, and physical activity facility. Specifically, it is important to tailor program and policy recommendations to meet the unique needs of diverse age groups and consider the families’ support systems. Additionally, establishing a community environment with easily accessible physical activity facilities can further enhance participation and engagement in physical activity.

## Associations between meeting movement guidelines and physical fitness
The current study did not find a significant association between meeting 24-hour movement guidelines and physical fitness, including body mass index, waist-hip ratio, body fat percentage, vital capacity, handgrip strength, flexibility, balance, and reaction time among adults, which were inconsistent with some previous studies. A favorable association between meeting more guidelines and body composition (e.g., BMI, waist circumference) and aerobic fitness was observed among adults [ , ], middle-aged and older adults with obesity or overweight [ ]. The reason for the insignificant association found in this study might be related to the large age variance of the sample (age range: 20–79 years; SD = 14.56), including young adults, mid-age adults, and older adults. Further analyses should be warranted to examine the relationship between 24-hour movement guidelines and physical fitness in adults as the mixed results. In addition, the overall time used during the 24-hour period should be considered to investigate the combined effect of 24-hour movement guidelines on physical fitness using compositional data analysis techniques in the future [ ].

## Associations between meeting movement guidelines and depressive symptoms
The findings in this study indicate a dose-response relationship between meeting 24-hour movement guidelines and depressive symptoms. Meeting more movement guidelines was associated with a lower risk of depressive symptoms. This association coincides with previous literature among adults [ , , ], children and youth [ ]. Previous evidence supports the negative association between 24-hour movement guidelines and depressive symptoms. Sufficient physical activity and good sleep hygiene can benefit positive emotions and well-being of adults [ ]. Physical activity has been recognized to alleviate depressive symptoms through a range of biological and psychosocial processes, such as neuroplasticity, inflammation, endocrine system, self-esteem, social support, and self-efficacy [ ]. However, sleep deficiency contributes to depressive symptoms by increasing inflammatory cytokines and abnormalities of rapid eye movement (REM) sleep, which dysregulates depression-related neurotransmitters [ ]. Prolonged sedentary time has been found to induce inflammatory risk markers associated with depression [ ]. Adults with depressive symptoms often exhibit lower levels of physical activity, shorter sleep duration, and longer sitting time, highlighting the bidirectional negative association between adherence to 24-hour movement guidelines and depressive symptoms [ , ]. To advance our understanding of the effects of 24-hour movement guidelines on depressive symptoms, more research is warranted to explore the mechanism underlying integrated movement behaviors and depressive symptoms. Future intervention studies should be designed to identify causal relationships, thereby contributing to the development of new approaches for mitigating depressive symptoms in adults by enhancing compliance with 24-hour movement guidelines.

## Limitations
The findings should be interpreted cautiously as they have some limitations. Firstly, a cross-sectional design cannot confirm the causality between meeting movement guidelines and health outcomes. Intervention studies should be warranted to identify how meeting 24-hour movement guidelines impact physical and mental health. Secondly, the present study used a self-reported questionnaire to estimate movement behaviors (MVPA, SB, and sleep duration) separately rather than report the duration in a 24-hour circle. In addition, sleep duration was measured using only one item, although the validity and reliability of the item were approved in a previous study [ ]. The findings of the questionnaire survey may have been affected by recall errors, social desirability bias, and measurement bias. Objective assessment, such as accelerometer, should be applied to improve the precision of behavior aspects. However, device-measured PA, SB, and sleep might not be feasible on large-scale surveillance due to the limited resources and longer measurement periods (at least 7 days). Objective and feasible measurement tools of movement behaviors should be considered in large-scale samples. Thirdly, only demographic correlates were examined in this study; social-cognitive correlates (i.e., self-efficacy, social support, planning, and action control) may also be important for adherence to 24-hour movement guidelines [ ]. Fourthly, the study emphasized the association between integrated guidelines and health indicators but did not explore the association between movement behaviors and health using specific combinations. Future research is needed to examine the relationship between movement behaviors and health in 24-hour periods using compositional analysis. Fifthly, many participants (n = 20,767) were excluded from this study due to their invalid or missing data. The study findings might lack representativeness due to different characteristics between included and excluded participants. Additionally, this surveillance was limited to Hubei province in central China. Still, it did not extend to other regions in China and excluded adults with exercise contraindications due to the safety of testing, which may affect the generalizability of the findings.
Regardless of these limitations, this study provided updated evidence for prevalence, correlates of meeting 24-hour movement guidelines, and its association with physical fitness and depressive symptoms among a wide age range of Chinese adults by large-scale surveillance. It fills the evidence gap that few 24-hour movement guideline studies conducted among all ages of Chinese adults. Moreover, the current study supplemented the association between movement guidelines and fitness (strength, flexibility, balance, and reaction time) compared with previous research targeting adiposity or cardiorespiratory fitness [ ]. The findings may contribute to promoting movement behaviors in the context of lifestyle disruption during and beyond a pandemic to improve physical and mental health.

## Conclusions
This study found that only 25.54% of Chinese adults met the overall 24-hour movement guidelines after the pandemic lockdown. Adults who are aged 65 years and above, unmarried, and living near PA facilities had a higher likelihood of meeting movement guidelines. Meeting more movement guidelines was associated with a lower likelihood of depressive symptoms. No significant associations were observed between meeting 24-hour movement guidelines and physical fitness. The findings indicate the need to promote adherence to all movement guidelines among Chinese adults. Future policy encouraging adults to meet movement guidelines should consider demographic differences. The study suggested the potential effects of integrated guidelines on depressive symptoms. Future interventions should be applied to affirm the impact of compliance with overall 24-hour movement guidelines to mitigate depressive symptoms among adults.
