We are writing to inform the readership of Poultry Science about conversations that have occurred over the last year regarding the future state of poultry welfare globally. These conversations involved members of academia, industry, and allied stakeholders and were led by the Research and Innovation Committee of the International Poultry Welfare Alliance (IPWA). The IPWA conducted a series of seven icebreaker activities at poultry industry and scientific conferences to gather global perspectives on poultry welfare, looking ahead to 2050. This letter summarizes the key insights and discussions from these sessions, based on input from over 200 participants from nearly every continent. Discussion topics regarding the path to poultry welfare in 2050 covered housing and management, positive welfare measures, artificial intelligence (AI), cross-species applications of poultry welfare research, welfare from the end of production to processing, nutrition, robustness, and sustainability across all levels of the poultry industry. Across geographical regions, common themes emerged from these discussions, highlighting key points for improving poultry welfare, health, and sustainability globally as we move towards 2050. This letter summarizes the input received during these icebreaker events.

## What is IPWA?
The poultry industry is undergoing significant changes, driven by technological advancements, consumer demands, and a growing emphasis on poultry welfare. IPWA Research and Innovation Committee reflects the global poultry community, with the goal of providing science-based evidence regarding poultry welfare so that people can make informed decisions. The IPWA does not intend to be prescriptive, but rather to distill science to provide empirical evidence that can inform decision-makers. The IPWA represents advocates for continuous improvements in poultry welfare assurance based upon research.
The IPWA is a multistakeholder organization with broad and active participation across the global poultry chain, including retail, science, and policy, with a common interest in improving poultry welfare. The IPWA serves as an independent global authority on poultry welfare, providing insights and guidance for improvement. This alliance shares joint leadership with the US Roundtable for Sustainable Poultry & Eggs, utilizing their respective memberships and committee structures to drive improvement in the poultry industry. To date, the organization has nearly 100 industry stakeholder members, including integrators, processors, breeders, suppliers, growers, retail and foodservice companies, professional associations, and academic members from around the world. In recent years, the IPWA has focused on building outcomes-based Key Welfare Indicators (KWIs) and providing educational and networking opportunities through webinars, web resources, and icebreakers that facilitate meaningful discussions about poultry welfare throughout the value chain.
The IPWA maintains five Pillars as guiding principles for the organization that provide the foundation for IPWA’s committee structure, including:1.Research and Innovation: By engaging scientists, engineers, academic experts, students, and other professionals, IPWA encourages and supports research that leads to innovation. IPWA identifies research priorities, tracks ongoing research supports, and promotes scientific research and innovation to advance the understanding and improvement of poultry welfare outcomes.
2.Poultry Health and Welfare: This pillar supports the integration of welfare science into practical production by providing a forum for welfare practitioners to understand how welfare research, measurement, innovation, and emerging issues relate to the actual production of poultry.
3.Communication and Stakeholder Engagement: IPWA provides a platform for open and transparent communication among all stakeholders, promoting proactive communication, and performing outreach to internal and external stakeholders.
4.Education and Training: This pillar delivers welfare education, methods, and practical training materials to improve the handling, care, and treatment of poultry across all stages of life.
5.Sustainability: Poultry production and poultry welfare cannot be separate goals to improve and maintain the sustainability of the products the industry produces. While poultry welfare is IPWA’s focus, other aspects of sustainability can be affected by the decisions related to poultry welfare and vice versa. IPWA provides a venue for navigating aspects of sustainability that can be affected by welfare decisions, including environmental impacts, food security, worker safety, biosecurity, and food safety

## Icebreaker events
As the Research and Innovation Committee is responsible for identifying areas where further innovation in poultry welfare is needed, the committee launched a global engagement effort to gather opinions from stakeholders. To explore what poultry welfare might look like in 2050 and how to achieve it, the committee organized a series of icebreaker events. These events were held at high-profile poultry-aligned conferences around the world from January 2024 through April 2025 (International Production & Processing Expo (IPPE) 2024, Atlanta, GA, USA; 2024 Poultry Science Association (PSA) Annual Meeting, Louisville, KY, USA; 2024 American Association of Avian Pathologists (AAAP) Annual Meeting, St. Louis, MO; EuroTier 2024, Hanover, Germany; IPPE 2025, Atlanta, GA, USA; Latin American Poultry Summit 2025, Atlanta, GA, USA (held in Spanish, with subsequent translation of notes to English); VIV Asia 2025, Bangkok, Thailand). Collectively, they brought together over 200 participants from Europe, Asia, Africa, Oceania, and the Americas, including producers, members of allied industries and services, students, academics, and representatives from animal welfare, government, and consumer organizations.
Participants were asked, “What does poultry welfare look like in 2050, and how do we get there?” The purpose of these events was to provide a social environment in which participants could discuss both the current state and future of poultry welfare. To guide the discussions, the committee developed eight topics: 1) housing and management, with a focus on environmental enrichment, 2) positive animal welfare, 3) impact of artificial intelligence (AI) on the welfare of poultry and humans in the industry, 4) research applications to the welfare across species, 5) welfare from the end of production to processing, 6) welfare and nutrition management, 7) robustness, and 8) welfare and sustainability. Attendees selected which topics to engage with and participated in short, structured discussions. Participants then provided key points regarding challenges and opportunities, necessary actions, and future directions.

## Summary of participant key points

1.
Housing and Management, with a Focus on Environmental Enrichment

The future of laying hen housing is expected to be cage-free, in systems that improve welfare by providing more space, including vertical space, and promoting natural behaviors. In all poultry, automated climate controls and litter aeration will enhance comfort, while AI and big data will optimize housing conditions based on bird behavior. Further, AI-driven digital solutions and apps will allow farmers to receive precise feedback on housing conditions, including niche environments, in real time. Vertical housing systems have the potential to provide more space per bird while minimizing the land footprint. Across poultry species, studies on innovative environmental enrichments, such as soundscapes, engaging foraging devices, functional lighting zones, and fitness-enhancing perches and platforms, are needed.
2.
Positive Animal Welfare

A shift to objective, data-driven approaches for assessing positive (not only less negative) components of welfare is necessary. Advanced sensors will enable real-time behavior and health monitoring, and breeding programs will need to continue to prioritize welfare traits alongside productivity and environmental impact. Increased consumer demand for transparency in production standards will drive improvements in feeding and other management strategies to optimize comfort and satiety. A dynamic balance between environmental interventions (e.g., sensory, nutritional, occupational, and structural enrichments) will be required to promote resilience (e.g., to seasonal temperature conditions). Improvements in rapid veterinary support and interventions will help protect flocks and the people who work with them. Modular training programs, such as those provided by IPWA, are excellent examples of improving the knowledge and skills of our farmers, industry members, and students. Moving the industry towards a One Health integration of poultry production and welfare with human and environmental health is expected to bring future benefits.
3.
Impact of AI on Welfare of Poultry and Humans in the Industry

AI-assisted monitoring of individual bird behavior will allow for early detection and mitigation of stress and disease, and machine learning will enable automated welfare assessments and farm management. AI-driven flock management, also called precision livestock farming or ‘smart’ farming, will optimize housing conditions in real time, based on analysis of vocalizations and other behavioral stress indicators. Robotics and automation will reduce the need for manual handling of live birds and improve the numaneness of pre-laighter procedures. Flock-level health trend forecasting, anomaly detection of biosecurity breaches, and integration with apps to provide growers with actionable information in real time have great potential to assist the industry moving forward. These tools should be used to complement human observation, serving both as a safeguard for normal operations and as an early warning system to identify specific areas within a housing facility that require caretaker inspection. The use of virtual and augmented reality technologies shows great potential to improve preparedness and consistency in training both workers and animal welfare auditors.
4.
Research-Based Applications across Poultry Species

Poultry species-specific welfare indicators will be developed and fine-tuned based on scientific research. The value of scientific advancements in the welfare assessment of one poultry species may be applicable to other, less-researched species to some degree. Researchers, producers, and retail/food service personnel could also gain insights by being aware of welfare challenges and solutions presented in other food animal species. The biology of the species and their particularities need to be considered, however, in developing universal, cross-species approaches to indicators that promote positive animal welfare. Multispecies welfare dashboards would help to unify data streams. Relatedly, developing international research and development partnerships will help to unify welfare standards based upon local needs while establishing global standards. The focus of IPWA is on poultry species but ensuring that we consider and contribute to science from other disciplines is critical.
5.
Welfare from the End of Production to Processing

A priority for the future is to reduce transportation stress through sensor-based monitoring of transport conditions, handling, and logistics. AI and automation during processing will contribute to humane animal treatment, including controlled atmospheric stunning. Better training of animal handling personnel will improve poultry handling during catching and processing. Further, real-time contamination detection systems using novel biosensors and farm-to-plant traceability will improve welfare accountability, processing efficiency, and food safety. Future processing protocols that include post-processing welfare audits will provide benchmarking data to inform and drive on-farm improvements. Along with stakeholder workshops to refine end-of-line protocols, these steps will help to improve poultry welfare, food safety, and consumer confidence.
6.
Welfare and Nutrition Management

Precision nutrition will optimize feed efficiency and welfare outcomes. Using probiotics, insect proteins, and synthetic amino acids will enhance environmental sustainability. AI-driven analysis of feed composition will minimize environmental impact, and foraging enrichment strategies will improve bird engagement and contribute to broiler breeder satiety. Balancing amino acids to reduce phosphate emissions at different life stages will help protect the environment. Feed additives should be developed to enhance poultry immune responses and resilience under stress, along with chemosensory profiling and flavor enhancers to improve the foraging experience and advance poultry health, welfare, and production. Bird health and welfare could be further refined by AI-driven diet optimization based on flock-level performance. Also, farmer-focused nutrition education modules could be delivered through IPWA channels.
7.
Robustness

Genetic advancements will continue to focus on robustness to improve disease resilience and adaptability. In addition, AI-assisted decision-making will drive improvements in flock management. Technologies will only be adopted if they balance environmental sustainability, bird welfare, and productivity. Global collaborations harmonized cross-regional definitions of robustness, and shared data-benchmarking platforms for continuous improvement will define consistent welfare improvements. Research into advanced housing materials that are durable, hygienic, and have a low environmental impact will improve environmental sustainability, food safety, and consumer confidence.
8.
Welfare and Sustainability

Public education on animal welfare in schools and consumer campaigns will raise awareness. Transparency in environmental sustainability and welfare efforts will be achieved through economic models that align robustness, welfare, and environmental sustainability metrics that provide fact-based choices to improve poultry production. Balanced breeding will continue to be the basis for genetic advancements, aligning productivity, welfare, robustness, and carbon footprint. Consumer involvement is essential to social sustainability in reducing the disconnect between industry and consumers. Prioritizing long-term strategic planning over short-term production gains, in combination with public-private collaborations and community engagement and global events, will help to bridge gaps between producers, researchers, and consumers.

## Conclusion
The future of poultry welfare in 2050 will be shaped by technological advancements, consumer demands, and a commitment to continuous improvements in poultry welfare. The insights from the IPWA Research and Innovation Committee's icebreaker events provide a roadmap for achieving these goals. The IPWA icebreakers aimed to foster a multifaceted and holistic view of welfare. Our ultimate goal is to help guide poultry welfare towards a more cohesive and global approach, one that prioritizes stakeholder input while remaining anchored in validated research, to build a more credible, transparent, and effective framework for advancing poultry welfare.
