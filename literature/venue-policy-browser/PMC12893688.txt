Immune escape constitutes a central hallmark of cancer progression, encompassing multifaceted strategies employed by tumor cells to evade immune surveillance and subvert antitumor responses. This process is fundamentally rooted in the “three Es” theory of cancer immunoediting – elimination, equilibrium, and escape – first formalized in the early 2000s, which provides the conceptual framework for understanding how tumors evolve under immune pressure. In the context of LC immunotherapy, immune escape manifests through interconnected pathways, including the upregulation of immune checkpoint molecules, recruitment of immunosuppressive cell populations, metabolic reprogramming of the TME, and alterations in antigen presentation machinery. The TIME functions as the primary arena where these mechanisms unfold, characterized by dynamic interactions between cancer cells, immune effector cells, immunosuppressive cellular components, and stromal elements that collectively determine therapeutic outcomes. 
Despite the substantial clinical impact of immune escape on LC treatment, the current research landscape remains fragmented across diverse disciplines, methodological approaches, and geographic regions. While numerous individual studies have elucidated specific aspects of these mechanisms, a systemic overview of the field’s evolutionary trajectory, shifting research priorities, and knowledge structure is lacking. The rapid expansion of literature in this domain – spanning immunology, oncology, molecular biology, and computational sciences – necessitates a systematic approach to map the intellectual structure and identify emerging research frontiers. Furthermore, the geographic distribution of research efforts and collaborative networks remains poorly characterized, limiting our understanding of how global scientific communities contribute to advancing this field.
Therefore, this comprehensive bibliometric analysis – a quantitative methodology that uses mathematical and statistical tools to map scientific literature and research trends – aims to systematically map and evaluate the global research landscape of immune escape in LC immunotherapy from 1995 to 2025. As shown in , this study sought to: (1) quantify publication trends and identify developmental phases in immune escape research; (2) characterize geographic distribution, institutional collaboration patterns, and author networks contributing to this field; (3) analyze journal preferences and co-citation networks to understand knowledge dissemination patterns; (4) identify current research hotspots through keyword clustering and topic trend analysis; (5) uncover underdeveloped research frontiers using thematic mapping approaches; and (6) provide evidence-based insights to guide future resource allocation and research directions. Through this multidimensional bibliometric investigation, we aim to provide researchers, clinicians, and policymakers with a comprehensive roadmap that delineates research trajectories to inform strategic funding choices. While acknowledging that bibliometric trends reflect research attention rather than proving translational efficacy, this analysis offers a structural foundation for advancing the understanding and therapeutic targeting of immune escape in LC immunotherapy.
 

## Methods

## Database selection and search approach
Data were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) on August 9, 2025, covering the period from January 1, 1995, to August 9, 2025. To ensure the comprehensiveness and accuracy of the dataset, the search was constructed using a specific Boolean logic combining terms related to three domains: #1 (lung cancer related terms) AND #2 (immune escape related terms) AND #3 (immunotherapy related terms). The full verbatim search string, including all synonyms, wildcards, and Boolean operators, is provided in Supplementary Table S1. The detailed screening and selection process is illustrated in the PRISMA flow diagram (Supplementary Figure S1). To ensure reproducibility, the unique Web of Science Accession Numbers (UT) for all 2813 retrieved records are listed in Supplementary File 2.
For thorough and precise bibliometric analysis, the investigation predominantly employed the WoSCC. The database delivers key advantages, such as comprehensive inclusion of high-impact journals, standardized citation metrics, and readily exportable metadata compatible with advanced tools like VOSviewer, CiteSpace, and bibliometrix. While databases like Scopus, Google Scholar, and PubMed contain extensive scholarly output, WoSCC is distinguished by tighter data coherence and structural integrity, which enhances analytical rigor and the comprehensiveness of the literature surveyed. , 

## Selection criteria
Inclusion standards: The search was strictly limited to “Article” and “Review Article” document types to ensure the academic quality of the analysis. No language restrictions were applied to capture a global perspective.
Exclusion standards: (i) non-research document types such as editorials, meeting abstracts, book chapters, proceedings papers, letters, corrections, or retracted publications; or (ii) content irrelevant to the specific topic of immune escape in LC immunotherapy.

## Literature screening and refinement methodology
The initial bibliographic search yielded 2882 candidate documents. Prior to screening, duplicate records were identified and removed using the “Remove Duplicates” function in CiteSpace. Through a rigorous, multi‑stage screening process that evaluated titles, abstracts, keywords, and article type for relevance to immune escape in LC immunotherapy, 69 items were removed due to insufficient thematic alignment and limited substantive content. The refined corpus totaled 2813 publications, comprising 2020 original articles and 793 comprehensive reviews (Supplementary Table S1).

## Bibliometric analytical frameworks
Three advanced bibliometric platforms were systematically used in a complementary manner for a multidimensional examination of the literature. VOSviewer was selected for its superior capability in visualizing distance-based network clusters, , while CiteSpace was utilized specifically for its strength in capturing temporal dynamics and citation bursts. , Biblioshiny served as a comprehensive tool for statistical metric analysis and conceptual mapping. 
Specifically, VOSviewer (1.6.19) served as a central component to create and interpret complex bibliographic networks, covering: (i) intricate mappings of scholarly collaborations across countries, institutions, and individual researchers; (ii) comprehensive co-citation analyses of articles and their references; (iii) nuanced visualizations of keyword co-occurrence; and (iv) network representations that apply node-size scaling to publication volume.
CiteSpace (version 6.2.R3) enabled: (i) advanced dual-map overlay analyses that uncover inter‑disciplinary citation pathways; and (ii) detection of citation bursts to identify emerging research directions and turning points in the field.
Biblioshiny (www.bibliometric.com), the web-based interface for the R package bibliometrix (version 4.3.1), was used to: (i) visualize global distributions of scholarly production; (ii) conduct thorough analyses of collaboration networks across geographical regions; (iii) develop thematic mappings and trend analyses for research topics; and (iv) investigate collaborative interaction dynamics across international research territories.

## Data processing and visualization
The analysis relied primarily on Excel 2021 for quantitative processing, including trend derivation, citation metric assessment, and computation of collaboration indices. Statistical comparisons between geographic regions were performed using standardized bibliometric indicators, including total publications (NP), total citations (TC), and citations per publication (CPP), to evaluate and compare the research output and impact of different countries. Recognizing that citation distributions are typically skewed and that mean values (CPP) can be influenced by extreme outliers, we further incorporated the H-index for each country in . The H-index was selected as a robust, domain-specific alternative to medians or confidence intervals, as it effectively filters out the noise of sporadic highly-cited articles while providing a balanced assessment of both research productivity and impact. To accurately model the publication growth trajectory, both linear and polynomial regression analyses were conducted. A 5th-order polynomial model was identified as the optimal fit (R2 = 0.984) and was subsequently used to forecast research output for the year 2025. To rigorously evaluate the predictive accuracy and reliability of the forecasting models, we further employed mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) and root mean square error (RMSE) as validation metrics, comparing the 5th-order polynomial model against a linear regression baseline. Crucially, to provide a robust statistical estimation of this future trend and address uncertainty, 95% prediction intervals (PI) were calculated to establish the confidence boundaries of the forecast. 
Prior to analysis, rigorous data cleaning was performed to ensure accuracy. We utilized the “Organization-Enhanced” field from WoSCC, which standardizes institutional names. Additionally, synonymous variants (such as the name of institutions and authors) were manually identified and merged using a thesaurus file (provided as Supplementary File 3) to eliminate duplication and disperse counting. To ensure the reproducibility of the study, specific parameters for the network visualizations were set as follows:
VOSviewer Settings: For cooperation networks, the minimum number of documents (T) and attraction/repulsion values were set as follows: countries (T = 10, 1/‒1), institutions (T = 30, 2/‒1), journals (T = 18, 1/‒1) and authors (T = 8, 1/‒6). For co-citation networks, thresholds were set for journals (T = 1,600 citations, 1/0), authors (T = 200 citations, 2/‒1), and references (T = 120 citations, 3/‒1). For keyword analysis, the minimum occurrence threshold was set to 24 (attraction/repulsion: 2/0). The selection of these thresholds was determined to optimize the signal-to-noise ratio, ensuring that the core knowledge structure is represented with maximum visual clarity.
CiteSpace Settings: A time slicing range of 1995–2025 (1 y per slice) was applied. Node selection was performed using the g-index, with the scale factor k set to 25 for reference burst analysis and 5 for keyword burst analysis.

## Results

## Quantitative analysis of publications
We identified a total of 2813 publications related to immune escape in LC immunotherapy, encompassing contributions from 75 countries, 3421 research institutions, and 18,023 authors, distributed across 693 journals. Longitudinal analysis from 1995 to 2025 reveals three distinct developmental phases exhibiting an overall annual growth rate of 22.69% (calculated using data through 2024) ( ). The initial phase (1995–2010) was characterized by minimal activity with fewer than 20 annual publications and limited citations, indicating a nascent stage of exploration. The acceleration phase (2011–2018) marked a period of rapid expansion, with publications increasing from 9 to 187 and citations rising exponentially from 609 to 6496, reflecting surging scientific interest. The maturation phase (2019–2024) is distinguished by sustained high productivity, with publications stabilizing between 320 and 376 annually while citations continued a steep upward trajectory, reaching 23,804 by 2024. Based on this, a 5th-order polynomial model was identified as the most accurate fit (R2 = 0.984), projecting an estimated 399 publications for 2025 (95% PI: 324–474). While alternative projections using exponential models were initially considered to account for the post-2011 surge, the polynomial model demonstrated superior performance in capturing the subtle transition toward a ‘maturation phase’ observed in the most recent three years. This approach acknowledges that while the field remains in a high-output state, its growth trajectory is increasingly aligning with a logistic (S-curve) pattern rather than indefinite exponential expansion, indicating a shift from foundational discovery to systematic clinical refinement. In contrast, the linear model yielded a lower fit (R2 = 0.681) and projected approximately 259 publications, significantly underestimating the recent exponential growth trend observed since 2011. Model validation using additional metrics further confirmed the superiority of the polynomial approach: the polynomial model achieved a MAPE of 12.42% and an RMSE of 15.28, whereas the linear model exhibited a substantially higher MAPE (215.14%) and RMSE (68.46). This comparison confirms that the polynomial model more accurately captures the field’s rapid acceleration compared to a linear projection. Collectively, this evolution underscores the field’s transformation from exploratory research into an established, highly impactful scientific domain with consistent growth momentum.

## Discussion

## General information
The bibliometric landscape of immune escape in LC immunotherapy is characterized by several noteworthy trends. The field’s exponential growth, particularly the dramatic acceleration after 2011, closely parallels landmark clinical breakthroughs of ICIs and their FDA approvals, suggesting that research momentum in this domain is inextricably linked to translational milestones. Crucially, this sustained upward trajectory is a direct quantifiable reflection of intensified global scientific effort, encompassing the expansion of dedicated research personnel and significant strategic funding initiatives in major research hubs like the US and China. However, the recent stabilization of annual output (320–376 publications) during 2019–2024 suggests the field is entering a maturity phase, where progress is driven more by incremental refinement than by paradigm-shifting discoveries. The fact that publication volume remains robust during this maturation phase further underscores the persistent commitment of the scientific community to resolving the complexities of immune escape mechanisms.
A stark contrast exists between research quantity and quality across geographic regions. China’s dominance in publication volume (n = 1,382) coupled with a lower citation impact (33.04 citations per publication) compared to the United States (101.70 citations per publication) likely reflects a “time-lag bias” inherent in bibliometric analysis. Since a significant portion of China’s research output has emerged during the recent “maturation phase” (2019–2024), these publications have had a shorter window to accumulate citations compared to foundational studies from Western hubs that were published during the “initial” and “acceleration” phases. Furthermore, network analysis suggests that citation impact is closely correlated with international collaboration centrality. Established Western institutions often occupy central nodes in global cooperation networks, facilitating broader dissemination and visibility of their findings. As emerging research communities in Asia continue to integrate into these global networks and their recent high-volume output matures, the gap between quantity and quality is anticipated to narrow.
Journal analysis underscores the interdisciplinary nature of immune escape research, bridging basic immunology and clinical oncology, while the prominence of open-access venues indicates a strong commitment to democratizing knowledge access. Notably, the co-citation network is anchored by high-impact general science journals, suggesting that therapeutic innovations are often rooted in fundamental biological insights rather than purely clinical observations. Collectively, these patterns indicate that while the field has achieved global reach, high-level influence remains concentrated in established scientific centers with mature research infrastructures.
While recent bibliometric analyses have explored related domains, our study occupies a unique niche by bridging the gap between clinical application and mechanistic understanding specifically in LC. For instance, a recent analysis of PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors in LC primarily focused on clinical predictors, treatment monitoring, and adverse effects of specific drug classes. In contrast, our study delves into the underlying biological drivers of resistance (immune escape) rather than the drug metrics themselves. Similarly, while another recent study mapped the global landscape of tumor immune escape across all cancer types, its pan-cancer scope inherently dilutes the specific nuances of the lung TME. By focusing exclusively on immune escape in LC, our analysis provides more granular insights into organ-specific frontiers – such as spatial microenvironment architecture, hypoxia-mediated suppression, and ferroptosis-induced evasion – which are critical for developing next-generation precision immunotherapies.

## Current research hotspots: Therapeutic strategies, tumor microenvironment, and biomarker development
The keyword clustering analysis revealed five distinct but interconnected research domains within immune escape mechanisms in LC immunotherapy. These clusters can be systematically organized into three major research directions that collectively represent the current landscape of this rapidly evolving field. Beyond identifying these hotspots, the convergence of citation bursts and cluster connectivity allows for a strategic discussion on translational prioritization.
From a clinical perspective, we propose that the “therapeutic strategy” axis, particularly the modulation of ferroptosis, warrants the highest translational priority. The evidence consolidated in underscores ferroptosis as a highly actionable target that can be harnessed to overcome ICI resistance. Secondly, research into the “tumor microenvironment” (specifically the hypoxia-PD-L1 axis) should be prioritized for combination therapy development, as the strong co-occurrence between Cluster 3 and Cluster 1 highlights a critical synergistic opportunity to bypass immune exclusion. Finally, the “biomarker development” direction, supported by advancements in computational predictive modeling, is prioritized as the essential infrastructure for precise patient stratification. By aligning these evidence-based priorities, the scientific community can more effectively bridge the gap between bibliometric trends and tangible clinical outcomes. 

## Therapeutic strategies and treatment integration (clusters 1 & 4)
The therapeutic landscape is characterized by two complementary clusters that reflect the evolution from conventional cancer treatments to modern immunotherapy approaches. Cluster 1 encompasses the broader treatment paradigm, integrating traditional modalities including chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and targeted therapy with emerging immunotherapeutic strategies. This cluster emphasizes the critical challenges of treatment resistance, EMT, and metastatic progression that drive immune escape mechanisms. , The inclusion of combination therapy approaches reflects the current clinical trend toward multi-modal treatment strategies designed to overcome therapeutic resistance. Recent reviews have systematically highlighted that combining ICIs with chemotherapy, antibody-drug conjugates, or novel immunotherapies is essential to counteract the diverse resistance mechanisms that develop after initial checkpoint blockade. Within these novel strategies, ferroptosis has emerged as a critical focal point, with well-cited, high-throughput research revealing its complex, dual-natured impact on the LC immune microenvironment. On one hand, ferroptosis serves as a potent mechanism of immunogenic cell death that synergizes with immunotherapy. High-throughput transcriptomic profiling has demonstrated that IFN-γ released by activated CD8+ T cells downregulates the expression of the cystine glutamate antiporter subunits SLC3A2 and SLC7A11, thereby sensitizing tumor cells to ferroptosis and enhancing the efficacy of ICIs. , 
Conversely, emerging evidence from single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) has highlighted an immunosuppressive facet: the premature ferroptosis of tumor-infiltrating immune cells. For instance, lipid peroxidation within the nutrient-deprived TME can trigger the ferroptosis of CD8+ T cells and dendritic cells via the CD36 or GPX4 pathways, leading to impaired anti-tumor surveillance and immune exhaustion. , This dichotomous role – where ferroptosis acts as both a facilitator of tumor clearance and a driver of immune dysfunction – necessitates a balanced therapeutic approach in clinical integration.
Cluster 4 represents the contemporary focus on ICIs, specifically highlighting PD-1/PD-L1 and CTLA-4 pathways that have revolutionized NSCLC treatment. The prominence of specific agents like nivolumab and pembrolizumab, alongside considerations of immune-related adverse events (IRAEs), demonstrates the clinical maturation of checkpoint inhibitor therapy. , The integration of EGFR mutations within this cluster underscores the ongoing challenge of optimizing immunotherapy in molecularly defined NSCLC subsets. 
These therapeutic clusters are intrinsically linked in clinical practice, where ICIs are increasingly combined with conventional therapies to overcome both primary and acquired resistance mechanisms, representing a comprehensive strategy to combat immune escape.

## Tumor microenvironment characterization and immune cell dynamics (clusters 2 & 3)
The cellular and molecular characterization of the TIME represents a fundamental research direction encompassing two complementary clusters. Cluster 2 focuses on the cellular immune landscape, delineating the complex interplay between effector immune populations (CD8+ T cells, NK cells, dendritic cells) and immunosuppressive cell types {regulatory T cells (Tregs), MDSCs, TAMs}. Emerging research specifically emphasizes the pivotal role of dendritic cells (DCs) in this landscape. Novel strategies, such as neoantigen-based DC vaccines, are being developed to enhance antigen presentation and prime tumor-specific T-cells, offering a promising avenue to reverse immune tolerance and overcome ICI resistance. , The specific inclusion of small cell lung cancer (SCLC) alongside these cellular components highlights the histotype-specific immune profiles that influence therapeutic responses and immune escape patterns.
Cluster 3 represents the advancement toward sophisticated molecular characterization of the TME, integrating genomic alterations (EGFR mutations), epigenetic regulators (lncRNAs), and stromal components (CAFs) with immune infiltration patterns. The prominence of single-cell RNA-sequencing technology reflects the field’s movement toward high-resolution dissection of TME heterogeneity. , T cell exhaustion, a central mechanism of immune escape, serves as the conceptual bridge linking molecular characterization to functional immune dysfunction. , 
The convergence of these clusters is evident in contemporary research that employs advanced molecular profiling techniques to understand how cellular interactions within the TME contribute to immune escape, with mechanistic insights from Cluster 3 providing molecular explanations for the cellular phenomena observed in Cluster 2.

## Translational biomarkers and precision immunotherapy (cluster 5)
Cluster 5, though containing fewer keywords, represents a critical translational research direction focused on biomarker development and liquid biopsy applications. The emphasis on biomarkers reflects the urgent clinical need for predictive and prognostic tools that can guide immunotherapy selection and monitor treatment response. Exosomes, as components of liquid biopsy approaches, represent an emerging noninvasive method for monitoring immune escape mechanisms and treatment resistance in real-time. 
This cluster serves as the essential translational interface that connects mechanistic discoveries from the microenvironment-focused clusters (2 and 3) with clinical applications in the therapeutic clusters (1 and 4). The development of actionable biomarkers is crucial for implementing precision immunotherapy approaches that can anticipate and circumvent immune escape mechanisms before they lead to treatment failure.

## Thematic mapping analysis reveals underdeveloped research frontiers in lung cancer immune escape: From spatial microenvironment dynamics to computational predictive modeling
The thematic map analysis reveals that Quadrant 4 contains basic and transversal themes fundamental to immune escape in LC immunotherapy but remain underdeveloped, representing critical future research directions requiring enhanced investigation. These themes can be systematically categorized into several emerging research priorities that warrant detailed exploration.

## Cellular microenvironment dynamics and spatial architecture
The underdeveloped positioning of TME, TIME, and immune microenvironment themes reflects the need for deeper mechanistic understanding of spatial heterogeneity within these environments and how cellular interactions facilitate immune escape. , Recent evidence demonstrates that spatial heterogeneity in the TME significantly influences immunotherapy outcomes, with distinct spatial arrangements of immune cells determining therapeutic resistance patterns. The positioning of CAFs as a basic theme highlights the critical gap in understanding their diverse functional roles, as CAFs can directly alter the immunosuppressive effect of the TME through their own metabolism and influence immune cell aggregation through cytokine and chemokine secretion, reducing immune surveillance and promoting immune escape. , Specifically, CAF abundance is associated with insensitivity to combination immune checkpoint blockade (ICB), with CAFs suppressing CD8+ T-cell infiltration and conferring resistance to ICB. The basic positioning of TAMs, MDSCs, and Tregs indicates insufficient understanding of their dynamic interactions, particularly how extensive crosstalk between CAFs and cellular components of the immune system contributes to immune escape through both biochemical and biomechanical mechanisms. 

## Advanced computational integration and predictive modeling
Machine learning and single-cell RNA-sequencing appear as basic themes requiring further development to fully decode immune escape patterns, with recent studies demonstrating that single-cell transcriptome profiling can identify distinct therapy-induced cancer cell transcriptomes associated with clinical response and predict immunotherapy efficacy. , Current research reveals that single-cell RNA profiling provides additional prognostic information based on the microenvironment and may help predict therapy response while revealing possible target cell populations for future therapeutic approaches. However, the basic positioning indicates that computational approaches remain insufficient for real-time prediction and intervention of resistance mechanisms. The integration challenge is particularly evident in the need to combine multiplex immunofluorescence images, histopathology, and RNA sequencing data to characterize spatial organization and identify specific lymphocyte phenotypes predictive of immunotherapy response. The clinical significance of these computational advances lies in the potential to move beyond the simplistic PD-L1 biomarker. By integrating multi-omics data, AI-driven models can facilitate “precision immuno-oncology,” enabling clinicians to identify non-responders early and select optimal combination strategies for individual patients, thereby maximizing efficacy while minimizing unnecessary toxicity. Clinically, this transition from reactive to predictive treatment monitoring represents a shift toward mitigating acquired resistance before it manifests as radiological progression.

## Novel cell death pathways and metabolic reprogramming
The thematic map analysis identifies ferroptosis and pyroptosis as basic themes, highlighting them as foundational yet underdeveloped areas that represent significant research opportunities. Crucially, ferroptosis exhibits a complex dual role in cancer immunity. On one hand, it can promote immune escape, particularly in early tumor stages, as ferroptotic cancer cells may release immunosuppressive lipid mediators that favor immunosuppressive responses or induce ferroptosis in infiltrating T cells. , On the other hand, recent evidence indicates that ferroptosis, necroptosis, and pyroptosis represent newly discovered forms of immunogenic cell death that can remodel the TME and enhance antitumor effects when combined with ICIs. The positioning of these pathways as basic themes suggests that their therapeutic targeting requires a more sophisticated understanding of timing and context-dependent effects on immune function to maximize therapeutic efficacy while minimizing immune suppression. To clarify these complex interactions, we have summarized the divergent roles of ferroptosis in the context of antitumor immunity in , highlighting the distinction between its immune-evasive and immunogenic mechanisms described in recent literature.

## Precision immunotherapy optimization and biomarker development
The basic positioning of ICIs (PD-1, PD-L1, CTLA-4), combination therapy, and biomarkers themes indicates fundamental gaps in optimizing these clinically established approaches. Despite widespread clinical application, PD-L1 expression does not sufficiently predict ICI efficacy in NSCLC patients, with TIME heterogeneity making drug resistance more likely to occur. The underdeveloped nature suggests future research should focus on understanding resistance mechanisms and developing combination strategies that can overcome primary and acquired resistance.

## Hypoxia-mediated immune suppression and metabolic vulnerabilities
The basic positioning of hypoxia themes highlights a critical knowledge gap, as hypoxic tumor environments are known to promote immune escape but the mechanistic details remain insufficiently characterized. Specifically, studies have established a direct molecular link between hypoxia and immune checkpoint regulation: hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α) binds directly to the hypoxia-response element (HRE) in the PD-L1 proximal promoter, thereby upregulating PD-L1 expression on tumor cells and suppressing T-cell effector function. Understanding how oxygen-deprived conditions interact with metabolic reprogramming to create immunosuppressive microenvironments represents a promising therapeutic avenue. Clinically, this implies that targeting the hypoxic niche – for instance, through HIF-1α inhibitors or hypoxia-activated prodrugs – could “normalize” the TME. This strategy holds the potential to convert “cold,” immune-excluded tumors into “hot” environments, thereby re-sensitizing drug-resistant NSCLC patients to checkpoint blockade therapy.

## Cancer stem cell-immune interface and epithelial-mesenchymal transition
The positioning of cancer stem cells and EMT as basic themes indicates insufficient investigation into how these cellular programs interface with immune escape mechanisms. Cancer stem cells exhibit enhanced ferroptosis resistance, suggesting they may use alternative survival mechanisms to evade both traditional therapies and immune surveillance. These findings suggest that targeting the intersection of stemness, EMT, and immune evasion represents an underdeveloped but potentially transformative research direction for overcoming therapeutic resistance in LC immunotherapy. The clinical relevance here is substantial: since EMT and stemness are primary drivers of metastasis and acquired resistance, co-targeting these plasticity programs alongside immune checkpoints could effectively halt disease progression and prevent metastatic relapse, addressing the major cause of mortality in LC.
In conclusion, the thematic map analysis reveals that future research in LC immune escape should prioritize several underdeveloped but fundamental directions including “spatial microenvironment architecture,” “computational predictive modeling,” “alternative cell death pathways,” “precision biomarker development,” “hypoxia-immune interactions,” and “stemness-EMT immune interfaces.” These basic and transversal themes represent critical knowledge gaps that, once addressed, could significantly advance our understanding of immune escape mechanisms and lead to more effective immunotherapeutic strategies for LC patients.

## Limitation and future directions

## Research limitations
This bibliometric analysis presents several inherent limitations that warrant consideration. First, the exclusive reliance onWoSCC, while ensuring data quality and standardization, may have excluded relevant publications indexed in other databases such as Scopus or PubMed, potentially underrepresenting certain geographic regions or emerging research outputs. However, the integration of multiple databases remains technically challenging, as exported source files from different databases are often incompatible for merging, and even when partial integration is achieved, incomplete metadata frequently renders many analytical functions inoperable in bibliometric software platforms. Second, the analysis is constrained by the search strategy and keyword selection, which may have inadvertently omitted studies using alternative terminologies or novel conceptual frameworks related to immune escape mechanisms. Third, the citation-based metrics inherently favor older publications, potentially undervaluing recent breakthrough discoveries that have not yet accumulated substantial citations. Fourth, the language bias toward English-language publications may have excluded significant contributions from non-English speaking research communities, particularly given the substantial research activity in Asian countries. Finally, the dynamic nature of this rapidly evolving field means that emerging research directions and paradigm shifts occurring after the search date may not be adequately captured in this analysis. Furthermore, regarding the computational modeling and prognostic biomarkers discussed in this study, it is important to acknowledge limitations related to data heterogeneity. Much of the analyzed literature relies on retrospective datasets from public repositories (e.g., TCGA, GEO), which may exhibit batch effects and inconsistencies in sequencing platforms. Consequently, the predictive models and “risk signatures” identified in bibliometric clusters often require rigorous external validation in large-scale, prospective clinical cohorts to verify their reproducibility and clinical generalizability before they can be integrated into routine practice. Additionally, the inherent sensitivity of bibliometric clustering algorithms to threshold settings means that while core research themes are robust, minor variations in parameters can influence the inclusion of peripheral data points.

## Future improvement directions
Future bibliometric investigations should prioritize the development of standardized data integration protocols and advanced computational tools capable of harmonizing metadata across different database platforms to overcome current technical barriers in multi-database analysis. Advanced natural language processing and machine learning algorithms should be employed to identify emerging terminologies and conceptual frameworks that may not be captured by traditional keyword-based searches. Real-time monitoring systems could be implemented to track citation bursts and emerging research trends as they develop, enabling more timely identification of paradigm shifts in immune escape research. Integration of altmetrics data, including social media mentions, policy citations, and clinical guideline references, would provide a more holistic view of research impact beyond traditional academic citations. Additionally, incorporating patent databases and clinical trial registries would better capture the translational impact of immune escape research and identify the pathway from basic discoveries to clinical applications. Finally, developing specialized bibliometric tools that can analyze the semantic evolution of research concepts over time would enhance our understanding of how immune escape mechanisms research has evolved and predict future research trajectories more accurately

## Conclusion
This comprehensive bibliometric analysis of 2813 publications spanning 1995–2025 reveals the evolution of immune escape research in LC immunotherapy from nascent investigations to a mature scientific domain. The field’s exponential growth, driven by landmark clinical breakthroughs in immune checkpoint inhibition, demonstrates the successful translation of mechanistic discoveries into therapeutic innovations. The identification of research clusters and thematic maps highlights potential hotspots in therapeutic strategies and TME characterization. However, it is imperative to interpret these projections with caution, as bibliometric trends primarily reflect academic attention and funding shifts, which do not always equate to immediate clinical efficacy.
The practical utility of these bibliometric findings extends across the scientific ecosystem, providing a roadmap for diverse stakeholders. For funding agencies and policymakers, these results act as a strategic compass to identify research “blind spots” and emerging frontiers, enabling evidence-based resource allocation that avoids redundancy and targets high-potential domains. For physicians and clinical practitioners, this analysis serves as a navigational tool to stay abreast of the rapidly shifting therapeutic landscape, facilitating the transition from single-biomarker reliance toward integrated multi-omics profiling and helping identify leading collaborative networks for clinical trials.
To translate these findings into practice, we propose specific actions. Funding agencies are encouraged to issue targeted requests for proposals (RFPs) on high-impact subjects, such as the synergistic mechanisms of ferroptosis and PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, the development of standardized spatial multi-omics platforms for TME profiling, and the discovery of novel biomarkers for predicting immune-related adverse events (IRAEs). Researchers should focus on strengthening international collaborations – especially between high-output and high-impact regions. To ensure scientific rigor and transparency, we recommend that future bibliometric studies should strictly adhere to open-science principles by depositing full curated publication lists and raw metadata to public repositories such as Zenodo or GitHub, facilitating independent verification and cross-database replication. Finally, robust prospective trials remain the gold standard to confirm whether these bibliometric trends definitively translate into improved patient outcomes.
