## Abstract
Cultural heritage tourism faces growing challenges in providing personalized experiences while ensuring sustainable site management, necessitating advanced technological solutions. Existing recommendation systems often fail to capture the complex spatiotemporal dynamics and diverse preferences of tourists, limiting their effectiveness. To address this, we propose a novel deep learning framework featuring three domain-specific innovations: (1) Heritage-aware Graph Neural Networks (H-GNNs) that model cultural significance propagation and temporal visitation dynamics unique to heritage sites, (2) Cultural Spatiotemporal Transformers (C-STTs) with heritage-specific attention mechanisms that balance cultural value preservation with tourist preferences, and (3) Heritage-adaptive Transfer Reinforcement Learning (H-TRL) that incorporates cultural site constraints and preservation priorities into the reward structure. Experimental results demonstrate improvements, with the GNN module achieving a 14% increase in behavior prediction accuracy, the STT variant reducing route optimization time by 16%, and the TRL module enhancing recommendation relevance by 12% over time. This study highlights the potential of the proposed framework to enhance personalized tourism experiences while supporting the sustainable management of cultural heritage sites, offering a scalable and adaptable solution for the industry.

## Introduction
Cultural heritage tourism represents a critical intersection between cultural preservation and economic development, serving over 400 million international travelers annually and contributing significantly to global GDP , . However, this sector faces unprecedented challenges in balancing visitor satisfaction with heritage preservation, particularly as digitalization reshapes tourist expectations while heritage sites grapple with overcrowding, resource constraints, and the need for sustainable management practices . Traditional one-size-fits-all approaches to heritage site management and visitor guidance often result in suboptimal experiences, where visitors receive generic information regardless of their cultural background, interests, or learning preferences, while site managers struggle to distribute visitor flows effectively and protect fragile cultural assets , .
The complexity of cultural heritage tourism stems from its multidimensional nature, encompassing temporal dynamics (seasonal patterns, historical contexts), spatial constraints (site geography, accessibility), cultural factors (significance, authenticity), and individual preferences (interests, demographics, social influences) , . Traditional recommendation systems, primarily designed for commercial applications, fail to capture these nuanced requirements . Existing approaches typically rely on content-based filtering or collaborative filtering methods that inadequately model the spatiotemporal dependencies inherent in heritage site visitation, the cultural significance propagation between related sites, and the long-term evolution of tourist preferences within heritage contexts , . Moreover, these systems often ignore critical heritage-specific constraints such as carrying capacity limits, preservation requirements, and cultural sensitivity considerations.
Recent advances in deep learning have demonstrated potential for addressing complex recommendation challenges, yet their application to cultural heritage tourism remains limited and fragmented – . While Graph Neural Networks have shown promise in modeling complex relationships , existing implementations fail to incorporate heritage-specific cultural significance propagation. Similarly, transformer architectures have proven effective for sequence modeling , , but current spatiotemporal variants do not address the unique constraints of heritage site visitation patterns – . Furthermore, reinforcement learning approaches for recommendation systems have not been adapted to handle the dual objectives of tourist satisfaction and heritage preservation, nor have they addressed the challenge of transferring knowledge across diverse heritage contexts.
To address these fundamental limitations, this study proposes a novel deep learning framework specifically designed for cultural heritage tourism, featuring three interconnected modules that collectively tackle the unique challenges of this domain. Our approach is motivated by three key insights: (1) cultural heritage sites exhibit unique relationship patterns where cultural significance propagates through historical, thematic, and geographical connections, requiring specialized graph modeling techniques; (2) heritage tourism involves complex spatiotemporal optimization where route planning must balance visitor preferences with site-specific constraints such as opening hours, capacity limits, and preservation requirements; and (3) heritage tourism contexts are diverse and evolving, necessitating adaptive learning mechanisms that can transfer knowledge across different cultural settings while continuously refining strategies based on real-world feedback.
The proposed framework integrates three domain-specific innovations to address these challenges systematically. First, Heritage-aware Graph Neural Networks (H-GNNs) capture the unique cultural significance propagation patterns in heritage tourism by incorporating cultural importance weighting and temporal heritage dynamics into the message-passing mechanism. This module addresses the limitation of existing graph-based approaches that treat all connections equally, regardless of cultural significance. Second, Cultural Spatiotemporal Transformers (C-STTs) optimize heritage routes by incorporating preservation constraints directly into the attention mechanism, enabling simultaneous consideration of visitor preferences and site management requirements. This innovation addresses the gap in existing spatiotemporal models that ignore heritage-specific constraints. Third, Heritage-adaptive Transfer Reinforcement Learning (H-TRL) enables adaptive strategy refinement across diverse heritage contexts by incorporating cultural sensitivity into the reward structure and exploration strategy. This module addresses the challenge of building recommendation systems that can adapt to new heritage contexts while maintaining respect for cultural values and preservation goals.
The contributions of this paper are as follows: 
Methodological Innovation We introduce a deep learning framework specifically designed for cultural heritage tourism that systematically addresses cultural significance propagation, heritage-constrained optimization, and adaptive cross-cultural learning through the integration of Graph Neural Networks, Spatiotemporal Transformers, and Transfer Reinforcement Learning.
Technical Advancement We develop novel architectural components including heritage-aware message passing, cultural significance attention mechanisms, and heritage-constrained reinforcement learning that capture domain-specific requirements previously unaddressed in the literature, enabling simultaneous optimization of recommendation accuracy and heritage preservation objectives.
Empirical Validation Through comprehensive experiments on multiple datasets spanning different cultural contexts, we demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach with measurable improvements in behavior prediction accuracy, route optimization efficiency, and long-term recommendation relevance, as validated through extensive ablation studies confirming the necessity of each framework component.
Practical Impact We provide a scalable solution for heritage site managers and tourism operators that balances visitor satisfaction with preservation goals. Our framework’s modular design enables deployment across diverse heritage contexts, from archaeological sites to cultural museums, while maintaining cultural sensitivity and supporting sustainable tourism practices.
These contributions address a critical gap in the literature where existing recommendation systems fail to account for the unique characteristics of cultural heritage tourism, offering both theoretical insights and practical solutions for this important domain.

## Related work

## Graph neural networks for recommender systems
Graph Neural Networks have emerged as a powerful paradigm for building recommender systems , , capitalizing on their inherent ability to model complex relationships and interactions within structured data. These networks propagate information through graph structures where nodes represent entities such as users or items, while edges capture their intricate relationships. This foundational architecture enables GNNs to naturally accommodate diverse data types in recommendation scenarios, ranging from social networks to item co-occurrence patterns.
The technical taxonomy of GNN-based recommender systems primarily centers on their graph construction methodologies and information propagation mechanisms. Prevalent approaches encompass user-item bipartite graphs , knowledge graphs , and social networks , each tailored to distinct recommendation objectives. The propagation techniques span from elementary neighborhood aggregation to sophisticated attention mechanisms, facilitating the capture of both local patterns and global structures in user preferences.
A fundamental advantage of GNNs in recommendation tasks resides in their capacity to incorporate rich contextual information beyond conventional user-item interactions. They can effectively model higher-order relationships, including social influence networks and item similarity structures, which frequently yield more accurate and diverse recommendations. This capability renders GNNs particularly well-suited for scenarios demanding complex relationship modeling, such as social recommendations or knowledge-aware systems.
Nevertheless, GNN-based recommender systems encounter several challenges that affect their practical deployment. Scalability remains a primary concern, as computational complexity scales with graph size and density. Additionally, recommendation quality heavily depends on the graph construction process, necessitating careful design and domain expertise. Cold-start problems and dynamic data updates also present persistent challenges in real-world applications.
Looking ahead, GNN-based recommender systems continue to advance alongside developments in graph representation learning and neural network architectures. Emerging trends include temporal information integration, multi-modal data fusion, and explainable recommendation techniques. These developments hold promise for enhancing the effectiveness and applicability of GNNs across diverse recommendation domains while addressing current limitations and expanding the scope of personalized recommendations.

## Spatiotemporal transformer models
Spatiotemporal Transformer Models represent a significant advancement in processing data that exhibits variation across both spatial and temporal dimensions, synergizing the strengths of transformer architectures with spatiotemporal analysis capabilities. At their core, these models employ self-attention mechanisms to capture complex dependencies between spatial locations and temporal sequences, enabling simultaneous modeling of geographical patterns and temporal dynamics. This distinctive combination allows the models to effectively handle diverse data types, from urban traffic patterns to environmental monitoring systems, by learning intricate relationships between spatial entities and their temporal evolution.
In practical applications, Spatiotemporal Transformer Models have demonstrated remarkable versatility across numerous domains. They excel particularly in urban computing scenarios , including traffic flow prediction and crowd movement analysis , where understanding both spatial layouts and temporal patterns is essential. Additional notable applications encompass weather forecasting, where models process satellite imagery and time-series data, and human activity recognition, which requires analysis of spatial body positions over time. The models’ capability to handle irregularly sampled data and varying spatial resolutions further extends their applicability to real-world scenarios with complex data structures.
The technical advantages of Spatiotemporal Transformer Models lie in their parallel processing capabilities and ability to capture long-range dependencies across both spatial and temporal dimensions. Unlike traditional sequence models that process data sequentially, these models can simultaneously attend to relevant spatial locations and temporal points, resulting in more efficient and accurate predictions. Furthermore, the architecture naturally supports multi-scale analysis, enabling pattern identification at different spatial resolutions and temporal frequencies. While computational complexity remains challenging, ongoing research in efficient attention mechanisms and model compression techniques continues to enhance the practicality of Spatiotemporal Transformer Models for large-scale applications.

## Transfer reinforcement learning
Transfer Reinforcement Learning synthesizes reinforcement learning principles with transfer learning techniques, enabling the application of learned policies from source domains to target domains. This approach leverages pre-existing knowledge to accelerate learning in new environments, substantially reducing exploration requirements in target domains. The fundamental concept revolves around identifying and transferring relevant features, policies, or value functions from source tasks to enhance learning efficiency in target tasks. This integration facilitates more effective knowledge utilization across related yet distinct problem spaces.
In practical applications, TRL has demonstrated substantial value in scenarios where training from scratch is computationally prohibitive or economically unfeasible. Common applications include robotics control , where policies learned in simulation environments transfer to real-world operations, and game playing , where strategies developed for one game adapt to similar but different games. Other prominent applications encompass autonomous vehicle navigation , where learning from diverse driving conditions transfers to new environments, and industrial automation, where control policies adapt across different manufacturing processes.
The technical advantages of TRL center on its ability to reduce training time and computational resource requirements in new environments. By transferring relevant knowledge, learning agents can initialize with superior policies, leading to faster convergence and enhanced performance. This approach also strengthens the generalization capabilities of reinforcement learning systems, making them more adaptable to task condition variations. Additionally, TRL helps address the exploration-exploitation dilemma by providing informed starting points for new tasks.
Despite these advantages, TRL faces several challenges that impact its effectiveness. The primary challenge involves identifying appropriate source tasks and determining transferable knowledge, as irrelevant or misleading information can impede learning. Another significant concern is negative transfer, where transferred knowledge actually degrades performance in the target domain. Furthermore, TRL success heavily depends on the similarity between source and target domains, requiring careful consideration of task relationships. Current research focuses on developing more robust transfer mechanisms and automatic methods for identifying transferable knowledge, promising to expand TRL applicability across diverse domains.

## Overview
This study presents a novel deep learning framework for personalized cultural heritage tourism recommendations, structured around three interconnected modules that address the core challenges of tourist behavior analysis, route optimization, and long-term strategy adaptation. The first module utilizes GNNs to model tourist behavior by analyzing visitor interactions, social network data, and site characteristics, generating personalized interest profiles and attraction recommendation scores. The second module introduces a Spatiotemporal Transformer variant, which integrates spatial and temporal information to optimize tourism routes. This hierarchical structure ensures a coherent and adaptive recommendation process.
By integrating GNNs, transformer architectures, and reinforcement learning, the framework addresses critical challenges in cultural heritage tourism, such as cold start problems, dynamic site variations, and the need for long-term adaptability. The modular design allows for flexibility in handling diverse data sources while maintaining a unified recommendation logic. ## Dataset and benchmarks
The datasets used in this study include the following four:
The Foursquare Check-in Dataset comprises user check-in data from the Foursquare social networking platform. It includes detailed records of user locations, timestamps, and venue information, providing a rich source of spatiotemporal data. The dataset is instrumental in understanding user mobility patterns and preferences, which are crucial for developing personalized location recommendation systems. By analyzing the interactions between users and locations, this dataset enables the identification of popular venues and the prediction of future check-ins, thereby enhancing the accuracy of recommendation algorithms.
The Gowalla Check-in Dataset contains check-in data from the Gowalla location-based social network. It features 196,591 nodes and 950,327 edges, representing the friendship network, along with 6,442,890 check-ins recorded between February 2009 and October 2010. This dataset is valuable for studying the interplay between social connections and mobility patterns. The comprehensive check-in records allow for the analysis of user movement and the exploration of how friendships influence location choices, contributing to the development of more sophisticated social network models and location-based services.
The GeoLife GPS Trajectory Dataset consists of GPS trajectories collected from 182 users over a period of three years. The dataset includes detailed location histories, enabling the analysis of user movement patterns and transportation modes. It is particularly useful for applications such as travel experience sharing and personalized recommendation systems. By mining the GPS data, researchers can identify popular travel routes and points of interest, as well as measure user similarity for friend and location recommendations, thereby enhancing the user experience in location-based social networks.
The Toulouse Cultural Heritage Dataset provides information on points of interest (POIs) related to cultural heritage in Toulouse, France. It includes data on various cultural sites, such as museums, historical landmarks, and tourist attractions. This dataset is essential for mapping and preserving cultural heritage within smart cities. By analyzing the spatial distribution and characteristics of cultural POIs, researchers can develop strategies for promoting cultural tourism and enhancing urban planning, thereby contributing to the sustainable development of smart cities.
The baseline models used in this study included the following five:
Matrix Factorization (MF) decomposes the user-item interaction matrix into latent factor representations for users and items. By learning these low-dimensional embeddings, MF captures the underlying patterns of user preferences and item characteristics, enabling personalized recommendations based on latent similarities.
Collaborative Filtering (CF) leverages user-item interaction data to identify similar users or items. It predicts user preferences by aggregating the preferences of similar users or recommending items similar to those the user has previously interacted with, relying on the assumption that users with similar tastes will prefer similar items.
DeepWalk applies random walk techniques on graph structures to generate node sequences, which are then processed using Skip-gram models to learn node embeddings. This approach captures the structural properties of graphs, making it suitable for recommendation tasks where relationships between entities are crucial.
Node2Vec extends DeepWalk by introducing biased random walks, allowing control over the exploration of local and global graph structures. This flexibility enables the model to capture both homophily and structural equivalence in graphs, enhancing the quality of learned embeddings for recommendation purposes.
Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) generalize convolutional operations to graph-structured data by aggregating information from neighboring nodes. This allows GCNs to learn node representations that incorporate both node features and graph topology, making them effective for recommendation tasks that require modeling complex relationships.

These replacements aimed to isolate the impact of each module on the overall performance and demonstrate the necessity of their integration. Replacing the STT module with LSTM led to a 10% reduction in ROI, from 0.88 to 0.79, and a 9% drop in USS, from 4.5 to 4.1 on the GeoLife dataset. Finally, removing the transfer learning capability from the TRL module resulted in a 7% decrease in USS, from 4.5 to 4.2. These results demonstrate that each module contributes to the model’s performance, with the GNN module enhancing recommendation accuracy, the STT module optimizing routes, and the TRL module ensuring adaptability and user satisfaction.
 The analysis reveals that the proposed model’s integration of advanced techniques (GNN, STT, TRL) provides advantages over simpler alternatives. The GNN module’s ability to model higher-order relationships in user behavior and attraction features leads to more accurate recommendations. The STT module’s dual attention mechanism enables efficient route optimization by simultaneously considering spatial and temporal constraints. The TRL module’s transfer learning capability ensures adaptability to new contexts and evolving user preferences, enhancing long-term satisfaction. These findings underscore the importance of combining graph-based modeling, spatiotemporal attention, and transfer reinforcement learning in building a robust and effective recommendation system for cultural heritage tourism.

## Conclusion and outlook

## Conclusion
This study presents a comprehensive deep learning framework for personalized cultural heritage tourism recommendations, integrating Graph Neural Networks, Spatiotemporal Transformers, and Transfer Reinforcement Learning to address the multifaceted challenges of tourist behavior analysis and route optimization. The GNN module effectively models tourist behavior through the construction of heterogeneous graphs that capture spatiotemporal dynamics and social influences, while the STT module optimizes tourism routes by seamlessly integrating spatial and temporal information. The TRL module demonstrates superior adaptability by transferring pre-trained knowledge to novel cultural heritage contexts and refining recommendation strategies based on real-time feedback and management constraints.
Experimental evaluation demonstrates substantial performance improvements across multiple dimensions: the GNN module achieves a 15% enhancement in behavior prediction accuracy, the STT module reduces route optimization time by 20% , and the TRL module provides a 25% improvement in long-term recommendation relevance. These empirical findings validate the effectiveness of the proposed framework in harmonizing personalized tourist experiences with sustainable cultural heritage site management practices. The integrated approach successfully addresses the complex interplay between individual preferences, spatiotemporal constraints, and conservation requirements, establishing a foundation for intelligent tourism recommendation systems in cultural heritage contexts.

## Outlook
Despite the promising results, this study encounters several limitations that warrant future investigation. First, the current framework relies predominantly on static historical data for model training, which may inadequately capture the dynamic and evolving nature of tourist behaviors and preferences. This dependency assumes temporal consistency in behavioral patterns, an assumption that may not hold in rapidly evolving cultural heritage tourism environments. To address this limitation, future research will focus on incorporating real-time data streams into the model architecture through the development of adaptive algorithms capable of continuous model updates based on live data sources, including social media trends, meteorological conditions, and instantaneous visitor feedback. Furthermore, the integration of edge computing technologies will be explored to enable faster data processing and minimize latency in real-time recommendation delivery.
Second, the framework exhibits insufficient consideration for the heterogeneity of cultural heritage sites and their distinctive management constraints. The current generalized approach may inadequately address the specific requirements and challenges of diverse cultural heritage contexts, such as archaeological sites, museums, or natural heritage areas. Future research will prioritize the development of a modular framework enabling site-specific customization through specialized sub-models tailored to different heritage site characteristics. This approach will incorporate domain-specific knowledge and facilitate collaboration with site managers to refine recommendation strategies. Extensive case studies across diverse cultural heritage contexts will be conducted to validate the adaptability and effectiveness of these improvements.
Third, potential bias in training data poses a significant concern, potentially leading to inequitable or exclusionary recommendations. The current framework utilizes data collected from a demographically limited tourist population, which may not adequately represent the preferences and behaviors of diverse visitor groups, including international tourists, individuals with disabilities, or underrepresented communities. To mitigate this issue, future research will prioritize the collection and integration of more inclusive and representative datasets through carefully designed data collection protocols ensuring broad demographic representation. Additionally, fairness audits will be implemented to identify and address algorithmic biases, while explainable AI techniques will be developed to provide transparency in the recommendation process and ensure adherence to ethical guidelines.
This study contributes an innovative deep learning framework for personalized cultural heritage tourism recommendations while identifying critical limitations and establishing future research directions, thereby providing a foundation for achieving optimal balance between enhanced tourist experiences and sustainable heritage site management.
