## Abstract

## Background
Arthroscopic shoulder surgeries rank high among the most commonly performed orthopedic procedures at ambulatory surgery centers (ASCs). However, evolving Medicare reimbursement policies have impacted the financial landscape of these procedures. Understanding the utilization and reimbursement trends is paramount to providing accessible patient care for surgeons and facilities. This study reviews the recent national trends in utilization and billing practices for arthroscopic shoulder operations performed in ASCs for Medicare patients from 2013 to 2022.

## Methods
This study analyzed Medicare Part B claims data using the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services Medicare Physician & Other Practitioners database. Current Procedural Terminology (CPT) codes for arthroscopic shoulder operations were used to identify data from 2013 to 2022. The top 4 utilized arthroscopic shoulder CPT codes were included for analysis. Outcomes analyzed included yearly service counts and allowed reimbursement rates from Medicare. The reimbursements reported in the database were a combined value of both the surgeon fee and ASC facility fee. This data was stratified by geographical region. All monetary values for charges and reimbursements were adjusted to the 2022 US dollar.

## Results
A total of 435,094 arthroscopic shoulder Medicare claims over the 4 most utilized CPT codes were identified between 2013 to 2022. The number of procedures increased annually by an average of 0.7%, resulting in an overall increase of 6.9% over this 10-year period (from 37,796 to 40,397, P = .180). Arthroscopic rotator cuff repair (CPT 29827) increased by an average of 3.1% annually, a substantial overall increase of 36.1% (P = .004). During the study period, average reimbursements decreased by 2.4% (from $1,782 to $1,740, P = .086). Trends in utilization and billing in ASCs varied by procedure and region, with the South having the highest utilization consistently while the Northeast had the highest reimbursement rate despite a decline. Other regions, such as the West, Midwest, and South, exhibited growth in reimbursement trends.

## Conclusion
Arthroscopic shoulder procedures are increasingly performed in ASCs. However, reimbursements for these procedures declined during the study period (2013-2022). These findings highlight the growing dominance of ASCs as a preferred surgical setting for shoulder arthroscopy procedures. However, this pattern of decreasing reimbursement could threaten the financial sustainability of these procedures. Policy reforms aimed toward securing efficient and cost-effective avenues to provide high-value care while still fostering incentives for physicians to treat patients covered by Medicare are paramount.

Arthroscopic shoulder procedures are among the most frequently performed orthopedic surgeries in the United States, commonly used to treat conditions such as rotator cuff injuries, labral tears, and degenerative or traumatic shoulder pathologies. Ambulatory surgery centers (ASCs) have emerged as a preferred venue for performing arthroscopic shoulder surgeries and other routine orthopedic procedures. , , , These centers offer a more efficient and cost-effective alternative to traditional hospital outpatient departments (HOPDs) and inpatient facilities. Through careful patient selection, procedures performed at ASCs have been shown to achieve equivalent or better outcomes, coupled with shorter lengths of stay and lower complication rate , , , , , , , With shorter stays, lower complication rates, and streamlined workflows, ASC shoulder arthroscopy procedures have become pivotal in meeting the rising demand for minimally invasive shoulder surgeries.
While the demand for shoulder arthroscopy at ASCs continues to grow, the declining Medicare reimbursement trends in orthopedics have raised questions regarding the financial sustainability of these procedures. Medicare, the largest US insurer covering 21% of health-care spending, determines reimbursement rates annually through a fee-for-service model based on Current Procedural Terminology (CPT) codes. Between 2000 and 2020, Medicare reimbursement for shoulder and elbow procedures decreased by up to 30%, mirroring broader trends across orthopedics. , , , , These reductions threaten the feasibility of ASC-based surgeries, potentially discouraging providers from offering these services and exacerbating disparities in care access, particularly for Medicare beneficiaries.
Given the growing reliance on ASCs for performing arthroscopic shoulder surgeries, it is crucial to evaluate the financial dynamics of these procedures. A study by Smith et al analyzed utilization and reimbursement data for surgeons for shoulder surgery from 2000 to 2020. However, there is a paucity of data analyzing trends in Medicare reimbursement specific for ASCs. Moreover, while Harkin et al's study compared trends in reimbursement for facility and surgeon fees for ASCs from 2018 to 2024, there is a lack of context on utilization changes between CPT codes and regional differences. This study aimed to analyze national trends in utilization and Medicare reimbursement rates for arthroscopic shoulder procedures performed in ASCs from 2013 to 2022. By examining these trends, this study aims to provide a deeper understanding of the evolving economic landscape of arthroscopic shoulder surgeries and to highlight opportunities for policy interventions to preserve access to care.

## Materials and methods
A retrospective analysis was conducted using publicly available Medicare databases to examine trends in arthroscopic shoulder procedures performed in ASCs from 2013 to 2022. The study period was selected to include the most recent updated data (2022) and 10 years prior to this. CPT codes representing arthroscopic shoulder surgeries were identified. CPT codes were further verified to comply with the ASC Covered Procedures List, established by the American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons, to ensure the safety and appropriateness of procedures performed at these centers. From an initial list of 12 CPT codes representing arthroscopic shoulder surgeries, only the 4 most commonly billed codes were included in the final analysis ( ). The remaining 8 codes were excluded due to consistently low utilization, defined as fewer than 100 billed cases annually. This exclusion ensured that the analysis focused on procedures with sufficient data to yield reliable and meaningful insights.
 Based on the CPT code, the data for ASC shoulder arthroscopy procedures were accessed from the Medicare Part B database. The Medicare Physician & Other Practitioners database contains information for Medicare Part B claims, including utilization, billing (submitted charges, Medicare reimbursement), geographical location, and clinical setting. These databases have been widely used in orthopedic research to assess trends in financial metrics and utilization patterns. , , , , Of note, the reimbursements reported in the database were a combined value of the surgeon and ASC facility fees. The dataset was filtered for claims from orthopedic surgeons in ambulatory service centers. Any procedures performed outside of the United States or unknown locations were excluded. Key variables collected included yearly service counts and allowed reimbursement rates from Medicare for each CPT code and geographical location. The monetary metrics were adjusted for inflation to 2022 US dollars to ensure consistent financial comparisons over time. The geographical location was further grouped by the 4 regions of the United States (Northeast, Midwest, South, Southwest, and West). 

## Statistical analysis
Descriptive statistics, including mean and standard deviation, were calculated annually for reimbursements and procedure volumes for each CPT code. The total Medicare payments were calculated as the multiple of service volume and average reimbursement. The Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR%) was used to quantify the year-over-year growth of procedure utilization and reimbursement rates for each CPT code. CAGR represents the geometric progression ratio, providing a consistent annual growth rate over the specified period. This metric allowed for a standardized comparison of trends across CPT codes and settings. Linear regression analysis was performed for all values during the study period to determine the significance of changes (P value < .05 was considered significant). In addition, geographical variations in utilization per million population and reimbursement rates were analyzed across US regions to identify regional disparities. The utilization values for each region were adjusted to the region's population by calculating the utilization per million population. The visual representation of interstate variation was created using Plotly (version 5.23; Plotly Technologies Inc., Montreal, CA, USA). 

## Results

## Overall trends in utilization and reimbursement
From 2013 to 2022, a total of 435,094 arthroscopic shoulder procedures were performed in ASCs, reflecting an overall increase in utilization of 6.9% over the study period (CAGR = 0.7%, P = .180, ). Utilization peaked in 2019 at 52,479 procedures but subsequently declined to 40,397 in 2022. Average inflation-adjusted ASC reimbursement per procedure decreased from $1,782 in 2013 to $1,740 in 2022, representing a 2.4% overall decline (CAGR = −0.2%, P = .086). Despite declining per-procedure reimbursement, total Medicare payments for these arthroscopic shoulder procedures increased by 4.3%, from $67.4 million in 2013 to $70.3 million in 2022 (CAGR = 0.4%, P = .941). This increase was driven by the overall growth in utilization over the study period.
 

## CPT-specific trends
Analysis of individual CPT codes revealed marked variability in utilization and average inflation-adjusted reimbursement patterns. CPT 29822 (shoulder arthroscopy with superior labrum anterior-posterior (SLAP) débridement) experienced a significant decline in utilization, decreasing by 81.3% from 3,783 procedures in 2013 to 708 in 2022 (CAGR = −15.4%, P = .0001, ). However, reimbursement for CPT 29822 remained stable, increasing by 1.5% during the study period (CAGR = 0.2%, P = .982, ). In contrast, CPT 29823 (shoulder arthroscopy with extensive débridement) saw an 11.0% increase in utilization (CAGR = 1.0%, P = .128), while reimbursement decreased substantially by 63.4% (CAGR = −9.6%, P = .003). For CPT 29824 (shoulder arthroscopy with distal clavicle resection), utilization declined by 10.9% (CAGR = −1.1%, P = .372), with reimbursement showing negligible change at −0.4% overall (CAGR = 0.0%, P = .104). CPT 29827 (shoulder arthroscopy with rotator cuff repair) exhibited the most significant growth in utilization, increasing by 36.1% from 16,843 procedures in 2013 to 22,919 in 2022 (CAGR = 3.1%, P = .004). Reimbursement for CPT 29827 declined slightly by 1.6% (CAGR = −0.2%, P = .845).
 

## Regional trends
The regional analysis highlighted significant variability in utilization and average inflation-adjusted reimbursement across regions. In 2022, the South reported the highest utilization per million population (137.0), followed by the Midwest (104.3), Northeast (74.1), and the West (101.3) ( ). From 2013 to 2022, the Northeast showed a substantial rise of 37.2% in utilization (CAGR = 3.6%, P = .019), indicating significant regional growth. The Midwest exhibited modest growth in utilization, increasing by 4.6% (CAGR = 0.5%, P = .229), while the South had only a slight increase of 1.0% (CAGR = 0.1%, P = .194). In contrast, the West experienced an 8.8% decline (CAGR = −1.0%, P = .913), reflecting a notable decrease in procedural volume over the study period.
 Regarding reimbursement, the Northeast consistently maintained the highest average reimbursement levels, with an average reimbursement of $1,947 in 2022. However, the reimbursement decreased by 5.4% over the study period (CAGR = −0.6%, P = .173, ). In contrast, the West followed with an average reimbursement of $1,891.2 in 2022, showing an 11.2% increase (CAGR = 1.2%, P = .288). The Midwest saw an average reimbursement of $1,747.4 in 2022, reflecting a growth of 7.9% (CAGR = 0.8%, P = .385). Finally, the South demonstrated the lowest reimbursement in 2022 at $1,729.2, with a modest increase of 3.7% during the study period (CAGR = 0.4%, P = .145). These findings reveal a complex interplay between utilization and reimbursement trends, with the Northeast maintaining the highest reimbursement despite a decline, while other regions, such as the West, Midwest, and South, exhibited growth in reimbursement trends.
 

## Discussion
This study aimed to evaluate the national trends in utilization and reimbursements for arthroscopic shoulder surgeries performed in ASCs between the years of 2013 and 2022. The findings reveal a modest growth in utilization but a decline in inflation-adjusted average reimbursement rates, reflecting broader economic and policy challenges in ASC-based orthopedic care. Moreover, there were variations in utilization and reimbursement trends across different CPT codes and regions. These findings highlight the complex interplay between procedural volume, reimbursement rates, and regional variation in ASC usage, emphasizing the need for policy interventions to ensure sustainable care delivery.
The overall increase in utilization of shoulder arthroscopy procedures by 6.9% during the study period reflects the growing reliance on ASCs for delivering high-quality, cost-effective orthopedic care. This trend is consistent with prior studies documenting a nearly 9% annual increase in ASC procedural volumes among Medicare beneficiaries between 2012 and 2017. Notably, CPT 29827 (rotator cuff repair) exhibited the most substantial growth, while CPT 28722 (SLAP débridement) showed a large decline in volume during our study. While rotator cuff repair was rarely proposed to elderly patients in the past, recent studies have shown that rotator cuff repair was very effective in elderly patients with superior outcomes over the traditional choice of decompression alone. , In contrast, there has been an overall shift in biceps tenodesis becoming a preferred option of treatment over débridement alone in the management of SLAP lesions due to better clinical outcomes and increased incidence of concomitant rotator cuff tears, especially in the middle-aged to older patient population. , , These clinical findings align with the observed shift toward increased utilization of rotator cuff repair procedures and reduced reliance on SLAP débridement in the Medicare population. Nevertheless, despite the shift between CPT codes, the overall rise in total utilization underscores the increasing demand for ASC shoulder arthroscopy.
Significant regional disparities in utilization were observed, with the South having the highest utilization per population (143.2 per million people) and the Northeast having the lowest (74.1 per million people) in 2022. These findings may reflect uneven access to ASC services across the United States, driven by factors such as geographic availability of ASCs, regional payer policies, and demographic differences. However, regions with historically lower ASC utilization demonstrated significant growth during the study period, with the Northeast showing a 37.4% increase in utilization. This growth reflects efforts to expand ASC access, likely driven by the financial advantages and efficiency of the ASC model. ASCs are known for delivering the same high-quality care as HOPDs but at significantly lower costs. Wang et al reported a 26% average cost savings for orthopedic procedures performed in ASCs compared to HOPDs, driven by lower facility fees and patient payments. This cost-effectiveness benefits both patients and payers, making ASCs an attractive option for addressing rising health-care expenditures. The observed growth in ASC utilization, particularly in regions like the Northeast, underscores the success of these expansion efforts to match those in other regions like the South, which have implemented ASCs earlier. As hospitals and policymakers continue to promote ASC adoption, this trend holds promise for improving access to affordable, high-quality surgical care across the United States.
Despite an overall increase in procedural volumes for ASC-based shoulder arthroscopy, inflation-adjusted average Medicare reimbursement per procedure declined by 2.4% during the study period, a trend consistent with prior research showing that reimbursements for various procedures across subspecialties are often failing to keep pace with rising charges. , Pollock et al reported a striking 34% decrease in reimbursements for 21 CPT codes related to shoulder and elbow procedures between 2000 and 2020, underscoring the long-term financial pressures faced by ASCs. Declining reimbursement rates threaten the financial viability of ASCs, with prior studies noting that inadequate compensation has led some centers to exit the Medicare program, limiting access to affordable care for beneficiaries. , , Persistent declines in reimbursement may discourage providers from expanding ASC services, especially in regions with high overhead costs or lower reimbursement rates. To address these challenges, policymakers should prioritize reforms to the Medicare fee schedule to better align reimbursement with inflation-adjusted costs and implement incentives to support the continued growth and sustainability of ASC-based care.
Regional variation in reimbursement trends further complicates the financial landscape for ASCs. Substantial differences in regional reimbursement were observed during the study period, with the Northeast having the highest reimbursement levels, followed by the West, and similar values for the South and Midwest. Similar regional disparities were identified by Lopez et al in their analysis of total shoulder arthroplasty, with significantly higher reimbursements in the Northeast and West compared to the South and Midwest, findings consistent with our results and suggesting a broader pattern across orthopedic shoulder procedures. Geographic adjustments, such as the Geographic Practice Cost Index, may partially account for these trends, as regions with higher practice costs are often allocated elevated reimbursements. However, concerns about the adequacy and fairness of Geographic Practice Cost Index adjustments persist, particularly for rural and underserved areas. While urban areas may experience higher costs due to factors such as rent and salaries, rural providers face a unique challenge of higher operational costs relative to their income. Reimbursements for total shoulder arthroplasty were also reported to be significantly higher in metropolitan areas when compared to rural areas, corroborating our concern of rural-urban discrepancies. Policymakers must consider these disparities to ensure that variations in reimbursement between regions do not exacerbate issues with access to care, particularly in underserved and rural communities.

## Limitations and future directions
This study is subject to several limitations. First, the analysis is limited to Medicare beneficiaries, and trends among privately insured or self-pay patients may differ. Second, the database does not account for combined CPT codes or multiple procedures performed during a single surgical encounter, potentially underestimating the true cost dynamics of shoulder arthroscopy. In addition, while geographic variation was analyzed, factors such as local cost of living, workforce availability, and payer mix were not fully considered. Future research should aim to integrate clinical outcomes with financial metrics to provide a more comprehensive understanding of the impact of reimbursement trends on patient care.

## Conclusion
Arthroscopic shoulder procedures in ASCs have become increasingly utilized, reflecting their growing role in delivering cost-effective orthopedic care. However, declining Medicare reimbursement rates threaten the financial viability of these procedures. Policymakers must consider targeted reforms to the physician fee schedule and regional payment adjustments to sustain ASC-based care and ensure equitable access to high-quality services. Future studies should evaluate the relationship between financial and clinical outcomes to further guide evidence-based policy development.
