## Abstract

## Background
Drug resistance in oral cancer presents a significant challenge in clinical treatment. Understanding the research landscape through bibliometric analysis can provide valuable insights into the development and trends in this field.

## Objective
To comprehensively analyze the global research trends, collaboration patterns, and emerging topics in oral cancer drug resistance research from 2000 to 2024 using bibliometric methods.

## Methods
Publications related to oral cancer drug resistance were retrieved from the Web of Science core collection database. CiteSpace and VOSviewer were employed to analyze publication trends, research collaboration networks, and keyword co-occurrence patterns. The analysis included annual publication outputs, country contributions, institutional productivity, author collaborations, journal distributions, and research hotspots.

## Results
A total of 971 publications were identified, showing a significant increase in research output over the study period, particularly after 2014. China led in publication output (485 articles), followed by Japan (130 articles) and the United States (118 articles). China Medical University was the most productive institution (50 publications). The International Journal of Molecular Sciences, Cancers, and Oral Oncology were the primary publishing venues. Keyword analysis revealed an evolution from basic drug resistance mechanisms to emerging research areas including cancer stem cells, tumor microenvironment, and extracellular vesicles.

## Conclusions
This bibliometric analysis demonstrates the rapid growth and evolving nature of oral cancer drug resistance research. The findings highlight the shift towards more sophisticated research areas and strong international collaboration patterns. These insights can guide future research directions and collaboration opportunities in this field.

## Introduction
Head and neck malignancies encompass a diverse group of tumors affecting various anatomical sites, including both aggressive carcinomas and locally invasive tumors such as ameloblastoma [ , ]. Among these, oral cancer, particularly oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC), represents one of the most challenging malignancies due to its high mortality rate and treatment resistance [ ]. The management of OSCC is significantly complicated by drug resistance, which compromises treatment effectiveness and leads to poor prognosis. Particularly concerning is the development of resistance to standard chemotherapeutic agents such as cisplatin, 5-fluorouracil (5FU), and doxorubicin. A meta-analysis revealed that approximately 33% of oral cancer cases exhibit resistance to cisplatin, highlighting the need for alternative or adjunctive therapies to improve treatment efficacy [ ]. Various molecular mechanisms contribute to this resistance, including alterations in non-coding RNAs and cellular signaling pathways. Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) and microRNAs (miRNAs) play significant roles in drug resistance by modulating gene expression and cellular processes such as apoptosis, drug efflux, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) [ , ]. Differential expression of miRNAs, such as miR-375 and miR-27, has been linked to varying levels of resistance in oral cancer cell lines, suggesting their involvement in regulatory pathways that affect drug responsiveness [ ]. Pathways such as PI3K/AKT, NF-κB, TGF-β, and EMT are involved in enhancing cancer cell survival and resistance to chemotherapy, providing potential targets for therapeutic intervention [ ]. In addition, drug resistance in oral cancer can be intrinsic or acquired, influenced by factors such as genetic mutations, tumor heterogeneity, immune system interactions, and microenvironmental conditions [ ]. Strategies such as early detection, adaptive monitoring, targeted protein degradation, precision medicine, and novel drug development have been explored to overcome drug resistance [ ].
Bibliometrics is a powerful tool for uncovering research hotspots, trends, and knowledge structures across various fields. By employing mathematical and statistical methods to analyze textual information, bibliometrics provides a quantitative assessment of research outputs, revealing the developmental trajectories and thematic priorities within a discipline. Several previous studies focusing on various cancer types (e.g., breast cancer [ ], head and neck cancer [ ], bladder cancer [ ], ovarian cancer [ ], colorectal cancer [ ]) have utilized bibliometric analysis to explore the research landscape of drug-resistant tumors by analyzing publication trends, research hotspots, and collaborative networks. Specifically, bibliometric analysis can help identify key contributing authors, institutions, and countries, while also mapping the evolution of research themes and emerging frontiers in the field. Through visualization techniques and quantitative indicators, this approach offers valuable insights into the intellectual structure and development of scientific knowledge. In the context of oral cancer drug resistance research, a comprehensive bibliometric analysis would be particularly valuable given the rapid expansion of literature in this field and the pressing need to systematically understand research patterns and priorities to guide future investigations. To the authors' knowledge, there is still no comprehensive bibliometric analysis specifically focused on drug resistance in oral cancer research, despite its critical importance in clinical practice and the growing body of literature in this field.
Therefore, this study aims to conduct a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of drug resistance research in oral cancer from 2000 to 2024 using the Web of Science database. By utilizing visualization tools such as CiteSpace and VOSviewer, we systematically analyzed publication trends, research hotspots, collaborative networks, and emerging themes in this field. Specifically, we examined annual publication outputs, leading countries, institutions, authors, and journals contributing to this research area. Through keyword co-occurrence analysis, we identified key research themes and their temporal evolution. Additionally, we analyzed collaboration patterns among researchers, institutions, and countries to understand the intellectual structure of this field. The findings of this study not only provide valuable insights into the current state and developmental trajectory of oral cancer drug resistance research but also offer guidance for future research directions and potential collaborative opportunities in this critical area.

## Methods

## Data source and collection
A comprehensive literature search was conducted using the Web of Science Core Collection database. The search strategy was designed to capture publications related to drug resistance in oral cancer using the following query string (Table  ): (((TS = “oral cancer*” OR “oral tumor*” OR “oral tumour*” OR “oral neoplasm*” OR “oral carcinoma*” OR “mouth cancer*” OR “mouth neoplasm*” OR “oral squamous cell carcinoma*” OR “OSCC” OR “oral cavity cancer*” OR “oral cavity neoplasm*” OR “oral cavity tumor*” OR “oral cavity tumour*” OR “oral cavity carcinoma*” OR “intraoral cancer*” OR “intraoral carcinoma*” OR “tongue cancer*” OR “tongue carcinoma*”) AND (“drug resist*” OR “chemoresist*” OR “pharmacoresist*” OR “multi-drug resist*” OR “multidrug resist*” OR “MDR” OR “chemical resist*” OR “medicine resist*” OR “pharmaceutical resist*” OR “drug tolera*” OR “drug response*” OR “drug sensitivity” OR “drug insensitivity” OR “cisplatin resist*” OR “chemotherapy resist*” OR “Chemotherapeutic Resistance” OR “Chemotherapy resistance” OR “chemosensitivity” OR “cisplatin-resistant” OR “Resistance to cisplatin” OR “anticancer drug resist*” OR “anti-cancer drug resist*”))). The search was limited to publications from January 1, 2000, to December 31, 2024. To ensure data quality, notifications, files, newspapers, news items, and publications without author information were excluded. A total of 971 publications were retrieved for bibliometric analysis.
 

## Research tools and methods
This study employed a combination of sophisticated bibliometric analysis tools to ensure comprehensive evaluation of the literature. VOSviewer (version 1.6.20) and CiteSpace (version 6.2R6) were selected as the primary visualization software tools for their complementary capabilities in analyzing bibliometric networks and temporal patterns. These tools facilitated the creation of knowledge maps by analyzing relationships within the research information, enabling researchers to gain detailed insights into the development of the field. Additionally, Origin2024 was utilized for generating publication trend graphs and conducting statistical analyses.

## Data analysis and visualization
The bibliometric analysis encompassed multiple dimensions of the research landscape. Publication trends were examined through annual output analysis, revealing the temporal development of the field. Country and institutional analyses were conducted through co-authorship network analysis, identifying leading contributors and collaboration patterns. Author collaboration networks were mapped to understand research team formations and knowledge flow patterns. Journal analysis revealed the primary venues for research dissemination in this field. Research hotspots and trends were identified through keyword co-occurrence analysis and burst detection, with timeline visualization tracking the evolution of research themes over the study period. Raw data extracted from Web of Science underwent preprocessing to standardize institution names, author names, and keywords, ensuring consistency in the analysis. Network visualizations were optimized for clarity, with different colors representing distinct clusters or time periods, and node sizes indicating the relative importance of elements in the network. Through this comprehensive analytical approach, the study aimed to provide a thorough understanding of the research landscape in oral cancer drug resistance.

## Discussion
This comprehensive bibliometric analysis of oral cancer drug resistance research reveals several significant findings. Our analysis shows that publications in this field experienced substantial growth from 2000 to 2024, particularly after 2014, with a notable surge in annual publications from 33 in 2014 to 110 in 2024. China emerged as the dominant contributor with 485 publications, followed by Japan (130 publications) and the United States (118 publications), reflecting a significant shift in research leadership toward Asian countries. At the institutional level, China Medical University led with 50 publications, while the International Journal of Molecular Sciences was the most prominent publishing venue with 34 publications. The keyword analysis identified key research themes, with “expression” (265 occurrences), “head” (255 occurrences), and “oral squamous cell carcinoma” (253 occurrences) being the most frequently appearing terms. The clustering analysis revealed nine major research clusters, reflecting distinct research directions from basic drug resistance mechanisms to emerging areas such as extracellular vesicles and tumor microenvironment interactions. Citation burst analysis further indicated a temporal evolution in research focus, transitioning from fundamental drug resistance studies in the early 2000s to more sophisticated molecular and cellular mechanisms in recent years.
As shown in Fig.  , the bibliometric analysis of research on tumor resistance, specifically oral cancer, from 2004 to 2012 highlights the increasing focus on key terms such as “cisplatin resistance,” “multidrug resistance,” “chemoresistance,” and “cancer stem cells.” The frequency of the keyword “cisplatin resistance” (86 times) suggests that the resistance to cisplatin poses a significant challenge in cancer treatment. The research highlights several molecular mechanisms contributing to cisplatin resistance in oral cancer, including p22phox protein [ ], endoplasmic reticulum stress [ ], mitochondrial DNA mutations [ ], chromatin remodeling genes (e.g., HIST3H2A and HIST3H2B) [ ], and signaling pathways (e.g., p62-mTORC1 signaling [ ], Hippo-YAP signaling [ ]). Combining cisplatin with other agents, such as CXCR4 inhibitors or CDK4/6 inhibitors, has demonstrated synergistic effects in reducing tumor growth and overcoming resistance in oral cancer [ , ]. The term “multidrug resistance” appeared 78 times, indicating a broad interest in the phenomenon where cancer cells become resistant to multiple drugs. This resistance is primarily due to various cellular and molecular mechanisms that allow cancer cells to evade the cytotoxic effects of drugs, including the overexpression of ATP-binding cassette (ABC) transporters (e.g., ABCC1 and ABCG2) [ ], activation of the Hedgehog (Hh) signaling pathway [ ], and cancer stem-like cells especially side population (SP) cells [ ]. Many strategies have been developed to overcome MDR in oral cancer, for example, compounds like compound 43 [ ], mesoporous silica nanoparticles (MSNPs) modified with polyethylenimine (PEI) [ ], Celastrol compound derived from traditional Chinese medicine [ ], positively charged nanoshuttles, such as VCR-PEG-CuPani NSs [ ]. With 72 mentions, “chemoresistance” encompasses resistance to various chemotherapeutic agents, not limited to cisplatin. Various molecular mechanisms and cellular pathways (e.g., protein p22phox [ ], WNT/β-catenin signaling pathway [ ], Interleukin-8 (IL-8) signaling [ ], Akt/Erk-NFκB signaling axis [ ]) contribute to this resistance, necessitating a multifaceted approach to overcome it. Advanced image analysis using deep learning models like EfficientNet-B3 has been used to classify chemoresistant and non-resistant cancer cells based on subtle morphological changes [ ]. Such technology offers a promising tool for identifying chemoresistant cell populations and tailoring treatment strategies [ ]. The keyword “cancer stem cells” was noted 48 times, highlighting their role in tumor resistance. CSCs possess unique properties such as self-renewal, differentiation, and the ability to evade therapeutic interventions, making them a challenging target in cancer treatment. CSCs in OSCC are associated with dysregulated signaling pathways such as Wnt, Notch, and Hedgehog, which contribute to their self-renewal and chemoresistance capabilities [ ]. The loss of function in DNA damage response-related genes, such as the SON gene, has been linked to increased stemness and drug resistance in CSCs [ ].
Figure  shows that the emergence of “extracellular vesicles (EVs)” as a prominent keyword in oral cancer drug resistance research from 2019 to 2024 highlights a significant shift in the focus of scientific inquiry within this field. This trend reflects the growing interest in the role of extracellular vesicles (EVs) in cancer biology, particularly their potential in mediating drug resistance mechanisms. EVs carry various molecules, including ATP-binding cassette (ABC) transporter proteins, nucleic acids, and regulatory factors, which can induce multidrug resistance (MDR) in recipient cells [ , ]. Exosomes can facilitate drug efflux, a process where drugs are expelled from cancer cells, reducing their efficacy [ ]. Such mechanism has been observed in OSCC, where exosomes alter drug delivery and accumulation within cells, contributing to resistance against treatments like cisplatin [ ]. Figure  also shows the emergence of the keyword “NF-kappa B” in 2011 with a burst intensity of 4.83 in the context of drug resistance and oral cancer. NF-κB is activated by various stimuli, including chemotherapeutic agents, leading to the transcription of genes that promote cell survival and resistance to apoptosis [ ]. The activation of NF-κB signaling is a key factor in the development of resistance to drugs like cisplatin in OSCC [ ]. EMT-activating transcription factors can activate NF-κB, further promoting drug resistance in oral cancer [ ]. The NF-κB inhibitor celastrol has been shown to enhance the anti-cancer effects of gambogic acid in OSCC by suppressing NF-κB activity [ ]. The combination of HOXA1 silencing and cisplatin treatment has been shown to reduce NF-κB activity and increase the sensitivity of OSCC cells to cisplatin [ ]. In addition, Fig.  shows the emergence of the keyword “tumor suppressor” with a burst intensity of 5.02 in 2020 within the context of drug resistance and oral cancer. Tumor suppressor genes (TSGs) play a crucial role in regulating cell growth and apoptosis, and their inactivation can lead to resistance against chemotherapeutic agents. The downregulation of CYLD, a tumor suppressor gene, leads to reduced intracellular cisplatin accumulation and suppression of apoptosis via NF-κB hyperactivation [ ]. Mutations in p53, a well-known tumor suppressor, can lead to a failure in apoptosis induction, contributing to chemoresistance in oral cancer [ , ].
The significance and necessity of this bibliometric analysis are multifaceted. First, drug resistance in oral cancer represents a critical challenge in clinical practice, with approximately 33% of cases showing resistance to standard chemotherapy, leading to treatment failure and poor patient outcomes. Understanding the comprehensive research landscape in this field is crucial for developing effective strategies to overcome this resistance. Second, while the volume of research on oral cancer drug resistance has grown substantially, there has been no systematic analysis of the field's development, research priorities, and emerging trends. This gap hampers our ability to identify research opportunities and optimize resource allocation for future studies. Third, the rapid evolution of research in this field, particularly in areas such as molecular mechanisms, targeted therapies, and personalized medicine approaches, necessitates a thorough understanding of the current state of knowledge and research directions. Fourth, the global nature of oral cancer research requires an understanding of international collaboration patterns and regional research strengths to facilitate more effective research partnerships. Fifth, identifying research hotspots and emerging trends through bibliometric analysis can guide funding decisions, research planning, and policy development in this critical field. Finally, this analysis provides valuable insights for both established researchers and newcomers to the field, helping them understand the historical development, current status, and future directions of oral cancer drug resistance research. This comprehensive understanding is essential for advancing our knowledge and developing more effective treatment strategies for oral cancer patients facing drug resistance challenges.
Several limitations should be acknowledged in interpreting the findings of this bibliometric analysis. First, our study only included literature from the Web of Science core collection database, which may have missed relevant publications indexed in other databases such as Scopus, PubMed, or regional databases. This could potentially underrepresent contributions from certain regions or non-English publications. Second, the analysis was limited to articles published between 2000 and 2024, possibly overlooking some earlier foundational works in this field. Third, bibliometric analysis, while powerful for identifying research trends and patterns, cannot directly assess the quality or clinical impact of individual publications. Fourth, the rapidly evolving nature of drug resistance research means that very recent developments or emerging topics may not yet be reflected in the publication data. Fifth, the keyword analysis might be affected by variations in terminology usage across different research groups and time periods, potentially impacting the accuracy of thematic clustering. Sixth, institutional contributions might be underestimated due to variations in institutional name formats and affiliations. Seventh, the collaboration network analysis may not fully capture informal collaborations or research partnerships that have not resulted in co-authored publications. Eighth, citation analysis as a measure of impact has inherent limitations, as citations can be influenced by factors unrelated to research quality. Ninth, the focus on drug resistance in oral cancer specifically might have missed relevant insights from research on drug resistance in other cancer types that could be applicable to oral cancer. Finally, the bibliometric indicators used in this study provide a quantitative perspective but may not fully capture the qualitative aspects of research development in this field.
Based on our bibliometric analysis, several promising future research directions emerge that warrant further exploration in the field of oral cancer drug resistance. The temporal evolution of research themes revealed by our analysis suggests a need for deeper investigation into emerging areas such as extracellular vesicles, tumor microenvironment interactions, and cancer stem cell biology. Particularly, the recent surge in studies focusing on extracellular vesicles (burst strength 3.53, 2019–2024) indicates this as a fertile area for future research, especially in understanding their role in drug resistance transmission and potential therapeutic targeting. The increasing complexity of molecular mechanisms identified in our keyword analysis points toward the necessity of integrating multi-omics approaches and artificial intelligence-driven data analysis in future studies. Furthermore, the strong citation bursts observed for topics like “tumor suppressor” (burst strength 5.02, 2020–2022) and specific molecular pathways suggest opportunities for developing targeted therapeutic strategies. Our collaboration network analysis indicates the potential for expanding international research partnerships, particularly between established research centers in Asia and emerging research groups globally. The evolution of research themes from basic resistance mechanisms to more sophisticated molecular pathways suggests that future studies should focus on translational approaches, combining fundamental research with clinical applications. Additionally, the growing emphasis on personalized medicine approaches, as indicated by recent keyword trends, suggests a need for future research into patient-specific resistance mechanisms and tailored therapeutic strategies. The temporal analysis of publication patterns also highlights the potential for developing standardized protocols for resistance monitoring and implementing novel therapeutic approaches based on emerging molecular targets. These future directions, supported by our bibliometric findings, could significantly advance our understanding of drug resistance mechanisms and lead to more effective treatment strategies in oral cancer.
The findings of this bibliometric analysis provide several important implications for future research in oral cancer drug resistance. First, the increasing publication trend and emerging research hotspots suggest that more attention should be directed toward investigating novel mechanisms of drug resistance, particularly in areas such as tumor microenvironment interactions and extracellular vesicles, which have shown growing research interest. Second, the collaboration network analysis reveals opportunities for strengthening international cooperation, especially between leading research institutions and emerging research centers, to facilitate knowledge exchange and resource sharing. Third, the keyword analysis highlights several understudied areas that warrant further investigation, including the role of cancer stem cells, autophagy regulation, and novel drug delivery systems in overcoming drug resistance. Fourth, there is a clear need for more translational research that bridges the gap between laboratory findings and clinical applications, particularly in the development of biomarkers for early detection of drug resistance. Fifth, the emergence of artificial intelligence and high-throughput screening techniques as research tools suggests the potential for more systematic approaches to drug resistance studies. Sixth, the analysis indicates a need for increased focus on personalized medicine approaches, considering the heterogeneous nature of drug resistance mechanisms. Additionally, future research should emphasize the development of combination therapies and novel therapeutic strategies based on the identified molecular pathways and mechanisms. Finally, greater attention should be paid to clinical validation studies and real-world evidence to ensure the practical applicability of research findings in clinical settings. These directions could significantly advance our understanding of drug resistance in oral cancer and lead to more effective therapeutic strategies.

## Conclusion
This bibliometric analysis provides a comprehensive overview of the evolution and current state of oral cancer drug resistance research from 2000 to 2024. The field has shown remarkable growth, particularly after 2014, with significant contributions from Asian institutions led by China, demonstrating a geographical shift in research leadership. The analysis revealed several key research themes, transitioning from basic drug resistance mechanisms to more sophisticated areas such as cancer stem cells, tumor microenvironment interactions, and extracellular vesicles. The emergence of new research hotspots, particularly in molecular pathways and targeted therapeutics, suggests promising directions for future investigations in overcoming drug resistance in oral cancer. These findings not only provide valuable insights into the current research landscape but also offer strategic guidance for future studies and international collaborations in this critical field.
