## Abstract

## Background
Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly transforming the methods of educational delivery and knowledge acquisition within the nursing discipline. This study aims to explore the experiences of undergraduate nursing students regarding the integration of AI into nursing education and to improve learning outcomes and AI effectiveness.

## Methods
A qualitative descriptive study was utilized using semi-structured interviews. Twenty-one undergraduate nursing students were selected using the purposive sampling method. The interview data were collected and analyzed using the conventional content analysis method.

## Results
Five main themes and thirteen sub-themes emerged from the data analysis: functional experiences with AI, content-related experiences, performance-related experiences, attitudes toward AI and its behavioral impact, and considerations of academic ethics.

## Conclusion
AI-assisted instruction is an emerging trend in nursing education. To optimize its effectiveness, efforts should focus on improving learning outcomes, acknowledging AI as a supplementary tool, managing content-related risks, enhancing the technical performance of AI platforms, addressing issues of overreliance while integrating traditional and modern approaches, and strengthening academic integrity measures to mitigate ethical risks.

Keywords: Artificial intelligence, Nursing education, Undergraduate nursing students, Qualitative research

## Background
The growing interest in artificial intelligence (AI) has reached an unprecedented level in recent years [ ], especially following the emergence of generative technologies such as ChatGPT in late 2022. As a result, AI is increasingly reshaping the landscape of education, including the nursing field. This rapid advancement is influencing both the delivery of education and how students acquire knowledge. AI-generated teaching methodologies have improved communication skills, clinical reasoning, and overall academic performance among nursing students [ , ].
In response, many nursing students are proactively engaging in the exploration and utilization of AI technologies in a self-directed manner to support their learning. A large-scale cross-sectional study conducted by the Omari team involving 1,713 nursing students across 10 Arab countries, as well as Labrague’s research on 200 undergraduate nursing students in the Philippines, demonstrated a generally neutral attitude toward AI technologies among nursing students [ , ]. It is worth noting that, despite this neutrality, the usage of AI in educational practical application domains continues to expand.
AI is a system’s ability to interpret external data, learn from it, and adapt to achieve specific goals through flexible adaptation [ ]. AI in education has significantly improved administration, instruction, and learning by enhancing efficiency, fostering student retention, and enhancing curriculum personalization [ ]. This study focuses specifically on AI-driven technologies and platforms that are currently used in nursing education and directly engaged with by students. Although these tools vary in form and complexity, they share core AI capabilities, including learning, reasoning, and intelligent interaction as defined by the foundational concepts of artificial intelligence. Furthermore, these tools are purpose-built to support well-defined learning objectives and educational tasks in the nursing curriculum. Undergraduate nursing students engage with a variety of AI tools in their academic routines, including generative AI, bionic robots, virtual simulation platforms, and web-based virtual classrooms. Among these AI tools, the diverse applications and integration of Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Virtual Reality (VR) technologies are particularly prominent.
Existing studies highlighted the transformative potential of AI in nursing education. Innovations such as AI-based clinical decision support systems and computer-assisted monitoring provide students with real-time case analysis and accurate feedback, enhancing their clinical reasoning skills [ ]. VR simulations create immersive environments that foster collaboration and communication [ ], while synchronous e-learning platforms offer interactive experiences that replicate traditional classroom settings and facilitate role-playing exercises [ ]. These advancements help nursing students achieve key educational goals, including the acquisition of theoretical knowledge, development of clinical competencies, and enhancement of professional skills objectives, traditionally attained through conventional teaching methods. Thus, AI effectively augments established educational frameworks.
The integration of AI into nursing education holds substantial innovative potential; however, it also gives rise to ethical concerns and educational challenges that demand careful attention. When nursing students rely on Generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) to generate homework or exam answers, the likelihood of plagiarism or cheating increases. Moreover, overdependence on AI tools may hinder the development of critical clinical reasoning skills that are typically nurtured through conventional education. The application of AI also introduces new ethical and technical concerns, such as algorithmic bias, data privacy, and lack of explainability, which are not fully addressed by traditional ethical frameworks [ , ].
Most existing studies on the integration of AI in nursing education focus primarily on descriptively evaluating the effects of AI applications and related ethical considerations. However, these studies often overlook the subjective experiences of nursing students when interacting with AI, as well as their overall assessments of various AI forms. Consequently, this study aims to investigate the experiences of nursing students in utilizing diverse forms of AI integrated into learning and education through qualitative research methods. The results will offer a theoretical basis and practical guidance for enhancing the application of AI in nursing education.

## Subjects and methods

## Research design
This study employed a qualitative descriptive design using semi-structured interviews and the conventional content analysis method as outlined by Hsieh’s [ ] to explore the experiences of undergraduate nursing students. The Consolidated Criteria for Reporting Qualitative Research (COREQ) were followed [ ], ensuring transparency and methodological rigor in both the data collection and analysis processes.

## Participants
This qualitative study was conducted at a university in Zhejiang Province between October and December 2024. A purposive sampling method was used to select 21 undergraduate nursing students (female:17; male:4) from the university’s nursing school. All participants were de-identified in accordance with confidentiality principles, and pseudonyms (P1-P21) were used in place of real names. Inclusion criteria for participants were as follows: (1) Voluntarily participate in the interview and signing of informed consent; (2) Adequate language comprehension skills; (3) Full-time enrollment in the nursing undergraduate program; (4) Experience with using artificial intelligence. Exclusion criteria: Individuals who withdraw from this research for various reasons. The number of participants was determined after information saturation was reached during the interview collection, when no new topics arose and duplicate responses emerged. It was inferred that the data from the nineteenth interview was saturated, with two further interviews confirming this outcome [ ]. None of the participants withdrew from the study. The scores of all participants were exported from the teacher’s account backend. Demographic information about the participants is shown in Table  .
 

## Interview outline
An interview outline was developed by considering previous literature reviews [ – ].Under the guidance of the corresponding author, possesses extensive expertise in nursing education and qualitative research, three internal group discussions were conducted to refine the interview questions. The following is the interview outline for this study: (1) What types of AI have you encountered in your nursing studies? (2) How would you describe your experience with the utilization of these AI tools in nursing education? (3) Which type of AI do you find most/least appealing, and what factors contribute to your preference? (4) What is the greatest benefit of using AI for nursing studies? (5) Does using AI hinder learning? Please explain your reasons. (6) What are the most prominent features of AI compared to traditional teaching? (7) Please share your opinions and suggestions regarding the application of AI nursing studies.

## Data collection methods
The face-to-face semi-structured interview method was utilized. All interviews were conducted by a researcher (PDL), who is pursuing a master’s degree in nursing and had received formal training in qualitative research methods before this study started. Before the interviews, researchers established a trusting relationship with the participants to confirm their willingness to take part in the study. After obtaining informed consent, the researchers provided detailed explanations regarding the study’s objectives, content, confidentiality agreements, and ethical guidelines [ ]. The participants then signed the informed consent form for this research. The location and time of the interviews were arranged according to the interviewees’ preferences, typically in quiet, unoccupied classrooms. During the interviews, researchers paid close attention to non-verbal behaviors exhibited by the participants. The researcher listened attentively, responded promptly, and provided emotional support when appropriate. Each interview lasted approximately 20–30 min.

## Data analysis methods
Within 24 h of each interview, the audio recordings were transcribed into verbatim transcripts. Non-verbal cues, environmental details, and other relevant contextual information were carefully documented. Data collection and analysis were conducted concurrently. In order to increase methodological rigor, two researchers (PDL and END), both trained in qualitative research, independently performed coding and categorization. Transcripts were formatted in a Microsoft Word document, with original text in the left column and codes in the right. The researchers regularly discussed and revised emerging categories and themes. Any disagreements were resolved through discussion with the Corresponding author (XHZ) until consensus was reached. The final report was prepared by the first author (PDL) and approved by all co-authors. All raw data and analysed materials are securely retained by the Corresponding author.
This descriptive qualitative study employed the conventional content analysis method recommended by Hsieh [ ]. The key steps of the analysis proceeded as follows: (1) Immersion: Transcripts were read repeatedly to gain a comprehensive understanding of the data; (2) Inductive Coding: Text was analysed word by word without predefined categories to derive initial codes; (3) Category Formation: Similar codes were grouped into broader categories; (4) Theme Development: Categories were further analysed iteratively to identify overarching themes; (5) Contextual Interpretation with Exemplars: Representative quotes were extracted to illustrate key findings. A summary of the data analysis process is presented in Table  .
 

## Quality control methods
The quality control measures implemented in this study include the following: (1) All interviewers received systematic training in qualitative research before the study and were supervised by an associate professor with expertise in qualitative research methodologies; (2) Three individuals participated in preliminary (pilot) interviews. These sessions were conducted to adjust the interview outline and enhance the interviewers’ skills. The data from these pilot interviews were not included in the final analysis. Participants were informed of this, and their interview materials were destroyed in their presence, with formal apologies issued.

## Ethical approval
This study received ethical approval from the affiliated university, under approval number 20240913-1. All procedures were conducted in accordance with the ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki.

## Results
A total of five main themes and thirteen sub-themes emerged from the data analysis. These findings are visually presented in Fig.  .
 

## Theme 1: the functional experience of AI

## Overcoming temporal and spatial constraints
Most participants reported that the integration of AI technologies into nursing education has effectively removed the limitations of time and physical location. With AI tools, learning and practice are no longer confined to specific schedules or settings, allowing for greater flexibility and accessibility.

P5: “Sometimes I don’t fully understand the content presented in class, so I revisit the virtual online courses provided by the teacher. I find these courses reliable, and they help me grasp the key concepts more thoroughly.”
P12: “The virtual simulation platform is very helpful. Whenever I need to practice nursing procedures, I can access them anytime and anywhere, as long as I have an internet connection.”
P21: “The biggest change AI has brought to me is that when I learn stuff, I don’t have to be limited by time and space anymore.”

## Innovative AI enhances learning engagement
Participants generally expressed strong approval of AI technological advancements, highlighting their positive impact on learning experiences. The use of AI tools was seen to significantly increase learners’ interest and motivation to explore new knowledge, fostering more active and self-directed engagement.

P6: “What I found particularly impressive was that the robot could take my pulse, which helped me develop a deeper understanding of my own health. If I have some health issues in the future, I will know how to better adjust myself. The robot can also perform facial analysis, which sparked my interest in the field of face reading. I intend to further explore this area of knowledge”.
P14:“Taking AI as a trendy learning tool, the most significant thing I feel is that it can spark my ideas and give me suggestions. Based on the general suggestions it provides, I can go and look up information. As a result, I find more, better, and more in - depth stuff.”

## AI enhances the efficiency of learning
Compared with other majors, the undergraduate nursing curriculum is notably comprehensive and demanding, encompassing foundational medical sciences, specialized nursing courses, laboratory-based instruction, and an extensive ten-month clinical internship. Participants noted that AI tools serve as valuable educational supports, helping to improve learning efficiency and reduce the academic burden associated with this demanding curriculum.

P2: “If you use traditional search, the results will be listed one by one, and you have to click on each one to view. But with an AI assistant, it will summarize all the information for you, so you don’t have to go through them one by one.”
P12: “AI can help me quickly understand the research trends in this field. I will first ask AI to search for some information, and then I will do a search in the database myself. This way, I can better grasp the cutting-edge developments in this field.”
P17: “In a full virtual class, not all content is essential. AI helps me extract the key information I find important, which enhances my learning efficiency”.

## AI meets individualized research needs
Many participants reported AI has demonstrated significant potential in supporting nursing students’ academic development by providing structured guidance for academic writing, and fostering broader cognitive engagement.

P5:“For instance, when writing a paper, AI can help me sort them out and offer some suggestions. Then, I would combine AI’s suggestions with my own ideas to come up with a new version, which I feel would be of better quality.”
P14: “If I have no idea how to write this article, I will ask GenAI to generate something for me. Once I know the structure and outline provided by the AI, my ideas become very clear. Then I will go to CNKI and PubMed to look for some materials.”
P17: “There might be some ideas that I haven’t thought of or simply couldn’t think of, but after chatting with AI, my mind might gradually open up.”

## Theme 2: the content experience of AI

## AI is a supplement to traditional classroom teaching
Participants pointed out that AI serves as a valuable supplement to traditional classroom instruction by leveraging its unique advantages. For instance, AI robots enable students to engage in interactive training, thereby facilitating early adaptation in real-simulated healthcare environments. The virtual simulation platform enhances operational proficiency while reinforcing knowledge consolidation. GenAI can rapidly integrate teaching resources beyond the classroom setting.

P1:“The first time I interacted with a robot, I found it fascinating. It provided a realistic scenario, and I felt that if I were to encounter such a situation in real life, I would feel more confident performing nursing procedures.”
P13: “The virtual simulation platform serves as a kind of preview for the experimental class. Before performing the actual procedures, I already know what to expect. I can also simulate the process multiple times after class. While repeated simulation might be seen as a way to boost scores, it also serves as an effective review method.”
P15: “Teachers in traditional classrooms have specific teaching priorities. Due to time constraints, they don’t often elaborate on extracurricular topics. For areas that interest me, I turn to AI for further exploration.”

## Accuracy and reliability of AI-generated content
Despite the benefits, participants in this study identified several limitations in the use of AI for academic writing. Concerns included: (1) the generation of fabricated references that could not be verified through reputable academic databases; (2) inconsistent logical coherence in AI-generated outputs, which received mixed evaluations; (3) content that was often overly generalized and lacking in depth.

P3: “Yesterday, I used AI to write a paper. I instructed it to include only real sources and to follow the academic structure of a paper, including references. However, the references it provided were fabricated, and I couldn’t find them on CNKI.”
P4: “I feel what AI generates is just way too logical. It’s obvious it’s fake. There’s no way I could write something this good. I really don’t dare to use it for reference.”
P12: “What it says just doesn’t make sense logically. It doesn’t connect well from the start to the end. And sometimes, the subject, verb, and object in the sentences just don’t match up.”
P11: “I found that AI seems to compile commonly available content from various search platforms indiscriminately. It’s as if it collects both reliable and unreliable information without filtering, then presents it all together.”

## Theme 3: the performance experience of AI

## Multi-terminal adaptability of AI technology
Participants reflected on their experiences using AI across multiple devices. AI technologies demonstrated strong adaptability and seamless integration with mainstream electronic platforms, including smartphones, tablets, and personal computers. This adaptability was seen as a strength, contributing to the ease of access and use.

P8: “I have also tried virtual simulation experiments on my mobile phone, but the screen is too small to click easily.”
P16: “Nowadays, AI is very powerful. Any smartphone can be used.”
P19: “No matter what version of ChatGPT is released, it can basically be used. I don’t understand the algorithm issues, but they are all compatible.”

## Limitations of virtual simulation platforms and the need for back-end optimization
Participants emphasized that the objective limitations of AI technologies themselves significantly influence nursing students’ willingness to engage with these systems. A common issue was the absence of clear operational instructions, which forced students to adopt trial-and-error approaches. Moreover, accessibility was limited by technical constraints; for instance, the simulation platforms were only accessible through the campus networks, and even then, performance was often unstable and inconsistent.

P6: “I’m not quite sure how to proceed or where to click. There aren’t clear instructions, so I struggled to operate it.”
P9: “The guidance is not very clear. You just click around randomly to figure things out. There’s no real instruction.”
P3: “Whether I can log into the virtual simulation experiment platform depends on luck. It’s not easy to log in. Even when I used the computer room provided by the school, the system became very laggy after logging in.”

## Theme 4: attitudes towards AI and its influence on behavior

## Students’ expectations for the integration of traditional classrooms and AI
As AI applications continue to expand, participants recognized the increasing integration of AI-assisted tools with traditional teaching methods. Rather than advocating for the replacement of traditional classrooms, participants expressed a strong preference for a complementary approach that thoughtfully combines AI with conventional instruction.

P17: “From a teacher’s perspective, I believe traditional teaching is more effective. However, from a student’s perspective, the assistance provided by artificial intelligence can be beneficial. Ultimately, it depends on how learning resources are utilized. The key lies in achieving an organic integration of the two approaches.”
P18: “I hope the school can introduce specialized courses on the use of AI and incorporate them into the nursing curriculum.”
P20: “It is still necessary to combine traditional classroom instruction with artificial intelligence. I can’t say I prefer one over the other, as each has its own strengths and limitations. The most important goal is to leverage the advantages of both.”

## Dependence and inertial induced by generative AI
The most of participants in this study reported a growing reliance on GenAI, which they perceived as diminishing their motivation to engage in independent critical and reflective thinking.

P1: “I’m actually kind of dependent on it. Like, when I get an assignment that I figure AI can write for me, the first thought that pops into my head is, ‘Let me go chat with it first’ (smile).”
P8: “I have to submit an innovation and entrepreneurship plan and a PPT presentation next Monday, but I haven’t started writing yet because I think AI can assist me. I might put it off until tomorrow or even the day after tomorrow morning.”
P10:“Many times, as soon as I think of a question, I will ask it(GenAI). Even when the teacher asks questions in class sometimes, I will think of asking it.”

## Theme 5: ethical considerations for the use of AI

## Concerns regarding the generation of content that may duplicate existing work
A key ethical concern raised by participants is that content produced through GenAI does not stem from their own independent inquiry or original thought. Consequently, nursing students expressed apprehension about the potential for plagiarism detection when submitting AI-generated assignments.

P5: “Although I feel my current knowledge base is limited, I can’t allow others to see that there’s a serious issue. If the entire assignment is generated by AI, that’s problematic. What if it’s checked for plagiarism?”
P13: “I used to be quite careless, but last time the teacher mentioned in class that six assignments were exactly the same. If you copy from AI, can’t you at least change some of the keywords? Since then, whenever I use AI, I worry it might produce content too similar to others’.”

## Teachers emphasize the regulated use of AI
Many participants stated that teachers generally establish certain regulations regarding students’ utilization of artificial intelligence technology. However, they also noted variations in how different teachers approach and enforce these regulations, leading to inconsistencies in practice and expectations.

P13: “When I was helping my supervisor organize materials, the teacher repeatedly emphasized that AI-generated content should not be used because its traces are easily detectable. Nowadays, there is a strong emphasis on academic integrity.”
P19: “The teacher mentioned in class that students are not allowed to rely entirely on AI-generated content. Some instructors even stated explicitly that they would use IA tools to detect plagiarism.”
P20: “Our teacher advised us to manage the large language model on our phones responsibly, not to merely copy and paste from it. Instead, we were encouraged to train it properly and transform it into an academic tool.”

## Implementation of strategies to mitigate risks to academic integrity
In response to these ethical concerns, participants described a range of strategies they employed to safeguard academic integrity when using GenAI. These included: (1) generating multiple outputs from the same AI tool to compare and refine content; (2) using different AI platforms to produce diverse versions and then synthesizing them into a cohesive final product; (3) “training” or customizing AI tools to better reflect their personal writing style and their own voice.

P11: “I’ll ask Kimi or Dou Bao the same question a few times and have them give me answers over and over again.”
P6: “For a learning report, I might first use Doubao to generate a draft. After that, I use Kimi to polish the text. Then I search for relevant materials online and make further revisions.”
P9: “I will domesticate the AI. It can be fine-tuned over time, through continued adjustments, and it gradually learns to produce responses that align with the kind of content I need. Eventually, it can adopt a style more like mine, similar to how I usually speak.”

## Discussion

## Enhancing learning effectiveness through AI assistance
AI is transforming the paradigm of nursing education by introducing new opportunities for innovation while also posing new challenges [ ].
This research supports the view that AI’s capacity to extend learning across time and space. Virtual simulation platforms allow nursing students to preview or review nursing procedures at any time with a simple click. These platforms, in combination with mannequin-based simulation training, provide students with an immersive and realistic clinical environment while still on campus.
In recent years, AI-enhanced simulation devices incorporating advanced algorithms have become a focal point of research in nursing education [ ]. These tools aim to create more authentic and engaging clinical scenarios, thereby stimulating students’ enthusiasm for learning. Notably, participants in this study primarily discussed the use of GenAI, AI robots, and virtual simulation experiment platforms, highlighting their perceived advantages. However, other AI tools were mentioned less frequently. This suggests that the use of multiple AI technologies should be implemented thoughtfully. Excessive or indiscriminate use may inadvertently increase students’ cognitive and academic burden, rather than enhancing their learning experience.
AI also contributes to learning efficiency. Throughout the interviews, participants consistently emphasized the utility of GenAI. Recent studies have demonstrated that GenAI can be integrated into systematic review methodologies, utilizing automated algorithms to enhance the efficiency of literature screening [ ]. Additionally, nursing students can employ GenAI as a semantically enriched search engine, significantly reducing the cognitive and temporal cost associated with knowledge integration [ ].
As noted in the SWOT analysis by Abujaber, one of the strengths of GenAI lies in its ability to deliver personalized responses to students’ unique learning needs [ ]. Traditional scientific research is often limited by the researcher’s disciplinary background and personal biases. However, through qualitative exploratory approaches [ ], a new perspective has emerged: the cognitive structure of AI can be strategically leveraged to transcend individual cognitive limitations.
In summary, the advantages brought about by AI are evident. As nursing education evolves, it is no longer restricted to discipline-specific content but is increasingly characterized by interdisciplinary collaboration [ ].To prepare future nurses for technology-integrated clinical environments, curricular reforms are essential. These reforms should aim to develop students’ competencies in health informatics, digital literacy, and data interpretation [ ].

## The supplementary role of AI in education and related content risks
This study highlights the dual nature of AI in nursing education, its benefits, and associated risks. Participants clarified the advantages of artificial intelligence in providing immersive practical opportunities, supporting preview and repetitive learning, expanding learning resources and depth.
A descriptive qualitative study indicated that students improved their operational skills, such as breast examination, through authentic community engagement, but they encountered limitations in time, venue, and resources [ ]. However, the virtual patient system enables students to repeatedly practice the examination techniques for high - risk cases, without being constrained by real - world conditions. Based on the insights from Luo’s research, in the future, it is possible to explore AI-driven adaptive learning platforms to enhance training efficiency and achieve personalized teaching goals [ ].
While embracing the advantages of AI, it is critical to address the reliability of AI-generated content. A case study by Gosak et al. found that nursing diagnoses generated by ChatGPT lacked alignment with the NANDA-I International classification system and demonstrated insufficient specificity [ ]. Similar findings were also reported in a study conducted in Taiwan [ ]. This suggests that we need to assume a critical stance towards the content generated by AI. As noted by Joko Gunawan [ ], the information produced by tools such as ChatGPT is constrained by its knowledge cut-off date and may not always be current or entirely accurate. This can well explain the three main concerns that the participants in this study had about AI-assisted writing.
These issues reveal the “two-sided nature” of AI in education contexts. Nevertheless, the effectiveness of AI-assisted learning hinges on specific strategies of use [ ]. As Simms [ ] has argued, the use of AI should not be categorically dismissed; rather, it must be subject to critical evaluation and professional judgement. Blind adoption or simplistic copy-and-paste practices undermine educational goals.
In future nursing education, instructors are encouraged to design comparative learning activities that juxtapose human problem-solving with AI-generated outputs. Active participation in such exercises can help nursing students gain a deeper understanding of the limitations of AI and sharpen their awareness of its inherent biases. Through these practices, students’ critical thinking abilities can be meaningfully developed and reinforced.

## Back-end support for AI requires ongoing optimization
The multi-terminal adaptability of AI provides not only strong technical support for the promotion of AI technologies but also offers nursing students a novel learning experience distinct from traditional classroom settings. Previous studies have primarily examined students’ acceptance of AI from a subjective standpoint, focusing on factors such as self-assessed technological proficiency, understanding of AI technologies, availability of educational support, and so on [ ]. This study identifies objective factors influencing nursing students’ experiences with virtual simulation platforms, notably the lack of operational guidance and persistent network connectivity issues. These are primarily technical challenges and, in most cases, are relatively straightforward to address. Findings from a descriptive qualitative study involving educators suggest that student satisfaction is a critical driver for the adoption and successful implementation of educational technologies [ ]. Enhancing students’ satisfaction and acceptance of AI is of great significance in academic contexts.
In a qualitative study on VR simulation for pneumonia care, participants reported explanations of theoretical foundations but lacked feedback channels [ ]. Combined with this study’s findings, it further underscores that establishing a real-time instructor response mechanism is crucial. The more critical concern lies in the lack of timely communication between educators and students. Establishing effective feedback mechanisms is essential to ensure that instructors can receive and respond to students’ issues in real time. Addressing both technical shortcomings and communication gaps will facilitate a more supportive and responsive learning environment.

## Overcome dependence and integrate tradition with modernity
AI-assisted education represents an emerging and inevitable trend in the future of nursing practice. To keep pace with these advancements, individuals in the nursing field should commit to lifelong learning, acquire foundational knowledge of AI, and actively pursue opportunities for skill enhancement through relevant training programs and resources [ ]. However, this study found that some nursing students tend to adopt a utilitarian approach to AI, often overlooking the gradual, cumulative, and effort-intensive nature of true learning. Instead, many have become increasingly dependent on the convenience of AI offers. Lim warned of a similar trend in his research, describing GenAI is ‘Capable’ yet ‘Dependent’ [ ], cautioning against the risk of cognitive inertia.
Excessive reliance on AI may inadvertently erode essential human competencies such as interpersonal communication and empathy, which are of vital importance in the core competencies of nursing [ ]. These are precisely the key human characteristics that are difficult for AI to replicate and substitute. Rony and colleagues in their qualitative research stressed the importance of maintaining a human-technology balance, advocating that AI should serve as an auxiliary tool rather than replace human judgment, thereby reinforcing the indispensable value of humanistic care in nursing practice [ ].
Although AI excels in areas such as resource generation and data analysis, traditional classroom environments are superior in cultivating interpersonal and emotional competencies. Therefore, a blended educational model that integrates the technological strengths of AI with the relational benefits of traditional instruction is recommended. Such a model can support nursing students in developing a well-rounded skill set, encompassing not only theoretical knowledge and technical proficiency but also emotional literacy and empathy.

## Proactively engaging in the institutional construction of academic integrity
Migdadi et al. [ ] found a positive correlation between students’ ethical awareness regarding AI use and their intention to apply such technologies. As students’ ethical awareness increases, they develop a deeper understanding of the boundaries and guiding principles necessary for the responsible integration of AI in nursing practice. Similarly, Alqahtani [ ] emphasized that AI should serve as a supportive tool rather than a replacement for human input in academic research, particularly by assisting with text generation, data analysis, literature reviews, and peer review. These findings provide valuable insights into the scope and appropriate applications of AI within both academic and clinical environments.
China currently lacks specific and comprehensive guidance related to the application of AI in nursing education [ ]. Although numerous policy documents and regulatory frameworks have been issued to promote the standardized use of AI, China still faces challenges in formulating detailed criteria for determining liability in cases of ethical or legal infringement [ ].
Participants in this study demonstrated a paradoxical stance: while expressing concern over the academic risks associated with plagiarism detection in AI-generated work, they remained unwilling to forgo the efficiency benefits of these tools. As a result, they adopted various strategies to mitigate academic risks. Nevertheless, the development of an effective legal framework remains an ongoing and evolutionary process. In the current phase, it is crucial to strengthen the guiding and supervisory roles of educators. In response to the growing integration of AI in education, Simms [ ] underscores the urgent need for educators to revise their pedagogical approaches and curricula to cultivate systematic GenAI literacy among nursing students.
In the context of ethical governance, further elaboration is needed to establish clear and enforceable boundaries for academic integrity. The cultivation of students’ ethical awareness should not be viewed as a one-time intervention but as a continuous, dynamic process. Through sustained educational efforts, guided self-reflection, and hands-on practice, students can progressively internalize ethical principles, navigate complex real-world scenarios, and develop sound value judgements. This ongoing process is essential to strengthening their ethical foundations and ensuring the responsible use of AI in both academic and professional settings.

## Study strengths and limitations
This study further enhances existing literature by exploring the subjective experiences of nursing students when interacting with various forms of AI. Unlike previous research that often focuses on a single AI modality, this study investigates a broader spectrum of AI applications encountered by undergraduate nursing students in practical settings. By doing so, it provides a more comprehensive understanding of students’ perceptions and evaluations of AI integration in nursing education and practice. However, the study is subject to certain limitations. The sample was restricted to undergraduate nursing students from a single institution, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. Future research should consider employing a larger, multi-center sampling strategy across different universities or regions to enhance the representativeness and applicability of the results.

## Conclusion
This study revealed that undergraduate nursing students exhibit a diverse range of experiences when engaging with various forms of AI. AI-assisted teaching is emerging as a significant trend in the future of education. It is essential to avoid the outright rejection of AI technologies, as they offer numerous benefits, including enhanced convenience and the potential to foster innovation within nursing education. However, it is equally important to acknowledge and address the negative impacts that AI may have on the learning experience. This includes fostering critical thinking skills among nursing students, optimizing the technological performance of AI systems, and promoting the deep integration of intelligent tools with traditional classroom practices. Moreover, it is necessary to establish robust frameworks for academic integrity and to clearly define the ethical boundaries of AI applications to mitigate potential risks.
