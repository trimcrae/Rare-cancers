Immunotherapy has revolutionized cancer treatment by harnessing the immune system for durable anti-tumor responses [ ]. This therapeutic paradigm operates by overcoming tumor-induced immune evasion mechanisms and restoring the natural capacity of the immune system to recognize and eliminate malignant cells. Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) represent the most clinically successful immunotherapeutic approach, functioning by blocking inhibitory signals that tumors exploit to suppress immune responses. The primary targets include programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) and its ligand PD-L1, which normally serve as immune system “brakes” to prevent excessive immune activation, and cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 (CTLA-4), which regulates T-cell activation in lymphoid tissues [ – ].
ICIs have demonstrated remarkable clinical efficacy across multiple malignancies, including melanoma, non-small cell lung cancer, and colorectal cancer (CRC) [ – ]. For example, MSI-H/dMMR status serves as a distinctive predictive biomarker for ICIs across CRCs and non-CRCs, with objective response rates (ORRs) typically in the range of 40% to 60% [ ]. Additionally, previous ICI trials have included mucosal and vulvar melanoma, with reported ORRs ranging from 19% to 37% [ ]. However, their application in BC faces significant challenges, with clinical outcomes varying substantially based on tumor characteristics and patient selection criteria. Across most BC subtypes, response rates remain disappointingly low, reflecting the heterogeneous and often immunosuppressive nature of the breast tumor microenvironment (TME). In triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), where immunotherapy demonstrates the greatest potential due to higher tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes and PD-L1 expression levels, ORRs rarely exceed 20–30% in unselected populations [ – ], though recent landmark trials show encouraging but variable results depending on biomarker status and treatment setting. For instance, the pembrolizumab-chemotherapy group showed significantly better 36-month event-free survival (84.5% vs. 76.8%; HR 0.63, P < 0.001) than the placebo-chemotherapy group, with a median follow-up of 39.1 months [ , ]. In the metastatic setting, single-agent ICIs achieve ORRs of only 10–18% in unselected populations, with enhanced efficacy observed primarily in PD-L1-positive subgroups where response rates can reach 21–23% [ , ]. Importantly, only approximately 25% of TNBC cases present with immunologically “hot” TMEs characterized by robust immune infiltration, which may explain the heterogeneous clinical responses observed across unselected patient populations [ ].
The limited efficacy stems from multiple interconnected factors. The breast TME exhibits profound immunosuppression, characterized by abundant regulatory T cells, myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs), and tumor-associated macrophages that actively inhibit anti-tumor responses [ , ]. Many BCs display low mutational burdens and limited neoantigen expression, resulting in poor immunogenicity and inadequate T-cell infiltration [ , ]. Additionally, systemic administration of immunotherapeutic agents causes widespread immune activation, leading to severe immune-related adverse events that often necessitate treatment discontinuation [ ]. The large molecular size of therapeutic antibodies results in poor tumor penetration and limited bioavailability, while rapid systemic clearance reduces therapeutic exposure duration [ ].
These fundamental challenges have catalyzed unprecedented interest in developing sophisticated delivery systems (DSs) to enhance immunotherapy efficacy in BC [ , ]. Advanced platforms including nanoparticles, liposomes, extracellular vesicles, and biomaterial scaffolds offer transformative potential through targeted drug delivery, controlled therapeutic release, protection of labile biologics, and facilitation of combination therapies [ – ]. Importantly, these systems can modulate the TME directly, converting immunologically “cold” tumors into “hot” tumors through localized immune activation while minimizing systemic toxicity [ , ]. The rapid evolution of DSs reflects convergence across materials science, nanotechnology, immunology, and oncology, generating exponentially expanding literature characterized by diverse methodological approaches and varying clinical application levels [ , ]. This complexity necessitates systematic analysis to identify trends, evaluate research impact, and guide future priorities. However, comprehensive bibliometric analyses specifically focusing on DSs in BC immunotherapy remain absent, creating significant knowledge gaps that impede strategic research planning.
Bibliometric analysis provides powerful tools for systematically mapping scientific landscapes, identifying trends, and predicting future directions through co-citation analysis, keyword co-occurrence mapping, and temporal trend analysis [ , ]. Such approaches are particularly valuable in rapidly advancing interdisciplinary fields where traditional reviews struggle to capture full complexity.
Therefore, we conducted a systematic bibliometric investigation of the global research landscape spanning two decades (2005–2025). This study quantitatively analyzes publication trends, identifies productive countries and institutions, maps collaboration networks, examines thematic evolution, and projects future directions. Through rigorous application of multiple analytical frameworks, we provide the first comprehensive map of this rapidly expanding field, offering essential guidance for optimizing research investments and clinical implementation strategies.

## Materials and methods

## Database selection and search strategy
A comprehensive bibliometric study was conducted using the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database to systematically identify and analyze research publications from January 1, 2005, to July 3, 2025. The research implemented a sophisticated search strategy incorporating specialized terminology related to DSs in BC immunotherapy (Supplementary Table 1).
To ensure a thorough and accurate bibliometric analysis, this study primarily utilized the WoSCC platform. The database provides unique advantages, including extensive high-impact journal coverage, standardized citation metrics, and seamless metadata export compatible with advanced analytical tools like VOSviewer, CiteSpace, and bibliometrix. Although alternative databases such as Scopus, Google Scholar, and PubMed contain substantial academic literature, WoSCC stands out for its superior data consistency and structural integrity, thereby enhancing the analytical precision and research depth [ , ].

## Selection parameters

Inclusion criteria: (i) Academic publications comprising original research articles and comprehensive review papers published between 2005 and 2025; (ii) Research specifically investigating DSs in BC immunotherapy.
Exclusion criteria: (i) Non-substantive document types: editorial materials and retracted publications; (ii) Studies exhibiting negligible or no direct relevance to DSs in BC immunotherapy.

## Literature evaluation and refinement process
The initial bibliographic search yielded 677 potential research records. Following a rigorous screening approach involving title and abstract review, and assessing relevance to DSs in BC immunotherapy, two independent researchers (Jun-Yi Yang and Xin-Yu Li) conducted the evaluation process. Any disagreements between the two reviewers were resolved through discussion and consensus with the corresponding author (Xiao-Yi Wang). Following this systematic evaluation, two publications were methodically excluded. The final curated dataset consisted of 675 scholarly publications, comprising 355 primary research studies and 320 comprehensive review articles (Supplementary Table 1).

## Bibliometric analytical frameworks
Three advanced bibliometric analytical platforms were systematically employed for comprehensive multidimensional research exploration:
VOSviewer (version 1.6.19) was meticulously utilized to generate and visually interpret complex bibliographic network structures [ , ], encompassing: (i) intricate collaborative mapping of research interactions across geographical, institutional, and individual scholarly domains; (ii) in-depth co-citation examinations of academic publications and referenced scholarly literature; (iii) elaborate keyword co-occurrence network visualization techniques; (iv) network representation methodologies integrating node scaling (reflecting publication magnitude) and color-based differentiation (highlighting cluster patterns or temporal evolutionary trends), with interconnected pathways illustrating collaborative and citational relationships.
CiteSpace (version 6.2.R3) [ , ] facilitated: (i) sophisticated dual-map overlay analyses revealing intricate citation pathways between academic disciplines; (ii) identification of citation bursts to detect emergent research trajectories.
Biblioshiny (www.bibliometric.com) [ – ], the web-based interface for the R package bibliometrix (version 4.3.1), was applied to: (i) construct comprehensive global scholarly production distribution visualizations; (ii) conduct sophisticated collaborative network evaluations across diverse geographical landscapes; (iii) map thematic evolution and depict research topic transformation trends; (iv) analyze and interpret collaborative interaction dynamics across international research territories.

## Co-citation analysis methodology
Co-citation analysis represents a fundamental bibliometric technique for identifying intellectual relationships and knowledge structures within scientific fields [ , ]. This method operates on the principle that two documents are co-cited when they appear together in the reference list of a third citing document. The frequency of co-citation between any two documents indicates their thematic relatedness and intellectual proximity within the research domain [ , ].
For co-cited references specifically, the analysis identifies foundational and influential works that have shaped the field’s development. When multiple recent publications simultaneously cite the same set of earlier references, these co-cited references represent the core knowledge base and theoretical foundations underlying current research trends [ , ]. The strength of co-citation relationships is measured by the frequency with which pairs of references appear together across the literature corpus. Highly co-cited reference pairs typically share conceptual themes, methodological approaches, or address related research questions, thereby revealing the intellectual structure and evolutionary pathways of the research field [ , ]. This analysis enables identification of seminal works, emerging research fronts, and the interconnections between different research themes within DSs for BC immunotherapy.

## Data processing and visualization
Microsoft Office Excel 2021 was used for quantitative data analysis, including calculation of publication trends, citation metrics, and collaborative indices. Polynomial fitting was applied to publication data to project future research trends. All visualizations were standardized for clarity and consistency across different analytical tools.

## Temporal evolution and thematic development trajectory
Based on bibliometric analysis from 2005 to 2025, the theme evolution of DSs in BC immunotherapy exhibits distinct three-phase developmental characteristics (Fig.  A). The initial research phase (2005–2015) primarily focused on fundamental research in drug DSs and BC, establishing the theoretical foundation for subsequent technological developments. Research during this phase was relatively concentrated with limited thematic diversity, reflecting that the field was still in its nascent stage, with research emphasis on establishing basic delivery theoretical frameworks and understanding BC biological characteristics.

 

The intermediate development phase (2016–2022) demonstrated explosive growth in research themes and significant technological convergence trends. Immunotherapy, particularly anti-PD-1 ICIs, became the central focus, while diversified delivery technologies including nanomedicines, targeted DS, liposomes, and peptide carriers rapidly developed (Fig.  B). Notably, the emergence of novel concepts such as tumor-associated macrophage modulation, vessel normalization, and reactive oxygen species-mediated therapy marked the field’s transition from simple drug delivery to complex TME regulation. Research on paclitaxel DSs, BBB breakthrough technologies, and chemo-immunotherapy combination strategies provided crucial support for clinical adoption, while active targeting and biomaterial applications further enriched delivery strategies.
The recent phase (2023–2025) emphasizes precision medicine and multimodal therapies. TNBC has gained focused attention, reflecting individualized treatment trends. Advanced nanotechnology and combination therapies have become mainstream approaches. Nanotechnology and nanocarrier technologies have been further refined, with self-assembling materials and biocompatible carriers such as hyaluronic acid being increasingly widely applied. Combination immunotherapy and chemo-immunotherapy have become mainstream treatment modalities, while the integration of physical treatment approaches such as photothermal therapy demonstrates the developmental direction of multimodal therapy (Fig.  B). Particularly noteworthy is that tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) regulation, anti-tumor immunity mechanisms, and overcoming chemoresistance have become research focal points, with clinical trial numbers significantly increasing, reflecting the rapid translation trend from laboratory to clinic. Simultaneously, biodistribution optimization and intelligent design of nanomaterials have provided new technological pathways for precision delivery.
According to the latest research progress, this phase has also witnessed major breakthroughs in cell therapy. Tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) therapy and chimeric antigen receptor T-cell (CAR-T) therapy received historic clinical approvals in 2024, presenting new challenges and opportunities for DSs. The development of intelligent responsive nanocarriers (pH-sensitive, redox-sensitive, light-sensitive) has significantly enhanced drug release precision, with PEGylated nanoparticles achieving 3–4 times longer half-lives and lipid nanoparticles reducing off-target toxicity by 40% [ ]. The emergence of biomimetic nanocarriers has provided innovative solutions for overcoming immune clearance of traditional carriers.
Overall, the theme evolution trajectory of this field demonstrates a trend from basic delivery technologies toward precision, intelligence, and multifunctionalization, exhibiting distinct clinical translation-oriented characteristics. The research focus has shifted from single carrier development to systematic TIME regulation, evolving from universal therapeutic strategies to individualized delivery approaches targeting specific BC subtypes. This evolutionary process reflects that the application of DSs in BC immunotherapy is progressing toward more mature and clinically practical directions, providing important guidance for future therapeutic strategy optimization, novel cell therapy delivery, and intelligent nanomedicine development.

## Discussion

## General information
This comprehensive bibliometric analysis of 675 publications spanning two decades reveals the emergence of DSs in BC immunotherapy as a rapidly developing interdisciplinary field. The field is characterized by exponential growth and distinct geographical research patterns. The evolution occurs in three notable phases. The first phase shows a period of dormancy from 2005 to 2011. This is followed by a phase of steady development from 2012 to 2019. The most recent phase, from 2020 to 2024, is marked by explosive expansion. This progression reflects the influence of several key factors, including the clinical breakthrough of ICIs, the maturation of nanotechnology platforms, and increasing recognition of DSs as essential tools for overcoming limitations of immunotherapy. During the expansion phase, citation counts increased fivefold, whereas publication volume increased fourfold. This indicates not only growth in research output but also qualitative enhancement in research impact. It suggests a transition from exploratory investigations to clinical innovations with potential therapeutic applications.
The data also highlights a significant geographic concentration. China contributes 54.2% of the total publications. Meanwhile, the United States achieves a higher per-publication citation impact, with an average of 63.32 citations compared to 34.20 for China. These differences reflect contrasting national research strategies and broader policy frameworks. China’s high volume of publications likely results from substantial government investment in biotechnology infrastructure and incentives that prioritize publication quantity. In contrast, the United States emphasizes high-impact, translational research aligned with established pharmaceutical development pipelines. Notably, Spanish research demonstrates an exceptional citation performance, with an average of 85.18 citations per publication from only 11 papers. This illustrates that impactful contributions can arise from smaller research programs when characterized by strategic focus and international collaboration. The interdisciplinary nature of the field is evidenced by its distribution across 269 journals covering areas including materials science, oncology, and immunology. This diversity underscores the complex technological and biological challenges involved, which require convergent expertise from nanotechnology, cancer biology, and clinical medicine. Such interdisciplinary collaboration accelerates innovation but also presents challenges for standardization and clinical deployment.

## Advances in BC delivery technologies and therapeutic strategies

## Fundamental delivery technology platforms
As the foundation of modern BC immunotherapy, Cluster 1 encompasses the core technological infrastructure for DSs, featuring established platforms such as nanoparticles with unique physicochemical properties that address current treatment limitations such as drug resistance and nonspecific systemic distribution [ ]. Specific examples include liposomes, which resemble cell membrane structure and have been used as promising nanoparticles [ ], and tumor-derived exosomes that have favorable tumor-homing properties and low toxicity and immunogenicity in vivo [ ]. The platform particularly leverages extracellular vesicles, where studies have shown the promising potential of exosomes as effective drug delivery carriers in anti-BC treatment [ ].
Nanoparticles enhance cancer treatment by enabling targeted delivery, stimuli responsive drug release, and combination therapies including immunotherapy, thereby improving therapeutic efficacy, stimulating anti-tumor immune responses, and reducing side effects [ ]. Additionally, artemisinin-manganese oxide co-DSs have been developed for precise BC diagnosis and treatment [ ]. These foundational technologies demonstrate sophisticated biomarker-guided approaches and precision medicine applications that effectively manipulate the TME to overcome drug resistance mechanisms.
Building upon these fundamental delivery platforms, the next critical advancement involves engineering these systems to achieve precise immune modulation, which forms the basis for more sophisticated therapeutic applications.

## Immune modulation and biomaterial engineering
Advancing from basic delivery platforms, Cluster 2 focuses on engineering sophisticated biomaterial platforms to achieve precise immune system activation, particularly through DC-derived exosomes (Dex) that carry antigen-presenting particles like MHC-I and MHC-II, CD80 and CD86, and Hsp 70 and 90, enabling DC to increase T-cell activity towards tumor tissue areas [ ].
The mechanistic basis for exosome-mediated T cell enhancement involves multiple complementary pathways that directly address key immunotherapeutic challenges in BC. DC-derived exosomes facilitate antigen presentation through three distinct mechanisms: direct T cell activation via exosomal MHC/peptide complexes, MHC recycling onto recipient antigen-presenting cells, and complete antigen processing by recipient cells with endogenous MHC loading, thereby eliminating the requirement for MHC matching between donor and recipient [ ]. Importantly, engineered exosomes displaying anti-CD3 and anti-HER2 antibodies have demonstrated successful retargeting and activation of cytotoxic T cells specifically toward HER2-positive BC cells, showing high efficiency and specific antitumor activity in preclinical models [ ].
This represents a significant evolution from passive drug delivery to active immune system engagement. Practical implementations include lipid-based nanoparticles encapsulating ginsenoside Rg3 and paclitaxel within liposomes, employing a dual-targeting mechanism to overcome the TME’s protective barriers [ , , ].
Exosome-based platforms effectively overcome multiple resistance mechanisms that limit conventional immunotherapy efficacy in BC. These systems can reverse drug resistance by targeting key immunosuppressive pathways, including PD-L1-mediated immune evasion, where exosomal delivery of ICIs has shown enhanced therapeutic indices compared to systemic administration [ , ]. Additionally, exosomes can modulate the TME by reprogramming immunosuppressive M2 macrophages toward pro-inflammatory M1 phenotypes and reducing myeloid-derived suppressor cell infiltration, thereby converting immunologically “cold” tumors into “hot” tumors amenable to immunotherapy [ ].
Advanced biomaterial applications demonstrate significant therapeutic potential, such as CaO2@CuS-MnO2@HA (CCMH) nanoparticles for BC therapy, where CaO2 reacts with H2O to produce oxygen, promoting CuS-mediated PDT leading to immunogenic cell death [ ]. The cluster also encompasses engineered nanoparticles derived from Norovirus that promoted the maturation of bone marrow-derived DCs and induced strong tumor-specific T cell immune responses [ ], demonstrating the sophisticated integration of nanotechnology with immunomodulatory mechanisms.
The translational potential of these approaches is evidenced by advancing clinical applications, including STING agonist-loaded exosomes (exoSTING/CDK-002) currently in Phase I trials for metastatic TNBC, which target antigen-presenting cells in the TME to induce interferon gene expression and local immune activation [ ]. Furthermore, personalized DC-derived exosomes loaded with patient tumor lysates and tethered with IL-2 have demonstrated the ability to sensitize immunotherapy-resistant BC to immune checkpoint blockade through enhanced lymphocyte targeting and IL-2-mediated immune cell activation [ ].
These immune-modulating platforms, while powerful, require further refinement to address the heterogeneous nature of BC, leading to the development of subtype-specific precision therapies.

## BC subtype-specific precision therapy
Leveraging the immune modulation capabilities established in Cluster 2, Cluster 3 represents the medical implementation of delivery technologies specifically engineered for BC molecular subtypes, with particular emphasis on HER2-positive and TNBC applications. This progression from general immune activation to targeted precision medicine reflects the growing understanding of BC heterogeneity. Nanoparticle DSs present a totally new way of drug design and usage, changing the pharmacokinetic characteristics of conventional chemotherapeutic drugs with significantly reduced adverse effects [ ] for TNBC treatment. Specific targeting strategies include exosomes modified with GE11 peptide delivering let-7a miRNA into epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)-overexpressed BC cells (HCC70, HCC 1954, and MCF-7) [ ].
Clinical applications demonstrate enhanced therapeutic efficacy through exosomal doxorubicin that significantly increased the therapeutic index and limited crossing through myocardial endothelial cells, avoiding drug accumulation in the heart [ ] in BC models. Advanced combination approaches include exosomes delivering miR-567 that target autophagy-related proteins (ATG5) to reverse trastuzumab resistance in BC cells [ ], exemplifying the sophisticated molecular targeting capabilities required for personalized BC immunotherapy.
The success of these subtype-specific approaches has paved the way for the development of even more sophisticated multimodal therapeutic platforms that integrate multiple treatment modalities for enhanced clinical efficacy.

## Advanced therapeutic technologies and clinical translation
Building upon the precision targeting strategies of Cluster 3, Cluster 4 encompasses cutting-edge multimodal therapeutic platforms progressing toward clinical implementation, representing the natural evolution toward comprehensive treatment approaches. These systems integrate the foundational delivery technologies, immune modulation capabilities, and precision targeting into unified therapeutic platforms. Featured technologies include photosensitizer-based nanoplatforms (CAM-NPs) constructed with human serum albumin, composed of chlorin e6, axitinib, and dextro-1-methyl tryptophan that secrete significant immunogenic cell death biomarkers and reverse hypoxic situations [ – ].
Innovative combination strategies demonstrate substantial clinical potential, such as gold nanorod-polyethylene glycol and exosome-liposome paclitaxel systems that integrate thermal ablation, adaptive antitumor immunotherapy, and targeted chemotherapy for advanced BC [ – ]. The cluster also features binary cooperative prodrug nanoparticles (BCPN) that release oxaliplatin and NLG919 (an IDO-1 inhibitor) when triggered by acidic environments, achieving stronger EPR effects and promoting T lymphocyte infiltration [ – ], representing the sophisticated theranostic approaches advancing toward clinical application.
However, even these advanced multimodal approaches face significant challenges when addressing the most complex clinical scenarios, particularly when targeting anatomically challenging sites such as brain metastases.

## Specialized anatomical site treatment challenges
Representing the ultimate convergence of all previously described technological advances, Cluster 5 addresses the most challenging delivery scenarios in BC treatment, specifically targeting brain metastases through BBB penetration strategies. This cluster exemplifies how the foundational platforms (Cluster 1), immune modulation capabilities (Cluster 2), precision targeting (Cluster 3), and multimodal approaches (Cluster 4) must be integrated to overcome the most formidable physiological barriers. Ginseng-derived exosome-like nanoparticles (GENs) have demonstrated active BBB penetration and TME modulation, effectively recruiting M1 macrophages and inhibiting BC brain metastasis progression [ ]. Specifically for TNBC brain metastases, engineered exosomes derived from mesenchymal stem cells loaded with doxorubicin and modified with transferrin receptor-targeting peptides have demonstrated enhanced BBB crossing efficiency [ , ]. Additionally, exosomal delivery of miR-3182 via HUCMSC-derived exosomes markedly suppresses proliferation and migration and induces apoptosis in TNBC cells by downregulating mTOR and S6KB1. This supports exosomes as targeted carriers for miRNA-based therapy in TNBC, with potential to address brain metastasis by enabling delivery of therapeutic miRNAs across the BBB [ , ]. Furthermore, several studies report exosome-based or exosome-mimetic DSs to tackle TNBC and brain targets. A “triple-punch” exosome-mimetic vesicle enhances CD82, AS1411 targeting, and DOX delivery to inhibit TNBC migration and proliferation, suggesting potential for TNBC brain metastasis. Other approaches leverage BBB-penetrating exosome-derived nanoreactors or biomimetic exosome-liposome hybrids (pHybrid) to deliver therapeutic cargos across the BBB, targeting glioblastoma and glioma with synergistic cytotoxic and anti-angiogenic effects [ – ]. Advanced approaches include synthetic protein nanoparticles encapsulating transcytotic peptide iRGD that inhibit the CXCL2/CXCR4 pathway, decreasing CXCR4 + monocytic MDSCs infiltration and promoting immunogenic cell death [ – ].
The specialized nature of this challenge requires integration of multiple technological approaches, where M1-like macrophage-derived exosomes integrated with aptamer-conjugated liposomes create hybrid nanovesicles for targeted TME reprogramming in TNBC models [ , , ]. These applications represent the convergence of all previously described technological directions, demonstrating the sophisticated engineering required to overcome physiological barriers and achieve therapeutic efficacy in the most challenging anatomical sites.
This comprehensive technological progression from fundamental platforms to specialized applications illustrates the systematic advancement of BC delivery technologies, where each cluster builds upon previous innovations to address increasingly complex clinical challenges.

## Current technical challenges and bottlenecks
Despite remarkable technological advances, DSs in BC immunotherapy face several critical bottlenecks that limit translational development and therapeutic efficacy.
Biological barrier penetration: The heterogeneous TME presents formidable challenges, with dense extracellular matrix, elevated interstitial pressure, and aberrant vasculature significantly restricting nanocarrier penetration depths beyond 50–100 μm from blood vessels [ – ]. BBB penetration remains particularly challenging, with most conventional DSs achieving less than 0.1% brain accumulation [ , ].
Immunogenicity and biocompatibility: Repeated administration of DSs triggers complement activation and accelerated blood clearance, reducing therapeutic efficiency in subsequent doses [ ]. Synthetic nanocarriers often elicit undesired immune responses that can compromise both safety and efficacy profiles.
Manufacturing and scalability: Current nanoparticle synthesis methods demonstrate significant batch-to-batch reproducibility challenges, characterized by substantial variations in size distribution and inconsistent drug loading efficiency [ – ]. Scale-up from laboratory to commercial production remains technically challenging and economically prohibitive for many platforms.
Drug loading and release control: Drug loading capacities vary dramatically across different delivery platforms, creating significant challenges for therapeutic optimization. Traditional liposomal systems demonstrate relatively low drug loading capacities (approximately 0.4 wt%), while advanced platforms such as metal-organic frameworks can achieve substantially higher loading capacities of up to 25 wt%, representing more than a 60-fold improvement [ , ]. Polymeric nanoparticles typically achieve intermediate loading capacities ranging from 2 to 15 wt% depending on drug-polymer compatibility and formulation methods [ , ]. However, achieving optimal drug loading often requires trade-offs with other critical parameters including stability, release kinetics, and biocompatibility [ ].
Additionally, most systems lack precise temporal release control, resulting in premature drug release or inadequate bioavailability at target sites [ , ]. Multi-drug loading for combination immunotherapy presents additional complexity in maintaining drug stability and achieving synchronized release kinetics, with co-loading efficiencies often significantly lower than single-drug formulations [ , ].
Targeting specificity: Current active targeting strategies show inherent limitations in selectivity, with tumor-to-normal tissue discrimination falling short of desired precision, thereby constraining the therapeutic potential of targeted drug delivery approaches [ , ]. Off-target accumulation in liver, spleen, and kidneys continues to pose safety concerns and reduces therapeutic windows.
Regulatory and translation barriers: The complexity of nanomedicine characterization requires extensive analytical development, while regulatory frameworks struggle to accommodate rapid technological evolution. Clinical trial design faces challenges in standardizing DS evaluation metrics and establishing appropriate endpoints for efficacy assessment [ – ].
These technical barriers necessitate innovative engineering solutions and regulatory framework evolution to realize the full therapeutic potential of DSs in BC immunotherapy.

## Systemic transformations and future perspectives in BC immunotherapy
The temporal evolution analysis reveals fundamental shifts in research paradigms that transcend individual technological developments, establishing DSs as a transformative force reshaping the entire landscape of BC immunotherapy. This systematic transformation has generated profound implications for research methodologies, clinical practice frameworks, and healthcare infrastructure that extend far beyond the technical innovations themselves.

## Paradigmatic shift in research methodologies
The three-phase evolutionary trajectory demonstrates a fundamental transition from discipline-specific research to convergent interdisciplinary approaches. Early-stage research operated within traditional boundaries of materials science, oncology, and immunology as separate entities. However, the contemporary research landscape demands integrative expertise spanning nanotechnology, tumor biology, immunology, and clinical medicine, fundamentally altering how research teams are constituted and funded. This methodological evolution has established new standards for translational research, where basic science discoveries must demonstrate clear pathways to clinical implementation from conception [ , ].
The acceleration of translation timelines observed in recent years indicates a systemic restructuring of the research-to-clinic pipeline. Traditional linear progression from basic research through preclinical studies to clinical trials has evolved into parallel, iterative development processes that enable rapid concept validation and course correction. This transformation has profound implications for research funding strategies, intellectual property development, and academic-industry collaboration models.

## Healthcare system transformation requirements
The integration of sophisticated DSs necessitates fundamental restructuring of healthcare delivery models. Clinical practice must evolve to accommodate personalized treatment selection algorithms that integrate molecular profiling, DS optimization, and real-time therapeutic monitoring. This evolution demands healthcare infrastructure capable of supporting complex diagnostic workflows, specialized treatment administration protocols, and comprehensive toxicity management systems.
Healthcare economics face unprecedented challenges as sophisticated delivery platforms require substantial upfront investments in technology, training, and infrastructure development. The cost-effectiveness paradigms must evolve to account for long-term benefits of precision DSs, including reduced treatment failures, minimized adverse effects, and improved quality-adjusted life years [ , ]. Healthcare policy frameworks require fundamental revision to accommodate quickly developing technologies and establish appropriate reimbursement mechanisms for innovative delivery platforms.

## Strategic directions for field development
The convergence trends identified in the temporal analysis indicate future research priorities will center on developing universal delivery platforms capable of accommodating diverse therapeutic modalities and patient populations. This suggests a strategic shift from developing specialized systems for specific applications toward creating adaptable platforms that can be rapidly configured for emerging therapeutic needs. Such universal platforms would accelerate the translation of novel therapeutic concepts by leveraging established delivery infrastructure.
The increasing emphasis on barrier-penetrating technologies suggests future research investments should prioritize developing DSs capable of accessing previously intractable anatomical sites. This strategic direction has implications for drug development priorities, regulatory approval pathways, and clinical trial design methodologies. Furthermore, the integration of artificial intelligence and machine learning into DS design indicates future development will increasingly rely on predictive modeling and adaptive optimization algorithms [ , ].

## Long-term implications for cancer care
The systematic evolution toward precision delivery platforms suggests BC treatment will increasingly transition from population-based protocols to individualized therapeutic ecosystems. This transformation will fundamentally alter patient care experiences, requiring comprehensive molecular characterization, personalized DS selection, and continuous treatment optimization [ – ]. The implications extend to medical education, where healthcare providers must develop competencies in complex technology management and personalized treatment planning.
The global impact of these developments indicates potential for significant healthcare disparities as sophisticated DSs require substantial technological infrastructure and specialized expertise. Strategic planning must address equitable access to advanced delivery platforms and develop simplified, cost-effective alternatives for resource-limited settings. The long-term sustainability of healthcare systems depends on balancing technological advancement with accessibility and affordability considerations.
In summary, the systemic transformation of DSs in BC immunotherapy encompasses interdisciplinary research convergence, healthcare infrastructure restructuring, universal platform development, and individualized care ecosystem establishment, collectively reshaping the entire landscape from fundamental research methodologies through clinical practice frameworks to long-term cancer care paradigms.

## Limitation and future directions

## Research limitations
This bibliometric analysis presents several methodological constraints that may influence interpretation. First, the exclusive reliance on WoSCC, while ensuring data quality and standardization, may have omitted relevant publications indexed in other databases such as Scopus or PubMed, potentially underestimating research output from specific regions or publication venues. Second, the search strategy, despite comprehensive keyword selection, may have missed studies using alternative terminology or emerging nomenclature in this swiftly changing field. Third, the citation analysis reflects cumulative impact over time, potentially undervaluing recent high-quality publications that have not yet accrued substantial citations due to temporal lag.
From a field-specific perspective, the research landscape reveals significant limitations in translational readiness. Despite exponential growth in preclinical studies, the majority of DSs remain confined to laboratory settings with limited progression to clinical trials. The pronounced geographic concentration of research, particularly in China, may create knowledge gaps in patient population diversity and regulatory pathway optimization for global implementation. Additionally, the interdisciplinary nature of the field creates challenges in establishing standardized evaluation metrics and comparison frameworks across different technological platforms and therapeutic approaches.

## Future improvement directions
Future research should prioritize several critical development areas to enhance clinical adoption and therapeutic efficacy. First, establishing universal delivery platforms capable of accommodating diverse immunotherapeutic modalities while maintaining biocompatibility and manufacturing scalability represents a strategic imperative. These platforms should incorporate intelligent responsiveness to tumor microenvironmental stimuli and adaptive release mechanisms to optimize therapeutic timing and dosing.
Second, addressing anatomical barriers, particularly BBB penetration for brain metastases treatment, requires innovative engineering approaches combining physical penetration enhancement with biological targeting strategies. Third, developing comprehensive biomarker-guided patient selection algorithms will enable precision matching of DSs to individual tumor characteristics and immune profiles.
Looking specifically toward BC applications, exosome-CAR-T integration platforms offer transformative potential for TNBC, where CAR-T cell-derived exosomes demonstrate significant antitumor effects without acute toxicities associated with traditional CAR-T therapy [ ]. Similarly, AI-driven DS optimization for HER2-positive BC represents a critical frontier, with machine learning algorithms achieving improved targeting specificity and therapeutic response prediction, including enhanced HER2 scoring accuracy from 85.3% to 88.0% for treatment decision-making [ ]. The convergence of these technologies suggests unprecedented opportunities for AI-optimized exosome-CAR platforms that enable rapid customization based on individual tumor profiles [ , ].
Fourth, future bibliometric analyses should incorporate multi-database integration, real-time patent landscape monitoring, and clinical trial progression tracking to provide more comprehensive field mapping. Finally, establishing international collaborative frameworks for standardized evaluation protocols and regulatory harmonization will accelerate the translation of promising delivery technologies from bench to bedside, ultimately improving treatment outcomes for BC patients worldwide.

## Conclusion
This bibliometric analysis of 675 publications reveals that DSs in BC immunotherapy have evolved into a rapidly expanding field with exponential growth since 2020. Five research clusters were identified: nanocarrier platforms, immune modulation, precision targeting, translational development, and barrier-penetrating technologies, collectively addressing key limitations of traditional immunotherapy. The field demonstrates successful convergence of nanotechnology and cancer biology toward personalized treatment strategies.
Our findings highlight productive research networks and emerging technological paradigms, providing strategic guidance for optimizing future investments and accelerating clinical deployment. The integration of advanced DSs with immunotherapy represents a revolutionary approach with significant potential for improving BC treatment outcomes through enhanced efficacy and reduced toxicity.
