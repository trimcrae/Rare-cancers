## Background
Mpox, primarily endemic to Central and West Africa, has seen a concerning global rise in recent years. There is a critical need to synthesize evidence on successful strategies that have been used to contain and respond to mpox globally to inform policy and best practices.

## Methods
A systematic review was conducted using the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. Four databases were searched for publications between 2017 to August 2024. Articles were included if they reported on mpox response and containment strategies.

## Results
A total of 72 studies were included in this systematic review. During mpox outbreaks, screening was primarily conducted at sexual health clinics, including Sexually Transmitted Infections and PrEP (Pre-exposure prophylaxis) clinics. PCR (Polymerase chain reaction) testing was the most commonly reported diagnostic method. Three treatment options were identified: Tecovirimat, Vaccinia Immune Globulin, and Cidofovir. Additionally, vaccines were classified into two main categories: (i) JYNNEOS (also known as MVA-BN and IMVANEX) and (ii) ACAM2000. Studies highlighted several challenges associated with isolation measures, including difficulties in maintaining consistent follow-up, ensuring patient compliance, and managing large-scale isolation logistics. Surveillance strategies emphasized effective data collection, case investigations, and public health coordination to strengthen outbreak response. Pre-emergency preparedness efforts focused on international collaboration, the development of distribution networks, models of care for Mpox treatment, and the analysis of past outbreaks to improve future response strategies.

## Conclusion
This review synthesized strategies for mpox outbreak response and containment, highlighting the need to expand vaccine access beyond GBMSM/LGBTQIA + populations, integrate screening into routine sexual health services, adopt alternative delivery and diagnostic approaches, strengthen surveillance and pre-emergency preparedness, and promote context-specific research in low- and middle-income countries to inform evidence-based policy and practice.

## Introduction
 Mpox, formerly referred to as monkeypox, is a zoonotic viral disease caused by the monkeypox virus (MPXV), part of the Orthopoxvirus genus and closely related to the smallpox virus [ – ]. First isolated in 1958 from lesions on captive primates in Denmark, MPXV’s first human case appeared in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) in 1970, marked by symptoms resembling smallpox. Although mpox was primarily endemic to Central and West Africa, recent years have seen a concerning increase in cases, particularly in the DRC, prompting widespread attention [ , – ].
Traditionally, mpox outbreaks were sporadic, predominantly affecting those involved in hunting or handling bushmeat. MPXV transmission occurs via direct contact with the skin lesions or body fluids of an infected animal or person or through contaminated materials. After a 7–14 day incubation period, infected individuals typically experience fever, followed by a rash; however, clinical outcomes vary widely, from mild disease to severe complications such as encephalitis, sepsis, or bronchopneumonia. Immunocompromised individuals, especially people living with HIV (PLWHIV), face a higher risk of severe disease and mortality [ , , ].
Two main MPXV clades have been identified: clade I, linked to recurrent outbreaks in the DRC between 1981 and 2017 with high fatality rates, and clade II, which in 2017 caused a large outbreak in Nigeria with a lower case fatality rate [ , ]. In July 2022, the World Health Organization (WHO) declared a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC) due to a global mpox outbreak that spread beyond Africa, notably through sexual networks [ ]. By May 2023 WHO declared the PHEIC was over following a sustained decline in global cases. Between January 2022 and June 2024, 99,176 confirmed mpox cases, including 298 deaths, were reported in 116 countries [ ]. As of October 2024, Africa reported a total of 50,840 mpox cases, with 10,741 confirmed through real-time PCR testing and 1,083 deaths [ ].
By August 2024, the situation became critical, leading the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention (Africa CDC) to declare the mpox outbreak a Public Health Emergency of Continental Security (PHECS), mobilizing resources for vaccine distribution and bolstering surveillance and laboratory capabilities [ – ]. The WHO simultaneously declared a new PHEIC, underscoring the rapid spread of a new mpox clade, Clade 1b, primarily through sexual contact [ , ]. Despite significant control efforts, the exact animal reservoir of MPXV remains undetermined, though antibodies have been identified in several rodent and primate species, emphasizing mpox’s ongoing threat, especially in regions with limited healthcare infrastructure [ , ].
Global responses to mpox, particularly in Africa, have faced significant criticism for their inadequacy, with urgent calls for greater international collaboration, and bold investments in strengthening national responses [ , ]. The recent surge in mpox cases and the emergence of new strains underscore the critical need to draw lessons from previous outbreaks to enhance preparedness, response and containment strategies. Containment measures focus on preventing the spread of mpox within the community or to other regions. These actions include quarantine, isolation, contact tracing, vaccination campaigns, public health communications, and movement restrictions. Meanwhile response strategies encompass the broader set of actions taken by governments, health organizations, and communities to manage an mpox outbreak at various stages, from detection to recovery. These strategies include surveillance, emergency preparedness, rapid diagnostic testing, international collaboration, and community engagement, all aimed at controlling the outbreak and mitigating its impact on public health [ – ].
This systematic review seeks to provide a comprehensive synthesis of existing knowledge on previous mpox outbreaks, with the goal of identifying effective response strategies and management approaches, as well as uncovering gaps in the current literature. While the WHO released a global strategic preparedness and response plan for mpox in September 2024, there is still a critical need to synthesize evidence on successful strategies that have been used to contain and respond to the disease, to provide an overview of the effectiveness and applicability of various containment and response strategies across different regions and settings. This includes identifying best practices that have proven effective in outbreak management, to enhance future preparedness and response efforts. Although prior reviews have primarily focused on the clinical presentation, diagnostic tools, and treatment options for mpox [ , , , ], they did not review all mpox containment and response strategies as a whole. This review aims to provide a comprehensive synthesis of existing knowledge on past outbreaks, including response strategies, management approaches, and containment efforts, and to identify gaps in the current literature and offer insights to inform future research and policy development.

## Methods

## Protocol development
Using the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-analyses (PRISMA) guidelines and rapid review needs assessment methodology for outbreaks [ ], we developed the protocol for this review. The protocol was aimed at identifying existing evidence on response strategies, approaches and containment efforts of mpox to inform research gaps and policy development in containing the spread of mpox. The protocol was collaboratively developed by five researchers, public health professionals and physicians between 16-18th August 2024 (Appendix ).

## Literature search
A comprehensive literature search was conducted across four databases: Embase (via Ovid), Scopus, Web of Science, and Medline (via Ovid). The search was conducted by three persons from 18 to 19 August 2024 to identify studies related to mpox outbreak response strategies implemented in various countries. The search strategy was developed using a combination of keywords and Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) terms related to mpox, containment strategies, and outbreak management. Boolean logic operators “OR” and “AND” were employed to refine the search, and truncations were used for terms like “strateg*,” “manage*,” “contain*,” and “guideline*.” We utilised 04 search strings; (1) mpox AND strategy* OR Monkeypox AND Strategy* OR MPXV AND strategy*; (2) mpox AND manage* OR Monkeypox AND Manage* OR MPXV AND manage*; (3) mpox AND contain* OR MonkeyPox AND Contain* OR MPXV AND contain*; (4) mpox AND Guideline* OR Monkeypox AND Guideline* OR MPXV AND guideline*. (See Appendix for full search strategy).

## Inclusion and exclusion criteria
For this systematic review, we included any study with primary data collection that reported on mpox response and containment strategies conducted between January 2017 to August 2024 and published in English. The year 2017 was chosen as the start of the search because in 2017 mpox re-emerged in Nigeria, spreading to other countries. Additionally, reports of interventions and documentation detailing response and containment strategies from national health authorities or global health organizations were included. Exclusion criteria included systematic reviews, commentaries, editorials, opinion pieces, general narrative reviews, and research protocols. Studies focused solely on virology or immunology without direct application to outbreak response, non-specific outbreak studies, broad public health strategies not focused on mpox, and non-English or non-French language articles without reliable translations were also excluded.

## Data extraction and synthesis
Following the database searches, articles were downloaded and uploaded into Endnote where duplicates were removed using built in duplicate detection. Search outputs were input into Covidence where further duplicates were removed. Each title and abstract, and subsequently full texts, were screened independently by two reviewers. Conflicts at each stage were resolved in discussion with a third reviewer. A standardized data extraction form was used to extract relevant information from the included studies. Data points included study characteristics (author, year, study design), details of the outbreak, containment strategies, and relevant outcomes (screening, diagnostics, case management, vaccination, isolation, surveillance, and pre-emergency preparedness). Some papers reported on more than one or multiple containment and management strategies.

## Quality assessment
The quality of the selected observational, experimental studies, reports and publications was done using CASP (Critical Appraisal Skills Program) [ ] and the Critical Appraisal tool for Cross-Sectional Studies (AXIS) [ ]. The assessment was independently done by one assessor to give a final quality rating for each paper. Publications were categorized according to scores as follows; high quality (80–100%), medium quality (60–79%), low quality (< 60%).

## Results
Of the 5627 records identified, 2501 duplicates were removed. Titles and abstracts were screened for 3126 references and 2896 were excluded. Of the remaining 230 records, 72 studies met the eligibility criteria after full text screening and were included in the final analysis (Fig. ). The key themes identified in the mpox outbreak containment, response and management were: Diagnostics (n = 6), screening (n = 3), case management (n = 10), vaccination (n = 46), pre-emergency preparedness (n = 3), isolation (n = 5), surveillance (n = 14), with some studies addressing multiple domains. No study reported on triage as a strategy for containment. The studies reported containment, response and management strategies from 41 countries (Fig. ), with most papers coming from North America (n = 39) and Europe (n = 26), while only six papers were reported from Africa. Publication dates spanned from 2017 to 2024, providing a timeline of mpox management and responses over the past decades. Study designs varied, but mostly included retrospective studies (n = 18), prospective studies (n = 10), observational studies (n = 8), operational report (n = 10), case reports (n = 7), case control (n = 6), modelling (n = 4), cross sectional (n = 3), cohort (n = 3), case series (n = 2), mixed methods (n = 1) and qualitative study (n = 1). Literature quality varied, with 67 studies rated as high quality, one as moderate, and three as low. Study characteristics are summarized in Appendix , with quality assessments detailed in Appendix . One letter to the editor was included in view of the high quality of operational data [ ], but the quality was not reviewed using formal tools due to its format. Of all the papers reviewed, only 10 specified the strain of mpox outbreak with clade IIb being reported the most (n = 5) [ – ], followed by clade II (n = 4) [ – ] and lastly clade I (n = 1) [ ].

## Screening
Screening during mpox outbreaks was reported to be carried out at sexual health clinics (sexually transmitted infections (STI)and HIV pre-exposure prophylaxis(PrEP) clinics) [ – ]. Patients presenting at clinics for screening presented with co-infections (gonorrhoea, chlamydia, syphilis, co-infections) and predominantly male. Screening during mpox outbreaks identified a small proportion of clinically inapparent cases, with one patient testing positive before developing symptoms and 75 samples being missed by standard symptom-based screening in a study from Berlin [ ]. Clinically inapparent mpox cases were more commonly associated with concurrent STIs [ ]. No significant differences were observed in cycle threshold (Ct) values between physician- and self-collected specimens, demonstrating the feasibility of incorporating self-collection into mpox screening protocol [ ]. ## Diagnostics
Across six studies reporting mpox diagnostics (Table ), polymerase chain reaction (PCR) testing emerged asthe most reported diagnostic method [ – ].Specimens for diagnosis included blood [ ], swabs from skin lesions, oropharyngeal, nasopharyngeal and rectal swabs [ , , ]. The GeneXpert MPX/OPX (a portable rapid diagnostic device) assay demonstrated high sensitivity (98.8%) and specificity (100%) across all rash stages [ ], with comparable performance between crust and vesicle specimens. Paediatric mpox detection was less frequent than in adults (1.3% vs. 22.3%), with the highest positivity at the extremes of the paediatric age group [ ], while real-time PCR showed higher positivity in anogenital, skin, and serum samples compared to nasopharyngeal specimens [ ]. Kinganda-Lusamaki et al. [ ] found that by combining MPXV-specific antibody testing with PCR testing, 66 additional patient samples were positive for Mpox in the DRC, and that the positivity rate increased from 33.7% by PCR to 48.2% when both PCR and/or seropositivity were considered. This allowed for 14 additional health zones and 23 additional outbreaks to be identified between 2013 and 2022.

 

## Case management
Case management strategies were documented in 10 studies [ , , – ], with seven publications reporting the use of Tecovirimat and 3 reporting the use of cidofovir for treating patients (Table ). Eight studies discussed the establishment of dedicated response teams, with Crouch et al. [ ] describing how the South East Public Health Unit (SEPHU) in the UK implemented workflows for prioritized case management and contact tracing, which significantly reduced response times. Seven studies reported the use of Tecovirimat for treatment of mpox patients. O’Neil et al. [ ] utilised the hub and spoke model for service delivery, and reported that patient navigation service, provider and patient consultation lineswere key reasons they achieved higher than national average treatmentrates [ ]. One out of three studies reporting the use of topical cidofovir, reported that lesions cleared quicker in the cidofovir-treated group and shorter time to a negative viral PCR (10% PCR positive at 14 days compared to 62.5% in the non-treatment group) [ ]. Nine studies reported PLWHIV with mpox receiving treatment for mpox [ , – ]. Vo et al. [ ] highlighted PLWHIV experienced similar rates of recovery, bacterial superinfections, and hospitalization compared to patients without immunocompromising conditions, but prolonged viral shedding and higher rates of hospitalization for severely immunocompromised individuals, underscoring the need for tailored management for immunocompromised groups. Crouch et al. [ ] reported their use of local public health units in Australia, used in COVID-19, for their management of mpox, through capacity building of health professionals to increase early diagnosis, contact tracing, facilitating vaccine delivery, and community engagement, reporting this was an effective strategy [ ]. ## Vaccination
Vaccination emerged as a pivotal response and containment strategy with 46 studies [ , – , , – ] documenting two vaccine types (MVA-BN, ACAM2000) during mpox outbreaks (Table ). Studies (n = 42) reporting on MVA-BN either reported about the use of it the form JYNNEOS (n = 25), IMVANEX (n = 2) or MVA-BN (n = 16). Only one article reported the use of ACAM2000 [ ]. JYNNEOS vaccine was reported to be administered as one and two doses as a PrEP in 11 publications [ , – , , , , , , ]. One and two doses of JYNNEOS were reported to be administered for post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) in five articles [ , – , ]. Two dose administration JYNNEOS were reported in two articles as PrEP [ , ], two articles reported JYNNEOS being administered as PEP [ , ]. Three articles reported JYNNEOS administration as two doses without specifying if it was for PrEP or PEP [ , ].
For MVA-BV vaccine, it was reported to be used for use as PrEP in three studies [ , ] and PEP in two studies [ , ] using one and two doses. Eight studies reported the use of MVA-BN using one dose as PEP [ , , , , , ] and PrEP [ , ]. Two publications reported the use of MVA-BN without specifying the dosage or whether the vaccine was administered as a PEP or PrEP [ , ]. Of the 46 publications that reported vaccinations as a response strategy, 33 reported vaccinating men or men-having-sex with men or LGBTQ + persons (Table ). Faherty et al. [ ] reports the one patient who received the ACAM2000 vaccine (one dose) during the global mpox outbreak.
Seven models of care were identified across the included studies as approaches for delivering vaccines during mpox outbreaks. These include health facilities (including clinics, hospitals, health centers, n = 24), outreach (n = 7), sexual health clinics (n = 9), mobile clinics (n = 5), pharmacy (n = 1), home visits (n = 1) and telemedicine (n = 1) (Table  ).

 

## Isolation
Various isolation strategies were implemented across different settings to contain mpox outbreaks (Table ). In Cologne, Germany, a mandatory 21-day isolation period was enforced for PCR-confirmed cases, supported by regular telephone follow-ups conducted by trained personnel. However, follow-up was hindered for patients who left Cologne during their isolation period [ ]. Similarly, in other contexts [ ] response teams coordinated isolation efforts alongside other response efforts. Virtual assistants and clinic-based assessments were employed to address challenges in isolation follow-up and treatment, demonstrating adaptability in overcoming logistical hurdles. Yang et al. [ ] demonstrated through modelling estimates that a combined strategy of early detection, treatment, and isolation was more effective in controlling mpox than increased vaccine coverage alone. In Greece, suspected mpox patients were isolated throughout the infectious period, from the prodromal to rash stages, with isolation maintained until all lesions resolved [ ]. In other instances, home isolation was advised, such as in the study by Duque [ ], where confirmed cases were isolated until their lesions resolved, accompanied by a 21-day self-monitoring period for contacts. Across these studies, common challenges included maintaining consistent follow-up, ensuring compliance with isolation requirements, and addressing the logistical complexities of large-scale isolation measures.

 

## Case surveillance
Fifteen (n = 15) of the reviewed studies highlight a range of surveillance strategies employed during mpox outbreaks, emphasizing the importance of robust data collection, case investigation, and public health coordination [ – , , , , – ]. Key activities included laboratory-based surveillance, contact tracing, and real-time monitoring of systems, often supported by electronic databases such as the National Electronic Disease Surveillance System Base System (NBS) and proprietary platforms like Microsoft Power Apps and SharePoint (Table ). Innovative approaches, such as wastewater sampling and the use of demographic and clinical data from standardized interviews, further strengthened disease monitoring. However, significant challenges were reported, including delays in case reporting, missing patient contact information, limited testing accessibility, and inconsistencies in data management due to reliance on interim systems. Contact tracing was frequently hindered by incomplete or inaccurate contact details, particularly among cases involving multiple or anonymous partners. Despite these challenges, efforts to enhance surveillance capacity such as establishing emergency operation centres, deploying rapid response teams, and engaging community stakeholders highlighted the adaptability of public health systems to evolving outbreaks (Table ).

 

## Pre-emergency preparedness
Only three publications reported on pre-emergency strategies focused on international collaboration [ ], development of distribution networks and models of care for treatment [ ] and analysis of previous outbreaks [ ]. The hub and spoke model used in Los Angeles, built upon existing medical countermeasure distribution networks, for coordinating treatment of Tecovirimat, using community clinics, pharmacies and hospitals for treatment delivery achieved a higher than national average treatment percentage, and median time from testing to treatment of two days. ## Discussion
This systematic review synthesizes the evidence on mpox containment strategies employed by various programmes, national health authorities and international organizations during outbreaks. Mpox outbreaks pose unique challenges to healthcare systems [ , , , ], often requiring specialized approaches due to factors like asymptomatic transmission, diagnostic challenges in varied populations, and the need for rapid vaccine distribution to at-risk groups [ – , , ]. The systematic review included 72 studies that reported strategies to contain or respond to mpox outbreaks including; isolation, screening, diagnostic strategies, case management, vaccination, and pre-emergency preparedness. These approaches form core recommendations in the control containment and response to mpox globally and in Africa [ ]. Of the 72 studies included, seven focused on diagnostic approaches, primarily emphasizing PCR testing while screening was reported inthree studies that utilized outpatient, community and sexual health clinics to identify Mpox populations making use of health facilities for HIV PrEP or STI services. Case management strategies, documented in 20 studies, involving treatments mostly using Tecovirimat. Three papers reported the use of Cidofovir in treating mpox patients. Vaccination emerged as a central strategy amongst included papers, with 46 studies reporting MVA-BN (JYNNEOS, Imnavex) and ACAM2000 vaccines as response and containment strategies. Several of these studies were retrospective studies (n = 18), with no randomized controlled trials (RCTs). However, it is important to acknowledge that conducting RCTs may not be feasible during a public health emergency of international concern. Only six papers were from Africa, even though the escalating incidence of mpox has been reported to be a call for concern in the continent [ ]. This could highlight significant gaps in vaccine deployment, early detection, and intervention strategies in the African contexts. Also, the best practices reported majorly from high-income countries may not be applicable or successful in low-resource settings in Africa. This highlights the need for more contextual evidence on mpox responses in Africa and other low-income settings.
Results from this review highlight the importance of targeted testing (screening) in identifying cases, particularly in high-risk populations such as those attending sexual health clinics for STI and PrEP services [ – ]. Persons who were screened were predominantly men presenting with co-infections such as gonorrhea, chlamydia, and syphilis, reflecting the intersection between mpox and other STIs. Integrating mpox screening into broader sexual health services, where routine testing for STIs already occurs, seems to be an effective strategy to enhance case identification among high-risk groups. This approach could also be useful in cuttingsome costs associated with conducting mpox screening in stand-alone sites, and providing care and treatment for STIs in separate sites.
The high occurrence of mpox among individuals with concurrent STIs further underscores the need for refined approaches to screen these populations. These findings align with existing literature on infectious disease control, which identifies asymptomatic transmission as a key challenge in outbreak containment. A promising development highlighted in the review is the demonstrated feasibility of self-collected specimens for mpox screening, with no significant differences in cycle threshold (Ct) values between physician- and self-collected samples. This finding is critical for scaling up screening efforts, particularly in resource-limited or high-demand settings where healthcare worker capacity may be constrained. Self-collection not only reduces the burden on healthcare systems but also enhances accessibility and patient autonomy, particularly for stigmatized populations hesitant to seek in-person care. Koppe et al.’s [ ] retrospective study showed clinically inapparent mpox infections can be identified as part of screening of high-risk populations. This is important because asymptomatic individuals can still transmit the virus, and identifying these hidden cases is crucial to controlling the spread of mpox.
The reviewed studies highlight the evolving landscape of mpox diagnostics, emphasizing the critical role of PCR testing and the diversity of specimens employed to improve detection rates. The use of PCR testing as a primary diagnostic tool aligns with its proven accuracy in detecting Orthopoxviruses, as reported by other authors [ , ] and recommended by WHO [ ]. Swabs from skin lesions were reported in five of the seven articles highlighting the fact that this could be the specimen mostly used during outbreaks as reported by Tijtnti et al. [ ]. Notwithstanding, other specimen samples like mucosal swabs, including oropharyngeal, nasopharyngeal, and rectal swabs were identified in this review. This offered valuable alternatives, particularly in detecting infections in high-risk populations or in cases presenting with atypical symptoms. Notably, oropharyngeal, anogenital, blood and nasopharyngeal swabs demonstrated high positivity rates underscoring the potential of utilizing alternative specimen types alongside skin swabs for more comprehensive mpox diagnosis [ – ]. Additionally, GeneXpert (a portable rapid diagnostic device) has been shown to be effective in areas where laboratory are unavailable, with comparable sensitivities and specificities to PCR testing [ ], and may offer an opportunity for increased research from Africa especially in remote areas where traditional diagnostic infrastructure is lacking. It could greatly improve detection rates and reduce the time. Further studies would be helpful to confirm this result from different sources. More so, Kinganda-Lusamaki et al. [ ] retrospective analysis of samples from the DRC, shows that there is a potential role for antibody testing, in combination with PCR in certain circumstances. This combined approach may improve diagnostic accuracy and enhance outbreak management, especially in situations where PCR alone may not be sufficient. The review suggests the exploration of using multi-specimen, and combined diagnostic approaches to optimizing mpox detection, particularly in resource-constrained and high-prevalence settings.
The systematic review identified three treatment options for Mpox which are Tecovirimat, Vaccinia immune globulin and Cidiforvir, though Titanji et al. [ ] report five treatment options (including brincidofovir and trifluridine). Perhaps this discrepancy reflects differences in the scope of studies reviewed, the availability of therapies in specific regions, or the exclusion of case reports and virologic studies that did not have a direct application to outbreak response. Notwithstanding, a notable finding is the emphasis on Tecovirimat as a primary treatment option, especially amongst patients who are immunocompromised (PLHIV) reported in seven of the ten reviewed studies. Tecovirimat’s wide application aligns with its WHO-recommended use for individuals at risk of severe mpox, particularly immunocompromised patients, who were a large portion of those treated as per our review results [ ].
Vaccination remains a cornerstone in preventing the spread of diseases. In this review, two vaccines were identified (i) MVA-BN (also known as JYNNEOS and IMVANEX) and (ii) ACAM2000 as reported by other studies [ , , ]. Of these, MVA-BN and its variants emerged as the most frequently reported, highlighting its pivotal role in mpox outbreak management. The dominance of JYNNEOS in 41 publications highlights the growing reliance on safer, modern, and well-documented vaccines, particularly for high-risk populations [ ], especially given that ACAM2000 is associated with more known side effects and contraindications [ , ]. The use of JYNNEOS for both PrEP and PEP conforms to recommendations on its usage [ ]. However, inconsistencies in dosage reporting ranging from single doses to two doses, and some studies omitting whether administration was for PrEP or PEP highlight a probable lack of standardization in administration across countries and reporting practices. This gap raises concerns about the comparability of outcomes across studies and underscores the need for harmonized protocols in mpox vaccination strategies. Another critical observation is the targeted approach to vaccination, with 33 of the 47 studies focusing on GBMSM or LGBTQIA + populations. While this approach aligns with epidemiological evidence of higher mpox risk in these groups [ , ], it also highlights the need to address potential inequities in vaccine access for other at-risk populations, including women, children, immunocompromised persons, healthcare workers, household contacts, and other vulnerable community groups in endemic regions.
The seven models of care reported for vaccine delivery underscore the adaptability of health systems in responding to mpox outbreaks. Traditional health facilities, including clinics and hospitals, remain the predominant model of vaccine delivery, reported in 24 studies (Table ). However, the use of outreach services, sexual health clinics, mobile clinics, pharmacies, home visits, and even telemedicine illustrates the alternative and innovative strategies employed to increase vaccine coverage. These diverse modalities reflect attempts to overcome barriers such as stigma, accessibility, and resource limitations, though their effectiveness and scalability remain underexplored. Previous studies have reported the use of varied models of care in situations of emergencies [ , – ].
This review highlights significant strides in vaccination strategies during mpox outbreaks but also exposes critical gaps. These include inconsistencies in vaccine use reporting on dosage with some papers reporting one and two doses used for PrEP or PEP, or while other reported on vaccines use without mentioning dosage. Furthermore, there is limited data on the long-term effectiveness of different vaccination modalities, and inequities in vaccine accessibility. Addressing these gaps requires robust monitoring systems, targeted research on underrepresented groups, and evidence-based policy frameworks to optimize Mpox vaccination efforts globally.
The findings on pre-emergency preparedness strategies for mpox outbreaks reveal a concerning gap in reporting and documentation of a critical aspect of outbreak management. The emphasis on international collaboration [ ] and learning from previous outbreaks [ ] aligns with global calls for integrated and cooperative approaches to outbreak preparedness. However, the scant number of publications reporting on pre-emergency measures raises questions about the prioritization of proactive strategies in mpox research and response planning. This oversight is particularly concerning given the increasing recognition of mpox as a global health threat requiring comprehensive preparedness to mitigate its impact effectively. As made evident by the Covid-19 pandemic [ ], the lack of robust documentation and research on proactive measures undermines opportunities to develop standardized, evidence-based protocols that could enhance readiness and response capacities, particularly in resource-limited and high-risk settings. Addressing this deficiency may require an increased focus on pre-emergency preparedness in future mpox research and implementation. This could include prioritizing surveillance systems, resource pre-positioning, and the development of scalable models of care, such as the hub-and-spoke model described by O’Neil et al. [ ], which showed success in reducing testing-to-treatment timelines and improving treatment accessibility. Strengthened emphasis on pre-emergency preparedness will be instrumental in ensuring that health systems are better equipped to respond swiftly and efficiently to future outbreaks.

## Limitations
This systematic review focused primarily on publications available in English and French, which may have excluded relevant studies published in other languages. Our analysis also did not extend to non-peer-reviewed sources or government reports, which may contain valuable data on real-world containment efforts. Additionally, due to the heterogeneity in study designs, population characteristics, and outcome measures, a meta-analysis was not feasible. Lastly, our review does not fully capture the long-term outcomes of mpox containment strategies, as most studies focused on immediate or short-term effects rather than sustained impact over time. Despite these limitations, this review provides a foundation for future research on effective, context-specific containment strategies for mpox and similar infectious diseases.

## Gaps in literature/ challenge of research on mpox containment strategies
Despite the extensive focus on containment strategies in mpox outbreaks, this systematic review reveals several critical gaps in the literature that limit comprehensive understanding and policy guidance. First, although diagnostics and case management strategies are well-documented, there is a lack of economic evaluations assessing the cost-effectiveness of different containment strategies. Future research should include cost analyses that consider both the expenses for healthcare systems and out-of-pocket costs for patients, including transportation and care-related expenses, as these factors significantly impact access to timely diagnosis and treatment. More research is needed from settings with a high disease burden in Africa as this review identified most studies conducted in high income settings in Europe and North America, and utilising additional diagnostic techniques such as GeneXpert should be considered to facilitate this.
Additionally, while vaccination is a key containment strategy, limited data is available on vaccine coverage and access across different demographic groups. Few studies provide comprehensive data on population-level vaccination rates or address disparities in access by region, socioeconomic status, or other high-risk groups like children and women. Understanding coverage gaps would enable targeted vaccination strategies to reduce inequities, particularly in underrepresented and vulnerable populations. Moreover, the effectiveness of PEP and PrEP in different demographic contexts remains underexplored, with limited data on the optimal timing and dosage regimens for maximizing immunity, especially in immunocompromised individuals.
Another notable gap is the scarcity of experimental designs, such as RCTs or longitudinal studies, which could provide higher-quality evidence on containment strategies. Most included studies are observational, limiting causal inferences and generalizability. RCTs and longitudinal research focusing on the impact of specific interventions such as rapid diagnostics or combined treatment approaches would strengthen the evidence base and guide best practices for future outbreaks. Conducting randomized controlled trials during emergencies is often not feasible due to urgent public health needs, logistical constraints, and ethical concerns regarding withholding potentially life-saving interventions. Recommendation on conducting such studies have been documented in literature [ , ].
Further, this review did not include synthesis of emerging surveillance tools, such as wastewater monitoring. Future reviews should address this to inform comprehensive outbreak preparedness strategies.
Lastly, few studies address long-term health outcomes and effectiveness of containment strategies over time. Monitoring outcomes beyond the acute phase of mpox outbreaks, such as post-infection complications, vaccine durability, and sustained immunity, would provide valuable insights for developing long-term management guidelines. Future research should aim to document these outcomes comprehensively, using both quantitative and qualitative methods to capture the full impact of containment efforts across diverse populations and outbreak contexts.

## Conclusion
This review highlighted strategies used to respond to and contain mpox outbreaks, from which several policy recommendations emerge. Vaccination efforts should be expanded beyond GBMSM/LGBTQIA + populations to include women, children, healthcare workers, immunocompromised individuals, and household contacts in endemic and outbreak-prone areas, while exploring alternative delivery modalities such as mobile clinics, outreach services, telemedicine, pharmacies, and home visits to improve coverage in resource-limited or high-stigma settings. Mpox screening should be integrated into routine sexual health services for efficient case identification among high-risk populations, with self-collected specimens promoted where feasible to reduce healthcare burdens and increase patient autonomy. Multi-specimen and combined diagnostic approaches, including PCR, antibody testing, and GeneXpert, should be considered to enhance detection, particularly in resource-limited or high-prevalence settings. Strengthening regional surveillance systems, including community-based reporting, will inform more effective outbreak preparedness and response tailored to African contexts. Pre-emergency preparedness should be prioritized through resource pre-positioning, training, and scalable care models such as hub-and-spoke approaches to reduce response times and improve access to treatment. International collaboration and knowledge sharing are essential to standardize best practices and improve global outbreak readiness. Finally, context-specific research in low- and middle-income countries, particularly in Africa, is needed to generate evidence on vaccine effectiveness, screening, and care delivery models to inform policy and practice.
