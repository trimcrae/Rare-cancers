The Heart Rhythm Society (HRS) is the leading professional society of electrophysiology (EP) globally. With a mission to end death and suffering due to heart rhythm disorders, HRS has for decades been committed to education, innovation, and advocacy in EP. Education and training of the next generations of EPs have been longstanding top priorities for HRS delivered in different formats and venues. A critically important venue for education and training is the HRS annual meeting. The Heart Rhythm 2025 meeting that will take place from April 24 through and 27 in San Diego, CA builds on many years of experience in education and will undoubtedly surpass people’s expectations in the richness of content, wide variety of topics, innovative and transformative science, and numerous opportunities to connect with others. Although the meeting is intended for everyone involved in heart rhythm care, including adult and pediatric EPs, allied professionals, basic and clinical researchers, sponsors, and regulators, it offers tremendous educational and networking opportunities to fellows in training and early-career EPs.
Fellows in training and early-career EPs will benefit greatly from attending any of the 200 general sessions at Heart Rhythm 2025 that span all areas of EP, including cardiac resynchronization therapy via biventricular pacing or conduction system pacing, leadless devices, transvenous and nontransvenous implantable cardioverter-defibrillators, mapping and ablation technologies, basics of diagnosing and treating arrhythmias, basic science, digital heath, and health policy. Other important sessions for fellows in training and early-career EPs are the late-breaking clinical trial sessions that will showcase innovative, impactful, and cutting-edge science. New this year is a “Meet the Trialists” session in which attendees will have the opportunity to directly interact with some of the principal investigators of late-breaking clinical trials in a collaborative setting; this session will undoubtedly include exciting and informative conversations. Several hands-on sessions will help fellows and early-career EPs grow their procedural skills and enhance their outcomes, including 3 sessions on ablation biophysics, 2 sessions on cardiac mapping, and a session on cardiac anatomy. Another exciting session titled “Speed Mentoring” will allow fellows and early-career EPs to connect with leaders in the field to learn about strategies of success and growth, get career advice, explore possibilities of collaboration, or just have a relaxed conversation about any career-related topic. A roundtable session for fellows in training will give them opportunities to connect with one another as well as with other experts in the field. Other educational offerings relevant to fellows and early-career EPs are EP board review sessions, sessions on the business of EP, and the Foundation for the New HRS Research Network session. An important session that fellows and early-career EPs should not miss is a summit on the global impact of sudden cardiac death (SCD), which will tackle the prevention of SCD in different regions of the world. Given the major global magnitude of SCD and regional differences in SCD prediction and prevention, this session is intended for developing and informing strategies that can help the EP community lessen the burden of SCD globally. Another session that fellows and early-career EPs will find informative and inspiring is titled “Never Ending Conversation: A History of HRS and the Field of Electrophysiology,” from which attendees will learn about the history and evolution of HRS and the field of EP and understand how this knowledge has shaped the future. Three journal sessions titled “Best of Heart Rhythm: Basic Science,” “Best of Heart Rhythm: Clinical Research,” and “Best of Heart Rhythm Case Reports” will cover high-impact articles published in Heart Rhythm and Heart Rhythm Case Reports in the past year highlighting their potential implications for clinical practice. For those who are not able to participate in the meeting in person, platforms have been built that will enable virtual participation from all over the world.
The Heart Rhythm 2025 meeting will undoubtedly offer fellows and early-career EPs numerous opportunities to learn, advance, and grow, as well as to connect and share with others their passion for innovation, vision of transforming patient care, and commitment to advancing the EP field globally. It is a meeting that no fellow or early-career EP should miss.
