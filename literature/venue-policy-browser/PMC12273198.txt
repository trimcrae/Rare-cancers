## Abstract

## Introduction
Cheating in exams is a common problem in many educational settings. This issue is critical in the medical field because it can have a long-term impact on the health of society. Therefore, this study aims to explore the reasons why undergraduate nursing students cheat in exams.

## Methods
This qualitative content analysis study was conducted in 2024, involving 15 participants, comprising six undergraduate nursing students, five faculty members, a dean of the nursing college, a vice-chancellor of educational affairs, an expert in educational affairs, and an in-charge for exam administration, who were purposefully selected. Individual, in-depth, semi-structured interviews were conducted to collect information. Then, the data was analyzed using Elo and Kyngas’ inductive content analysis approach with the help of MAXQDA 2020 software. Also, Elo et al.‘s checklist was used to check the trustworthiness of the data.

## Results
The number of extracted final codes was 96, which were divided into two main themes and four categories, including personal (intrapersonal and interpersonal) and organizational factors (weakness in educational policies and implementation problems).

## Conclusion
These findings can significantly influence the development of future educational policies and practices. Therefore, educational policymakers should pay special attention to this issue and take adequate measures to address the root causes of exam cheating.

## Introduction
Excessive attention to educational qualifications rather than people’s skills and expertise has caused numerous problems in various societies. Some degree seekers want to achieve their goals in the shortest possible way, one of which is cheating [ ]. It is noteworthy that this issue is not limited to one country; instead, it can be said that cheating is a type of violation of norms and values that is as old as the history of exams, meaning that cheating has been around since exams were first created [ ]. Cheating is defined in different ways. Some have defined it as Attempting to use fake educational qualifications or pretending to be smart through trickery. In contrast, another group has defined it as scientific deviance, in which a student provides information, answers, or help to another student or receives help from them [ ]. Academic cheating has a broad scope, and therefore, several classifications have been proposed for it. In the most common classification, Rettinger and Kramer have mentioned two types of cheating in exams and cheating in homework [ ].
Cheating in exams among students is a serious concern for examiners and faculty members, who must decide whether to pass or fail students based on their scores [ ]. This type of cheating can cause a student’s grade to differ from their actual academic performance, thereby raising concerns about equity and negatively impacting both students and faculty members [ ]. This phenomenon is spreading rapidly, particularly among medical students [ ]. For example, in one study conducted in Iran, 96% of students reported having cheated at least once in previous exams [ ]. Cheating on exams is also very common among nursing students. For example, in Saudi Arabia, 90% [ ], in Greece [ ], and in Kenya [ ], 70%, and in Iran, 66% of nursing students have admitted to cheating on exams [ ]. Cheating among nursing students is more important than other academic fields. If these students become accustomed to engaging in unethical behavior within the educational environment, their future careers are more likely to be tainted by unethical issues [ ]. In addition, if these students are allowed to pass their exams by cheating, their literacy and knowledge will ultimately not reach the desired level, and this can also cause irreparable harm to patients [ ]. Therefore, when cheating occurs in nursing colleges, it can have serious consequences for individuals’ lives, societal values, and even the economy [ ].
Despite studies conducted on different aspects of cheating in various countries, researchers express dissatisfaction with the low statistics of these studies among medical science students [ , ]. Because they believe that if effective action is not taken to control or reduce cheating, it can disrupt the foundation of educational systems [ ]. The sensitivity of this issue is particularly high in specific fields, such as nursing, which has a direct impact on the health of people in society. However, in nursing, limited studies with a qualitative approach have been conducted in this field. In this study, an attempt will be made to explain the causes of cheating in exams among undergraduate nursing students. The hope is that by identifying the underlying factors, valuable steps will be taken to minimize this reprehensible phenomenon.

## Materials and methods

## Study design and sampling
This research is an inductive, qualitative content analysis conducted in 2024. This research method is used to discover people’s understanding of everyday life phenomena and interpret mental data [ ]. The complexity of meaning, the holistic view, and the dependence of content on context are among the reasons that content analysis can be used as a qualitative research method. By doing this, researchers want to understand the behavior, experiences, and concepts used in natural environments [ ]. In this method, content collection and analysis are conducted simultaneously to organize and extract meaning from the collected information, thereby deriving meaningful results [ ].
The participants in this study included faculty members, nursing students from semesters one to six, deans and vice-chancellors of educational affairs, experts in educational affairs, and exam administrators from two nursing schools affiliated with Zanjan University of Medical Sciences, Iran, who were selected purposively (i.e., the selection of participants was rich in information). For example, in selecting students, both top performers and those with a history of academic cheating were included.

## Data collection
To collect information, in-depth semi-structured interviews were conducted, with questions developed by the research team. First, open-ended questions were asked, followed by questions related to the research subject. In three cases, the researcher contacted the participant for a second time due to ambiguity in understanding the participant’s statements and deficiencies in the obtained information and also to further explore the issue. It was up to the participants to determine the time and place of the interview, and most interviews were conducted at their workplaces and colleges.
Before starting each interview, an explanation was provided regarding the research objectives, the reason for recording the interview, voluntary participation, confidentiality of information, and the identity of the interviewees.
Then, permission to record the audio was obtained from them. At the beginning of each interview, participants were asked to introduce themselves briefly, and then questions were asked about their demographic characteristics, including age, level of education, and work experience. Then, some questions related to the research topic were asked, and the participant’s views on the reasons for cheating in exams among nursing students were explored under the guidance of the interviewer. The discussion proceeded with timekeeping until the interviewer made sure that he had received the presented material well. The interview form consisted of two categories: basic and follow-up questions.

Each interview started with a question:

During your college time or work, have you encountered students who cheat on exams?” What are your experiences in this regard?

The main questions included:

In your opinion, what conditions facilitate the possibility of cheating in the exams among undergraduate nursing students?”
Which groups and peoples are involved in increasing the rate of cheating in the exams among nursing students?”
What is the impact of these people or groups?

The researcher tried to accurately reveal the answers of the participants to each of the mentioned questions by using follow-up questions and sentences, such as “Explain more about this:” or “What do you mean by this part of your speech?“.» The interview times varied between 30 and 60 min. All interviews were conducted by the corresponding author over three months. With the participants’ permission, the interviews were recorded using a mobile phone. Immediately after the interview was completed, it was typed word by word as soon as possible to provide the necessary feedback for the subsequent interviews. Then, data analysis was conducted using the MAXQDA 2020 software. After analyzing 14 interviews, the researchers reached saturation, indicating that no new subcategories or categories emerged. However, one more interview was conducted to ensure saturation and confirm the findings.

## Data analysis
The three-stage model proposed by Elo and Kingas was used to analyze the data [ ]. In the preparation stage, the written interviews were read several times to gain a general sense of the data. Then, both the obvious content and the hidden content were analyzed. In the organization stage, primary ideas were first extracted from the textual data using open coding and listed in a table format. Additionally, after reading each text word by word and line by line several times and pausing sufficiently during the interviews, all codes related to the research purpose were noted in the margins of the text. Then, the extracted codes were compared in terms of differences and similarities, and after determining the label, they were categorized into subcategories. Then, the information was summarized in order to form categories. During the reporting phase, the main themes were identified based on the codes and content within the categories and subcategories. It should be noted that all the steps mentioned during the research were carried out several times, back and forth.

## Trustworthiness
Additionally, to ensure the trustworthiness of the data, the checklist by Elo et al. was used. This checklist includes three parts: preparation, organization, and reporting, and has 29 questions. The preparation part includes questions about data collection methods, sampling strategies, and selection of the analysis unit [ ]. The current research, it was tried to increase the trustworthiness of the findings with the help of these strategies: using the purposeful sampling method and collecting information from different groups of participants in order to obtain the most suitable information, establishing proper communication with the participants in order to form suitable space for free and in-depth interviews, use of the member checking method by participants to confirm the accuracy of data, use of semi-structured questions format for better coherence of the interview process and having the researcher’s educational background regarding the research topic. The second part of the checklist is the organization stage, which includes seven questions regarding the classification and abstraction of findings, interpretation, and representativeness [ ].
In this section, these strategies were used to improve the rigor of the findings: using the member check method by the participants to confirm the correctness of the extracted data and codes or correcting them and reviewing the data by the authors and two expert faculty members who did not participate in the research to match and ensure the agreement of the classes with the statements of the participants. The third and final part of the checklist is called the report stage, which includes questions about reporting the results and the analysis process of the report [ ]. An attempt was made to increase the rigor of the study in this section by using these approaches: holding joint meetings of the research team in order to maximize cooperation and consensus in order to present the findings systematically and resolve conflicting interpretations, efforts to report data clearly and understandable and provide a complete description of the process of analyzing the findings in the project report.

## Results
In this research, 15 participants, including six undergraduate nursing students, five faculty members, a dean of the nursing college, a vice-chancellor of educational affairs, an expert in educational affairs of the college, and an in-charge of exam administration, with an age range of 20 to 61 years, were present. The participants were working and studying in two nursing colleges affiliated with Zanjan University of Medical Sciences.  After coding the interviews, 189 initial codes were created, which were reduced to 96 after several revisions and the incorporation of experts’ opinions, and then further refined through reduction, deletion, and integration at various stages. Finally, these codes were placed in 14 subcategories, four categories, and two themes (Table  ).
 The participants in this study believed that academic cheating among nursing students is a complex and important issue that warrants further discussion and investigation. According to them, the reasons for academic cheating in nursing students included two groups of personal and organizational factors.
Personal factors: This theme included two categories: intrapersonal and interpersonal factors. Regarding intrapersonal factors, the participants believed that students’ tendency to cheat is affected by inherent factors such as knowledge, attitude, skill, personality, experience, and motivation.

“Something very important in cheating is the special personality of some students. For example, some students do not have proper and purposeful planning during the semester and collect all the lessons by the end of the semester. Or, some students are looking for attention in class and want to be the center of attention in the classroom by any means.” (P 15).

Additionally, according to the participants, an important factor contributing to the tendency of nursing students to cheat in exams is their surrounding environment, which is categorized as interpersonal factors and comprises three subclasses: family, peers, and society.

“In my opinion, an important issue in the issue of student cheating is the family issue. Unfortunately, some families’ expectations of students are too high and unreasonable. This issue causes students to bear much pressure and have to behave like this. Unfortunately, the conditions of the home environment are sometimes not good. Therefore, there are no calm conditions where the student can concentrate and study.” (P 13).

Organizational factors: From the participants’ perspective in the research, an important part of the reasons for nursing students’ tendency to cheat in exams was related to organizational factors, which included two categories: weaknesses in educational policies and implementation challenges. Based on this, some participants believed that problems in macro-education policies, such as curriculum weaknesses and insufficient deterrence of anti-cheating laws, have aggravated the occurrence of this phenomenon. On the contrary, some other interviewees stated that in many cases, cheating is related to flawed policies at the micro level in colleges, such as scheduling classes and exams at inappropriate times during the semester.

“A large part of the problem with cheating is due to poor planning by the college. That the exams are planned and held in a tight time frame. Well, this makes the student not have enough time to study each lesson. Or sometimes, a little time is given for difficult exams, and on the contrary, more time is given for easier exams. Well, this planning disturbs the students.” (P 5).

In addition to the weaknesses related to policy making, the participants also believed that a significant part of the reasons for cheating among nursing students is related to implementation problems, which were categorized into three areas: faculty members, exam organizers, and physical environment. According to the participants, the existence of some weaknesses in faculty members, such as difficulties in establishing proper communication with students or weaknesses in academic abilities, leads them to cheat. Additionally, one should pay attention to the individuals responsible for administering exams and the issues related to the physical environment of the exam venue.

“In my opinion, those responsible for conducting exams are very effective in this field. Some of these people do not take cheaters seriously or do not do their job properly. I have seen many times that they do not sit in their seats during the exam or that they somehow leave the hall during the exam.” (P 9).

## Discussion
This study was conducted to explain the reasons for cheating in exams among undergraduate nursing students. Based on the study’s findings, these factors were classified into two themes: personal and organizational. The personal factors were divided into two categories: intrapersonal and interpersonal. In this regard, Orok et al. stated that the positive and negative behaviors of each person reflect their inner characteristics, and especially when analyzing the causes of specific wrong actions, such as cheating, it is necessary to explore people’s inner intentions [ ]. According to the findings of this study, one of the most significant intrapersonal factors contributing to cheating among students was their specific personality type.
In Wang et al.‘s study, students with extroverted personality types cheated more [ ]. Since trying to attract attention and being stressed are two key characteristics of extraversion [ ], this finding aligns with the results of the present study. Knowledge, attitude, and skill were three other factors that influenced students’ cheating on exams. In line with this finding in the study of Başalaniz et al. [ ] and Kiekkas et al. [ ] lack of knowledge, the research of Loripoor et al. positive attitude [ ] and the study of Abdaoui et al. weakness in time management was mentioned as one of the main factors of cheating [ ]. It can be said that, based on the theoretical background, weaknesses in both theoretical and practical capabilities lead people to dishonesty and trickery [ ]. Additionally, some individuals may not have a positive attitude toward themselves or their profession despite possessing an acceptable level of competence. Therefore, for this reason, instead of devoting time and enduring the hardships of studying, they turn to deceitful behaviors such as cheating [ ].
According to the findings, the other effective category for cheating in nursing students’ exams was interpersonal factors, which included three subcategories: peers, family, and society. In line with this finding, Jahan et al. stated in their study that some parents seek to fulfill their educational aspirations through their children. This issue has generated high expectations among students, placing significant pressure and stress on them. Therefore, students turn to cheating behaviors [ ]. Moreover, Shahbazian Khonig et al. noted that the significant impact of academic performance on the prospects of people in Iran has led to double pressure from parents on students, which, in addition to psychological damage, is one of the primary factors contributing to their tendency to cheat [ ]. Also, the results of another systematic review study revealed that students who, under the influence of the class atmosphere and peer pressure, seek to showcase their abilities to others or are afraid of receiving a poor grade and being humiliated as a result are more likely to cheat [ ].
Regarding society’s impact on the phenomenon of cheating, Hendy et al. also relied on Akers’ social learning theory. According to this theory, the interaction between the three important elements of observation, imitation, and modeling, along with positive reinforcement, shapes people’s behaviors. Therefore, in a society where abnormal behaviors such as cheating are considered a value, people tend to learn undesirable behaviors [ ].
Based on other findings, the second theme, obtained from the participants’ opinions about the reasons for cheating in exams among nursing students, was related to organizational factors, which were classified into two groups: weaknesses in educational policies and implementation problems. In the department of macro educational policies, the main challenge was related to the nursing curriculum. According to van Kraaij et al. undergraduate nursing curricula worldwide have weaknesses in structure and content, one of the reasons for which is the tendency to copy curricula from other fields, such as medicine and psychology, without considering the unique nature of the nursing field [ ]. In line with this perspective, Abadian et al. noted that one of the weaknesses of the undergraduate nursing curriculum in Iran is the incompatibility between the titles and the number of units of the main courses. For example, the course for adults and the elderly in the third semester includes five titles, while only three-course units form the 20 units of the student’s academic semester. Undoubtedly, this issue puts considerable pressure on students during exams and creates a basis for cheating [ ].
Furthermore, Sadeghi et al. identified overemphasis on high academic qualifications and the lack of strong deterrent laws as two main challenges in the field of educational macro-policy making that lead learners to cheat on exams [ ]. Some of the problems raised in the field of policymaking were also at the micro level. For example, from the participant’s point of view, one of these problems was the intensive planning of the end-of-semester exams. In the study of Sadegi Far et al. [ ] and Akbari Khudlan et al. this issue was raised as one of the reasons for the increase in cheating in exams [ ]. Implementation problems were another factor affecting cheating in nursing students’ exams. Here, the participants highlighted the roles of faculty members, exam organizers, and the physical environment of the exam. Based on this, the faculty member’s personality and lack of scientific ability, as well as his incorrect teaching and evaluation, were identified as the most significant factors contributing to students’ tendency to cheat, which aligns with the findings of some studies conducted in this field [ , , ]. Regarding the holding factors and physical environment, Aliverdinia et al. emphasized in their study that, in addition to the strict control of the test site by CCTV cameras, test proctors should be more responsible for the strict implementation of test rules [ ].
Although this study employed conventional content analysis, the findings are consistent with Bandura’s Social Learning Theory, particularly in explaining how interpersonal and social factors, such as peer influence and classroom norms, influence cheating behaviors [ ]. Furthermore, the individual attitudes and perceptions identified in this study can also be interpreted through theories such as the Theory of Planned Behavior, which emphasizes the roles of attitudes, subjective norms, and perceived control over behavior [ ]. Acknowledging these connections with behavioral theories provides a broader perspective for interpreting the phenomenon of exam cheating among nursing students.

## Strengths and limitations
Conducting in-depth interviews, leading the interviews by the responsible author, and involving all members of the research group in the data analysis process were among the most notable strengths of this study. Another strength of the study was that four of the participating students had a recent history of cheating on exams, which added richness to the work. The most important limitation of the study was the lack of detailed discussion of the extracted themes, given the complexity and widespread nature of the cheating phenomenon. Therefore, by employing other qualitative approaches, such as phenomenology and grounded theory, the lived experiences of cheating students can be explored more deeply, and the formation process of this phenomenon can be analyzed and explained.

## Conclusion
In this study, the reasons for exam cheating among undergraduate nursing students were explored and divided into two main groups: personal factors and organizational factors. Ignoring the problem of cheating in nursing education can seriously harm the competency and quality of future nurses. If this problem continues, it could harm public health and create serious problems for the healthcare system. To solve this issue, policymakers and educational managers need to focus on it. Besides making better policies at all levels, they should also take practical actions to help prevent cheating. For example, organizing integrity workshops, using more formative assessment methods, and revising the curriculum to include ethical decision-making skills can help reduce cheating. Faculty and exam administrators should also receive training, and the test environment and questions should be standardized to prevent further instances of dishonest behavior. Taking these actions can help promote a culture of honesty and improve the quality of nursing education.
