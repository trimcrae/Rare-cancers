## Abstract

## Background
Enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS) protocols have revolutionized perioperative care in lung cancer resection, significantly improving postoperative outcomes. However, a comprehensive analysis of the global research trends, intellectual structure, and evolving hotspots in this specific domain over the past 15 years remains lacking. This study aims to bridge this gap by conducting a comprehensive bibliometric and visual analysis of literature from 2010 to 2025.

## Methods
Literature regarding ERAS in lung cancer surgery published from 2010 to 2025 was retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC). Bibliometric analyses were conducted using VOSviewer, CiteSpace, and R-bibliometrix to visualize spatial distributions, collaboration networks, co-citation structures, and keyword trends.

## Results
A total of 472 eligible publications were identified, exhibiting an exponential growth trajectory particularly after 2018. China emerged as the leading contributor with 137 documents, followed by the United States with 107 publications, while European nations demonstrated exceptional citation impact. The University of Copenhagen and its affiliated hospitals formed the dominant institutional cluster, and the Journal of Thoracic Disease served as the primary publishing venue. Analysis of citation bursts and keyword evolution revealed a paradigm shift in research focus. The field evolved from initial feasibility studies involving lung resection to protocol standardization and safety. Most recently, the emphasis has shifted towards patient-centered outcomes represented by quality-of-life (QoL) assessments and precision minimally invasive techniques.

## Conclusions
Research on ERAS for lung cancer is rapidly expanding and has matured from establishing safety to refining comprehensive care. The current trend emphasizes the integration of precision surgery with patient-centered QoL assessments. This study provides a global roadmap for researchers to identify key collaborators and emerging frontiers in thoracic oncology.

Keywords: Enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS), lung cancer, bibliometric analysis, research trends, quality of life (QoL)

The intellectual focus has evolved from early feasibility/safety studies to protocol standardization (e.g., 2019 guidelines) and now emphasizes patient-centered outcomes [quality of life (QoL)] and precision minimally invasive techniques.

## What is known and what is new?
• ERAS protocols are proven to enhance recovery after lung cancer surgery, reducing complications and hospital stay. There are standardized guidelines in the field.
• This study provides the first bibliometric analysis (2010–2025), mapping global trends and quantifying a paradigm shift from protocol standardization to a focus on patient-centered outcomes and precision surgery.

## What is the implication, and what should change now?
• The implication is that ERAS has matured into a standard requiring integration of surgical precision with holistic recovery metrics. Future research and clinical practice should prioritize international collaboration to bridge volume-impact gaps, tailor protocols to individual patient needs (precision ERAS), and consistently embed patient-reported outcomes and QoL assessments into care pathways.

## Introduction
Lung cancer remains the leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, imposing a substantial burden on global public health systems ( , ). Surgical resection, including lobectomy and segmentectomy via video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS), is currently established as the standard curative treatment for early-stage non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) ( , ). However, despite advancements in minimally invasive techniques, thoracic surgery is inherently associated with significant physiological stress, postoperative pain, and pulmonary complications, which can prolong hospitalization and delay functional recovery ( , ). Consequently, optimizing perioperative management to improve patient outcomes has become a paramount priority for thoracic surgeons globally ( ).
To address these challenges, the concept of ERAS, originally pioneered by Professor Henrik Kehlet in the 1990s, has been widely adopted across surgical disciplines ( , ). ERAS is a multimodal, multidisciplinary approach designed to attenuate the surgical stress response and accelerate rehabilitation through a series of evidence-based protocols, such as preoperative counseling, opioid-sparing analgesia, and early mobilization ( ). In the domain of thoracic surgery, the application of ERAS has witnessed exponential growth over the past decade ( ). A pivotal milestone was the publication of the 2019 guidelines by the ERAS Society and the European Society of Thoracic Surgeons (ESTS), which standardize care pathways for lung resection ( ). Subsequent high-level evidence, including systematic reviews and meta-analyses, has confirmed that ERAS protocols effectively reduce the length of hospital stay and hospitalization costs without compromising safety or increasing readmission rates ( - ).
As the field matures, the research focus has gradually shifted from feasibility and safety to more complex dimensions, such as patient-reported outcomes (PROs), quality of life (QoL), and the integration of ERAS with uniportal or robotic surgery ( , ). Furthermore, the rapid proliferation of academic publications in this domain has created an information overload, making it increasingly challenging for researchers to discern the evolving knowledge structure, key collaborative networks, and emerging frontiers ( ). Traditional systematic reviews often focus on specific clinical questions and may lack a macroscopic, quantitative view of the field’s developmental trajectory ( ).
Bibliometric analysis offers a solution by utilizing mathematical and statistical methods to visualize the structural and dynamic characteristics of a scientific domain ( ). While bibliometric studies exist in other medical fields ( , ), there is a paucity of updated analyses specifically targeting the global trends of ERAS in lung cancer resection over the last 15 years, particularly covering the post-pandemic era and the recent paradigm shift towards patient-centered care.
Therefore, this study aims to bridge this gap by conducting a comprehensive bibliometric and visual analysis of literature from 2010 to 2025 using VOSviewer, CiteSpace, and R-bibliometrix. By mapping the geographical distribution, collaboration networks, intellectual structure, and keyword evolution, this study intends to identify current research hotspots and predict future frontiers, providing a valuable reference for thoracic surgeons, anesthesiologists, and policymakers to guide future investigations. We present this article in accordance with the BIBLIO reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-1-0124/rc).

## Methods

## Data source and search strategy
The literature search encompassed publications from January 1, 2010, to December 31, 2025, using the following search query: TS = ((“Enhanced recovery after surgery” OR “ERAS” OR “Fast-track surgery”) AND (“Lung cancer” OR “Pulmonary resection*” OR “Lobectom*” OR “Segmentectom*” OR “Pneumonectom*”)). Two independent reviewers conducted the search, with discrepancies resolved through consensus discussion or, when necessary, third-party consultation. The complete dataset was exported in plain text format on December 31, 2025. Although the year 2025 has just concluded and its indexing may be partially incomplete, data up to December 31, 2025, were included to capture the most recent early-access and online-first publications, ensuring the identification of the latest emerging trends. The overall workflow of data collection and analysis is illustrated in .
 

## Data extraction and inclusion criteria
Two independent researchers performed the literature search and screened the titles and abstracts to ensure relevance to ERAS in lung cancer. After filtering, the full records and cited references were exported in “Plain Text” format with the filename prefix “download_”. To ensure the exceptional citation impact and relevance of the retrieved data, rigorous inclusion and exclusion criteria were applied. Specifically, the document types were strictly restricted to ‘Articles’ and ‘Review Articles’. Meeting abstracts, editorials, letters, early access notes, and book chapters were comprehensively excluded. Additionally, the language of the publications was restricted to English. Because data were extracted solely from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC), internal duplicate records were primarily managed by the database’s default system and subsequently verified and removed using the built-in deduplication algorithms of Cite Space and R-bibliometrix prior to the visual analysis.

## Bibliometric visualization
Bibliometric analysis was conducted using VOSviewer (v1.6.20), CiteSpace (v6.3.R1), and R-bibliometrix (v4.1.2). VOSviewer was utilized to visualize collaboration and co-citation networks across countries, institutions, authors, and keywords. CiteSpace was employed to identify research hotspots and temporal trends through citation burst detection and cluster analysis. R-bibliometrix was applied to calculate quantitative metrics (e.g., H-index, total citations) and generate the three-field plot, while the Journal Citation Reports (JCR) 2024 were consulted to retrieve impact factors and quartile rankings for journal assessment.

## Statistical analysis
Quantitative metrics, including the number of documents, total citations, and H-index, were calculated to assess academic impact. For network construction in VOSviewer, a fractional counting method was applied. In CiteSpace, the g-index (k=25) was used for node selection, and the Pathfinder pruning method was employed to simplify the network. The reliability of the clustering analysis was statistically validated by modularity (Q) and mean silhouette (S) scores. Based on the software algorithms, a Q value >0.3 and an S value >0.7 were considered to indicate significant community structure and high clustering credibility, respectively.

## Results

## Annual publication trend
The literature search yielded a total of 472 publications related to ERAS in lung cancer resection from 2010 to 2025. The annual research output exhibits a significant upward trajectory ( ). In the early stage, the field was in a nascent phase with relatively low annual productivity, generally fewer than 20 papers per year. However, a sharp escalation has been observed since 2018, with both publication volume and citation frequency exhibiting an exponential growth pattern that peaked around 2023–2024. This trend aligns with the global acceptance of ERAS protocols in thoracic surgery and indicates a rapidly growing academic interest shifting towards standardized perioperative management. The correspondence between the rise in publications and citations suggests that the quality and influence of research in this domain have grown synchronously with the output volume.
 

## Global geographical distribution and collaboration network
The global research landscape was analyzed through a combination of scientific production and international collaboration. details the scientific output across highly active nations. China emerged as the most productive country with 137 documents, followed closely by the United States with 107 publications. As shown in the geographical distribution map ( ), while China leads in volume (n=137), European and North American nations demonstrate exceptional citation impact. For instance, the United Kingdom achieved an average of 56.77 citations per paper, while Denmark and Switzerland reached 50.59 and 71.50 respectively, suggesting the exceptional citation impact of their contributions. As illustrated in , the international collaboration network reveals distinct clusters of cooperation. The United States and the United Kingdom occupy central positions with extensive links and act as bridges between European and Asian research hubs. Notably, while China leads in volume, the collaboration links suggest that its research is increasingly integrating with the global network. The temporal overlay indicates that countries like China and Spain have seen a more recent surge in research activity compared to the USA and UK, which were earlier adopters.

The emergence of Asian institutions in the network periphery indicates their recent entry and rapidly growing contribution to the global collaborative network.

## Keyword co-occurrence and trend topics
The intellectual structure and temporal evolution of research topics were visualized through keyword clustering and citation burst analysis. As shown in , the keyword co-occurrence network exhibits a high modularity (Q =0.7579) and a high weighted mean silhouette (S =0.8873), indicating a clearly defined and credible thematic structure. The network is organized into 13 distinct clusters that delineate the multidisciplinary nature of ERAS in lung cancer. The largest clusters, such as “#0 Minimally Invasive Surgery”, “#5 Video-assisted Thoracoscopic Surgery (VATS)”, and “#7 Non-small Cell Lung Cancer”, constitute the surgical foundation of the field. Intertwined with these are clusters representing perioperative management, specifically “#2 ERAS Protocols”, “#9 Multimodal Analgesia”, and “#4 Perioperative Period”. This topological arrangement confirms that the current research landscape is built upon a dual axis: the refinement of surgical techniques and the optimization of anesthetic and nursing protocols.
 Complementing this static structure, the analysis of keywords with the strongest citation bursts ( ) reveals the dynamic shifts in research hotspots over the past 15 years. In the earlier phase, high-strength keywords such as “lung resection” (2013–2018) dominated, reflecting the initial period of establishing ERAS feasibility in major pulmonary procedures. Subsequently, the focus transitioned towards standardization and safety, evidenced by the bursts of “complications” (2020–2021) and “guidelines” (2021–2023). This period marks the consolidation of evidence-based practices into formal consensus statements.
The post-2022 period marks a shift toward patient-centered outcomes and technical precision. The rise of “quality of life” (2022–2023) shows the academic focus has moved beyond traditional metrics like hospital stay to include overall patient well-being. At the same time, growing attention to “thoracoscopic surgery” (2023–2025) and “outcomes” (2022–2025) reflects ongoing efforts to improve minimally invasive techniques, reduce trauma, and speed recovery. Together, these trends show a field that has moved from feasibility studies to standardized care and now aims for precision surgery combined with QoL assessment.

## Discussion

## Global research dynamics and geographical disparities
The exponential growth of publications regarding ERAS in lung cancer resection, particularly the sharp escalation observed after 2018, mirrors the broader paradigm shift in surgical oncology towards value-based care. Our analysis reveals that while the concept of “fast-track surgery” was introduced decades ago, its specific application to thoracic oncology has only recently matured into a distinct research hotspot. This trajectory closely aligns with the release of seminal consensus guidelines, suggesting that high-level evidence acts as a primary catalyst for academic productivity. A striking feature of the global landscape is the dichotomy between publication volume and citation influence. China has emerged as the volume leader, a trend consistent with bibliometric analyses in other medical fields, driven by substantial state funding and a vast surgical case volume ( , ). However, the “citation gap” between China and Western nations, specifically Denmark, the UK, and the USA, remains significant.
The dominance of European institutions, particularly the University of Copenhagen cluster, underscores the historical roots of ERAS. Professor Henrik Kehlet’s foundational work in Denmark laid a physiological basis for multimodal recovery, creating a legacy that sustains high-impact research ( ). The high average citation rates of nations like Switzerland and the UK suggest that these regions produce the “canonical” literature, including guidelines and multi-institutional trials, which forms the intellectual backbone of the field. Conversely, the rapid rise of Asian institutions indicates a shift in the center of gravity for clinical implementation and validation, particularly in integrating ERAS with high-volume minimally invasive surgery ( ). For the field to advance, future collaboration networks must bridge the gap between European theoretical leadership and Asian clinical volume.

## The intellectual evolution: from feasibility to standardization
The visualization of co-citation networks and citation bursts elucidates a clear three-stage evolution in the intellectual structure of this domain. The first stage (2010–2015) was characterized by “Feasibility and Safety”. This defensive posture is quantitatively supported by the earliest strong citation bursts of keywords like ‘lung resection’ (2013–2018) in , alongside the initial foundational works by Madani et al. and Fiore et al. ( ). Early high-impact works, such as those by Madani et al. and Fiore et al., were primarily concerned with proving that accelerated pathways did not increase readmission rates or morbidity ( , ). This defensive posture was necessary to overcome skepticism regarding early discharge after major pulmonary resection.
The second stage (2016–2020) marked the era of “Standardization”. This era is directly validated by the strong, concurrent keyword bursts of ‘complications’ (2020–2021) and ‘guidelines’ (2021–2023) in . Bibliometrically, this stage is anchored by the prominent centrality of the 2019 ESTS guidelines node in our reference co-citation network ( ), which harmonized disparate protocols into a cohesive care bundle. Our journal analysis further supports this, showing a strong interplay between surgical journals (Eur J Cardiothorac Surg) and anesthesia journals (Anesthesiology). This multidisciplinary coupling confirms that the maturity of ERAS depends on breaking down silos between the surgeon’s technical execution and the anesthesiologist’s physiological management, such as the adoption of opioid-sparing analgesia and locoregional blocks ( ).

## Current hotspots and future frontiers: precision and patient-centeredness
The most significant finding from our keyword trend analysis ( ) is the recent paradigm shift toward “quality of life” and “patient-reported outcomes” from 2022 to 2025. The transition to this third stage of ‘precision and patient-centeredness’ is evidenced by the most recent emergence of keyword bursts such as ‘quality of life’ (2022–2023) and ‘outcomes’ (2022–2025), indicating that the research focus has definitively moved beyond traditional metrics. This signals that the research community has moved beyond traditional metrics like length of stay (LOS) and complication rates, which are now considered baseline expectations rather than novel endpoints. As ERAS protocols become standard of care, the scientific frontier has advanced to understanding the quality of survivorship. Recent studies suggest that while minimally invasive techniques reduce physical trauma, the psychological and functional recovery assessed by PROs requires nuanced perioperative interventions ( ).
Furthermore, the integration of “Thoracoscopic Surgery” and “Robotic Surgery” with ERAS represents the technological frontier. The convergence of precise, uniportal VATS or robotic platforms with physiological optimization aims to minimize the stress response to its absolute nadir ( ). This “holistic minimization” connects the surgical trauma theory with rehabilitation science. Additionally, the emergence of keywords related to specific high-risk subpopulations such as elderly and frail patients suggests a move towards “Precision ERAS”, characterized by the tailoring of protocols to individual physiological phenotypes rather than applying a one-size-fits-all approach ( ).

## Implications for clinical practice and policy
The findings of this study provide a roadmap for future investigations in ERAS for lung cancer resection, emphasizing the need for enhanced international collaborations to bridge productivity gaps between high-volume and high-impact regions. Emerging frontiers, such as the integration of robotic surgery with QoL metrics, warrant further exploration to refine patient-centered protocols, particularly in diverse populations ( ). Policymakers and clinicians can leverage these insights to promote standardized ERAS adoption, potentially reducing healthcare burdens associated with lung cancer surgery ( ). Overall, this bibliometric visualization not only maps the current landscape but also anticipates a future where precision surgery and multidisciplinary care converge to improve outcomes in thoracic oncology.

## Limitations
While this study provides a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of ERAS in lung cancer resection, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, data retrieval was restricted to the WoSCC. Although WoSCC is considered the most authoritative database for bibliometric analysis, this selection may have excluded relevant studies published in journals indexed solely in Scopus, PubMed, or other regional databases. Consequently, this reliance on a single database may introduce a degree of distortion in country productivity rankings and citation metrics. Specifically, WoSCC’s stringent indexing criteria traditionally favor established, English-language journals, which might inherently overrepresent the visibility and citation metrics of Western nations. Conversely, because databases like Scopus offer broader coverage of regional and newly emerging journals, the total publication volume of high-output countries, such as China, might actually be underrepresented in our current dataset relative to their true global output. Second, despite no specific language restrictions being applied during the search, the predominance of English-language publications in WoSCC may have led to the omission of significant non-English studies. Third, bibliometric indicators such as total citations and H-index are inherently influenced by time. Recently published high-quality studies (particularly those from 2023 to 2025) may not yet have accumulated sufficient citations to reflect their true academic impact, leading to a potential underestimation of the most recent trends (“citation lag”). Furthermore, the potential incompleteness of late 2025 indexing might introduce minor biases in the trend curve and the most recent citation burst detection. Fourth, it is important to note that citation metrics are inherently influenced by publication age (citation window bias), journal visibility, self-citation patterns, and network centrality effects. High citation counts in this study are interpreted as indicators of extensive academic impact and visibility rather than an absolute measure of intrinsic methodological quality. Finally, while synonym merging was performed (e.g., unifying “VATS” and “thoracoscopic surgery”), the inherent algorithms of VOSviewer and CiteSpace may still contain minor semantic discrepancies that could slightly affect the clustering precision.

## Conclusions
This study provides the first comprehensive bibliometric visualization of global trends and research hotspots in ERAS for lung cancer resection from 2010 to 2025. The results demonstrate that this field has evolved from a nascent stage into a rapidly expanding domain of thoracic oncology, characterized by an exponential growth in academic output since 2018. Geographically, a distinct pattern has emerged where China leads in total publication volume, whereas the United States and European nations, particularly Denmark and the United Kingdom, exert profound influence through high-quality research and extensive international collaboration anchored by the dominant University of Copenhagen institutional cluster. Crucially, the evolution of keywords and citation bursts reveals a significant intellectual paradigm shift where the research focus has transitioned from initial technical feasibility and protocol standardization toward patient-centered care. The recent surge in keywords such as “quality of life” and “outcomes” reflects a growing consensus that successful ERAS is defined not merely by clinical metrics but by the holistic well-being of patients. Ultimately, the future of this domain lies in the seamless integration of precision minimally invasive surgery with multidisciplinary perioperative management, and this study serves as a vital roadmap for guiding future investigations toward these emerging frontiers.
