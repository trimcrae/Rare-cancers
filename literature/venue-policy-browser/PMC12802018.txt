As the official journal of the Korean Gastric Cancer Association (KGCA), the Journal of Gastric Cancer (JGC) has sought to disseminate rigorous, clinically meaningful advances across the spectrum of gastric cancer—from prevention and early detection to multimodality treatment, surgical innovation, and survivorship. In April 2024, JGC reached a major milestone by being indexed in MEDLINE, widely regarded as the most authoritative biomedical bibliographic database curated by the U.S. National Library of Medicine. The journal’s trajectory has become increasingly visible to the global community: JGC now reports a 2024 Journal Impact Factor (JIF) of 3.8 and a 5-year JIF of 2.9, positioning the journal firmly within the competitive landscape of both gastroenterology/hepatology and oncology.
While a journal’s influence cannot be reduced to a single metric, it is still worth reflecting on what drove this upward movement—particularly because the reasons are directly aligned with what readers most need: trustworthy evidence synthesis, transparent methodology, and practice-changing data anchored in real-world clinical contexts. Among multiple contributors and initiatives, one figure stands out for sustained, structurally transformative impact: Dr. Keun Won Ryu, former KGCA Chairman and former Editor-in-Chief (EIC) of JGC. This editorial is written to recognize how his academic life’s work—spanning evidence-based guideline leadership and national-level data initiatives—helped JGC mature from a respected society journal into a globally consulted reference point.
A defining feature of JGC’s recent evolution has been the journal’s role as the primary publication platform for the Korean Practice Guidelines for Gastric Cancer, created through a multidisciplinary and explicitly evidence-based process. The 2018 guideline—published in JGC in 2019—represented a major inflection point: it offered structured key questions, transparent evidence grading, and consensus recommendations intended not only for domestic standardization but also for international readability and comparability [ ]. Importantly, this was not a one-time achievement. The guideline effort continued through major revision cycles. The Korean Practice Guidelines for Gastric Cancer 2022 were subsequently published (with broad updates reflecting evolving evidence in systemic therapy, surgical approaches, and diagnostics), reinforcing JGC’s position as a “go-to” source for authoritative, methodologically explicit guidance [ ]. The momentum persisted with the Korean Practice Guidelines for Gastric Cancer 2024, published again in JGC, reflecting the journal’s sustained commitment to iterative, evidence-driven updating rather than static statements [ ].
These guideline publications did not occur “automatically.” They required organizational leadership, consensus-building skills, and the editorial courage to prioritize high-effort, high-impact scholarship that demands intensive peer review and careful stewardship. Dr. Ryu’s leadership—first as a central driver of guideline development and later as EIC and society chairman during key publication periods—helped institutionalize the expectation that JGC would be the journal where evidence is not only reported, but also organized for clinical decision-making.
If guidelines reflect “what to do,” then national surveys and epidemiologic analyses define “where we are.” JGC has increasingly served as a venue for large-scale Korean datasets that are not merely descriptive, but strategically essential: they provide denominators for health policy, quantify practice variability, benchmark outcomes, and generate hypotheses for the next generation of trials. A key example is the KGCA-led nationwide survey series, which has provided a structured snapshot of surgically treated gastric cancer in Korea—data that are uniquely valuable because they reflect real practice patterns at scale rather than single-center experience [ ]. Equally important, JGC has strengthened its role in anchoring national context through population-based epidemiology and screening research. Using Korea’s central cancer registry and national screening survey data, these studies delineate long-term trends in incidence and survival and quantify how the coronavirus disease 2019 pandemic perturbed screening participation—evidence that informs interpretation of national progress, remaining unmet needs, and future preparedness in cancer control [ , , ]. These “infrastructure” publications do more than report statistics. They strengthen the journal’s credibility as a place where practice is examined with scientific seriousness—where variation can be documented, progress can be tracked, and gaps can be defined without defensiveness.
Taken together, the guideline series and national-level data initiatives exemplify Dr. Ryu’s distinctive editorial leadership: the cultivation of scholarship that endures. They are logistically complex manuscripts—large collaborative groups, substantial evidence tables, high public visibility, and a need for consistent standards of transparency and reproducibility. Sustaining that pipeline is a form of academic public service, and it has materially contributed to JGC’s current standing.
Dr. Keun Won Ryu now transitions from formal leadership roles while continuing to serve the society and the journal as a senior advisor and editorial board member. This is not an endpoint. Rather, it reflects a continuity of purpose: to ensure that JGC remains a journal that clinicians can rely upon when evidence is uncertain, when technologies evolve quickly, and when policy questions demand data rather than opinion.
For JGC, the next stage will require the same attributes that characterized the period we honor today: methodological rigor, international readability, and an editorial culture that rewards both innovation and verification. As we pursue that path, we do so with gratitude for the legacy that helped make it possible.
On behalf of the KGCA and the JGC community, I offer sincere appreciation to Dr. Keun Won Ryu—for strengthening the editorial foundations that support evidence-based guidelines and nationwide data resources, and for helping JGC speak to the world with greater clarity, credibility, and impact.
