## Abstract

## Introduction
Given the importance of the internship course in enhancing student capabilities, it seems necessary to employ appropriate techniques to manage and organize the training process. This research was conducted with the goal of identifying the essential data elements required for the development of an internship monitoring system in Health Information Technology.

## Methods
This qualitative research was conducted in 2022 using the Delphi technique. The study population consisted of professors from the Health Information Technology departments in medical universities in Iran (n = 85), selected through a convenience sampling method. The research instrument was a researcher-developed questionnaire, designed based on the approved educational curriculum, literature review, and analysis of existing systems. Its validity was confirmed by a panel of Health Information Technology experts, and its reliability was established with a Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of 0.83. The results of the Delphi rounds were analyzed and presented using descriptive statistical methods (Percent, Average, Standard deviation) with SPSS software, version 16.

## Results
Twenty professors from the Health Information Technology departments across the country participated in this research. The final model of the essential data elements for developing an internship monitoring system in Health Information Technology comprises five main data sections as follows: management data elements with three data axes, user data elements section with four data axes, functional data elements section with six data axes, output data elements section with two data axes, and key performance indicators section with five data axes.

## Conclusion
In this research, a comprehensive set of essential data elements for developing an internship monitoring system in Health Information Technology was compiled. The proposed framework can facilitate the integration of data collection and provide a coherent approach to student evaluation.

## Introduction
Scientific and academic centers are among the most important factors in the growth of countries and serve as the starting point for societal developments in various fields [ ]. Students also form the backbone of various organizations and will become integral parts of society in the future. The mission of the educational system, especially in universities of medical sciences, is to train specialized human resources to take on the responsibilities of society in the field of health and treatment. A skilled workforce plays an effective role in its profession and can also contribute to the development and progress of the country, meeting the needs of the people and society [ , ].
Medical and paramedical students, like other candidates entering the labor market, must acquire the necessary knowledge and skills to be efficient in their profession and develop the required abilities in a real-world environment. The internship, a crucial component of their educational activities, plays an important role in the acquisition of professional skills and abilities [ ].
The results of various studies indicate that numerous problems exist within internship management across different fields. These issues often stem from a lack of policy guidance, the absence of a standardized management model, inadequate teaching plans, and unclear training objectives. Additionally, some problems arise from unscientifically structured learning content, such as the misalignment and lack of coordination between available courses and their practical application, as well as the absence of an appropriate internship schedule [ ]. Additionally, numerous criticisms have been raised concerning the timing and location of internships, including the excessive workload of employees, a shortage of available professors, and insufficient consulting time to address the educational needs of students [ ]. This issue contributes to widening the gap between practical and theoretical education.
The review of published studies on Entrustable Professional Activities (EPA) in clinical education for undergraduate medical students indicated that implementing strategies for curriculum-based internships is vital. Additionally, it is necessary to establish a specific framework for the valid evaluation of students. In this regard, faculty members need to devote more time and focus to the internship period.
In order to improve the effectiveness of internships, particularly in the field of medical sciences, various measures have been implemented by universities worldwide. In many disciplines, portfolios are utilized to monitor internships more closely [ ]. A portfolio is a collection of documents that provides substantial evidence of a student’s abilities and the learning process over time. Additionally, electronic portfolios are capable of recording the multimedia experiences of learners [ ].
Electronic portfolios are increasingly used by nurses. Initially, these tools were merely simple translations of information from paper, online curriculum vitae, and sometimes contained scanned documents such as certificates of achievement and job records. However, by discovering multiple potentials, they are now used as valuable learning tools that promote personal development and support continuous career growth.
In some countries, comprehensive internship portals are utilized. These portals are modern services that facilitate communication between learners and internship opportunities within a digital framework [ ]. With the advancement of technology, various studies have highlighted the application of information systems in managing internships. A management system with a comprehensive framework offers numerous capabilities, including process transparency, facilitated communication, and data-driven decision-making [ ].
With the expansion of science and the emergence of new technologies in this century, health care organizations need to employ experts in the field of technology application. These experts should be proficient in computer knowledge and also have a sufficient familiarity with the health system and hospitals. In Iran, these professionals are trained in the field of health information technology at Universities of Medical Sciences. To develop the basic skills and acquire the necessary abilities, students spend about 25% of their training course completing internships [ ]. Given the significance of the internship course in enhancing the capabilities of health information technology graduates, employing a monitoring system can be instrumental in organizing and managing the training process. The monitoring system is a web-based platform (accessible via the internet or intranet) tailored to the organization’s workflow. Such systems are developed with the goal of streamlining communication, generating diverse management reports, and enabling the monitoring and follow-up of individuals’ responsibilities [ ].
In this context, the implementation of an internship monitoring system for health information technology at Birjand University of Medical Sciences has been considered. The system, by creating a learning guide, can transfer a portion of the learning responsibility to the students. It also aims to target internship training, equalize learners’ experiences, and systematize the training process. Furthermore, by documenting learning activities and experiences, the system enables the evaluation of learners, provides feedback, assesses the internship, and offers the capacity to revise the curriculum. Determining the essential data elements is the first step in developing information systems. This is one of the most significant structured tools for documenting information, allowing for data comparison in a uniform language and facilitating the exchange of information between institutions and individuals [ ].
Various studies have determined data elements for the design of information systems, such as essential data elements in the differential diagnosis of oral medicine, the COVID-19 minimum data set and interoperable reporting framework, the national minimum data set (MDS) for the information management system for disability, and the minimum data set for monitoring gene therapy in hemophilia, among others. This research is conducted with the goal of identifying the essential data elements needed to develop the internship monitoring system for health information technology.

## Methods
This study was conducted in 2022 using the Delphi decision-making technique. The Delphi method, also known as the Estimate-Talk-Estimate (ETE) technique, is a systematic and qualitative forecasting method. It is achieved by collecting the opinions of a group of experts through several rounds of questionnaires [ ]. 
The study population included faculty members from the departments of Health Information Technology, Medical Informatics, and Health Information Management at medical sciences universities in Iran. The data collection tool was a researcher-developed questionnaire. After reviewing related texts and observing similar educational systems, a draft of data elements based on the approved educational curriculum was compiled, and the initial format of the questionnaire was created. Its validity was then assessed by a group of Health Information Technology professors, and its reliability was confirmed with a Cronbach’s alpha of 0.83.
Subsequently, an online Delphi survey was conducted to prioritize the essential data elements. In this study, two Delphi rounds were carried out with the goal of achieving more than 75% agreement among experts for each item. The most important point in the Delphi process is that participants understand the goals of using the Delphi method. In case of misunderstanding, we will receive irrelevant answers from them. The respondents must have sufficient knowledge in the relevant field and be familiar with the subject literature of the discussed category. Therefore, health information technology faculty members were selected as subject experts and the main users of the system. Additionally, in the first round, questionnaires were distributed via email along with a note introducing the research and its objectives. In the first round, the questionnaires were distributed via email, accompanied by a note introducing the research and its objectives. At the end of each section of the questionnaire, participants had the opportunity to suggest additional necessary data elements or modify the existing ones. The criterion for accepting essential data elements in the proposed model was the level of agreement among experts. Therefore, essential data elements with an agreement level of less than 50% were removed in the first round. Those with an agreement level between 50% and 75% were reconsidered in the second round, and those with an agreement level of more than 75% were accepted in the first round. In the second round, the revised questionnaire was distributed to the participants, allowing experts to review the opinions of their peers. The results of the Delphi rounds were analyzed using descriptive statistical methods (Percent, Average, Standard deviation) with SPSS software version 16. Ultimately, a set of essential data elements was established in the proposed model, which included only those elements that achieved more than 75% agreement after two Delphi rounds. This study has been registered with the ethics code IR.BUMS.REC.1401.094 at Birjand University of Medical Sciences.

## Results
In this study, 20 experts participated. The demographic and scientific characteristics of the participants in the Delphi technique revealed that 70% of the participants were women, representing the highest gender frequency. The most represented age group was 40–49 years. In terms of the field of study, 65% were from health information management, which had the highest frequency, while 15% were dedicated to medical informatics, representing the lowest frequency. Among the participants, those with the academic rank of assistant professor were the most common, at 50%. Regarding work experience, those with less than 10 years constituted the majority, at 60%. ## Discussion
The final model presented in this research, aimed at determining the essential data elements for developing an internship monitoring system in Health Information Technology, includes 5 main data sections as follows: Management Data Elements with 3 data axes (system, location, access levels) and 8 sub-branches, User Data Elements section with 4 data axes (student, instructor, group manager, board member) and 7 sub-categories, Functional Data Element section with 6 data axes (courses offered, organizers, venue, experience registration, report registration, evaluation) and 23 sub-sections, Output Data Elements section with 2 axes (comparative charts, statistical reports) and 10 subsections, and Key Performance Indicators section with 5 data axes (health information management, health indicators and data analysis, application of health information systems, patient file coding, coding of causes of death) and 14 subsections.
In the section on Management Data Elements, in addition to the requirements for introducing the system and the university where it is implemented, the determination of user access levels is discussed. This is one of the most important security measures for information systems. Digital information security is defined as the protection and preservation of the confidentiality, availability, and integrity of organizational data. Based on this definition, a system can be considered secure when these three factors are given equal consideration, and the necessary tools and controls are utilized to create and implement them [ ]. The users of the monitoring system include various groups: students, internship instructors, group managers, and board members, each of whom has their own specific requirements, features, and usage. Therefore, in the User Data Elements section, details of the system users’ profiles with different access levels and usage are specified.
The Functional Data Element section was prepared in accordance with the educational curriculum and the experiences of the internship supervisors. Since graduates of the Health Information Technology field are required to assume multiple roles in hospitals and service centers to manage health data, they receive training in various locations during their internships, such as the admissions and discharge department, laboratory, radiology, pharmacy, and various treatment departments. The possibility of recording experiences in different locations within the monitoring system should be considered. In addition, students need to learn the necessary skills to work with various information systems and registries in different university departments, health centers, private clinics, insurance companies, and more. For this purpose, various site visits are organized, and the reports from these visits need to be recorded in the monitoring system. Furthermore, to familiarize students with current topics, it is recommended to hold educational workshops and journal clubs as part of the curriculum, which can also be recorded in the system.
In a study that designed a comprehensive internship system for medical students [ ], interns were able to record the following items on a daily basis in the software: attending the internal department, attending rounds, number of patients visited, diagnostic procedures defined for the intern based on the educational curriculum, presenting an article in the club journal, morning report, and attending the clinic. Through their panel, professors can monitor daily recorded activities and send feedback to trainees. In the present study, necessary data elements to record information on training courses, journal clubs, etc., as well as the possibility of monitoring student evaluations by teachers, have been proposed.
In the present study, it is possible to evaluate learners and record feedback for the internship instructors. In addition, in the Output Data Elements section, it is possible to monitor the recorded activities of students in the form of comparative charts and statistical reports for the group manager and board members. This approach allows for the performance of students and instructors during the internship to be documented, enabling comparisons of performance across different departments, hospitals, and even universities. Consequently, if there is insufficient attention to the training of certain professional skills, the internship process can be adjusted through timely monitoring. Monitoring is one of the critical factors in the successful implementation of the internship. Previous studies have shown that monthly feedback from instructors assigned to each individual has increased participation by approximately 50 to 100% [ ].
In a study that created a surgical portfolio as an educational tool for French plastic surgery residents [ ], the tool included sections on objectives, knowledge, clinical skills, technical skills, and procedures. In each section, the evaluated skills were divided into three levels: basic, intermediate, and advanced. The aim of this series was to achieve national unity in plastic surgery education. In the current study, with a special focus on Key Performance Indicators, the unification of internship training in the field of health information technology has been considered. In the Key Performance Indicators section, the main skills required of learners in the Health Information Technology field are divided into five primary groups according to the educational curriculum. The details of the functions required for each skill are also specified separately based on the opinions of subject matter experts.
Several studies have utilized the Delphi decision-making technique to determine essential data in system design. In a study aimed at developing an intelligent system, Ehtsham et al. determined the set of essential data elements for the differential diagnosis of oral diseases. In the final model, the administrative data category included 17 elements, and the historical data category comprised 23 elements, both of which were considered in diagnosing various oral diseases. The paraclinical and clinical indicator categories included necessary data elements aligned with the 6 main axes of oral soft tissue diseases as outlined in Burket’s Oral Medicine: ulcerative, vesicular, and bullous lesions; red and white lesions of the oral mucosa; pigmented lesions of the oral mucosa; benign lesions of the oral cavity and jaws; oral and oropharyngeal cancer; and salivary gland diseases [ ]. 
In order to design a surveillance system, Shanbezadeh et al. developed a minimum data set (MDS) for COVID-19 and an interoperable reporting framework. The proposed MDS was divided into administrative and clinical sections with three and eight data classes, and 29 and 40 data fields, respectively. For each data field, structured data values were defined along with SNOMED-CT codes, in accordance with the HL7-CDA standard [ ]. 
Ahmadi et al. have proposed a national MDS for the information management system for disability in Iran, which includes administrative and clinical categories with 130 and 345 data elements, respectively. Of these, 238 data elements were mandatory (administrative: 60, clinical: 178), and the rest were optional [ ]. 
A minimum data set for monitoring gene therapy in hemophilia was proposed. It is structured into 3 sections and includes an abridged list of 6 topics for routine gene therapy follow-up. The researchers hope that this collection will address concerns related to data management and patient safety [ ]. 
In the research conducted by Akbari et al., a National Consensus Minimum Data Set for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Oral Cancer was developed. The framework proposed for managing the data generated during the diagnosis and treatment processes of oral cancer is divided into six sections: management data with four axes, historical data with four axes, paraclinical indicators with two axes, clinical indicators, data related to therapeutic measures, and mortality data [ ]. 
In a study that aims to establish a generic wound assessment MDS to underpin clinical practice, a literature review was used to extract data elements, identifying 68 potential assessment items. Then, by collecting the views of experts, they designed the MDS framework, which included 37 data elements in the following categories: general health information, baseline wound information, wound assessment parameters, wound symptoms, and specialists.
The current research was conducted with the aim of identifying essential data elements for development of an internship monitoring system in Health Information Technology. Besides facilitating the monitoring of students’ performance, this system serves as a learning guide by outlining the goals and competencies expected from the course for learners and provides the possibility of self-evaluation based on the goals set in the educational curriculum. The proposed framework can facilitate the integration of data collection and provide a coherent approach to student evaluation. It also lays the foundation for equalizing students’ learning experiences and systematizing education across the country.
The possibility of monitoring an individual’s learning process over time is considered for the training instructors so that evaluations based on competence can be conducted. The feedback from the instructors can clarify the strengths and weaknesses of the students and help them in self-regulated learning.
But one of the most important limitations of the present study is the lack of a similar information system. In this study, the basis for determining data elements was the educational curriculum and internship scenarios in the field of Information Technology. The researchers found no similar studies that specified data elements or developed a system with this level of detail for internships. It appears that publishing the results of this study and utilizing them in the design, implementation, and evaluation of an information system could serve as a suitable model for applying technology in internship management across other academic fields in medical and paramedical sciences.

## Conclusion
In this research, the set of essential data elements for developing an internship monitoring system in Health Information Technology was compiled with input from faculty members, who are subject matter experts, as well as the main users of the system. Because the participation of end users is crucial to the system’s design process, effectiveness, and success, utilizing the findings of this research can be an important step in designing the system. Due to advances in health information technology, there is a possibility of changes in the professional processes of this field. Therefore, it is suggested to review the data elements of the system, especially the parts related to key performance indicators, at regular intervals with input from experts. The experience of designing and implementing a monitoring system in the field of health information technology will be published in future research. Considering the importance of the internship period in all fields of medical sciences, this experience can serve as a model for other fields in systematizing and enhancing the internship period. However, the financial consequences of implementing educational innovations should be considered.
