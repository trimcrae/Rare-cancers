## Introduction
Geotourism is a form of tourism that specifically focuses on geology and landscape (Ross and David ). It constitutes a form of sustainable tourism that focuses primarily on experiencing the earth’s geological features in a way that fosters environmental and cultural understanding, appreciation and conservation; moreover, this type of tourism is beneficial for localities. Geotourism is an integral part of UNESCO geoparks and is essential to their development (Dowling ). Although geotourism has been developing for more than a decade, it is still emerging as a new global phenomenon compared to traditional tourism (Ross and David ). China has the largest number of geoparks in the world, and a network of diverse types of geoparks has been established throughout the country. Nevertheless, the literature on UNESCO Global Geoparks (UGGps) is scant. A literature review revealed that few articles have addressed questions pertaining to geoheritage, geodiversity and the evaluation of geoparks (Yinlu et al. ). In China, geoparks have always overlapped with many other tourism forms; however, under the leadership of municipal governments, the development of tourism in a particular region has relied on the local government’s emphasis on resource utilization and brand promotion based on the benefits provided by the brand (Nyíri and Nyíri  ). Dunhuang is a city rich in tourism resources where many tourism brands have emerged (Figs. and ). Therefore, this study investigates the development of geotourism in Dunhuang. 
Dunhuang is located in the western part of Gansu Province in northwestern China. Situated in an oasis in the Gansu-Xinjiang desert region, it is bordered by the Gobi Desert to the north, Sanwei Mountain to the east, Mingsha Mountain to the south, and Lop Nur to the west (Zhibao et al. ). Dunhuang is located on an east–west downsloping basin plain with high elevations in the north and south and low elevations in the centre; its average elevation is 1139 m in a total area of 31,200 km2. Of that area, the oasis covers 1400 acres, accounting for merely 4.5% of the total area (Yanjie et al. ).
The general characteristics of tourism in China differ markedly from those in Western contexts. Tourism in China is a recent phenomenon (beginning only in the 1980s) (World Tourism Organization , Nyíri and Nyíri  ). Tourism has grown in Dunhuang since the mid-1980s. The city is the site of the renowned Mogao Grottoes, which were designated a UNESCO World Heritage site in 1987. With the increased demand for and development of domestic tourism, Dunhuang has developed many tourism products that combine natural and cultural landscape resources; examples are the Mogao Grottoes, the ruins of the Yangguan and Yumenguan passes on the Silk Road, the ruins of the Great Wall of the Han Dynasty, the ruins of Hecang City, the Mount Mingshashan and Crescent Moon-shaped Spring (MM&CS), the West Lake National Nature Reserve, and the Dunhuang UGGp, of which four have international significance (Table ). Sightseeing has dominated tourism activities in Dunhuang, while ecotourism, geotourism, outdoor adventures and themed tourism have developed relatively slowly. 
In 2020, Dunhuang received 6.5844 million tourists, yielding 8.03 billion yuan in total tourism revenue. Domestic tourists accounted for 6.5768 million visitors, and there were 0.76 million inbound tourists. Although the number of tourists and tourism revenues decreased by more than 40% in that year due to the impact of COVID-19, tourism still accounted for 73.6% of the GDP, making it an important pillar among the industries of Dunhuang (Statistical Communiqué of Dunhuang City about the 2020 National Economic and Social Development, 2020).
Consumers’ preferences are highly dependent on brands or brand recognition, which have become essential factors in the ways in which people choose travel destinations. Compared with other brands, the World Heritage designation is considered the most popular tourism brand, ranking at the top of travellers’ preferences and brand recognition (Nyíri 50ff; Poria et al. ). The risks for other brands occur mainly in the form of potential competition from the World Heritage label or lesser visibility than that label (Aemail et al. ; Mariani and Guizzardi ). The Mogao Grottoes, inarguably the greatest treasure of Buddhist art found in a single locality anywhere in the world, comprise numerous temples and shrines containing countless sculptures, murals and manuscripts (Agnew et al. ; Jinshi and Shengliang ). Collectively, they offer invaluable insights into ancient Chinese and Central Asian history, geography, politics, religion, literature, art, science and technology (Jinshi and Shengliang ; Agnew et al. ). As the Mogao Grottoes have attracted an increasing number of visitors, Dunhuang has developed its brand based on image building and tourism promotion in connection with the caves. Additionally, the local government has supported the Mogao Grottoes politically and financially in terms of promotion, route design, transportation and venue construction. The Dunhuang UGGp, which covers an area of 2180.75 km2, is also an international tourism brand. However, today, the visibility of the UGGp is far inferior to that of the World Heritage site. According to data from Dunhuang Smart Tourism, the Mogao Grottoes, MM&CS, Dunhuang Night Market and Yangguan Pass are the scenic spots most visited by tour groups, which on average stay there for 2 days. Compared with tour groups, individual tourists spend more than 2.5 days in Dunhuang and might travel to the yardang, which is considered the main area of the UGGp based on its geological features.
To date, no research has been conducted on tourism destination preference predictions in Dunhuang. Do people recognize the UGGp more than they recognize the World Heritage label as an international brand? How visible is the Dunhuang UGGp in tourism marketing? What kind of tourist is most likely to be attracted to Dunhuang because of the UGGp brand? Is geotourism a valuable trend for the future of tourism in Dunhuang? Through field trips and semistructured interviews, this research reveals the obstacles faced by geotourism in Dunhuang and offers solutions.

## Method
The following methods were used to explore the development of geotourism in Dunhuang and to assess tourists’ cognition of Dunhuang and its UGGp as well as the visibility of the UGGp brand.

## Random Interviews
Random interviews were conducted with 22 tourists in Dunhuang in March, May, July and August 2019 to determine their tourism preferences and travel modes (Table ). To learn about the respondents’ interests, all conversations were recorded with their permission, and NOVIVO software was used to analyse the interviews and highlight word frequency statistics. 

## Data Collection

Literature review

By using the keywords “Dunhuang”, “Dunhuang + tourism”, “Dunhuang + geology”, “Dunhuang + culture”, “Dunhuang + ecology”, and “Dunhuang + protection”, we sought to understand the extent to which Dunhuang tourism has been studied.Data collected from tourism questionnaires and websites

A nonscale questionnaire was designed and issued to tourists who had visited or were currently travelling in Dunhuang. We focused on tourists’ perceptions, cognition and tourism behaviours in Dunhuang to determine (1) their understanding of Dunhuang tourism and cognition of tourism brands in Dunhuang; (2) the itinerary arrangement of Dunhuang tourism (time, accommodation, expense, travel destination, experience projects, etc.); and (3) their satisfaction with Dunhuang tourism and suggestions. The questionnaire was divided into three blocks and was composed of 25 questions, including 5 items on demographics; 18 of the questions were designed with closed answers and a series of alternative responses for the tourists to choose from, and 2 allowed the respondents to formulate their answers.

## Semistructured Interviews
Our team provided technical support and participated in the revalidation mission of the Dunhuang UGGp in 2019 and continued to communicate with local stakeholders, such as UGGps officials, officials from the visitor centre, tourism operators, guides, and specialists (scientists), from 2019 to 2022. Based on the criteria in Revaluation Document B, marketing and promotional activities, educational activities, scientific popularization and sustainable development were chosen for the design of the interviews, and the targeted interviews were conducted according to the following outline:

## Analysis and Results
Based on the interviews and data collection, our group focused on three perspectives to analyse tourists’ understanding of and participation in geotourism in Dunhuang: cognition, perception and satisfaction (Fig.  ). 

## Tourists’ Cognition

Symbol and landmark

One hundred percent of the respondents were familiar with the Mogao Grottoes and MM&CS, and 96% knew that the Mogao Grottoes had been designated a World Cultural Heritage site; additionally, 82% of the interviewees knew that the yardang is located in Dunhuang. In regard to Dunhuang, the words that first came to people’s minds were Mogao Grottoes, camel, desert, raisin and Crescent Moon-shaped Spring, and people considered the Mogao Grottoes to be the symbol and representation of Dunhuang."I have heard about the murals in Mogao Grottoes for a long time, and I feel very impressed after seeing them. They are treasures of Buddhist art. The Flying Apsaras are amazing, and I want to buy all the cultural and creative products here". (A respondent from Tianjin 20)
“To see these grottoes, I waited in line for hours; the weather is too hot, and I would not be here if I had known I could visit them on the internet; anyway, it is well worth it.” (A respondent from Gansu 11)
"I have been waiting in Dunhuang for 2 days, but I have not booked my visit to the Mogao Grottoes. I have been to all the surrounding areas. I feel that I have come to Dunhuang in vain and have not seen the Mogao Grottoes”. (A respondent from Hong Kong 10)
Touristic image

Ninety-two percent of the tourists knew that Dunhuang has been recognized as a World Cultural Heritage site. Forty-one percent were aware that the UGGp constitutes one of Dunhuang’s tourism brands. Half of the tourists knew about the yardang but were not willing to go there. Night performances were listed as some of the most fascinating activities available in Dunhuang. The most recurrent keywords in the responses of tourists who had been to Dunhuang were “Mogao Grottoes”, “night performances”, “desert”, “long journey” and “show”. These results are consistent with the results of the textual analysis of online travelogue texts.Limitations

Within a brand marketing strategy, the main attraction for tourists is diversity; however, tourists’ knowledge has still been limited to Dunhuang culture. After the Mogao Grottoes became part of the early development of World Cultural Heritage sites in China in 1987, they became the gold standard for tourism in Dunhuang. The World Heritage brand drove and promoted the desert scenery, and city gradually took on the image of the Pearl of the Silk Road; the marketing strategy was then structured around these themes. Tourists gradually came to regard the Mogao Grottoes as the symbolic image of Dunhuang, and that image shaped their awareness.

## Tourists’ Perceptions

Visitors

Forty-seven percent of tourists have toured Dunhuang in groups, and the Mogao Grottoes and MM&CS are must-visit scenic spots for these groups (data source: Dunhuang Tourism Bureau  ). Compared with the number of tourists visiting Dunhuang and the Mogao Grottoes, the number of tourists to the yardang has dropped sharply; visitors to the yardang accounted for only 10% of the total number who had been to Dunhuang. Fewer than 30 people had visited the area daily during the off-season, and these tourists had all come on their own. These statistics are consistent with the conclusion of Ma Binbin et al. ( ) in an investigation of the kernel density of tourist flows in Dunhuang from 2016 to 2018 (Fig.  ). The hot spots of Dunhuang tourism flows are primarily in the main urban area and suburbs, including the MM&CS, the Mogao Grottoes, Dunhuang Ancient City, Danghe River scenic line, Dunhuang night market, and digital exhibition centre of the Mogao Grottoes. The main subcore hot spots were the yardang and the Yumenguan and Yangguan passes. Due to the impact of COVID-19 and policy restrictions within the national reserve, the yardang has been open only for individual tours, which has reduced the area’s visibility and caused a drop in tourism over the past 3 years. Activities

Tourists tend to be impressed by experiential activities. The MM&CS is a natural landscape offering a wide variety of experience-based amenities that attract tourists. In visiting the wonders of the desert, tourists are amazed at the coexistence of springs and sand and actively participate in camel riding, sand skiing, paragliding, sand motorcycling, helicopter rides and other activities. In comparison, tourists are seemingly less curious about the geological factors behind the wonders of the desert.
Statistics on tourists’ opinions of the MM&CS show that tourists are attracted to (1) the rides featured as part of the attractions, (2) the amazing natural beauty, and (3) the relevant service facilities that can satisfy their demands. The interviews showed that most tourists pay little attention to geologic formations or geoheritage but tend to share photos on social media based on mere sightseeing.“Sand skiing is exciting. It is fun here”. (A respondent from Beijing 2)
“This is my first time seeing and riding a camel; they looked tame. But it is too hot here, and there were too many people; I had to queue for everything. ” (A respondent from Sichuan13)
“There is a spring in the desert, and the water is particularly clear; it is amazing”. (A respondent from Hubei 14)

After tourists visit the Mogao Grottoes, in addition to the exquisite Buddhas, murals and decorative patterns, their most impressive memories are the stories of the Taoist Wang, who stumbled on the cave (Wang discovered a cave full of Buddhist scriptures and sold valuable classic Buddhist works to foreigners at nominal prices, inflicting huge losses on the national cultural heritage of China). However, few tourists are curious about the reasons for selecting the site of the grottoes, and they do not care about the conditions of the caverns, the reasons people chose particular painting materials, or why these paintings have lasted so long. Moreover, tour guides’ explanations and souvenirs include few geological elements.
When asked about how they felt about the tourism activities offered in the yardang, 80% of the tourists said that the journey was too long and the scenery was monotonous and boring. Moreover, they found that activities on the spot were insufficient and expensive and lacked participants. Tourists reported traveling to the yardang for the following reasons: (1) to see the sunset and explore the wonders of the desert, (2) to be independent in their travels, and (3) because they enjoy driving. This type of tourist is generally interested in the geological landscape, photography, adventure and freedom in choosing how to spend their time.

## Tourists’ Satisfaction
Eighty-three percent of the tourists were very satisfied with the tourism amenities of Dunhuang. Ninety-eight percent were aware that the growing number of tourists would have a certain impact on the environment. More than 60% put forward suggestions for improvement. This manuscript is based on data collected from the internet and empirical data gathered over 3 years in the field. The following problems in the development of Dunhuang geotourism are identified.Geosites with low accessibility
Uneven distribution of tourism resources

There are two touristic routes in Dunhuang: the eastern route and the western route. The main tourist attractions on the eastern route are the MM&CS and Mogao Grottoes. Dunhuang Landscape Avenue connects the main road and nodes in a network, and the nodes of the network are the MM&CS, the Mogao Grottoes, the international airport, bus stations, railway stations, and exhibition centres, which are highly concentrated in an area of tourism influx.
Tourism resources located on the western route are patchy and scattered, composed mainly of ruins and yardangs. It takes more than 2.5 h to travel between nodes; the long distances and speed limits make the round trip last at least 1 day.Unreasonable design of the tour

The Mogao Grottoes once had a museum with displays explaining the geological background of the grottoes, the reasons for the site selection, and the relationship between the mural pigments and locally available minerals. However, few people knew about the museum because of its location, which was not on the circuit; moreover, there was a shortage of geointerpretation; hence, the museum was closed.
The tourist service centre of the MM&CS has a geological exhibition hall with exhibition panels that vividly reveal the secrets of the MM&CS. The newly built hall is equipped with virtual reality (VR) equipment and microscopes that can help tourists understand the geology. However, as an increasing number of people purchase their tickets online and the check-in procedures have been simplified, tourists can easily enter the attraction without having to purchase tickets in person; as a result, the hall is rarely visited.Geoproducts need to be optimized
Boring sightseeing

Sightseeing is the main activity for tourists in the yardang. Tourists can take buses with guides to sightsee in the yardang area. Because of the yardang’s geography, environment, complex geological structure and extreme climate, touristic activities there must be carried out under guidance. Moreover, the routes for off-road vehicles are prearranged.The pursuit of profits

The MM&CS is operated by the Dunhuang Cultural Tourism Group. To increase sales and maximize profits (Ross and David ), the company has encouraged tourists to pay to participate in certain activities, such as riding camels and renting vehicles to sightsee. During the remainder of their time there, tourists are encouraged to take part in adventures and entertainment activities, such as sand motoring, off-road rides, paragliding and helicopter flights.Lack of competition

An analysis of domestic tourism platforms, such as Ctrip, Qunar and Mafengwo, reveals that Dunhuang is an important node on the Gansu-Qinghai Loop or the Great Northwest Tourism Route. The 3-day, 2-night group tour is arranged as follows: Day 1: Arrive on the first night and enjoy the Shazhou Night Market; Day 2: Visit the Mogao Grottoes in the morning and the MM&CS in the afternoon and enjoy a night show; and Day 3: Leave in the morning. The itinerary focuses only on the eastern route. By comparison, the main feature on the western route is the yardang and its numerous geological wonders, which constitute a georoute. However, at present, only individual and chartered tourists visit the western route, and few groups travel there.
A yardang is a typical landform caused by wind erosion. It develops under the influence of directional external forces, such as wind and water, and nondirectional forces, such as weathering, gravitational collapse, salt weathering and cracking (Shuai and Ha ). Due to the recent emergence of yardangs in Qaidam, Qinghai, Xinjiang, and Wusute, which have developed into attractions over the past 2 years, the number of tourists visiting Dunhuang’s yardang has decreased significantly. Moreover, Wusute’s yardang is superior in terms of aesthetics and accessibility (Fig.  ). Compared with yardangs that emerge in arid regions, those that emerge on water are more characteristic. Moreover, the Wusute yardang is located next to Highway No. 315 on the Gansu-Qinghai Loop, and it takes only half a day to visit it, which is convenient for groups. Due to the tight schedule of tours, Dunhuang’s yardang has not been included in the design of the route for group tours because of accessibility, profits and the demand of tourists for a site made famous by the internet (Table ). Contents
The number of tourists to the Mogao Grottoes is limited

Due to spatial limits, large numbers of tourists would disturb the environment in the grottoes. The exhaled carbon dioxide and dust from people walking may adhere to the walls, and the original mural paint may come off with cleaning. The colour of the murals would fade and disappear due to wind erosion and dust pollution. Therefore, based on the environmental carrying capacity of the Mogao Grottoes, touristic flows there have been limited by capping the number of visitors and the duration of their visits. Some tourists who go to Dunhuang but fail to visit the Mogao Grottoes might have regrets, and the difficulty of obtaining tickets for them during the peak season could also aggravate the complaints of tourists.Weather

Dunhuang is located deep in the mainland. Its climate is prone to droughts and aridity, with rare rainfall and high levels of evaporation. Tourists often feel uncomfortable in the dry climate. The ultraviolet light is strong, and the surface temperature is higher than 40° Celsius in the daytime during the summer. Tourists are prone to heatstroke if they stay outside for too long. The average temperature in winter is 15° below zero, making it difficult to carry out outdoor activities such as camping and stargazing.

## Discussion

## Rescheduling the Tour Route

Optimizing the western route

The western route needs to be redeveloped as a key route for geotourism, and popularisation of science and technology (PST) activities should be added to the itinerary. The original tour started at 10 a.m. from Dunhuang City and took tourists to Dunhuang Ancient City, the West Thousand Buddha Cave, the Yangguan Pass, the Yumenguan Pass, and the yardangs, in that order, before returning to the city at approximately 10 p.m. We suggest that this itinerary be revised as follows: it should start at 8 a.m. from the city ❶ and then take visitors to the Dunhuang Photoelectric Industrial Park ❷, Dunhuang Ancient City ❸, the West Thousand Buddha Cave ❹, the Yangguan Pass ❺, the Yumenguan Pass ❻, the water flow measurement for the Shule River and the river terrace of the Shule River ❼, the wildlife observatory in West Lake wetland ❽ and the yardangs ❾, in that order; finally, tourists should be taken to stargaze before returning to the city (Fig.  ). 
We suggest adding three PST sites along the way and having a geoguide provide explanations. (1) When passing by the power plant, new energy sources and the leading advantages of solar energy and energy storage techniques should be introduced; (2) tourists should be shown the “Hills and Sandhills” and “Sleeping Buddha-shaped” Range on the way to Dunhuang Ancient City; (3) tourists should see the river terrace of the Daquan River and become familiar with the site selection of the West Thousand Buddha Cave; (4) when driving through the Gobi Desert, tour guides could communicate with tourists about water resources, power supply, fibre-optic coverage and other thought-provoking issues to enable them to understand the difficulty of infrastructure and the importance of defence needs; (5) in passing by the West Lake Wetland, wildlife observation could be developed, and the water could be sampled and the flow of the Shule River measured to enable tourists to participate and feel fulfilled.Developing high-end tourism routes

The success of geotourism in meeting the goals of geoheritage education, sustainable development and geoconservation is ultimately dependent on the quality of the visitor experience (Newsome et al. ). According to Yang’s statistics (Jingya ), 86.2% of domestic tourists in Dunhuang are self-guided, and only 13.8% come as part of a group. Therefore, self-guided tours have gradually become the norm in Dunhuang tourism. Geotourism should take advantage of the environment and unique resources to provide personalized, exclusive services and information to tourists and to increase their awareness and appreciation in ways that meet high-end needs and serve the segmented market.
The yardang-Lop Nur adventure route could be fully developed. Although Lop Nur is located in Xinjiang, Dunhuang has more convenient transportation and travel facilities. Yardangs could be gradually used as distribution centres to bring tourists to the depopulated area of Lop Nur; thus, the route must be redesigned.
Campsites should be established in the yardang area, and luxury tents could be set up to meet the demand for a stargazing experience. Field observation tours could be allowed or conditional; people would be accompanied by a botanist or zoologist in the West Lake Reserve. Aerial photography routes could be developed to meet the needs of photography groups. The layout of the landscape and different props could create an exoplanet environment in the yardang area to attract tourists who want to take photos. Drones could be allowed under specific conditions. Hot-air-balloon sightseeing tours could be introduced.

## Geoscience Popularisation

Increasing the visibility of geoscience

The role of geoparks as instruments of education and public conservation needs to be highlighted (Ross ; Hose ). Educational agents at all levels, including those affiliated with higher education institutions, political leaders, senior managers and directors of public and private institutions, should be required to incorporate respect for evidence of the Earth’s history into their teaching practices (Henriques et al. ). The broader interpretation will arouse enthusiasm in casual geotourists and those simply wishing to “be there”, not only enhancing their experience but also increasing their awareness of geoheritage and the need for its conservation (Branka ; Dowling ; Newsome and Johnson ; Watson ). The introduction of videos about geology in the digital display centre is suggested, and we suggest adding displays explaining the site’s geology, especially the geological background of the Mogao Grottoes, to the exhibition centre of Dunhuang Academy.Encouraging inspirational education

Hiking trips to visit the yardangs are organized by the Dunhuang Nanjie Primary School every year. Due to the long journey, the uninspiring scenery along the way and the lack of activities, students have always fallen asleep on the bus. In 2019, our team conducted a comparative experiment with two classes of the same grade. For one class, participatory and interactive activities were organized during the ride, while for the other class, no activities were arranged. During the trip, the students in the first group actively participated in the experimental process and remained excited after they finished the hike; in their diaries, they commented that the day was meaningful and that they had learned a lot. In the second group, the students merely recorded in their diaries how hard the hike was (Fig.  ). Lack of education is thought to be one of the greatest threats to geoheritage. The essence of solving such a problem involves raising public awareness of the value of geoheritage through geoeducation (Van Loon ; Hose ; Rutherford et al.  ). Education can also highlight messages about environmental issues and the sustainable use of natural resources, including the consequences of ignoring the loss of geoheritage (Brocx and Semeniuk ). From this comparative experiment, our team learned that students’ learning interests and enthusiasm could be enhanced through the formulation of questions. Based on the distribution of resources and the characteristics of different scenic spots, inquiry-based learning should be developed for students in geoparks (Table ). Echoing real life and fostering easy learning

The geoheritage features themselves must have a strong story to tell, particularly in engaging the interest of the nonspecialist visitor (Gordon ). The Mogao Grottoes stratum is composed of calcareous and argillaceous gravel layers formed by fluvial and proluvial processes and is generally approximately 20–40 m thick. The Mogao Grottoes are distributed mainly in the Jiuquan Formation, which is composed of a gravel layer with gravel, sand and clay accounting for 70%, 25% and 5% of the layer, respectively (Neville ). This information, if used as part of a tour guide’s interpretation, could sound too academic. Therefore, we encourage guides to use metaphors that echo people’s real life.
The strata in which the Mogao Grottoes are located are generally horizontal and multilayered, slowly consolidating under long-term self-weight pressure. In addition, eluviation in the arid climate has been conducive to calcium enrichment, so the gravel layer has remained relatively solid even in a semidiagenetic state. The thick sand and gravel layer is the most practicable layer for cave excavation, as it lies nearly horizontal with a dense structure and can form a sound vault (Guojun ). We compare the stratum in which the Mogao Grottoes are located to a thousand-layer pancake, which is commonly eaten by Chinese people; imagine the consolidation of the stratum as the superposition of layers of cake; and compare the sand to scallion and sesame. The results show that the process of making such a pancake appears as a compacted formation (Fig.  ). 
The yardangs located in Dunhuang are the most typical yardang landforms in China. They were formed as a result of the combined action of tectonic uplift, wind, running water and gravity on incompletely consolidated Quaternary fluvial and lacustrine sediments (Qinghe et al. ). Halimov and Fezer ( ) attempted to identify different stages of yardang development. Dong (Zhibao et al. ) proposed that the development of yardangs in the Kumtagh Desert passed through 4 stages, namely, embryonic, adolescent, mature, and recession. The great variety of yardang landforms in Dunhuang reveals the evolutionary stages of their formation, which are suggested to correspond to the prenatal, embryonic, adolescent, mature, recession, and decline stages. These stages have great significance for the study of the evolution of landforms. During the field trip, we suggested that the tour guide prepare a tiramisu to simulate these evolutionary stages and motivate tourists to imagine the process by enjoying a delicious cake, observing the whole cake at first and then cutting the cake into pieces with a knife, eating it gradually, and considering the debris left at the end (Fig.  ). Intuition

Tourists are often told that the climate in Dunhuang is dry and rainless, with an average annual precipitation of 42.2 mm and an average annual evaporation of 2505 mm. For most tourists, these are boring numbers; however, if we intuitively use the volume of bottles as an example and mention that the annual evaporation in Dunhuang corresponds to as much as 5 bottles of water and the rainfall corresponds to less than 5 bottle caps, tourists would be more likely to remember these data.
The Mogao Grottoes are the largest and best-preserved Buddhist art treasure in the world. One of the secrets of the vivid beauty of their fresco paintings is the pigments used for the Dunhuang murals, which can be roughly divided into three categories: (1) mineral pigments, such as cinnabar, lasurite, calaite, and ochre, which do not fade; (2) plant pigments, such as gamboge, indigo, and rouge, some of which fade with age; and (3) shell powder. Most visitors do not know much about minerals and are unable to imagine and understand the painting process on the basis of the guide’s interpretation. Therefore, we recommend that tour guides carry some mineral specimens with them to facilitate people’s intuitive understanding. We suggest that the interpreter attach accessories made of lasurite, calaite or pearls to his or her clothing.

## Promoting Sustainable Development

Highlighting geotourism

Geotourism is founded on conservation and protection. In enhancing tourists’ awareness of geoprotection, geotourism emphasizes that geoheritage is essentially a nonrenewable resource and that on no account should people engage in violent behaviour towards geoheritage. Furthermore, geoparks are good tools for both geoconservation and rural development, especially in vulnerable environments (Ólafsdóttir and Dowling ).
The MM&CS is a magical waterscape influenced by geological processes and hydrological conditions in a desertic environment. The spring is approximately 200 m long, flowing from east to west, and approximately 25 m wide on a south-north axis, with an average water depth of 0.8 m. The spring has been so named because it is shaped like a crescent moon. Why is there such a spring in the desert? Where does it come from? Why does it never dry up? Two explanations have emerged. The first is based on the spring’s location in a depression between the Danghe River and Xishui Ditch (two ancient alluvial-proluvial fans), which makes it prone to catchment (Chunli et al. ). The other explanation is based on the surrounding terrain; owing to deposits made by the wind, a large quantity of sand piled up south of the spring and its nearby areas, and the accumulated quicksand was aerodynamically carried to the other side of the ridge by winds to form unique landforms that made the spring and sand coexist (Weimin et al. ; Yingjun et al. ).
In the 1970s, due to reclamation, irrigation, population growth and economic development, the vegetation around the spring was destroyed, resulting in a decline in the groundwater level. In 1975, the spring almost ran dry because of the construction of a reservoir on the upper reaches of the Danghe River, which cut off the supply of water. In 1986, a new strategy was implemented to dig deeply into the spring and install water pipelines to raise the water level by artificial injection. After the project was completed, the water level rose by 65.2 cm because of the injection but dropped by 61 cm when the injection was stopped; moreover, the spring water was separated from the injection water, which is apparent in their different colours (Jiuj ).
Currently, a project for the restoration of the spring water supply is underway. A dam was constructed on the Danghe River to divide the bed longitudinally into 2 parts: one side is the flood channel, and the other side is the seepage field (Fig.  a). The maximum water storage of the seepage field is 980,000 m3, and the designed water diversion from the main canal is 17.5 million cubic metres/year. By increasing the infiltration and recharge of the surface water, the time it takes to recharge the spring has been lengthened, the original groundwater environment of the spring has been restored, and the self-repair ability of the spring has eventually improved. At the same time, the mining of the spring area’s groundwater has been restricted to alleviate the surrounding groundwater recession. 
Geosites, as elements of the geological heritage of the Earth and the subject of study in geoconservation, represent important educational resources. These resources may enhance learning opportunities, integrating the three interactive teaching meanings of geoconservation as a geoscience (Ross and David ). Therefore, we suggest that the spring restoration and water replenishment project be labelled a geosite to achieve a closed loop on the MM&CS route, which would highlight the characteristics of geotourism. The short distance between the seepage site and the spring is only 4.5 km. Setting the seepage site as one of the stops could help tourists understand where the spring comes from and make them feel a shared responsibility to protect the environment.Limiting or reducing the number of tourists

The development of geotourism needs to be based on the limitation or reduction in the number of tourists based on environmental carrying capacity (Ping ) (Fig.  b). The uncompromising policy decisions by the managers of the Mogao Grottoes to privilege preservation of the cultural resource over visitor access is a judgement that requires informed decision-making about the values of the site, but it may be necessary to depend on scientific data to determine how this can best be done. Due to environmental pollution and the increase in domestic waste caused by increasing numbers of tourists in the peak season, countermeasures should be taken with corresponding severe punishments. In fragile environments, environmental damage could be reduced by raising environmental awareness, conservation, and waste reduction and by reducing tourism activities. For the Mogao Grottoes, the “zero tolerance” policy means that no detectable change or degradation to the wall paintings and sculpture due to visitation is acceptable (Demas et al. ).
Served as technical support and participated in the revalidation mission of the Dunhuang UGGp; our team had the opportunity to identify the problems in the development of geotourism in Dunhuang. After conducting the targeted interviews and questionnaires, we learned tourists’ perceptions and cognition of Dunhuang tourism, confirmed the imbalance and potential of geotourism in Dunhuang, and finally proposed corresponding solutions (Table ). 

## Conclusion
Although the Dunhuang geopark has a wide scope and entails many tourism brands, the Mogao Grottoes, which constitute a cultural tourism attraction, have dominated the tourism market, and the development of geotourism has been relatively slow. In the future, planners in Dunhuang should foster geotourism and focus on the development of the western route. With the help of georesources, tourists are encouraged to participate in PST activities; at the same time, by encouraging tourists to dig and craft the geological stories behind cultural attractions, tourism practitioners would make tourists feel that geoparks echo their lives.
