## Background
As the global population of older adults continues to rapidly increase, there is a pressing need to address diverse concerns related to their physical, cognitive, and psychosocial well-being. In this scoping review, we aimed to map the existing literature on opportunities and challenges inherent in the implementation dynamics of scaling up a community-based health promotion and exercise programs designed for older adults. The research question is ‘What are the opportunities and challenges of successfully scaling up a community-based health promotion and exercise program for older adults?’

## Methods
Three databases (EBSCO Host Medline, The Cochrane Library, and Scopus) were searched for articles published between January 2013 to December 2023. Utilizing a three-step screening process, 17 articles describing the opportunities and challenges of implementing a community-based health promotion and exercise program for older adults at scale were selected for inclusion in this scoping review. Opportunities and challenges were categorized through the lens of the RE-AIM (reach, evaluation, adoption, implementation, maintenance) framework.

## Results
The multifaceted key opportunities and challenges identified on the implementation dynamics of scaling up a community-based health promotion and exercise program for older adults across the RE-AIM framework encompassed issues related to engagement, participation, recruitment, accessibility, awareness, inclusivity, evaluation, adherence, baseline predictors, complexity, motivation, responsiveness, partnership, planning, viability, adaptation, optimization, training, infrastructure, resources, stewardship, and retention.

## Conclusions
Addressing these opportunities and challenges comprehensively is imperative to ensure the successful implementation and enduring sustainability of scaled-up programs of this nature.

## Background
As the global population of older adults continues to rapidly increase, there is a pressing need to address diverse concerns related to their physical, cognitive, and psychosocial well-being. Ageing is linked to issues such as skeletal muscle mass loss, metabolic decline, and diminished functional ability [ ]. Moreover, prevalent health problems, sensory impairments, heightened fall risks, restricted mobility, and cognitive decline further contribute significantly to the disability burden among older adults [ , ]. While prioritizing the health and well-being of this vulnerable demographic is paramount, healthcare systems globally face considerable challenges in achieving this goal [ ]. It is evident that health promotion and prevention programs at the community level emerge as viable solutions to address these multifaceted challenges [ – ].
Health promotion, as per the World Health Organization's (2016) definition, empowers individuals to proactively manage their health and well-being [ ]. As such, community-based interventions, including initiatives by primary care practitioners, contribute to healthier environments through risk management and reduction, supporting successful ageing and primary prevention by emphasizing self-management and adopting healthy living practices in later life [ , ]. Participation in health-promoting activities among older individuals is associated with improved health, reduced mortality, enhanced quality of life, maintenance of productivity, reduced risk of comorbid conditions, and increased adaptive capacity to navigate life changes [ , ]. Moreover, such programs for older adults not only enhance physical abilities, spiritual fulfillment, and a sense of accomplishment but also prioritize the psychosocial dimensions of health, addressing loneliness, fostering social connections, providing social support, and increasing resilience [ , – ].
Key features of successful community-based health promotion and exercise programs for older adults include the critical role of sustained health effects, especially in programs lasting 12 weeks or more [ ]. Additionally, tailoring programs to recognize the unique needs of older adults is necessary [ ]. Moreover, it is imperative to ensure the accessibility and acceptability of these health promotion services for older adults across diverse backgrounds, including ethnic minorities and socioeconomically vulnerable individuals [ ]. Above all, it is imperative that these programs are underpinned by evidence and have demonstrated effectiveness in prior research.
The process of taking health interventions that have been demonstrated to be effective in controlled or smaller settings and expanding them into broader policy or real-world practice is commonly referred to as "scale-up" [ , ]. Enabling scale-up beyond initial funding models is a crucial step in realizing a perceived public health impact, particularly concerning community-based interventions for health promotion and exercise programs [ ]. Authors of several studies have highlighted the urgency and importance of scaling evidence-based health promotion and exercise programs for older adults [ – ]. Expanding these initiatives has the potential to significantly broaden access to preventive care for more older adults, addressing the existing accessibility challenges within primary healthcare.
However, the effective execution of extended community-based health promotion and exercise programs for older adults could encounter sustainability challenges, as a result of insufficient funding, gaps in reimbursement, limited awareness of local resources among healthcare workers, and inadequate collaboration between community organizations and healthcare providers [ ]. Literature highlights several facilitators, suggesting that such programs should be co-created by incorporating participants' and stakeholders' input, actively engaging the community and organizers, adapting to address cultural diversity and having supportive policies for promotion and scale-up [ – ]. Primarily, while implementing a standardized approach for the scaled-up program, flexibility should be accommodated [ ].
In this scoping review, our objective is to chart and discuss the current literature in regard to the opportunities and challenges on the implementation dynamics of scaling up a community-based health promotion and exercise program for older adults. Drawing these insights from the literature in this field may provide implementers with information on adaptable framework, improve planning and success, guide future research and practice, enabling more informed decision-making and strategic planning for optimized impact and sustainability of scaled up programs.

## Methods

## Stage 1: Identifying research question
The following research question was addressed in this scoping review: ‘What are the opportunities and challenges of successfully scaling up a community-based health promotion and exercise program for older adults?’ We followed Arksey and O'Malley's approach [ ], excluding the optional Step 6 (consultation with stakeholders), as our objective was to map and synthesize existing literature rather than generate new stakeholder input. Consistent with methodological guidance, many published scoping reviews similarly omit stakeholder consultation [ ]. The method for scoping review searching, screening, and reporting were modified and utilized as suggested by Levac, Colquhoun and O’ Brien [ ]. In addition, as a guide, we utilized the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Extension for Scoping Review (PRISMA-ScR) checklist (see Additional file 1 for details). The PRISMA-ScR checklist is specifically designed to facilitate the development of a deeper comprehension of relevant terminology, fundamental concepts, and essential items to report for scoping reviews among readers [ ]. By using the PRISMA-ScR checklist, we seek to give an in-depth comprehensible description of our review strategy which will aid in replication and improve the validity of our results.

## Stage 2: Identifying relevant studies
We searched for studies published between January 2013 and December 2023 to identify publications that are relevant to this review. A 10-year timeframe was selected to capture the most contemporary evidence reflecting recent developments in community-based health promotion and exercise programs for older adults. We searched for studies published between January 2013 and December 2023, selecting this 10-year timeframe to capture contemporary evidence and recent developments in community-based health promotion and exercise programs for older adults. The PCC (population, concept and context) used to identify relevant evidence in regard to this review is depicted in Table  . 
The research team members created search terms using PCC, keywords, subject headings, and synonyms such as "opportunities”, “challenges”, “implementation", "scale up", “community-based”, “health promotion”, “exercise program”, and " older adults" to discover appropriate resources in the databases. The set of keywords and search terms produced are as listed in Table  . The search terms within related keywords were combined using Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT), which were tailored to the syntax of each database. The search strategy produced are as shown in Table  . 
The electronic databases searched for relevant studies, included EBSCO Host Medline, The Cochrane Library, and Scopus. Filter applied to the results of the online database search for articles were published in English language and within January 2013 to December 2023. The inclusion and exclusion criteria for the search is as outlined in Table  . 

## Stage 3: Selection of studies to be included
The search term from the second stage was employed to search databases, retrieving the results, which were exported in RIS format and subsequently uploaded into Rayyan AI. Duplicate results were then eliminated. A three-step procedure was used for the screening and selection of articles. In the first step, two reviewers (SGS and DKAS) went through every title from the database search results. All publications regarding implementation dynamics for scaling up a community-based health promotion and exercise program in older adults were taken into consideration. The second step involved a blinded screening among the two reviewers (SGS and DKAS) who worked individually to screen through the selected titles and abstracts from the first step of studies that are potentially relevant to the objectives of the present review. The reviewers collaborated in order to compare the findings and resolve differences. Similar to the second step, the third step involved both reviewers working independently to filter through all the retrieved full text articles. Unavailable full papers online, were obtained via the university library. The full-text articles were reviewed to ensure they met the objectives. Upon completion, reviewers convened to compare the results and address any disagreements. Microsoft Excel was used to record the results of the search for relevant articles, whilst articles that did not meet the objectives were removed. The PRISMA-ScR (Extension for Scoping Reviews) checklist was used as the guide to the PRISMA full review process. The procedure for selection is as shown in Fig.  . In this scoping review, we did not involve expert consultation. 

## Results

## Stage 4: Charting the information from the selected studies
A PRISMA flowchart used in the paper selection process for this scoping review is depicted in Fig.  . Once duplicates were eliminated, searches across the three databases produced 6130 articles. The majority of these papers did not meet the inclusion criteria after screening the titles and abstracts of the publications. Initially, 251 publications were identified as potentially relevant; however, 234 of them were subsequently rejected because the articles were not related to the implementation of a community-based health promotion and exercise program in older adults, leaving 17 papers to review. The extracted data are presented in Table   and include study design, population age, nation, name of the program and its duration, as well as the opportunities and challenges associated with the implementation program based on the RE-AIM framework. The RE-AIM framework [ ] was developed to tackle the problem of slow and unequal translation of scientific advancements into practice, especially in public health and policy-making contexts [ ]. RE-AIM serves as a planning and evaluation model, emphasizing five crucial dimensions; Reach, Effectiveness, Adoption, Implementation, and Maintenance, that are essential for program effectiveness and sustainability [ ].

At this point, the emphasis was to compile, analyse, and present the acquired data. To complete this stage, important findings from selected studies were assembled, identified opportunities and challenges data was arranged accordingly, aligned with the RE-AIM framework consolidating similar issues and discarding overlapping findings in Table  . ## Discussion
To the best of our knowledge, this scoping review is the first of its kind focusing on the opportunities and challenges of implementation for scale up of a community-based health promotion and exercise program among older adults. Restructuring the opportunities and challenges using the RE-AIM framework revealed that generally similar and overlapping factors emerged in various reports across different stages, influencing the dynamics of scaled-up community-based health promotion and exercise programs for older adults.
The successful implementation of a scaled-up health promotion and exercise program hinges on the initial fundamental step of “reaching” the targeted groups. Active community engagement is crucial for successful implementation and long-term effectiveness of health promotion programs [ , ], while addressing participants needs can enhance engagement and overall program outcomes [ , ]. However, recruitment remains challenging due to health conditions, COVID-19 restrictions, socioeconomic disparities, and limited awareness among older adults and practitioners [ , , , ]. Moreover, the lack of diversity in participant characteristics raises concerns about representativeness and highlights the importance of subtle cultural adaptations, particularly in rural settings [ ].
Recruitment and retention difficulties are often more pronounced among minorities and marginalized groups, emphasising that implementation strategies cannot be assumed to work uniformly across populations [ , ]. To ensure equitable access, programs may require culturally relevant adaptations, flexible scheduling, and tailored communication strategies that address the needs of ethnic minorities, socioeconomically disadvantaged communities, and rural populations [ , , ]. Purposeful recruitment and equity-focused approaches can help overcome these challenges, reduce the risk of excluding vulnerable groups, and ultimately strengthen the scalability and long-term impact of such programs [ , , , ].
Next, through program evaluation, areas for further development can be identified while also constructively measuring the interventions' “effectiveness” [ ]. Monitoring program adherence allows researchers and practitioners to identify those requiring additional support and develop interventions addressing barriers to nonadherence, thereby promoting successful compliance [ ]. While, tailoring interventions based on baseline predictors of adherence related to cognition, mobility, and functional status among older adults enhances program success [ ]. The complex design of health promotion programs targeting older adults, especially in promoting physical activity and exercise, faces challenges such as participant burden, resource intensity, evaluation complexity, and implementation intricacies [ ]. Strategies to get around these obstacles and make the interventions workable include simplifying the process, tailoring, conducting pilot test and involving stakeholders [ ]. Program sensitivity and adherence are best supported through community engagement, culturally tailored interventions, and ongoing responsiveness to local needs [ , , ]. Stakeholder involvement across the entire program cycle, from design to evaluation ensures contextual relevance, acceptability, and sustainability [ – ]. Collaboration with stakeholders and older adults can help anticipate barriers, foster ownership, and improve both short- and long-term adherence [ ], while social interaction in group settings further motivates regular participation [ ]. Nevertheless, few studies have rigorously assessed the long-term effectiveness or scalability of these strategies across diverse populations, highlighting the need for longitudinal and multi-site evaluations to guide policy, resource allocation, and the sustainable scale-up of community-based programs for older adults.
In examining the 'adoption' domain within the RE-AIM framework for community-based programs targeting older adults, robust regional community collaboration with local stakeholders and populations particularly facilitated implementation [ , – , , ]. However, sustaining these partnerships may encounter challenges such as power dynamics, trust issues, sustainability concerns, resource allocation, and alignment of goals between academic, healthcare professionals and community partners [ , ]. To overcome these challenges, it's important to maintain clear communication, fair cooperation, and mutual respect, ensuring a balance of priorities and long-term commitment [ ]. Additionally, assessing community readiness is crucial in developing culturally sensitive and sustainable program designs, ensuring interventions are tailored to community needs, thereby improving adherence, cost-effectiveness, and health outcomes [ ]. There is also a need for capacity building which consist of providing practitioners with training and support to improve their skills and knowledge [ ], as well as developing resource mobilization abilities through partnerships to secure funding for program implementation and sustainability [ , ].
The effective approach to “implementation” of evidence-based programs or interventions to a wider audience firstly depends on the establishment of dissemination strategy [ ]. The establishment of this strategy aims to ensure that programming guidelines are delivered with fidelity [ ] as well as achieving the desired impact on the populations [ ]. Secondly, tailoring the program for older adults improves its relevance, acceptance, and efficacy, thereby increasing participation, social integration, and ultimately, health outcomes [ , ]. Thirdly, despite adaptation efforts, challenges remain in diverse settings, necessitating delivery partners to carefully balance program adaptation with intervention fidelity to achieve desired outcomes [ ]. For instance, the LIFT program allows for adaptability, including translation into other languages and customization for different proficiency levels, to broaden its reach within the community [ ]. Fourthly, resource constraints and inadequate infrastructure present significant challenges to delivering scaled-up programs, particularly in meeting growing demand and ensuring sustainability [ ]. However, addressing these challenges can enable the establishment of sufficient infrastructure in rural areas, improve access to resources [ ], ensure long-term sustainability [ ], and facilitate the expansion of services to remote or rural audiences by translating both content and delivery mechanisms [ ]. Next, providing high-quality training to the coordinators is essential not only for enhancing competency but also for promoting implementation fidelity, fostering motivation and confidence, overcoming obstacles, and serving as a mechanism for maintaining high standards [ ]. Additionally, environmental challenges such as extreme weather can impede program implementation by impacting accessibility, safety during outdoor activities, and transportation, necessitating adjustments to scheduling, venues, or delivery strategies to mitigate these issues [ ]. Finally, well-defined roles among stakeholders enhance clarity, reduce misunderstandings, and contribute to the success of the program [ ].
In regard to the ‘maintenance’ domain within the RE-AIM framework, financial sustainability, funding management, technical support, system-level interventions and participant retention were the main findings in this review. The primary concern regarding sustainability was the challenge of securing adequate funding over extended periods [ ], with limited funding in certain regions hindering the training of new coordinators and thereby jeopardizing program retention [ ]. To address this issue, it is crucial to develop strategies ensuring funding management, sustainable funding streams, explore innovative funding sources, and prioritize long-term sustainability planning [ ]. Additionally, the availability of resources for system-level interventions and technical support should be ensured. Clarifying the role of the program coordinator in managing time pressures, providing booster sessions, conducting performance evaluations, and identifying ongoing training needs are also essential [ ]. Moreover, when addressing retention challenges, it's common for programs aimed at older adults to struggle with less-than-ideal adherence. Community collaboration, implementation planning, ongoing coordinator training, tailored interventions, and multi-sector involvement are factors influencing program sustainability [ , , ], as discussed across different domains of the RE-AIM framework. Additionally, adherence [ ], accessibility [ , ], community network collaboration [ ], tailoring, adaptation [ ], and flexibility [ ] are also crucial for program sustainability.
Several limitations should be acknowledged when interpreting the findings of this scoping review. Although the RE-AIM framework provided a useful structure for synthesizing evidence from the 17 included studies, the small body of literature limits the ability to draw definitive conclusions. The studies varied considerably in design, reporting quality, and program context, which complicates cross-study comparisons and weakens the consistency of insights across RE-AIM domains. Moreover, most of the available evidence originates from high-income countries, with limited representation from low- and middle-income settings, thereby constraining the generalizability of the findings. In addition, inadequate reporting on implementation processes in several studies hindered the extraction of detailed information on opportunities and challenges. Together, these limitations accentuate the need for more rigorous, contextually diverse, and transparently reported implementation research to strengthen the evidence base and guide the effective scale-up of community-based health programs for older adults.

## Conclusion
In this scoping review, we delineated opportunities and challenges using the RE-AIM framework associated with the implementation dynamics of scaling up community-based health promotion and exercise programs for older adults. Addressing these opportunities and challenges comprehensively is imperative to ensure the successful implementation and long-term sustainability of such programs, promoting overall health and independence among older adults.
