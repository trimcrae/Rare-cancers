The location carried special significance as the University Teaching Hospital of Zambia had established its inaugural stroke unit in the country and showcased the early success in patient outcomes. This was the second hybrid format following the 2023 conference which was held in Ibadan, Nigeria after the first two conferences in 2021 and 2022 were held virtually because of the COVID19 pandemic. The hybrid format facilitated participation from 80 in-person attendees and 95 virtual participants, with full session recordings accessible to African Stroke Organization (ASO) members. Notable dignitaries included Zambia’s Ministry of Health Permanent Secretary, Dr. Kennedy Lishimpi.

## Introduction
Data on the global burden of stroke over the last three decades continues to show increasing incidence of stroke and stroke-related mortality [ ]. However, there is a significant disparity between high-income countries (HICs) and the low-income countries (LICs) when it comes to how this burden is distributed across the globe, both in terms of its size and the pace of its growth, affecting LICs negatively. While HICs have registered reducing stroke incidence, the age-standardized mortality and stroke-related disability-adjusted life years were reported to be 3.6 and 3.7 times higher, respectively, in lower- and middle-income countries (LMICs) compared to HICs [ ]. Africa has experienced the brunt of this health crisis, with amongst the highest stroke incidence, prevalence, and mortality worldwide, and stroke typically occurs at a younger age in Africa, exacting a much higher price in society.
The ASOC was launched in 2021 as the continent’s preeminent scientific stroke conference to address this burden through translational research, capacity building and advocacy – primarily by African researchers and clinicians in the African continent and in the diaspora. The past ASOC events have consistently met the intended goals by providing a forum for highlighting local stroke research output and a rich environment for extensive collaboration with prominent scientific institutions and experts and creating opportunities for networking and professional development.

## African stroke organization conference 2024 proceedings
The ASOC 2024 was held November 4 to 5, 2024 in Lusaka, Zambia. This hybrid event was also presented on a virtual platform where both live and pre-recorded presentations were hosted. This was the second hybrid event after the 2023 conference that was held in Ibadan, Nigeria – whereas the first two conferences were held virtually because of the COVID-19 pandemic. The event opened with the singing of the Zambian national anthem, followed by a cultural performance featuring a local band and dancers, and a flag ceremony representing the countries of the attending delegates. This was followed by two days of scientific presentations and discussions. The conference included a World Stroke Organization (WSO) hands-on training workshop on stroke management and the visit to the newly opened Acute Stroke Unit at the University Teaching Hospital of Zambia. The conference was organized through the efforts of a Zambian team led by Dr. Deanna Saylor, in collaboration with planning committee members representing various institutions, professional societies, and academic organizations. (See below for list of ASOC 2024 collaborating Institutions).

## Conference planning committee
Prof. Mayowa Owolabi (chair) and Dr Mehari Gebreyohanns (Cochair) led the Conference Program Planning Committee (PPC). The ASOC chairs have conducted successful conferences (e.g. International Stroke Conference, African Neurorehabilitation Conference) in the past and both have extensive research and leadership experience. Prof. Owolabi and Gebreyohanns have an established working relationship in authoring peer-reviewed publications and co-leading NINDS grants, as well as a long track record of mentoring trainees and junior faculty on published papers and funded trainee research. In addition, members of the PPC (See on list of program planning committee) have extensive stroke research experience in Africa with outstanding publication records. Each PPC member is an established scientist with complementary expertise and experience in key areas of advancing stroke research and practice in Africa. The PPC and its members developed the conference agenda and designed its implementation, identified scholars, graded submitted abstracts, reviewed evaluation reports, made recommendations, facilitated discussions, and led individual fora at the conference. Additional individuals were sought to serve as ASOC collaborators and facilitators throughout the planning process. Several meetings (12x) since May 2024 were held to strengthen program activities prior to the conference days with impressive participation and attendance by all PPC.

## Conference announcement and dissemination
ASOC was publicized via an extensive network of communication, including via electronic mailings, the ASO Website, ASO quarterly webinars, and social media networks. The PPC members also used their wide network of affiliations with professional societies and academic institutions to disseminate the announcements. Announcements of the meeting were sent to teaching hospitals throughout Africa, including the University Teaching Hospital in Lusaka.

## The Osuntokun award lecture
The Osuntokun Award Lecture honored scientists whose work reflects a lifelong commitment to stroke research in Africa. The 2024 lecture, titled Africa’s Quest for a Masterstroke Against Stroke, was delivered by Prof. Kolawole W. Wahab of the College of Health Sciences, University of Ilorin, Nigeria.

## Hot topics session
This year’s hot topics session featured highlights from stroke meetings conducted across various part of the globe. Presentations were received from Prof. Tudor Jovin (Chair, International Stroke Conference, Prof. Robin Lemmens (European Stroke Organization), Prof. Ramani (Asian – Pacific Stroke Organization) and Prof. Ossama Mansour (Middle East North Africa Stroke and Interventional Neuro-Organization - MENASINO). This one hour and fifteen minutes discussion, moderated by (Dr Laura Benjamin, University College London, UK) highlighted various activities undertaken by each organization; conference statistics (attendance etc.), awards, spotlight presentations and findings, contextrelevant recommendations and future directions.

## Lancet commission on stroke session
The 2024 ASOC featured the second in its series titled ‘Lancet Commission on Stroke. This session, moderated by Prof Mayowa Owolabi (lead co-chair, The WSO-Lancet Commission on Stroke) and Prof Jeyaraj Pandian (President, WSO) showcased latest updates across its four pillars namely: acute care (Prof Sheila Martins - Brazil), Rehabilitation (Prof Jeyaraj Pandian - India), Prevention (Prof Bo Norrving - Sweden) and Surveillance (Assoc. Prof Seana Gall – Australia).

## Travel awards
Travel scholarships were awarded to 11 Young Investigators from the African continent courtesy of NIH funding. We received 74 abstracts eligible for consideration from early career African investigators, with the majority residing in Africa, with very few from residents outside the continent. The abstracts were evaluated by the PPC for scientific rigor, innovation and relevance and ranked accordingly. The top eleven 11 presented full oral presentations; the remaining 56 were presentations in poster format which were displayed on Parade in the virtual conference hall, where they could be viewed at any time, and, for those attending the conference in-person, physically at the conference venue, too. Best presenter awards for outstanding work were presented to Dr. Nana Boakye Agyeman Badu-Prempeh (Ghana) for oral presentation and Dr. Eitzaz Sadiq (South Africa) for poster presentations.

A total of 175 registrants attended the ASOC 2024 – 80 were inperson attendees while 95 participated virtually, with participants joining from countries including the United Kingdom, Australia, India, Brazil, and across Africa. Participants had access to ask questions, whether attending in person or online. Full session recordings have been made accessible to conference participants.

## Discussion
The organizing principles for the African Stroke Organization Conference have always been based on four key operational pillars: stroke research, capacity building, development of stroke services, and advocacy and collaboration with stakeholders [ ]. These principles are consistent with the ASOC 2024 theme of Strategies in Reducing the Burden of Stroke linked to the rising stroke burden in Africa, which is expected to grow significantly due to lifestyle changes, increasing urbanization, and insufficient healthcare infrastructure [ ]. The conference followed the release of the Lancet Neurology Commission’s report, which highlighted concerns that it will be impossible to meet the Sustainable Development Goal to reduce the burden of NCDs by one-third in 2030 given the very fast pace of growth of stroke and its associated burden of disability in LMICs [ ].
Despite the growing health burden, Africa contributes disproportionately little to global stroke research. Scholars from the continent produce less than 1 % of the world’s research output, and much of this research is heavily reliant on collaborations with HICs institutions [ ]. The lack of intra- and inter-African collaboration further complicates efforts to implement evidence-based practices regionally. To address this, there is a concerted push to create career development pathways and strengthen local research capacities, with organizations like the Global Forum for Health Research emphasizing the need for fostering African talent in stroke research [ ]. The ASOC continues to serve as a platform to address these challenges [ ].
Just like preceding conferences, ASOC 2024 proved to be a platform for African researchers, enabling networking opportunities and fostering collaboration both within Africa and globally. Career development was a key focus, with sessions offering young scholars the chance to connect with mentors, explore funding opportunities, and receive feedback on their work. These initiatives support the development of research capacity in Africa and contribute to efforts to address the increasing burden of stroke.
ASOC continues to facilitate stroke research and advocacy efforts in Africa. The 2024 conference maintained ASOC’s commitment to building stroke research, developing stroke services, and advocating for policy changes. It also focused on emerging topics like Digital Health and Artificial Intelligence and Interventional Neurology. Implementation Science was an important feature of this conference which provided the ‘how to’ of establishing stroke therapy in a resource-limited country such as Zambia. The conference’s dedication to career development, especially through the Vascular Brain Trust and Moving On Up sessions, is helping bridge the gap in African stroke research by providing young scholars with mentorship and opportunities for professional growth. We can confidently state that ASOC 2024 has reaffirmed its role as a leading conference for stroke research in Africa, setting the stage for continued success and innovation in the years to come.

## Challenges, benefits and future directions
Organizing the ASOC has presented several challenges, despite the success of the past three sessions. The vast size of the continent with its varying languages, the wide distribution of presenters across various time zones, and the organization’s limited financial and technical resources continue to present logistical challenges. However, these obstacles are expected to be overcome as ASOC increases its membership, gains publicity, and attracts more funding and support from collaborators. The conference’s success in its initial years has laid a strong foundation for future iterations to build upon. ASOC has attracted interest for collaboration from organizations and foundations with common interests which could help the conference build more logistical resources.
Looking forward, ASOC aims to further invigorate stroke research and education in Africa, continuing to serve as the premiere scientific stroke conference on the continent. After holding past conferences in the western and southern parts of the continent, future conferences will be held in other parts of the continent to reflect the diversity of experiences and to showcase successful research and clinical progresses by Africans in those countries. One of ASOC’s key strengths will remain its diversity in ideas, committee composition, and speaker lineup. The inclusion of " " sections and invitations to top experts from leading global stroke organizations like the International Stroke Conference and World Stroke Congress continues to enrich the experience for attendees. We will continue to strive to ensure the makeup of ASOC presenters increasingly represents faculty and researchers from the African continent.
Future ASOC events will continue to incorporate more hands-on elements, such as stroke care teaching courses and workshops, as they were highly attended, and the attendees indicated a high level of interest in those activities. It was widely felt that the two-day conference was densely packed with too many activities. Future designing of the conference will take this concern into consideration.
The conferences will continue to bring together key stakeholders in the field, aiming to identify knowledge gaps, set priorities for stroke prevention and care, and accelerate the translation of research findings into real-world applications. By focusing on these goals, ASOC hopes to enhance stroke outcomes across Africa and continue to position itself as the leader in advancing stroke care and research on the continent.
