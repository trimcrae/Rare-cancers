## Abstract

## Background
Chronic myelogenous leukaemia (CML) is a pathological clinical condition with a yearly incidence between 10 and 15 cases per million people, 14 in Italy. Its incidence increases with age, reaching 20–25 yearly cases per million individuals in people over 70 years. A growing importance has been given to the need of a multidisciplinary approach (MDA) for the management of patients with CML.

## Objective
To analyse the importance of MDA as compared with the current Italian standard of care for the management of CML patients based on the involvement of several health professional figures.

## Methods
A group of healthcare professionals from several healthcare structures were gathered in a first Advisory Board (AB) and divided into as many groups as the number of belonging health structures representative of the Italian provision of therapeutic approaches for CML. In a second AB, the results were validated by the same panel of experts.

## Results
The number of dedicated health professionals within the dedicated ward ranged from 1 to 13. Most structures rely on several professionals intervening only in case of necessity. Only one centre provides a booking service based on clinical needs to avoid waiting times. Most centres report there is basically no disagreement in the definition of the clinical pathway and there is a high adherence to national and international CML guidelines.

## Conclusions
The development of forms of interorganisational and interprofessional coordination to improve the diagnosis and the treatment of CML patients have been for long on the agenda of policy makers, managers and professionals for more than 20 years. This analysis represents a starting reference to consider for the evaluation of an MDA in the Italian context.

## WHAT IS ALREADY KNOWN ON THIS TOPIC
A growing importance has been given in recent times to the necessity of a multidisciplinary approach for the management of patients with chronic myelogenous leukaemia allowing to assist the patient during all the phases of the disease, improving the response to treatments and promoting timely access to rehabilitative and supportive therapies.

## WHAT THIS STUDY ADDS
in Italy, the multidisciplinary approach is not yet widely spread uniformly in all regions. The multidisciplinary teams, although formally constituted in several regional contexts, lack in terms of uniformity, members and methods for the identification of the best therapeutic pathway.

## HOW THIS STUDY MIGHT AFFECT RESEARCH, PRACTICE OR POLICY
The heterogeneity found in the current clinical practice should be considered in the implementation of further multidisciplinary teams to improve and extend the experiences conducted in the Italian setting.

## Background

## Chronic myelogenous leukaemia
Chronic myelogenous leukaemia (CML) is a pathological clinical condition determined by the uncontrolled monoclonal proliferation of a single multipotent cell affected by the leukaemogenic event. The disease might involve the myeloid, monocytic, erythroid, megakaryocyte and sometimes the lymphoid compartments. The years 2005–2010 have seen a radical change in the therapy and prognosis of CML, obtained through the newly acquired knowledge on disease’s molecular pathogenesis; the improvements achieved were compared with the discovery of antibiotics in the treatment of bacteria-caused diseases. Unlike acute leukaemias and many other cancers, the causes of CML are unknown, not associable to genetic, environmental, infectious or nutritional factors; CML is not a familial, neither hereditary nor transmissible disease and there are no known factors predisposing to its development. The cause is to be found in a clonal abnormality of a myeloid stem cell. CML is one of the first diseases for which it was possible to identify a specific chromosomal abnormality as the cause of the pathology: the Philadelphia chromosome (or Ph) reported in about 95% of all patients affected by CML but also in 30%–50% of adult patients with acute lymphoblastic leukaemia. CM’s yearly incidence ranges between 10 and 15 new cases per million people, 14 in Italy. There are no appreciable differences across countries and ethnicities and its onset is generally very rare among children (one case per million individuals yearly) and adolescents (two cases per million individuals yearly). The frequency increases with age, reaching 20–25 yearly cases per million individuals in people over 70 years. The incidence among industrialised countries is 2 cases per 100 000 people/year; concerning patients’ sex, the most affected individuals are males (ratio M:F=2: 1). It affects every age and 20%–25% of cases are over the age of 60. The median age at diagnosis is 45–50 years. In Italy, there are about 800–1000 new cases every year and the total number of patients is about 10 000, constantly increasing. 

## The multidisciplinary approach
A growing importance has been given in recent times to the necessity of a multidisciplinary approach (MDA) for the management of patients with CML : the multiprofessional management in oncology allows to assist the patient during all the phases characterising the disease, improves the response to treatments, promotes timely access to rehabilitative and supportive therapies and allows an effective management of any recurrence. The collaboration of teams composed by urologists, radiotherapist oncologists, medical oncologists, pathologists, radiologists, nuclear doctors, psychologists but also rehabilitators, andrologists, physiotherapists, palliative therapies’ experts, if well organised and structured, allows the patient to be placed at the centre of the therapeutic path. Furthermore, the MDA offers objective and non-contradictory information on the options available for the management of patients’ illnesses and avoids the need of multiple consultations. MDA has been given an increasing importance by health specialists for years through an increasing number of scientific publications in medical journals. In Europe, the situation varies among national settings, with Countries, such as France, Germany, Holland, UK, where the management of oncologic patients through a multidisciplinary and multiprofessional approach is already the state of the art. In Italy, there is an increasing attention to the opportunity and need to reorganise the assistance considering multidisciplinary teams but such process is still in progress and deserves the promotion of a cultural change as well as an organisational support.
Regardless of the chosen form of inter-organisational coordination to achieve the integration of professional skills, there is a series of operational coordination needs required for the daily provision of the therapeutic strategies to patients. The literature on this topic, particularly relevant considering the scarcity of resources characterising the current healthcare delivery systems, is full of examples and evidence underlining the impact that structured forms of teamwork are able to provide for the improvement of patient care and the appropriate allocation of resources within the patient care pathways. These organisational forms, and the literature that analysed them, come primarily from the US setting and, recently, from the evidence developed in the British National Health Service (NHS) care setting. 

These evidences include the so-called Tumour Board (TB), or cancer conference, whose aim is to involve multiple medical specialties (radiology, surgery, general pathology, oncological radiotherapy) in the management of patients affected by CML in order to appropriately evaluate both chronic cases and newly diagnosed individuals. 

TBs have shifted their focus over time towards treatment planning as a method of building consensus on diagnostic and management decisions.
With the development of strategies for fighting cancer based on multidimensional therapies, the coordination of care has evolved towards a proactive structure involving a wide range of specialists. More recently, forms of virtual TB (VTB) realised among different clinical centres in video-conference were tested. 

The natural evolution of TB (or VTB) is the multidisciplinary team (MDT), a form of clinical and organisational coordination characterised by the promotion of collaborative and uninterrupted consultations along the therapeutic pathway. This approach allows to better consider patients’ psychosocial needs in the development of care pathways’ protocols to achieve wider reference networks both for assistance and for research. Two key elements distinguish the MDT from the TB are: the adoption of a team structure and the involvement of patients in the definition of the therapeutic pathway. The team structure is an integral part of the effective functioning of the multidisciplinary care, given the need to communicate and collaborate among different clinical disciplines. Patients’ involvement in MDT is essential to ensure patient-centred care and the correct understanding by patients and families of the diagnosis and treatment options available.
MDTs are defined by the European Partnership for Action Against Cancer (EPAAC) as an alliance of all medical and healthcare professionals related to a specific tumour disease whose approach to cancer care is guided by their willingness to agree on evidence-based clinical decisions and to co-ordinate the delivery of care at all stages of the process, encouraging patients to assume an active role in the definition of their care. 

The British NHS National Cancer Action Team in 2009 identified 86 essential characteristics for the effectiveness of a well-structured MDT. These elements are systematically grouped into four crucial macro domains ( ). 

 The MDT is based, rather than on a set of tools to be implemented, on a cultural sharing about the superiority of the systemic approach to management of care over the more traditional decision-making model centred on the science and conscience of each professional. According to EPAAC, there are five essential elements for the overall effectiveness of the MDT:
Clear objectives of assistance, shared within the members of the MDT and patients, able to cover issues related to diagnosis, treatment and survival.
The internal organisation of the MDT which establishes the appropriate forms of clinical leadership and operational coordination, identifies a single point of contact for patients and families, systematically uses the comparison (benchmarking) to integrate new knowledge in a timely manner and provides resources for doctors and other healthcare professionals.
Shared databases capable of recording clinical decisions, results and indicators, facilitating the evaluation of progress and the identification of areas for improvement.
A patient-centred approach based on available and understandable information on the clinical and psychosocial aspects of the care process, clear communication channels between the patient care group and family members capable of promoting engagement.
Clear support from national and regional health authorities, scientific societies and patient organisations, in order to integrate the action of the MDT in the context of institutional and scientific initiatives dedicated to patients affected by a specific disease.
The aspects here identified highlight how an MDT bases its functionality and effectiveness on two fundamental issues:
The availability of professionals’ excellent skills both from a technical-scientific and behavioural point of view suitable for fostering positive social relations.
While considering the involvement of individual team members as crucial as a result of a personal awareness of the importance of cooperation among professionals, it is through a progressive process that the team becomes effective. The MDT, consequently, needs to progressively codify roles, skills, leadership, operational standards, relational models that flow into organisational codes (procedures, check-lists, etc). 

## From organisation to outcomes: the available evidence
The effectiveness of the several forms of coordination to data implemented has been tested in numerous healthcare settings. Some studies have shown how the adoption of the aforementioned forms of coordination have led to a real organisational transformation, resulting in improvements in the quality of the decision-making processes. Few studies have taken the next step by estimating the impact on the gain in terms of clinical endpoints (eg, absolute survival) following the adoption of an MDT or TB. Bumm et al
 showed the progress following the institutionalisation of a daily TB thanks to the availability of a unified database in various German centres. The team had developed treatment guidelines for stages of oesophageal cancer pathology based on the analysis of preoperative risks by prospectively analysing the psychological stress of over 6000 patients. The study highlighted how the adoption of these solutions increased the compliance between treatment decision and implementation of the clinical path, and verified how the opinions chosen during TB changed the decisions on the path of care of patients with obvious clinical and psychological improvements.
Also noteworthy is the study by Lamb et al
 based on a sample of 1421 patients with prostate cancer between December 2009 and April 2011, before and after the implementation of organisational solutions aimed to improve the quality of work among groups to accelerate the clinical decision process for a more timely treatment associated with the development of written protocols and on-the-job training for operators. All results improved considerably after the organisational transformation.
Over 50 studies are included in a review of the evidence on the organisation and impact of MDTs on cancer treatments from November 2005 to June 2012. MDTs have produced clinical and process-related results showing that the adoption of these forms of coordination are associated with changes in the clinical diagnostic and therapeutic decision-making process.

## Objective
This study aims to analyse the importance of the MDA as compared with the current Italian standard of care for the management of CML patients based on the involvement of several health professional figures (ie, cardiologist, angiologist, pulmonologist; psycho-oncologist, nurse case manager) through the realisation of two advisory boards (ABs) aimed at determining, at a national level, the main key performance indicators (KPIs) characterising the care pathway. Both approaches will be analysed in order to assess the possible convenience for the National Health System, in terms of patients’ outcome, of implementing a care pathway based on the involvement of several professional figures.

## Methods

## Health professionals involvement
A group of healthcare professionals belonging to five regional context ( ) of the Italian NHS involved in the provision of care strategies aimed at the management of patients suffering from CML, were gathered in a first AB (7 November 2019) and divided into as many groups as the number of the belonging health structures representative of the Italian provision of therapeutic approaches for CML:
 Policlinic Sant'Orsola-Malpighi (Bologna).
Azienda Ospedaliero-Universitaria Città della Salute e della Scienza (Turin).
Fondazione IRCCS Ca' Granda Ospedale Maggiore Policlinico-Clinica Regina Elena (Milan).
Ospedale San Bortolo (Vicenza).
Grande Ospedale Metropolitano Bianchi-Melacrino-Morelli (Reggio Calabria).
Istituto Europeo di Oncologia (Milan).
Ospedale San Bortolo (Vicenza).
The health professionals taking part to the AB were mainly haematologist physicians, but the teams were also composed by biologists, nurses as well as clinicians belonging to other specialisations who contributed to analyse the way the activities are organised within the departments and how the team operates considering the contribution of professionals with different backgrounds.
During the first meeting, the discussion was focused on the representation by each team not only of the general therapeutic pathway and the characteristic of the structure aimed to the management of the patient affected by CML, but also the strengths and weaknesses characterising each therapeutic pathway. The professionals were assisted by a team of researchers from the Graduate School of Health Economics and Management (Alta Scuola di Economia e Management dei Sistemi Sanitari) in the determination of the KPIs to be considered for the monitoring of the differences among the therapeutic pathways, in order to define a benchmark for the assessment of the changes to implement in light of the criticalities underlined throughout the meetings.
During the second AB (2 December 2019), the health professionals from each structure presented the KPIs identified for their own therapeutic pathway and discussed such parameters with those determined by the groups belonging to the other health structures. At the end of the second meeting, the main points to be developed at a national level were identified for the purpose of comparing the different approaches in the care pathway for the management of patients with CML employed in the structures considered.

## Patient and public involvement
Patients were not involved in the analysis as the study focused on the frameworks to date in use in the Italian setting concerning the interactions among the health professionals in charge for the management of patients affected by CML. Nevertheless, the analysis entailed specific questions concerning the eventual involvement of the patient in the definition process of its therapeutic pathway.

## Results
After the first AB, the KPIs worthy of investigation underlined by the multidisciplinary teams resulted to be:
The nature and number of professionals operating in each centres’ CML department.
The procedures (meetings, activities, use of materials, etc) carried out during the first diagnosis of the pathology.
The average waiting time for the patient during a follow-up visit.
The management of possible disagreements within the multidisciplinary team.
The methods for the prioritisation of cases to be discussed by the multidisciplinary team.
The involvement of the patients in the definition of the therapeutic pathway.
The predisposition of a report on board’s decisions.
Considering the KPIs thus identified, a questionnaire was predisposed in order for the health professionals to identify the value of each of the parameters considered in the analysis representative of their clinical practice. During the second AB, the professionals from each health structure were asked to present in a brief introduction the main characteristics of the belonging membership body.
The teams from the several health structured were asked to represent the number and nature of figures operating within each department differentiating those dedicated to the department’s activities from those intervening in case of necessity but belonging to other departments ( ).
 From this analysis, it emerges that the department with the highest number of health professionals is the one belonging to Policlinic Sant'Orsola-Malpighi. This structure is also the one with the highest number of dedicated professionals among those analysed, with only six figures intervening in case of necessity ( ).
 On the other side, the structure characterised by the smallest team is the Grande Ospedale Metropolitano Bianchi-Melacrino-Morelli with nine figures constituting the overall team, two of which are called only in case of necessity.

Most centres report there is basically no disagreement as they usually comply with national and international guidelines: the case is discussed collectively and any other investigation is planned in order to better clarify the clinical picture and standardise the decision. All the centres schedule a subsequent evaluation in order to assess the results of the chosen therapeutic approach. In case of disagreement on the therapeutic plan, pros and cons are discussed in order to achieve a final decision.
Furthermore, the survey proposed investigated on whether the clinicians employ a prioritisation method to face the cases to be discussed. The respondents agree that all the necessary cases are discussed without a specific process of prioritisation. Moreover, the centres agree on the need of a prioritisation based on clinical urgency. In general, there is no misalignment between the answers to this specific question, considering the heterogeneity of the Regions included in the analysis.
The results concerning patient/caregiver’s participation at the board meeting are also shown in : in case of negative answer, the professionals indicate the figure that communicates the board’s decision to the patient/caregiver. Among the centres that participated in the survey, only one centre provides for patient involvement in the board meeting. In addition, in all centres the figure that communicates the board’s decision to the patient/caregiver is a clinician. It is therefore clear that the communication with the patient regarding the aspects related to the treatment is up to the specialist doctor.
As to the eventual predisposition of a report concerning board’s decisions, the analysis shows that over 50% of the centres involved prepare a report on the strategies the board decided to select to manage patients’ diseases.

## Conclusions
The development of forms of interorganisational and interprofessional coordination to improve the diagnosis and the treatment of patients with cancer have been on the agenda of policy makers, managers and professionals for more than 20 years. The evidence is consistent in showing positive consequences for the management of patients in multiple dimensions, which should encourage the development of structured multidisciplinary care, minimum standards and the exchange of best practices. The present analysis showed as the multidisciplinary teams, although formally constituted in several regional contexts, they lack in terms of uniformity, members by which the teams are composed and methods through which the best therapeutic pathway is identified. Thus, we cannot say that everything that could have been done was achieved, at least not in Italy. In recent years, much attention has been paid to the development of forms of inter-organisational coordination which have seen the main development driver in regional health authorities. The development of oncological networks in Italy has certainly been characterised by a new impetus following the introduction of recent guidelines in the period 2014–2015 (see the Technical Guidance Document to reduce the burden of cancer: Years 2012–14 and the DM n. 70/2015) even if since 2000 some Regions have formalised network solutions. The current panorama, however, shows an extreme territorial variability in the adoption of regulatory guidelines on the topic and in the implementation of organisational coordination solutions well known in the literature and associated with robust evidence about their effectiveness. At the same time, however, even in the most advanced contexts, we do not have robust empirical evidence about the real impact of formalised cancer networks on the real transformation of care pathways. Compared with other countries, there are no published studies on the real capacity of oncological networks and other forms of coordination in modifying decision-making processes. It seems that to date the focus has been posed mainly on the institutional and formal aspects of cancer networks and we have witnessed long phases of planning, replanning and negotiation to reach a network structure in which all centres can find an adequate location. These long negotiation processes focus the attention on the creation of an effective community of practices capable of promoting the exchange of knowledge, tools, resources and solutions. Noteworthy is the absence of patients/caregivers’ involvement in the board meeting with the only exception of the Grande Ospedale Metropolitano in Reggio Calabria. In view of a greater patients’ participation in the therapy definition process, this aspect should be taken into consideration in order to offer patients/caregivers the opportunity to better understand the chosen treatment path and increase their commitment.
In light of these considerations, there are to date two lines of activities to focus on: in the first place, it seems necessary to shift the focus from institutional to organisational, social and relational dynamics in order to foster the emergence of a culture of mutual trust, which leads to effective organisational structuring processes. The willingness to collaborate and stick to a decision-making approach based on the sharing of scientific evidence, including those related to the care experiences of patients, are the basis for this cultural transition that is already partially underway. The regional resolution and the consequent formal organisational design that identifies bodies, structures, information networks and responsibilities is only the first step of a path that in many cases is still long.
In this context, however, it is necessary to engage a second and perhaps more important process of sharing and contamination. It is unacceptable that in many parts of the Country, what are now consolidated practices elsewhere, are still the exception.
For this reason, it is essential to recall the role of the National Government and the collaboration between the regions so that the results achieved in some parts of the Country can spread rapidly by identifying the barriers to be broken down and the opportunities to be seized. This is not only a responsibility for national and regional health policies, but above all the objective that a professional community must face to equally support affected people in every part of Italy.
The analysis conducted is characterised by several limitations: first, if on one side the health professionals considered belonged to the main centres in the Italian setting, on the other side, the analysis should be improved by considering the structures belonging to the regional contexts that were not here analysed. This aspect is particularly important considering that the results of the present analysis might be considered for the dissemination of an awareness on the MDA subject to achieve a good sensibilisation to the matter by a significant share of professionals operating in the Italian context.
Furthermore, the recourse to an MDA increases the awareness by the team for the management of CML patients regarding the pharmacological alternatives available in the reference setting, whose selection can thus be based not only on the assessment of a reference physician, but also on the basis of advice from professionals with different backgrounds who contribute to define the optimal care strategy for each patient. This approach tends to reduce the recourse, charactering the Italian healthcare context, to the therapeutic cycling, namely the use in subsequent therapeutic lines of drugs already employed in previous lines and for which a loss/lack of response was experienced. The early recourse to therapies that better suit the needs of each patient also imply a higher outcome in terms of health experienced and a saving in terms of resources absorbed. Although the present analysis is characterised by the aforementioned limitations, it represents a starting reference to consider for the evaluation of an MDA in the Italian context.
