## Background
Youth physical health remains a significant challenge in global public health governance. Low sports participation among youth in China significantly impedes the progress of the Healthy China 2030 initiative. While policy tools are essential for addressing this issue, their combined effects and regional adaptability are not well understood.

## Methods
This study uses a mixed-methods approach, combining procedural grounded theory and fuzzy-set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA). We first reviewed 39 national youth physical health policies to identify four main types of policy tools: substantive, incentive, capacity-building, and symbolic and persuasive tools. Then, fsQCA was used to analyze 25 provincial cases, exploring how different combinations of policy tools influence youth participation in sports.

## Results
This paper identifies three configuration paths: Configuration 1 (substantive, incentive, and capacity-building tools) shows that these three categories cover 40.1% of the cases (consistency = 0.874). For example, in Jiangsu province, youth sports participation has been successfully increased through institutional coercion, financial incentives, and investments in facilities. Configuration 2 (substantive, incentive, and symbolic and persuasive tools) reveals that capacity-building tools are not essential (consistency = 0.973). For instance, Liaoning province replaces capacity-building tools with administrative accountability mechanisms and health communication strategies under resource constraints. Configuration 3 (symbolic and persuasive tools, capacity-building tools) indicates that a non-coercive pathway is viable (consistency = 1.000). The case in Guangdong province demonstrates that when health concepts are internalized into social norms through symbolic and persuasive tools, community sports self-organizations and digital service platforms—supported by capacity-building tools—can form and operate spontaneously, which may help reduce reliance on substantive and incentive-based tools.

## Conclusions
Chinese policymakers tend to favor substantive and incentive-based tools, which are seen as producing faster results. However, they often overlook capacity-building, along with symbolic and persuasive strategies. We suggest adopting flexible, region-specific approaches that consider local resources and cultural contexts. These insights might provide practical guidance for improving the World Health Organization’s Global Action Plan on Physical Activity across different socioeconomic settings.

## Background
Globally, adolescent physical health has emerged as a major priority for governments and social organizations. Physical inactivity and sedentary behavior (SB) are widely recognized as significant risk factors for chronic non-communicable diseases (NCDs), including cardiovascular disease, cancer, and diabetes [ ]. In response, the World Health Organization (WHO) and national health agencies have issued guidelines on recommended levels and patterns of physical activity (PA) and SB [ – ], thereby establishing an essential foundation for policy development.
China, as the most populous country, plays a critical role in shaping youth health, with significant implications for global public health. The Chinese government has long prioritized the physical well-being of young people and has introduced a series of nationwide initiatives to promote youth physical fitness. Since the founding of the People’s Republic of China, 131 central-level public policy documents on youth sports have been released, averaging 1.77 documents per year [ ]. Major initiatives include the Healthy China 2030 Plan [ ], the Healthy China Initiative (2019–2030) [ ], and Opinions on Deepening the Integration of Physical Education and Sports to Promote the Healthy Development of Young People [ ]. Collectively, these policies aim to increase youth participation in physical activity and improve overall health, ultimately helping to prevent chronic non-communicable diseases.
Despite these national efforts, Chinese youth continue to face substantial challenges to their physical health. Academic pressures, spatial constraints associated with urbanization, limited participation in sports, and weak enforcement of existing policies may impede improvements in youth health outcomes [ , ]. Moreover, disparities in resource allocation, levels of economic development, and cultural contexts across regions have led to uneven implementation and effectiveness of youth sports and health policies [ – ]. Previous studies indicate that many challenges stem from delays in policy development and adjustment, the absence of detailed implementation plans, and an overreliance on certain types of policy tools [ ]. Although numerous national-level policies have been introduced to promote youth physical health, gaps in implementation and inefficient application of individual policy tools remain [ , ]. These issues raise a central question: how can policymakers identify and integrate the most effective policy tools to strengthen youth physical fitness in China?
Policy tools are fundamental instruments for achieving policy objectives and shaping the factors that influence their effectiveness [ ]. Internationally, researchers have made substantial progress in this area [ ], drawing on theoretical approaches such as public choice theory [ ] and policy networks theory [ ]. By contrast, Chinese scholarship on policy tools, particularly in the field of youth physical health, developed relatively late. Earlier studies have primarily emphasized the definition [ ], classification [ ], and selection of specific policy tools [ , ]. For example, Cheng applied content analysis to categorize Chinese youth physical health policy tools into three groups: supply, environment, and demand [ ]. Collectively, these studies provide an initial theoretical foundation for the selection and application of youth health policy tools.
Nevertheless, important research gaps remain. Previous studies have often overlooked how policy tools operate within specific social, economic, and cultural contexts—particularly within the framework of Healthy China—and have given limited attention to the complexity and dynamic interactions of different policy tool configurations. To address these gaps, it is necessary to adopt a methodology that can generate theoretical insights directly from empirical data [ , ]. Grounded theory is well-suited to this objective, as it enables the systematic identification of key policy tools through in-depth coding and analysis of youth health policy documents [ ]. This step is crucial for developing a theoretical framework for studying youth physical health policy. Initially introduced by Glaser and Strauss in The Discovery of Grounded Theory: Strategies for Qualitative Research, grounded theory has since evolved into three primary schools: classical grounded theory, which emphasizes theory generation through induction [ ]; procedural grounded theory, which highlights the iterative process of data collection and analysis and the researcher’s role in identifying patterns [ ]; and constructivist grounded theory, which stresses subjectivity and creativity in the process of theory development [ ].
In this study, we applied procedural grounded theory to code and analyze Chinese youth physical health policy texts issued over the past two decades, thereby identifying key policy tools and their corresponding categories.
We then employed fuzzy-set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA) to examine the relationships between different combinations of policy tools and variations in youth physical health outcomes. As an asymmetric data analysis method, QCA combines the strengths of both qualitative and quantitative approaches, enabling researchers to understand how multiple configurations of conditions interact to influence outcomes [ ]. Using fsQCA, we examined 25 Chinese provinces as case studies, with policy tools serving as the conditioning variables and youth sports participation as the outcome variable. This analysis identified three main configurational pathways that highlight factors associated with higher levels of youth sports participation [ ].
This study selected youth sports participation as the outcome variable because it represents the most direct behavioral pathway through which policy tools are associated with physical fitness [ , ]. For instance, the socio-ecological model suggests that policy interventions may increase participation frequency by improving environmental conditions, such as access to sports facilities and community-level support (e.g., organizational programs), which are in turn linked to improved physical fitness [ ]. Policy environments, including legal and economic systems, are essential factors that influence regular sports participation and impact population health outcomes [ ]. This focus on sports participation aligns with the WHO’s emphasis on “weekly frequency of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity (MVPA)” as an essential behavioral indicator of physical fitness [ ]. Additionally, evidence consistently shows strong positive links between sports participation and key physical fitness metrics, including cardiorespiratory endurance, muscular strength, and body composition [ , ].
The specific objectives of this study include: (1) clarifying the basis for classifying youth physical health policy tools in China at the macro level; (2) developing a theoretical model of youth physical health policy tools in China using grounded theory; (3) further exploring the pathways and causal relationships involved in combining youth physical health policy tools in China through fsQCA analysis.
The innovation of this study lies in its systematic analysis of policy texts related to the physical fitness of Chinese youth, combined with the use of two research methods—grounded theory and fsQCA—for a mixed-methods approach. This approach offers a new methodological reference for future policy text research. Through this study, we aim to contribute to the academic literature by presenting policy cases on the development of youth physical fitness in China and providing an empirical basis for informed policymaking.

## Literature review and theoretical perspectives

## Policy research review
Regarding physical inactivity among adolescents, the policy being considered is defined as “legislative, regulatory, or policymaking actions that may affect physical activity” [ ]. For example, the Shanghai Sports Development Regulations explicitly state that schools should ensure students participate in at least one hour of physical activity per day during the school day [ ]. Effective PA policies are widely recognized as crucial for addressing low participation rates in China [ – ]. Policy formulation and implementation are dynamic processes involving different stages, all of which are recognized to promote adolescent PA and physical fitness [ ]. Enhancing youth fitness is also linked to national well-being, and sports participation as a public health strategy has attracted growing attention [ ]. For example, the International Society for Physical Activity and Health (ISPAH) released the Toronto Charter, which calls for political commitment to achieve more excellent physical activity opportunities [ ]. The Policy Evaluation Network (PEN) was established to provide Europe with tools for identifying, assessing, and comparing policies that address lifestyle behaviors contributing to overweight and obesity [ ]. Alongside the nationwide rollout of the Healthy China 2030 plan, scholars have increasingly studied youth sports policy through public policy research. With the launch of the Healthy China 2030 plan, research on youth sports policy has increased, focusing on policy development, implementation, and evaluation. Studies emphasize that effective youth health governance relies on transparent decision-making and legitimate procedures [ , , , ]. Therefore, it is essential to establish a cooperative mechanism involving all stakeholders during policy development while strengthening legal oversight to ensure adherence to democratic principles and regulatory frameworks [ , ].
Regarding policy implementation, ineffective efforts, such as interdepartmental goal conflicts and inefficiencies like fragmented resource allocation across provinces, significantly hinder youth sports participation. Addressing these requires stronger top-level design to enhance enforcement and ensure sustainable outcomes [ , ]. Regarding policy evaluation, robust evaluation systems improve policy development and implementation by facilitating continuous feedback, adjustments, and improvements [ ]. At the policy tool level, initiatives such as the ‘integration of sport and education’ aim to strengthen the role of sport in education through various mechanisms [ – ].
In summary, academic research has thoroughly examined the origins of youth physical health policy issues and their critical evaluation, encouraging innovation in methods for studying youth physical health policy tools. However, two limitations still exist in current studies: (1) a context-specific framework for classifying and applying adolescent physical health policy tools tailored to China’s sociopolitical environment, and (2) limited empirical research on how policy tool configurations interact with regional disparities to affect outcomes. This study addresses these gaps by using grounded theory to develop a localized typology of policy tools and incorporating fsQCA to identify policy tool configurations that can be adapted across regions.

## Theoretical perspectives: policy tools
Since the 1980 s, many scholars in the U.S. and around the world have used policy tools to achieve their policy objectives [ ]. Their unique and powerful ability to explain phenomena, along with their broad applicability, has made them popular in public administration and policy science [ ]. This study employs the “policy tool” of youth physical fitness as a theoretical framework to demonstrate how to translate policy goals into management actions and policy ideas into tangible results [ ].
As a vital part of policy science, scholars have classified policy tools according to various criteria. However, there has been no universally accepted standard for this classification for a long time. The first attempt was made by German economist Kirschen, who listed 64 general policy tools but did not offer a systematic classification or clear guidelines [ ]. Hughes later refined Kirschen’s approach by dividing it into four categories: supply, subsidy, production, and regulation [ ]. Howlett et al. classify policy tools as voluntary, mandatory, and hybrid instruments based on the level of coercion involved [ ]. Schneider and Ingram argue that policy tools come in various forms, including incentive-based, hortatory, and capacity-building tools [ ]. Rothwell et al. categorize policy tools into three groups: supply-type, demand-type, and environment-type; a classification frequently used in studies of relevant sports policy tools in China [ ]. McDonald and Elmore separate policy tools into command-type, incentive-type, capacity-building, and systemic change instruments based on the goals of policy implementation, emphasizing the mediating role of policy tools in achieving policy objectives [ ]. Chinese scholars further divide policy tools into authoritative, policy incentive, informational, competence, and symbolic tools [ ]; market-based tools, business administration techniques, and socialization tools [ ], drawing on classification criteria and theoretical approaches from foreign research [ ].
In summary, the academic community has examined policy tools from various perspectives, providing a theoretical framework and scholarly basis for research on youth physical health policy tools. However, there remains room for further exploration and improvement. First, from a rationalist perspective, Guy Peters contends that a policy instrument is effective only if its characteristics align with the policy context, goals, and target audience [ ]. Therefore, policy tools must account for specific social, economic, and cultural contexts when categorizing and applying them. Especially in youth physical health policy, existing studies have focused on a single theoretical perspective based on established policy tools, neglecting China’s unique social structure and policy environment [ , ]. Second, most current research concentrates on the effectiveness of individual policy tools, with fewer studies examining how combinations of policy tools influence youth physical health. Given China’s unique social, cultural, and policy environment, detailed localized research on youth physical health policy tools is especially important.

## Methods

## Procedural grounded theory
Procedural Grounded Theory, as a well-established approach within grounded theory methodology, upholds the core principle of ‘generating theories from data’ through systematically organized procedures [ , ]. This approach facilitates theory development from empirical data, which is usually analyzed through three main steps: open coding, where the raw data are conceptualized and categorized [ , ]; axial coding, in which links between categories are further discovered and established [ ]; and selective coding, in which links between main categories are further analyzed to form a theoretical framework [ ]. In addition, theoretical saturation validation using new sample information is often required to test the quality of the analytical results [ ].

## Sources of policy texts
This study’s selection process began with policy documents published since 2000, obtained from official government portals (State Council, Ministry of Education, and General Administration of Sport) and Peking University’s Legal Database. Keyword searches included “youth,” “youth physical fitness,” “youth sports,” “youth health,” and “youth health promotion,” leading to the identification of 39 national-level youth physical fitness and health policies as the final sample (Fig. ). These 39 policies were randomly split into two groups: 36 documents were analyzed through coding and theoretical model development, while the remaining three were set aside for testing theoretical saturation (See Appendix 1). 
This study follows three primary principles for selecting policy texts: (1) Authority: Only policies issued by China’s State Council, Ministry of Education, and General Administration of Sport are included, ensuring consistency with national governance priorities [ ]. (2) Timeliness: We included policies and regulations that remain applicable after the year 2000 to cover the key period of reform in China’s youth sports policy and to align with the policy life cycle theory in China [ ]. (3) Relevance: Texts had to specifically focus on promoting youth physical health through direct mandates or indirect support mechanisms [ , ].

## Open coding
The research was based on the policy text and used grounded theory to analyze the source material and organize it hierarchically, primarily through open coding. Open coding involves conceptualizing and abstracting words, phrases, and fragments from the raw data to identify concepts and categories. Two researchers (MW and KY) independently employed NVivo 12.0 software to manually encode and extract concepts, utilizing the software’s node and tree node features. Any disagreements were discussed and resolved with the corresponding author (YZ). For this study, 921 raw sentences were initially selected and conceptualized by tagging them individually. To reduce subjectivity, the original language of legal and policy texts was used as much as possible for the codes. Concepts that could not be categorized were eliminated [ ], resulting in 149 concepts and 76 initial categories (see Table ). 

## Axial coding
Once the open-ended coding is finished, it must be integrated and categorized based on its inherent logical relationships, known as axial coding. The main goal of axial coding is to establish specific analogical relationships at the conceptual level by exploring the intrinsic connections between categories. This process, which involves both inductive and deductive methods, links subcategories to main categories through a series of steps. The initial 76 categories mentioned earlier were further consolidated into 16 subcategories, including concepts related to health, venue and facility resources, institutional improvements, financial incentives, and more. Additionally, based on the class characteristics outlined by the conceptual classification, four main categories were summarized and condensed: substance-based, symbols and persuasion, capacity-building, and incentive tools (see Table ). 

## Selective coding
Selective coding aims to identify a core category from the main categories and establish relationships between the core category and other categories in a storytelling manner, thereby refining the theoretical research model. This process was the final step in the coding analysis and the development of the theoretical framework. The core category identified in this study was “adolescent physical fitness policy tools,” which included four main categories: substantive tools, symbolic and persuasive tools, capacity-building tools, and incentive tools.

## Test of theoretical saturation
The theory saturation test aims to confirm that the examined theory accurately describes and explains the main concepts and relationships within the field of interest. When no new information or insights emerge, it suggests that further analysis and coding are unlikely to yield significant new findings, indicating that the theory has reached saturation. This study found no additional conceptual categories or associative relationships during the coding and analysis of the remaining three texts. Therefore, the developed theoretical model has achieved saturation.

## Explanation of the youth physical health policy tools
Substantive tools are often used to achieve policy objectives through direct intervention or support, such as strengthening organizational structure and management, and promoting educational integration. For example, the Circular on Further Strengthening the Management of Primary and Secondary School Students’ Physical Fitness and Health from the General Office of the Ministry of Education indicates that provincial education authorities should coordinate the management of physical fitness and health in their regions, report regularly to the party committee and government, and encourage local efforts. Many policy documents related to young people’s physical fitness include substantive tools. These texts frequently use words like “strengthen,” “organize,” “should,” “establish,” etc., which mainly involve mandatory, direct interventions and commands, reflected in system improvements, mechanism development, standard-setting, and enforcement actions.
Symbolic and persuasive tools (SHT) are mainly used to motivate young people to participate in physical activity by conveying a specific vision or value. Their key connecting words in youth physical health policy documents are typically ‘encouragement,’ ‘appeal,’ ‘advocacy,’ ‘concept,’ or ‘idea.’ For instance, the Opinions on Deepening the Integration of Sports and Education to Promote the Healthy Development of Young People (State General Administration of Sport, 2020) highlight: ‘We should establish the concept of health-first education, ensure full implementation of physical education curricula for all students, and empower students to enjoy physical exercise, enhance physical fitness, improve personality traits, refine their willpower, and achieve holistic development of both mental and physical well-being’ [ ].
Capacity-building tools (CT) involve providing additional human and financial resources to individuals or institutions, such as sports venues and facilities, sports organization resources, event resources, human resources, sports popularization efforts, and digital information services. These tools are essential for encouraging young people to actively participate in sports, and they are a primary focus for both national and local governments when creating policies on youth physical fitness. The content of these capacity-building tools typically emphasizes venues and facilities, organizations, events, sports instructors, and scientific and technological equipment.
Incentive tools (IT), including financial and reputational incentives, encourage policy implementers and youth sports service providers to act through tangible rewards. Economic incentives include lottery public welfare funds, financial subsidies, and government-purchased services. For example, the 13th Five-Year Plan for Youth Sports issued by the General Administration of Sport states that the government should promote the purchase of public services for youth sports. A diversified youth sports development fund-raising mechanism has been created. Reputational incentives involve performance evaluations and awards or commendations, such as the Ministry of Education’s Circular on Effectively Guaranteeing One Hour of Sport and Physical Activity for Elementary School Students Every Day, which stresses establishing a system for recognizing, rewarding, and holding accountable those who ensure elementary school students get one hour of sport and physical activity daily on campus.

## Fuzzy-set qualitative comparative
Based on this, we used the fsQCA method to investigate how different configurations of policy tools influence youth physical participation. Using the province as the unit of analysis, the policy tool generated through the above coding process is treated as the condition variable. In contrast, youth sports participation in each province of China is considered the outcome variable. The non-linear relationship between the condition variable and its combination with the outcome variable is assessed using the coverage and consistency measures of the fsQCA method.

## Definition of condition variables
The main reasons for selecting local policy tools as conditioning variables in this study are: first, local government policies are tangible implementations and adaptations of national policy ideas at the regional level, which can directly impact youth sports participation and offer a practical way to observe and assess the effects of national policies. Second, examining local government policy tools helps to understand how national policies are implemented, innovated, and adjusted in real-world settings, while considering region-specific factors such as economic and cultural differences to ensure accurate and reliable analysis. Therefore, by searching databases such as local government portals, the Peking University Fabulous Law Database, and the General Database of Chinese Legal Knowledge Resources, and by maintaining consistency in variables while limiting the timeframe to before 2018, this study ultimately identified 25 youth sports-related policy documents enacted by local governments. The qualitative comparative analysis method can analyze data from 10 to 60 small- to medium-sized samples [ ]. The sample size of 25 youth sports-related policy texts enacted by provincial governments is appropriate for this approach. Additionally, the QCA method can explain how different combinations of conditioning variables may lead to the same outcomes and how the specific arrangement of variables might produce different results due to external environmental factors [ ].

## Definition of outcome variable
The link between sports participation and physical health is well-supported by research [ , ]. Therefore, this study used data on youth participation in sports activities in each Chinese province as the outcome variable, analyzing it on a province-by-province basis. To measure youth sports participation, the “China Youth Sports Development Report (2018)” selected “frequency of physical activity in a week” as the basis [ ]. The frequency of sports participation is the most direct target of policy tools (e.g., the Healthy China Initiative explicitly requires “more than 3 times per week of in-school exercise”), making it easy to evaluate the policy’s effectiveness [ ]. Accordingly, the study used the question “frequency of physical exercise in the past week” from the China Family Panel Studies (CFPS) conducted by the China Center for Social Science Survey at Peking University in 2018. During data screening, participants were limited to those aged 7 to 18, with a focus on the year 2018. Incomplete questionnaires were also excluded, resulting in data from 3,006 youth sports participants across 25 provinces and municipalities.

## Calibration
Before starting the fsQCA study, sample calibration was performed to convert raw variable values into fuzzy-set membership scores, which range from 0 to 1 [ ] (see Appendix 2).

## Conditional variables calibration
Two independent coders (MW and JW) analyzed policy texts using NVivo 12.0. Each unique clause or paragraph referencing a specific policy tool type was coded as one occurrence, while repeated mentions of identical content were not counted as duplicates. Policy tool categories not used in documents were assigned a membership score of 0. Following the direct calibration method typically used in fsQCA [ ], anchor points were defined as fully in (membership = 0.95), crossover point (membership = 0.50), and fully out (membership = 0.05).

## Outcome variable calibration
We collected the data at the provincial level and used Fiss’s calibration framework [ ]. We converted continuous variables with quartile thresholds: the Upper Quartile (Q3) as full membership (≥ 4 weekly sports activities), the Median (Q2) as the crossover point (≥ 3 weekly sports activities), and the Lower Quartile (Q1) as full non-membership (≤ 2 weekly sports activities). This approach aligns with the WHO’s focus on activity frequency as a key behavioral indicator [ ] and supports comparisons with international public health standards.

## Constructing a truth table
This study constructed the truth table using fsQCA 3.0 software, with the raw consistency threshold set at 0.8 and the proportional reduction in inconsistency (PRI) threshold at 0.6. Condition configurations meeting these thresholds were coded as Y = 1, while others were coded as Y = 0 (see Table ) [ ]. Through counterfactual analysis of logical remainders, we derived a contradiction-free truth table that provides essential data for subsequent Boolean minimization. 

## Results

## Univariate necessity analysis
Before conducting the group analysis, it is essential to perform a necessary condition analysis to examine the relationship between each condition variable and the outcome variable, thereby identifying whether each variable is essential for increasing youth sports participation. Therefore, this paper uses the fsqca3.0 software to analyze the consistency and coverage of each explanatory variable, as shown in Table . Consistency in QCA measures how much a condition (or set of conditions) is a subset of the outcome [ ]. It shows how reliably a specific policy tool setup leads to the observed result, such as youth sport participation. Coverage measures the practical importance of a condition (or setup) by indicating how much of the outcome is “explained” by that pathway [ ]. 
Suppose the consistency results of each explanatory variable do not reach the critical value of 0.9 or higher. In that case, they are not necessarily influential on the outcome variable [ ]. The results shown in Table indicate that none of the individual antecedent variables (policy tools) affecting the frequency of youth sport participation have an average value below the set critical threshold of 0.9. Therefore, none serve as a necessary condition. This finding suggests that individual antecedent variables have limited explanatory power regarding youth sports participation and do not significantly influence the outcome. The study further examines how multiple antecedent variables, working together in synergistic configurations, explain youth sports participation and investigates the pathways through which these configurations impact participation.

## Conditional configuration analysis
Based on the analysis of necessary conditions, the research examines four antecedent variables—symbolic and exhortation tools, capacity-building tools, incentive tools, and substantive tools—in a cohort manner. Drawing on the thresholds commonly used in research [ ], the study set the consistency threshold at 0.8 and the PRI consistency threshold at 0.6 for validity checking. The fsQCA method produces three solution types: complex (excluding logically plausible remainders), intermediate (including theoretically plausible remainders), and parsimonious (allowing all remainders) [ ]. We chose the complex solution because it strictly follows empirical evidence and avoids counterfactual assumptions, aligning with our goal to identify policy tool configurations based on observed cases [ ]. This approach produced three distinct policy tool configurations. As shown in Table , the consistency of the three paths exceeds 0.75 (0.874, 0.973, 1), indicating that all three better explain the policy instrument’s effectiveness. Additionally, the consistency of the overall conditional configuration is 0.898, meaning that 89.8% of the policy tools in the three groupings of states provide strong explanatory power. The overall coverage is 0.523, indicating that these three condition configurations account for 52.3% of the cases. 

## Discussion
Table in the previous section shows the three identified configuration pathways, described by the various combinations of policy tools associated with youth sports participation.
Configuration 1 (ST* IT* CT) shows a potential synergistic effect among Substantive tools, Incentive tools, and Capacity-building tools, which may be linked to an increase in youth sports participation (Fig. ). It also explains that the government usually intervenes directly in youth sports through policies and may boost youths’ enthusiasm for sports activities with various incentives. Meanwhile, the government utilizes capacity-building tools, such as improving sports facilities, expanding human resources, and strengthening support for sports organizations, to provide more abundant and accessible sports resources for young people. This approach encourages their participation in sports and could contribute to improvements in their physical fitness. This configurational mechanism aligns with Schneider and Ingram’s policy coherence theory, which posits that addressing complex public health issues necessitates the systematic integration of authoritative governance, economic incentives, and resource allocation [ ]. 
Hubei and Jiangsu provinces exemplify Configuration 1, with their use of substantive, incentive, and capacity-building tools ranking among the top two out of the 25 provinces (including autonomous regions and municipalities directly under the central government) in terms of policy tool supply. Both provinces prioritize fundamental tools over operational rulemaking and monitoring ratings, have developed incentive programs for the organization, conduct, and rating of youth sports activities, drawing on foreign experiences [ ]. This approach closely aligns with the WHO Global Action Plan on Physical Activity (GAPPA, 2018), which encourages “multisectoral collaboration to address structural barriers.” A prime example is Jiangsu Province, where the coordinated efforts of education, sports, and urban development departments led to the adoption of the 13th Five-Year Plan for Youth Sports Development in Jiangsu Province and related policies [ ]. These measures combined educational reforms (e.g., mandatory sports curricula), infrastructure investments (e.g., upgraded community sports facilities), and community resource mobilization (e.g., corporate-sponsored sporting events), thereby promoting cross-departmental policy coherence.
Configuration 2 (ST * IT * SHT) shows no significant link between the number of capacity-building tools provided and youth sports participation (Fig. ). It suggests that when the quality of physical education and sports teaching at local schools is low, leading to infrequent youth sports involvement, the government tends to rely on substantive and incentive tools to potentially influence the behavior of institutions or individuals involved in developing youth sports, either through potential incentives or disincentives. When necessary, symbolic and persuasive tools may complement substantive tools by leveraging their top-down authority to potentially improve the effectiveness of policy implementation [ ]. 
Specifically, Liaoning and Zhejiang provinces exemplify the Configuration 2 approach. In Liaoning Province, youth sports governance depends on strict incentives and assessment systems. Districts and schools that experience three consecutive years of decline in youth physical fitness levels face a ‘one-vote veto’ policy in education evaluation and meritocracy. At the same time, the province highlights that the regular review of student physical fitness test data should serve as a key indicator in the performance evaluation of school and education department leaders [ ]. Zhejiang Province emphasizes the combined use of substantive, incentive, symbolic, and persuasive tools. Following the State Council’s 2016 release of the Opinions on Strengthening School Sports to Promote the Comprehensive Development of Students’ Physical and Mental Health, the General Office of the People’s Government of Zhejiang Province published the Opinions on Implementing Measures to Strengthen School Sports for Students’ Overall Physical and Mental Growth. The document stresses the need to accelerate the integration of local youth sports competitions through incentives and penalties, improve the mechanism for organizing competitions, and clarify organizational titles. It also specifies the annual funding allocated for youth sports training and competitions [ ]. The Zhejiang provincial government effectively leverages the potential of incentive tools, combining them with symbolic and persuasive methods to achieve its goals. It employs a mix of financial and reputational incentives to motivate primary and secondary schools to improve football instruction in line with physical education and health curriculum standards, and to support colleges and universities in offering football elective courses. It also aims to expand training channels for school football teachers: schools with football characteristics should have at least one full-time football teacher and are encouraged to involve football coaches, referees with excellent professional skills and proper ideological standards, as well as teachers from other disciplines and community volunteers with football expertise, to serve as part-time football teachers. Physical education teachers should be treated the same as teachers of other subjects regarding professional title evaluations, benefits, merit-based selection, and promotion.
Configuration 3 (SHT*CT) suggests a potential synergistic effect among symbolic, persuasive, and capacity-building tools, which may be associated with variations in youth physical fitness outcomes (Fig. ). If local governments, such as those in Guangdong Province, focus on promoting youth sports participation within their communities, a natural increase in youth sports involvement might happen even without coercive measures, provided that sufficient sports resources and a supportive environment are available. This strategy could help reduce reliance on coercive tools. In this context, the focus is mainly on supportive actions (e.g., providing resources) aimed at strengthening the capacity of policy implementation entities and target groups, which could improve policy effectiveness [ ]. 
Notably, in public health, policy is seen as a vital tool for driving structural and environmental changes that promote healthy physical activity (HEPA) [ ]. The GAPPA, launched by WHO in 2018, not only sets a goal to reduce diseases related to physical inactivity by 15% by 2030 but also closely links this goal to the United Nations’ Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) [ ]. In the same year, General Secretary Xi Jinping emphasized at the National Education Conference the importance of a policy to “adhere to the path of development of socialist education with Chinese characteristics and foster socialist builders and successors who are well-rounded in morality, intelligence, physical fitness, and aesthetics.” As a result, China’s educational system has experienced a significant shift from “balanced emphasis on four aspects of education (morality, intellect, physical fitness, and aesthetics)” to “equal emphasis on all five aspects of education (morality, intellect, physical fitness, aesthetics, and labor skills),” marking a key evolution in its development framework [ ]. This shift emphasizes the CPC Central Committee’s key role and high expectations for the overall development of young people. It clearly communicates to all parts of society the importance of physical education and sports in youth development [ ]. The current focus of the “decentralization” of youth sports policy is on using capacity-building tools, funding, venues, and preferential policies for higher education, as well as organizing after-school sports activities and providing tracking services for top reserve talents. The “decentralization” of policy implementation means that the government is no longer the sole entity responsible for public services related to youth sports, resulting in greater involvement from multiple stakeholders [ ]. For example, Guangdong Province issued the “Guangdong Province Public Sports Facilities Construction Implementation Opinions,” emphasizing the need to mobilize society to increase the supply of public sports facilities. It also encourages municipal and county sports departments to develop digital platforms for national fitness public services with local features, promoting active participation in national fitness and youth sports [ ]. Guangdong Province emphasizes the importance of community organizations, such as street offices, in promoting youth sports by engaging all sectors of society to establish a comprehensive youth sports public service system. Their efforts include using newspapers, radio, TV, the Internet, and new media to run youth sports service columns and hosting seminars on youth sports development to increase community awareness and encourage discussion on the youth “issue network.” The topic of “issue networks” has attracted interest from various sectors for discussion [ ].

## Research strengths and theoretical contributions
Using multiple methods, including grounded theory and fsQCA, this study systematically clarifies how policy tool combinations affect youth physical health governance through multi-case configuration analysis, offering potential progress in theory, methodology, and empirical research for the field of public policy.

## Theoretical reconstruction
This study uses a grounded theory approach to develop a bottom-up system of policy tool categories, identifying “capacity-building tools” within China’s specific context (e.g., physical education teacher preparation guarantees, preferential policies for further education) and applying configuration analysis to assess their broader relevance.

## Method innovation
Embedding the fsQCA method into a study of policy tools may help address the limitations of traditional assessments of single-policy tool effectiveness by using configurational analysis and comparing cases across provinces (Jiangsu, Liaoning, and Guangdong). For example, configuration 2 (the Zhejiang model) illustrates the complementary nature of incentive tools (such as preferential titles for soccer teachers) and symbolic and persuasive tools (like the “model school” selection) [ ], which appears to support Rhodes’ theory of policy networks. It also shows that collaboration among multiple actors (such as community-based organizations and digital platforms) may serve as an alternative to traditional hierarchical authority.

## Practical paradigm
Configuration 3 (the Guangzhou case) presents a potentially viable governance model for developing countries, aligning with the WHO’s Health in All Policies (HiAP) initiative. It may help enhance the cultural inclusiveness of the HiAP framework. By incorporating the policy of “equal emphasis on all five aspects of education” (such as including physical education in the evaluation system for promotion to higher education), health objectives become deeply integrated into the education system, supporting the need for coordinated governance between SDG 3 (health) and SDG 4 (education) [ ].

## Limitations
This study has several limitations. First, the policy texts analyzed are limited to those issued by the central government, which may not fully encompass all relevant policy literature. Second, the use of subjective judgments during the coding process with grounded theory may have influenced how categories and concepts were identified and classified, potentially affecting the broader applicability of the results. Third, the data for the outcome variables used in the fsQCA analysis come from a specific survey report (i.e., the China Family Tracking Survey (CFPS) conducted in 2018), which may introduce potential sample bias and impact the representativeness of the findings. Due to the time frame and geographic focus of the policy texts and data sources, their applicability to other countries or public health policy areas is limited. Future research should include a larger sample, utilize a broader range of data sources, and consider additional influencing factors to address these limitations.

## Conclusion
This study applied grounded theory to code and analyze Chinese youth physical fitness policy texts from the past 20 years, identifying four categories of policy tools: substantive, incentive, capacity-building, and symbolic and persuasive tools. Based on this, qualitative comparative analysis (QCA) was employed as an empirical tool to uncover three significant configuration paths: configuration 1 (substantive tools * incentive tools * capacity-building tools), configuration 2 (substantive tools * incentive tools * symbolic and persuasive tools), and configuration 3 (symbolic and persuasive tools * capacity-building tools). The study yields the following insights:

There is a relationship among the substantive, incentive, capacity-building, symbolic and persuasive tools in the Chinese adolescent physical fitness policy tools.
Focusing solely on expanding a specific type of youth physical health policy tool, such as significantly increasing capacity-building tools, does not appear to be sufficient to improve the physical health of China’s youth. According to the univariate necessity analysis, relying on a single policy tool as a conditional variable is neither necessary nor sufficient. This highlights the importance of using a strategic combination of policy tools to achieve more comprehensive results.
Overall, China tends to rely on mandatory substantive and incentive policy tools to promote the healthy development of youth physical fitness, meaning that configurations two and three both include these tools. These make up about two-thirds of the identified configurations, suggesting that such combinations may lead to noticeable short-term benefits or help establish a relatively stable policy environment over time. However, relying solely on substantive or incentive tools might reduce the effectiveness of symbolic persuasion and capacity-building tools. The frequency and structure of policy tools may need further adjustment to improve coordination and achieve more balanced policy outcomes.

## Recommendations

Policymakers should focus on the interconnectedness of substantive, incentive, capacity-building, symbolism, and persuasion tools, rather than relying solely on one type. Because youth physical health policy tools are non-essential and incomplete, policymakers can flexibly adjust how often and how they use these tools, depending on policy results and environmental changes, to improve policy outcomes.
Strengthen the use of symbolism, persuasion, and capacity-building tools. For symbolism and persuasion, focus on inspiring youth’s enthusiasm for sports participation through positive encouragement and recognition; for capacity-building, emphasize investing not only in sports facilities but also in developing resources for sports organizations and student sporting events.
Properly evaluate the effectiveness of substantive tools. For provinces and municipalities where youth sports are underdeveloped and youth physical fitness levels are low, it is important to establish the core role of these tools as soon as possible. It is crucial to avoid the misconception that broad policy coverage alone can change the current situation and to recognize the value of more targeted and effective policy tool configurations.
