The 2022 African Stroke Organization Conference (ASOC) was held on November 2 and 3, 1 year after the first conference in 2021. It built on the success of the first iteration, which provided a platform for young scholars of African origin to present their scholarly work while featuring high‐caliber experts in the field of stroke medicine and research. It was a forum that featured new topics such as poststroke pain, stroke rehabilitation, and intracerebral hemorrhage. Addressing COVID‐19 as an emerging challenge in understanding subtypes of stroke and discussing artificial intelligence as a tool going forward in diagnosis were notable new features of the conference. This virtual conference attracted 107 registered attendees. The format was very similar to the previous year's conference. Day 1 featured the Distinguished Policy Maker Lecture by Dr Florence Baingana, the Regional Advisor for the World Health Organization African Region, which was followed by several scientific presentations and concluded with scholar abstract presentations and the Vascular Brain Trust Career Development Session. Day 2 opened with the Osuntokun Award Lecture by Dr Carolyn Jenkins followed by scientific presentations that touched on several areas including epidemiology, genetics, technology, and stroke burden due to the pandemic and endemic infectious causes such as HIV. Afterwards came additional scholar abstract presentations with the Moving On Up Career Development Session as the final event of the day. The ASOC was once again an effective forum for a spectrum of scientific topics and scholarly research highlighting the burden of stroke in Africa and the promise of addressing it through vigorous scientific research and innovation.
Periodic analysis of the global burden of stroke continues to show rising incidence of stroke as well as related mortality rates over the past 3 decades. This rise is noted to be the steepest in the lower and middle‐income countries. In 2019, the age‐standardized mortality rate was 3.6 times higher and the age‐standardized stroke‐related disability‐adjusted life year rate was 3.7 times higher in lower‐ and middle‐income countries, compared with higher income countries. This burden from lower‐ and middle‐income countries is estimated to represent 70% of the deaths and 87% of disability‐adjusted life years globally. The higher‐income countries have achieved improved risk factor control and public awareness, seeing a decline of stroke incidence over the past several decades. , , 

It is against this backdrop that ASOC 2022 provided an opportunity to discuss the challenges the African continent faces, to increase dissemination of the latest practices specific to stroke, to highlight the scholarly research output from young and seasoned professionals of African origin, and to provide a venue for effective networking and professional development. The participation of academic and research leaders and representatives of regional and global organizations allowed for a meaningful and impactful discussion of addressing these serious problems while giving opportunity for young scholars to learn about resources to further their professional research pursuits.

## AFRICAN STROKE ORGANIZATION CONFERENCE 2022 PROCEEDINGS
The second ASOC was held November 2 to 3, 2022 on a virtual vFairs platform that presented prerecorded presentations along with live discussions on the topics. Originally conceived as a venue for a live meeting that would allow young Africans to travel and present their scientific work, ASOC 2021 and 2022 took place virtually due to pandemic restrictions. Only 2 years since its launch, ASOC remains an invaluable preeminent transcontinental platform for scientific scholarly works in the African continent. Like the previous year, the second conference remained true to its mission of facilitating the implementation of translational research to improve stroke outcomes on the continent. This was accomplished by designing a plan to prioritize gaps that exist in areas of knowledge and care delivery by collaborating with important organizations. Some of the collaborating organizations included African Academy of Neurology, Society of Neuroscientists of Africa, World Stroke Organization, World Federation for Neurorehabilitation, and World Hypertension League (Table  ).
 

## Conference Planning Committee
The 2022 ASOC Program Planning Committee expanded its makeup both in numbers and diversity of members, bringing together several experts with different backgrounds/areas of expertise and geographic representation. The Program Planning Committee constituted women and men of African origin with excellent records of scientific research and publication as well as experts from the rest of the world with stroke research experience in Africa. Members of the committee actively participated in selecting scientific topics including expanding the categories to include neuroendovascular interventions and intracerebral hemorrhage. They served as category chairs, participated in identification and recruitment of eligible early career scholars and selection of the Distinguished Policy Maker Lecturer and the Osuntokun Lecture Awardee, and participated in the program as speakers, panelists, and presentation moderators.

## Conference Announcement and Dissemination
ASOC 2022 was announced and disseminated through multiple mechanisms. This included a social media presence on Facebook and Twitter (@2022ASOC). An extensive network established through WhatsApp and email was used to reach a wide range of individuals in many academic institutions throughout Africa and the rest of the world. The African Stroke Organization also played the leading role by publicizing the conference through its newsletters and by featuring the announcement on its website.

## Invited Speakers
The conference speakers came highly recommended in their area of expertise and presented innovative content (Table  ). Although the presentations themselves were prerecorded, the speakers were available in a live question‐and‐answer session to respond to the questions and comments from the audience. The category chairs for the respective topics typically moderated the sessions. In addition to being available to answer the questions, speakers made themselves available in the platform chat room and interacted with attendees in networking and career development sessions.
 

## The Osuntokun Award Lecture
Every year, ASOC features the Osuntokun Award lecture to feature achievements of a highly accomplished scientist who has contributed and continues to contribute a lifetime of research in Africa. In 2021, this lecture was delivered by Professor Richard Walker of Newcastle University from the United Kingdom. In 2022, it was delivered by Dr Carolyn Jenkins of the Medical University of South Carolina where she serves as the Ann Darlington Edward Endowed Chair in Nursing and a research professor. Dr Jenkins's career spans several decades in addressing noncommunicable diseases including stroke in underserved African American South Carolinians. Her recent work explores genomic factors in stroke through the H3 Africa Stroke Investigative Research and Education Network.

## Travel Awards
Even though the COVID‐19 pandemic was waning in the year 2022, the planning for the meeting had begun almost immediately after the conclusion of the 2021 meeting. At that time, the future of travel restrictions across countries was not clear and a decision was made to hold the conference virtually for a second consecutive time. For this reason, we were not able to fulfill the original vision of granting travel scholarships to young investigators but plan to do this for future ASOC meetings.

## Moderated Oral Abstract Sessions
A total of 6 presenters presented their oral abstracts over the 2‐day period. Each day featured 3 presenters for a 30‐minute session. There were 2 moderators assigned per session: Drs Gilles Naeje of Guinea and Chokri Mhiri of Tunisia. The presenters responded to questions entered via chat from the audience and collected by the moderators (Table  ).

## Networking Sessions

Vascular Brain Trust: Three early career African scholars presented articles or grants in development to established stroke researchers from Africa and the United States. The stroke researchers who moderated the session then asked questions and gave feedback. Sola Ogunniyi and Yomi Ogun of Nigeria and Drs Daniel Lackland and Carolyn Jenkins of the United States chaired these sessions (Table  ).

Moving On Up: This session gives presenters an opportunity to discuss early career development with highly experienced scholars who are familiar with the workings of funding agencies. Dr Njideka Okubadejo of Nigeria and Dr Muhyadin Hassan Mohamed of Somalia chaired these discussions (Table  ).

## Conference Attendance
A total of 107 people registered for ASOC 2022. All attendees were at a master's degree level or above (MD, PhD, etc), except 1 attendee who was at a bachelor's degree level.

## DISCUSSION
The ASOC is one of the main pillars of implementing the African Stroke Organization's vision, and the 2022 conference has remained true to the organization's goals of fostering stroke research, building capacity, developing stroke services, and advocating and collaborating with stakeholders. , 

The latest report on the global burden of diseases indicates that stroke continues to be a leading cause of mortality and disability across the globe. Certain risk factors of stroke have become more prevalent than others due to changing lifestyles and urbanization. However, hypertension still remains to be the most important modifiable risk factor especially in Africa where there is less awareness, fewer guideline‐based treatments, and less adherence to medications. In recognition of these challenges, the conference dedicated a substantial amount of time on topics dealing with secondary prevention of stroke, control of hypertension, and discussions on intracerebral hemorrhage, which is often a result of uncontrolled hypertension.
Due to the times, the pandemic was foremost on everyone's mind during our virtual conference. Therefore, we addressed COVID‐19 in a context of stroke during this conference. The presentation titled “Variation in Global Stroke Burden, SARS‐CoV‐2 Impact and Influence on Clinical and Brain VCI [Vascular Cognitive Impairment] Phenotypes” was one of the most fascinating topics, which resulted in a robust discussion.
The conference also showcased current and cutting‐edge scientific information including endovascular therapy for ischemic stroke, contemporary interventional management of intracranial aneurysm, the future of artificial intelligence in stroke imaging, and the role of genetics in determination of stroke risk.
Finally, true to its goals, ASOC 2022 created a platform to display the scholarly outputs of its young investigators. Although several abstracts were presented in poster format, a select few were presented orally in a longer format. The Vascular Brain Trust and Moving On Up served as excellent networking opportunities for young investigators to connect with influential mentors familiar with funding mechanisms of organizations and receive feedback on grants and articles under development. These sessions addressed the deficit that exists right now; <1% of research output comes out of the African continent, which represents 12% of the global population. 

ASOC 2022 was successful in delivering a high‐quality program that is worthy of its status as the premiere scientific stroke conference of the African continent. Going forward, the conference organizers will reflect on the experience gained from the past 2 conferences and strive to maintain excellence and address emerging cutting‐edge topics in the realm of stroke.

## CHALLENGES, BENEFITS AND FUTURE DIRECTIONS
Even though the past 2 ASOC sessions have provided tremendous opportunity for African researchers to display their work, increase their knowledge, and network, organizing such a conference at a continental level proved to be challenging. Some of this has been due to the sheer size of the continent and the wide distribution of many of the presenters across the globe across time zones and the lack of financial and technical resources of the conference given its young age. These are challenges that the conference surely will overcome by increasing its membership, publicizing its activities, and attracting funds and support from collaborators.
Although the success of ASOC over the past 2 annual sessions laid a strong foundation, we hope to achieve much more going forward as the premiere scientific stroke conference. Future iterations will be expected to lead the charge in invigorating research and education and bring to light the scientific output from the continent's investigators. With the pandemic mostly in the rearview mirror, future conferences will adopt a hybrid platform. The conference will continue to maintain one of its strengths: diversity in ideas and makeup of the organizing committee, speakers, and presenters. ASOC plans to enrich the experience of the participants by adding Hot Topics sections in its future program by inviting top experts from organizations that host premiere scientific conferences such as the International Stroke Conference, World Stroke Congress, European Stroke Organization Conference, and Middle East & North Africa Stroke Organization. In future live conferences we plan to hold stroke teaching courses and hands‐on workshops in stroke care. We anticipate that future ASOC events will continue to bring together key stakeholders; identify gaps in knowledge; set priorities for stroke prevention, care delivery, and research; and accelerate the translation of research findings to improve stroke outcomes in the region.
