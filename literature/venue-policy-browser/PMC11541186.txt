Healthy life expectancy is low nationwide, which may be related to sarcopenia and frailty. Uwajima City contains a progresses aging of the population in the Ehime Prefecture. Therefore, preventing heart failure requires treatment and patient education not only for the underlying disease, but also for the complications associated with aging, such as sarcopenia and frailty. In 2020, we began working with the fitness club LocomoK.O to focus on prevention after the onset of disease and prevention before onset. We have commenced surveys of healthy older adults living in the community in Uwajima City, including the Short Physical Performance Battery, physical functions such as handgrip strength, and body composition assessment using in-body tests. The data showed that the rate of sarcopenia was extremely high, at 70% in patients with acute heart failure and 39% in the healthy older group, and that 24% of patients with acute heart failure had severe sarcopenia. Based on the collected data, we started health seminars in 2023 to educate citizens about the disease to prevent the onset and recurrence of heart disease, which is in line with the current situation in Uwajima City. We hope that our activities will help those struggling with local medical care.

In particular, senility, which has been increasing rapidly over the past decade, is thought to be due to both longevity, which can be described as a ‘benefit of success’, and an increase in frailty and sarcopenia, which can be described as the ‘cost of success’. 
Ehime Prefecture has the highest age-adjusted death rate from heart disease in Japan for both men and women, and also the highest rate of heart failure for both men and women. Furthermore, the healthy life expectancy is low, at age 71.5 years for men and 46th place, and age 74.58 years for women, 44th place. Our hospital is in Uwajima City, south of Ehime Prefecture. Owing to its geography, which has been described as an ‘isolated island’, the aging rate is 41.1%, as of July 2024; we believe that the impact of age-related complications, such as sarcopenia and frailty, is strong. Furthermore, the implementation rate of outpatient cardiac rehabilitation (CR) in Uwajima City is 0%, and there is no denying that there is a lack of continuous treatment and patient education. In addition to shortages in hospital systems and staff, Kinugasa et al. reported that regional medical care differs from urban areas in that insufficient access to hospitals, lack of self-care, and lack of knowledge about heart failure may increase the risk of readmission. As hospital stays become shorter, there are limitations to education that can be provided to patients in acute-care hospitals. Therefore, we planned for physical therapists from within the city to gather together to hold ‘health seminars’ to educate patients about various illnesses.
In 2020, physical therapists from 3 facilities in Uwajima City gathered to hold kick-off meetings ( ). Between January and June 2022, we investigated physical function and the prevalence of sarcopenia in people aged ≥65 years with heart failure who are independent in activities of daily living (ADL) and healthy community-dwelling older adults. ‘Health seminars’ for citizens began in April 2023. A multicenter study began in May 2023 to investigate the prognosis of patients with acute heart failure in Ehime Prefecture. As of 2024, the organization is launching a nonprofit organization (NPO) corporation. In this article, we report the efforts made at the kick-off meeting to collect sarcopenia data and host the health seminar.
 

## Kick-Off Meeting
We held monthly meetings for data collection, analysis, and academic presentations in Uwajima City. The goal of this activity was to ‘provide information to citizens for disease prevention’. Each physiotherapist had qualifications in their specialty: senior registered instructor of cardiac rehabilitation, registered instructor of cardiac rehabilitation, respiratory therapists, and a Pilates instructor. The clinical settings of the physical therapists included in the present study were acute care hospitals, home-visit nursing care services, and fitness clubs. Therefore, the patients and users differed greatly in their physical function and probable sarcopenia ( ).
 

The home-visiting nursing care service at Japan Community Health Care Organization Uwajima Hospital (JCHO Uwajima Hospital) showed low Short Physical Performance Battery (SPPB) and handgrip strength, a high rate of future ADL decline risk (SPPB ≤9), and a high possibility of sarcopenia. In contrast, LocomoK.O had a high SPPB score, and probable sarcopenia had the lowest.
Next, the physical function of patients with heart failure and healthy community-dwelling older adults, as well as the sarcopenia rate were investigated using body composition analysis (InBody 770; InBody Japan, Tokyo, Japan), and a policy was decided by holding health seminars based on the data.

## Sarcopenia Research Results
The survey results are summarized in . The heart failure group had N-terminal pro B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) 5,169.0 (2,308.5–14,195.5) pg/mL, left ventricular ejection fraction 52.4±14.0%, and 6-min walk distance of 249.5 (150.0–352.5) m, and all patients were independent in ADL. The proportion of men was significantly higher in the heart failure group (75% vs. 11%, P<0.01); however, there was no significant difference in handgrip strength.
 

In terms of physical function, the heart failure group had lower SPPB scores, shorter single-leg standing times, and lower walking speeds than the healthy group. In each component of the SPPB, the heart failure group had lower values for the chair rise and gait speed components, except for the balance component (P<0.01).
Sarcopenia was significantly more frequent in the heart failure group (70% vs. 39%; P=0.019). In the analysis of covariance, even after adjusting for sex, height, and weight, the heart failure group had lower physical function and a higher prevalence of sarcopenia (P<0.05). Furthermore, the incidence of severe sarcopenia was significantly higher in the heart failure group (24% vs. 0%; P<0.01).
Honda et al. reported that the prevalence of sarcopenia in acute care hospitals was 23.2%. In contrast, Yamada et al. reported that the prevalence of sarcopenia in community-dwelling older individuals was 21.8% in men and 22.1% in women. This study significantly exceeded the results of previous studies for both the heart failure and community-dwelling older groups, suggesting that many older adults in Uwajima City may already be suffering from sarcopenia.

## Contents of the Health Seminar
Considering the number of attendees and convenience, a multipurpose hall in a rest stop located in the city center was chosen as the venue. The event took place on Saturdays from 10:30 am to 12:00 noon. Posters were created to promote the event and displayed on bulletin boards in public facilities around the city. Lectures were held 6 times a year and were free to attend, and covered topics such as heart failure, myocardial infarction, frailty, sarcopenia, and dementia.

In addition to lectures, the course also incorporated practical training, such as assessing physical function and exercise methods, taking advantage of the experience of physical therapists. The participants’ ages ranged from 60 to 92 years, most participants were female (96%), and the average number of participants in each seminar was 25.
 
 

At each seminar, the lectures focused on heart failure stages A and B, rather than stages C and D. In addition to the high rate of sarcopenia in Uwajima City, Shah et al. reported that >80% of older adults living in the area had stage A or B heart failure. In addition, Meng et al. reported that frailty in stage B heart failure with reduced ejection fraction increases the overall mortality. Frailty and sarcopenia overlap in some ways, and preventing these conditions can lead to reduced hospitalization and admission to facilities. Therefore, we emphasized the importance of prevention before the onset of disease and provided information on daily exercise and nutritional therapy. 

## Future Outlook
Our hospital is the only acute care hospital in southwestern Shikoku, and CR mainly involves patients in the intensive care unit. Although a multidisciplinary team guided heart failure management during hospitalization, follow up was not provided after discharge.
It has been reported that a system of heart failure education hospitalization has been introduced in Europe. In Japan, Kamiya et al. reported that participation in outpatient CR after discharge from an acute care hospital was associated with a better prognosis regardless of age, sex, or comorbidities. However, intensive heart failure management programs in Japan are limited to medical centers in large cities, and their availability is limited in rural hospitals. Among these, the cooperation of fitness clubs, which are popular among the general public and have high health awareness, is essential.
The fitness club, LocomoK.O, not only offers free health classes with transportation but also has physical therapists as staff who are certified registered instructors of cardiac rehabilitation, and respiratory therapists who encourage patients who wish to take advantage of the classes when they are discharged from acute care hospitals ( ).
 

Because our activities are self-funded, we are preparing to launch an NPO corporation to expand our activities. The members are all physical therapists, but they are considering requesting cooperation from other professionals and holding a multidisciplinary ‘health seminar’. We hope that our activities will help those struggling with local medical care.
