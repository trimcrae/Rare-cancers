JiaoJianyi ].Since the reform and opening-up policy, China has undergone a significant social transformation from a planned economy to a socialist market economy. The changes in China’s class structure determine the direction of its future development [ ].
At the same time, with the development of the market economy, people in the pursuit of material benefits at the same time gradually reduced emotional ties, this social change has led to more and more people longing for spiritual solace, so since the 1980s, China has appeared the phenomenon of “religious fever” [ ].This phenomenon is also reflected in other countries. For example, some studies in Europe have also demonstrated that after socio-economic development, people may tend to seek inner support [ ]. Residents’ sense of social class identity is a subjective reflection of their objective social class status, representing people’s perception, imagination, and interpretation of social structure. Residents’ social class identity is also regarded as an important dimension for measuring the modernization of a country’s social structure [ ].
After the 18th National Congress of the Communist Party of China, the Chinese government has paid more attention to people’s livelihoods. General Secretary Xi Jinping proposed the need to “constantly improve the sense of gain, happiness, and security of the people.” Residents’ sense of social class identity, as an important part of people’s sense of gain, is also seen as an important measure of the effectiveness of reform [ ]. However, over the past half-century, despite China’s rapid economic development and the continuous improvement of people’s living standards, there has been a deviation between residents’ sense of social class identity and the level of economic and social development. Most residents’ sense of social class identity is lower than their actual social status. Moreover, overall, residents’ sense of social class identity in China is at a medium to low level globally, and there has even been a downward trend in recent years. Therefore, studying the factors influencing social class identity is of great practical significance.
Research on social class identity can be traced back to the structural position theory proposed by Marx and Tawney, which suggests that objective economic status determines social class identity, and the development of the economy and society will inevitably bring about changes in social class identity [ ]. Subsequently, American sociologist Heiman proposed the reference group theory, pointing out that social class positioning is influenced by subjective factors, resulting in a “drive-to-the-middle” tendency or “relative deprivation,” thereby affecting people’s sense of social class identity.
Social class identity has always been a hot topic in sociology. Many scholars have conducted empirical research based on the above two perspectives [ ]. Early scholars mainly focused on Western European countries and found that people in developed countries already had class consciousness [ ]. Subjectively, most people position themselves in a certain social class, with the majority considering themselves to be in the middle class, which also aligns with Tawney’s expectation of the continuous expansion of the middle class.
More recent scholars have begun to focus on the formation mechanism of social class identity.previous study [ ] found through a study of 43 capitalist and socialist countries that the political system influences citizens’ sense of social class identity, with citizens of socialist countries experiencing slightly higher social class identity than those in capitalist countries during prosperous periods. previous study [ ] conducted empirical research on 44 countries and found that Gross National Product (GNP) positively affects residents’ sense of social class identity. In addition, factors such as education [ , ], social mobility [ ], political parties [ ], relative income [ ], changes in industrial structure [ ], and physical and mental health [ ] all affect social class identity.
Chinese scholars have studied the influence of residents’ social class identity more from the dimension of subjective factors. Previous study [ ] demonstrated through empirical research on the Uyghur population in Xinjiang, China, that ethnic identity is closely related to social class identity. Previous study [ ] concluded through a survey in Ningbo, China, that relative deprivation affects people’s social class identity, especially among the working class. Will religious beliefs as a subjective factor also affect social class identity? Meanwhile, in the past few decades, the number of religious believers in China has increased. According to the article “Policies and Practices of Guaranteeing Freedom of Religious Belief in China” on April 3, 2018, approximately 200 million citizens in China believe in religion. Therefore, studying the impact of religious beliefs on social class identity and potential mechanisms is of great academic value and practical significance.
Based on this, this paper uses data from the 2021 CGSS survey to empirically test the impact of religious beliefs on residents’ social identity and analyze potential mechanisms. Conducting heterogeneity analysis for different groups is conducive to providing more targeted policy guidance and suggestions to improve residents’ social class identity in the next step.
The innovation of this paper lies in two aspects. First, the innovation of research perspective. Previous studies mostly explored social class identity from macro backgrounds and personal conditions, with little attention to the variable of religious beliefs. Therefore, exploring the impact of religious beliefs on social class identity makes a marginal contribution. Second, the innovation of mechanism analysis. Selecting economic capital and social capital as mechanisms to analyze the impact of religious beliefs on farmers’ social class identity, through the analysis of “hard power” and “soft power” mechanisms, effectively enhances residents’ social class identity and promotes the modernization of national social structure.
The structure of this paper is as follows: the first part poses questions and briefly outlines the background of the issue; the second part explains existing research on relevant issues and related theoretical doctrines, based on which hypotheses are proposed; the third part presents relevant empirical analysis results and explanations; the fourth part provides brief conclusions and discussions.

## Theoretical hypotheses

## Religious belief and social class identity
The study of the relationship between religion and society has always been a hot topic in sociology. In the early 20th century, Max Weber argued that religious beliefs would promote the development of social economy and the transformation of social structure. Various religions establish church schools for religious education [ ]. On one hand, religions provide opportunities for cultural participation and education for believers, nurturing their knowledge and skills, enhancing their learning abilities, increasing their cultural capital, thereby improving their core competitiveness and social status [ ].
On the other hand, church schools can impart basic religious concepts and instill correct values in students [ ]. Most religions, both domestically and internationally, emphasize theoretical values such as forgiveness and generosity [ ]. For example, Buddhism emphasizes the cessation of greed, “renunciation,” “renunciation,” and “non-attachment.” Islamic doctrine mentions that “those who live a simple and contented life are truly happy,” and believers may reduce excessive attention to external conditions such as symbols of status and economic levels by integrating these values into their attitudes and behaviors, thereby increasing their social class identity.
Moreover, religions provide material assistance and spiritual comfort to believers. Among believers of the same religion, there exists an informal social assistance mechanism, providing economic assistance to those in need [ ]. According to Maslow’s hierarchy of needs theory, the self-esteem of the giver is realized, and the physiological and safety conditions of the recipient are alleviated, enhancing their social class identity.
Additionally, religion subtly transforms believers’ worldviews and alters their cognitive structures, such as Buddhism’s ideas of “cause and effect” and “reincarnation,” or Christianity’s notion of “entering heaven after death,” which help believers mitigate the impact of traumatic events to some extent, providing value and hope for the future, and offering spiritual solace [ ]. Furthermore, religious beliefs also elevate subjective happiness among religious residents, thereby enhancing their sense of identity [ ]. Based on the aforementioned analysis, this paper proposes:
H1: Religious belief will increase residents’ social class identity, and residents with religious beliefs will have a stronger social class identity.

## Mechanisms of the influence of religious belief on social class identity
Religious belief enhances residents’ social class identity by increasing social capital. Due to the long-term existence of China’s hierarchical structure and ethical orientation, social capital, as a fundamental social resource, embodies certain social statuses and is an important evaluative element of social class identity. Social capital refers to resources incubated in social networks, composed of social networks, social norms, and social trust [ ].
Religious belief has organizational and belief effects. On one hand, due to the fast pace and high technology usage in modern society, people pay more attention to personal life and career development, reducing social activities and interactions. As a formal organization, religion has high internal cohesion and inter-organizational connections. Believers regularly participate in religious organization ceremonies, such as Christian worship and Islamic congregational gatherings. When believers participate in regular activities, they expand their social circles, and religious beliefs enhance social capital by expanding social networks through organizational effects [ ].
On the other hand, religious belief has belief effects. Most religions advocate compassion and exhort people to do good deeds, enhancing believers’ ethical morals and social behaviors, thereby influencing the level of social trust through the transformation of worldviews and values. At the same time, shared spiritual pursuits reduce ideological differences among believers. Residents who share similar values within the trust boundary are more likely to generate trust and social norms [ ]. Maslow’s hierarchy of needs theory also proposes that social capital is a product of social needs. The increase in social capital enhances residents’ evaluation of social class identity. Based on this, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H2a: Religious belief will increase residents’ social class positioning by enhancing social capital.
Religious belief enhances residents’ social class identity by increasing economic capital. Social class identity is considered a reflection of residents’ objective economic status [ ]. Therefore, the higher the economic capital, the higher the residents’ social class identity [ ]. As a spiritual force, religious beliefs typically emphasize principles such as honesty, integrity, diligence, and fair trade.
These principles can shape individuals’ behaviors and decision-making processes in economic activities in the long run, thereby improving economic conditions in the long term [ ]. Marx believed that religion is a comforter that can relax the soul. After attending worship, Christians purify their souls, reduce negative emotions, thereby improving work efficiency and economic status [ ]. At the same time, religious beliefs provide believers with a sense of purpose and mission, inspire positive work attitudes, cultivate moral values, and foster thrifty and financial habits, which positively impact individuals’ economic conditions. Based on the above analysis, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H2b: Religious belief will increase residents’ social class identity by increasing economic capital.

## Research design

## Data selection
This study utilizes data from the 2021 China General Social Survey (CGSS). There are two main reasons for choosing this dataset. Firstly, the CGSS data, designed and released by Renmin University of China, constitute a nationwide, comprehensive, and longitudinal dataset. It adopts a stratified sampling design, covering 31 provinces, municipalities, and autonomous regions, thus possessing a certain level of authority and representativeness.
Moreover, the data are considered objective and authentic.CGSS(2021) data participants, mostly university students and teachers, were selected after systematic professional training to conduct a cross-sectional data survey of more than 10,000 households based on the resource endowment and socio-economic development conditions of the eastern, western and central regions of China. Secondly, the CGSS dataset contains relevant variables on religious beliefs and social class identification needed for this study, such as religious affiliation, frequency of participation in religious activities, and corresponding individual characteristics.
Additionally, it covers issues related to self-perceived social class, which aligns well with the research questions of this study. After removing missing values and outliers related to religious beliefs and social class identification, the actual sample used in this study has been maximized to the extent possible.

## Variable definitions

## Dependent variable: social class identification
Social class identification is people’s self-identification of their class status [ ]. Previous study [ ] assigned three levels (upper, middle, lower) to social class identification in empirical studies across 44 countries. Previous study [ ] measured social class identification using a ten-level ladder scale, which has been empirically validated for its reliability and validity. Following study [ ], this study adopts the question “Overall, in the present society, which social class do you consider yourself to be in?” and assigns ten levels to social class identification, with higher levels indicating higher social class identification. According to Table  , the average social class identification among current Chinese residents is 4.286, indicating a relatively low level of self-perceived social class identification among Chinese residents.
Core Explanatory Variable: In this paper, religious belief is divided into two aspects: whether to believe in religion and whether to participate in religious practice. With the development of internet technology, religion is moving towards secularization. Therefore, many residents in China may have religious beliefs even though they have not formally joined any religious organizations. Following the approach of study [ ], this study measures religious beliefs through attitude variables. The attitude variable of religious belief is measured by the question “Do you have any religious beliefs?” Religious belief is assigned a value of 1 for those who have religious beliefs and 0 for those who do not. Religious practice is also one of the measures of religiosity, and study [ ] point out that the frequency of religious participation also affects the subjective perception of residents. Therefore, in this paper, the robustness test is carried out through the religious belief participation variable, which is measured by ‘how often do you participate in religious activities’ and assigned a value of 1–9, with the higher value representing the more frequent participation in religious activities.

## Mediating variables

## Social capital
Social trust is shaped by and accumulates social capital, and it is the basis of the subjective capacity level of capital. Most scholars use social trust to quantify social capital in empirical studies. Social trust is divided into general trust and particular trust [ ]. After China’s reform and opening up, with the development of market economy, the refinement of social division of labor, and the enhancement of social mobility, the “acquaintance society” gradually disintegrated, and people interacted more with various strangers. The logic of particular trust has gradually transitioned to general trust, and the interaction logic of particular trust is no longer applicable to the current society. Therefore, based on this, this study selects the question from the CGSS data “Generally speaking, do you think people are always trustworthy, or is it not too careful when dealing with people?” People who think others are always trustworthy are assigned a value of 1, while those who think they are not trustworthy are assigned a value of 0.

## Economic capital
Family economic status reflects individuals’ or families’ financial capabilities, economic opportunities, and social economic inequality. It directly or indirectly affects the rights and status of family members in society, serving as an important indicator of economic capital. Family economic status is measured using the question “Which level does your family’s economic status belong to in your locality?” from the CGSS data, where the economic status of the sample is divided into 5 levels, with level 1 indicating that the family’s economic status is much lower than the local average and level 5 indicating that the family’s economic status is much higher than the local average.

## Empirical results

## Baseline regression results
In this study, we first conduct a probit ordered regression on the sample data according to Eq. ( ), and the regression results are presented in Table  . The entries labeled (1)-(2) in Table  indicate the impact of religious belief attitudes on social class identification, while entries labeled (3)-(4) represent the effect of the frequency of participation in religious activities on social class identification.
As shown in Table  , both religious belief attitudes and the frequency of participation in religious activities have a significant effect on social class identification at the 10% level, with corresponding coefficients being positive. After controlling for variables, religious belief attitudes exhibit a significant effect on social class identification at the 5% level, while the frequency of participation in religious activities shows a significant effect on social class identification at the 1% level.
This suggests that religious belief has a significantly positive impact on social class identification; in other words, residents with religious beliefs have a 11.3% higher level of social class identification compared to those without religious beliefs, thus confirming the hypothesis.At the same time, the increase in the frequency of religious beliefs may also increase the probability of self-identity by 3.4%. ## Endogeneity exploration and robustness tests

## Instrumental variables method(two-stage least squares)
Given the potential endogeneity issues arising from omitted variables and reverse causality in employing modal regression to assess the impact of religious beliefs on residents’ social identity, a more rigorous analysis leveraging the instrumental variable (IV) approach is conducted [ ]. Multiple scholars have utilized metrics such as the number of religious venues, the proportion of believers in the total population, and the degree of religious participation to quantify religious beliefs. However, in China, official statistics on religious beliefs are scarce, and the nationwide proportion of believers or their participation levels are rarely disclosed, posing challenges in acquiring accurate data. Consequently, some databases resort to questionnaires to ascertain residents’ religious affiliation and the extent of their beliefs, which constitute the core data utilized in our benchmark regression.
Furthermore, given that religious venues represent religious supply activities, the theory of supply and demand suggests that the quantity of supply, to a certain extent, mirrors religious demand, while the quality of supply reflects the intensity of religious activities among believers [ ]. Hence, the number of religious venues serves as a pivotal factor influencing residents’ religious beliefs. In this study, we employ the logarithm of the number of religious venues at the provincial level in China as an instrumental variable for religious beliefs. This data was sourced from the China State Administration for Religious Affairs, which released information on legally registered religious venues in four batches from April 2014 to the end of 2015, enabling the calculation of venue counts per province.
To ensure robustness and comparability, given the prevalence of micro-survey data in our analysis, we apply logarithmic transformations to various variables. Additionally, a weak instrument variable test was conducted, revealing that regardless of whether control variables were included or not, the associated F-statistics exceeded 10, rejecting the hypothesis of weak instrumentality.
Table  presents the regression results using the logarithm of the number of religious venues as the instrumental variable. In the first-stage regression, as evident from columns (1) and (3), the instrumental variable exhibits a statistically significant positive relationship with the measure of religious beliefs (p < 0.01), indicating that, ceteris paribus, an increase in the number of religious venues enhances the likelihood of residents’ religious beliefs. In the second-stage regression, after mitigating for endogeneity through the instrumental variable, religious beliefs continue to exhibit a highly significant positive effect on residents’ social identity (p < 0.01), thereby reinforcing the validity of our benchmark regression findings.

 

## Propensity score matching (PSM)
To ensure the robustness of the analysis conclusions, this study employs the propensity score matching (PSM) method to conduct a robustness test of the effect of religious belief on social class identification. The PSM method divides the sample into treatment and control groups based on the core explanatory variable of whether there is religious belief. According to the matching principle, the characteristics of the samples between the two groups are maximally similar, and the difference in social class identification between the treatment and control groups is used as the effect of religious belief on social class identification.
From Fig.  , it can be visually observed that all control group values fall within the common range, while only a small portion of the treatment group is outside the common range (Off support), resulting in a minimal loss of samples during matching. After successful matching, an OLS regression is conducted, and as shown in Table  , religious belief still has a significant impact on social class identification. Therefore, it can be concluded that the model in this study exhibits strong robustness.

Taking into consideration some existing analyses, in order to ensure the robustness of the analysis conclusions in this study, attempts were made to utilize the aforementioned two regression models for regression. After attempting regression using OLS and Ologit models, as shown in Table  , the regression results of both OLS and ologit models are consistent with those in Table  . It is found that attitudes towards religious belief and the frequency of religious participation have a positive and significant impact on residents’ social class identification, thus indicating that religious belief significantly enhances residents’ social class identification. ## Transformation variables
Residents’ current sense of social class identification can influence their anticipation of future social class identification. Therefore, to further validate the robustness of this study, a regression is conducted using residents’ anticipation of future social class identification as a substitute variable. The question “In which social class do you expect to be in 10 years?” (Aft) is selected for measurement. The results, as shown in Table  , indicate that both the variables of religious belief and attitudes towards religion continue to have a significant effect on social class identification at the 1% level. This further underscores the robustness of the model and conclusions in this study.

 

## Analysis of transmission mechanisms
Table  presents the impact of religious belief on residents’ social class identification after controlling for mechanism variables. The first column controls for social capital variables, while the second column controls for economic capital variables. The third column simultaneously controls for both variables. The regression results indicate that both social capital variables and economic capital variables have a significant positive effect on residents’ social class identification. Increasing social capital and economic capital indeed promote the improvement of residents’ social class identification.
However, more crucially, the variables of attitudes towards religious belief and participation in religious belief still exhibit significant facilitating effects. This suggests that religious belief can influence residents’ social class identification by enhancing their social capital and economic capital. In other words, religious belief enhances residents’ social class identification, with social capital and economic capital serving as its important mechanisms.

 

## Heterogeneity analysis
In order to explore this point in more detail, the sample is further divided into different subgroups to investigate potential heterogeneity issues among different subgroups. Firstly, the religious population is segmented by gender into two sample groups, with males assigned a value of 1 and females assigned a value of 0, to assess the impact of religious belief on the social class identification of residents of different genders. According to the regression results in Table  , it is evident that religious belief significantly promotes the social class identification of female residents, indicates that religious female residents have a higher sense of social class identity This may be attributed to some religious doctrines advocating for gender equality, encouraging respect and freedom for women, thereby granting them more decision-making power in the family, community, and workplace. Additionally, women play a more active role in shaping societal values and enhancing social class identification through broader social engagement.

 

Next, a heterogeneity analysis of religion is conducted. Considering that the doctrines of religions vary both domestically and internationally, for instance, domestic religions, predominantly Taoism, advocate for ideas such as “treating people equally” and “unlimited kindness towards others”, promoting equality among individuals. In contrast, foreign religions like Hinduism propose a strict hierarchical system. Therefore, drawing from the research of [ ], this study selects Buddhism, Taoism, and folk beliefs (such as worshiping Mazu and Guan Gong) as domestic religions (Dor) assigned a value of 1, while other religions are categorized as foreign religions (Abr) assigned a value of 0.
Probit regression is then conducted, as shown in Table  . The results indicate that foreign religions have a more significant impact on residents’ social identification. This could be attributed to several factors: on one hand, foreign religions exhibit significant differences from traditional Chinese values, leading believers of foreign religions to potentially widen the “class distance” between themselves and non-believers, thereby enhancing social identification. On the other hand, life experiences serve as the basis for social class identification. Followers of Christianity, for instance, are more inclined to engage in activities such as attending concerts, exercising, and reading newspapers, which are relatively sophisticated, thereby enhancing their social class identification [ ].

 

Finally, different educational attainment groups may also exhibit varying impacts. Since China implements nine years of compulsory education, educational attainment below high school is classified as the low educational attainment group, while high school and above are classified as the high educational attainment group. Probit regression is conducted separately for each group to test the impact of religious belief on social class identification.
The results, as shown in Table  , indicate that regardless of whether control variables are added, religious belief has a significant positive effect on the self-classification of the low educational attainment group. In other words, individuals in the low educational attainment group who hold religious beliefs tend to have higher social class identification. This may be attributed to the fact that compared to the low educational attainment group, the high educational attainment group possesses higher cultural capital, which in turn enhances their economic strength and social status, thus increasing their social class identification.

 

## Conclusion and policy recommendations

## Conclusion and discussion
Enhancing residents’ social class identification can contribute to social stability and cohesion. Based on the China General Social Survey (CGGS 2021) data, this study deeply analyzes the influence of religious belief on residents’ social class identification, revealing the underlying mechanisms through which religious belief affects social class identification. The conclusions drawn are as follows:
Firstly, religious belief significantly enhances residents’ social class identification(p < 0.05). This conclusion remains valid after robustness tests such as propensity score matching, model replacement, and substitution of the dependent variable. This finding aligns with those of [ ].
Secondly, religious belief can enhance residents’ social class identification by increasing their social capital(p < 0.01) and economic capital(p < 0.01).This mechanism analysis lays a solid foundation for future research on religious beliefs and self-identity, and possesses considerable reference value as well.
Thirdly, heterogeneity analysis shows that residents who believe in foreign religions (p < 0.05) have higher social class identification compared to those who believe in domestic religions. The effect of religious belief on enhancing identification is more significant among residents with lower educational attainment (p < 0.05). Additionally, female believers have higher social class identification compared to male residents (p < 0.05).
The conclusion of this article has some corresponding points with [ ]. Compared with people without religious beliefs, people with religious beliefs have clearer beliefs, clearer goals, greater self-esteem and a stronger sense of meaning and value in life [ ].This study meticulously delves into the relationship between religious beliefs and self-identity, marking an innovative contribution that provides vital literature support for related research. For scholars, this exploration has broadened the research horizon within this field. For policymakers, it offers a means to discern the effectiveness of policies and devise pertinent measures accordingly.It is worth noting that such research may also have some correlations with aspects such as group emotional exposure and illnesses like strokes, which can be further explored and researched in the future [ ].
Additionally, this study utilizes the 2021 China General Social Survey (CGSS2021) data to investigate the impact of religious belief on social class identification. The research is limited to a single time point, thus the potential findings may be limited. If there are updates to later CGSS data, this study will be supplemented accordingly. While CGSS data possess a certain level of objectivity and authority, they are existing data and not specifically designed for the research topic. Therefore, many concepts are measured using approximate questions.

## Policy recommendations
First, according to the benchmark regression finding that religious beliefs can enhance the residents’ sense of class identity, the Government may confer more rights and raise interests of the residents to participate in the lawful activities of religious organisations. China has always respected the rights of all legal religions and actively guided them to adapt to socialist society. This is not only because legal religions play a charitable role in assisting the poor and improving residents’ economic capital but also because religion helps maintain social stability by expanding residents’ social networks, enhancing social capital, and thus increasing social class identification. This aligns with the government’s focus on improving people’s livelihoods.
Secondly, the results of the heterogeneity analyses indicate the need to respect the freedom of belief of women and groups with low levels of education. A significant reason why women and those with lower educational attainment believe in religion is the existing discrimination they face in society. Therefore, women and those with lower educational attainment turn to religion for solace. As China is currently implementing a strategy of common prosperity, it is crucial to achieve spiritual prosperity alongside economic prosperity. Thus, future policy implementation should pay attention to these groups, actively guide them, enhance their sense of social fairness, and consequently increase their social class identification.
Finally, it is important to prioritize the ideological education of religious believers. Integrating China’s excellent traditional culture, religious beliefs, and socialist core values is conducive to enhancing residents’ social class identification and achieving long-term social development and stability.
