## Abstract
This study evaluates the effectiveness of a current training program for new nurses over a period of time, identify any existing issues in the program, and put forward recommendations to alleviate these issues, towards improving the structure and management of the training program. A total of 201 newly recruited nurses and 52 head nurses in a Grade-A tertiary hospital (Wuhan, China) were included in this longitudinal study with a 6-month duration. Using the Kirkpatrick model, we evaluated the effectiveness of the training against 4 levels of criteria: reaction, learning, behavior, and results. Cluster sampling was used to select participants, and data were analyzed using SPSS 19.0, including descriptive statistics, u tests, and 1-way analysis of variance to assess theoretical scores, operational scores, and behavioral evaluations. The reaction level responses were that the new nurses were generally satisfied with the training. The learning level responses showed that there was a significant difference in exam scores between new nurses after 3 months of training and after 6 months of training. The behavioral level responses indicated that the clinical nursing abilities and interpersonal skills of new nurses improved after 6 months of training. In the self-evaluation, new nurses gave themselves higher scores than the scores given by head nurses, and the difference between the 2 scores was statistically significant. According to the quality check data from the nursing department, the results level responses showed that the complaint rate against new nurses was 0, the accident rate was 0, and new nurses accounted for 20% of all the commendation letters. The systematic and comprehensive evaluation of new nurse training programs based on the 4 levels of the Kirkpatrick model provides valuable insights for nursing managers.

## 
1. Introduction
With the development of medical and health care services and advances in people’s standards of living, the shortage of nursing staff has become a common problem faced by hospitals all over China, especially large Grade-A tertiary hospitals.[ ] In 2020, the number of registered nurses in China reached 4.45 million, and a large number of new nurses joined clinical nursing work.
The standardized training of new nurses is a key link that bridges the education that new nurses received in nursing school with their postgraduation education. This training not only improves the level of theoretical knowledge and procedural skills of new nurses to a certain extent, but also facilitates the transformation of their roles from nursing students to clinical nurses.[ – ] Therefore, standardized training of nurses is highly valued and prioritized by most nursing managers and the concerned state departments.
In 2016, the National Health Commission of the People’s Republic of China issued the Training Syllabus for New Nurses (for Trial Implementation),[ ] which requires new nurses to undergo standardized training for 2 years. However, the training is organized in-house by hospitals themselves, and there are significant differences in the training set up, course contents, training formats, and the quality of the training. A systematic evaluation of the training is the only way to assess whether the training has been effective or not.
At present, there are several models for evaluating training effectiveness, and the ones commonly used in the United States include the Context, Input, Process, and Product evaluation model, the Context Evaluation, Input Evaluation, Reaction Evaluation, Output Evaluation model, and the outcome-based evaluation model which measures results. Among the internationally accepted ones, the 4-level Kirkpatrick model is the most widely used in training in China and abroad. It is considered one of the best evaluation methods,[ ] and has been used by many scholars for the evaluation of training in the health specialty.[ – ]
The Kirkpatrick model was put forward by American scholar Donald L Kirkpatrick,[ ] and includes 4 aspects: reaction, learning, behavior, and results. The reaction level assesses the trainees’ attitude and response to the training, which can be evaluated through the satisfaction questionnaire. The learning level evaluates the effectiveness of learning for trainees, that is, the degree of mastery of the training program, including the mastery of theoretical knowledge, procedural skills, attitude, confidence, and commitment based on their participation in the training. The evaluation at this level mainly consists of a theoretical written examination and skills assessment. The behavior level assesses the change in behavior and attitude of trainees after training, based on applying what they learned during training when they are back on the job. This assessment method can be multi-pronged and can include self-evaluation by trainees, assessment by leaders, colleagues, and teachers. The results level evaluation refers to the contribution of trainees to the organization after training in terms of targeted outcomes. The evaluation at this level reflects the ultimate purpose of the effectiveness of the training. It needs to be evaluated through indicators such as accident incidence and performance appraisal with the help of senior leaders and heads of other relevant departments.
Several scholars in China have cited the Kirkpatrick’s model for a systematic evaluation of nurse training,[ – ] but there are few reports available in literature regarding the use of the model for evaluating standardized training of new nurses involving a multi-dimensional evaluation of training. This gap highlights the need for a comprehensive assessment. The purpose of this study is to systematically evaluate the effectiveness of a standardized training program for new nurses using the Kirkpatrick model. By assessing the program across 4 levels – reaction, learning, behavior, and results.

## 
2. Materials and methods

## 
2.1. Participants
We used the 4-level Kirkpatrick model to evaluate the effectiveness of training of new nurses in the selected hospital (Wuhan, China). According to the principle of using the cluster sampling, we included 201 nurses who were newly recruited in June 2017 and 52 head nurses who participated in training activities in our hospital. We excluded nurses who were interning with the hospital, or those who had attended refresher courses.

## 
2.2. Duration
The training officially began in July 2017 and was evaluated at 3 months and 6 months of this study plan, so the evaluation was respectively conducted in September and December 2017.

## 
2.3. Implementation
The evaluation of training effectiveness was divided into 4 levels. At the reaction level, we used a satisfaction questionnaire that we developed for this evaluation (Supplementary File 1, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/P108), which included a satisfaction survey of 3 aspects pertaining to the teachers involved in the training, training course and content, and structure of the training, with a total of 13 items.
At the learning level, the evaluation consisted of comparison of the theoretical knowledge assessment results and procedural skills assessment results of trained nurses, after 3 months of training and 6 months of training. The hospital staff of nursing department prepared 2 theoretical tests, and 1 test was administered to nurses after 3 months of training, and the other test was administered to nurses after 6 months of training. The theoretical examination paper is prepared by the nursing department according to the hospital training courses and the 3 basic contents of nursing. The theory test is an online test, consisting of 50 multiple-choice questions, 2 points for each question, and a total of 100 points. For the assessment of procedural skills, the new nurses drew lots to choose among cardiopulmonary resuscitation, intravenous indwelling needle techniques, and intramuscular injection. The total score for the assessment was 100. The nurses were divided into 3 groups for the assessment. Two examiners evaluated their skills simultaneously and we took the average of their scores as the final score of the examinee. The 3 examiners received unified training before the test and referred to uniform standardized scoring criteria.
For the behavioral evaluation, we used the new nurses’ behavior, attitude, and role ability scale that we developed. The new nurses were asked to evaluate themselves at the end of 3 months and 6 months, and the head nurses were asked to evaluate them after 3 and 6 months of training. The scale included 3 dimensions: work attitude, clinical nursing ability, and interpersonal ability, with a total of 15 items. The scale was developed by the authors and tested with good reliability and validity and can be used. The Cronbach’s alpha coefficient for the overall scale was 0.957, with coefficients for each dimension of 0.914, 0.887, and 0.881, respectively. The Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin value of the scale was 0.938, with a significant probability value (P < .05), indicating that the scale was suitable for factor analysis.
At the result level, the evaluation consisted of assessing whether the training helped in the nurse’s contributed to the organization, using indexes such as the incidence of adverse events, patient satisfaction, and rate of patient complaints.
After 3 month of training, 201 self-evaluation questionnaires were distributed to new nurses, and 201 were collected. All the returned questionnaires were valid, with the effective rate of 100%. After 6 months of training, 199 self-evaluation questionnaires were distributed, and 199 were collected back. Two invalid questionnaires were excluded, and 197 were effectively recovered. The effective questionnaire recovery rate was 99%.
After 6 months of training, a total of 52 evaluation questionnaires were distributed to head nurses, and 52 were collected back, among which 52 were valid questionnaires, and the effective recovery rate was 100%.

## 
2.4. Statistical method
The SPSS 19.0 statistical software was used for statistical analysis and processing. The statistical methods used in this research include: The frequency and percentage were used to describe the general information of the research subjects and the satisfaction of the training nurses with the training program; The u test was used to analyze the theoretical scores and operational scores of new nurses during the 3 months of training and the 6 months of training, the behavioral attitudes of new nurses and post ability. The difference between the head nurse and the new nurse in the behavior attitude and post ability evaluation scale was analyzed by u test. The relationship between general data and behavioral attitude and post competence were analyzed by 1-way analysis of variance.

## 
2.5. Ethical considerations
This study was conducted with approval from the Ethics Committee of Renmin Hospital of Wuhan University. This study was conducted in accordance with the declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

## 
4. Discussion
The training program for new nurses showed high response rates and effectiveness, with 100% valid returns after 3 months and 99% after 6 months. Head nurses also had a 100% valid return rate for their evaluations. New nurses expressed high satisfaction with the training, particularly in areas such as well-prepared content, training evaluation methods, and the use of multimedia. Statistically significant improvements were noted in theoretical knowledge and procedural skills between the 3-month and 6-month assessments, with the most significant gains in cardiopulmonary resuscitation skills. Self-evaluation scores from new nurses were higher than those from head nurses, with significant differences in several areas, except for A1, A3, B4, and C11. New nurses showed more improvement in abilities related to B6, B9, C13, and C15. Factors such as being a fresh graduate, initial educational degree, and highest educational qualification influenced clinical nursing ability and interpersonal skills, indicating the importance of these factors in nurse training outcomes.

## 
4.1. Satisfaction evaluation and analysis

## 
4.1.1. New nurses are less satisfied with the training schedule, teaching methods, and whether they can meet their job needs
This study understands the satisfaction of new nurses with training from training teachers, training content and training organization. Consistent with Li’s[ ] research, new nurses had the lowest satisfaction with the training. Analyzing the reasons why the new nurses are not satisfied with the training schedule may be a heavy training task. In addition to completing the training task of the nursing department, the new nurses also have the training task of the department. The centralized training of the nursing department is once every 2 weeks, and the class time is mainly between 5 pm and 7 pm. Department training courses more frequently, most of the department of new nurses have 1 to 2 weekly training courses. New nurses are in the transitional stage between school and professional work, and the combination of learning classes and completing heavy training tasks may reduce their learning enthusiasm. This finding aligns with studies emphasizing the importance of emotional intelligence and self-efficacy in managing training-related stress and improving learning outcomes.[ , ] Therefore, in the new nurse standardization training, should be according to the actual situation of the new nurse and clinical work time to arrange training courses, pay attention to the quality of training, let the training really play its value.[ ]

## 
4.2. After the training, the theoretical and operational performance were improved, but the theoretical performance was lower than the operational performance
In this study, the average score at 3 months of training was 69.48 ± 5.62; the average score was 78.26 ± 8.27. During the 6 months of training, the average theoretical assessment score of new nurses was 79.63 ± 4.72, and the average score of operation assessment was 89.22 ± 6.13. The results showed that the average operation score of the 2 tests was higher than the theoretical score. This is consistent with the research results of Gang[ ] and Li.[ ] By comparing the average score of the theoretical knowledge and skill test after the training, Xue[ ] also found that the theoretical score was lower than the operation test score, and believed that this may be due to the frequent application of operational skills and the mastery of the theoretical knowledge requires long-term continuous independent learning and memory consolidation. This highlights the need for integrating effective learning strategies into training programs to enhance theoretical knowledge retention, as supported by studies on postgraduate medical education.[ ]

## 
4.3. Behavioral layer evaluation and analysis

## 
4.3.1. Clinical nursing ability and interpersonal skills of new nurses were improved after training
In the self-evaluation scores of the new nurses, The most promoted items are: “B6-New nurses after training, Can comprehensively assess the patient’s physical, mental, social and mental health status”, “B9-ward emergency (water cut, power cut, etc), Can respond quickly and initiate emergency plans”, “C13-can clearly verbally express facts, ideas and opinions”, “C15-written expression clearly, Written appropriately”, In Xue’s study,[ ] The items with the most significant scores were slightly different in problem solving ability, emergency response ability, education and education ability, communication and coordination ability, and behavior standardization. Meanwhile, in the present study “A2-new nurses after training, can abide by professional norms and rules and regulations,” “A3-new nurses after training, have ownership in the work, work earnestly, conscientious, excellence” 2 before and after the training no obvious difference, refer to the opinions of other scholars,[ ] may with “law, ethics practice” more emphasis on theoretical knowledge, part of the new graduate nurse during the school through theoretical study laid a certain legal ethics, the legal consciousness has existed before the entry.

## 
4.3.2. Except for the work attitude, the self-evaluation of the new nurses is generally higher than that of the head nurse
In this study, except for the 3 items under the work attitude dimension, the self-evaluation of all other items was higher than that of the head nurse. The high evaluation of the new nurses may be caused by the subjectivity of their own evaluation, but the new nurses have a good evaluation of themselves, which to some extent shows that the new nurses have improved their behavior, attitude and ability level through training. The reason why the head nurse’s evaluation is lower than the self-evaluation may be that, as a manager, the head nurse has high expectations for the new nurse,[ ] and hopes that the new nurse can complete the role change as soon as possible and devote themselves to the clinical nursing work. Li’s[ ] research results also show that new nurses are higher than their scores in terms of medical safety awareness and basic nursing technology operation ability, and believe that this may be caused by the strong subjectivity of their own evaluation and not objective enough.

## 
4.3.3. Analysis of the influence of the general information on the behavioral attitude ability of the new nurses
Factors influencing new nurses’ working attitude and role ability included the 1st degree, highest degree, mode of obtaining highest educational qualification, whether the nurse was a fresh graduate or not, and duration of internship. In this study, the 1st degree of new nurses was divided into 3 groups: “junior college,” “undergraduate” and “master and above.” For clinical nursing, “junior college,” “undergraduate” and “master and above” were significantly different, and new nurses with junior college scored lower in clinical nursing ability than new nurses with “undergraduate” and master’s degree or above. Moreover, new nurses were divided as “fresh graduates” and “non-fresh graduates.” New nurses gained higher scores in behavior and attitude ability than non-fresh graduates, but new nurses scored lower in clinical nursing ability and interpersonal skills. The study by Faraji et al[ ] found that theoretical knowledge, education level, and highest degree are positively correlated with nursing competence, consistent with the findings of this study. However, in certain aspects of competence, educational level is not directly proportional to ability. For instance, the study by Zhang et al[ ] demonstrated that in terms of infectious disease emergency response capabilities, nurses with a master’s degree or higher perform worse than those with a bachelor’s degree, highlighting the negative impact of higher education without practical experience.

## 
4.4. Evaluation of the achievement layer
Some scholars evaluate this level through the retention rate, cost-effectiveness and reflection diary. Stephenson’s[ ] research results confirm that the training of new nurses can significantly improve the retention rate of nurses. To analyze the turnover rate of new nurses, Asber[ ] found that after the implementation of standardized training of new nurses, the turnover rate of nurses decreased significantly, so the labor cost also decreased. In this study, patient complaint rate, incidence of adverse events and commendation letter were used to evaluate the outcome layer, that is, whether it can have a positive impact on the quality and performance of clinical nursing services in the department.
By the WHO estimates, 40% of working nurses are expected to retire by 2023, requiring about 12.9 million new nurses to maintain good health care.[ ] And the effect Evaluation is an important part of the standardized training of new nurses to improve the clinical practice ability of new nurses and nursing service quality has a heavy to mean. At present, the evaluation of new nurse training in our country has certain limitations. There is a lack of systematic, comprehensive and objective evaluation.[ – ] There is also lack of feedback, and lack of a mechanism to communicate evaluation results. Evaluation of training effectiveness is an indispensable part of a training program. Managers should attach importance to training evaluation, incorporate training effectiveness evaluation into their training plans, improve evaluation methods to assess effectiveness of standardized training for new nurses, and establish a more reasonable evaluation mechanism that can be used in the training of new nurses.
Based on the results of the training effect evaluation, this study recommends as follows:
Simplify the training process to ease the burden on new nurses. At present, the organization department of the training includes the nursing department, the general department and the work department. It is suggested to decide which department to implement the teaching according to different course types. For the training of policy regulations and humanistic quality, the whole hospital can be taught by the nursing department. For the theory and operation training, because each department has its own professionalism, it is suggested to be organized by the undergraduate department. The teaching content of the general department and the content of the department should be properly designed to avoid overlap and increase the learning burden of new nurses.
Carry out stratified training according to the academic qualifications. The current educational levels include “Specialty,” “Undergraduate college,” and “Postgraduate.” The theoretical foundation of college students is relatively weak, which can increase the theoretical learning time and the intensity of assessment. At the same time, because college students have no foundation for scientific research and learning, they cannot quickly.
The establishment of a perfect evaluation mechanism can apply the long-term Kirkpatrick model to the evaluation of new nurse training. At the end of each training task, a timely feedback and evaluation should be conducted.
The introduction of the “Training Syllabus for New Nurses” complies with the requirements of the development of contemporary medical and health undertakings. It not only provides a reference for medical units and nursing managers to carry out normative and standard training for new nurses, but also provides a learning direction for new nurses. The effect evaluation of the standardized training of new nurses will promote the rationalization and comprehensiveness of the training of new nurses in China and improve the overall level of nursing service.

## 
4.5. Limitation
The limitations of this study are as follows:
Although the outline has been introduced, the actual situation may differ for different hospitals in the implementation of a new nurse standardization training program since there will likely be individual differences in the management of the training work, and the effect evaluation cannot provide extensive data collection with a single hospital collecting sample data, so the research object is limited.
Due to the limitation of time and funds, the effect of the training was evaluated during the period of 6 months, so it was impossible to calculate the value of the training to the hospital, such as the improvement of patients’ satisfaction with nurses.
To ensure the sample size, the cluster sampling method was adopted, and the exact sample size was not calculated.

## 
4.6. Conclusion
The systematic and comprehensive evaluation of training programs for new nurses based on the 4 levels of the Kirkpatrick model can help nursing managers to identify the shortcomings in the standardized training for new nurses and the aspects that need improvement in their clinical work. Such analytical assessments can actively reduce the challenges found in training, so that training programs for new nurses can be truly effective. Future studies should focus on expanding the scope of evaluation to include multiple hospitals, extending the follow-up period to assess long-term outcomes, and employing more rigorous sampling methods to further validate the findings.
