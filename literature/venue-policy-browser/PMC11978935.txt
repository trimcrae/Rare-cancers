## Introduction

## Research background and motivations
With the rapid development of the global sports industry, digital technologies are profoundly influencing its operational models and management approaches. The structural optimization of the sports industry involves multiple dimensions, including event organization, resource allocation, spectator experience, and marketing. How to leverage advanced intelligent technologies to enhance industrial efficiency and optimize decision-making processes has become a core issue in the industry’s development – . In this context, the application of artificial intelligence and deep learning (DL) technologies has gradually emerged as a crucial means to promote the intelligent transformation of the sports industry. Among these, the Back Propagation Neural Network (BPNN), as a classic DL method, has demonstrated significant potential in data-driven optimization problems due to its powerful nonlinear mapping capabilities and adaptive learning characteristics . In recent years, the BPNN has been widely applied in areas such as intelligent prediction, optimized scheduling, and precision marketing, helping enterprises and organizations improve operational efficiency. However, despite its successful applications in various industries, research on its use in the structural optimization of the sports industry remains relatively limited . Sports industry data typically exhibit characteristics such as high dimensionality, dynamism, and multivariable coupling, making traditional optimization methods inadequate for effectively handling such complex data relationships. Therefore, exploring intelligent BPNN-based methods for optimizing the structure of the sports industry holds significant practical importance for enhancing decision-making capabilities in areas such as event scheduling, resource allocation, and industrial revenue.
In the development of the sports industry, managers face numerous challenges, including how to accurately predict event demand, optimize venue resource allocation, and improve user experience. Traditional decision-making methods often rely on empirical judgment and simple statistical analysis, which are insufficient for adapting to the complex data environment of the modern sports industry. Hence, the introduction of intelligent BPNN can leverage their robust data learning capabilities to enhance the scientific rigor and precision of optimization solutions.

## Literature review
In previous studies, Keshkar et al. found that the digital transformation of the sports industry was not only related to technological innovation and application but also closely tied to the overall restructuring of the industrial ecosystem. Stalmachova et al. , using empirical analysis, revealed that applying mobile internet, big data, and cloud computing technologies in the sports industry showed initial success by improving user experiences and optimizing resource allocation. However, they noted that the overall maturity of the transformation was low, and the integration of the upstream and downstream segments of the industry chain required significant improvement . Gilch and Sieweke focused on the challenges and opportunities within the digital transformation of the sports industry. They emphasized the critical role of supportive industrial policies, fostering an innovation-driven culture, and seizing cross-industry collaboration opportunities to promote a healthy and rapid digital transformation . Rathore highlighted the unique advantages of BPNN in handling nonlinear problems. BPNN’s flexible network structure and self-learning and self-optimizing capabilities provided powerful tools for data processing and decision-making across various industries . Liu et al. discovered that the application of mobile digital twins technology in the transportation sector enhanced the perception systems of connected and autonomous vehicles by creating high-fidelity, actionable geometric representations. It was especially critical to digitize traffic participants such as pedestrians and vehicles. Their study proposed a mobile digital twins visualization framework and designed an end-to-end system architecture to implement the visualization scheme. Moreover, its fidelity and synchronization were evaluated between physical and digital systems under different image sampling scenarios and wireless network conditions .
However, traditional research has often concentrated on specific aspects of the sports industry’s digital transformation, such as sports marketing or live event broadcasting. Comprehensive studies on optimizing and transforming the entire industry chain remain limited. This gap leaves a lack of systematic theoretical research on effectively achieving structural optimization and digital transformation at the whole industry chain level. This study addresses this gap by integrating interdisciplinary theories from information technology, artificial intelligence, and sports industry management. Specifically, it explores the deep application of intelligent BPNN technology in restructuring the sports industry, establishing a new paradigm for its digital transformation. This study focuses on the impact of digital technologies on isolated fields. Meanwhile, it comprehensively investigates how intelligent BPNN can optimize the overall structure of the sports industry, driving digital transformation and enhancing the efficiency of the entire industry chain.
A review of existing literature shows that prior research has provided valuable theoretical foundations and practical insights for understanding the digital transformation of the sports industry. However, the practical implementation of deep optimization across the entire industry chain remains an underexplored area. The innovation lies in leveraging the advantages of intelligent BPNN to propose a smart methodology for the global optimization of the sports industry, addressing the gap in industry chain-level optimization. This study offers new perspectives and approaches for future research in this domain.

## Research model

## Intelligent BPNN
BPNN is a multi-layer feedforward artificial neural network trained by error backpropagation. This network adjusts its weight parameters dynamically by propagating input signals forward and errors backwards, ultimately achieving input signals’ accurate mapping and prediction – . The structure of a BPNN is mainly divided into input, hidden, and output layers. The input layer receives raw data signals from external sources. One or more hidden layers process these signals and are responsible for the abstraction and characterization of the features. The output layer generates the network’s final decision or prediction . Nodes in different layers are associated through weighted links, with nodes in the same layer typically not directly connected – . Its algorithm flow is presented in Fig.  . 
BPNN relies on large datasets and complex mathematical iterations to gradually “learn” and “approximate” the required mapping relationship. This capability makes it highly effective for solving many problems, such as function approximation, classification prediction, feature extraction, and pattern recognition – . Through such algorithmic flow, BPNN has become a vital technology in AI and ML .

## Construction of the optimized model based on intelligent BPNN
In today’s data-driven era, intelligent BPNN, a powerful ML tool, has been extensively applied in optimization and decision-making in various industries – . In the sports industry, adopting intelligent BPNN can improve decision-making accuracy and optimize the industrial structure, fostering healthy development – . The core design concept of this model involves the intelligent analysis and DL of key data within the sports industry, uncovering potential optimization space and growth points in the industrial structure. This includes but is not limited to event organization, audience engagement, marketing effectiveness, the utilization rate of sports facilities, etc. By analyzing these critical factors, the model aims to provide scientific and accurate decision support to help the sports industry achieve the optimal allocation of resources and ongoing structural improvements. The specific type of BPNN selected for this study is a multi-layer feedforward neural network. This is based on a comprehensive consideration of its task suitability, alignment with the characteristics of the data, and the balance between model complexity and performance. This choice not only meets the needs of optimizing the sports industry structure but also ensures a good balance among computational resources, practical operability, and model performance. It incorporates a feature selection and dynamic weighting mechanism, where key features (e.g., event time, venue utilization) are selected through feature importance ranking at the input data layer. Combined with dynamic weight allocation, the model effectively captures the most significant features while discarding irrelevant or weakly correlated data. This mechanism successfully avoids the redundant computations typically encountered by traditional BPNNs when handling high-dimensional data, thereby enhancing the model’s generalization capability. In addition, the model design introduces an innovative multi-objective optimization strategy that balances multiple goals, such as maximizing resource utilization, enhancing user satisfaction, and minimizing operational costs. The optimized model can dynamically balance multiple decision objectives rather than optimizing a single performance metric by embedding the multi-objective function into the loss function and adjusting model parameters through the backpropagation algorithm. Furthermore, the optimized model employs an adaptive dynamic learning rate strategy. This allows the model to converge rapidly in the early stages of training while dynamically adjusting the learning rate to prevent overfitting in later stages. Unlike traditional BPNNs, which use a fixed learning rate, this dynamic adjustment improves both the stability and accuracy of the model as the data scale increases and with prolonged training periods. In terms of architecture, the optimized model integrates advanced ideas, such as skip connections and residual structures, in addition to the traditional BPNN framework. These modifications help mitigate the gradient vanishing problem and enhance the model’s ability to learn from high-dimensional, complex data. As a result, the model exhibits improved expressive power and training efficiency. Despite its complexity, the optimized model reduces the risk of overfitting, often associated with high-dimensional data, through the introduction of various optimization strategies, including regularization, dynamic feature selection, and early stopping. 
The optimized model of sports industry structure based on intelligent BPNN offers scientific and effective support for its structure optimization and decision-making through the in-depth study and analysis of industrial data. As technology advances and the data volume grows, the model is expected to play an even more critical role in the development of the sports industry.

## Experimental design and performance evaluation

## Datasets collection
The dataset used in this study is the Kaggle-Sports Category dataset, a globally renowned data science and ML competition platform. It can provide a stage for data scientists, researchers, and ML engineers to demonstrate and improve their skills. Kaggle allows users to access and download various datasets while providing an interactive community where users can share insights, solutions, and code. In Kaggle’s “Sports Category, the user can find a variety of sports-related datasets. These datasets cover football, basketball, baseball, and many other sports. The dataset consists of approximately 50,000 samples, each representing a record relevant to the sports industry. These records may contain information about specific events, venue utilization, or the effectiveness of marketing campaigns. Each sample includes approximately 15–20 features encompassing various aspects of sports activities. The primary features are categorized as follows:

Event-related: Event time
Event type (e.g., football, basketball, baseball)
Participating teams or athletes
Event outcome (score, win/loss)

Audience and marketing-related:Audience size
Audience satisfaction rating
Box office revenue
Merchandise sales volume

Venue and resource-related:Venue utilization rate
Venue maintenance costs
Venue equipment usage
Labels (target variable)

For this study, different labels from the dataset are used to construct the model. The dataset is divided according to the common 80:20 ratio, with 80% allocated for training the model to learn the relationships between features and labels and the remaining 20% reserved for evaluating the model’s performance and testing its ability to generalize on unseen data.
The primary reason for selecting this dataset is that it not only covers a wide range of sports but also provides detailed match results and player performance data. The data can effectively support research on the digital transformation and structural optimization of the sports industry. Specifically, this study aims to optimize the structure of the sports industry and improve resource utilization efficiency, particularly in event scheduling, audience engagement, and facility management. Sports event scheduling involves multiple factors, including venue, time, and participating teams. By analyzing match data, team rankings, and related statistics from the dataset, insight into the impact of events at different times can be gained. Thus, it helps to optimize event schedules and maximize audience engagement and venue utilization at various stages. Audience engagement is a critical component of the sports industry. By analyzing match data, the popularity of different types of events can be assessed, providing data support for optimizing event scheduling and audience engagement. For example, analyzing audience traffic, viewing duration, and other information for specific periods or event types can help organizers develop strategies to attract audiences. The utilization rate of sports venues is crucial for the industry’s operational efficiency. The player statistics and event schedules in the dataset reflect the demand for venues for different matches, which can help optimize venue scheduling and resource allocation. Moreover, information on match outcomes and team performance can assist managers in predicting demand fluctuations for various venues, thus improving the flexibility and efficiency of venue management.
In summary, the Kaggle—Sports Category dataset provides rich, real-world data. It can effectively support model training and optimization, particularly in addressing specific challenges faced by the sports industry, such as event scheduling, audience engagement, and facility management. Therefore, selecting this dataset ensures the practical relevance and high applicability of the experimental results, enhancing this study’s applied value and practical significance.

## Experimental environment and parameters setting
The experimental environment is shown in Table .
For the validity of the experimental results, the model’s parameters are uniformly set. The learning rate determines the step size for parameter updates in the model. In the experiment, the learning rate is set to 0.01, as it typically provides a good balance during the training process. It is not too large to cause instability nor too small to result in excessively slow convergence. For most neural network models, 0.01 is a commonly used starting value, particularly when employing the Adam optimizer. The Adam optimizer adaptively adjusts the learning rate for each parameter, allowing it to accommodate different stages of training. The number of hidden layers is set to 3, which directly impacts the model’s expressiveness. Three hidden layers are chosen because this is generally a balanced choice, providing sufficient complexity to capture the nonlinear relationships in the data without making the model overly complex, thus avoiding overfitting. Three hidden layers are a typical configuration for most BPNN-based optimization tasks, as they effectively handle task diversity. The number of nodes in each hidden layer is 64. The number of nodes determines the capacity of each hidden layer, i.e., the model’s complexity. Sixty-four nodes are typically sufficient to meet the information processing needs of sports industry optimization tasks while avoiding the waste of computational resources. This value is neither too large, which helps prevent overfitting due to excessive model complexity, nor too small, ensuring the network has enough learning capacity. The batch size is set to 52, which represents the amount of data used for each model update. A batch size of 52 is selected to balance efficiency during training and memory usage. Smaller batch sizes can speed up training and reduce memory requirements, while larger can accelerate gradient computation and reduce noise. In this task, 52 is a relatively moderate and commonly used choice, ensuring training stability and efficient utilization of computational resources. The optimizer used is Adam, which is one of the most commonly used optimization algorithms. Adam combines the advantages of Momentum and adaptive learning rate optimization algorithms, allowing for the adaptive adjustment of each parameter’s learning rate, thus accelerating convergence and improving training stability. Adam performs well on tasks involving large datasets and complex structures, which is why it is chosen for this study. The number of training iterations is set to 50, determining the number of times the model would be trained on the data. The selection of 50 iterations is based on experimental experience and a reasonable estimate for the dataset size. For most neural network problems, 50 iterations are typically sufficient for the model to learn the patterns in the data without overfitting. Excessive training iterations can lead to overfitting the training set, negatively impacting the model’s generalization ability on the test set. The loss function used is Mean Squared Error (MSE), a commonly used loss function for regression tasks, which calculates the MSE between predicted and actual values. During optimization, MSE effectively reflects the discrepancy between the model’s predictions and the exact target, guiding the update of model parameters. MSE is chosen as the loss function in this study because the goal is to make the predicted values as close as possible to the actual values, thereby improving the overall model accuracy. The activation function used is Sigmoid, an S-shaped curve function commonly applied for output layers in binary classification problems but also applicable to hidden layers. Its output range is between 0 and 1, with good smoothness and nonlinear properties. Sigmoid is chosen as the activation function because it effectively maps inputs to the 0–1 range, which is particularly suited for tasks requiring probabilistic outputs or outputs constrained within a specific range. The regularization function used is Dropout, an effective technique for preventing overfitting in neural networks. Dropout randomly disables a portion of neurons during training, preventing the model from relying on specific nodes and thus improving its generalization ability. Dropout is selected as the regularization function because it effectively reduces overfitting during training, especially in deeper networks or when the dataset is small. Dropout is a simple yet efficient regularization method widely applicable to most neural network models.
The selection of parameters is grounded in a thorough understanding of the BPNN’s training process, incorporating established empirical guidelines and the specific requirements of this study. Key parameters, such as learning rate, batch size, and the number of iterations, are chosen to ensure training stability and facilitate faster convergence. The selection of hidden layer node counts and optimizers aims to enhance the model’s learning capacity and predictive performance. Furthermore, the activation function and loss function are specifically chosen to align with the demands of regression tasks. Through these carefully calibrated parameter configurations, the BPNN is effectively employed to optimize the structure of the sports industry, yielding promising experimental results in practical applications.

## Performance evaluation

## Comparative experiment of model performance
The experimental comparison models are Support Vector Machine (SVM) and Random Forest (RF), and the comparison performance indicators include model precision, accuracy, F1 score, and recall. Precision measures the proportion of true positive samples among those predicted as positive by the model. In other words, precision reflects the proportion of true positives among all the samples classified as positive by the model. High precision means that the model makes fewer false positive predictions when identifying the positive class. Accuracy measures the proportion of correct predictions (both positive and negative) among all predictions made by the model. Specifically, accuracy is the ratio of all correct predictions to the total number of predicted samples. Accuracy provides an overall measure of the model’s correct prediction ability, but in cases of imbalanced data, accuracy may mask the model’s performance on the minority class samples. The F1 score is the weighted average of precision and recall, mainly used to evaluate the model’s overall performance in classification tasks concerning the positive class. The F1 score considers the balance between precision and recall, making it a crucial indicator for tasks with imbalanced data or when high recognition of the positive class is required. A higher F1 score means the model can maintain a high precision without sacrificing recall. Recall measures the proportion of true positive samples correctly identified by the model among all actual positive samples. In other words, recall reflects the model’s ability to identify all positive samples. High recall means the model can identify as many positive samples as possible, minimizing the chance of missing true positives. The experimental results are revealed in Fig.  . 
Figure  demonstrates that in terms of precision, as data volume increases, the precision of the optimized model rises from 0.85 to 0.90, showing a significant improvement. This indicates that intelligent BPNN can effectively utilize more data for learning, thus enhancing the model’s prediction precision. In comparison, the precision of the SVM increases gradually from 0.82 at 1000 data volumes to 0.86 at 3000, demonstrating a positive but less pronounced improvement. The precision of RF shows a similar trend, with its precision rising from 0.80 at 1000 data points to 0.85 at 3000, although the gain is also more limited. Considering accuracy, at a data volume of 1000, the optimized model achieves an accuracy of 0.85, outperforming SVM (0.83) and RF (0.82). As the data volume increases to 3000, the accuracy of the optimized model improves to 0.91, significantly surpassing SVM’s 0.88 and RF’s 0.87. In terms of recall, the optimized model consistently outperforms SVM and RF at all data volumes, and its recall increases the most with adding data volume, from 0.83 to 0.89. This suggests that the optimized model can learn more efficiently from the increased data, enhancing its ability to correctly identify positive classes as positive. Regarding F1 scores, as the data volume grows, the three models’ F1 scores all present an upward trend. The F1 score of the optimized model increases from 0.84 at 1000 data volumes to 0.92 at 300, showing the most prominent increase.

## Discussion
Through comparative experiments on model performance, the optimized model demonstrates exceptional adaptability and advantages across multiple performance metrics, strongly supporting the substantial impact of digital transformation on the structural optimization of the sports industry.
In terms of accuracy, the optimized model exhibits remarkable adaptability, particularly in handling large-scale data, where its performance shows significant improvement. This suggests that, in the context of optimizing the sports industry structure, the model can enhance the precision of data processing, leading to improved decision-making processes. For instance, in resource scheduling or venue allocation, higher accuracy enables managers to predict activity demand with greater precision, facilitating more efficient resource allocation, reducing waste, and enhancing operational efficiency. Regarding precision, the optimized model showcases excellent feature learning and data processing capabilities. As the data volume increases, its precision improves substantially. This is particularly vital in the sports industry, where industry data often involves high complexity and multidimensional characteristics. The model can more accurately predict audience demand and venue usage by improving precision, enabling more precise resource allocation decisions. For example, in event scheduling, enhanced precision helps optimize both the audience experience and venue utilization. In terms of recall, the optimized model demonstrates notable improvement, benefiting from its deep structure and nonlinear mapping ability, which allows it to better identify and classify complex data patterns. This capability is essential for the structural optimization of the sports industry. For example, accurately identifying popular events or projects helps managers seize market opportunities and optimize resource allocation. An increase in recall enables the model to more comprehensively detect potential demand or opportunities, providing more comprehensive decision support in event planning and facility management, while reducing the risk of overlooking substantial demand. With respect to the F1 score, the optimized model exhibits significant performance improvements as data volume increases, demonstrating its ability to maintain high precision while effectively identifying a greater number of positive instances. This capability is crucial for practical applications within the sports industry. For example, an improved F1 score in event scheduling enables managers to accurately predict demand while ensuring that all key sports events or target audiences are covered, ultimately improving overall resource utilization and operational efficiency.
In the simulation experiments, the optimized model’s efficiency in resource optimization was clearly demonstrated. This improvement in efficiency primarily results from the model’s ability to predict and optimize resource allocation with greater accuracy, leading to a significant increase in resource utilization in practical applications. Additionally, in terms of user satisfaction, the model exhibited considerable potential by enhancing venue management, event organization, and service quality, thus improving the overall user experience. This aspect is crucial for various sectors within the sports industry.
Regarding economic benefits, the optimized model generated higher returns from peripheral product sales and sponsor investments, owing to its data analysis and DL capabilities. For instance, the model’s ability to predict event attractiveness and market demand more accurately helps operators develop targeted marketing strategies and partnership models. However, the model’s high operational costs serve as a reminder for decision-makers to conduct thorough cost–benefit analyses before implementation to ensure that the economic returns can offset the associated costs.
Compared to Miao , this study places a stronger emphasis on the specific context of the sports industry. By integrating regularization and feature selection techniques, as well as applying dynamic weighting to data features, this study mitigates interference from redundant features in high-dimensional data, further enhancing the model’s generalization ability. In contrast to the work of Chadwick et al. , this study not only adopts an adaptive learning rate strategy but also incorporates domain-specific knowledge to address key challenges in the sports industry, such as resource optimization and event scheduling. This targeted optimization has significantly improved the model’s adaptability and practical utility in complex task scenarios.
In conclusion, the application of the optimized model in the digital transformation of the sports industry demonstrates significant potential. However, further improvements are required in areas such as computational efficiency, data quality, and interpretability to enhance its practicality and adaptability. Future research should explore the model’s long-term effects and sustainability in dynamic environments, thereby providing more robust support for the ongoing digital transformation of the sports industry.

## Conclusion

## Research contribution
This study has made remarkable achievements in applying intelligent BPNN to optimize sports industry structure and has multiple contributions in theory and practice. Firstly, at the theoretical level, this study represents the first systematic exploration of using intelligent BPNN technology to optimize sports industry structure. It provides a new theoretical perspective for the digital transformation of the sports industry and broadens the theoretical boundary of sports industry research. Secondly, from a methodological standpoint, based on intelligent BPNN, the proposed model optimizes resource allocation and improves user satisfaction by accurately processing and predicting large-scale data. Meanwhile, several model optimization technologies are explored, significantly enhancing model performance and application efficiency. Practically, by comparing with other models, this study confirms the dominant advantages of intelligent BPNN in enhancing economic benefits, increasing user satisfaction, and advancing resource utilization. This offers valuable insights and guidance for sports industry managers and decision-makers. Furthermore, this study has a vital social contribution to the sustainable development of the sports industry, enhancing the market competitiveness of sports events and improving the public’s enthusiasm for sports activities. In summary, the findings of this study enrich the theoretical knowledge base related to sports industry structure. Moreover, these results also provide effective solution strategies and practical guidance for promoting scientific and technological innovation and industrial upgrading of the sports industry, which has significant academic value and application prospects.

## Future works and research limitations
Although this study has advanced the application of intelligent BPNNs for optimizing the structure of the sports industry, several limitations persist, highlighting potential directions for future research. First, the dataset utilized in this study is primarily derived from publicly available data, which may limit the depth and breadth of the model’s training. Future research could aim to incorporate more diverse data sources, such as internal enterprise data, social media data, and Internet of Things sensor data, to more comprehensively capture key features within the sports industry. This expansion of data sources would not only enhance the model’s generalization ability but also improve its applicability and accuracy in real-world scenarios. Second, while the current intelligent BPNN model performs well across various areas, its “black-box” nature in decision-making may hinder its acceptance by management and stakeholders. Future research should focus on enhancing the model’s interpretability. Techniques such as visualization methods, attention mechanisms, or interpretability algorithms could be employed to render the decision-making process more transparent and intuitive. This would help management and stakeholders better understand the model’s operations, thereby increasing its practical value and fostering greater trust in its recommendations. Furthermore, the experiments and simulations conducted in this study primarily address short-term effects without evaluating the model’s long-term outcomes and sustainability. Future research should develop extended evaluation frameworks to assess the model’s stability and sustained benefits over time. Such evaluations are crucial for validating the model’s adaptability in dynamic environments and ensuring the long-term sustainability of the sports industry.
In summary, future research should deepen exploration in areas such as data sources, model interpretability, and long-term effect evaluation. These efforts will contribute to providing more practical and forward-thinking solutions for the digital transformation and structural optimization of the sports industry.
