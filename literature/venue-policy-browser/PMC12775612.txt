As we close the December 2025 issue, the Journal of Spine Surgery (JSS) marks 10 years of publication and outlines the direction for its next decade.

## Transition to monthly publication
From January 2026, JSS will move from a quarterly to a monthly format. This shift reflects increased submission volume, expanding global readership, and the need for more rapid dissemination of clinically meaningful data. Monthly publication will support ongoing dialogue around emerging techniques and technologies, trial outcomes, and translational research, while giving authors a more responsive platform for their work.

## A decade of growth and influence
Since its launch in 2015, JSS has progressed from a new entrant to a firmly established and influential spine-surgery journal with a global readership. Our 2023 CiteScore reached 5.6, reflecting steady citation growth, and inclusion in the Emerging Sources Citation Index confirms the journal’s trajectory toward receiving its first official impact factor in the upcoming cycle. Importantly, several JSS publications now rank among the highest-cited works in contemporary spine literature. Foremost among these is the landmark article on lumbar interbody fusion published in our inaugural issue ( ), which has become one of the most cited papers in the entire spine-surgery field over the past decade. This level of impact highlights JSS’s capacity to publish work that not only informs practice but shapes international discourse within our specialty.

## Special issues as engines of thought leadership
One of the journal’s defining strengths has been its thematic special editions. These volumes have allowed JSS to highlight areas undergoing rapid evolution and to consolidate expert perspectives in a single reference point. Among them, the 2019 Endoscopic Spine Surgery special issue stands out ( ). At the time, endoscopy was accelerating in global prominence, and our early support of that edition helped bring emerging techniques, workflows, and evidence into mainstream academic discussion. It remains a highly accessed collection and reflects our willingness to spotlight technologies and concepts ahead of the curve.

## Reviewers: the foundation of scientific integrity
The strength of JSS rests on a dedicated and expert reviewer cohort. Their work preserves the accuracy, transparency, and reliability of our publications. As scientific output increases worldwide, and as authors adopt new digital and artificial intelligence (AI)-based tools, detailed peer review becomes even more critical. Our reviewers provide methodological scrutiny, nuanced clinical interpretation, and constructive guidance that shape the final published record. JSS’s reputation is directly tied to its expertise and dedication.

## Scientific integrity in the age of AI
AI’s role in biomedical publishing is expanding rapidly. These tools offer efficiency in drafting, data processing, and figure preparation, yet they also raise concerns about authenticity, fabrication, and subtle distortion of results. JSS is actively developing robust AI-use and disclosure guidelines, paired with strengthened screening processes for image manipulation, text-generation artefacts, and statistical inconsistencies. While AI may assist scholarship, it cannot replace human judgement, ethical reasoning, or the contextual understanding that defines rigorous scientific work. Our commitment moving forward is to maintain publication standards that reflect transparency, accountability, and clinical relevance.

## The future: consolidating JSS as a premier venue for academic and clinical leadership
Looking ahead, our focus is to preserve and strengthen JSS as a leading platform for academic excellence and thought leadership in spine surgery. This includes:
promoting high-quality clinical, translational, and basic science research;
expanding our global contributor base;
nurturing collaborations across orthopaedics, neurosurgery, biomechanics, informatics, and rehabilitation;
supporting early-career researchers;
providing space for debate on evolving technologies, policy, ethics, and workforce issues in spine care.
As the field becomes more multidisciplinary and technology-driven, we aim to ensure JSS remains a journal where new ideas are tested, refined, and disseminated with clarity and rigor.

## Closing remarks
To our authors, reviewers, readers, and editorial board, thank you for contributing to the growth of JSS over the past decade. Your work has shaped the journal into a trusted, globally recognised source of spine-surgery scholarship. With monthly publication, strengthened reviewer networks, and forward-looking editorial priorities, we enter the next decade with purpose and momentum.
