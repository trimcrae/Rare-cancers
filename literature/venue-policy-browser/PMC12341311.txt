## Abstract
This paper empirically investigates the impact of religious beliefs on residents’ happiness based on CGSS data from 2010 to 2021. The specific impact of religious beliefs on residents’ happiness is estimated by gender, urban-rural status, health status, and regional distribution. Endogeneity is addressed through the use of instrumental variable and propensity score matching methods. A mediation effect model is employed to test the significance of the intermediate path played by residents’ self-evaluation of social status and depression frequency between religious beliefs and happiness. The conclusions are as follows: (1) Religious belief has a significant promoting effect on happiness, and this promoting effect satisfies causal inference. (2) Religious belief has a specific impact on the happiness of different groups. The positive promoting effect of religious belief on the happiness of groups in the western region, men, urban areas, and those in poor health is stronger. (3) The mechanism analysis shows that religious belief indirectly acts on the improvement of residents’ happiness by enhancing their self-assessed social status and providing emotional support. The depression frequency does not play the intermediate mechanism role of religious belief affecting happiness, but residents with a higher depression frequency can obtain a greater increase in happiness through religious belief.
Keywords: Religious belief, Happiness, Self-rated of social status, Mechanism analysis, Empirical analysis

## Introduction
The concept of happiness can be defined as a comprehensive assessment and special spiritual experience that is shaped by individuals according to their personal standards regarding their social and economic standing, occupational and living conditions, physical health, and life experiences. The pursuit of happiness represents the ultimate aspiration of every nation and individual, as well as the value pursuit of economic growth and social development. Consequently, happiness research has received extensive discussion and special attention from sociologists, psychologists, and economists. The prevailing view in traditional economic theory is that sustained economic growth is the primary determinant of human well-being and happiness. In order to enhance the well-being of residents, it is essential to guarantee the continuity of economic expansion. However, Easterlin (2010) reached a different conclusion on the relationship between income and happiness among American residents after World War II. He found that economic growth does not consistently lead to an increase in residents’ happiness, which challenges the traditional economic viewpoint. [ ]. Empirical research has indicated that after a specific threshold of economic development is reached, it becomes relatively easy to fall into Easterlin happiness trap. The advent of the paradox of happiness has prompted a shift in the focus of academic research on the determinants of happiness towards non-economic factors [ ]. In parallel with China’s economic growth, social transformation and changes in residents’ production and lifestyle, material living conditions have improved markedly for residents, there has been a notable enhancement in the material living conditions of residents, a progressive improvement in the social security system and an overall rise in the social welfare level. However, the observed increase in wealth accumulation has not been accompanied by a corresponding enhancement in the subjective well-being of residents. In certain periods, there has been a decline in happiness levels, which suggests that the advancement of economic prosperity and material well-being does not inherently lead to an increase in happiness [ ]. Human beings are a complex entity, comprising three key dimensions: materiality, sociality, and spirituality. The correlation between material resources and happiness is not straightforward. The quality of social relationships, the state of psychological well-being, and the presence of positive spiritual guidance also contribute to the overall happiness of residents. Happiness is influenced by a multitude of factors, including objective material life experiences and subjective mental states. Religion serves as a factor regulating people’s spiritual needs and psychological conditions. Does the belief in religion contribute to the improvement of residents’ sense of happiness? If religion can facilitate the advancement of residents’ sense of happiness, then what constitutes the reasonable way for religion to “bestow happiness”? The aforementioned questions have emerged as the key topics that researchers need to focus on and the core of empirical tests [ ].
Chinese religious culture boasts a lengthy and profound history, characterized by intense clan and folk beliefs. From the initial formation of ancient totem worship and immortal systems, to the rise of local religions and the integration of Buddhism with traditional Chinese values, and then to the wide-ranging dissemination of Western religions in China, diversified religious culture gradually fuses profoundly with Chinese traditional culture. The unique architecture, cultural customs, and religious scriptures bequeathed by religion are spread throughout the country, highlighting the influence of religious culture and indirectly reflecting the faith needs of the residents. Over the past few years, China’s rapid economic growth and social structural changes have not only changed residents’ production and lifestyle, but also had an undeniable impact on their spiritual life and religious beliefs [ ]. The White Paper on China’s Policies and Practices to Safeguard Religious Belief and Freedom, released in 2018, shows that there are as many as 200 million religious believers and over 380,000 religious clergy in China. Religious beliefs play a significant role in value guidance, spiritual consolation, grassroots governance, and the dissemination of religious culture. The integration of diversified religious culture and traditional Chinese culture gradually accommodates the needs of social and economic transformation in long-term development, forming a religious culture with typical Chinese characteristics and indigenous features. Chinese religions differ from Western religions in terms of institutionalization and organization, with local religions exhibiting more obvious characteristics of loose religious organization and low religious institutionalization as a diffused religion. Consequently, within the institutionalized and organized religious framework in the West, applicability scope of Western scholars’ research on the relationship between religious beliefs and happiness in the cultural background of China’s religious system is limited restricted.
Researchers have extensively explored various factors that determine happiness from the perspectives of macroeconomic environment such as economic growth, income distribution, inflation, and environmental pollution, as well as individual characteristics such as social status, income level, work status, and education attainment [ , ]. However, from the perspective of the happiness effect of faith, research on the promotion effect of religious faith on happiness is still relatively scarce. The research on the economic consequences and subjective welfare impact of religion as an informal system demands urgent deepening and broadening. Due to the availability of data, research on the relationship between faith and happiness often uses local survey data or small sample case analysis. The conclusions drawn are limited in terms of external validity and policy applicability, and there is an urgent need to use nationally representative large sample data to conduct accurate empirical tests on the relationship between the two. On this basis, using data from 8 waves of Chines General Social Survey conducted between 2010 and 2021, this study empirically investigates the impact of residents’ religious beliefs on their sense of happiness from three aspects: whether they believe in religion, the type of religious belief, and the frequency of religious activity participation. The study also explores the group heterogeneity of the relationship between religious beliefs and residents’ sense of happiness based on gender, urban-rural distribution, health status, and regional distribution. The mechanism analysis examines the mediating role of self-evaluation of social status and depression frequency in the relationship between religious beliefs and residents’ sense of happiness effect.
The contribution and innovation of this paper can be summarized in three aspects. Firstly, this article provides a nuanced and multifaceted examination of the relationship between religious beliefs and happiness. It delves into the positive effects of religious beliefs on happiness from various dimensions, such as religious affiliation, type of belief, and frequency of religious activities. Furthermore, it acknowledges the heterogeneity of this relationship across different groups, offering a more comprehensive and nuanced understanding of how religious beliefs contribute to individual happiness. This analysis provides empirical evidence for promoting happiness through non-economic factors like spiritual beliefs. Secondly, to ensure the robustness of its findings, multiple advanced econometric techniques have been employed to address potential endogeneity issues and self-selection biases. By using instrumental variable methods and propensity score matching, this study effectively corrects for biases that could arise from reverse causality or non-random religious decision-making. This rigorous approach strengthens the credibility of the causal relationship between religious beliefs and happiness, enhancing the reliability and generalizability of the findings. Thirdly, to uncover the underlying mechanisms that explain how religious beliefs contribute to happiness, this paper utilizes a mediation effect model. By examining the role of residents’ self-evaluation of social status and depression frequency as potential mediators, this study sheds light on the indirect pathways through which religious beliefs can positively impact happiness. This analysis provides empirical support for the spiritual guidance function and instrumental value of religious beliefs, further elucidating the complex interplay between spiritual beliefs and individual well-being.
The remaining part of this paper is structured as follows. The second part constitutes a literature review and hypothesis formulation. The third part introduces the data sources, model settings, and conducts descriptive statistical analysis on the relationship between religious beliefs and residents’ happiness. The fourth part presents the empirical test results and analysis. The fifth part performs robustness tests and endogeneity discussions on the self-selection bias and endogeneity issues. The sixth part employs a mediation effect model to test the mediating effect of self-evaluation of social status and depression frequency on the relationship between religious beliefs and residents’ happiness. The seventh part extracts the main research conclusions and corresponding policy recommendations.

## Literature review and theoretical hypotheses

## The influence of religious beliefs on happiness
Extensive research establishes key determinants of happiness. Income growth, quality-of-life improvements, and access to public services enhance well-being, while unemployment, inflation, income inequality, and environmental pollution undermine it [ – ]. The relationship between religious belief and happiness remains contested, yielding conflicting findings [ ]. Predominantly, studies confirm that belief provides solace and belonging, enhancing mental health and subjective well-being by instilling life meaning and inner peace [ ]. Religiosity is further associated with altruistic and conscientious traits, potentially boosting psychological well-being relative to non-believers [ ].
Empirical studies confirm religion’s positive happiness correlation in Asia [ ], though mechanisms remain unexplored. Using nationally representative data, Green & Elliott (2010) finds religious identity associates with enhanced well-being and health independent of work, family, financial, and social support conditions [ ]. ​However, other research finds no significant link between religious belief/practice and well-being. SLSCR 2007 data reveals no significant link between religiosity and well-being in China, with Taoism and Christianity even exhibiting negative impacts [ ]. ​​Research on Western traditional religions suggests that while they are typically associated with enhanced well-being, they may also reduce well-being through mechanisms such as religious anxieties and psychologically demanding ritual practices [ ].​ The relationship between religious belief and well-being remains confusing, and needs to be empirically tested with higher-quality nationally representative data. Based on this, competing hypotheses 1a and 1b are proposed:

## Hypothesis 1a

Religious belief can positively facilitate the enhancement of residents’ happiness.

## Hypothesis 1b

There is no significant correlation between religious belief and residents’ happiness, and even leads to the deterioration of residents’ happiness.

## The mechanism by which religious beliefs affect happiness
The following theoretical interpretations can be applied to the internal mechanism of the influence of religious beliefs on happiness. One is the spiritual guiding function of religious beliefs [ ]. In the opinion of Freud, one of the key roles of religious culture is to safeguard and enhance the well-being of humanity. This can be exemplified by the ways in which Buddhist customs facilitate the regulation of negative emotions [ ]. Religion enhances well-being via spiritual resources within communities and therapeutic contexts, facilitating cognitive-emotional restructuring and catharsis, as evidenced in practices like prayer and spiritually integrated therapy [ ]. Religious values encourage spiritual fulfillment over excessive materialism and utilitarian pursuits. In the face of traumatic crises and economic adversity, those who experience such circumstances often find solace and a sense of well-being through spiritual and psychological resilience. Based on this, the following hypothesis is proposed:

## Hypothesis 2

Religious beliefs contribute to an increase in residents’ happiness levels by reducing depression frequency.

Religious beliefs prompt adherents to direct their attention towards spiritual fulfilment, as opposed to pursuing material satisfaction and a sense of superiority that transcends reference groups. This diminishes the psychological impact of material living standards, social structure, and external shock events on residents, bolsters believers’ positive self-evaluation and subjective identification of their own socio-economic status [ ]. Religious practices expand social networks and reinforce trust, which constitute the core elements of social capital​​ [ ]. ​Concurrently, virtues such as diligence and integrity advocated by religious teachings enhance economic capital through improved productive behaviors​​ [ ]. ​​The mediating effects of these social and economic capital channels significantly enhance subjective socioeconomic status perception among adherents. In addition, empirical research demonstrates that subjective socioeconomic status (SES) identification exerts a significant positive effect on well-being. This effect is even more pronounced than that of objective SES [ , ]. In light of this, one potential path is put forth for the influence of religious beliefs on happiness:

## Hypothesis 3
Religious beliefs improve residents’ self-rated socio-economic status, thereby enhancing their level of happiness.
Religion’s instrumental value stems from social network embeddedness. Social networks facilitate information sharing, resource support, and risk-sharing, expanding residents’ capacity for purposive action. Social interactions foster reciprocal trust and resource exchange through informal constraints, reducing uncertainty and enhancing embedded members’ belonging and security. It has been observed that religious activities have the potential to expand the size of the social network of believers [ ], provide believers with a sense of belonging to the social network, emotional solace and social support through organized religious activities. The social relationships established in religious activities and the shared participation in religious activities have a positive promoting effect on happiness, which is also an important reason why religious participation brings happiness enhancement to people [ ]. Religion has a certain risk-sharing effect. By providing social support and mutual benefits after negative external shocks, religion mitigates the dampening effect of external risk events on residents’ happiness. China has a strong relationship-based society and the characteristics of a hierarchical social network. People build a hierarchical social network based on blood, geography, industry, and even common beliefs. Within the network, risk sharing and resource sharing are achieved through human relationships and social mutual assistance mechanisms. Based on this, the following hypothesis is proposed:

## Hypothesis 4

Religious beliefs improve happiness by expanding residents’ social networks and providing emotional support.

## The heterogeneity of the impact of religious belief on residents’ happiness
Based on frequency, stability, and institutionalization, religion can be categorized as institutional or diffused. Institutional religion is characterized normative stability, predictable frequency, and formal organization of its practices. Diffused religions include mainly folk beliefs and ancestor worship. The religious rituals and organizations of diffused religions are deeply integrated into the concepts and structures of secular society, with the features of low frequency, instability, and low degree of organization of religious activities. The different frequency and forms of religious activities between the two types of religions result in different religious commitments and participation behaviors among their adherents, which ultimately affect their sense of happiness. As foreign religions have higher organizational characteristics compared to native religions in China, followers are more likely to build a tool-based social network with information sharing mechanisms, resource reciprocity mechanisms, and risk sharing mechanisms through frequent religious activities, thereby reducing residents’ uncertainty and anxiety and promoting the improvement of their well-being. On this basis, hypotheses 5 and 6 are proposed.

## Hypothesis 5
There is heterogeneity in the effect of religious belief on residents’ happiness. Compared with native religions, belief in foreign religions has a higher promoting effect on residents’ happiness.

## Hypothesis 6
Frequent participation in religious activities is significantly positively correlated with residents’ happiness, as it leverages religion’s spiritual comfort and social network’s instrumental value to enhance happiness.
Religious belief is a compensatory choice for residents in response to real-life difficulties. There are two theoretical interpretations of the purpose of religious belief, namely religious market theory and religious rational choice theory [ ]. The former believes that the structure of the religious market and the balance of supply and demand for religious beliefs determine the equilibrium quantity of religious services. The latter believes that religious decision-making and behavior are rational choices made by people on the basis of rational cost-benefit analysis, reflecting their rational behavior of maximizing the benefits of religious beliefs under the established constraints of material living standards and spiritual and cultural needs. In societies with economic stagnation, inequality, unemployment, or weak social safety nets, religion mitigates distress. Conversely, robust economic security may reduce religious demand [ ]. Thus, the welfare effects of religion remain central to analysis, as belief reflects rational choice. European evidence confirms that national economic development and cultural contexts moderate the religion-happiness relationship [ ]. The uneven development of China’s regional economy has led to disparities in income levels, social security, and living standards among residents in different regions. According to the “realistic dilemma compensation hypothesis” of religious beliefs, in the eastern regions with higher levels of economic development and social security, the promoting effect of religious beliefs on happiness has weakened. However, in the central and western regions with lower levels of income and social security, there is more room for the positive impact of religious beliefs on happiness.

## Hypothesis 7
There is regional heterogeneity in the influence of religious beliefs on residents’ happiness. In regions with a higher level of economic development, the promoting effect of religious beliefs on happiness is lower.
In the division of labor pattern of “men outside and women stay” in traditional Chinese families, men assume the function of acquiring family economic income to a larger degree [ ]. In the highly competitive labor market and workplace domain in China, economic and career development pressures such as obtaining employment opportunities, improving income levels, and job promotions fall more on male roles. Compared with women, men are more likely to endure the pressure caused by family responsibilities and economic burdens conferred by family division of labor roles. After experiencing negative shock events, men are not good at or unwilling to vent their emotions through ways such as pouring out or crying. At this time, religious beliefs have room to play a role in regulating stress and enhancing inner balance. The promoting effect of religious beliefs on happiness may have specificity between genders. The positive influence of religious beliefs on the happiness of the male group may be higher.

## Hypothesis 8
Compared with women, male residents can achieve a greater enhancement of happiness by believing in religion.
Residents with poor health conditions may suffer from the double torment of declined physical functions and poor mental conditions. Compared with residents in good health conditions, this group needs more spiritual compensation and consolation to cope with the painful experience after the loss of health [ ]. Religious beliefs can play the spiritual consolation function to a greater extent among the patients. Therefore, compared with healthy residents, residents with poor health conditions are more likely to obtain an improvement in the level of happiness from religious beliefs.

## Hypothesis 9
Compared with residents in good health conditions, residents with poor health conditions can obtain a higher subjective welfare effect of religious beliefs.
Cities are modernized regions with relatively high economic development and more complete social transformation. Cities have highly competitive labor markets, more specialized labor divisions, economic environments with a higher degree of marketization, and a faster-paced work and life. Compared with rural residents, urban residents endure greater pressures in terms of employment, work, and life, and are more prone to fall into tense and anxious emotions. Therefore, religious beliefs can alleviate the pressures and negative emotions of urban residents to a greater extent, thereby enhancing their level of happiness. Based on the above analysis, hypothesis 10 is proposed:

## Hypothesis 10
In contrast to rural residents, urban residents can obtain a higher enhancement of happiness by religious belief.

## Literature review
Although previous studies have examined religion’s impact on happiness, key gaps remain. The heterogeneous effects across religious types, group-specific patterns, and underlying mechanisms require deeper exploration. Rational choice theory suggests religious belief stems from cost-benefit analysis, potentially introducing self-selection bias as happiness-influencing factors also shape religious decisions. Previous studies have not dealt with the self-selection bias well, thereby influencing the inference of causal relationships. Previous studies often neglect this endogeneity concern, particularly reverse causality whereby happiness levels may influence religious adherence. Without robust identification methods, observed associations may reflect correlation rather than causation.

## Data sources, model specification and descriptive statistical analysis

## Data sources
This paper adopts the data of the 8 waves of the Chinese General Social Survey (CGSS) from 2010 to 2021. This survey is a continuous and national comprehensive social survey that began in 2003. It adopts the multi-stage PPS sampling method to randomly collect over 10,000 households for investigation across the country. By comprehensively collecting data at multiple levels such as society, families, and individuals, it reveals the changing trends of economic development and social transformation, and provides important data support for social science research and government policy-making. Since 2010, this dataset has conducted detailed investigations into the happiness and religious belief status of urban and rural residents, providing high-quality and nationally representative data support for the research on the relationship pattern between religious belief and happiness. Considering that the survey data of the years before 2010 did not involve residents’ happiness and in order to control the transformation of the relationship pattern between religious belief and happiness over time, mainly the 8 rounds of survey data implemented after 2010 are adopted. After processing the missing values and outliers according to the research purpose and variable settings, the 8 periods of data are integrated into a pooled cross-sectional data covering multiple periods of survey data. The advantage of employing pooled cross-sectional data lies in its capacity to enhance sample size and estimation precision, while facilitating multi-period examination of the relationship between religious affiliation and happiness. While the CGSS provides valuable cross-sectional data, it is not a panel survey and thus does not observe the same individuals repeatedly. As a result, controlling for individual fixed effects is infeasible.
Based on variable definitions and measurement methods, the dataset was cleaned with the following procedures: observations with missing values were deleted, and variables containing outliers were winsorized. After data cleaning, 49,500 observations comprising 58.1730% of the original sample entered the empirical analysis. Tests for differences in means between retained and excluded observations revealed no statistically significant differences. The final analytical sample is thus representative of the original sample. The sample selection process thus produced an unbiased sample for econometric analysis.

The religious belief status is measured from three aspects: “Whether to believe in religion”, “The type of religious belief”, and “The frequency of participation in religious activities”. “Whether to believe in religion (Relig)” mainly reflects the respondents’ self-identification as a specific religious believer and the psychological recognition of specific religious doctrines and values. This variable is a binary variable. When the respondents have a religious belief, it is assigned a value of 1, and when they have no religious belief, it is assigned a value of 0. “The type of religious belief (Religtype)” reflects whether residents’ beliefs are native religions or foreign religions. The values of this variable are 0, 1, and 2, representing “no belief”, “belief in native religions”, and “belief in foreign religions” respectively. Native religions mainly include Buddhism, Taoism, and folk beliefs (such as worshipping Mazu, Guan Gong, etc.), and foreign religions mainly include religions such as Islam, Catholicism, Christianity, and Orthodox Christianity. “Religious activity participation frequency (Religfreq)” measures the religious belief status of residents from the perspective of religious practice, focusing on measuring the degree of participation in religious activities and the piety of religious belief of the respondents from the behavioral frequency level. The participation frequencies of “never participated”, “several times a year” and “several times a month” are encoded as 0, 1 and 2 respectively, and “never participated” is taken as the benchmark group for regression analysis. is a series of control variables. Referring to the existing research, the demographic characteristics and family characteristics variables of the respondents are taken as control variables to enter the model. Specifically, the individual characteristic variables mainly include the gender (Gender), age (Age), the square term of age (Agesq), ethnicity (Race), urban-rural distribution (Urban), educational level (Educ7c), health status (Health), employment status (Naw), and regional distribution (Region) of the respondents. The family characteristic variables mainly include variables such as income level (Lincome) and marital status (Marsta). Income is one of the key determinants of happiness. In order to estimate the relationship between income and happiness more accurately, the nominal income is adjusted to the real income according to the price index of each province with 2010 as the base period, so as to achieve cross-regional and cross-period comparability of income data. Then, a 1% two-sided winsorizing treatment is carried out on the real income to exclude the distortion of the estimation result caused by outliers. Finally, the income is log-transformed to reduce the impact of the skewed distribution on the estimation result. To eliminate the influence of period factors on the respondents’ happiness, the year dummy variable (i.Year) is introduced in the control variables.
To clarify the intermediate functional paths of the relationship model between religious belief and residents’ happiness, three mediating variables, self-assessed social status (Nowrank), depression frequency (Depress), and emotional support (Emo_Sup) are introduced. The value range of the self-assessed social status (Nowrank) variable is from 1 to 10. Respondents make the judgment based on the socio-economic status of their families in the social structure. The larger the value, the higher the respondents’ evaluation of their own social status. The depression frequency (Depress) variable measures the frequency of respondents experiencing depressive emotions in the month before the interview. The frequencies from high to low are “always”, “often”, “sometimes”, “rarely” and “never”, and the above frequencies are encoded as 1 to 5 in sequence. Emotional Support (Emo_Sup) is measured through a composite index of respondents’ frequency of social interactions with relatives and friends.

## Descriptive statistics analysis
Table  presents the average level of happiness of respondents under different religious belief and conducts statistical tests on the significance of the mean difference. The analysis reveals that, from the perspective of the mean difference in happiness between religious believers and non-believers, the mean value of happiness of religious believers is higher than that of non-believers, and the mean difference passes the significance test at the 1% level. From the perspective of the frequency of participation in religious activities, the respondents with a higher frequency of participation in religious activities have a higher average level of happiness. The above analysis of mean differences provides preliminary evidence for the relationship pattern between religious belief and happiness, but the causal relationship between the two awaits rigorous empirical tests.

## Heterogeneity analysis

## Heterogeneity analysis in regional distribution
Table  presents the estimation results of the influence of religious belief on residents’ happiness by region, gender, health status and urban-rural distribution. Models (1)–(4) estimate religious belief’s effect on happiness across eastern, central, western, and northeastern China. By contrast, it can be known that the influence intensity and significance of religious belief on the happiness of residents in the western region are higher than those in other regions. The influence on the happiness of residents in the northeastern region comes second, followed by the eastern region. The influence on the central region is not significant. The above conclusions basically confirm Hypothesis , that is, religious belief can better improve the happiness level of residents in economically underdeveloped regions, but the estimation results of the central region do not strongly support this conclusion. Further analysis reveals heterogeneity in the relationship between religious belief types and resident happiness across regions. Adherence to native religions exhibits a statistically significant positive effect on happiness in the western and northeastern regions, while the effect is insignificant in the eastern and central regions. These results indicate significant regional heterogeneity in the impact of religious belief type on residents’ happiness. ## Endogeneity discussion and robustness examination

## Discussion of endogeneity
The previous text adopted econometric analysis methods to estimate the influence of religious belief on residents’ happiness. Formal religious belief has a significant subjective welfare effect. However, whether the relationship pattern between religious belief and happiness conforms to causal inference still needs more rigorous empirical tests. The two-way causality potentially biases causal inference. Religious belief affects residents’ happiness through direct and indirect paths such as the spiritual guidance function and the instrumental value function. However, the residents’ happiness may also influence their religious belief. Residents who have experienced major negative shock events and whose happiness has dropped severely are more likely to seek spiritual support through religious belief. This reverse causality may lead to the phenomenon that residents with religious belief may have even lower levels of happiness. Although the regression analysis reveals that religious belief significantly and positively influences happiness, if the reverse causality issue is not dealt with, the happiness-enhancing effect of religious belief may be underestimated.
Therefore, the instrumental variable approach is employed to handle the two-way causality problem. The selection of instrumental variables requires simultaneously fulfilling the relevance assumption and the exclusion assumption, namely, the instrumental variables should be highly correlated with the religious beliefs of residents but have no direct influence on the outcome variable “happiness”. Based on the criteria, the “proportion of regional religious residents” is utilized as the instrumental variable of residents’ religious beliefs for two-stage least squares estimation. This instrumental variable is obtained by calculating the proportion of residents who believe in religions in each province (city) to the total number of residents in the province (city). The “proportion of regional religious residents” may affect the possibility of an individual believing in religions through the demonstration effect of the social network and regional religious culture. This variable meets the correlation assumption. Meanwhile, the “proportion of regional religious residents” does not have a direct and significant impact on the happiness of a specific resident. Therefore, this variable conforms to the exclusionary assumption of the instrumental variable.
The two-stage least square estimation results of the impact of religious belief on happiness are presented in Table  . The analysis shows that the under-identification test rejects the null hypothesis that the instrumental variable is unidentifiable. The Cragg-Donald Wald F statistic of the weak instrumental variable test is much higher than the eigenvalue of 16.3800 at the 10% level, indicating that there is no issue of weak instrumental variables in the model. The selection of the instrumental variable is reasonable and effective, and causal inferences can be made. The estimation results of the first stage show that the “proportion of regional religious residents” has a significant positive impact on individual religious belief. The estimation results of the second stage confirm that, after correcting for the potential endogenous bias, believing in religions can significantly improve the happiness level of residents.
 Considering potential violations of the exclusion restriction when using the “proportion of regional religious residents” as an IV, provincial religious venue density (Relig_Den) is adopted as an alternative instrumental variable in a supplementary analysis to strengthen the empirical robustness. This density measure, defined as the log-transformed number of registered religious venues per million population within a province, aims to better satisfy the core IV assumptions. It exhibits relevance, demonstrated by a statistically significant positive relationship with individual religious belief in the first-stage results. Crucially, the exclusion restriction is more plausibly satisfied, as venue density directly affects the availability of religious exposure but is unlikely to exert an independent direct effect on individual happiness outcomes. The second-stage IV estimates, controlling for endogeneity, confirm that individual religious belief maintains a statistically significant positive impact on happiness. Notably, the value of this estimated coefficient is close to that obtained using the initial religious proportion IV, thus enhancing the robustness of the core finding regarding religious belief’s positive effect. The above estimation results provide more rigorous empirical evidence for Hypothesis 1a.

## Robustness check
The religious beliefs of residents might be their active choices. There could be significant differences in a series of covariates between religious believers and non-believers, that is, residents do not randomly choose whether to have religious beliefs but make active choices under the influence of a series of covariates. These factors might simultaneously have a significant impact on residents’ sense of happiness. To handle the estimation bias caused by self-selection bias, the propensity score matching (PSM) method is adopted to further estimate the net effect of religious beliefs on residents’ sense of happiness. This method adopts the processing idea of a quasi-natural experiment, divides the samples into the treatment group and the control group according to the treatment indicator variable, and estimates the propensity score value of the samples based on a series of covariates. Then, the matching method is used to match the samples of the treatment group and the control group according to the size of the propensity score value to eliminate the systematic differences in covariates between the two groups of samples. Common matching methods include nearest neighbor matching, radius matching, kernel matching, and local linear regression matching methods. To investigate the stability of the results under different matching methods, all four methods are simultaneously used for matching, and the estimation results are presented in Table  .
 The propensity score matching analysis is based on the premise that the balance test is satisfied. The balance test results of the nearest neighbor matching method are shown in Fig.  . The analysis shows that before the matching is completed, there are systematic differences in a series of covariates between the treatment group and the control group. After the matching is completed, the standardized deviations of the covariates are all controlled within 10%, indicating that there are no systematic differences between the groups and the balance test shows that the matching quality is good. Due to space limitations, only the balance test results under the nearest neighbor matching method are reported. It can be seen from the analysis in Table  that the ATT values under the four matching methods are all positive and the differences are minor, confirming that after eliminating the systematic differences between the samples, religious belief still has a significant positive promoting effect on residents’ happiness. The estimation results under the propensity score matching method again confirm Hypothesis 1a.

The above analysis has confirmed the positive promoting effect of religious belief on residents’ happiness. After dealing with endogeneity and self-selection bias, this positive promoting effect is still significant. The heterogeneity analysis of the relationship pattern between religious belief and happiness has confirmed that heterogeneous relationship patterns exist among different genders, between urban and rural areas, among residents with different health conditions, and among residents in different regions. However, the above analysis has not yet clarified the intrinsic mechanism of the subjective welfare effect generated by religious belief. ## Research conclusions and policy implications
This study reviewed the impact of religious belief on residents’ happiness and its intermediate functioning mechanism through detailed literature review, and extracted ten research hypotheses to be tested based on theoretical analysis. Empirical analysis methods were adopted to test the hypotheses one by one. Specifically, based on the data of the Chinese General Social Survey from 2010 to 2021, the impact of religious belief on residents’ happiness was empirically studied. The subjective welfare effects of residents’ religious belief status were examined from multiple aspects such as whether they believe in religions, the types of religious beliefs, and the frequency of participation in religious activities. The main conclusions are as follows: (1) Religious belief has a significant promoting effect on residents’ happiness, and this promoting effect remains significant after dealing with endogeneity and self-selection bias, indicating that the subjective welfare effect of religious belief satisfies causal inference. The types of religious belief also have a significant positive impact on residents’ happiness. Foreign religions with a higher degree of organization have a greater promoting effect on happiness than native religions. The more frequently residents participate in religious activities, the higher the level of happiness they can obtain. The frequency of participation in religious activities is an important manifestation of the devoutness of religious belief and the embedding depth of the social network. The spiritual guidance function exerted by devout beliefs and the social support effect generated by the embedding of the social network are the possible sources of the subjective welfare effect of religious belief. Therefore, this implies that the influence of religious belief on happiness is to some extent through participation in religious activities and then has an impact on happiness. (2) Religious belief has a specific impact on the happiness of different groups of residents. The heterogeneity analysis and the significance test of coefficient differences show that religious belief has a stronger positive promoting effect on the happiness of male groups, urban groups, and groups with poor health conditions. Some evidence suggests that religious belief has a stronger impact on the happiness of residents in economically underdeveloped areas. The negative impact on happiness caused by the incomplete social security system and the relatively low material living standards in these areas can be partially compensated by the happiness promoting effect of religious belief. (3) The mechanism analysis demonstrates that religious belief can increase residents’ self-rated social status, and thereby indirectly affect the enhancement of residents’ happiness. The proportion of the mediating effect is 27.6326%, that is, 27.6326% of the total effect of religious belief on residents’ happiness indirectly plays a role through improving residents’ evaluation of their own social status. Religious belief weakens believers’ pursuit of external material welfare and secular success, and leads them to pursue spiritual satisfaction more inwardly, thereby enhancing their self-perception of their own social status. This higher recognition of the state of one’s social status has a positive impact on happiness. However, the frequency of depression does not act as the intermediate mechanism through which religious belief influences happiness. Respondents with a higher frequency of depression are more likely to seek spiritual comfort from religious belief. After controlling for the frequency of depression, the happiness promoting effect of religious belief is enhanced. This suggests that residents with a higher frequency of depression can achieve a greater increase in happiness by means of religious belief. Furthermore, a statistically significant mediating role of emotional support is confirmed, accounting for 3.6368% of religious belief’s total effect on happiness and validated via the Sobel-Goodman test.
The policy implications based on the main research conclusions are as follows: Firstly, the government should fully respect the freedom of religious belief of residents and fully exert the promoting effect of religious belief on residents’ happiness. Especially, it is necessary to guide religious organizations and religious activities to adapt to the social and economic development and the spiritual and cultural demands and social network demands of residents, and fully exert the functions of religion in providing spiritual comfort, guiding people towards goodness, enhancing social cohesion and grassroots governance functions, so as to promote the continuous enhancement of residents’ happiness level. Second, different guiding measures for religious belief should be adopted according to the heterogeneous relationship model between religious belief and the happiness of different groups. Especially for male residents, urban residents, residents in underdeveloped areas, and residents with poor health who believe in religions, it is necessary to pay attention to the important and positive role of religious belief in “bringing happiness” when they are experiencing material deprivation, long-term illness, and high mental stress. The pursuit of the spiritual aspect should be regarded as an important way to make up for the real predicament. Third, it is necessary to unleash the positive effect of religious belief on the happiness level of residents from the intermediate path of the subjective welfare effect of religious belief. It is necessary to guide religious belief to be compatible with Chinese traditional culture and socialist core values, guide people to pursue spiritual abundance and satisfaction, weaken the psychological imbalance caused by excessive social comparison, and improve residents’ self-awareness of their own social and economic status, thereby promoting the improvement of residents’ happiness.
Two principal limitations warrant consideration in interpreting the findings. Firstly, the instrumental variable strategy employing regional religious adherence rates may raise questions about potential violations of the exclusion restriction. Although robustness tests indicate minimal direct effects of regional religiosity on happiness beyond individual belief pathways, unobserved regional confounders, such as localized cultural norms or institutional characteristics, could theoretically bias estimates. Future research employing alternative identification strategies may help strengthen causal inferences. Secondly, the aggregated treatment of religious traditions may overlook critical heterogeneities across China’s diverse religious landscape. Buddhism, Taoism, Islam, Christianity, and folk religions differ substantially in cultural embeddedness, doctrinal emphases, and societal roles. For instance, Islam’s collectivist orientation and Buddhism’s individualized contemplative practices likely engender distinct pathways influencing happiness. Disaggregating effects by religious affiliation could reveal distinct mechanisms shaping happiness. Future research should systematically compare cross-religious heterogeneities in happiness effects, particularly through analyzing differentiated mechanisms across Buddhist, Islamic, Christian, and folk religious practices within their specific socio-cultural contexts.
