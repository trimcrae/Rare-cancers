Quality of life of patients after 1 year of Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting surgery and Percutaneous Coronary Intervention: A Comparative Study

Dr Vikas Dhaka

Resident,Dept of Psychiatry, Govt Medical College,Kota
Background: Coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) and percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) are two commonly done interventions for Coronary artery disease (CAD). Determination and comparison of quality of life after these procedures becomes important to choose between these two.
Aims: To assess and compare Quality of life in patients after one year of CABG and PCI.
Methods: It was a cross sectional comparative study. Study included 50 post CABG and 50 Post PCI fulfilling specific inclusion criteria laid down for study. A semi structured especially designed Proforma and World Health Organization Quality of Life Assessment scale WHOQOL – BREF, (Hindi version) were used. Data collected were classified, tabulated and analysed using appropriate statistical methods. SPSS version 23.0 was used for statistical analysis.
Results: Mean score of QOL in domains of physical health, environment and total quality of life was significantly higher for CABG as compared to PCI. Total QOL and physical health domain of QOL after CABG negatively correlated with comorbidities. QOL after PCI correlated positively with education and occupation while correlated negatively with comorbidities and time after PCI.
Conclusion: Study revealed better total quality of life scores after one year of CABG as compared to PCI.
Keywords: CABG, PCI, QOL

Relationship between Obsessional beliefs and Insight in patients with obsessive - compulsive disorder

Tamanna Hooda

Junior Resident IMHH Agra
Introduction: OCD is a heterogenous entity in terms of symptoms and insight. Various models have been formed to explain the complexity of OCD and how obsessive beliefs play a vital role in the etiology and psychopathology of OCD. Evaluating the association between these beliefs and insight can be very advantageous.
Method: aim: to determine the relation between obsessive beliefs and the level of insight in obsessive compulsive patients, Venue: The study was conducted at the institute of mental health and hospital, Agra, Design: The study is a hospital based cross sectional study. sampling: 80 ocd patients as per ICD-10 diagnostic criteria were selected through purposive sampling. Tools: Patients assessed using Obsessional belief questionnaire-44 (OBQ-44) and Scale to Assess Unawareness in Mental Disorder (SUMD).
Result: the data was analysed with product moment correlation and it was found that OBQ-ICT has negative correlation (r = -0.36) with SUMD indicating controlling thought promote insight in patients. No significant correlation was found between OBQ-RT and OBQ-PC and insight.
Conclusion: In this study importance of control of thought was the only variable which is significantly correlated with level of insight. Implications of these findings for conceptualising the relationship between obsessive beliefs and insight have been discussed.
Key Words: OCD, Obsessional belief, Insight, SUMD.

Assisted vs Unassisted domiciliary alcohol detoxification: A Randomized controlled trial

Dr Sneha B Suresh

SR Inst of Psychiatry & Human Behaviour, Bambolim Goa
Background: Alcohol consumption has been a major risk factor for burden of mortality and morbidity resulting in 3.4 million deaths and 14.7 million disability-adjusted life years for the year 2019[1]. In a densely populated country with limited mental health resources, it is challenging to treat people with alcohol use disorders (AUD) appropriately. Detoxification is an important step in the treatment of AUD.
While home detoxification is routinely practiced, there is scarce data on the outcome of this approach.
Assisted domiciliary detoxification is a process in which individual’s withdrawal status is monitored whilst being provided psychosocial support and detoxification, at home [2].
Aim: To study outcomes of assisted over routine domiciliary alcohol detoxification in a randomized controlled trial.
Methods: 100 consenting male patients in alcohol withdrawal who were advised domiciliary detoxification were randomized into intervention (n=49) and control group (n=51). A predesigned semi structured questionnaire was used to assess the socio-demographic and drinking-related variables. Both groups received routine care for withdrawal from respective treating doctors, in addition, the intervention group received 2-sessions of Brief Intervention for alcohol and daily phone-call assistance. Records were kept for adverse events. Phone-calls were discontinued once detoxification was completed. Outcome was measured in terms of successful completion of detoxification, and abstention from alcohol at the end of one month.
Results: The study demonstrated that the intervention group had significantly higher successful detoxification rates (85.7% vs 62.7%, p=0.008) and achieved significantly higher abstinence days at the end of one month as compared to the control group (22 vs10 days, p<0.001) with an effect size of 1.2.
Conclusion: This study demonstrates that providing assistance during the acute phase of detoxification helps in successful detoxification and maintain longer periods of abstinence. This simple but effective intervention has an important policy implication in terms of scaling up of services to address the huge treatment gap for AUD.
Key words: Alcohol use disorders, Alcohol withdrawal, Domiciliary alcohol detoxification, home detoxification, Brief Intervention, Psycho-social interventions, Treatment gap.

A comparative study of Psychiatric Morbidity and Stressful life events in patients with Organic and Functional dyspepsia.

Dr Krishna Prakash
Background: Dyspepsia is one of the commonest complaints presenting to the clinic and is known to be associated with psychiatric morbidity and stressful life events. The Indian perspective on this subject is limited and much of the international literature also focuses only on functional dyspepsia rather than including organic dyspepsia as well.
Aims and Objectives: To study anxiety, depression, stress and stressful life – events in outpatients with dyspepsia and to compare patients with organic and functional dyspepsia on these parameters.
Methodology: 100 consecutive out-patients being posted for upper gastro-intestinal endoscopy for the indication of evaluation of dyspepsia at the Department of Surgery, were recruited prior to endoscopy. The anxiety, depression, stress was evaluated using the Depression, Anxiety and Stress Scale – 21. Stressful life events were noted using the Presumptive Stressful Life Events Scale. The endoscopic findings were obtained from the surgeon after the procedure and the patients with normal findings on endoscopy were classified as Functional dyspepsia, while those with abnormal findings on endoscopy were categorized as Organic dyspepsia. These two groups were compared on the psychological parameters.
Results: The prevalence of depression, anxiety and stress were noted to be 79%, 81% and 53% respectively. 55% of the patients reported stressful life events in the past one year. Prevalence of organic dyspepsia was 30 % while that of functional dyspepsia was 70 %. The patients of organic and functional dyspepsia did differ in the prevalence or severity of psychiatric morbidity or of stressful life events.
Discussion: There is robust support from literature for the high prevalence of psychiatric morbidity noted in patients with dyspepsia which could be an indication for assessment of these factors among patients with dyspepsia. The lack of differentiation between organic and functional dyspepsia indicate that there assessments may not be limited to functional dyspepsia alone.

Acute neuromodulatory effects of single session biphasic TMS on brain connectivity in OCD-A TMS fMRI study

Dr Chandramouli Roy

SR CIP Ranchi
BACKGROUND: The neurobiology of Obsessive Compulsive Disorder (OCD) is complex with recent studies suggesting dysconnectivity between various regions of brain especially between the large brain networks like Default Mode Network (DMN), Salience network and Fronto-Parietal Network. Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (TMS) has been used to alter the cortical plasticity and connectivity but the immediate effects have not been studied in the understanding of functional connectivity in OCD.
AIM: The aim was to investigate alterations in functional connectivity and measure changes in the functional connectivity (FC) after single session of Biphasic TMS in patients of OCD.
METHODOLOGY: 27 drug free or drug naïve patients of OCD and 27 healthy controls were recruited. Resting state fMRI images were obtained from a 3Tesla scanner before and immediately after application of TMS by MRI compatible TMS machine positioned over left DLPFC. fMRI images were processed using CONN 18.b and FC between region of interest (ROIs’) was calculated. Statistical analysis was done using SPSS 25.
RESULTS: There was significant difference in FC in patients of OCD as compared to healthy controls with hypoconnectivity in various regions including the DMN. Post application of TMS there was increased connectivity in the posterior part of DMN.
CONCLUSION: The study provides evidence for altered functional connectivity in patients of OCD and immediate effects of TMS over the brain. The knowledge of short-term effects of TMS on brain connectivity can unravel novel stimulation sites as well as design novel precision led TMS/rTMS protocols in future.
Key words: Functional connectivity, OCD, TMS, fMRI
