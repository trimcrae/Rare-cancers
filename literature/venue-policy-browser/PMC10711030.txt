## Background
According to the 2014 National Physical Fitness and Health Monitor, people aged 9 to 22 exhibit limited physical activity, with a large proportion exercising for less than 1 h daily. China has focused on the study of college student sports motivation. This study comprehensively analyzed sports motivation studies and their relevant factors on Chinese college students.

## Methods
The researchers searched CNKI (China National Knowledge Infrastructure), WANFANG DATA, Cqvip, Scopus, Web of Science, PubMed, and Cochrane Library for sports motivation in Chinese college students. Articles from 2018 to 2023 were searched. The final analysis included 30 pieces of literatures that met the topic criteria.

## Results
The results show that Chinese college students' sports motivation studies used large sample sizes and covered most of China. The main sports motivations are health, shape, and relaxation. Demography, psychology, social support, physical environment, and policy aspects affect sports motivation. Lack of use of qualitative and longitudinal studies.

## Conclusion
In the future, scholars who research the sports motivation of Chinese college students need to pay more attention to social support, physical environment, and policy. Furthermore, scholars may try to use qualitative and longitudinal studies.

## Introduction
With rapid development in the social and economic spheres, people are increasingly emphasizing their health and well-being. However, lack of physical activity (PA) is still a widespread global problem that poses a significant threat to public health [ ]. In many countries, most college students cannot achieve at least 150 min of moderate-intensity physical activity per week [ , ]. This phenomenon is particularly pronounced in China [ ]. In 2019, approximately 75 % of Chinese college students have not achieved the recommended guideline standard for physical activity, which is 150 min of moderate-intensity aerobic exercise or 75 min of vigorous-intensity aerobic exercise [ ]. According to the 2014 National Physical Fitness Monitoring Bulletin, age groups between 9 and 22 have limited physical activity, with a large proportion exercising for less than 1 h per day. The age groups with the highest prevalence were identified as 18-year-old males and 21-year-old females, with 82.5 % and 89.8 %, respectively [ ]. Some scholars have suggested that the intensity of motivation for physical activity positively correlates with participation in physical activity [ ]. Improving sports motivation can enhance participation in physical activity [ ].
Sports are defined as institutionalized rule-governed contests of human physical skill, such as recreational sports, exercise sports, sports training, blood sports, outdoor sports, dance sports, yoga sports, board games sport, and card games sport [ ]. The term motivation refers to “the power and direction of behavior” [ ]. It is a complex psychological phenomenon rather than an observable entity [ ]. For example, athletes are often assumed to have strong sports motivation because they constantly repeat their training, even though researchers do not measure motivation [ ]. Motivation generally consists of intrinsic motivation (e.g., the need for companionship) and extrinsic motivation (e.g., prizes), which interact with each other to motivate people to take action [ ]. In academic research, the motivation can serve as an antecedent or a consequence. For example, self-determined motivation can produce higher levels of achievement [ ], whereas rewards reduce intrinsic motivation [ ]. Furthermore, motivation can play a mediating role. For example, motivation mediates the interaction between perfectionism and burnout [ ], and between coach/parent autonomy support and happiness [ ]. Self-determination theory has become a popular framework for investigating the relationship between sports motivation and physical activity [ ]. This review explores the influencing factors of sports motivation among Chinese college students, and sports motivation serves as a consequence. Therefore, this review only included literature on sports motivation as a consequence.
This systematic review analyzes the literature on the current status and influencing factors of Chinese college students' sports motivation in the past five years (2018–2023). The included literature came from seven databases, including Chinese and English, with a total of 30 articles. The authors organized the study in the following steps: Chapter 2 defines the research methodology, including protocol and registration, search strategy, search process, eligibility criteria, and data extraction. Chapter 3 presents the results, including the quality assessment of the literature, descriptive analysis, and the main findings of the literature. Chapter 4 discusses these results. Chapter 5 provides conclusions.
There are many research findings on the sports motivation of Chinese college students. However, comprehensive and systematic reviews are lacking to identify future research directions. In order to fill this gap, this study investigates the current research status and influencing factors of Chinese college students' sports motivation. It analyzes the research trends in the past five years. Moreover, this review identifies gaps in current relevant research and provides suggestions for future research.
To address these research gaps, we developed research hypotheses. H1: The sports motivation of Chinese college students will be influenced by various factors, including demographic, psychological, social support, physical environment, and policy.

## Materials and methods
2.1

## Protocol and registration
This review was conducted utilizing the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-analysis (PRISMA) guidelines [ ]. The protocol was pre-registered in PROSPERO(CRD42023438487).

2.2

## Search strategy
A comprehensive search was performed utilizing a search algorithm across seven electronic databases, namely China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), WANFANG DATA, CQVIP, Web of Science (WoS), Scopus, Cochrane Library, and PubMed. In order to include the Chinese college student population, this study had major English databases and three central Chinese databases, namely CNKI, WANFANG DATA, and CQVIP. The search algorithms include the following relevant keywords: physical activity, motivation, and Chinese college students. The additional material contains comprehensive documentation on the search strategies for each database.

2.3

## Search process
Searches were conducted by one researcher (Shuao), and all identified studies were imported into Endnote reference management software (version X9). Duplicates were first eliminated using Endnote's Find Duplicates function. Titles and abstracts were then manually screened by two researchers (Shuao and Xiaoling) to determine eligibility. Eligible articles were then reviewed in full by two researchers (Shuao and Xiaoling). Any discrepancies between reviewers were resolved through discussion, and, if required, a deciding vote was cast by an independent researcher (Chunwei). provides full details of the process. 

2.4

## Eligibility criteria
Articles were included if published in peer-reviewed journals in English and Chinese and reported specific data on the sports motivation of Chinese college students participating in physical activity.
Inclusion criteria: (1) study design: a cross-sectional study, intervention or experimental, retrospective, or prospective cohort study; (2) study population: undergraduate students in China; (3) a study reporting on the sports motivation of Chinese college students; (4) country/region: mainland China; (5) article type: peer-reviewed article; (6) time frame: published between January 1, 2018, and May 12, 2023; (7) language of article: English and Chinese.
Exclusion criteria: (1) article type only review, press release, commentary, conference announcement; (2) full text not available; (3) insufficient information; (4) published before January 1, 2018; (5) a study of sports motivation as an antecedent.

2.5

## Data extraction
Data extraction forms were created in order to document pertinent data for the studies that were included. The data pertaining to the following categories were extracted: authors, year of publication, sample size, number of male and female participants, research area, research method, study design, sports category, statistical methods, specific sports motivations, categories of relevant factors, and main findings. The data extraction process involved the participation of two reviewers (Shuao and Xiaoling). Any errors or inconsistencies were rectified by a third person (Chunwei). The data that has been extracted is presented in , and .

## Results
The flow chart in illustrates the process of selecting studies for inclusion in the analysis. A comprehensive search strategy was used, with an advanced search by search formula, which retrieved 381 articles, including 32 articles from CNKI, 204 articles from Wanfang data, 34 articles from Cqvip, 40 articles from Scopus, 41 articles from Web of Science, 24 articles from PubMed, and six articles from the Cochrane Library. In total, 30 articles were selected for analysis after applying predetermined inclusion and exclusion criteria. These included six articles in English and 24 papers written in Chinese, as shown in .
An overall number of 381 studies were initially identified. Following the process of exclusion, a total of 30 studies were selected as suitable for inclusion in the final review. The sample of 30 studies included the use of quantitative and mixed methods research, with differences observed in the selection of measurement instruments. Due to the observed differences, the possibility of conducting a meta-synthesis and/or meta-analysis was precluded. Consequently, a narrative synthesis was implemented.
3.1

## Study quality assessment
The Mixed Methods Assessment Tool (MMAT) and its accompanying user guide were utilized to conduct a comprehensive evaluation of research quality [ ]. The MMAT was developed with a clear goal of assessing systematic reviews, including various research methodologies such as qualitative, quantitative, and mixed methods of study. The MMAT initially employed two comprehensive assessments: (1) Does the research question display clarity and precision? (2) Does the data collected effectively match the research question that was formulated? In the present review, all studies under consideration satisfied both criteria, thereby prompting the application of the MMAT to assess their methodological quality through inquiries concerning the study design. Each question was answered with either “yes,” “no,” or “cannot tell.” The calculation of an overall score was not undertaken as it was not the primary objective of employing the MMAT. displays the quality assessments provided for each criterion for every study. The included studies were assessed independently by two researchers (Shuao and Xiaoling), with any disagreements being resolved by a third researcher (Chunwei).
The literature presented in this review provides comprehensive statements regarding the criteria utilized for the inclusion of the sample, the study population, and the environment. The methods employed for measuring and statistically analyzing outcome indicators were deemed to be both have high validity and reliability. The existing body of literature on the identification and management of confounding factors is limited. Only Kahar Abula employed a randomized group experiment to mitigate the effects of confounding in Study 2 [ ].
All literary works obtained a score of seven on the quality distribution, suggesting that there were no significant issues in the design and execution of the research (See ).

## Discussion
Sports motivation as a recognized influencing factor can affect the frequency and duration of participation in physical activity [ ]. It is influenced by various factors such as demographics, psychology, social support, physical environment, and policy [ ]. Although there have been many recent studies on sports motivation, there is a lack of empirical research among Chinese college students. Due to the current low level of physical activity among Chinese college students, it is crucial to determine the influencing factors of sports motivation to promote physical activity among college students. Consequently, the study aims to analyze the current research status and influencing factors of sports motivation among Chinese college students through the included literature.
By reviewing 24 published articles in Chinese and six articles in English, this review analyzes the current research status and influencing factors of Chinese college students' sports motivation. From the dimensions of the survey area, research method, research design, sports category, specific sports motivation, analytical strategies, influencing factor categories, and main findings, the review examines the published articles in this field in the past five years.
According to the findings of this systematic review, the geographical range of the research included in this paper encompasses most of the Chinese regions, and the survey area spans four levels: university, city, province, and country. The majority of articles used large sample sizes, valid statistical methods, and cross-sectional research design to investigate the influencing factors of sports motivation.
In terms of methodology and research design, most researchers have used quantitative research methods and cross-sectional research designs. However, there were few longitudinal studies on sports motivation. Scholars have argued that athletes’ ongoing sports experiences change daily [ ]. Motivation is also a dynamic change process [ ]. Therefore, cross-sectional research designs have limitations in what they can reveal. In contrast, longitudinal studies could better observe the change process.
The study by Kahar is the most special of these studies because they employed two research designs to develop a new mixed-methods approach for assessing the influencing factors of sports motivation [ ]. Research design one used a cross-sectional study like the other articles; however, research design two used an additional experimental design. The experiment randomly assigned physical education teachers into an intervention and control group. An autonomy support intervention program was created to fit the Chinese physical education setting based on prior interventions for physical education that supported autonomy. Students of the teachers provided information on perceived autonomy support, motivation for autonomy in physical education classes, and motivation for autonomy in leisure-time physical activity. Comparing students taught by teachers in the intervention group with those taught by teachers in the control group will reveal the importance of autonomy support in promoting motivation for physical activity. To track the effects of various teaching method interventions on motivation for athletics, Kahar created a teaching experiment and a longitudinal study. It provides a fresh perspective for further study.
Most of the research did not separate specific sports. However, each sport has unique traits and motivational factors. Investigating various sports' unique sport motivational traits would help scholars better understand college students' sports motivation. It is important to note that article number 10 mentioned eSports as an emerging sport in the research on sports motivation. Since the State Sports General Administration of China first introduced the concept of eSports [ , ], it has attracted extensive discussion among people. The Olympic Committee organized a high-level meeting in Korea in October 2017 and eventually ensured that competitive eSports activities may be classified as a form of sports activity [ ]. Moreover, article number 27 examined the sport of bridge [ ]. In 2018, the 18th Asian Games (Jakarta Asian Games) added the bridge as an official event of the Asian Games [ ]. Therefore, the sports categories in this review have included eSports and bridges.
According to the findings of this review, the influencing factors of Chinese college students' sports motivation include demographic, psychological, social support, physical environment, and policy factors.
In the past five years, motivation research has focused primarily on intra-individual aspects, that is, demographic and psychological factors. However, there is relatively less research on the extra-individual aspects regarding social support, physical environment, and policy. The socio-ecological model theory posits that human behavior and consciousness are shaped by a combination of intra-individual factors, including gender, age, positive emotions, and perceived self-support, as well as external environmental factors, such as social structures, physical environments, policy, and culture [ ]. The theoretical framework posits four distinct categories of influence factors: the intra-individual, social, physical environment, and policy. In this study, twenty-two articles discussed intra-individual factors of influence, nine examined social support influencing factors, three explored physical environment influencing factors, and only two focused on policy factors. Therefore, future researchers should pay more attention to the relationship between extra-individual factors and sports motivation.
4.1

## Demographic factors
Firstly, demographic factors significantly influenced Chinese college students' sports motivation. According to previous research, demographic factors are the most common and essential factors influencing a person's ideology and behavior [ , , , , , , ].
The demographic factors influencing Chinese college students' sports motivation include gender, age, major, sports level, years of exercise, amount of exercise, BMI, household registration, and consumption level.
Furthermore, article number 6 reported gender differences in sports motivation. Male students are more concerned with their health, according to the specific sports motivations of college students. A significant amount of physical activity selections is made with stress release in mind, related to male students’ preference for competitive ball sports, including badminton, soccer, basketball, and table tennis. High-intensity competitive sports can help male college students effectively reduce stress levels. At the same time, males demonstrate significantly higher intrinsic motivation based on their competitive nature [ ]. Males are more easily motivated by competition and challenges than females [ ]. In contrast to male college students, females scored higher than males on appearance-related extrinsic motivation [ ]. Nearly 50 % of female college students hope to shape their bodies through sports. In addition, many female students want to improve their social abilities through sports. By participating in sports together, female students can effectively promote communication, help them make more friends, expand their social circle, and enjoy their after-school lives.
The impact of age on sports motivation cannot be ignored. Socioemotional selectivity theory may explain this phenomenon, as people's motivation shifts with age from future-oriented instrumental goals to emotionally meaningful goals for the present [ ]. Previous studies have shown that younger people are more likely to exercise alone than older people [ ]. This is consistent with the findings of this review.
College students from different majors also showed variability in sports motivation. This may be because science students scored higher in logical-analytical thinking and lower in divergent thinking power; The opposite is true for art students [ ]. Currently, there are fewer studies on the differences in sports areas among arts and science students. More research is needed in the future to explain this difference.
Different skill levels, years of exercise, amount of exercise, and BMI significantly differ in sports motivation. People are growth-orientated and naturally tend to fulfil their basic psychological needs [ ]. Change in skill level, years of exercise, amount of exercise, and BMI increases psychological needs for sports. When psychological needs are not fulfilled, people are continually looking for ways to satisfy their needs [ ].
There are significant differences in sports motivation among college students of different household registrations and consumption levels. Sports require higher field equipment and a financial expense. There is still a gap between rural and urban areas regarding sports venues and equipment. The infrastructure in cities is better than that in rural areas, with more schools, parks, and commercialized sports venues. College students with high consumption levels can buy good sports equipment for a good sports experience. According to Maslow's theory, human needs are divided into low-order and high-order levels. Sports needs belong to the category of higher-order needs. Only when the needs of survival and safety are fully satisfied individuals will pay more attention to sport's needs [ ].

4.2

## Psychological factors
Psychological factors significantly influenced Chinese college students' sports motivation. Psychological factors include personality anxiety, cognitive level, interest, stress, and sports adherence.
According to the research on self-determination theory [ ], Positive psychology is positively related to intrinsic motivation, and negative psychology is negatively related to intrinsic motivation and negative psychology is positively correlated with amotivation.
Cognitive level, interest, and exercise compliance can increase the pleasure and satisfaction of exercise [ ], thus increasing intrinsic motivation. Personality anxiety and stress, on the other hand, reduce intrinsic motivation [ ]. The results in this review validate the self-determination theory.

4.3

## Social support factors
Social support factors are autonomy support, teaching behavior, comments from internet visitors, opinions from close friends, sports partners, relationship status, and leaders' attention, which positively influence Chinese college students' sports motivation.
The concept of social support is related to instrumental, emotional, and informational support [ ]. This support refers to resources provided by a variety of people, such as family, friends, and teachers. The level of social support can vary depending on the amount and frequency of support provided and whether the support is positive or negative. This increases the possibility of participation in the exercise [ ].
Scholars have argued that sports partners contribute to the quality of youth physical activity [ ]. Building friendships during physical activity can generate positive sports motivation [ ]. Physical activity has social attributes, especially reflected in the sports that Chinese college students prefer to participate in, such as basketball, soccer, e-sports, and bridge. These sports cannot be done alone. Therefore, social support is essential in Chinese college students' sports motivation.

4.4

## Physical environments factors
Physical environments in the included studies involved game content, game operators, and venue equipment, which all positively influenced Chinese college students' sports motivation.
E-sports inspire a great deal of goal-oriented behavior. Tens of thousands of college students participate in eSports each week, forgoing other leisure opportunities and investing significant energy in cooperating, exploring, and competing with each other in virtual worlds. The attraction of eSports is inherent in the properties of the experiences they provide. Game content and game operators determine this intrinsic property [ ].
When people exercise, their attention is diverted to the environment rather than the intrinsic feeling of fatigue, which reduces perceived exercise [ ]. Therefore, the physical environment influences people's willingness to participate in physical activity and helps to maintain the intensity of exercise in the moment [ ].

4.5

## Policy factors
Policy factors are closely related to Chinese college students' sports motivation. Policy factors consist of sports policies and methods of physical education tests.
Many Chinese college students are more responsive to policy interventions. These students are motivated to participate in sports to avoid the potential punishment of sports policies. College students' attitudes toward school sports policies were positively correlated with their performance on sports tests. Ignorance and disagreement with the policy made college students fear or even avoid participating in relevant physical activity [ ].
There are few previous studies on sports policy because sports policy is difficult to quantify without a measurement tool. However, the results in article number 3 indicate that Bo Lin developed a scale about Chinese college students' sports policy and validated the influence of policy factors on sports motivation [ ]. It provided a new perspective to understand the impact of government policies on sports motivation.

4.6

## Gaps identified by the review
All the studies from the past five years included in this review used quantitative and mixed research methods; there were no purely qualitative studies. Despite the mixed studies, there was also a more emphasized quantitative section. However, qualitative methods such as interviews or observation can better describe and explain the subjects, events, phenomena, and people [ ]. It also can better understand the research questions.
Most of the studies in this review employed cross-sectional study designs, while longitudinal studies are rare. Longitudinal studies provide a better understanding of the development process of social phenomena and enable comparison of changes in different periods [ ]. The sports motivation of college student groups is in a state of dynamic change. In future research, employing more longitudinal studies will better observe the change process in sports motivation.
Do various sports lead to differences in motivation due to differences in sports models? No comparative studies on motivation to participate in different sports were found in the included articles. There were fewer studies on non-traditional sports like eSports and bridge. These are all potential focuses for future research.
Many studies have focused on exploring the relationship between intra-individual factors and sports motivation. A person's psychological plasticity is influenced not only by intra-individual factors but also by external factors. However, research on extra-individual factors has not received the amount of attention it deserves, especially the influence of policy factors on sports motivation. In the future, scholars can explore more about the relationship between extra-individual factors and sports motivation.

4.7

## Limitations of this review
This systematic review is the first attempt to comprehensively investigate the sports motivation for physical activity among Chinese college students. It yielded several new findings, but there are also limitations. One potential limitation is that it focuses on a sample of college students in mainland China, which may limit the applicability of the findings to other countries due to differences in cultural and political contexts. In addition, the limitations of this investigation are related to its design, as most of the established literature is a cross-sectional study with different instruments and influencing factors that do not allow for additional meta-analysis.
Another limitation is the possibility that the literature included is not comprehensive. Since the population of the study was Chinese college students, the authors searched mainstream Chinese and English databases. However, this review excluded studies published in languages other than English and Chinese, grey literature or other supplementary materials, and less commonly used databases. It may result in omitting some information from some kinds of literature.
The search strategy employed in this review was considered appropriate, and the data extraction process was rigorous. However, the included articles did not contain studies in languages other than English and Chinese, grey literature or other supplemental materials, and less commonly used databases. MMAT allows for a uniform assessment of all studies through a standardized tool that does not include a scoring system. Therefore, using the MMAT to compare quality scores between included articles is not feasible. Although an overall score was not provided, it is worth noting that the studies included in this review exhibited a commendable level of quality. The data presented herein can be utilized with a reasonable degree of confidence.

5

## Conclusions
Chinese college students' sports motivation is influenced by various factors, including demographic, psychological, social support, physical environment, and policy factors. Most research on Chinese college students' sports motivation over the past five years has focused on intra-individual factors. However, relatively little attention was paid to social support, physical environment, and policy factors. It was also lacking in qualitative and longitudinal study design.
In future research, the findings of this study can be recommended to relevant scholars for further investigation. Furthermore, researchers could further investigate extra-individual factors of sports motivation, such as social support, physical environment, and policy factors. And scholars can experiment with qualitative and longitudinal research methods.
