## Abstract
Optimizing the distribution of renewable energy production and developing a resilient and reliable supply network are particularly critical in the climate change era. This study examines the spatial characteristics and driving factors of the spatial network of renewable energy production from 2010 to 2022, revealing a progressively stabilized structure characterized by a core-edge pattern centered around Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Shandong, Fujian, and Hubei. It also shows that economic conditions, industrial structure, numbers of vehicles, and climate factors significantly shape the network’s dynamics, as well as the importance of provinces in networks. Moreover, the types and intensities of extreme climate events have heterogeneous impacts on various renewable energy categories. It underscores the necessity for region-specific energy transformation strategies to enhance network resilience and efficiency in response to climate variability.

## Introduction
Given the increasing significance of global climate change, it is evident that extreme climate events have occurred more frequently recently. Studies have found that climate change is responsible for an estimated annual economic loss of over $143 billion , and the world economy could experience a 19% reduction within the next 26 years due to climate change . Furthermore, studies have revealed the detrimental effects of climate change on various aspects like health , food security , poverty , inequality , and energy consumption , . Among these facts, countries initiated their efforts on climate conservation and climate mitigation by setting greenhouse gas emissions reduction targets, designing and implementing carbon tax schemes, and establishing emissions trading systems .
Energy consumption is widely acknowledged as a significant contributor to carbon emissions . In 2023, global carbon emissions from energy use reached a staggering 35,129.8 million metric tonnes . Therefore, another promising approach to mitigating climate change is the transition from conventional energy sources such as coal and oil to renewable alternatives like solar and wind power. This shift has demonstrated significant potential for reducing carbon emissions and enhancing environmental quality , . Renewable energy has had significant global growth in recent years. In 2023, there was a remarkable increase in renewable electricity capacity, with around 560 GW of new capacity added, setting a record for annual additions (The relevant data was sourced from the official website of the International Energy Agency. Further details are available at: https://www.iea.org/energy-system/renewables). In the long run, substituting traditional energy for renewable sources is a highly effective way to alleviate the impacts of climate change.
In this scenario, building a robust and reliable network for renewable energy production, as well as understanding the structure of renewable energy production networks and critical production nodes, have become imperative. Prior research has made substantial strides in promoting the development of renewable energy networks, particularly in the areas of renewable energy production and utilization. Multiple studies highlight the significance of spatial linkage, autocorrelation, and spillover in relation to renewable energy consumption , . Generally, the trans-regional power transmission mission, along with economic and energy technological cooperation across provinces, has led to the evolution of renewable energy consumption into a complex network structure. The complex consumption network structure is also determined by the significant impact of neighboring regions’ energy usage patterns on energy production , and the significant spatial dependence characteristics of energy consumption among regions , . However, there have been few studies that have focused on the spatial network of renewable energy production. Few researchers have conducted a limited study on spatial correlation and spillover , largely ignoring the spatial network discussion. Meanwhile, previous discussion of the spatial production network or renewable energy was mainly based on the exploratory spatial data analysis (ESDA) and Geo-Detector model , K-means algorithm , and spatial Durbin model (SDM) . Although these methods could provide the spatial connection, correlation, and spillover, they do not treat the production of renewable energy in each region as a complete spatial network but only discuss local clustering or related effects in space. It is also impossible to systematically examine the development of renewable energy systems. Therefore, it is valuable for further understanding the structure of renewable energy production networks and recognizing critical production nodes.
Meanwhile, recent literature is also beginning to focus on potential factors that may impact renewable energy production, such as technology, environmental factors, supply factors, economic growth, government support, household energy efficiency, carbon emissions, and the influence of political and economic extreme shocks – . However, the generation of power through renewable energy is heavily dependent on weather conditions. The occurrence of catastrophic climate events not only disrupts renewable energy production but also affects renewable energy infrastructure, leading to power shortages . Therefore, current literature also increasingly highlights the impact of climate change on the reliability of renewable energy production, including solar power generation , , , as well as wind and water power generation , . Simultaneously, the presence of severe climate events will interrupt the transfer of electrical power both inside and among different areas .
Given the unique nature of renewable energy production, rural regions frequently host it ; consequently, the primary challenge in advancing renewable energy is its cross-regional production and transmission between rural and urban areas. The extreme weather not only impacts the generation of power by renewable energy but also increases the transmission costs and reliability of renewable energy systems. Consequently, studying not only the economic conditions, government support, etc., but also the impact of extreme climate events on renewable energy production is highly significant for the advancement of renewable energy and the transition to a low-carbon economy. Building a reliable and stable renewable energy supply network is a central priority towards the ambitious net emission goals and climate change mitigation, and investigating the impacts of extreme climate events on spatial renewable energy production networks is particularly important in this scenario.
Therefore, this article makes an initial attempt to assess the structure and characteristics of the renewable energy production (REP) spatial network and to explore the influence of extreme climate events on it. Meanwhile, the entire REP network has been separated into three sub-networks in this paper to offer additional insights on solar, water, and wind generation. In addition, the driving factors are studied as well, particularly regarding the impacts of climate extremes. This study derives its findings from an evaluation of data from all 31 provinces in China, which takes into account several key considerations: First, Asia is highly vulnerable to extreme weather, accounting for 65.1% of global disasters from 2000 to 2021, and the frequency of disasters is also the highest among all continents in 2021(The relevant data was obtained from the Global Disaster Data Platform, Further details are available at:: https://www.gddat.cn/newGlobalWeb/#/riskAssessment). Besides, China is more susceptible to climatic calamities in comparison to other Asian countries . China’s peculiarities make it an ideal venue to discuss the impacts of major climate events on the renewable energy production network.
Second, China, which is a significant contributor to carbon emissions, released a staggering 11681.465 Mt of CO2 into the atmosphere in 2024 (The data on carbon dioxide emissions were obtained from the Carbon Monitor platform, available at: https://carbonmonitor.org/). The challenges it faces in transitioning to a low-carbon economy are unprecedented, and it also underscores the challenges that climate change poses to the advancement of China’s renewable energy sector. Therefore, China presents a compelling case for examining the network of renewable energy production. Third, China plays a pivotal role in the global renewable energy landscape. In 2022, it accounted for approximately 50% of the worldwide net increase in wind and solar capacity while generating a substantial 2,542.145 TWh of electricity from renewable sources (Fig.  a and Table ). Furthermore, the share of renewable energy installed capacity first surpassed the traditional one in 2023 and reached 56.4% of the total installed capacity in 2024 (Fig.  b), which also presents its vital potential for global renewable production. An investigation of China’s renewable energy production network and the effects of severe climate events will provide a prompt assessment of the influence of extreme weather on renewable energy systems. Additionally, it will offer valuable insights for countries worldwide seeking to shift to renewable energy sources.
 Finally, China’s renewable energy production is highly regional, with provinces such as Xizang, Qinghai, Sichuan, and Yunnan having a higher share of renewable energy production in total energy production. Conversely, the production capacity of renewable energy in developed areas like Beijing and Shanghai is clearly insufficient (Fig.  c). In the meantime, certain provinces in China, like Yunnan, Xizang, and Sichuan, primarily utilize hydropower as their renewable energy sources, whereas Inner Mongolia heavily depends on wind energy (Fig.  ). Therefore, studying the renewable energy production network can help identify important nodes, clarify the role of renewable energy in the production network, and ensure the stability of the interregional renewable energy supply. Moreover, taking into account extreme weather events will also help improve the ability to respond to energy shortages and deployment under extreme conditions.
 Compared to existing literature, this study has made several significant contributions. (1) This paper uses a social network analysis (SNA) model to provide an in-depth assessment of the production network in 31 Chinese provinces, which is critical for identifying the key provinces in the process of energy transition and ensuring sustainable futures with zero net emissions. (2) This article integrates the occurrence of extreme climate events and the spatial distribution of renewable energy production, providing valuable insights for addressing the uncertainties posed by climate change. Meanwhile, it is providing several clues to identify and mitigate the most urgent obstacles to extreme climatic occurrences. (3) It incorporates current studies on the spatial network of renewable energy, focusing on the factors that drive its development and the potential implications of weather pattern evolution. This information is particularly valuable for promoting the use of renewable energy at a regional level. However, it has to admit that these insights in this paper are closer to the theoretical potential of the renewable energy production under optimized conditions and may be regulated by the constraints of the power grid in practice. It can incorporate detailed transmission capacity data and more integrated dispatching models in future research, which will be able to provide a more refined assessment.
The rest of this paper is organized as follows: Part 2 presents the research design, empirical methods, and data used in this paper. Part 3 presents the results of this paper; part 4 discusses the related results and indications. Part 5 summarizes the conclusions.

## Spatial network analysis
The SNA model is frequently used in production networks, which effectively summarizes the global and node characteristics (Table  ) of spatial networks . The first part mainly includes the analysis of the number of network relationships, network density, hierarchy, efficiency, and network connectedness. Among them, the network relationship number and network density measure the correlation degree of the whole network, that is, the production correlation degree of REP in 31 provinces of China. The larger the value is, the stronger the production correlation exists. The network hierarchy determines whether there are significant grade differences across the entire network. Network efficiency gauges the redundancy of network relations throughout the entire network, and the higher the value, the more dependable and efficient the network is. Network connectedness represents the accessibility between individuals in the network . The higher value indicates a more stable spatial network relationship. However, when calculating the connectedness, the matrix must be symmetric.
 Second, this paper characterizes each node (province) in the REP network by assessing its degree centrality, betweenness centrality, and closeness centrality . Degree centrality delineates each province’s central position and influence. A province with direct connections to many other provinces has a central position within the network, resulting in enhanced control capabilities and elevated centrality. The betweenness centrality rating signifies the ‘intermediary’ function of provinces in managing resources and information. A province situated among several provinces will exhibit a higher betweenness centrality rating. Ultimately, closeness centrality denotes the degree of proximity among provinces within the network. The greater the value, the more significant the impact of one province on the dissemination of renewable energy knowledge or the transfer of associated product resources within the network.

## Driving factors analysis of the REP network

## Total network driving factors
Given that ordinary least squares regression analysis requires the independence of variables, a certain connection across network nodes will definitely arise when detecting network components. The QAP technique is a non-parametric method that tests relationships between matrices by examining their structural similarity. This study employs QAP to examine the association between the renewable energy production spatial network, represented by a difference matrix, and the difference matrices of various driving factors, particularly climate factors, as well as the spatial weight matrix included in the analytical procedure . Unlike many conventional statistical techniques, QAP does not require the assumption of variable independence , . Moreover, as one of the few methods capable of identifying drivers in spatial network structures, it is widely used in studies on spatial distribution patterns and decoupled networks.
The QAP analysis is typically conducted in two stages : correlation analysis and regression analysis. The correlation analysis involves the following steps: (1) converting the two matrices into long vectors, each containing n×(n − 1) elements, and computing the Pearson correlation coefficient between them; (2) for getting a robust result, this method simultaneously permuting the rows and columns of one matrix multiple times randomly and recalculating the correlation coefficient each time; (3) repeating the process to generate a distribution of random correlation coefficients. The initial observed correlation is then compared against this random correlation distribution. Statistical significance is assessed based on whether the observed coefficient falls within the critical region of the reference distribution. The principle of QAP regression analysis is similar to that of correlation analysis but is used to examine the relationship between multiple independent matrices and one dependent matrix. It also evaluates the significance of the goodness-of-fit (R²) through random permutation.
By referencing previous literature , , this paper primarily examines the spatial distance (AM), the differences in industrial structure (IS), economic condition (GDP), government power (GP), vehicle ownership (VED), and environmental regulation (EN) as factors that influence the formation of the REP network. Additionally, this paper takes into account climate considerations such as extreme event intensity (EEI) and alterations in temperature patterns (ETC). The selection of determinants is grounded in a multi-dimensional framework encompassing geographic, economic, institutional, and environmental drivers of network formation. First, spatial distance (AM) is included as a fundamental factor for connecting renewable energy production infrastructure. Second, economic and industrial factors (GDP, IS, VED) capture the capacity, demand, and structural complementarity that motivate renewable energy production cooperation. Third, institutional and policy factors (GP, EN) represent the governance capacity and regulatory incentives that translate willingness into actionable projects. Last, climate factors (EEI, ETC) are incorporated as critical external shocks and long-term risks that reshape energy security calculus and necessitate interconnectedness. ## Driving factors of nodes importance
Identifying the factors that determine the importance of REP network nodes helps to further stabilize the relationship in REP networks, identify the determinants of weak nodes, and further improve the spatial connection between weak nodes and other network members. The method of moments quantile regression (MMQR) effectively addresses challenges associated with outliers and heavy-tailed distributions by elucidating heterogeneous effects throughout the entire conditional distribution . In contrast to conventional mean-based regression methods, the MMQR model distinctly captures the differential influences of covariates on both the location and scale of the dependent variable . This capability allows for precise identification of the asymmetric and heterogeneous impacts of extreme climate events on regions with varying renewable energy production capacities, thereby yielding more comprehensive and policy-relevant insights than what can be obtained from average effects alone. Therefore, this paper uses a MMQR model to further identify the important factors for each network node of REP.
Machado and Silva (2019) proposed the MMQR technique, an effective instrument for resolving the endogeneity issue, offering a thorough assessment of each fixed effect within the model . This method seeks to elucidate the non-linear complexities inherent in the relationship between driving factors and degree centrality of the REP network, especially for extreme climate events. This sophisticated methodology facilitates a deeper comprehension, transcending the constraints of linear methods, and establishes a basis for a more thorough analysis of the complex interactions among various factors. ## Research data
The data on renewable energy production are sourced from the China Energy Statistical Yearbook, which is compiled by the National Bureau of Statistics of China . The control variables are from the Chinese Statistical Yearbook (see Table  ) . The data on extreme climate events comes from the EM-DAT database , which provides comprehensive information on climate events’ timing, duration, fatalities, and other relevant details. Numerous research institutes that specialize in the study of extreme events use it extensively . As presented in Table  , renewable energy production varied substantially across provinces from 2010 to 2022, indicating significant interprovincial disparities. A further discussion and analysis of the spatial distribution of these disparities—particularly under the influence of extreme climate events—can help optimize the spatial planning of renewable energy production in the context of climate change and support the ongoing energy transition. Similarly, the distribution of extreme climate events exhibits substantial cross-provincial heterogeneity, reflected by the large range of values. This notable variation underscores the divergent climate risks and challenges faced by different regions, resulting in asymmetrical impacts on renewable energy production. Consequently, conducting a detailed discussion and quantile analysis is essential to better inform strategies aimed at enhancing the resilience of the REP system in the face of climate change.
 

## Results

## Characteristics of REP network in China

## The overall distribution of renewable energy production
As depicted in Fig.  , it is evident that the renewable energy production has notable differences between regions. Sichuan and Yunnan are the two provinces that generate the most electricity from renewable sources, accounting for nearly 30% of the country’s electricity. Figure  reveals that these two provinces primarily depend on hydroelectric power generation, making extreme events like floods and droughts a significant threat to their production capacity. At the same time, it could also lead to power disruptions and shortages in provinces that rely on renewable energy from these two provinces. In addition to these two provinces, Xinjiang, Inner Mongolia, Hubei, and Hebei are also large renewable energy generation provinces, and unlike Hubei, which relies on a large amount of hydroelectric power, Xinjiang and Inner Mongolia mainly rely on wind power, while Hebei mainly relies on wind and solar power. This shows that different geographical characteristics drive the development of different types of renewable energy, and the occurrence of different types of extreme climate events will inevitably bring different degrees of impact on renewable energy production. Therefore, studying the characteristics of REP networks and local areas, especially for the different extreme climate events’ impacts, is critical for the construction of stable and reliable renewable energy supply networks.
 

## Characteristics and trend of the overall network
Before assessing the impact of extreme climate events, it is crucial to establish the REP network. The spatial matrix of China’s REP network from 2000 to 2022 was calculated based on Eq. ( ), and the UCINET and Gephi software were used to calculate the real relationships and characteristics of the REP network among 31 provinces from 2010 to 2022. The spatial network structure of REP for 31 provinces in 2010, 2015, 2020, and 2022 (Fig.  ) indicates the existence of strong spatial relationships between provinces. Furthermore, the spatial network is not solely dependent on geographical proximity but is also developing a core-edge structure. This network places certain provinces, such as Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Shandong, Fujian, and Hubei, at its core. These provinces have strong interprovincial connections and play a crucial role in REP’s coordinated regional development. On the other hand, Guangxi, Hainan, Tianjin, Anhui, Ningxia, and Xizang are located at the edge of the network, showing minimal correlation with other provinces. Notably, Hainan and Tianjin have experienced years of isolation from the rest of the provinces. Nevertheless, the interconnectedness of the spatial network structure of the REP among the 31 provinces indicates that it is suitable to examine the spatial linkages of the REP using the SNA method, which relies on the spatial correlation matrix derived from the modified gravity model.
 Back to the characteristics of the REP network, it is worth noting that the number of network relationships and network density indicate a strong interconnection between provinces in terms of renewable energy production. The total number of relationships increased from 180 in 2010 to 214 in 2022 (Fig.  ), suggesting the close spatial connections between provinces on renewable energy production. Additionally, the spatial network density experienced a notable increase from 0.194 in 2010 to 0.230 in 2022. This reveals the growing significance and stability of the renewable energy network, as well as the advancements in renewable energy technologies, materials, and interprovincial interactions. The level of network connectedness has increased from 0.873 to 1.000 and remained stable in recent years, suggesting the formation of a stable spatial network. The value of 1.000 means that there are no isolated nodes, and information or resources can flow between any nodes. Simultaneously, the network efficiency has declined from 0.712 to 0.692, which suggests a boost in the REP network’s spatial spillover, resulting in multiple overlaps and the gradual formation of the final spatial network structure. It is noteworthy that the network hierarchy has experienced substantial shifts, indicating that provincial governments’ emphasis on the energy transition has led to an astonishing surge in renewable energy productivity, breaking the old rigid levels of supply capacity.
 However, during the pandemic, the number of relationships and network density experienced a significant decrease, reflecting the profound impact of COVID-19. The pandemic control policies, which restrict regional mobility, have significantly slowed down the development and research of renewable energy and renewable energy infrastructure projects. Additionally, there has been a shift in focus towards prioritizing the prevention and control of infectious diseases and ensuring the material security of life. Therefore, during the pandemic, the density of REP networks has decreased due to various factors such as transportation obstacles, personnel limitations, and funding constraints. Meanwhile, provinces have also cut their investments in renewable energy infrastructure, slowed down the advancement of renewable energy technology, and experienced significant disruptions in the transportation and trading of renewable energy materials. Hence, during this time, the REP network hierarchy also experienced increase.

## Characteristics and trend of the individual network
As depicted in Fig.  , the centrality of each province has not undergone a drastic change, with Zhejiang, Jiangsu, Fujian, Hubei, and Guangdong consistently occupying important positions in the REP network. This indicates that coastal cities are the center of the entire renewable energy production network, and changes in their energy transition policies and the chain effects caused by unpredictable external shocks they face will directly affect the stability of the REP network. However, there are certain differences in the reasons why these cities play an important role in the REP network. Zhejiang, Jiangsu, and Fujian have become significant nodes in the REP network because they receive renewable energy input from multiple provinces, which contributes to their high degree of integration. Not only does Hubei Province receive renewable energy technology and talent from other provinces, but it also delivers renewable energy to various places. Meanwhile, as the trend of in-degree and out-degree centrality shifts (Fig.  ), provinces like Gansu and Inner Mongolia, while not playing as pivotal a role in the REP network as coastal cities overall, continue to contribute renewable energy to the network, making them an indispensable part of it.
 Unlike coastal cities that play an important role in the REP network, Tianjin, Shanghai, Jilin, and Heilongjiang are located at the edge of the network with extremely low centrality, partly because these provinces are not suitable for producing renewable energy due to climate and geographical factors. Meanwhile, Tianjin and Shanghai have a lower demand for renewable energy due to their economic structures, which primarily rely on tertiary industries rather than heavy energy industries. However, the limited land area of these two regions also makes them unsuitable for large-scale renewable energy production. For Jilin and Heilongjiang, they also have a relatively low role in the REP network, attributed to their economic structure, heavy industry and manufacturing, strong dependence on traditional energy, and large geographical and economic distances from other regions.
The results of betweenness centrality and closeness centrality are similar to the results of degree centrality (Fig.  ). Jiangsu, Zhejiang, and Fujian provinces not only play a crucial role in the REP network but also maintain close connections with other members. Moreover, these provinces also serve as intermediaries and bridges in the REP network. It is also obvious that compared with 2010, the average of closeness and betweenness centrality has a significant uptrend, which also hints that renewable energy production in each province is forming a more stable and closely connected REP network in recent years. In addition to the common characteristics of the degree of centrality, the results of the betweenness centrality show that Sichuan, Hebei, and Gansu also play a pivotal and bridging role in the renewable energy network, which is mainly related to the geographic location of the three provinces, whose geographic location makes it possible to assume an important role in the communication between the North and South and well-connected the West and the Middle East. Also, the closeness centrality also hints that Sichuan, Hebei, Inner Mongolia, and Gansu have relatively close distances with other provinces, which also indicates the inseparable roles of these provinces in the REP spatial network.
 

## Subnetwork characteristics of renewable energy
In order to gain a deeper understanding of China’s renewable energy production details, this paper separates the renewable energy network into three sub-production networks: solar, water, and wind (since solar production data was only disclosed beginning in 2015; therefore, the discussion of sub-networks also starting in 2015). Similar to the main network, the renewable energy sub-network’s relationship intensity and network density have increased to varying degrees, indicating that the production of these three types of renewable energy has begun to take shape in China. Notably, the hydropower network has the closest production relationships between provinces in the sub-network, with a total of 208 relationships as of 2022. This is primarily due to the fact that water energy, being the first extensively developed and utilized renewable energy source, has reached a relatively mature stage and is beginning to take shape in many provinces.
The cooperation and exchange between provinces in the process of solar energy production is the newest of the three sub-networks. In 2015, the number of relationships in the solar energy production network was only 152. However, due to its rapid development, the relationships surpassed the wind energy sub-network in 2022, reaching 198. Partnerships among the provinces in the wind sub-network were the most stable, growing from 173 in 2015 to 178 in 2022, which could be attributed to wind energy’s more stringent requirements for climate conditions and the slower iteration speed of wind power technology compared to photovoltaic. Conversely, endogenous cost declines and efficiency improvements in photovoltaic technology, together with demand catalyzed by China’s ‘dual-carbon’ commitments—carbon peaking before 2030 and carbon neutrality before 2060 (announced on September 22, 2020, at the 75th session of the United Nations General Assembly)—generated a virtuous cycle that both accelerated industrial restructuring and unlocked downstream demand, resulting in a markedly higher growth rate than wind power.
Aside from that, the three types of REP networks have different regional characteristics (Fig.  ). With the exception of Zhejiang, Guangdong, and Jiangsu, which all play a significant role in each of the sub-networks, the other regions have their own characteristics in three different renewable energy networks. Among them, Guangdong, Shandong, Shanghai, and Hebei play an important role in the solar energy production network. Shandong and Hebei produce 21.18% of the country’s solar energy, so they are important exporters in the production network. Sichuan, Yunnan, Fujian, Guizhou, and Hubei play a pivotal role in the water energy production network, supplying 62.04% of the water energy in 2022 (Fig.  and Fig. S3). Although Zhejiang, Guangdong, and Jiangsu are in the core position in the hydropower network, the status of these provinces mainly depends on their huge absorption capacity. If governments want to further secure the stability of the hydropower production network supply, they need to concentrate on the hydropower production status of Sichuan, Yunnan, and Hubei.
 Shandong, Hebei, and Inner Mongolia are the three most important provinces in the wind energy production network after Zhejiang, Jiangsu, and Guangdong, which produced 27.27% of the country’s wind energy in 2022. Due to its extensive regional planning and natural climatic conditions, Inner Mongolia excels in wind energy production across all provinces, making it a significant exporter in the wind energy network. At the same time, combined with the overall wind energy network and wind energy production, although Fujian has the important role in wind power production, China’s main wind energy production deeply depends on land wind farms, and the corresponding offshore wind power development is not perfect.

## Driving factors analysis

## Total network driving factors
This paper applies the QAP correlation and QAP regression to further detect the impact of extreme climate events on the REP network. There are strong negative links between extreme climate change and the REP network, as shown by the QAP correlation results (Fig.  ) from 5,000 random replacement calculations. This means that extreme weather events such as floods and storms will make it harder for provinces to share information, technologies, and materials about renewable energy, slowing down the pace of the stable REP network formation. In addition to climate change impacts, factors such as spatial distance, differences in economic levels, government power, vehicle ownership, and environmental regulations will intensify relationships within the REP network.
 This paper also employs the QAP regression after 5,000 random replacement calculations, with Table  providing the coefficients and P-values. The results indicate that AM significantly influences the development of the REP network at a significance level of 1%, which suggests that a closer geographical distance can create more convenient transportation conditions for the growth of renewable energy. This, in turn, can foster coordinated regional renewable energy development and establish communication channels for the exchange and innovation of new renewable energy technologies.
 The IS coefficient is significantly negative at the 1% level, indicating that similar industry structures are not conducive to the establishment of renewable energy production networks. The service industry, which lightly relies on energy, dominates the tertiary industry, while the measure of IS is the added value of tertiary industry proportion in the gross national product. Therefore, if two regions in this industry are closer to each other, the demand for renewable energy and the exchange of production technologies will decrease.
The variations in economic conditions also contribute positively to the formation of a stable renewable energy spatial network. The distribution of renewable energy primarily occurs in regions with low economic development levels, such as Xinjiang in the western region, which is a significant area for solar energy development . Inner Mongolia is an important producing area for wind power, while coastal cities with high economic development levels, such as Jiangsu and Zhejiang, often do not have suitable conditions for renewable energy production. As a result, the difference in economic conditions can promote trans-regional transportation and renewable energy sharing to a certain extent.
The vehicle ownership difference also has a positive impact on the REP network at the 5% statistical level. The number of vehicles in a region will partially determine the energy demand in that region; therefore, if two regions have notable differences in vehicle numbers and the ability to produce renewable energy, there is a high probability for these two regions to exchange power and related resources and technologies. This will also improve the REP relationship and create a favorable environment for the development of renewable energy.
Last, the coefficient of EEI is negative at the 1% level, which means the difference in the intensity of extreme climate events will hinder the REP network formation. This is due to the significant dependence of renewable energy production on weather conditions. Different inter-regional climate models and the varying frequency and patterns of climate disasters hinder the development of renewable energy production at the same pace and level of cooperation, resulting in a weaker spatial correlation. However, differences in government power, environmental regulations, and temperature patterns have not significantly impacted the formation of the REP network correlation.

## Nodes importance driving factors
The total network driving factors are based on the mean REP network, and they cannot reflect the determinates of each province. Furthermore, it does not offer any additional details regarding the impact of extreme climate events. As a result, this paper uses degree centrality as the dependent variable to examine the impact of extreme climate events and temperature pattern evolution on the REP network.
The MMQR regression results have shown many intriguing hints (Table  ): First, the extreme climate events have unequal impacts on nodes in the REP network. For core nodes in the REP network (quantile 80), the occurrence of extreme climate events does not affect their vital roles. In contrast, for peripheral nodes (quantile 70), extreme climate events will significantly increase their importance within the network. The reason is that provinces at the edge node will suffer certain losses in renewable energy production capacity due to the material donation and energy transfer of other provinces after the disaster when climate disasters occur. Therefore, support for energy and related advanced renewable energy technologies in these regions will bring them closer to other provincial nodes and increase their importance. According to the total network results, the temperature pattern change also has no significant impact on the REP network.
 The upgrading of industrial structures can significantly improve the influence of low-importance provincial nodes in the REP network, but its effect is limited. The nonlinear relationship between economic development level and the importance of the REP network is surprising. For provinces with low importance, the rise in economic level will reduce their role in the REP network. The possible reason is that these provinces’ development focusses on economic development and industrial structure upgrading, so they pay less attention to renewable energy. However, for the more important provinces in the REP network, the improvement of their economic conditions can provide a favorable economic foundation and material conditions for the development of their renewable energy and increase their influence in the REP network.
There is an inverted U-shaped relationship between the number of vehicles and the importance of nodes in REP networks. The expansion of vehicle scale leads to a corresponding increase in energy demand for edge provinces, thereby elevating the significance of a province in the REP network. However, as the number of vehicles continues to grow at crucial locations, the productivity of current renewable energy frequently struggles to keep pace with the increasing number of motor vehicles within a brief period. Consequently, this shift in energy demand to traditional sources diminishes the significance of these provinces in the REP network.
Beyond the marginal effects among dependent variables, the driving factors also influence the distribution of renewable energy production. Improvements in climatic and economic conditions shift the entire distribution rightward, thereby increasing the importance of renewable energy production across nodes. In addition to affecting the location of the distribution, factors such as temperature patterns, industrial structure, and vehicle numbers may reduce interprovincial disparities, whereas economic growth tends to widen regional gaps. These findings further suggest that traditional regression methods, which focus solely on conditional means, overlook the potential influence of independent variables on the shape of the dependent variable’s distribution—underscoring the appropriateness of using the method of MMQR in this context.
To further verify and deepen the understanding of the above findings, a bootstrap quantile regression (BSQR) is employed as a robustness check. BSQR employs bootstrap-based quantile regression to estimate and make inferences about how explanatory factors differentially affect nodes with different levels of importance. Therefore, it could be used to do a robust check in this part. The results align with those obtained from the primary analysis (Table  ), confirming the robustness of the baseline conclusions.
 To further detect the frequency and the intensity of extreme climate events’ impact on the REP network, the duration and the chain reaction produced by the event, that is, whether there is a secondary disaster, are also considered in this paper. Results (Table  ) show that the duration is not that important for the nodes core role formation, but the intensity of climate events do have a magnificent effect on low impaction provinces in the REP network, which hints that in provinces where renewable energy is just beginning, the occurrence of extreme weather events will have irretrievably powerful consequences for local renewable energy systems, and this will also harm their position in the REP network. Furthermore, this paper examines various event categories such as flood, storm, drought, and extreme temperature. Corresponding results interpret that the floods are the most extreme climate events that cannot be ignored because of their high frequency and extremely destructive nature, which can have a serious impact on renewable energy production, especially in start-up provinces.
 Further, the heterogeneity impacts of extreme climate events on solar, water, and wind spatial networks are discussed in this paper (Table  ), and there is a distinct difference between networks. In the case of solar and wind networks, the high frequency of extreme climate events will reduce the importance of provinces in their networks, particularly those with high degrees of centrality. This is attributed to the development process of these two renewable energy sources, as the scale of wind and solar energy production has significantly expanded in recent years. Additionally, the implementation of policies such as photovoltaic poverty alleviation policies (China’s photovoltaic poverty alleviation policies empower impoverished households by installing solar power stations, enabling sustainable income through electricity sales while advancing renewable energy goals.) and the involvement of thousands of villages have accelerated the development of solar energy. However, its late start and low degree of promotion determine its vulnerability to extreme climate shocks. Therefore, the occurrence of extreme climate events will significantly reduce the province’s importance in the energy network. On the contrary, extreme climate shocks have an enhanced effect on the importance of provinces in the hydropower network. This is because the development of hydropower needs a certain hydrological environment as support and has a certain development threshold, which makes the generation of shocks strengthen the attention on the stability of hydropower production. Simultaneously, water energy production plays a crucial role in China’s renewable energy production. As a result, external climate shocks will intensify the focus on renewable energy policies, ensuring the stability of the water energy production network. Meanwhile, extreme external climate shocks will further solidify the importance of water energy production in the hydropower production network.
 

## Discussion
Optimizing the distribution of renewable energy generation, establishing a robust and stable production and supply network, and augmenting the resilience of the renewable energy production network necessitate consideration of the temporal and spatial characteristics, as well as the influencing factors, particularly climatic variables. Previous discussions have dedicated significant efforts to examining the developmental stages, influencing factors, spatial structures, and various characteristics of China’s renewable energy production , ,. However, existing research fails to address the network characteristics of renewable energy production networks and their major sub-networks, which are crucial for establishing a stable and reliable renewable energy production network during the low-carbon transition. Therefore, this paper strives to address these problems.
The findings of this paper indicate that China’s renewable energy exhibits significant regional disparities while progressively developing into a robust and interconnected network. It suggests that the spatial network is influenced not solely by geographical proximity but is also progressively establishing a core-edge configuration, with provinces such as Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Shandong, Fujian, and Hubei situated at the network’s center. Simultaneously, sub-networks, encompassing solar, water, and wind, possess distinct features regarding the overall network and its nodes. The development of these three forms of renewable energy has commenced in China, with the hydropower network exhibiting the most interconnected production relationships among provinces. However, the advancement of the solar energy production network is comparatively swift, as upgrades in solar technologies occur at a faster pace than in others. The ‘Hu Yun Chuan’ (Hubei, Yunnan, and Sichuan) region, which has extensive water resources, makes hydropower advancement a major priority in these provinces, underscoring their importance in the water hydropower network. Regions like Xinjiang and Inner Mongolia have abundant solar radiation and a sparse population, creating favorable conditions for the growth of solar and wind energy, thereby establishing them as integral components of the renewable energy production network. These findings also suggest that policymakers should leverage regional climatic and geographical advantages to enhance regional autonomy and initiative in order to establish a stable and organized renewable energy production cooperation network. Governments should develop a regional renewable power dispatch network and long-distance transmission between adjacent power grids to achieve the renewable power consumption guarantee mechanism .
In contrast to previous research that looked at the causes and spatial effects of producing renewable energy – , this paper aims to explain what determines the total REP network relationships to form and how the network connects as a whole. This research also examines the influence of extreme climatic shocks and temperature pattern changes on the entire REP network, as well as the significance of its nodes, while acknowledging the climate reliance of renewable energy. The geographical distance, variations in industrial structure, economic conditions, and vehicle quantity among provinces significantly influence the establishment of the REP network. The aforementioned aspects exert a diverse influence on the status of provinces within REP networks, underscoring the necessity to thoroughly evaluate the geographical location, climatic conditions, and economic state of each province in the establishment of REP networks.
Extreme climatic events will influence the establishment and stability of REP networks. Among all impact events, flood is the most exasperating. Furthermore, the analytical results of the sub-networks indicate that, during the initial phase of establishment for solar and wind energy networks, climatic events will facilitate energy substitution among provinces and diminish their motivation to advance solar and wind energy initiatives. Established renewable networks, like hydropower systems, accentuate this effect by strengthening the status of nodes within the network. The findings underscore the challenges posed by climate change to renewable energy development and suggest that the government should priorities climate change considerations, acknowledge the unique characteristics of renewable energy and climate vulnerability, and devise tailored development policies.
Despite the significant implications of this paper, there are specific limitations. Initially, due to the limitations of energy data transparency, this study primarily collected data at the provincial level. Future research that focuses on smaller units and real-time energy production data could provide more nuanced insights into the advancement of renewable energy. Moreover, for its applicability to real-world energy systems and to provide more specific insights on renewable energy production, further studies should incorporate the transmission capacity data or dispatching models to simulate the actual production and transmission scenarios. Further, although this paper selects the world’s largest renewable energy producer and applies a universal analytical framework, related results may have regional bias; therefore, further investigation could broaden the research area to get a more inclusive result. Second, limited data availability on smaller-scale climate events precluded the examination of specific disaster attributes, such as economic losses and shifts in climate expectations. Although this constraint narrows the scope of granular analysis, the present study is designed to capture the broader influence of extreme climate events on the renewable energy production network and its potential drivers. Discussions based on events of a certain scale remain valuable for identifying overarching climate patterns and their systemic impacts on energy infrastructure. Nevertheless, more detailed event-level data would undoubtedly help explain regional climate-energy interactions. Future studies should therefore incorporate finer-scale extreme climate information to better capture localized spatial impacts.
Third, despite drawing on previous literature to select a wide range of relevant driving factors, the analysis may still be subject to omitted variable bias. Moreover, due to methodological constraints in analyzing relationships between spatial network matrices, this study relies exclusively on the QAP method to examine driving effects within the overall spatial network. Future research could adopt emerging techniques to further validate the robustness of these findings. Finally, although the MMQR is generally robust, it relies on specific functional form assumptions and cannot account for feedback effects among variables. Therefore, more flexible or advanced methodologies should be explored in subsequent studies.

## Conclusions and policy implications
This paper examines the spatial structure and network characteristics of REP networks in China by gathering data on renewable energy production from 31 provinces between 2010 and 2022. Besides, an analysis is conducted on the driving factors behind the establishment of the REP network and its associated subnetworks by integrating EM-DAT and weather station temperature data alongside economic data, with particular emphasis on the potential effects of short-term climatic events. The primary findings are encapsulated as follows:
(1) The characteristics of the total and nodes of the REP network indicate that it is influenced not just by geographical proximity but also gradually forming a core- edge pattern, with provinces such as Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Shandong, Fujian, and Hubei situated at the network’s center. Water, wind, and solar energy generation are progressively establishing a robust and interconnected network, with hydropower exhibiting greater stability. These three networks create a distinctive sub-center alongside the shared coastal central nodes, specifically the sub-centers of Hubei-Yunnan-Sichuan, Hebei-Shandong-Shanghai, and Hebei-Inner Mongolia-Shandong.
(2) Spatial distance, industrial structure, economic conditions, and vehicle quantity all have a significant influence on the establishment of the REP network; however, governmental interventions and environmental restrictions have no significant effect on its formation. Nonetheless, these elements exert a disparate influence on the significance of each province within the spatial network. In some regions, certain variables are considered ‘meat’, while in others, they may be deemed ‘poison’. Energy regulations tailored to local conditions can significantly improve the spatial configuration of renewable energy production networks and address resource distribution disparities.
(3) Extreme climate events will influence the establishment and stability of REP networks. However, we can disregard the changes in temperature patterns when establishing a stable and reliable REP network. Additionally, the analyses of the sub-networks reveal that extreme weather events hinder the growth of solar and wind energy networks, which are substituted by other energy sources. While, in established renewable networks under the extreme climate shocks, such as the water energy network, further underscore its significant influence by enhancing the status of nodes within the network.
The findings of this study offer several important insights for policymakers seeking to optimize the spatial layout of renewable energy production networks and enhance their resilience. First, strengthen regional collaboration and establish a ‘core-periphery’ hierarchical development pattern. The results reveal that provinces such as Jiangsu, Zhejiang, and Shandong are at the network center and have already formed distinct sub-centers. Therefore, policymakers should further leverage the technological spillover and capital radiation effects of the eastern coastal central nodes to drive the edge provinces to connect to the network. For the three characteristic sub-centers that have already been primarily formed, a cross-regional power coordination mechanism should be established to break administrative barriers and achieve the optimal allocation of resources across the country through the improvement of ultra-high voltage transmission channels.
Second, implementing differentiated energy policies. Studies have shown that economic level and industrial structure have a significant impact on network formation, and the same factor may have completely opposite effects in different regions. Therefore, policymakers should abandon the ‘one-size-fits-all’ macro-energy policies and implement precise energy governance. For provinces with a strong industrial foundation, emphasis should be placed on enhancing the climate resilience of energy production; for provinces with dense transportation and logistics, efforts should be made to strengthen the coupling of transportation energy use with renewable energy, etc. In addition, local governments should formulate differentiated incentive policies that are in line with the local industrial structure and economic conditions based on their position and endowment in the network.
Last, it is crucial to enhance the resilience and defense capabilities of the production network against extreme climates. Results have shown that extreme weather has differential impacts on renewable energy networks, especially sub-networks. Therefore, a climate risk warning and multi-energy complementary system should be established. During periods of frequent extreme weather, the stability of the hydropower network should be utilized to play the role of a ‘balancing stone,’ and through cross-temporal complementarity among different energy types (such as hydropower, wind power, and solar power), the gaps caused by damage to wind power and photovoltaic power should be filled. At the same time, efforts should be focused on strengthening infrastructure construction in areas significantly affected by extreme weather and enhancing the physical resilience of the power grid.
