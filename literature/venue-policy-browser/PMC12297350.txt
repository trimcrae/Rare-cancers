Macao (or Macau) ranks as most densely populated tourist city of the world, with a population density of approximately 21,000 person per square kilometers , . Additionally, it attracts over 35 million visitors annually, alongside around 190,000 non-resident workers commuting daily from the mainland , . Consequently, the public bus service is crucial in alleviating the traffic congestion within this already overburdened transport system . Macao includes the Macao Peninsula, Taipa, and Coloane, covering a compact area of just 33.3 square kilometers and housing a population of approximately 673,000 residents. The Macao Peninsula serves as the core of urban development, with numerous large international hotel resorts and supporting infrastructure situated on the reclaimed land between Taipa and Coloane, particularly in the newly developed district known as Cotai , . Over the years, Macao has evolved from a tiny fishing village into a major international city. In 2005, it achieved a significant milestone when the Historic Centre of Macao was successfully added to the UNESCO World Heritage List, which has allowed Macao to present its distinctive historical and cultural heritage on a global stage. Currently, Macao is positioned as the World Center of Tourism and Leisure, aiming to enhance its status as a premier tourist destination .
The Macau Grand Prix represents an annual motor-sport road racing event featuring both automobiles and motorcycles, taking place on the Guia Circuit in Macau. This prestigious event encompasses the Formula Regional and Motorcycle Grand Prix title races, along with various competitions for touring, grand touring, and sports cars . Initially launched in 1954, the first Macau Grand Prix focused on sports car racing. The inaugural Macao Motorcycle Grand Prix debuted in 1967, further enriching the legacy of the event. In 2008, the introduction of a GT3 race transformed the event into the FIA GT World Cup . Traffic planning for major events often relies on empirical methods, and this subject has garnered minimal attention in existing literature. A key reason for this oversight is that many events (e.g., Olympics for a few weeks) uniquely occur only once for a limited duration. Consequently, the experiences from these events cannot be easily applied elsewhere, leading to solutions that predominantly rely on the local knowledge of each city . The Guia Circuit (aka. Circuito da Guia) spans 6.120 km and is situated in the southeastern region of the Macao Peninsula (see the left side of Fig.  ). This renowned street circuit hosts prestigious events such as the Macau Grand Prix, the Macau Motorcycle Grand Prix, and the Macau Guia Race. The track features a mix of long straights and tight corners, embodying the typical characteristics of street circuits which is narrow, bumpy, and presents limited opportunities for overtaking . This circuit was initially designed in 1954 as a treasure hunt route through the streets of the city, which quickly sparked the idea to hold an amateur racing event for local motor enthusiasts on the same track . By 1967, following the introduction of motorcycle races, the circuit evolved into a prominent venue hosting both motorcycle and automobile racing events . 
The Macao bus service operates frequently across the Macao Peninsula, Taipa, and Coloane, providing essential connectivity throughout these areas. Route information, displayed in both Chinese and Portuguese, is available at each bus stop, offering details about destinations, schedules, and stops. As of 2024, the bus fare is set at MOP6.00, and passengers are advised to have the exact change ready, as no change is provided on the buses, which are all air-conditioned. A total of 74 bus routes facilitate transportation to various locations, including Macao International Airport, with some buses navigating the narrow streets and alleys characteristic of the region . To enhance your travel experience in Macao, it is advisable to avoid peak hours when using the bus service. Having a map on hand can greatly assist you in navigating to your desired location more effectively. The primary operators of public bus transportation in Macao are TRANSMAC (Transportes Urbanos de Macau, S.A.R.L.) and TCM (Sociedade de Transportes Colectivos de Macau), ensuring a comprehensive network for residents and visitors alike . During the Grand Prix seasons in Macao, certain streets on the Macao Peninsula are closed to all traffic, including public transportation, because of the car and motorcycle races taking place. Consequently, bus routes that typically pass through these areas, particularly those aligned with the Guia Circuit, undergo modifications to accommodate the restrictions of the event. The public bus dataset from the DSAT contains two distinct unique features which are the passenger flow and the traffic condition . The passenger flow data is represented by a numerical scale ranging from  −1 to 5, designed to indicate the level of crowding inside the bus during the recorded interval. A value of  −1 is specifically used to denote situations where data regarding passenger load was not available for that particular entry. The values from 0 to 5 are employed to represent increasing levels of passenger density within the vehicle. A value of 0 suggests minimal to no crowding, while higher numerical values correspond to progressively more crowded conditions. The maximum value of 5 indicates the most crowded state, signifying that the bus is likely at or near its capacity. This scale provides a granular measure of passenger load, offering insights into the demand and comfort levels experienced during bus journeys. The traffic condition feature provides a quantitative description of the prevailing traffic conditions encountered on the specific road segment traversed by the bus between its departure point and its arrival point. The data utilizes a categorical scale to represent varying degrees of traffic congestion. The possible values recorded are −1, 1, 2, and 3. A value of −1 is specifically assigned to indicate instances where no traffic data was available for that particular segment and time. Conversely, values ranging from 1 to 3 denote progressively increasing levels of traffic congestion. A value of 1 suggests relatively light traffic, while a value of 2 indicates moderate congestion. The highest value, 3, signifies heavy traffic conditions, implying significant delays and reduced travel speeds on the road section. This structured categorization allows for a clear understanding of the traffic environment influencing bus travel times. Additional features obtained from the DSAT are detailed in Table  .

## Methods

## Data Collection
The dataset utilized for this analysis was compiled by crawling real-time data from the DSAT (Regional Government Transport Bureau) database, an authoritative source that provides up-to-date transportation information as described in Fig.  . This vital data was subsequently restored into a structured format, specifically as comma-separated values (.csv), to facilitate easier manipulation and analysis. The dataset encompasses information collected within a defined timeframe, reflecting the operational performance of bus routes during both regular operational periods and the renowned Grand Prix days. By employing this methodology, we ensure that the dataset accurately represents the varied dynamics of public transport in Macao, incorporating a broad spectrum of relevant features that are crucial for in-depth analysis and subsequent interpretations. 
The original dataset had contained 276,667 samples and the outliers exceeding ±3σ of the arrival duration arrivalDuration were removed. Finally the dataset has contained 217,545 samples (21.4% removed). Approximately 4.8% of the normal season data and 56.6% of the Grand Prix season data were excluded. Detailed dataset sizes are provided in Table  .

## Data Records
The data collection for the off season (or the normal season) occurred over a two-week period from October to December in 2024 . This data facilitates a thorough understanding of typical transit patterns within that time frame. Notably, the 71st Macau Grand Prix took place during the third week of November 2024, specifically from November 11 to November 18. The dataset corresponding to the Grand Prix season was meticulously gathered over the course of this one-week period. This concentrated data collection is essential for analyzing the impact of high-profile events on public transportation. By comparing the datasets from both the off season and the Grand Prix, researchers can derive meaningful insights regarding fluctuations in passenger volumes and traffic conditions, ultimately aiding in the optimization of transport strategies during significant events and ensuring better service delivery for commuters. Data for the five selected bus routes across both Grand Prix and normal seasons were not acquired concurrently due to the crawling system for this research being restricted to a maximum of six routes per day (see Table  ). To mitigate potential annual seasonal dependencies with the Grand Prix period, the dataset for normal period was collected during comparable days. However, the computational capacity of the available data crawling system limited daily collection from the DSAT database to a maximum of five bus routes. Consequently, this research was constrained to collecting data for only five bus routes which slightly exceeded the optimal system performance. As a result, certain dates are absent in Table  (e.g., October 20 for bus route 26 during normal period, November 17 for bus route 15 during Grand Prix period, among others) because the system experienced crashes on these specific dates. Regarding the data imbalance, the text explicitly acknowledges that the Grand Prix season dataset is significantly smaller than the normal season dataset. This disparity is attributed to the inherent nature of the Grand Prix event, which occurs for a limited duration annually. While acknowledging the imbalance, the paper notes that future data collection efforts aim to accumulate more Grand Prix data over several years to mitigate this issue. 
A detailed overview of the data descriptions of the collected features is provided in Table  . Additionally, a supplementary document is available on the GitHub repository, accompanying the dataset for further reference and insights. 
A dataset comprising five selected bus routes was compiled from the 74 routes operational in Macao. These specific routes were carefully chosen based on the five years of experience in Macao held by the research team members, aiming to ensure a representative sample for the data collection process. Notably, some of these routes exhibited differences in their route paths between the Grand Prix season and the off season; for instance, routes 3, 73, and AP1 observed variations, while other routes such as 15 and 26 maintained consistent route paths across both seasons . During the Grand Prix days, buses often follow distinct routes compared to their regular schedules, and these changes typically persist until after the races conclude around 6 PM. Data collected from the DSAT database encompasses several key features, including the starting station startStation, the arriving station endStation, the timestamp of arrival, the day of the week (from Monday to Sunday), passenger flow, current traffic conditions, the bus’s license plate number, and the arrival duration between stations. This comprehensive set of data provides valuable insights into the operational dynamics of the bus network, particularly during significant events that alter usual transit patterns.
The bus routes selected for analysis include 3, 15, 26, 73, and AP1, with a total of over 217,000 bus arrival time samples collected. This dataset encompasses both the normal season, which contains around 179,000 samples, and the Grand Prix season with approximately 38,000 samples, all compiled after removing outliers (see Table  ). Limitations and unintended biases are affecting the dataset utility for other users include its collection over specific, limited time frames for both normal and Grand Prix seasons, the selection of only five out of 74 available bus routes, and the removal of outliers. The focus on the unique context of the Macao Grand Prix may also limit the direct generalizability of findings to other urban transportation systems or events. Data collection occurred over specific, limited periods for Grand Prix season potentially not capturing the full spectrum of annual variations in traffic and passenger behavior outside these windows. The selected bus routes for analysis, rather than the entire network, could introduce bias if the chosen routes are not fully representative. The methodology for outlier removal, while intended to clean the data, might inadvertently exclude valuable information or skew statistical distributions. The significant imbalance in data volume between the normal and Grand Prix seasons might be potentially affecting comparative analyses and the robustness of models trained on this data. These elements warrant careful consideration by users utilizing the dataset for purposes beyond the scope of the original study. 

## Technical Validation
The input to the data processing pipeline is a set of query results from the DSAT. As the trip updates from the government open data contain short-term forecasts of arrival and departure times, the quality of the data processing pipeline output depends on both the quality of these forecasts as well as the accuracy of the data crawling. To ensure the quality of the forecasts as inputs to the data collection, the data processing output is manually checked against in-person observations of bus arrival times. Bus arrivals were observed at multiple bus stops as daily life activities along with the Macao bus application. For technical validation, two observers monitored the arrival times of bus route 3 for approximately 30 minutes at bus station M70 onsite. Predicted arrival times from the Macao bus application were compared against actual arrival times recorded at the station for each bus route 3. The observed average arrival duration at M70 from the preceding station M161 was 22 seconds, which corresponds to approximately a 0.36σ level when compared to the standard deviation derived from the dataset . It is noted that technical validation through onsite observation faced significant limitations due to constraints in human resources. Despite these limitations, onsite validation represented the sole available method for verifying the collected data accuracy.
