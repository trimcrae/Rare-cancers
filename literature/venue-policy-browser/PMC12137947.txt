## Introduction
The world population is aging rapidly. China has now entered a “moderate aging” society. Physical Activity (PA) is defined by Caspersen as any bodily movement produced by skeletal muscles that results in energy expenditure . As age increases, the physiological functions of the older adult decline, chronic diseases increase, and physical activity decreases, which pose a threat to their physical and mental health. Appropriate physical activity has a positive effect on physical and mental health of the older adults and can effectively improve or maintain their health level .
King and colleagues reported only 7.8% of older adults meeting the PA guidelines . In U.S. and Swedish population studies employing objective activity measurement, fewer than 5% of adults over age 65 met physical activity recommendations , . Some studies have found that 72.4% of the older adults stated that they engaged in ≥ 5 h of physical activity each week , 56.8% of the older adults were meeting the recommended physical activity level . There is little evidence about the overall physical activity level of the older adults in China. Nearly 80% of the older adults in Qinhuai District, Nanjing, China did not reach the physical activity required to maintain health . It can be seen that the total physical activity of the older adults is insufficient. How to make up for physical inactivity by increasing leisure-time physical activity (LTPA) has become a trending topic in the world . In a survey conducted in 15 provinces of China in 2015, it was found that the participation rate of leisure-time physical activities of the older adults aged 65 and over was low (6.6%) . A large number (67.3%) of the older adults in China are engaged in household activities (such as shopping, picking up and dropping off children, etc.) . It is impossible to comprehensively evaluate the total physical activity level of the older adults only by specific types of physical activity. Indeed, it is the total amount of activity that is deemed to affect health . Therefore, it is necessary to pay more attention to the total physical activity level of the older adults in China and what factors affect it.
In order to promote the physical activity (PA) of older adults, it is necessary to understand the related factors of PA. Previous researchers guided by the social ecological model to study that older adult individual having complex interrelationships among the intrapersonal, social and physical environment of a neighborhood , . The intrapersonal factors related to physical activity included gender, age, socioeconomic status, education level, marital status, chronic diseases, obesity . The existing articles that focus on the relationship between the physical environment and total PA , , and specific areas of physical activities—recreational PA , – , recreational walking and transportation walking , , – in older adults mainly involve in North-America, Europe, Australia , with less studies conducted in Asian countries such as Japan, China. Although researches examining the relation among physical activity and neighborhood physical environment were carried out in many countries, empirical evidence of the influence of several aspects of the neighborhood built environment on physical activity among older adults is unclear and inconsistent , . This may be explained by variations in the environmental measures using objective measures, perceived measures or both, study populations , or perception of the environment may vary with specific countries, regions and cultural backgrounds. There is a need for empirical research to examine the influence of community physical environment using objective and perceived measures on older adults to better understand the relation between community environment and older adult physical activity .
The Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) and the Health Belief Model (HBM) are two commonly used theoretical models to learn about beliefs influencing health-related behaviors, including physical activity . In the TPB, attitudes, subjective norms and perceived behavioral control are three main constructs that could determine intentions to health-related behaviors . In the HBM, perceived susceptibility and perceived severity, perceived benefits and barriers, self-efficacy are main components affecting health-related behaviors . Social cognitive psychological factors such as perceived barriers and self-efficacy have also been proved to be related with physical activity. Carlson et al. found that walkability, social support and self-efficacy were associated with total MVPA min/week . The factors influencing the physical activity of pregnant women included perception obstacles, action clues, exercise self-efficacy . The linear regression model revealed a significant relationship of physical activity among (pre)frail older adults with descriptive norms, perceived barriers and attitudes . This study found that intention did not correlate with the amount of PA undertaken . Attitude and subjective norm were the strongest constructs associated with PA levels . Intention is an important determinant of future PA behavior . However, this study found that intention did not correlate with the amount of PA undertaken . The association between intention and physical activity levels of older adults in China remains unknown. Previous studies have investigated the physical activity of older adults based on TPB or HBM, but none of studies has used the integrated model of TPB and HBM to explore the association between these psychological variables and the physical activity intention or behavior of older adults in China. The integrated application of the TPB and HBM has been proved to more elucidate the beliefs related the health-related behavior, such as physical activity intention among (pre)frail older adults , helmet use among electric bikers . We believed that the integration of TPB and HBM would better determine the socio-cognitive associations with intention to undertake physical activity among older adults. In order to make up for this gap, this study aimed to determine the social-cognitive psychological factors based on TPB theory and HBM model associations with the intention and level to undertake physical activity (PA) among Chinese older adults.
Previous studies on physical activity levels mainly referred to the recommended standards of WHO , using the weekly and daily minutes of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity (MVPA) , , weekly and daily minutes of light-to-vigorous physical activity (LVPA) , meeting physical activity requirements (≥ 5 h per week) as the dependent variable. However, this study found that over 70% of the older adults almost never participate in moderate-intensity or above leisure physical activities and the older adults tend to engage in low-intensity physical activities in their daily lives , which has been seldom considered in previous studies when quantifying the total physical activity level. Although the method of objectively measuring physical activity based on accelerometers , has high accuracy, its testing cost is high, while the classical scale survey method has the advantages of saving time and convenience , . PASE scale is a classic scale to test the physical activity of the older adults, and its reliability and validity have been verified by the older adults in different countries .
From the above analysis, it is considered necessary to consider several different levels of factors, including individual characteristics (demographic variables, health-related status, travel characteristics), subjective and objective community environment factors and social cognitive psychological factors of the older adults, especially the newly included variables such as whether the residential building had an elevator, community walkability, and psychological perception variables. The broader study focused on several research questions :

Are there significant differences in cognitive psychological factors among older adults with different physical activity levels?
What is the relationship between subjective and objective neighborhood physical environmental features and physical activity in older adults? Neighborhood walkability was not significantly associated with physical activity among older adults in Canada and United States . So is the community walkability associated with total physical activity of Chinese seniors?
What is the relationship between cognitive psychological factors and physical activity level among older adults?

This study would contribute to a deeper understanding of the complex mechanisms underlying the older adults’ physical activity level and provide more comprehensive references for policy makers. This would be of great significance for improving the physical activity level of the older adults and enhancing/maintaining their health.

## Methods and data

## Sample

## Survey region
China has now entered a moderately aging society. By 2023, the number of older people over 60 years old in China has reached 296 million, accounting for 21.1% of the total population in the country. At the end of 2023, the urbanization rate of China’s resident population was 66.16%, which means that two thirds of Chinese people live in urban areas. The resident population of megacities, super cities, Type I large cities, and Type II large cities accounts for about 54% of the total population in China. Based on the resident population size of urban areas, urbanization rate, and aging rate, three cities selected in this study included megacity (first tier cities in the world)—Beijing (B), super city (regional central city)—Qingdao (Q), and Type I Large City (coastal open city)—Yantai (Y) (see Table  ).

 

## Respondents
People aged ≥ 60 years old are called the older adults in China. Referring to the retirement age of Chinese residents, the subjects selected in this study must meet the following conditions: (1) they are under 60 years old but have retired, or over 60 years old; (2) Living in the community for more than 6 months; (3) They can participate in physical activities independently.

## The sampling procedure
This study is divided into two stages: pilot investigation and formal investigation.
In the pilot investigation stage: Firstly, we conducted face-to-face communication with some older respondents in the form of interviews to understand their needs for using community service facilities, as well as the common difficulties and perceived obstacles they face during their community daily trips. Through sorting and summarizing, we formed questionnaire survey questions; Then, several representative respondents were selected for a pilot investigation, and the questionnaire was tested and adjusted to ensure its validity and credibility.
Formal investigation stage: A questionnaire survey was conducted through a combination of online and offline methods from March 2023 to March 2024. This study first divides residential communities into two types based on unified standards: old and new communities. Communities built before 2000 are called old communities, while those built after 2000 are called new communities. The number of residential communities surveyed offline was based on a ratio of 1:2 between old and new communities. A total of 63 residential communities were selected, with a sample size of 5–20 per community. The sample for the online survey was randomly obtained from urban older people who were distributed questionnaires through channels such as WJX and WeChat.

## The sampling size
Boomsma suggests: “When using maximum likelihood method to estimate structural equation models, the sample size should not be less than 200 . The sample size for structural equation modeling (SEM) needs to be 5–10 times that of the observed variables . This study obtained a total of 529 valid questionnaires, meeting the sample size requirements for structural equation modeling estimation methods in the field of behavior.

## Measures

## Demographics
On the basis of summarizing the previous literatures that affect the older adults’ physical activity level, demographic variables (age, gender, income, education level, residence or marital status, health status (number of chronic diseases, Body Mass Index (BMI), self-rated health status) were collected by survey. Living time in the community and living conditions were also included in this study. The collected data also included the duration of a single trip, the frequency of daily trips, auxiliary tools, the PA scope (inside/outside the community) and the number of years of PA.

## Psychosocial factor measures
The integration of Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) and Health Belief Model (HBM) was validated, and it has been widely applied to explore the influence path of consumption behavior , , driving intention , using intention , beliefs of PA , behavior intervention. Based on TPB and HBM, this study examined the relationship of perceptual psychological variables with older adults’ PAL.
In this study, subjective norm (SN) refers to the influence of society and people around (such as family members, sick relatives or friends, doctors, neighbors) on the older adults’ physical activities. Perceived benefits in HBM are similar to (positive) behavioral attitude (ATT) in TPB. Perceived Benefit (PBE) refers to the benefits that the older individuals get from physical activities, such as relieving pain and getting spiritual satisfaction. Perceived behavioral control (PBC) refers to the cognition that an individual can complete a specific behavior according to the resources and opportunities he/she has mastered and the expected obstacles, as well as the accessibility and obstacles of available resources, which can be divided into personal factors, self-efficacy and external resources. In this study, PBC was defined as the influence of personal factors such as physical health, income and free time on the older adults’ physical activities. External factors refer to the factors that the older adults think may obstruct their participation in physical activities, including weather conditions, venue conditions, activity facilities, convenience of traveling in community, etc. , and thus introduced two variables: perceived barriers to facilities (PBAF) and perceived barriers in travel (PBAT). Perceived barriers to facilities (PBAF) refers to the obstacles perceived by the older adults when they use service facilities during PA. Perceived barriers in travel (PBAT) refer to the obstacles perceived by the older adults when they travel from home to the activity destination. Self-efficacy (SE) refers to the confidence to individual ability in the process of executing a certain behavior. Perceived severity (PSE) refers to an individual’s awareness of the severity of the consequences caused by diseases, such as shortened lifespan and inconvenience in daily life. Perceived susceptibility (PSU) refers to the probability that the older individuals think they are susceptible to chronic diseases.
The specific observation items of latent variables were shown in Appendix Table 1. Participants’ were asked about their level of agreement (1 = strongly disagree, 2 = somewhat disagree, 3 = neutral (neither agree nor disagree), 4 = somewhat agree, 5 = strongly agree) in response to the all measured indicators.

## Objective environment measures
The community physical environment factors associated with the older adults’ physical activities in the previous research are usually composed of density , , , , , diversity , , , design (including path quality , , , road network linear density , intersection density , , , , , density , , or amounts of facilities , , , , greening rate , ,plot ratio ), the distance to transit , , , , , destination accessibility , , , and distance to city center.
Walk Score was widely used in the measurement of walkability in European and American countries. Walk Score reflects the daily facilities configuration and the accessibility level of walking space within a certain range around the starting point. About half of the older adults travel within 10 min (about 500 m). A single point Walk Score for each participant based on a 500-meter buffer around his/her home was created . Walk Score measured by ArcGIS contains design and destination accessibility. Therefore, this study mainly considers several variables such as community type (old or new), whether there are elevators in residential buildings, walk score, population density from the objective measurement of community environment. Walk Score is normalized to 0-100. Walk score was divided into two categories: Car-dependent community with poor Walk Score < 40 means that most of daily trips rely on a car, and walkable community with good Walk Score ≥ 40 means that most daily trips rely on walking.

## Perceived environment measures
Neighborhood Environment Walkability Scale (NEWS) is designed to subjectively measure residents’ perception of the walking environmental attributes of their local area, and it is widely used , . The questionnaire assessed the following environmental characteristics: aesthetics , , – , – , pedestrian traffic safety and crime safety , – , – , residential density , , , land use mix–diversity , , , street connectivity , – , ease of access to infrastructure , – , – . These items instrument measured perceived attributes of neighborhood environment hypothesized to be related to physical activity . Scholars have translated and revised the NEWS scale to make it suitable for local residents (such as Chinese seniors , adults and urban community residents ). This study will adopt the Chinese Walkable Environment Scale for urban community residents revised on the basis of Neighborhood Environment Walkability Scale-Abbreviated (NEWS-A) , which subjectively measuring Chinese older adults’ perception of the community walking environmental attributes (see Appendix Table 2).
The scale includes the following aspects: ease of access (convenience) of supporting service facilities, road condition and aesthetics, traffic and safety. Participants were asked about their level of agreement (strongly disagree, somewhat disagree, neutral (neither agree nor disagree), somewhat agree, or strongly agree) in response to the questions listed in the scale. Each item is graded from 1 to 5. The higher the score, the better the walking environment around the community , .

## Physical activity level
Physical Activity Scale for Elderly (PASE) is an instrument that measures the level of physical activity for individuals aged 65 and older. It is considered that PASE scale can effectively and objectively measure the physical activity of the older adults in China . The PASE is comprised of self-reported occupational, household and leisure items over a one-week period.
PASE scores are calculated from weights and frequency values for each of activities . The total score of PASE is between 0 and 360. The higher the PASE score, the higher the physical activity level . The PASE scores of the respondents were ranked from low to high, and the valid samples were equally divided into three groups , that is, there were 176 people in the low physical activity level group, 177 people in the medium physical activity level group and 176 people in the high physical activity level group.

## Hypotheses

## Hypotheses about PAL
Based on the social ecological model, this study assumed that the physical activity level of the older adults would be related to socio-economic attributes (H1a), health status (H1b), living conditions (H2), activity-travel characteristics (H3), objective (H4a) and subjective (H4b) community environment. According to the TPB, intention was an important determinant of behavior, and perceived behavioral control can be a direct predictor of behavior . It was assumed that the older adults’ physical activity intention (PAI) was positively related to the physical activity level (PAL) (H5a). It was also assumed that there was a positive correlation between perceived behavioral control and older adults’ physical activity level (H5b). Therefore, the model framework of PAL among the older adults was shown in Fig.  .

 

## Hypotheses about PAI
Behavioral intention can be explained by three social-cognitive determinants - attitude, subjective norm, and perceived behavioral control . When the more the older adults were influenced by family members, sick relatives/friends, doctors, neighbors, society and policies, the more they agreed with the statement of the physical benefits brought by participating in physical activities, the more they were supported by personal factors(such as income, health status, time) and the more confident they were able to participate in physical activities, the higher their intention to participate in physical activities would be; The older adults were afraid of the serious consequences of suffering from chronic diseases, and if the older adults encountered more obstacles in the process of undertaking physical activity and daily trips, their intention to engage in physical activity could be weaken. the following hypotheses H6a ~ H6g are proposed:
Subjective norm (H6a), activity attitude/perceived benefits (H6b), perceived behavioral control (H6c), self-efficacy (H6d), perceived susceptibility (H6e), perceived barriers to facilities (H6f), perceived barriers in travel (H6g), and perceived severity (H6h) are significantly associated with the older adults’ physical activity intention (PAI).
Bamberg et al. found that subjective norm had positively effect on attitude , and Cai Yunhao et al. found that subjective norm have a positive and significant impact on the driving attitude of the older adults . It was found that perceptual behavior control had a positive effect on attitude , while Perceptual barriers was negatively associated with attitude , . Therefore, the following hypotheses are proposed:
Subjective norm (H7a), perceived behavioral control (H7b), perceived susceptibility (H7c), perceived severity (H7d) are positively related to the older adults’ attitude towards PA. Perceived barriers to facilities (H7e), perceived barriers in travel (H7f) are negatively related to the older adults’ attitude towards PA. There was a significant correlation between SN and PBC, PBAF, PBAT, and PBAF is correlated with PBAT (H7g).
Through the warning of sick older people around them, listening to the advice of doctors and other professionals, the older adults would realize that they were prone to chronic diseases and the serious consequences if they get sick. The people who were prone to suffering from chronic diseases would be more aware of the serious consequences they bring to individuals and families. Therefore, the following hypotheses are proposed:

## H8a
Subjective Norm is associated with Perceived Susceptibility;

## H8b
Perceived Susceptibility is associated with Perceived Severity;

## H8c
Subjective Norm is associated with Perceived Severity;
The greater the support of the people around them, society and policies, the more they agreed with the statement that participation in physical activities brought physical benefits, the higher the support of personal factors (economy, time, health, etc.), the more they thought that they were prone to suffering from chronic diseases and the more they were afraid of the serious consequences caused by illness, the more confident they would be able to participate in activities. If the older adults encounter more obstacles in physical activity and daily trips, their perception of self-efficacy would weaken. From this, we put forward the following assumptions:
Subjective norm (H9a), attitude (H9b), perceived behavioral control (H9c), perceived susceptibility (H9d), perceived severity (H9e), perceived barriers to facilities (H9f) and perceived barriers to travel (H9g) are associated with self-efficacy.

## Method
The research includes the analysis process of hypothesis-modeling-testing. Statistical analysis was calculated using SPSS 19.0. The Goodman and Kruskal tau-y coefficient was used to measure the asymmetric correlation between two categorical variables, which was also applicable to measuring the correlation between a categorical variable and an ordinal variable. The asymmetric correlation between two ordinal variables was measured using the dy coefficient. The difference of physical activity level among the old people with different attributes was tested by Pearson Chi-Square, and the difference of psychological perception among the older adults with different physical activity levels was analyzed by one-way ANOVA. The test level is α = 0.05.
The SEM model constructed using Amos 17.0 was used to examined the relationship of latent variables including perception of community environment and cognitive psychological factors with PAI and PAL among older adults. On this basis, the observable variables such as demographic variables, health status, travel characteristics and objective community environmental factors such as Walk Score, population density, were further combined with the latent variables in the SEM model, and the relationship between these variables and physical activity level was examined by using SEM-Logit model.

## Reliability and validity
The value of Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin (KMO) and Bartlett’s Test of Sphericity were employed to examine whether the data were suitable for the factor analysis . The KMO of social cognitive psychological variables in model (a) and perceived community walking environment elements in model (b) were more than 0.8, which the data were suitable for the factor analysis. Exploratory factor analysis (EFA) were used to examine the construct validity of this instrument. Some items including SN4, SN5, SN6, ATT4, PBE4, PBAT1, PBAT2, CSF3 had a factor loading less than 0.50 in the first EFA. The final retained items can be found in Appendix 1. The reliability and validity test results of each latent variable were shown in Table  . All items had a factor loading more than 0.50, which suggested that observed variables sufficiently represented the latent variables . Cronbach’s α > 0.60. Composite reliability (CR) > 0.60, the average variance extracted (AVE) > 0.50 or AVE > 0.40 and CR > 0.60 can be meted simultaneously, which presented adequate convergent validity .

## Model result analysis
As can be seen from the results of path analysis,Subjective norm (β = 0.32), perceived benefits/attitudes (β = 0.27), perceived behavioral control (β = 0.13) and self-efficacy (β = 0.27) had significant positive effects on the older adults’ physical activity intention. Perceived severity (β=- 0.12) and perceived barriers in travel (β=- 0.10) had significant negative effects on the older adults’ physical activity intention.
Perceived barriers to facilities had no direct effect on the PAI, but indirectly affect the older adults’ physical activity intention through perceived benefits/attitudes (β=- 0.18) and self-efficacy (β=- 0.19).
Perceptual susceptibility has no direct effect on the older adults’ activity intention, but indirectly affects older adults’ physical activity intention through perceived benefits/attitudes (β = 0.30), perceived severity (β = 0.29) and self-efficacy (β = 0.27).
The physical activity intention (β = 0.23) had a direct positive impact on the older adults’ physical activity level. However, perceived behavioral control, subjective norm, perceived benefits/attitudes, self-efficacy, perceived susceptibility, perceived severity, perceived barriers to facilities and perceived barriers in travel had no direct effect on the older adults’ physical activity level, but can indirectly affect the physical activity level by influencing the physical activity intention.
The perception of community walking environment factors also plays an important role in the older adults’ physical activity level, and one of the variables, namely the perceived road condition (PRC), had a significant negative impact on the older adults’ physical activity level (β=- 0.10).

The effect of each latent variable on the older adults’ physical activity intention includes direct effect and indirect effect, as shown in Table  . The results showed that self-efficacy (SE) and perceived severity (PSE) only had a direct impact on the older adults’ physical activity intention (PAI), while perceived barriers to facilities (PBAF) and perceived susceptibility (PSU) only had an indirect impact on the older adults’ physical activity intention (PAI), while subjective norms (SN), activity attitudes (ATT) / perceived benefits (PBE), perceived behavioral control (PBC) and perceived barriers in travel (PBAT) had both direct and indirect effects on the older adults’ physical activity intention (PAI).

 

The effect of each latent variable on the older adults’ physical activity level includes direct effect and indirect effect, as shown in Table  . The results showed that the physical activity intention (PAI) and perceived road condition (PRC) had only direct effects on the older adults’ physical activity level (PAL). The other perceived variables had only indirect effects on the older adults’ physical activity level.
From the total effect, it can be seen that perceived barriers to facilities (PBAF), perceived barriers in travel (PBAT) and perceived road conditions (PRC) all had negative effects on the physical activity level (PAL). Overall, physical activity intention (PAI) and subjective norm (SN) were the strongest constructs positively associated with physical activity level (PAL).

 

## SEM-Logit model
Quantitative perceptual psychological variables were obtained through the results of the SEM model mentioned above. Combining with socio-economic attributes, living conditions, health status, travel characteristics and objective community environment measurements, SEM-logit model was constructed to analyze the relationship between these independent variables and physical activity level (PAL). Odd ratio (OR) and 95% confidence intervals [95% CIs] were used to evaluate the association between the dependent variable, physical activity level, and independent variables .
Based on the estimation results of the model parameters (see Table  ), it can be seen that gender, age, self-rated health status, living conditions, with/without elevator, PA scope, PA time, Walk Score, perceived road condition (PRC) and physical activity intention (PAI) had significant effects on the older adults’ physical activity level.

 

## Relation between demographic variables and physical activity level (PAL)
Socio-demographic and health related variables were also associated with the older adults’ physical activity level (PAL), and in the expected direction . Significant associations were observed for gender, such that male respondents (vs. female: OR [95% CIs] = 0.44 [0.31–0.62]; p < 0.000) were less likely to improve the physical activity level. The probability that the PAL of older men increasing one unit was 0.44 times that of older women. Significant associations were observed for age, such that respondents ages 60–64 years (vs. ≥75 year olds: OR [95%CIs] = 11.69 [6.05 –2.61]; p < 0.000), were more likely to improve the physical activity level. The probability that the PAL of the older adults aged 60–64, 65–69 and 70–74 increasing 1 unit was 11.69 times, 9.55 times and 4.61 times that of the older adults aged ≥ 75 years old, respectively. Similarly, self-rated health as poor (vs. good) was found to have a negative association with the older adults’ physical activity level (PAL) (OR[95%CIs] = 0.64 [0.41–0.99]; p < 0.05). Individuals who living with partner (vs. with others: OR [95% CIs] = 1.50 [1.03– 2.17]; p < 0.05) were more likely to improve the physical activity level.

## Relation between activity-travel characteristics and physical activity level (PAL)
Not going outside the community (vs. going outside the community) was found to have a negative association with the older adults’ physical activity level (OR[95%CIs] = 0.53 [0.37 –0.78]; p < 0.01). After controlling for all other independent variables in the model , individuals who stated that physical activity lasts less than 5 year (last < 1 year: OR [95%CIs] = 0.34 [0.19–0.61]; p < 0.01), were less likely to improve the physical activity level compared to individuals who stated that physical activity lasts more than 5 years (last 1–5 years: OR [95%CIs] = 0.43 [0.29–0.63]; p < 0.01).

## Relation between community environment and physical activity level (PAL)
With regards to objective community environment features, two variables were found to be negatively associated with physical activity level after controlling for all other independent variables. Without elevator in residential buildings (vs. with) was found to have a negative association with the older adults’ physical activity level (PAL) (OR[95%CIs] = 0.62 [0.44 ~ 0.92]; p < 0.05). Specifically, the probability of PAL increase one unit for the older adults without elevator in residential buildings was 0.44 times that of the older adults with elevator. Community Walk Score as poor (vs. good) was found to have a negative association with the older adults’ physical activity level (OR[95%CIs] = 0.38 [0.15–0.97]; p < 0.05).
With regards to subjective community walking environment features, one variable, perceived road condition (PRC) (OR[95%CIs] = 0.80 [0.66 ~ 0.96]; p < 0.05), was found to be negatively associated with physical activity level after controlling for all other independent variables. It shows that the older adults’ perception of the road condition around the community increasing one unit, and the probability of the older adults’ physical activity level was improved by one level was 80% of the quondam.

## Relation between psychosocial variables and physical activity level (PAL)
With regards to psychosocial factors, after controlling for all other independent variables in the model, one variable, the older adults’ physical activity intention (PAI) (OR[95%CIs] = 1.26 [1.01–1.58], was found to be positively associated with physical activity level (PAL); p < 0.05). When the older adults’ physical activity intention increasing one unit, the possibility of the older adults’ physical activity level being improved by one level was 1.26 times that of the original.

## Discussion and conclusion

## Discussion
This study constructed an integrated model of TPB&HBM, and used exploratory factor analysis (EFA) to determine the socio-cognitive associations with intention (and level) to undertake physical activity among community dwelling Chinese older adults. The integrated model has good reliability and validity, so the TPB&HBM integration model can effectively explain the older adults’ physical activity. It was found that the older adults’ intention to undertake physical activity was positively correlated with their physical activity level in our study, which highlights the good translation of behavioral intention into behavior . Different from our findings, prior research among (pre)frail older adults in China and Chinese female college students with vulnerable conditions , intention was not associated with physical activity . The weakened intention-behavior relationship might be because vulnerable or frail individuals encountered physical difficulties in exerting their intentions . Another study among community dwelling British South Asian adults aged 60 and over also failed found the association. It remains to be determined if intention is a significant determinant of behavior for this population group in different countries . Nonetheless, this study still provides an important contribution to our understanding of the relationship between Chinese older adults’ intention and level of undertaking physical activity . Our study also found that perceived behavioral control (PBC) was not associated with physical activity level, although PBC associated with physical activity intention. This indicates that perceived behavioral control (PBC) play distinct roles in the different stages of physical activity among Chinese older adults . We found that the perceived severity (PSE) was negatively correlated with the older adults’ physical activity intention. Different from our finding, the research on the (pre)frail older adults found that the perceived physical threat was positively related to the intention of physical activity . We also found that perceived barriers in travel (PBAT) was negatively correlated with the older adults’ physical activity intention. Individuals’ perception of risks of negative consequences and barriers encountered in the process of daily trips may prompt the older adults to reduce their opportunities and initiative to participate in physical activities in order to reduce risks or barriers. In line with prior research , attitude and subjective norm were related to intention to undertake PA.
In this study, the SEM-logit model was further constructed to explore the relationship between individual socio-economic attributes, psychological perception variables based on TPB and HBM, objective and subjective community environment characteristics and older adults’ physical activity level. The following findings were mainly discussed around social-economic characteristics and subjective and objective community environment.
The study found that physical activity level among Chinese older adults was related to gender, age, living conditions, self-rated health, going outside the community. Older women had a higher level of physical activity. With the increase of age, the possibility of improving the older adults’ physical activity level were less likely. The possible explanation for this phenomenon is that with the increase of age, the physiological functions of the older adults continue to decline and the activity ability further declines . The study found that good health had a positive role in promoting the leisure physical activities of the older adults , it was found that the physical activity level of the older adults who self-rated health as good was higher. This phenomenon may be related to the fact that older adults with chronic diseases were often constrained by their physical conditions and it was difficult to maintain regular physical activities for them . One important finding was that the older adults with elevators in their residential buildings have a high level of physical activity. From this, we initially think that the installation of elevators in residential buildings may be of great significance to improve the physical activity level of the Chinese urban older adults.
We also found that half of the older adults participate in physical activities within 500 m from home. Based on the community walking score of the 500-meter buffer around the home, it was found that the older adults living in walkable communities had a higher level of physical activity. Different from our findings, neighborhood walkability was not significantly associated with meeting physical activity requirements . Likewise, older people who can go outside the community have a higher level of physical activity. In line with prior research , we also found that there was no significant correlation between population density, intersection density and the older adults’ physical activity level, which may be related to the dense community population and road network and the large number of intersections in China. In this study, it was found that the increase of population density was helpful for the older adults to participate in leisure physical activities, and the intersection density and greening rate had negative effects on the physical activities of the older adults .
For the subjective community walking environmental factors, we found that the perceived road conditions had a significant negative impact on the older adults’ physical activity level. In line with prior research , , the convenience of supporting living facilities, traffic conditions, aesthetics and perceived public safety were not related to the older adults’ physical activity level, which may be related to the fact that the older adults at different physical activity levels had no obvious differences in perceived community environmental factors. Perceived neighborhood environment was related with walking for specific purposes , , it is found that aesthetics, street connectivity and accessibility are the main built environment factors that affect the leisure walking level of the older adults – , neighborhood aesthetics are associated with adults’ recreational walking . Built environmental factors (e.g., sidewalks, crosswalks, crossing lights, benches, lighting) are important predictors of physical activity, especially for older adults whose daily activities often contract to more immediate surroundings (e.g., home environment, neighborhood) .
Social support and self-efficacy were associated with physical activity . According to the research results from SEM, we found that social cognitive psychological variables based on TPB and HBM models indirectly were related to physical activity level (PAL) through physical activity intention (PAI). Overall, perceived barriers to facilities (PBAF) (e.g., exercise venue, benches, public toilet), perceived barriers in travel (PBAT) (e.g., disorderly parked vehicles, steps) and perceived road condition (PRC) (e.g., lighting, smoothness of sidewalks and crosswalks, greening rate, cleanliness) all had negative effects on the older adults’ physical activity level (PAL). Physical activity intention (PAI) and subjective norm (SN) were the strongest constructs positively associated with the older adults’ physical activity level (PAL).

## Conclusions
Based on the integrated model of TPB and HBM, this study explored the relationship between psychological variables and older adults’ physical activity intention and level. The integration model has been proved to be effective and reliable. Based on SEM-logit model, this study comprehensively considered comprehensively considers the subjective and objective community environment, individual socio-economic attributes and social cognitive psychological variables at different levels, and determined the relationship between these factors and the older adults’ physical activity level. This would contribute to understanding the complex influencing mechanism underlying the physical activity level among the older adults.

## Practical implications
This study found that older adults’ physical activity level was closely related to multiple levels of factors, including socioeconomic attributes, living conditions, health status, activity travel characteristics, subjective and objective community environmental factors, and social cognitive psychological factors based on TPB and HBM, which recognizes the complexity of older adults’ physical activity. Older adults living in a walkable community are more likely to improve their physical activity level. A walkable community environment with supportive infrastructure, such as flat and clean roads, various service facilities, can play an enabling role in mobility and physical activity in older adults. The psychological perception of physical activity among the older adults, such as perceived benefits/attitudes, self-efficacy, perceived severity, and perceived barriers in travel, was indirectly related to their physical activity level through their intention to undertake physical activity. The findings can also provide reference opinions for urban planners and health managers. Therefore, relevant measurements or implementing policies proposed from the aspects of transforming the community travel environment and guiding social cognitive psychology of older adults, which can provide reference for maintaining or improving their physical activity level and health.
In China, efforts are being made to comprehensively promote the renovation of old urban communities, with a focus on renovating those built before the end of 2000. Based on the findings in this study, the following measures have been proposed in terms of community hardware facilities: (1) Residential buildings in old communities would be equipped with elevators to increase opportunities for outdoor activities for older adults; (2) Based on the usage needs of older adults for community service facilities (CSF) such as leisure chairs, public toilets, and activity areas, improve the configuration of CSF and reduce the perception barriers to facilities (PBAF) among older adults; (3) By improving the community walking environment, such as checking ramps and road conditions, the perceived barriers in travel would be reduced; and (4) creating a walkable community enables the older adults to reach the destinations of various activities within a suitable walking distance, which increases the opportunities of travel for the older adults and helps to improve their physical activity level.
On the individual psychological level, experts who are familiar with psychology, kinematics, medicine and other fields help to improve the older adults’ awareness of health beliefs, make the older adults participate in physical activities suitable for themselves, and meet the needs of the older adults for disease prevention and treatment, strengthen body, enjoy life, social interaction, and so on.

## Limitations
Some limitations should be mentioned in our study. First, all the participants were randomly surveyed in large cities in China, which precluded the generalizability to other populations, especially those from urban areas, especially those from small cities, and rural areas. Considering the tendency of population’s aging and the extension of life expectancy, Further studies recruiting a wider range of older adults with larger sample sizes are recommended. In China, there are many older adults living in old communities, and future research can focus on old communities. In addition, the measurement of the older adults’ physical activity level in this study was based on the self-reported PASE scale, and the older adults may have some deviations in recalling their activities in the past week. The data reflecting physical activity can be obtained by objective measurement method such as motion sensors, which can reflect the real level of physical activity and increase the scientific and reasonable research results. In addition, this study is limited by the cross-sectional design, and longitudinal research can be carried out in the future.
