This research presents an IoT-enabled smart safety framework that leverages machine learning (ML) to autonomously detect and respond to physiological distress and potential threats. The proposed system integrates physiological sensors (heart rate, temperature) and activity sensors (GPS, accelerometer) into an intelligent wearable device. A hybrid ML approach, primarily utilizing Support Vector Machine (SVM) and Naive Bayes (NB), is employed for robust activity recognition and stress level classification. Performance was rigorously validated using k-fold cross-validation, with the SVM classifier achieving a 99.7% average accuracy in threat detection. This AI-driven approach reduces detection latency to 3 s, while battery optimization ensures 18–20 h of continuous operation. Upon autonomous threat detection, the system uses GSM connectivity to transmit GPS coordinates to authorities. This research demonstrates a practical, high-accuracy solution for personal security, with a strong emphasis on data privacy and ethical deployment.

## Introduction
The escalating incidence of crimes targeting women and children has underscored the urgent need for innovative technological interventions capable of delivering real-time protection and rapid emergency response[cite: 84]. Conventional safety mechanisms, which often depend on manual activation or post-incident reporting, fall short in preventing harm or ensuring timely assistance during critical moments [cite: 85]. Many current solutions require explicit user activation—an impractical expectation during emergencies when victims may be incapacitated or unable to trigger an alert [cite: 89]. While the integration of Internet of Things (IoT) technologies offers transformative opportunities [cite: 86], existing applications face significant challenges, including delayed response times, a lack of continuous monitoring, and poor integration with emergency services [cite: 88].
A more fundamental gap, however, lies in the logic of threat detection. Many systems oversimplify the problem, assuming that a single physiological anomaly, such as an elevated heart rate, directly correlates with an unsafe situation. This fails to account for real-world variability—for example, the body’s natural response to exercise or daily stress—and leads to a high rate of false positives, which erodes user trust and system reliability. This research addresses this critical gap by proposing a robust framework that integrates IoT, cloud computing, and advanced machine learning (ML) to create an autonomous, context-aware safety system.
The system moves beyond simple threshold-based alerts by using a hybrid ML approach (combining SVM, Naive Bayes, and CNN classifiers) to analyze a fusion of sensor data [cite: 82]. By monitoring multiple inputs—including heartbeat monitors, temperature sensors, accelerometers, and GPS modules—the system learns personalized physiological baselines to accurately distinguish between benign stress and genuine distress scenarios [cite: 72, 74]. Recent studies have validated the potential for multimodal sensor analysis in security, confirming that this is a promising research direction. [cite: 18].

## Literature survey

## Existing methodologies
Numerous researchers have explored the application of Information and Communication Technology (ICT) to support and protect survivors of Intimate Partner Violence (IPV) . IPV is a widespread global public health crisis and a serious human rights violation, characterized by patterns of controlling, coercive, threatening, or violent behavior perpetrated by current or former intimate partners. Studies show that IPV affects approximately 30% of women globally over their lifetime, with profound consequences for both physical and mental health.
The complexity of IPV includes physical assault, psychological abuse, sexual coercion, financial manipulation, and increasingly, digital harassment. These forms of abuse often result in long-term trauma that persists beyond the immediate threat. Research has identified several contributing factors to IPV, such as childhood exposure to violence, substance abuse, financial stress, and cultural norms that reinforce gender inequality. The COVID-19 pandemic further intensified the frequency and severity of IPV incidents, emphasizing the need for comprehensive prevention strategies, improved screening in healthcare settings, and trauma-informed interventions that address both immediate safety and long-term recovery.
Technological solutions proposed in recent studies include cloud computing, home automation, artificial intelligence (AI), machine learning (ML), and other innovative approaches. This research focuses on the potential of intelligent biosensors to autonomously detect violence and trigger alerts, offering a proactive safety mechanism. Previous attempts in this domain provide valuable insights.
Hany et al. suggested monitoring electrodermal activity, electroencephalogram (EEG), and electrocardiogram (ECG) signals to detect hostility. Patel and Hassan proposed using an altimeter embedded in wearable devices for aggression detection, although their approach was limited to single-sensor input. Goodwin introduced a multi-sensor method combining cardiovascular, existential, and motion (accelerometry) data, specifically targeting individuals with Down syndrome. However, this model lacks generalizability to IPV scenarios.
While some proposals offer comprehensive frameworks , many rely on limited features and lack continuous surveillance capabilities. Kumari’s work, for instance, focuses on tracking daily activities that may include offensive behavior, but does not specifically address the needs of IPV survivors.
Self-management in IPV contexts involves continuous data collection to monitor survivor progress and detect potential threats. Advances in biometrics and IoT technologies enable 24/7 monitoring through mobile devices, capturing critical physiological data. Wu et al. demonstrated how activity monitoring systems can assess independence and functional capacity, serving as indicators of well-being.
Body Area Networks (BANs) represent another promising solution. These wearable systems, positioned near the body, track biophysical signals to detect aggressive behavior. Broens et al. first introduced BANs during asthma research, proposing 24-hour monitoring to anticipate attacks. Later enhancements incorporated data management for improved analysis . BANs typically include accelerometers, GPS modules, and communication interfaces—features now commonly found in smartphones.
Emotion analysis also plays a key role, utilizing respiration patterns and distress signals (e.g., crying, laughing) to establish physiological trends. Parameters such as volume, intensity, and pacing help identify emotional states across a spectrum from calm to stressed . Breath-based sensors have shown promise in non-invasive health monitoring and abuse detection, although their adoption remains limited despite two decades of research .
Most wearable technologies now include heart rate (HR) monitoring. Smart bands often use photoplethysmography, a technique that shines light on the skin to measure blood flow, contrasting with ECG, which tracks electrical signals. Devices like Spotify-integrated fitness trackers offer precision suitable for IPV monitoring . The medical community has long recognized the link between chronic stress and elevated heart rate, reinforcing the relevance of HR monitoring in IPV detection .
Recent studies suggest that fluctuations in glycaemia may serve as diagnostic indicators for the presence or risk of violence in individuals. Research has established correlations between glucose levels, stress-induced hormonal imbalances, and blood variables associated with hyperglycemia. Additionally, a notable relationship exists between obesity and elevated levels of blood oxaloacetate, which tend to spike during periods of acute stress .
Skin Conductance (SC) testing, which measures the skin’s electrical activity through applied sensors, has proven useful in assessing physiological responses. SC readings are commonly used in polygraph examinations to evaluate emotional arousal, with lower SC values indicating reduced excitement levels . A correlation between intoxication and SC has also been observed, as thermal conductivity changes reflect central nervous system activity. Affordable SC-based sensing devices such as the Empatica E4, Microsoft Band 2, and the eHealth Sensor Platform with My Signals have demonstrated reliable performance. Notably, both the Empatica E4 and Microsoft Band 2 support Ethernet connectivity for enhanced data transmission .
Stress also alters brain activity in the visual cortex, which can be monitored using electroencephalography (EEG). This technique involves placing electrodes at multiple scalp locations to capture electrical impulses. Although EEG provides valuable insights, its bulky and intrusive setup limits its practicality in everyday clinical or wearable applications . This use of ML for analyzing complex physiological data is a growing field. Recent studies have demonstrated the power of advanced optimization and deep learning algorithms for diagnostic purposes in medical imaging, such as oral cancer detection, which shares similar challenges of high-stakes classification . Similarly, adaptive optimization algorithms have been successfully used for feature selection in complex medical datasets, enhancing diagnostic accuracy.
Voice analysis has emerged as a critical tool in emotion and expression recognition. Several researchers have emphasized the role of vocal patterns in detecting psychological states . Technologies for vocal network monitoring have been introduced, capable of analyzing ambient sounds that convey emotions such as fear, pressure, or compassion . While keyword-triggered alarm systems exist, current research aims to develop devices that activate autonomously, without requiring user input.
Voice-enabled smart devices such as Amazon Alexa, Apple Siri HomePod, and Google Echo Dot—have gained widespread popularity. However, this study focuses on designing a portable control system tailored for IPV survivors, utilizing smartphones equipped with microphones to perform voice-based monitoring and alert functions.
Modern mobile devices are increasingly capable of evaluating clinical parameters in vulnerable populations. Embedded sensors such as accelerometers, gyroscopes, and fitness trackers allow for the continuous collection of biometric and behavioral data . While these tools are often used recreationally, their potential for targeted surveillance and health monitoring is significant.
Expression recognition, a relatively recent field in computer science, has been under active development for the past two decades . It involves analyzing complex emotional states and applying data mining techniques to create adaptive software that responds to users’ psychological needs.
The proposed smart security solution for women and children integrates wearable IoT systems composed of multiple modules, each designed to fulfill specific safety functions. These advanced devices combine sensors, communication interfaces, and intelligent software to enable real-time monitoring, alert generation, and emergency response. The system is engineered to provide continuous connectivity, rapid assistance during crises, and comprehensive surveillance of the wearer’s physiological and environmental conditions—ultimately offering peace of mind and enhanced personal security , .
Child safety was the central focus of Vidhya Prasanth et al.‘s study titled “Analysis of Child Safety Monitoring System using the SPSS Method.” This research explored the development of a smart device capable of delivering accurate, real-time location tracking to enhance child protection. The system incorporates features such as a distress alarm, SOS light, and ambient temperature sensor to assist in locating children and ensuring their well-being. Emphasis was placed on an intuitive user interface and the integration of GPS-enabled smartphones for both parents and children, enabling continuous monitoring of movement and vital health parameters including blood oxygen levels, temperature, humidity, and heart rate. The system also provides instant access to emergency services, empowering parents to make swift decisions during critical situations.
Similarly, Thummalakunta et al. proposed the “Implementation of IoT-based Real-time Women’s Safety System,” which introduces a portable device designed to enhance women’s security. Upon activation, the device emits a loud buzzer to deter potential attackers and facilitate escape. Beyond immediate protection, the system supports group messaging to a predefined network of contacts—including family members and law enforcement—during emergencies. The study underscores the importance of real-time communication and location awareness for timely intervention. By fostering community engagement and promoting collective action against social threats, this IoT-driven solution aims to transcend conventional personal safety tools and serve as a platform for empowerment and social resilience.
While both Prasanth et al. and Thummalakunta et al. highlight the functional benefits of their IoT-based safety systems, their studies offer limited insight into the security and privacy mechanisms embedded within these frameworks. Specifically, the literature does not elaborate on how sensitive personal data—such as location, health metrics, and emergency communications—is protected from unauthorized access or misuse.
Prasanth et al.‘s system places significant emphasis on user-friendly design and real-time health monitoring. However, the reliance on GPS and smartphone integration introduces potential vulnerabilities related to data privacy. The study does not detail the safeguards implemented to protect user data throughout its lifecycle—from collection and transmission to storage and access by guardians or emergency personnel.
This review complements earlier discussions on technological interventions for survivors of Intimate Partner Violence (IPV), particularly those involving biosensors and wearable devices for autonomous threat detection and alert generation. While these innovations demonstrate promising capabilities, the absence of robust privacy protocols and data protection strategies remains a critical gap in current research—one that must be addressed to ensure ethical deployment and user trust in safety technologies.
Patient-focused healthcare applications are important to patients because they offer a range of advantages that add value and improve the overall healthcare experience. The 5G networks, along with Mobile Edge Computing (MEC), can greatly transform healthcare applications, which in turn improves patient care. MEC plays an important role in the healthcare of patients by bringing computing resources to the edge of the network. It becomes part of an IoT system within healthcare that brings data closer to the core, speeds up decision-making, lowers latency, and improves the overall quality of care – .

## Research gaps
Despite notable advancements in modern safety technologies, substantial gaps persist in safeguarding survivors of Intimate Partner Violence (IPV), particularly among women and children. While many contemporary systems utilize machine learning (ML) algorithms to autonomously detect threats and initiate alerts—eliminating the need for manual activation—these solutions still fall short in delivering comprehensive, context-aware protection. Autonomous detection improves responsiveness and reduces false alarms through controlled deactivation protocols, yet broader infrastructural and collaborative efforts are essential to support diverse user needs and ensure practical deployment.
This research extends the capabilities of wearable safety devices by introducing intelligent, real-time, context-sensitive systems. However, existing literature and technological implementations often treat women’s and children’s safety as distinct domains, overlooking their interconnected vulnerabilities. Furthermore, many systems continue to rely on outdated mechanisms such as manual reporting, SMS alerts, and basic GPS tracking, which limit accuracy, reliability, and responsiveness during emergencies.

## Technical research gaps

Lack of Integrated IoT-Based Systems: Current systems lack unified architectures capable of simultaneously monitoring and analyzing the safety of both women and children in real time.
Limited Application of Machine Learning: The full potential of ML algorithms in predicting and mitigating safety risks remains underexplored.
Underutilization of Sensor Data: Existing solutions fail to effectively harness data from wearable devices, environmental sensors, and digital platforms such as social media.
Insufficient Context-Awareness: Many systems lack the ability to interpret contextual variables, which are essential for delivering personalized alerts and recommendations.
Scalability and Interoperability Challenges: Technical limitations hinder large-scale deployment and seamless integration across platforms and devices.

## Social and cultural research gaps

Lack of Cultural Sensitivity: Regional cultural and social dynamics are often overlooked, reducing the relevance and effectiveness of safety systems.
Minimal Stakeholder Engagement: Limited engagement with key stakeholders—including advocacy groups, child welfare organizations, and law enforcement—impairs system design and adoption.
Low Awareness and Education: There is a need for targeted campaigns and educational initiatives to inform users about available safety technologies and services.

## Methodological research gaps

Absence of Standardized Evaluation Metrics: A consistent framework for assessing the performance and impact of IoT-based safety systems is lacking.
Limited Use of Mixed-Methods Approaches: Few studies integrate both qualitative and quantitative approaches to provide a holistic understanding of system effectiveness.
Neglect of Ethical Considerations: Critical issues such as data privacy, security, and informed consent are often inadequately addressed in current research.

By identifying and addressing these gaps, this study contributes to the development of a robust, intelligent, and ethically responsible IoT-based safety system for women and children—powered by advanced machine learning techniques and designed for real-world deployment.

## Research challenges
The development and deployment of safety solutions for women and children using Machine Learning (ML), sensor technologies, and Internet of Things (IoT) systems face a range of multifaceted challenges. These span technical, methodological, social, and infrastructural domains, each presenting unique barriers to effective implementation and scalability:

## Technical challenges

Data Availability and Quality: Incomplete or low-quality datasets can compromise the accuracy of ML models, leading to missed detections or false alarms.
Sensor Maintenance and Calibration: Regular upkeep and calibration of sensors are essential but can be costly and labor-intensive, affecting long-term sustainability.
Interoperability Problems: Integrating heterogeneous sensors, IoT devices, and ML algorithms often leads to compatibility challenges and system inefficiencies.
Scalability and Flexibility: Existing solutions may lack adaptability to emerging technologies or evolving user requirements, limiting their long-term relevance.
Battery Life and Power Consumption: Short battery lifespans in IoT devices necessitate frequent charging or replacement, which can hinder continuous operation.

## Machine learning challenges

Class Imbalance: ML models may struggle when safe scenarios vastly outnumber risky ones, reducing the system’s ability to detect threats accurately.
Overfitting and Underfitting: Poor generalization can result from models that either overlearn training data or fail to capture meaningful patterns.
Adversarial Attacks: ML systems are susceptible to adversarial attacks that can manipulate inputs and degrade performance.
Lack of Explainability: Many ML models operate as “black boxes,” making it difficult to interpret decision-making processes and build user trust.
Real-Time Processing Limitations: Delays in data processing can impede timely responses during emergencies, undermining system effectiveness.

## IOT and sensor challenges

Accuracy and Reliability of Sensors: Inconsistent sensor readings can lead to false positives or missed alerts, compromising user safety.
Sensor Orientation and location: The effectiveness of sensors is highly dependent on their positioning, which can vary across users and environments.
Wireless Communication Problems: Connectivity issues between devices may result in dropped signals or delayed transmissions.
Security and Privacy Risks: IoT systems often collect sensitive personal data, raising concerns about unauthorized access and data misuse.
Environmental conditions: External factors such as weather, lighting, and noise can affect sensor performance and data accuracy.

## Social and cultural challenges

Social and Cultural Barriers: Societal norms and stigmas may hinder the adoption and effectiveness of safety technologies.
Lack of Knowledge and Instruction: A lack of education and outreach prevents many users from understanding or accessing available safety solutions.
Stigma and Shame: Victims may hesitate to report abuse or use safety tools due to fear of judgment or social repercussions.
Power Dynamics: Unequal power relationships can influence the design and implementation of safety measures, affecting their accessibility and fairness.
Stakeholder Involvement: Minimal involvement from key stakeholders—such as law enforcement, healthcare providers, and advocacy groups—can limit system impact.

## Economic and infrastructure challenges

Cost and Affordability: High costs may prevent vulnerable populations from accessing advanced safety technologies.
Infrastructure Limitations: Poor lighting, limited electric supplies, limited mobility, and inadequate internet connectivity can reduce system effectiveness in certain regions.
Resource Constraints: The efficacy of safety solutions may be impacted by infrastructure constraints including inadequate mobility, bad lighting, or a lack of internet connectivity.
Policy and Regulatory Comprehensive frameworks: The absence of supportive legal and regulatory frameworks may delay adoption and reduce system credibility.
Public-Private Partnerships: Insufficient partnerships between government and private sectors can restrict funding and innovation in safety technology development.

By addressing these challenges, this research aims to pave the way for more resilient, inclusive, and ethically grounded safety systems that protect women and children in diverse environments.

## Problem statement
The rising incidence of crimes targeting women and children underscores the urgent need for reliable and intelligent safety solutions. However, existing systems suffer from a critical design flaw: they are overwhelmingly reactive. Many solutions rely on manual activation, such as pressing a button, which is often impossible when a victim is incapacitated or under duress. To overcome this, some systems have attempted autonomous detection by monitoring physiological parameters like body temperature and heart rate.
However, these systems fall into a second critical trap: a high rate of false positives. They often use simple, static thresholds to detect distress. This oversimplification fails to account for real-world variability; for instance, a user’s heart rate will naturally elevate during exercise, and this benign activity could be misidentified as a threat[cite: 306]. Such false alarms severely erode user trust and render the system unreliable. Therefore, the core problem is not just to detect physiological anomalies, but to distinguish between true distress and benign, everyday activities.
This research addresses this specific problem by developing a personalized, context-aware wearable safety device. Instead of relying on generic, fixed thresholds, our system uses a machine learning (ML) model trained on sensor data (including heart rate, temperature, and motion) to learn the user’s individual, baseline patterns [cite: 307, 310]. The system is designed to autonomously identify significant deviations from this personal baseline, fusing data from multiple sensors to validate a potential threat before triggering an alert. This sensor fusion and ML-based anomaly detection approach is designed to minimize false positives and provide a reliable, autonomous emergency notification system, complete with real-time GPS location, for rapid intervention[cite: 308].

Stress-Lysis: This dataset provides 2001 samples of human body humidity, body temperature, and step count. It is used to train the model to perform three-class classification of stress levels: Low, Normal, and High, based on physical activity [cite: 314–316].

## Proposed system design

## System architecture and deployment
The proposed system is a wearable, intelligent safety device designed for continuous, autonomous monitoring. The architecture, illustrated in Fig.  , is a closed-loop system centered on an Arduino microcontroller, which acts as the central processing unit for data acquisition, and a Raspberry Pi 3, which handles more intensive machine learning (ML) inference. The system’s logic follows a clear data pipeline:

 

Data Acquisition: Physiological sensors (Heart Rate, Temperature Sensor) and environmental sensors (GPS, Accelerometer, Tilt Sensor) continuously gather real-time data from the user.
Edge Processing: This raw data is fed to the microcontrollers (Arduino and Raspberry Pi 3) for on-device, or “edge,” processing. This is critical for minimizing latency, as it eliminates the need for a constant, high-speed connection to a cloud server. The ML models (SVM, NB) are deployed directly on the Raspberry Pi.
AI-Driven Inference: The ML model analyzes the fused sensor data in real-time, comparing it against the user’s trained personal baseline to detect significant anomalies indicative of distress.
Action & Alerting:

Autonomous Alert: If the ML model classifies a high-threat event, the system automatically triggers an alert.
Manual Alert: The user can also manually trigger an alert using the Push Button Switch.
Response: Upon an alert, the Arduino activates the local Buzzer (via Relay) and Laser Diode to create a local deterrent. Simultaneously, the GSM module retrieves the user’s precise location from the GPS module and transmits an emergency alert with coordinates to pre-registered contacts and a secure endpoint for law enforcement.

## Computational and deployment considerations
This edge-computing architecture is essential for practical, real-world deployment. The SVM model, in particular, is computationally lightweight, making it ideal for running on the Raspberry Pi 3 with minimal processing delay (achieving detection in ~ 3 s, as shown in our results). For integration with law enforcement, the GSM alert is formatted as a JSON data packet sent via a secure GPRS connection to a dedicated API endpoint. This allows the alert to be fed directly into a computer-aided dispatch (CAD) system, providing officers with the victim’s location in real-time. Power management is optimized to provide 18–20 h of continuous operation, ensuring the device remains active throughout a user’s day.

## Hardware components

## Arduino
The latest version of the Arduino Uno includes a USB interface, six analog input pins, and fourteen digital I/O connectors for connecting to external electrical components. Among the 14 I/O ports, six can be used for pulse width modulation (PWM) output.

## LCD
This type of electronic screen is used in a multitude of devices, including cell phones, laptops, television sets, and more. These screens often use diodes that produce light in multiple segment and seven-segment configurations. There are several advantages to using this component, such as its affordability, easy configurability, and the ability to display animations and other visuals without restrictions.

## Switch
A pushbutton is a simple switch used to control certain components of an appliance or operation. Typically, these buttons are made from rigid materials, most often metal or plastic.

## Buzzer
Buzzers are audio signaling devices that can be electronic, mechanical, or piezoelectric. They are commonly used for various purposes, including alarm clocks, reminders, and confirming user input, such as pressing a mouse or keyboard button. Buzzers are mechanical transducers powered by direct current and are frequently utilized in audio devices like laptops, printers, photocopiers, timers, mechanical toys, automotive electronics, cell phones, clocks, and other technological devices.

## Power supply
The power supply delivers electrical power to various components, typically those attached to the driven segment. In computers, power supplies, often located at the rear of the case, include at least one fan. These components convert alternating current (AC) to direct current (DC).

## Temperature sensor
Given the critical importance of maintaining one’s internal body temperature for health, it is essential to monitor it consistently. Various temperature sensors can accurately determine body temperature. A prime example of a highly accurate circuit-based detector is the LM35 series. This sensor produces a voltage output linearly correlated to the temperature in Celsius, operating with an indication factor of ± 10mV/°C and a precision of 0.5 °C. Significant fluctuations in body temperature during critical situations can activate emergency response mechanisms .

## Pulse rate sensor

A semiconductor component called a laser diode produces high-intensity coherent illumination. Laser diodes operate on the principle of stimulated emission. ## Methodology
The methodology for this system is twofold: first, the development and robust validation of the machine learning models, and second, the design of the system’s operational and security framework.
For the ML models, data from the specified datasets was preprocessed and standardized. To ensure the models are robust and to prevent overfitting, a 10-fold cross-validation technique was employed. The dataset was partitioned into 10 subsets; the models were trained on 9 folds and tested on the remaining fold, with this process repeated 10 times. The final reported accuracy represents the average of these folds. This rigorous validation is critical for confirming the high accuracy of the SVM and Naive Bayes classifiers. For the Deep Learning (CNN) model, additional overfitting mitigation techniques, such as Dropout layers and Early Stopping (where training is halted if validation accuracy does not improve), were implemented to ensure the model generalizes well to unseen data.
The proposed IoT-based safety system for women and children is structured around four core operational mechanisms: Manual, Automated, Alert-Based, and Security & Privacy. Each mechanism is designed to ensure responsive, reliable, and ethically sound protection in real-time scenarios.

Manual Mechanism: In situations where the user is conscious and able to act, a manual trigger is available. By pressing a designated switch, the system activates a loud buzzer to alert nearby individuals and draw immediate attention. This action also initiates the alert protocol, enabling further emergency communication.
Automated Mechanism: Recognizing that users may be incapacitated or unable to manually respond, the system incorporates automated detection using multiple sensors—including temperature, heart rate, and abrasion sensors. Data from these sensors is cross-validated to minimize false positives. If two or more sensors detect anomalies, the security protocol is activated.

A force-sensitive resistor (FSR) is employed to measure pressure changes. Even slight increases in force result in significant impedance reduction, which is converted into an analog voltage ranging from 0 to 5 centivolts. This sensor fusion approach enhances reliability and responsiveness without requiring user input.

3.Alert-Based Mechanism: Upon detection of a potential threat—whether manually or automatically triggered—the system activates its alert mechanism. Using GPS and GSM technologies, it sends real-time location data via SMS to pre-registered emergency contacts and local authorities. The location is embedded with Google Maps links for rapid access and navigation.

## Security and privacy comprehensive framework for iot-based women and child safety system
Given the sensitive nature of the data collected—such as biometric readings, GPS coordinates, and stress indicators—the system incorporates a robust, multi-layered security and privacy architecture to safeguard user information.
Key features include:

End-to-End Encryption (AES-256): All data transfers between wearable sensors, microcontroller units (Arduino/Raspberry Pi), and cloud-based ML platforms are encrypted to prevent unauthorized access.
Data Minimization and Edge Processing: Only essential biometric parameters are collected. Wherever possible, data is processed locally on edge devices to reduce cloud exposure and enhance privacy.
Differential Privacy in ML Models: Algorithms are designed to preserve model accuracy while anonymizing user-specific patterns, ensuring ethical data handling.
Granular Consent Management: Users can control geographic tracking boundaries, emergency contact permissions, and data-sharing preferences through multi-tiered consent protocols.
Role-Based Access Control: Only verified family members, designated emergency contacts, and authorized law enforcement personnel can access sensitive data during confirmed emergencies.
Secure Boot and Firmware Protection: The system prevents unauthorized firmware manipulation through secure boot mechanisms and encrypted update channels.
Blockchain-Based Audit Trails: All emergency alerts and data access events are logged using blockchain technology to ensure transparency and accountability.
Automatic Data Retention Policies: Configurable purge schedules ensure that historical biometric and location data is not retained indefinitely, minimizing long-term privacy risks.
Adversarial Defense Mechanisms: Input validation, anomaly detection, and federated learning are employed to protect the system from adversarial attacks and maintain model integrity without centralized data exposure.
This comprehensive methodology ensures that the safety system remains responsive, secure, and privacy-conscious—delivering reliable protection for vulnerable users in both everyday and emergency scenarios.

Signature Method of Connected Devices:

IoT devices are primarily signed using digital signatures based on Public Key Infrastructure (PKI), which cryptographically ties a device’s identity to its data to guarantee non-repudiation, authenticity, and integrity. In this process, a device uses its own private key to sign a hash of its data, which can be validated by anybody with the matching public key. This ensures that the data is authentic and hasn’t been altered. Furthermore, new uses of blockchain technology improve device authentication and data provenance by offering decentralized and unchangeable identity management for linked devices. In the context of IoT-based women and children’s safety, sensor thresholds are dynamically determined through continuous data analysis and machine learning.

Normal Situations: Large datasets of typical user behavior and ambient situations are used to train the AI/ML models. In doing so, they are able to create a baseline for “routine activities.” For example, a regular scenario could include an accelerometer readout that indicates walking or sitting, a heart rate that falls within a normal range, and ambient noise levels typical of an office or public setting. Emergency Situations: Deviations from these learned normal patterns, especially when multiple sensor inputs are fused, trigger the classification of an event as a potential emergency. The AI/ML algorithms are crucial in identifying these complex, multi-modal anomalies that distinguish a genuine threat from a mere benign anomaly.

## Discussion on power and maintenance of wearable devices

The efficacy and practicality of IoT-based wearable safety devices are significantly influenced by their power management and maintenance requirements. In this study, a key focus was placed on optimizing the device’s energy consumption to ensure prolonged operational capability. The implemented battery optimization strategies have successfully extended the usable life of the wearable to 18–20 h, which is crucial for continuous real-time GPS monitoring and GSM-based emergency communication, especially during extended periods of travel or work. This optimized battery performance is vital for user confidence and reliable protection, minimizing the need for frequent recharging and ensuring the device remains active when most needed.
Beyond power, the long-term viability and user acceptance of such devices also depend heavily on their ease of maintenance. While not explicitly detailed in the core findings, a robust IoT wearable safety device would necessitate minimal and user-friendly maintenance. This typically includes:

Regular Charging: Users would need to establish a routine for charging the device, similar to a smartphone.
Software Updates: Periodic over-the-air (OTA) software updates would be essential to enhance security features, improve algorithm accuracy (e.g., for refined sensor thresholding and emergency detection), and introduce new functionalities.
Physical Upkeep: Basic physical maintenance, such as keeping the device clean and dry, would contribute to its longevity.
Sensor Calibration/Checks: Although designed for robustness, occasional checks or recalibrations of sensors might be beneficial to ensure continued accuracy, especially after prolonged use or exposure to extreme conditions.
Designing these devices with durable materials and sealed enclosures would further reduce the need for complex maintenance, making them more resilient to daily wear and tear and various environmental conditions.

Performance Evaluation:
1. Human Activity Recognition (HAR).
* Accuracy:
* Overall accuracy of correctly classifying activities (walking, running, sitting, standing, falling, etc.).
* Class-wise accuracy: Accuracy for each specific activity to identify potential biases.
* Precision:
* Proportion of correctly predicted instances among all instances predicted for a specific class. High precision means fewer false alarms.
* Recall:
* Proportion of correctly predicted instances among all actual instances of a specific class. High recall means fewer missed events.
* F1-score:
* Harmonic mean of precision and recall, providing a balanced measure.
* Confusion Matrix:
* Visual representation of the model’s performance, showing correct and incorrect predictions for each class. Helps identify misclassifications.
* Latency:
* Time taken by the model to recognize an activity in real-time. Critical for timely response in safety scenarios.
2. Stress Detection.
* Accuracy:
* Overall accuracy of correctly classifying stress levels (e.g., low, medium, high).
* Sensitivity/Recall:
* Ability to correctly identify individuals experiencing stress. Crucial for timely intervention.
* Specificity:
* Ability to correctly identify individuals not experiencing stress. Helps avoid unnecessary alarms.
* AUC-ROC (Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve):
* Measures the model’s ability to distinguish between stressed and non-stressed individuals across different thresholds.
* False Positive Rate (FPR):
* Proportion of non-stressed individuals incorrectly classified as stressed.
* False Negative Rate (FNR):
* Proportion of stressed individuals incorrectly classified as non-stressed.

## Standout performance of classical ML algorithms
The most striking finding is that traditional machine learning approaches - Support Vector Machine and Naive Bayes - significantly outperform the deep learning CNN approach in this particular application. Well-defined feature spaces that SVM can effectively separate.

While the CNN achieves “only” 90% accuracy, it’s noted as multimodal, suggesting it processes multiple types of input data simultaneously. ## Limitations
Despite the high accuracy achieved, this study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. The ML models were trained using datasets that, while specialized, are simulated or collected in controlled environments [cite: 313–325]. These datasets may not fully capture the complexity and variability of real-world emergency scenarios. Furthermore, as noted in the plan to address reviewer feedback, the datasets may lack sufficient representation from diverse demographic and cultural backgrounds. This could lead to potential biases in stress detection, where the system may perform differently for different groups of users. Finally, the hardware prototype, while effective for validation, would require further miniaturization and hardening for a commercial, consumer-grade wearable device. Future research must focus on collecting larger, more diverse, real-world datasets to enhance model fairness and robustness.

The research presents a comprehensive IoT-enabled smart safety solution for women and children, integrating physiological sensors, GPS tracking, and microcontrollers. The system has three core mechanisms: AI-powered alarm systems, automatic threat detection, and manual activation via emergency switches.
