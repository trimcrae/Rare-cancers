## Abstract

## Introduction
The Ministry of Health, Treatment and Medical Education in Iran has tried integrating the primary science curriculum with the clinical program. This means educational reform has been implemented in most medical schools in Iran. In the study of the medical entomology Discipline, prioritization of educational needs, and determination of appropriate solutions to improve the status of the entomology Discipline in the Faculty of Medicine and Health, the basis of experts’ opinions were examined and analyzed. Objetivo: Insert the subject of medical entomology into the Medicine Discipline at the Faculty of Medicine and Health.

## Methodology
The Nominal Group Technique (NGT), which was the papers for recording the opinions of the professors, was done after the focus group. In the focus group, the professors discussed the results obtained from the NGT. This method used the Delphi technique to exchange opinions (disagreement and consensus of experts).

## Results
According to the findings of this study, after two technical sessions of the Nominal group, the weak and strong points and required subjects for revision of the chapters of the medical entomology curriculum were determined. Medical entomology in Iranian universities of medical sciences is one of the important topics of medical parasitology Disciplines for general medical students because it provides information about Disease-vector insects and ways to control them (Results two rounds of Delphi).

## Conclusion
The educational program of the entomology Discipline needs to be revised and evaluated after the implementation of the revised program.

According to the above issues and in connection with the focus group meetings with the presence of the professors of medical and health entomology departments and also using the opinions of the professors of the medical education departments of the Iran University of Medical Sciences, a review of entomology disciplines in the field of general medicine was carried out based on the opinion of experts.
The basic science training Discipline forms the foundation for academic progress and provides a comprehensive understanding of the content in subsequent general medicine Disciplines. The knowledge gained in this Discipline equips medical students to solve clinical problems and make informed clinical decisions in the future. Basic science is one of the essential parts of medical knowledge, and it, as an academic subject, forms a considerable part of medical education programs. This study was based on the information obtained from the purposeful sampling method and initially by searching and reviewing the curriculum of several prestigious universities with medical entomology content. ## Materials and methods

## Type of research
This research is looking for the Nominal Group Technique (NGT) of entomology Disciplines in the field of general medicine based on the opinion of experts. NGT is a qualitative research method that identifies, analyzes, and interprets the semantic patterns of qualitative data. The NGT and thematic analysis are widely used in management studies and social sciences. Considering the frequent use of the NGT, researchers must familiarize themselves with the nature and principles of this method. In terms of the practical purpose and terms of the method, the present research is among the researches of the NGT, and the general algorithm of the mixed research method includes a qualitative part using the NGT of specialized texts to the basic categories of the phenomenon under study, especially in other countries. It was found by conducting specialized interviews with experts using structured and semi-structured interviews and the focus group method, recording and saving the interviews in the form of text and NGT and extracting primary indicators.

## How to discuss the noun group technique (NGT)
First, the participants were selected by purposeful sampling; then, people were invited by phone call and written invitation. The medical entomology department’s conference room was considered the venue for the meeting and was reminded again the day before the meeting.
In the first session, the group discussion started with a brief description of the importance of research. The student initiated the conversation. Notes were taken from all discussions of the meeting. In order not to bias the necessary information, it was reviewed several times and compared with the notes of two colleagues.
The participants included experts and veterans of medical entomology education at Shiraz University of Medical Sciences, Iran. The working method was NGT, and the purpose of the meeting was to define and analyze the current situation. It is worth mentioning that the duration of the meeting was about two hours.

## Description of the first session
In the first session of the Nominal Group Technique (NGT), participants explored the gap between the ideal and current states of medical entomology education. The process involved raising key questions, collecting opinions, and analyzing responses. The questions included:

Current needs and issues in medical entomology education.
Areas for improvement in the program.
Priorities in education.
Adaptation to other universities’ programs.
Useful experiences from similar institutions.

The process involved examining questions, summarizing views, and identifying gaps and educational priorities.
The starting quorum of the meeting is 4–6 professors from the Department of Biology and Control of Disease Vectors, Faculty of Health, Shiraz University of Medical Sciences, and the type of meeting is combined (the NGT was the same as the papers for recording the opinions of the professors that was conducted after the focus group). In the focus group, the professors discussed the results obtained from the NGT.
The discussion was on entomology topics in the field of general medicine, and in fact, we want these topics to be reviewed with a new and more practical view. A series of suggestions were given, so the initial work and preliminary studies were done. Finally, seven topics from the studies were extracted, and issues that should have been removed were written in front of these seven titles. The papers were distributed anonymously among the professors. They studied the titles and exclusions of each item and gave each title a score from one to five (one score is the least important topic and can somehow be removed from the medical entomology section of the field It was general medicine and a score of five was the most important subject and should be paid more attention to.
If the professors had specific opinions about any item, these were discussed and prioritized during the focus group session after the initial scoring. The group then collectively decided whether each item should be removed or given more attention. Currently, the allocated teaching time for entomology in the medical curriculum is four hours (including both theory and practical sessions). Through their scoring, the professors effectively assigned weight to each item. Then, the items that brought the most points were reviewed in terms of presentation in 4 h. Also, those who brought the most points were again selected by weight for 4 h of presentation.
Using their experience teaching medical students and the feedback they got from them during and after teaching, the professors chose the topics that were most applicable to the environment. Therefore, the items were rearranged based on the teachers’ textbook, and the items were combined. Moreover, none of them overlapped with each other, and the items were transparently converted into four items (Table  ).

## Results
In the first session of the NGT revealed critical issues in medical entomology education, such as the need to update programs, enhance resources, and adopt innovative teaching methods. Participants emphasized improving faculty expertise and promoting research development. ## Participants’ comments
The current program in medical entomology at Shiraz University of Medical Sciences may face resource limitations. This includes books, articles, laboratory equipment, and online resources. Lack of resources can harm the quality of education and research in the field of medical entomology. The imbalance between theoretical and practical Disciplines: the medical entomology curriculum of Shiraz University may have an imbalance between Discipline and practical. This means that practical and laboratory experiences are not sufficiently provided for students, and practical exercises are not provided sufficiently.
Medical entomology is a dynamic and evolving field, requiring constant updates to educational content. However, the current program at Shiraz University may not adequately reflect recent advancements, limiting the transfer of up-to-date knowledge to students. Additionally, the program appears to lack a strong emphasis on research and scientific projects, which are essential for fostering practical skills and innovation in the field. Strengthening these aspects could enhance the overall quality of education and better prepare students for future challenges. This can lead to a reduction in students’ ability to conduct research operations and gain practical experience in the field of medical entomology. The current educational programs at Faculty of Medicine have not been reviewed based on the author’s information; therefore, the specific strengths of the current program cannot be easily identified. To investigate this issue further, referring to internal sources from the university or people directly involved in the educational programs is better.

## Discussion
Medical entomology in Iranian universities of medical sciences is one of the important topics for general medical students because it provides information about Disease-vector insects and ways to control them. The following topics can be mentioned among the reasons why the medical entomology Discipline is important for general medical students. Understanding disease transmission and medical entomology provides students with knowledge of Disease-vector insects such as mosquitoes, ticks, and fleas. This knowledge is important for general medical students because they need to understand the transmission of diseases for effective diagnosis and treatment. In order to prevent and control vector-borne diseases the medical entomology section teaches students ways to prevent and control vector-borne diseases. They should also educate their patients about ways to prevent these diseases.
The medical entomology chapter equips students with the skills to identify arthropods, distinguishing between those that transmit diseases and those that are harmless. Additionally, it provides comprehensive knowledge on managing arthropod populations, focusing on effective control strategies for species of medical importance. Students should learn effective methods for controlling disease-causing arthropods, focusing on strategies to reduce their populations and minimize their role in disease transmission. For this purpose, it is necessary for students to pay attention to this Discipline and its educational content and to update these Disciplines according to the dominant fauna of pathogenic insects in Iran and all around the world, according to the opinion of professors in the related fields. On the other hand, it will be necessary to consider students’ educational needs and their willingness to learn the presented topics to improve the education process. Continuous evaluation of educational programs, identification of strengths and weaknesses, and revision and development of new lesson plans make it possible to improve the programs.
According to the results, it can be said that the professors generally consider this Discipline to be necessary. Moreover, they emphasized that general medical students are aware of the role of insects in causing important diseases such as malaria So, according to the opinion of all the professors, they considered the number of units allocated to this topic for medical students to be low; however, the inconsistency of opinion was observed in the two dimensions of theoretical and practical teaching, and almost all professors agreed to increase the practical teaching hours of the medical entomology unit. The professors acknowledged that this topic shows the interest and curiosity of medical students in the practical topics of medical entomology, and it can be the source of the change in planning for the number of practical hours and teaching theory in entomology.
In line with the present study, another study further evaluated students’ interest in practical topics of entomology [ ]. A Study conducted on revising virology education requirements for medical students showed that medical education is a dynamic process and states that the curriculum should change whenever necessary. Aligned with the recent research, most other studies conducted agreed that revising the Discipline and adding new subjects to the current curriculum would be the best way to bring out this change [ ].
Regarding the next part of this lesson, you should pay attention to the quality and quantity of its content. The first and essential point in this regard, in line with other studies, is to reduce the generality of the lesson from sections such as the importance of arthropods, methods, mechanisms of disease transmission, morphology, and physiology of insects, which all professors and students approve. For this reason, reforms in these departments can be the most necessary reduction of lesson hours.
By reducing the teaching hours of sections such as the importance of arthropods and the morphology and physiology of insects to 1 h, it is possible to provide more time for teaching in the form of Problem-Based Learning (P.B.L). In this regard, changing the methods and mechanisms of disease transmission can also help students to increase their teaching hours. The analysis of the next specialized sections showed that the specialized topics of the medical entomology Discipline were questioned separately. Based on the experts’ opinions regarding including or excluding topics within the medical parasitology Discipline to adequately cover medical entomology, the focus should primarily be on arthropods prevalent in the country. Key arthropods of concern include mosquitoes (particularly Aedes species -family Culicidae), houseflies, lice, and other flies [ , , ].
The professors want to reduce the teaching hours of triatomines as vectors of Chagas disease in the first specialized department of the medical entomology unit. The areas affected by Chagas disease are mostly in South and Central America. This disease is common in most South American countries, including Brazil, Peru, Bolivia, Colombia, Venezuela, Ecuador, and Argentina. Also, the highest incidence of this disease is observed in rural and low-income areas of these countries. These areas include unsanitary living conditions and a lack health and treatment resources [ ].
So, it seems logical that, in the opinion of both students and professors, this case should occupy fewer hours in teaching medical entomology in our country, which is far from this pathogen. Also, the opinion of the professors confirms the importance of human lice and pathogenic arthropods for humans, and they want to increase the teaching hours of human lice in the first specialized section of the medical entomology unit.
Head lice (Pediculus humanus capitis) are parasitic insects that feed on human blood and attach their eggs to the hair shaft using a sticky substance. They primarily infest the scalp, particularly behind the ears and at the nape of the neck. Although they do not transmit diseases, head lice are a significant public health concern due to their rapid spread, especially among schoolchildren. Their increasing resistance to common insecticides highlights the need for detailed education on their management and control in medical curricula to equip future healthcare providers with effective intervention strategies [ , , ].
The study emphasized the need to enhance doctors’ knowledge of vector-borne diseases. Experts evaluated the inclusion and exclusion of topics within the medical parasitology curriculum to better integrate relevant medical entomology content, ensuring comprehensive coverage of disease-transmitting arthropods (medical parasitology focuses on parasitic organisms that cause human diseases, while medical entomology deals specifically with insects and arthropods that act as vectors for these diseases). The primary focus should be on common arthropods in the country, such as mosquitoes (especially Aedes mosquitoes), gnats, lice, and flies, due to their significant roles in public health and disease transmission. Additionally, the professors aim to reduce the hours devoted to teaching about flies in the specialized department of the medical entomology unit, as their importance as disease vectors is well understood. Tabanidae family female members are particularly significant because they bite animals, including humans, to feed on blood, thus playing a role in the transmission of various pathogens [ , ].
According to the professors, this case should be allocated fewer hours to teaching medical entomology in our country, which is far from these pathogens. The professors most often want to increase the teaching hours of mosquitos, especially Aedes mosquitoes, in the specialized department of the medical entomology unit. The Culicidae family is one of the most important families within the (Ordem: Diptera; Subordemn Nematocera and Bivalve orders, with 64 species and three subspecies reported in Iran. Mosquitoes in this family are crucial as vectors of several significant diseases. Of the 43 identified genera of Culicidae mosquitoes, 15 are particularly important, including species of Anopheles, Aedes, and others. These mosquitoes are responsible for transmitting malaria, arbovirus infections, and filariasis, making them critical subjects of study in medical entomology and public health [ , ]. Mosquitoes of the Culicidae family are medically significant due to their role in transmitting diseases to humans and animals. They are also a veterinary concern, spreading pathogens among domestic and wild animals. The lack of comprehensive ecological data on Culicidae in Iran underscores the need for further study and curriculum emphasis. Professors and students have highlighted the importance of focusing on mosquitoes, particularly invasive Aedes species, which pose a public health risk by transmitting dangerous arboviruses, including those found along the northern shores of the Persian Gulf [ , ].
Also, in the opinion of several expert professors, the issue of venomous arthropod (venomous arthropods -inoculate poison) (the frog is poisonous) bites, especially scorpions and spiders, the important medical insect fauna of the country and every region, should be emphasized. Therefore, the professors want to increase the teaching hours of scorpions in the specialized section (discussions other than insects) of the medical entomology unit. Scorpions are medically significant due to their venomous stings, which can cause severe health complications and fatalities. In Iran, scorpions are responsible for the highest sting rates globally, with species like Hemiscorpius lepturus, Compsobuthus matthiesseni, and Androctonus crassicauda posing significant risks. Timely treatment within six hours of a sting greatly improves survival outcomes. Given these risks, expert recommendations emphasize the need to expand medical entomology curricula to cover scorpion-related health issues, particularly in medical parasitology, to enhance student preparedness in managing scorpion envenomations [ , , , ].
In the first NGT meeting, aimed at identifying and addressing gaps in medical entomology education, the discussions and expert surveys highlighted that curriculum revision should be a continuous, dynamic process. Regular updates are necessary to adapt to evolving knowledge and societal needs, ensuring the curriculum remains relevant and effective. Continuous revision is essential for monitoring and improving the educational system. As the needs of any dynamic society are constantly evolving, the educational system must adapt accordingly to train the necessary workforce. This adaptation is achieved through ongoing revisions of educational programs, ultimately leading to more effective outcomes. Therefore, it seems that in order to continue the review, standard methods for its implementation should be designed and presented. The necessity of determining the timing of the implementation of the review and formulating the evaluation program to ensure the results of the review and to achieve the new goals of the subjects that must be changed in the educational program is one of the important elements of the implementation of the continuous review program in the educational system, especially in Iran.
To change and revise the title of the medical entomology unit in the medical parasitology Discipline. In the general section, all the professors agreed to increase the practical teaching hours of the medical entomology unit. It can indicate the need to increase the teaching hours of practical topics. Therefore, it is suggested that the practical teaching hours of this Discipline be increased. In the general and preliminaries section of medical entomology, the importance of arthropods, the methods and mechanism of disease transmission, the morphology and physiology of insects, which are agreed upon by the professors, is reduced, so it is suggested to improve the subject matter and increase students’ interest in this Discipline by reducing these topics.
According to the professors’ feedback, there is a need to increase the teaching hours dedicated to human lice, Culicidae mosquitoes, and scorpions in specialized medical entomology courses. These topics were consistently identified as critical in the survey, highlighting their importance in addressing public health concerns and ensuring that medical students are well-prepared to manage related health issues effectively. Professors recommend reducing teaching hours for triatomines, which are vectors of Chagas disease, and for mosquitoes and other arthropods that are not relevant to Iran and the surrounding regions. They suggest focusing more on locally significant vectors and diseases. Topics related to non-native vectors and diseases should either be removed from the curriculum or allocated minimal time to ensure the content remains regionally relevant and practical for students.

## Conclusion
This study emphasizes the need to revise Iran’s medical entomology curriculum to address current challenges. Key issues identified include insufficient teaching hours, outdated content, and limited practical training. Revisions should focus on expanding hands-on learning, updating content to reflect prevalent disease vectors, and adopting student-centered approaches like Problem-Based Learning (PBL). Priority topics include mosquitoes, human lice, and scorpions due to their public health importance. Continuous curriculum evaluation and flexible strategies are essential to align education with evolving societal and medical needs, ultimately enhancing future physicians’ preparedness to manage entomological health challenges.
