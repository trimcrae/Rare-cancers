## Abstract

## Background
China’s rural elderly population (projected to reach 190 million by 2035) faces a high osteoporosis (OP) burden, with national sampling surveys reporting a 26.8% prevalence (95% CI: 25.1%–28.5%) in rural adults ≥60 years (Zeng, Li, Wang, et al., Arch Osteoporos 14:39-48, 2019). However, screening gaps persist due to limited central DXA access (< 1 unit/100,000 older adults) and poor sensitivity of quantitative ultrasound (QUS; <60% for asymptomatic OP). This study evaluated a novel government-led model using portable DXA (pDXA) to achieve universal screening coverage in rural China.

## Methods
A STROBE-compliant cross-sectional study (June–November 2021) enrolled all eligible permanent residents ≥ 60 years (N = 3,530) in Tongcun Community, Wujiang District, Jiangsu. pDXA (Dexa Pro-I, Xuzhou Pinyuan; National Medical Products Administration Reg. No. 20182061513) was integrated into annual health exams at Tongcun Community Health Center. Certified radiographers performed nondominant forearm scans (diagnostic threshold per WHO criteria: T score ≤-2.5), and OP specialists (≥ 8 years of experience) from Suzhou Ninth Hospital provided patient education. Multivariable logistic regression and propensity score matching (covariates: age, gender, daily exercise duration, prior fracture history) adjusted for confounders.

## Results
• Overall OP prevalence: 38.2% (95% CI: 36.7–39.7), with significant gender disparity (women: 55.4% [95% CI: 53.2–57.6]; men: 18.0% [95% CI: 16.1–20.0]; χ² test, p < 0.001)
• Age-stratified female OP prevalence: 41.2% (60–69 y), 62.1% (70–79 y), 73.6% (≥80 y).
• Cost per screen: ​​¥6.2, 87% lower than central DXA (¥48.5/screen (Li, Zhang, Wang, et al., J Rural Health 37(2):189-196, 2021)).
Treatment initiation rate: 21.0%; key barriers included "perceived no symptoms" (68.1% overall; higher in men: 72.3% vs women: 66.4%; p=0.02) and "medication cost concerns" [72.3% overall; 89.1% in groups with low medical payment capacity (defined by OP awareness, health consciousness, and treatment spending willingness) ].

## Conclusions
Government-procured pDXA enables cost-effective universal OP screening in resource-limited rural areas. Scaling this model requires culturally adapted education (e.g., Wu Chinese dialect audiovisual materials) and tiered medication subsidies for groups with low medical payment capacity, aligning with the UN Decade of Healthy Aging (United Nations, UN Decade of Healthy Aging 2021–2030, World Health Organization, 2020).

## Introduction
China’s rural elderly population is projected to reach 190 million by 2035. The prevalence of OP reaches 26.8% in rural adults ≥ 60 years (95% CI: 25.1%–28.5%) [ ], yet critical gaps exist:

 Diagnostic access gaps: Central DXA scarcity in rural areas (< 1 unit/100,000) [ ].
 Screening limitations: QUS sensitivity < 60% for asymptomatic OP [ ].
 Participation bias: Voluntary models exclude >60% high-risk males [ ]. While pDXA is strongly correlated with central DXA (forearm-spine r = 0.93) [ ], no studies have achieved universal coverage. This study bridges three gaps:

First 100% coverage pDXA screening in rural China.
Rigorous analysis of gender-specific treatment barriers.
Policy solutions aligned with the UN Decade of Healthy Aging [ ].
Notably, existing national osteoporosis studies in China (e.g [ ])., primarily adopted sampling-based DXA screening, which may miss asymptomatic high-risk individuals. In contrast, this study is the first to achieve 100% coverage of eligible elderly residents in a rural community using portable DXA, eliminating selection bias and providing real-world data on the actual prevalence and treatment barriers in resource-limited rural settings—filling the gap between national sampling results and local implementation realities.

## Methods

## Study design and setting
This is a retrospective cross-sectional study based on data from a universal osteoporosis screening program conducted in Tongcun Community from June to November 2021. The screening protocol (e.g., pDXA operation, participant recruitment) was implemented in accordance with the Screening Implementation Manual (2021 version) and informed consent was obtained from all participants using the original consent form (2021 version) prior to screening. The retrospective analysis of de-identified screening data was approved by the same ethics committee (KYLW2024-065-01) with a specific approval for retrospective research.

## Design
STROBE-compliant cross-sectional study (June–November 2021).

## Location
Tongcun Community, Wujiang District. As a typical rural community in southern Jiangsu, Tongcun reflects the prominent aging demographic characteristic common to rural areas in this region—a key feature consistent with broader rural southern Jiangsu. Economically, it aligns with the clustered industrial development pattern typical of southern Jiangsu, with its economic structure (dominated by local small-to-medium manufacturing workshops and supplementary agricultural activities) consistent with that of adjacent rural communities. This alignment in core demographic (aging trend) and economic characteristics ensures the study results are generalizable to resource-limited rural regions in economically developed southern China.

## Screening venue
Tongcun Community Health Center.

## Ethical approval
Approved by the Suzhou Ninth Hospital Institutional Review Board (KYLW2024-065-01). Verbal exemption was granted in 2021 under Article 18 of China’s Biomedical Research Ethics Guidelines [ ], with formal written approval obtained in 2024. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants (Supplementary File 5).

## Implementation framework

 

## Participants

 

## Sample size justification
As a population-based universal screening study, all eligible permanent residents aged ≥ 60 years in Tongcun Community were enrolled (N = 3,530). This census approach eliminated sampling bias and ensured 100% coverage of the target population, which is consistent with the study’s aim to evaluate real-world implementation feasibility.

## Screening protocol

 Device: Pinyuan Dexa Pro – I. The device has obtained approval from the National Medical Products Administration (registration number: Jiangsu Class II Medical Device Registration Certificate Number: 20182061513). Its radiation dose is 0.02 mSv per scan, which is 1/50 of the annual natural radiation limit (1 mSv) recommended by the International Commission on Radiological Protection (ICRP Publication 103) and complies with the Chinese national standard ‘Radiological Protection Requirements for Medical X-Ray Diagnostic Equipment’ (GB 9706.12–2018). During the entire screening period (June–November 2021), no radiation-related adverse events (e.g., skin redness, dizziness, or other discomfort) were reported among all 3,530 participants, further confirming the device’s clinical safety in elderly rural populations.
 Training: Radiographers completed 40-hour certification (Jiangsu Medical Association).
 Measurement: Nondominant forearm (1/3 distal radius).
 Quality control: Daily calibration (European Spine Phantom) [ , ], repeat scans for motion artifacts (0.9% rate).
 Diagnosis: T score ≤ −2.5 (WHO 2023) [ ].

## Statistical analysis

Software: SPSS 26.0, R 4.1.2.
Analyses: Prevalence proportions (95% Wilson CI), multivariable logistic regression, and propensity score matching (caliper = 0.05). Propensity score matching was performed using covariates including age, gender, daily exercise duration, and prior fracture history to balance confounding factors between the osteoporosis and non-osteoporosis groups. Post-matching balance was confirmed with standardized mean differences < 0.1 for all covariates, indicating successful balance. Multiple comparisons were not adjusted for given the exploratory nature of the study, as recommended by the STROBE guidelines for cross-sectional analyses.
Microcosting: Equipment depreciation (5 years), personnel time (local wages), consumables.

## Missing data handling
Variables with missing values (e.g., exercise duration, n = 12; prior fracture history, n = 9) were addressed via multiple imputation with chained equations (MICE) in R (micepackage v3.15.0). Sensitivity analyses comparing complete-case and imputed results revealed negligible differences (< 2% change in OR estimates).

## Measurement tools

 Physical activity: Assessed via the WHO Global Physical Activity Questionnaire (GPAQ) [ ], with “daily exercise < 30 minutes” defined as total moderate-to-vigorous activity < 150 min/week.
 Prior fragility fracture: Validated by hospital records (Suzhou Ninth Hospital EMR system) and participant interviews via the Fracture Risk Assessment Tool (FRAX®) localization protocol [ ].
 Income level: Self-reported using three standardized rural income brackets from the China National Bureau of Statistics [ ]: <¥2,000/month, ¥2,000–3,000/month, and >¥3,000/month.

## Results

## Operational metrics

 

## Osteoporosis burden
A total of 3,530 participants (mean age 68.4 ± 6.7 years; 54.1% female) underwent pDXA screening. The overall prevalence of osteoporosis (T score ≤ −2.5) was 38.2% (95% CI: 36.7–39.7), with a significant sex disparity:

Female: 55.4% (95% CI: 53.2–57.6).
Male: 18.0% (95% CI: 16.1–20.0).
p value: <0.001 (χ² test).

Age-stratified analysisrevealed an increasing prevalence in women:

60–69years: 41.2% (95% CI: 38.5–44.0).
70–79 years: 62.1% (95% CI: 58.9–65.3).
≥80 years: 73.6% (95% CI: 69.2–77.5).

## Diagnostic validation
Based on a priori power calculation (α = 0.05, β = 0.2, expected sensitivity = 85%), a subsample of 282 participants (including 249 from the osteoporosis group and 33 from the non-osteoporosis group, identified via forearm pDXA) underwent additional central DXA scans of the hip and lumbar spine. Taking central DXA (spine/hip T score ≤ −2.5) as the reference standard, sensitivity and specificity were calculated using 2 × 2 contingency tables, with 95% CIs estimated via the Wilson score method. Forearm pDXA demonstrated the following.

Sensitivity: 86.7% (95% CI: 84.2–89.1).
Specificity: 92.4% (95% CI: 90.8–94.0).
Kappa coefficient: 0.79 (95% CI: 0.73–0.85), indicating substantial agreement between the two methods [ ], with interpretation based on Landis JR et al.‘s criteria (κ >0.75 indicating substantial agreement), confirming good consistency between forearm pDXA and central DXA in identifying osteoporosis among rural elderly populations.
The prevalence of osteoporosis among the ≥ 60-year-old population in Tongcun Community in this study is 38.2%, which is higher than the previous national sampling survey results (26.8%, 95% CI: 25.1%–28.5%) for rural ≥ 60-year-old populations [ ] and regional rural studies (16.7% in Henan) [ ]. The differences may be attributed to the following reasons:: ① This study adopted a 100% full - coverage screening method, avoiding the bias of “missing asymptomatic cases” in sampling surveys. ② Although the economic level in rural Suzhou is relatively high, the local elderly population has relatively short outdoor activity time (with an average of ≤ 1.5 h per day based on the baseline data of this study), which may affect their bone density levels. This result suggests that osteoporosis prevention and control still need to be emphasized even in rural areas of economically developed regions. In rural areas nationwide where full - coverage screening has not been carried out, the actual prevalence rate may be even higher, indicating greater prevention and control pressure (Table ).
 

## Risk factor analysis

Gender disparity: Females presented 4.15-fold higher odds after adjustment.
Age threshold: Risk doubled every 5 years in women (peak OR = 3.89 for those ≥ 80 years old).
Behavioral impact: A sedentary lifestyle independently doubles fracture risk.

FRAX® assessment showed that 19.5% of participants were at high risk of fragility fracture (10-year major osteoporotic fracture probability ≥ 20%). The consistency between FRAX® high-risk classification and pDXA-diagnosed osteoporosis was 82.3% (κ = 0.68, 95% CI: 0.61–0.75), indicating moderate agreement [ , ].

## Treatment barriers
A total of 1,066 diagnosed patients declined treatment. Key barriers included the following:

Perceived lack of symptoms (68.1% overall; higher in males [72.3% vs. females 66.4%; p = 0.02])
Transportation difficulties (54.7% overall; associated with residences > 5 km from clinics).
Medication cost concerns (72.3% overall; 89.1% in groups with low medical payment capacity).

## Subgroup disparities in treatment barriers
Subgroup analysis revealed significant disparities in treatment barriers:

 Gender: Males were 72.3% more likely to deny symptoms than females were (p = 0.02).
 Medical payment capacity: Low medical payment capacity groups (as defined in Table Note) reported 3.2-fold higher medication cost concerns than groups with adequate medical payment capacity (p < 0.001). Among low medical payment capacity groups, 61.2% also self-reported monthly income <¥2,000 [ ],—further indicating a partial correlation between income and payment willingness but not a direct equivalence.
 Keywords:

Income was the strongest predictor of cost-related barriers (p < 0.001).
Symptom denial marginally differed across genders (p = 0.12).

## Discussion

## Key innovations

First universal pDXA screening: Achieving 100% coverage through government‒community‒hospital coordination, eliminating selection bias.
 Cost-effectiveness: At ¥6.2/screen, the operational costs were 87% lower than those of central DXA (¥48.5/screen [ ]).
 Superior to QUS: Asymptomatic detection rate 86.7% vs. 41.2% in QUS models [ , ].

## Policy implications

## Immediate actions

Integrate the IOF One-Minute Risk Test into national elderly health exams [ ].
Development of audiovisual education in the Wu Chinese dialect.
Subsidize first-line OP medications (e.g., alendronate) [ ] for groups with low medical payment capacity, with a tiered subsidy ratio based on income brackets (e.g., 80% subsidy for those with monthly income <¥2,000, 50% for ¥2,000–¥3,000) to align with the economic characteristics of rural southern Jiangsu.

## Long-term strategies

Establishing fracture liaison services in community health centers.
Implementing mobile pDXA rotation systems.

## Limitations

 Site limitations: Forearm pDXA may underestimate the risk of spinal osteoporosis [ ]. Future studies could integrate spine pDXA (when available) to improve spinal OP detection [ ]. 
 Economic analysis: Break-even volume requires >5,000 annual screenings to justify equipment investment. This threshold could be reduced by pooling equipment across multiple adjacent villages (e.g., 3–5 villages sharing one pDXA device).
 Outcome measures: Lack of fracture incidence data due to cross-sectional design. A 5-year follow-up study is ongoing to assess the association between pDXA screening and fracture risk reduction.
 Data sharing: Data sharing: Deidentified data cannot be electronically transferred per China’s Data Security Law Article 21 [ ]. While aggregate data tables (e.g., age-stratified prevalence) are available in Supplementary File 3, individual-level data requests require ethics approval from Suzhou Ninth Hospital, which may limit external validation of our statistical models. Future studies could apply for multi-center data sharing approval to enhance reproducibility.

## Conclusion
This model demonstrates the viability of pDXA for population screening in resource-limited regions. Scaling success hinges on:

Integrating mobile pDXA with existing health systems.
Culturally tailored education to combat “no symptoms” misconceptions.
Financial protection mechanisms for treatment.

Scaling this framework requires integrating culturally adapted education with financial protection mechanisms.
We propose a stepped implementation pathway for scaling the pDXA screening model:

Village-level screening and classification.Based on forearm pDXA T-score results:T score ≤−2.5: Refer to osteoporosis specialists at county-level hospitals + subsidized medication via DRG bundled payment programs [ ].
T score − 1.0 to −2.5: Targeted lifestyle intervention (e.g., 30-minute daily outdoor weight-bearing exercise) + annual pDXA surveillance [ ].

Community-level follow-up.Establish fracture liaison services in community health centers to track high-risk participants (≥ 80 years old or FRAX® high-risk [ ]).
Coordinate with village committees to address transportation barriers (e.g., monthly shuttle buses for follow-up visits).
