## ABSTRACT
Robust public health training can be integrated into a didactically and clinically rigorous pediatric dentistry training program. Our program is the first of its kind to offer an MS‐MPH dual‐masters‐degree track, producing graduates who understand the scientific basis of dentistry along with population oral health needs. We describe the planning, implementation, recruitment, retention, and evaluation process of a dual‐masters‐degree track in pediatric dentistry and public health (MS‐MPH) in its first 5 years. We achieved necessary approvals, designed a streamlined curriculum, and have consistently recruited, retained, and graduated trainees since implementation. Graduates are working in rural areas or with underserved populations, applying their combined pediatric dentistry and public health skills. Early success has led to expansion of funded positions available.

## Introduction
Pediatric dentists play an important role in dental disease prevention, early intervention, and behavior guidance to prepare young children for life‐long oral health. Advanced programs in pediatric dentistry are accredited by the Commission on Dental Accreditation (CODA), and standards include biomedical sciences, clinical sciences, research, advocacy, and interprofessional collaboration. Programs develop curricula to teach trainees how to prevent and treat dental disease for infants and children through adolescence, including individuals with special health care needs [ ]. Some pediatric dentistry programs confer master‐level degrees, such as a Master of Science (MS) for this didactic and clinical coursework, but most award certificates only. The specialty is increasing in terms of the number of programs and graduates in the United States [ , ], largely a result of Health Resources and Services Administration (HRSA) funding Title VII programs [ ], and most programs are 2 years long.
The evolution from disease‐ or procedure‐driven health care to value‐based health care has put a spotlight on population health. An additional public health degree allows dentists a broader understanding of population‐based approaches to prevention and management of oral disease and provides tools to apply these principles and carry out programs achieving disease reduction in underserved and vulnerable populations, especially children. Master‐level programs in public health (MPH) are accredited by the Council on Education for Public Health (CEPH) [ ]. Knowledge in population health, and specifically that obtained via an MPH degree, is desired when appointing members to health agencies or hiring dental faculty in academic programs with a community outreach or disease prevention focus.
Pediatric dentistry and public health are natural partners, largely due to Medicaid's comprehensive dental coverage for children and pediatric dentistry's focus on both disease prevention and advocacy. In today's changing care landscape, where pediatric dentists are placed in community health centers and in rural practice through state and federal loan repayment programs, public health training will serve to bridge direct and population‐based pediatric oral health care. Furthermore, innovative solutions in pediatric dentistry, such as community‐based services and school‐based dental clinics, teledentistry, minimally invasive techniques, and other approaches, bring together cutting‐edge knowledge from both dental public health and pediatric dentistry.
By integrating public health principles within pediatric dentistry training, pediatric dentists will be better equipped to serve their communities. A few combined training programs exist in the United States, where pediatric dentists obtain an MPH alongside a certificate in pediatric dentistry [ , , , ], and an interview of 20 alumni of one institution found they were all using public health principles in leadership roles, research, or teaching [ ]. Here we describe the planning, implementation, recruitment, retention, and evaluation process of integrating an MPH degree track into a 2‐year pediatric dentistry residency whose program already required an MS degree.
1.1

## Cross‐College Collaboration
The MS‐MPH track is co‐hosted by The Ohio State University's Colleges of Public Health and Dentistry. Developing the Master of Science‐Master of Public Health (MS‐MPH) dual‐masters‐degree track involved a planning year, during which pediatric dentistry faculty, with the support of administration and other stakeholders, collaborated with the MPH‐Program for Experienced Professionals (PEP). The university's MPH‐PEP emphasizes population health management “to develop leaders in public health, health care, and community health who can work across sectors to create real change in the populations they serve.” [ ] It is designed for working professionals, which includes residents, fellows, and attending clinicians spanning the health sciences, such as dental hygienists and dentists. This fully online graduate degree is completed via synchronous and asynchronous distance coursework. We chose MPH‐PEP because of its structure, purpose, and feasibility of integration into the existing MS‐track pediatric dentistry residency.

1.2

## Overlapping Coursework Identified to Optimize Dual‐Masters‐Degree Curriculum and Schedule
The MS‐only program requires 65 credit h and the MPH‐only program requires 42 credit h, but combined they requires only 93.5 total credit hours. Dual‐masters‐track trainees learn biostatistics through the College of Public Health (COPH) separate from their MS cohort, along with the other MPH core content required for accreditation. Specific MS courses (i.e., research, advocacy) containing public health content were selected as MPH electives. By sequencing and adjusting coursework, trainees avoid complicated waivers to approve credit hour overload. Additionally for the MPH, they complete an applied practice experience and an integrated learning experience, where they apply theoretical learning to achieve practical goals and skills under the supervision of a preceptor and faculty advisor. Examples include quality improvement projects, grant proposals, research projects, training modules with dissemination, policy white papers, and community needs assessments. These can supplement or enhance their research projects or other MS coursework. See Supporting Information Appendix for curriculum details.

1.3

## Combined Research Project
The traditional MS thesis is credited as the MPH capstone project so long as it includes MPH competencies. It is also supplemented by activities of the advanced practicum experience. In doing so, the traditional pediatric dentistry thesis project entails a practical application to public health programming.

1.4

## Additional Protected Educational Time With Extended Program Length
Trainees pursuing the MS‐MPH track are given additional protected educational time compared to other pediatric dental trainees (on average, an additional half workday each week). This results in extending their pediatric dentistry residency program by 6–8 weeks, which makes the final program length between 25 and 26 months.

1.5

## Institutional and Accreditation Body Approval
The university evaluated the proposal (see Supporting Information Appendix ) and approved this dual‐masters‐degree program. CODA approved the new dual‐masters‐degree offering and the pediatric dentistry program's need for a temporary increase in trainee complement during the last 6–8 weeks of training. The COPH is undergoing reaccreditation, and CEPH approval is pending.

1.6

## Built‐In Faculty Support
The pediatric dentistry residency program has several MPH‐trained faculty serving as mentors. The MPH‐PEP program director (a pediatrician trained in public health) also serves as a mentor and committee member on the MS thesis/MPH capstone projects.

1.7

## Arranging Tuition and Financial Support
Funding for this process, trainee and faculty support, and MPH tuition was provided through an HRSA grant. Trainees utilize grant funding for conference travel, educational needs, or professional development.

1.8

## Evaluation
Program evaluation of dual‐masters‐degree trainees differs from the MS‐only trainees. Formal evaluation is completed each semester via surveys solely designed for the dual‐masters‐degree trainees (see Appendix ). Informal evaluation is completed through mentorship check‐ins. Additional evaluation measures include the number of pediatric dentistry applicants applying to the MS‐MPH track, the number of trainees enrolled in and graduating from this track, these trainees’ scholarship or participation in national conferences, and their future career outcomes.

1.9

## Recruitment and Retention
The dual‐masters‐degree program is highlighted on pediatric dentistry residency and application service websites. Interested applicants, who are matched into the pediatric dentistry program, are then interviewed by a group of three faculty to select the dual‐masters‐degree trainees. The trainee must apply to the MS program through the university's Graduate School and the MPH program through the COPH.

1.10

## Board Eligibility
Graduates become board‐eligible only in pediatric dentistry. They must complete a dental public health residency, of which the MPH is a first step or prerequisite, to become board‐eligible in dental public health.

## Summary
Three‐quarters (74.6%) of pediatric dentists work in a private practice dental setting [ ]; there is a current national shortage of pediatric dental faculty, and our state has been without a Director of Oral Health for several years. The competencies of an MPH program provide the knowledge instrumental for the future dental workforce [ ]. Dentists with MPH degrees are more likely to enter careers in academics and dental public health. Medicine has long recognized the need to streamline medical and public health education to develop population‐level expertise for these reasons. Dentistry has been less progressive in integrating public health into dental education, and we aimed to fill this gap.
Since the HRSA grant planning year, the MS‐MPH program as implemented has been successful in recruiting, retaining, and supporting students through graduation. Each year since inception, between 6 and 13 pediatric dentistry applicants have indicated interest in the dual‐masters‐degree program. Consistent trainee survey results indicate they are generally pleased with their public health coursework and faculty support, and they find relevance to their future career plans. Trainee suggestions have been used to improve course sequencing and semester planning.
The addition and integration of the MPH degree have had a snowball effect to improve public health awareness and knowledge in the residency program through peer teaching and learning. The residency maintains a weekly seminar series covering contemporary topics related to pediatric dentistry, including public health. This venue has allowed MS‐MPH trainees to present and discuss dental public health topics, broader health systems and health reform issues, and their MS thesis/MPH capstone projects.
Following graduation, we expect our MS‐MPH graduates to make considerable impact on oral health system transformation to improve access, quality, and cost‐effectiveness of oral health care. All graduates so far have presented their research projects at national conferences and published in, or submitted to, peer‐reviewed journals. The three graduates are either practicing pediatric dentistry in an urban hospital clinic fulfilling an NHSC service obligation or in a rural area and treating children with Medicaid. At this early stage, none have pursued a dental public health residency program, but with an MPH, they would now be eligible. We cannot yet evaluate the career paths and leadership positions of dual‐masters‐degree graduates; however, future evaluations will determine if the MS‐MPH graduates pursue career paths and leadership positions within community, state, or national professional organizations different from those of the MS‐only graduates.
More dual‐trained pediatric dentists in public health will lead to a workforce more prepared to tackle oral health on a larger, population level. We had initially budgeted and planned for one dual‐masters‐degree trainee per class, but due to trainee interest, faculty confidence in our recruitment and retention processes, and budget and process feasibility, we expanded to two incoming dual‐masters‐degree trainees for the Class of 2026 and also support additional trainees wanting to begin MPH coursework intra‐residency.
Positively, CODA and educational institutions are emphasizing population health competencies in preparing the future workforce, and pediatric training programs are noticing synergy between their clinical training programs and public health programs, finding ways to educate students in both [ , , , , ]. Providing dual‐track programs in various geographic areas and on different timelines allows trainees more access to these curricula and leads to broader geographic distribution of graduates.
As our training program is a hybrid program between a dental school and a large children's hospital that cares for the socially and medically disadvantaged and that is associated with an accountable care organization that contracts with Medicaid plans, the potential exists for these dual‐masters‐degree trainees to test and implement real‐world health system solutions that can improve population oral health, even during their training program. Other areas of future exploration include examining the existing competencies and accreditation standards from public health and pediatric dentistry programs to develop a version for MS‐MPH programs, to later be evaluated and validated.

3

## Conclusion
Robust public health training can be integrated into a didactically and clinically rigorous pediatric dentistry training program. Other advanced dental specialty programs can use our process and seek similar public health education routes for their trainees. Our program is the first of its kind to offer an MS‐MPH track within a 2‐year period, producing graduates who understand the scientific basis of dentistry along with population oral health needs and who are working in settings that utilize both aspects of their training. The early success has led to expanding the number of funded positions available. Our future research will evaluate these trainees’ long‐term workforce outcomes, their participation in population‐based public health activities, and their impact on population oral health.
