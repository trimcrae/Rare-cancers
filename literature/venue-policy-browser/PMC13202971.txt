## Aim:
This study was to synthesize qualitative evidence to identify the core competencies essential for nursing practice in earthquake settings.

## Methods:
A meta-synthesis was conducted to integrate findings from qualitative studies exploring nurses’ competencies during earthquake responses. Peer-reviewed English-language articles were identified through searches in four electronic databases. Studies employing qualitative methodologies were included, and thematic synthesis was used to analyze the data. 

## Results:
Seven core competency-related domains emerged from 35 included reports: (1) psychological resilience and emotional regulation, (2) adaptability and problem-solving, (3) clinical expertise under resource-constrained conditions, (4) effective communication and teamwork, (5) leadership and coordination, (6) knowledge of disaster-specific protocols and practices, and (7) cultural competence and language proficiency. These competencies were illustrated through first-hand accounts of nurses engaged in earthquake response. 

## Conclusion:
This meta-synthesis highlights the critical role of nurses in disasters, emphasizing the importance of preparedness, adaptability, and resilience. Findings inform targeted education, training, and policies to strengthen nursing responses to earthquake emergencies.

## What is already known on this topic?
Nurses are frontline responders during earthquake disasters, playing a critical role in patient care and disaster relief.
Previous studies have described the challenges faced by nurses in disaster settings, but the specific competencies required for effective earthquake response remain underexplored.

## What this study adds on this topic?
This study synthesizes qualitative evidence to identify seven core competency domains essential for nursing practice in earthquake response, including psychological resilience, adaptability, clinical expertise under constraints, and leadership.
The findings provide an evidence-based framework to guide the development of disaster-specific nursing education, training, and policy initiatives.

## Introduction
Earthquakes are among the most devastating natural disasters, characterized by their sudden onset, widespread destruction, and long-term socio-economic and health impacts. According to the United Nations’ 20-Year Review, earthquakes emerged as one of the most devastating types of natural disasters, both in terms of human and economic loss. Over the two-decade period, 563 earthquakes, including those that triggered tsunamis, were responsible for 56% of all disaster-related fatalities, resulting in the loss of 747,234 lives ( ). The increasing frequency and severity of earthquakes worldwide have underscored the importance of disaster preparedness in healthcare systems ( ). Nurses, as the largest and most widely distributed group of health professionals, are central to disaster response. In earthquake scenarios, they operate under extreme conditions, providing immediate care, coordinating relief efforts, and supporting affected communities, often with limited resources.
The International Council of Nurses (ICN) Framework of Disaster Nursing Competencies Version 2.0 provides a foundational structure for disaster nursing across three progressive levels: generalist (Level 1), designated disaster responders (Level 2), and deployable disaster experts (Level 3). However, while comprehensive, the framework lacks the granularity needed to address disaster-specific competencies, such as those required in earthquake settings. Its eight domains, ranging from preparedness and communication to intervention, recovery, and ethical practice, offer a strong basis, but further adaptation is needed to tailor competencies to the unique challenges posed by earthquakes (International Council of Nurses, ).
Unlike hurricanes or floods, which may allow for some degree of preparation and warning, earthquakes strike suddenly and without warning. This unpredictability can lead to widespread panic, infrastructure collapse, and mass casualties, creating a chaotic and high-pressure environment for healthcare responders. Key challenges in earthquake response include significant infrastructure damage that compromises hospitals and clinics, limiting access to essential medical supplies and equipment; mass casualties that overwhelm healthcare systems and demand rapid triage and prioritization of care; ongoing aftershocks that endanger both responders and survivors, complicating rescue and relief operations; and widespread psychological trauma affecting both victims and healthcare professionals, underscoring the need for robust mental health support and emotional resilience ( ; ; ; ). These multifaceted and evolving challenges necessitate that healthcare professionals, particularly nurses, develop specialized competencies beyond general disaster nursing principles to respond effectively in high-pressure, resource-constrained environments ( ).
Despite the critical role of nurses in earthquake response, research specifically examining the competencies required in these contexts remains limited. Existing disaster nursing studies often offer broad, generalized frameworks that do not fully capture the unique clinical, ethical, and operational challenges posed by earthquake scenarios. This gap hinders the development of targeted training, preparedness initiatives, and policy frameworks tailored to the realities of earthquake response. To address this need, the present study systematically synthesizes qualitative findings from existing research, providing evidence to inform education, policy, and practice in earthquake nursing.
This study aimed to identify the essential competencies required of nurses to respond effectively to earthquake-related emergencies by synthesizing qualitative experiential evidence.

## Methods

## Study Design
This study employed a meta-synthesis approach ( ) to analyze qualitative data from peer-reviewed articles containing firsthand accounts of nurses who have responded to earthquake emergencies. This report adheres to the Enhancing Transparency in Reporting the Synthesis of Qualitative Research guidelines ( ).

## Literature Search
The primary author developed the search strategy, with additional guidance from a medical librarian. The initial search was conducted on January 8, 2025, followed by an updated search on April 26, 2025. To minimize the risk of duplicating existing work, a preliminary search was performed on PROSPERO and the Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews to identify the current or ongoing systematic reviews on the topic.
Data were collected from two electronic databases, including PubMed, CINAHL, APA PsycArticles, and ERIC, using key terms such as “nurse,” “earthquake response,” “earthquake,” and “qualitative research.” Inclusion criteria focused on studies published in English that examined nurses’ experiences in earthquake emergencies and provided qualitative data. Studies were excluded if they addressed general disaster nursing without a specific focus on earthquakes. Boolean operators (AND, OR) were used to refine the search, and filters were applied to select relevant study types. The databases were searched from their inception to the search date, with no time restrictions to ensure comprehensive coverage. Additionally, the reference lists of all selected studies were reviewed to identify further relevant studies. A complete search string was provided as a supplementary file.

## Study Selection
After conducting the systematic literature search, all identified citations were uploaded into EndNote v.20 (Clarivate Analytics, PA, USA), and duplicates were removed. The primary author initially reviewed the titles and abstracts to assess their relevance to nursing experiences during earthquakes. Subsequently, the primary author carefully reviewed the full texts of the selected studies, providing brief justifications for exclusions, which were then verified by the second author. In cases of disagreement, the primary and second authors engaged in discussions to resolve the issue. Throughout the process, an experienced qualitative research expert was available for consultation to ensure methodological consistency and rigor. A random sample of 20% of excluded studies was selected for further review by the primary author. To randomly select the sample, a list of excluded studies was created, each assigned a unique number. The RAND() function in Excel was then used to generate random numbers for each study. The list was sorted by random numbers, and the top 20% was selected. This random sampling method ensured a representative and unbiased review of the exclusion process.

## Quality Evaluation
Given the interpretive nature of this qualitative synthesis, a formal quality appraisal was not undertaken. The emphasis was placed on capturing diverse experiential insights rather than assessing methodological rigor. Rigid exclusion based on quality criteria may overlook valuable contextual information, especially in under-researched areas like disaster nursing. Therefore, inclusion was based on the richness of textual data describing nursing experiences and their contributions, rather than study quality.

## Data Extraction
Two tables were employed to systematically record the extracted data, organized into two main categories: the first table presented Citation Details, including author, year, country, sample characteristics, methods, and key findings, while the second table contained citation and quotations, featuring relevant citations and participant quotations from each included study. The data extraction process adhered to a two-step quality assurance procedure: the second author performed the initial extraction, and the primary author verified its accuracy.

## Data Analysis
An interpretive qualitative meta-synthesis was conducted to explore the core domains of nursing competence in earthquake response. Given the diversity of included qualitative studies and the aim of generating an overarching understanding of key competency areas, a thematic synthesis approach ( ) was employed. This method involves line-by-line coding of study findings, the development of descriptive themes, and the generation of analytical themes that capture higher-order patterns across studies. In this study, thematic synthesis was applied within an interpretive framework, emphasizing the identification and interpretation of major recurring categories rather than detailed sub-themes. This approach allowed for the integration of findings across diverse contexts while preserving interpretive depth, ensuring that the resulting synthesis reflects the conceptual foundations of nursing competence in earthquake settings.

## Results

## Study Inclusion
The search results and study selection process are shown in the PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses) flow diagram ( ). A total of 217 records were retrieved from four electronic databases. After removing 49 duplicates, 168 records remained for screening. Title and abstract screening excluded 103 records. The remaining 65 full-text articles were assessed for eligibility, and 33 were excluded after detailed evaluation. This resulted in 32 studies meeting the inclusion criteria. An additional three studies were identified through citation tracking and hand-searching, yielding a final total of 35 included studies.

## Characteristics of Included Studies
This qualitative synthesis encompassed 35 studies published between 2002 and 2024, representing a diverse geographical distribution: Türkiye (n = 12), Japan (n = 7), Iran (n = 6), China (n = 6), New Zealand (n = 2), Taiwan (n = 1), and Haiti (n = 1). Collectively, these studies involved 648 participants, with sample sizes ranging from single-case designs (e.g., et al., 2014) to larger cohorts of up to 85 individuals ( ).
A variety of data collection methods were employed, including semi-structured interviews (n = 19), in-depth interviews (n = 5), focus group discussions (n = 1), and combined interview techniques (n = 10). Analysis strategies varied across studies, reflecting the methodological heterogeneity typical of qualitative research. The most frequently applied analytic approaches were content analysis (n = 14), thematic analysis (n = 6), and phenomenological analysis (n = 6), followed by grounded theory (n = 4). Less commonly used methods included qualitative descriptive analysis, ethnography, constant comparative analysis, narrative inquiry, and hermeneutics (each n = 1). Seven themes emerged: psychological resilience and emotional management; adaptability and problem-solving; clinical expertise under constraints; effective communication and teamwork; leadership and coordination; knowledge of disaster-specific protocols; and cultural competency. ## Synthesized Results

## Psychological Resilience and Emotional Management
Psychological resilience and emotional management is the ability to maintain emotional stability, manage stress, and provide psychological support to oneself and others in high-pressure and traumatic situations. Nurses in disaster settings often encounter traumatic experiences that challenge their emotional resilience. The ability to cope with the emotional toll of these situations and continue to provide compassionate care is essential. Emotional regulation helps nurses not only to care for themselves but also to offer support to patients, colleagues, and affected communities.

## Adaptability and Problem-Solving Skills
Adaptability and problem-solving skills is the capacity to adjust to rapidly changing environments, make swift decisions, and develop innovative solutions amidst chaos and limited resources. Earthquakes are unpredictable and chaotic events, requiring nurses to adapt quickly to constantly changing conditions. Whether it’s improvising with limited resources or swiftly adjusting to shifting priorities, nurses must employ problem-solving skills to manage the complexities of disaster response.

## Clinical Expertise Under Constrained Conditions
Clinical expertise under constrained conditions is the proficiency in delivering medical care despite limited resources, equipment, and challenging environments. Disaster settings often involve shortages of medical supplies and equipment, requiring nurses to apply their clinical expertise in innovative ways. Nurses need to be flexible and resourceful, using their knowledge to deliver care efficiently even in suboptimal conditions.

## Effective Communication and Teamwork
Effective communication and teamwork is the ability to communicate clearly and collaborate effectively with multidisciplinary teams, local populations, and other stakeholders under stress. During a disaster, communication and teamwork are essential to ensure that resources are used effectively and that patient care is coordinated. Nurses must communicate clearly with diverse team members and affected populations to ensure timely and accurate care.

## Leadership and Coordination Abilities
Leadership and coordination abilities includes skills in leading teams, organizing resources, and coordinating efforts to ensure efficient and effective emergency response. Nurses often step into leadership roles during disasters, ensuring that teams function smoothly and that resources are deployed efficiently. Good leadership is essential to maintaining order and ensuring that the response is both effective and efficient.

## Knowledge of Disaster-Specific Protocols and Practices
Knowledge of disaster-specific protocols and practices is the understanding and application of standardized procedures and guidelines tailored to disaster response scenarios. Nurses need to understand and apply disaster-specific protocols to ensure that they are responding effectively and efficiently. A lack of proper knowledge can lead to confusion and inefficiencies in care delivery, so thorough preparation is crucial.

## Cultural Competency and Language Skills
Cultural competency and language skills refers to understanding and respecting cultural differences and possessing language skills to effectively communicate with diverse populations during emergencies. Nurses often work in culturally diverse environments during disasters. Effective communication, especially in terms of language and cultural sensitivity, is crucial for building trust with patients and providing effective care.

## Discussion
This study explored the essential competencies required for nurses responding to earthquake emergencies, highlighting seven interrelated domains that emerged through a thematic synthesis approach. These competencies reflect not only the technical and clinical expertise expected in disaster settings but also the psychological, interpersonal, and adaptive capacities that enable nurses to function effectively in extreme and unpredictable environments.
Psychological resilience emerged as foundational to all other competencies. Nurses described emotionally taxing experiences, including trauma, fear, and awe, highlighting the need for emotional regulation and support. Expressions such as “I had nightmares” and “I never felt so honored to be a nurse” illustrate the duality of distress and professional pride in disaster work. These findings emphasize the importance of integrating resilience training and trauma-informed support systems into disaster preparedness programs, both pre- and post-deployment. Previous research has linked psychological resilience with improved disaster nursing competence ( ), and training programs have been shown to enhance confidence and crisis management ability ( ). Integrating such strategies into disaster nursing education can strengthen both individual and team performance in emergencies.
Closely linked with resilience, adaptability, and problem-solving were evident in nurses’ ability to adjust to rapidly changing circumstances with limited resources. These competencies align with the Quality and Safety Education for Nurses (QSEN) framework, emphasizing critical thinking as essential for safe, effective care ( ). Problem-solving supports not only patient care but also interprofessional collaboration and professional role fulfillment. Structured educational programs targeting these skills are essential for preparing nurses to navigate the dynamic and high-acuity contexts of disaster response ( ; ).
Nurses reported making clinical decisions using available resources and adapting standard practices to crisis contexts, reflecting a tension between ideal professional standards and situational limitations. Disaster nursing curricula must therefore prepare nurses for real-world adaptations, emphasizing both theoretical knowledge and flexible application in resource-scarce settings.
Effective communication was critical for coordinating care in complex, multi-stakeholder environments. Challenges such as fragmented interprofessional communication impede response efficiency and resource allocation ( ). These findings reinforce previous research ( ) and align with the ICN Disaster Nursing Competencies framework, which highlights communication and coordination as core domains. Interprofessional simulations and team-based training can enhance communication skills, promoting cohesive action during disaster response.
Nurses are often expected to take on leadership roles to organize care, delegate responsibilities, and manage triage when formal systems are strained. The ICN emphasizes the nursing profession as a critical resource in disaster risk reduction and response, in line with the Sendai Framework (Aitsi-Selmi et al., ). Developing leadership competencies from undergraduate education through ongoing professional development is critical to enhancing workforce contribution and operational efficiency in emergencies.
Nurses’ ability to understand and apply “Disaster Specific Protocols and Practices” emerged as a critical factor influencing the effectiveness of emergency care. Participants who were unfamiliar with these protocols reported confusion and inefficiencies during disaster response. Broader challenges related to preparedness and planning in disaster nursing span multiple systemic and institutional levels, affecting crisis response capacities across diverse settings ( ). Beyond system-level issues such as inadequacies in pre-hospital emergency systems ( ) and insufficient coordination among stakeholders ( ), additional challenges include the unpredictable nature of disasters, disparities in emergency nurses’ preparedness, gaps in institutional readiness, and inconsistent support from leadership and colleagues ( ). Incorporating these protocols into nursing education and certification programs can strengthen system resilience and workforce readiness.
Cultural Competency and Language Skills proved vital in gaining community trust and delivering effective care. Drawing on a model, core domains of cultural competence—cultural desire, awareness, knowledge, and skills—were consistently reflected in nurses’ experiences ( ). These domains were consistently reflected in the literature, emphasizing their relevance for nurses delivering care to disaster-affected populations from diverse cultural backgrounds. The integration of global health education and cultural immersion experiences into disaster nursing training is essential. While language barriers and cultural differences posed significant obstacles, nurses were often able to collaborate successfully across professional and community boundaries.

## Study Limitations
A key limitation of this analysis is its reliance on qualitative data derived primarily from retrospective accounts of nurses, which may not fully represent the diverse realities of earthquake response across varying geographical, cultural, and resource settings. Notably, over 90% of the included studies were conducted in Asian countries, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. Furthermore, while the identified competencies are grounded in rich experiential narratives, they may not capture the full range of skills required in less documented or less researched disaster contexts. The review was also limited to studies published in English, excluding literature in other relevant languages such as Japanese, Persian, and Turkish, which are regions with high earthquake prevalence, thereby omitting potentially valuable insights.

## Conclusion and Recommendations
To prepare nurses for the increasing frequency and complexity of disasters, disaster nursing competencies should be integrated into core nursing curricula. Both undergraduate and postgraduate nursing programs should adopt the ICN Framework of Disaster Nursing Competencies and the World Health Organisation (WHO) Health Emergency and Disaster Risk Management competencies as foundational requirements rather than electives. This approach ensures that all nursing graduates possess a baseline capacity to respond to emergencies, making disaster preparedness a standard component of professional competence rather than a specialized skill.
In addition to curricular reform, simulation-based training and mental health support systems should be prioritized. High-fidelity simulations replicating chaotic, resource-limited disaster environments can enhance nurses’ adaptability, leadership, and interprofessional collaboration. Equally important is addressing the emotional impact of disaster response. Structured pre-deployment resilience training and post-deployment counseling should be standardized across institutions to safeguard nurses’ mental health and support long-term workforce readiness.
To formalize and incentivize disaster nursing as a professional pathway, the development of specialized certification and continuing education programs is critical. Certifications aligned with global standards, such as the WHO Emergency Medical Teams framework, can establish disaster nursing as a recognized subspecialty and promote lifelong learning. Coupled with accessible disaster protocol repositories and cultural competency training, these measures will ensure that nurses are clinically and contextually equipped to lead and deliver care during emergencies, both locally and globally.
This study highlights the critical role of nurses in disaster settings and the need for preparedness, adaptability, and resilience. Actionable recommendations include integrating disaster nursing competencies into core curricula, implementing simulation-based and interprofessional training, and providing structured programs to support nurses’ physical and mental well-being. These actions can strengthen workforce readiness, inform disaster response protocols, and enhance healthcare system capacity during emergencies.
