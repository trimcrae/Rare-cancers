SmartSport comprises two core modules: The CrowdVision module aims to achieve automated monitoring of facility usage. The network employs Cross-Scan Modules to perform feature scanning from four image directions to capture global spatial dependencies, and designs a multi-level feature pyramid structure to fuse high-level semantic information with low-level spatial details, enhancing perception capability for crowds at different scales. Additionally, a point query mechanism is introduced to model crowd counting as a decomposable point prediction process, providing precise counting while supporting crowd localization. The lightweight network design enables deployment on edge computing devices for real-time inference, with the module automatically capturing images at preset intervals, completing head counts, and uploading results to the management platform to form time-series usage data for each facility. The LLM-Advisor module aims to achieve intelligent generation of management recommendations. This module employs large language models as the core analysis engine, with a workflow divided into three stages: first, the data integration stage structurally organizes crowd time-series data output by the CrowdVision module with facility geographic location, surrounding population density, transportation accessibility, and supporting facilities to construct facility profiles; second, the analytical reasoning stage designs specialized prompt templates for facility management scenarios based on prompt engineering techniques, guiding the large language model to perform systematic analysis across three dimensions including usage pattern recognition (such as peak hours and periodic patterns), supply-demand diagnosis (such as facility overload or underutilization), and optimization recommendation generation (such as operating hour adjustments and new facility siting); finally, the report generation stage integrates analysis results into natural language management reports containing facility usage descriptions, problem attribution analysis, and actionable configuration optimization recommendations. Experimental results on our constructed dataset demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed SmartSport framework. The CrowdVision module achieves a crowd counting accuracy of 93.8% on test sets containing outdoor basketball courts, fitness trails, soccer fields, and other scenario types; management recommendations generated by the LLM-Advisor module received a practicality rating of 4.2 out of 5 points through blind evaluation by 12 domain experts.
The contributions of this study are as follows: (1) proposing SmartSport, a public sports facility management framework integrating computer vision and large language models, achieving a complete pipeline from automated facility usage monitoring to intelligent management recommendation generation; (2) designing CrowdVision, a crowd perception module based on density estimation, optimized for open-access sports facility scenarios and supporting low-cost deployment with continuous monitoring; (3) developing LLM-Advisor, an intelligent analysis module based on large language models that integrates multi-source heterogeneous data through prompt engineering techniques to automatically generate management reports containing problem diagnosis and configuration recommendations.

## Related work

## Public sports facility management and utilization assessment
Spatial layout optimization and utilization assessment of public sports facilities are core issues in sports public service research. Geographic Information Systems (GIS) and spatial analysis methods have been widely applied in this field. Researchers have utilized GIS spatial analysis tools based on Point of Interest (POI) data to study the spatial distribution characteristics of sports venues , or examined the spatial clustering characteristics and accessibility of public sports facilities at street and grid scales . Regarding facility layout equity assessment, existing studies have proposed spatial equity measurement methods based on multi-source urban data and GIS network analysis , or employed buffer analysis and Lorenz curves to reveal facility distribution patterns . While these studies have made significant progress in spatial layout analysis, they exhibit notable deficiencies in dynamic monitoring of actual facility usage. Current research predominantly relies on potential demand estimation based on demographic data or subjective reports from questionnaire surveys, making it difficult to obtain continuous, objective data on facility usage. Some studies have attempted to introduce intelligent monitoring approaches, with IoT sensors being applied in smart sports stadiums . However, these solutions primarily target large enclosed venues with high deployment costs, making them difficult to extend to the vast number of open public fitness facilities. The CrowdVision module proposed in this study provides a feasible solution for low-cost, continuous monitoring of open sports facilities through lightweight visual counting technology.

## Crowd counting and density estimation techniques
Crowd counting, which aims to estimate the number of people from images, is a classic task in computer vision that has been systematically reviewed in numerous surveys . CNN-based methods represent the current mainstream framework, with key techniques including visual attention mechanisms, dilated convolutions, and spatial pyramid pooling, which have significantly improved density map estimation quality. Representative works such as SC2Net employ pyramid dilated convolutions for scale-aware counting, HRANet proposes hierarchical region-aware networks for handling complex scenes, and BL enhances counting robustness through Bayesian loss functions. Transformer architectures compensate for CNN’s limitations in global modeling through self-attention mechanisms: CCTrans first introduced Transformers to crowd counting, TransCrowd achieved pure Transformer-based weakly supervised counting. State Space Models (SSM) represent an emerging paradigm in visual modeling. Vision Mamba first introduced bidirectional SSM into visual representation learning, achieving global context modeling while maintaining linear complexity, with inference efficiency significantly superior to Transformers. VMamba further explored scanning strategies adapted to 2D visual data, while LocalMamba focuses on local dependency modeling. However, SSM applications in crowd counting remain in the exploratory stage. The CrowdVision module in this study introduces visual state space models to sports facility scenarios, combining point query mechanisms to achieve efficient and accurate crowd counting and localization.

## Large language models in urban management
Large Language Models (LLMs), with their powerful natural language understanding and generation capabilities, are becoming new intelligent engines for urban management and decision support , with primary applications in planning management, public services, and transportation mobility. In urban activity management, UrbanLLM decomposes urban queries into subtasks and invokes spatiotemporal AI models to generate comprehensive responses, significantly outperforming general LLMs on complex planning problems. Participatory urban planning research generates planning schemes meeting diverse needs through LLM multi-agent collaboration frameworks. PlanGPT integrates local databases to support policy drafting and zoning assessment, while agents such as TrafficGPT enable interactive analysis and report generation for traffic data. The development of multimodal LLMs has further expanded application boundaries, with vision-language models demonstrating capabilities for automated analysis and report writing in fields such as medical report generation . Regarding prompt engineering, research has found that structured prompt formats can significantly improve LLM response quality , InsightLens adopts the ReAct paradigm for data analysis planning and execution, and prompt engineering techniques for time-series data can effectively perform pattern recognition and anomaly detection , . Although LLMs are widely applied in urban management, existing work primarily focuses on macro-level planning. The LLM-Advisor module in this study applies LLMs to sports facility management scenarios, integrating visual counting data with multi-source geographic information through specialized prompt templates to achieve automated generation of usage pattern recognition, supply-demand diagnosis, and configuration recommendations. More broadly, LLMs have shown growing potential in time series and spatio-temporal data analysis. Cross-modality alignment techniques have been explored to bridge numerical time series and LLM token spaces , , while continuous learning frameworks address catastrophic forgetting in streaming spatio-temporal prediction  . Recent reviews further systematize spatio-temporal foundation model pipelines  , and efficient dataset condensation methods reduce the cost of large-scale temporal data management  . These advances provide broader methodological context for our LLM-Advisor module, which integrates crowd time-series data with multi-source contextual information for facility management decision-making.

## CrowdVision module
The CrowdVision module aims to achieve automated monitoring of facility usage, with core challenges including handling wide fields of view, uneven crowd distributions, and complex lighting variations in open-access scenarios. To address these challenges, we propose a crowd counting network based on a lightweight visual state space model, which achieves global context modeling through selective state space mechanisms while maintaining linear computational complexity, making it better suited for the characteristics of sports facility scenarios.

Unlike standard state space models that use fixed parameters, we introduce a selective mechanism that allows parameters to dynamically adjust based on input content. This mechanism enables the model to perceive local density features, enhancing information propagation in dense regions to model complex spatial relationships, while simplifying state updates in sparse regions to improve computational efficiency. ## Point query-driven crowd localization and counting
Traditional density estimation methods achieve crowd counting through density map regression, but have two key limitations: first, the supervision signal of density maps is sparse, and the superposition of Gaussian kernels easily leads to confusion of responses from adjacent targets; second, they only provide region-level counting results and cannot support precise individual localization. Meanwhile, Transformer-based methods suffer from quadratic computational complexity, making real-time edge deployment challenging. To address these issues, we propose a point query-driven crowd detection paradigm that models crowd counting as a combination of point detection tasks, simultaneously achieving counting and localization within a unified framework while maintaining computational efficiency.
Inspired by the end-to-end object detection method DETR, we design a set of learnable point query vectors , where N is the preset maximum number of queries. Each query vector serves as a detection slot, specifically responsible for detecting one potential person location in the image. Unlike object queries in object detection that need to predict bounding boxes, point queries are more lightweight, requiring only prediction of 2D coordinates and existence confidence, significantly reducing prediction complexity. ## Multi-task joint optimization strategy
The CrowdVision module needs to simultaneously optimize two interrelated task objectives: crowd counting and location localization. Directly combining two types of loss functions may lead to training instability or one task dominating the optimization process. To address this, we design a multi-task joint optimization strategy with a three-level loss function, where each level targets a distinct optimization objective: (1) global counting consistency to ensure accurate total count estimation, (2) local position regression to achieve precise individual localization, and (3) confidence classification to enable reliable foreground-background discrimination. This hierarchical design provides complementary supervision signals at different granularities, ensuring balanced learning of both counting and localization tasks while maintaining training stability. The strategy establishes correspondences between predictions and annotations through optimal bipartite matching, and employs adaptive weight mechanisms to balance the three loss components.

## LLM-Advisor module
The LLM-Advisor module employs large language models as the core analysis engine, integrating crowd counting data with multi-source heterogeneous data in a structured manner, and generating management reports containing problem diagnosis and configuration recommendations through specially designed prompt engineering techniques. As illustrated in Fig.  , the module consists of four key components: multi-source data integration, prompt engineering framework, LLM analysis engine, and RAG-based knowledge base enhancement. 

## Multi-source data integration
To enable large language models to perform effective analysis and decision-making, the system needs to integrate multi-source heterogeneous data and construct structured facility profiles. Specifically, input data includes the following four dimensions: (1) Crowd time-series data: Real-time headcount statistics and historical usage records from the CrowdVision module, forming time-series features after anomaly detection and smoothing processing; (2) Geospatial information: Facility geographic location, POI density of surrounding residential/commercial/transportation facilities, and transportation accessibility indicators; (3) Demographic characteristics: Service population size and age structure distribution estimated based on census and mobile signaling data; (4) Facility attribute information: Basic attributes such as facility type, site area, equipment configuration, and operating hours. The above multi-dimensional data is standardized and input to the large language model for analysis in the form of structured text.

## Prompt engineering design
To guide large language models in generating high-quality analysis reports, we design a multi-stage reasoning prompt framework based on Chain-of-Thought (CoT). This framework integrates role-playing, step-by-step reasoning instructions, structured output specifications, and few-shot examples to guide the model in conducting systematic facility operation analysis. ## Knowledge base enhancement
To improve the professionalism and accuracy of analysis, we construct a sports facility management knowledge base as an external knowledge source for Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG). The knowledge base contains three types of content: (1) Best practice case library: Collects excellent facility management cases from domestic and international sources, recording problem contexts, measures taken, and implementation effects; (2) Policy and regulation library: Integrates policy documents, management specifications, and applicable conditions related to sports facilities; (3) Technical standards library: Compiles construction standards, operational specifications, and evaluation indicators for various types of facilities. When generating analysis reports, the system retrieves relevant knowledge entries based on current query semantics and injects them as context into the prompt, making the large language model’s output more professional and actionable.

## System deployment
Considering the reality that sports facilities are widely distributed with varying network conditions, this system adopts an edge-cloud collaborative deployment architecture. On the edge side, each facility is configured with an embedded computing node responsible for video capture and real-time crowd counting inference, uploading counting results rather than raw video to the cloud, which both reduces bandwidth requirements and ensures privacy security. The CrowdVision model, after quantization and operator optimization, is deployed on edge devices with single-frame inference latency controlled within 50ms. On the cloud side, the LLM-Advisor module integrates data reported from each facility for analysis and decision-making, generates management reports, and distributes them to management terminals. The system uses message queues to implement asynchronous communication between edge nodes and the cloud, ensuring no data loss during network fluctuations. Additionally, the system follows the data minimization principle, with image data processed only on the edge side and only anonymized statistical data stored in the cloud, complying with relevant data protection regulations.

## Experiments

## Datasets

## Crowd counting evaluation datasets
We evaluate the CrowdVision module on two public benchmark datasets: ShanghaiTech Part B  (716 images, averaging 123 persons per image) and JHU-CROWD++  . The former’s medium-to-low density scenarios align well with real sports facility usage; the latter covers diverse scenes including sports venues and parks. We supplement JHU-CROWD++ with sports venue images to construct the JHU-Sports dataset (856 images). Evaluation metrics include MAE, RMSE, and Counting Accuracy (CA).

## SmartSport-facility dataset
To evaluate the overall efficacy of the SmartSport framework, we construct the SmartSport-Facility dataset. For visual data, we select 15 public sports facilities in a city, collecting 52,400 images from March to August 2024, with 6,200 images independently annotated by 3 annotators (Fleiss’ Kappa=0.85). We also collect multi-source auxiliary data including geographic location, POI distribution, and meteorological information. Additionally, 12 domain experts conduct blind evaluations of 256 LLM-generated management reports.

## Implementation details

## CrowdVision module
Network configuration: The backbone network adopts ResNet-18 pretrained on ImageNet, with dilation rates of dilated convolutions set to . ## LLM-Advisor module
Model selection: We evaluated five large language models as the analysis engine: GPT-4o, DeepSeek-R1, Qwen-Plus, LLaMA-4, and Claude Opus 4.1. The inference temperature is uniformly set to 0.3 to balance output diversity and stability, with a maximum output token count of 4,096.
Prompt configuration: We adopt the chain-of-thought prompt template described in the previous section, retrieving the top-5 relevant cases from the knowledge base as context for each analysis. To improve output consistency, we employ a self-consistency strategy with 5 sampling iterations per query, determining the final recommendation through majority voting.
Knowledge base construction: The knowledge base is built on FAISS vector indexing, with text embeddings generated using the text-embedding-3-large model (dimension 3,072). The knowledge base contains: 156 best practice cases, 89 policy and regulation documents, and 42 technical standard files, which are chunked into 2,847 knowledge fragments. Retrieval employs cosine similarity matching with a similarity threshold of 0.75.
System integration: Edge nodes communicate with the cloud through Apache Kafka message queues, with message serialization using Protocol Buffers. The cloud service is built on the FastAPI framework, supporting asynchronous request processing. Data storage utilizes InfluxDB (for storing crowd statistics) as a time-series database and PostgreSQL (for storing facility attributes and analysis reports). The system is deployed on Alibaba Cloud, with the LLM-Advisor module configured on a compute instance with 8 CPU cores and 32GB memory.

## Main experimental results and analysis

## Crowd counting performance evaluation
We evaluate the performance of the CrowdVision module on the ShanghaiTech Part B and JHU-Sports datasets, comparing against representative methods from 2020–2024. 
As shown in Tables  and  , CrowdVision achieves the best performance on both datasets. On ShanghaiTech Part B, CrowdVision achieves an MAE of 5.2 and a CA of 92.4%; on JHU-Sports, it achieves an MAE of 9.1 and a CA of 91.5%. Compared to the second-best method APGCC, the MAE is reduced by 5.5% and 7.1% on the two datasets, respectively. This advantage is primarily attributed to: (1) the Visual State Space Module enabling efficient global context modeling through its selective mechanism; (2) the cross-directional scanning strategy aggregating spatial information from four directions; and (3) the point query mechanism decomposing the counting task into independent point predictions, avoiding the Gaussian kernel overlap problem in density map regression.
Furthermore, we validate the model’s practical application effectiveness on the self-constructed SmartSport-Facility dataset, with results shown in Table  . 
This dataset features scenarios closer to real sports facility applications with moderate crowd density (averaging 7.7 persons per image). CrowdVision achieves a CA of 93.8%, consistent with the results reported in the abstract, demonstrating that the model possesses good generalization capability in practical sports facility scenarios.

To evaluate the practical deployment feasibility, we compare the computational efficiency of CrowdVision with representative methods. All experiments are conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU with input resolution of . Table  presents the comparison results.

## LLM-Advisor module evaluation
Table  presents the human evaluation results of different large language models on the management report generation task. Twelve domain experts conducted blind evaluations of 256 reports on a 5-point scale across four dimensions. 
As shown in Table  , Claude Opus 4.1 achieves the highest scores across all evaluation dimensions, with an average score of 4.20. GPT-4o ranks second with 4.14 points, performing close to Claude Opus 4.1 in information completeness and analytical logic. DeepSeek-R1, as a domestic model, demonstrates outstanding performance, particularly achieving 4.15 in the analytical logic dimension, exhibiting strong reasoning capabilities. Among open-source models, Qwen-Plus outperforms LLaMA-4 overall but still shows a notable gap compared to closed-source models.
Further analysis of scores across dimensions reveals that “Recommendation Feasibility” is the lowest-scoring dimension for all models, reflecting that large language models still have room for improvement in generating specific actionable recommendations. This may be related to the models’ lack of specialized knowledge in the sports facility management domain. After introducing the RAG knowledge base, the average improvement across all models in the “Professional Standardization” dimension is 0.35 points, validating the effectiveness of domain knowledge injection in enhancing output professionalism. Considering both model performance and inference cost, this framework adopts Claude Opus 4.1 as the default analysis engine.
To ensure evaluation quality, we implemented a two-stage quality control mechanism for expert ratings. In the first stage, statistical anomaly detection identified ratings deviating more than (Median Absolute Deviation) from the median for each report-dimension pair. In the second stage, flagged ratings underwent LLM-assisted semantic review to detect content-rating inconsistencies and systematic biases, followed by manual confirmation by the research team. Approximately 6.5% of ratings were excluded as anomalous, while all 12 experts retained valid ratings in the final analysis.
Based on the filtered data, we assessed inter-expert consistency using established reliability metrics. The overall Krippendorff’s indicates substantial agreement according to standard guidelines. The Intraclass Correlation Coefficient ICC(2,k) values for each dimension were: Information Completeness (0.81), Analytical Logic (0.78), Recommendation Feasibility (0.73), and Professional Standardization (0.79), all indicating good inter-rater reliability. The relatively lower ICC for Recommendation Feasibility reflects the inherently subjective nature of assessing actionability across experts with different management backgrounds.

## Broader application prospects
The proposed LLM-Advisor module exhibits strong conceptual connections with modern context-aware recommendation systems (CARS). Similar to dynamic intention-aware recommendation frameworks  that model users’ preferences based on contextual features and item attributes, our LLM-Advisor incorporates real-time environmental context (crowd density, temporal patterns, weather conditions) and structured facility attributes through the RAG knowledge base to generate situation-appropriate management recommendations. This design pattern aligns with recent advances in hierarchical attention networks for context-aware user preference modeling  , group attention mechanisms for attribute-aware collaborative filtering  , and positional encoding techniques for capturing temporal dependencies in sequential recommendations  .
This conceptual alignment suggests several promising directions for extending the SmartSport framework. First, the LLM-Advisor architecture can be generalized to other smart city scenarios requiring context-aware decision support, such as traffic management, public event coordination, and emergency response systems. Second, integrating collaborative filtering mechanisms could enable cross-facility learning, where successful management strategies from similar facilities inform recommendations for new scenarios. Third, the attribute-aware design pattern can be extended to incorporate user-specific preferences, enabling personalized facility recommendations based on individual activity patterns.

## Limitations and future work
Limitations The current SmartSport framework adopts a two-stage design: the first stage employs the CrowdVision module for crowd counting, while the second stage uses the LLM-Advisor module for management report generation. Although this decoupled design reduces system complexity, it exhibits notable limitations. On one hand, CrowdVision only transmits numerical counting results to subsequent modules, while the rich semantic information contained in the original images (such as crowd density distribution, activity types, and facility occupancy conditions) is not fully utilized, resulting in information loss. On the other hand, errors between the two stages accumulate progressively, with counting deviations in the first stage directly affecting the accuracy of the second stage analysis. Furthermore, the CrowdVision module experiences decreased counting precision under extreme lighting conditions and severe occlusion scenarios, and only outputs headcount statistics without fine-grained understanding of crowd behavior patterns, limiting the depth of subsequent analysis. Moreover, the current pipeline does not explicitly propagate estimation uncertainty from the CrowdVision module to the LLM-Advisor. Future work will explore uncertainty-aware interfaces, such as providing confidence intervals alongside point estimates, to enable the LLM-Advisor to generate more calibrated recommendations when counting reliability varies.
Future work To address the aforementioned limitations, future work will focus on exploring end-to-end architectures based on multimodal large models. Specifically, vision-language models (such as GPT-4o and Qwen-VL) can be leveraged to directly process facility surveillance images, simultaneously accomplishing scene understanding, crowd analysis, and report generation within a single model, achieving end-to-end mapping from raw visual input to management recommendations. This architecture can preserve complete semantic information from images, avoid the information loss and error accumulation inherent in two-stage designs, while simplifying system deployment and maintenance workflows. Additionally, future research will explore video understanding models to capture temporal dynamic features, as well as multi-agent collaboration mechanisms to achieve regional-level facility resource coordination and allocation.

## Conclusion
This study addresses the challenges of low utilization monitoring efficiency and insufficient decision-making intelligence in public sports facility management by proposing SmartSport, an intelligent management framework integrating computer vision and large language models. The framework comprises two core modules: the CrowdVision module employs a lightweight network based on visual state space models to achieve automated monitoring of facility usage, utilizing cross-directional scanning strategies and point query mechanisms to achieve precise crowd counting and localization while maintaining computational efficiency; the LLM-Advisor module employs large language models as the analysis engine, integrating crowd time-series data with multi-source geographic information, and generating professional management reports through chain-of-thought prompting and RAG knowledge base enhancement. Experimental results demonstrate that the CrowdVision module achieves a counting accuracy of 93.8% on the self-constructed SmartSport-Facility dataset, with performance superior to existing methods on public benchmark datasets; management recommendations generated by the LLM-Advisor module received a rating of 4.2 out of 5 points through blind evaluation by 12 domain experts.
