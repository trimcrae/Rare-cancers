## Abstract No: 002

Cardiorespiratory Risk among Non-Smoker Metal Fume Exposed Industrial Workers: A Cross-Sectional Comparative Study

Ankit Viramgami, Sarang Dhatrak, Rakshit Shah, Ankit Sheth

Introduction: Workers are exposed to metal fumes through inhalation routes during various metal-cutting operations. Inhalation of heavy metal fumes enhances free radicals and oxidative stress in the biological system, resulting in local and systemic inflammatory responses. Long-term exposure may impair pulmonary function and enhance the risk of cardiovascular disease risk. The study aims to study and compare the cardio-respiratory risk among fume-exposed and un-exposed controls.
Methodology: The present cross-sectional study involved 222 male non-smoker participants, 190 exposed (metal cutters) & 32 age-matched unexposed control (admin staff). Relevant socio-demographic, occupational, and medical history was reported on the pre-tested standardized questionnaire. Pulmonary function test performed following American Thoracic Society (ATS)/European Respiratory Society (ERS) guidelines. Risk for cardiovascular events assessed through QRISK3 score system. Data was analysed with SPSS version 26.0.
Results: The prevalence of cough (15.8%) and dyspnoea (37.4%) were higher among metal cutters in comparison to controls (3.1% & 12.5%, respectively). Compromised pulmonary function test parameters were observed among 17.9% of the metal cutters, the same observed only among 6.2% of the controls. Metal cutters were observed with significantly reduced air conduction parameters (FEV1/FVC & FEF25-75%) in comparison to control (p < 0.05). Observed a significant difference in relative risk (1.52 v/s 1.07) for ischemic heart disease between metal cutters and control groups (p < 0.05).
Discussion and Conclusion: The study warrants an urgent need for workplace intervention to reduce the risk of metal fume exposure among metal cutters. Also, it advocates for detailed cardiorespiratory evaluation during occupational health check-ups for early diagnosis of illness.

Smoking On-Duty, Ignoring the Safety, Mishaps Occurred Deadly: An Accident Analysis and Case Study

Hariraj Sellamuthu

Introduction: The informal unorganised sectors encounter serious hazards that might cause mishaps, inability, and even long-term health problems. The risk of accidental fires is greatly increased when a workman smoking in the workplace. Smoking-related fires not only put lives in threat but also seriously damage property and hamper the business. Smoking at work is the hazard in all aspect equally, which maybe health, safety or environment and hence should not be under rated.
Case Study: A 26-year-old, painter by occupation. He was smoking a cigarette while working and unclosed paint thinner tin kept above his height, in high shelf, felt on him accidentally. This a case of Deadly Accidental fire with smoking a cigarette at work, where he was succumbed after 9 days. Burning cigarette produce heat of about 400 degrees Celsius and it will increase to 900 degrees Celsius while puff. Also, cigarette contains chemicals, which keep them alighted the fire.
Discussion and Conclusion: Heat, fuel and oxygen are the parts of fire triangle. Flash burns produce rapid combustion, when it has contact with heat source and flammable materials. At least 70 of them are proven carcinogens. Even if they do not smoke themselves, workers who are exposed to second-hand smoke are more likely to experience heart problems, lung cancer, and respiratory problems. Also, over time, smoking breaks might accumulate and result in a large loss of productivity. Smokers tend to take longer breaks than non-smokers do on average, which adds up to many hours of missed work every week. ## Abstract No: 004

A Study on the Impact of Lean Six Sigma Implementation on Work Environment in a Chemical Process Shop

G. S. Swaminathan

Introduction: The study was conducted in a Chemical process shop engaged in electroplating operations. The hazards under study are alkali dust, hydrochloric acid, phosphoric acid, sulphuric acid mists, chromium, zinc and ammonia in selected work stations. Process and engineering control measures were implemented after conducting Lean Six Sigma projects by production team. Another set of industrial hygiene study was conducted with the same methodology after 12 – 15 months after the implementations of the Lean Six Sigma methodologies. Aim: The object of the study is to assess the work room air quality by static sampling and the distribution of these hazards in the plant before and after implementation of Lean six sigma studies and its effectiveness.
Methodology: A cross-sectional study was conducted in each sub locations of the main processes. Convenient sampling method was adopted to collect 15 static samples in each sub processes in two different conditions. The work exposures were compared for compliance with the occupational standards and for the effectiveness of the implemented Lean Six Sigma measures. Statistical Analysis: Plant wise and operation-wise hazard distribution analysis was done on the compiled data. The data was analysed by using the IBM-SPSS version-20 and the Mini-Tab Version-16 and the results were tabulated using p < 0.05 as statistically significant. Paired t tests, Chi Square tests and Pearson’s correlation were performed to establish the effectiveness.
Results: For all the measured hazards under study in two different conditions, Correlation and association analysis were done and it is found to be at 5% level of significance and 95% confidence interval level.
Discussion and Conclusion: This study has demonstrated that the chemical air borne factors were varying based on the process, operational conditions and control mechanisms. It is concluded that there is a significant level of association (p < 0.05) before and after the implemented Lean Six Sigma measures and it is found to be effective to mitigate the hazard exposures.

## Abstract No: 009

Advancing Cardiovascular Health Management through Proactive Strategies during employees Health assessment in a Large Petrochemical Plant.

Aditya Paliwal, Nikunj Desai

Introduction: Work can promote health, yet many individuals develop cardiovascular disease (CVD) during their working lives. Occupational cardiology aims to prevent and manage these risks. This study focuses on identifying high-risk cardiac cases during periodic health checkups and implementing management strategies to reduce morbidity and mortality among employees in a large petrochemical plant.
Methodology: A cross-sectional study of 455 employees was conducted from April 2023 to March 2024, targeting individuals aged 40 to 58 years working in Office and Plant. The cardiac screening program utilized the Framingham Risk Score (2018 edition), incorporating factors such as gender, age, total cholesterol, HDL cholesterol, systolic blood pressure, and smoking status. Additional evaluations included medical history, obesity, blood glucose levels, electrocardiogram, treadmill testing, and stress questionnaires. Key interventions involved personalized counselling at healthcare facilities, lifestyle management clinics, and the use of digital tools for ongoing support. The definitive treatment of treadmill high risk (from Identified TMT positive cases) cases was done by cardiac surgical interventions.
Results: Out of 455 employees screened, 45% were over 40 years of age, 16% were diagnosed with lifestyle- related diseases, and 22% had a BMI greater than 28. Definitive treatment was provided to 1.6% of participants who tested positive on the treadmill test. The implementation of wellness interventions, the establishment of a Lifestyle Management Clinic at OHC, stringent PME follow-up, Department wise shop floor interaction, Family counselling, Dietician and Physician consultation at OHC enabled the early identification of cardiovascular disease before the onset of symptoms, significantly reducing morbidity and mortality within the identified group.
Discussion and Conclusion: The results from this focused cardiac screening during periodic medical examinations underscore the value of early detection and intervention. Identifying high-risk individuals before the manifestation of cardiovascular symptoms has proven critical in reducing long-term health risks. The incorporation of wellness programs and lifestyle management clinics, coupled with regular follow-ups, has contributed to a proactive approach in managing cardiovascular health, emphasizing the importance of targeted screening in occupational health settings.

## Abstract No: 010

Effectiveness of HEART Score in Patients with Chest Pain in the Emergency Department

Mitul Mandaliya, Bhavesh Khodadiya, R. Rajesh

Introduction: Chest pain is one of the most common & potentially serious presenting complaints for adult emergency department (ED) visits. The challenge of acute coronary syndrome (ACS) identification with appropriate disposition is quite significant. Many of these patients are low risk and can be managed non-urgently in the outpatient environment; other patients, however, are intermediate to high risk for ACS and should be managed more aggressively, likely with inpatient admission and cardiology consultation. The HEART (History, Electrocardiogram, Age, Risk factors, and initial Troponin) score is an easy-to-apply instrument to stratify patients with chest pain according to their short-term risk for major adverse cardiac events (MACEs), The HEART score was developed in the Netherlands in 2008 by Six, Backus, and Kelder & It is also validated in Indian setups. The study aims to measure the effectiveness of HEART score on patient outcomes and use of health care resources.
Methodology: In this Retrospective Cohort Study, we assessed HEART score for all patients presenting with chest pain at emergency department. According to the severity of HEART score we advised immediate treatment & follow up with cardiologist. We did follow up for their further investigations and outcomes like major adverse cardiac events (MACE), such as myocardial infarction, need for revascularization, or cardiac death and compared that data with their score severity to check significance of the HEART score
Results: Total 365 cases in Emergency department with complaints of chest pain were evaluated with HEART score out of which 7 cases were under high-risk category, 27 cases were under moderate risk category, 331 cases were under mild risk category. All 7 cases of severe category underwent angioplasty or CABG. Out of 27 cases of moderate category, total 20 cases underwent angioplasty within 6 months & rest cases were evaluated and managed with medications. Out of 331 mild category, <1% cases required further intervention in next 2 years. All 27 above mentioned cases which underwent angioplasty were having risk factors like diabetes mellitus, hypertension or dyslipidaemia which are also a contributory factors for the adverse event & correlate with severity of HEART score.
Discussion and Conclusion: The HEART score facilitates accurate diagnostic and therapeutic choices. The HEART score is an easy, quick and reliable predictor of outcome in chest pain patients which helps in identification of risk in the undifferentiated chest pain patient, is potentially quite useful as an adjunct to physician medical decision-making. The HEART score identifies patients at low, intermediate, and high risk for short-term adverse outcome resulting from ACS.

## Abstract No: 012

Ergonomic Interventions and Work Rotation: Addressing Musculoskeletal Pain in a Manufacturing Setting

Remya Mahesh

Introduction: Musculoskeletal pain is a prevalent occupational health issue, particularly in industries requiring manual labour and repetitive tasks. At our plant, we conducted a pilot study to address the complaints of musculoskeletal pain among assembly line operators, with the goal of reducing discomfort and improving workplace well-being.
Methodology: From June 2024 onwards, we selected 44 assembly line operators for the study, based on medical records that indicated frequent complaints of musculoskeletal pain. A comprehensive assessment was conducted, including a standardized musculoskeletal pain questionnaire and an ergonomic evaluation of workstations. This helped to identify the key risk factors contributing to the discomfort. The study focused on two production lines, where operators were intermittently reporting pain in areas such as the back, shoulders, and neck, legs. Based on the assessment results, several interventions were implemented. We introduced a work rotation schedule to reduce repetitive strain. Operators were trained on maintaining proper working posture to minimize physical stress, and low-impact exercises were introduced for short breaks to improve flexibility and relieve tension. These exercises focused on stretching and posture correction. Additionally, workstations were evaluated, and minor adjustments were made to ensure proper ergonomic alignment, reducing awkward body positions.
Results: A follow-up conducted after one month showed improvements in the overall well-being of the study group. Operators reported nearly 30 percent reduction in musculoskeletal discomfort and an increased awareness of proper posture and movement. The ergonomic minor adjustments and rotation schedules were well-received and led to noticeable improvements in operator comfort. Further assessments will be conducted after an additional two-month follow-up to evaluate the results.
Discussion and Conclusion: Early results suggest that ergonomic interventions, work rotation, and posture training and exercises effectively reduce musculoskeletal pain in manufacturing environments. Regular follow-ups and ongoing evaluations will be essential to ensuring lasting improvements in worker health and productivity.

## Abstract No: 013

Prevalence of Anaemia in Crèche Babies: A Case for Early Haemoglobin Assessment

Remya Mahesh

Introduction: Anaemia is a widespread public health issue, especially affecting young children Iron deficiency is the most common cause of anaemia in children, but a wide range of inherited and acquired aetiologies must also be considered In India, over 58% of children aged 6–59 months are anaemic, according to NFHS-5 (2019–21). This study focuses on the prevalence of anaemia among children in a crèche setting and highlights the importance of early haemoglobin (Hb) assessments to prevent developmental delays, recurrent illnesses, and cognitive impairment in infants and toddlers.
Methodology: In May 2024, following a case of an 8-month-old infant with a haemoglobin level of 8.5 g/dL in our creche, we conducted Hb screenings for all 15 children aged 6 months to 5 years. Results: Through consistent follow-ups, the haemoglobin levels of affected children improved significantly. Early identification allowed timely interventions, reducing the risk of developmental delays and recurrent illnesses. This approach also helped mitigate absenteeism among working mothers, contributing to a healthier workforce.
Discussion and Conclusion: Routine haemoglobin screening in day-care and crèche settings is essential for the early detection of anaemia. Timely intervention through nutrition, medical consultation, and parent education is vital. Not only does this enhance child health outcomes, but it also promotes workforce productivity by reducing maternal absenteeism and ensuring child well-being.

## Abstract No: 015

Prevalence of Osteoporosis in Indian Women above 40 years of age in automotive industries in Pondicherry.

Kalaivani V

Introduction: Osteoporosis is a common skeletal disorder characterized by reduced bone mass and deterioration of bone tissue, leading to increased fracture risk. Indian women, particularly post-menopausal women, are at a higher risk due to nutritional deficiencies, lack of sun exposure, and hormonal changes. This study investigates the prevalence of osteoporosis among Indian women aged 40 to 52 working in an automotive industry, along with the effectiveness of supplemental calcium, vitamin D3 and nutritious diet added in the company’s canteen menu in improving bone health. Osteoporosis is a worldwide health problem with a serious social impact on women. Our Pondicherry plants are unique with its large female workforce. The aim of study is to assess the prevalence of osteoporosis in peri menopausal age groups.
Methodology: This cross-sectional study included 102 women aged 40 to 52 years working in an automotive industry. Data on anthropometric measurements, including height, weight, and body mass index (BMI), were collected for each participant. Serum calcium and vitamin D3 levels were measured to evaluate the participants’ nutritional status related to bone health. Bone mineral density (BMD) was assessed using dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA) to diagnose osteoporosis or osteopenia. Women diagnosed with low BMD were provided with calcium (500 mg/day) and vitamin D3 (1000 IU/day) supplements for six months. Follow-up evaluations were conducted to assess changes in serum calcium, vitamin D3 levels, and BMD.
Results: The prevalence of osteopenia in this population was found to be significant, with 35% of the women diagnosed with osteopenia. Serum calcium and vitamin D3 levels were below the normal range in 60% and 70% of participants, respectively. After six months of supplementation with calcium and vitamin D3, there was a notable improvement in serum calcium and vitamin D3 levels, with 80% of participants reaching normal levels. Bone mineral density showed a mild to moderate improvement in 70% of the women who had been diagnosed with osteopenia.
Discussion and Conclusion: The study highlights a high prevalence of osteopenia among Indian women in the automotive industry aged 40 to 52 years. Nutritional deficiencies, particularly in calcium and vitamin D3, were prevalent in the study group. Supplementation with calcium and vitamin D3 resulted in significant improvements in serum levels and bone health, suggesting that early detection and nutritional interventions are essential in preventing osteoporosis progression in this population.
