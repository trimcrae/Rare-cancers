## Background
Invasive meningococcal disease (IMD) is a severe but rare infection in Japan. Changes in population mobility before, during, and after the Coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic may have influenced IMD trends; however, recent epidemiological patterns in Japan remain incompletely described.

## Methods
National surveillance data from January 2017 to October 2025 were analyzed. Temporal, age-specific, and geographic patterns were evaluated across three phases: Phase 1 (2017–2019, pre-COVID-19), Phase 2 (2020–2022, pandemic period), and Phase 3 (2023–October 2025, post-pandemic period with relaxed preventive measures). The relationship between annual IMD case counts and inbound international travel was explored using Spearman’s rank correlation.

## Results
A total of 288 IMD cases were identified. Annual case numbers decreased markedly from 48 in 2019 to 1 in 2021 and subsequently increased to 66 in 2024 and 68 by October 2025. Case counts were significantly lower in Phase 2 than in Phase 1 (p = 0.0003) and significantly higher in Phase 3 than in Phase 2 (p < 0.0001). Older adults consistently accounted for the largest number of cases, and adolescents showed a clear rebound in Phase 3. Geographic patterns demonstrated substantial prefectural heterogeneity; Tokyo showed the largest proportional increase, whereas Osaka and the Kansai region did not show a corresponding increase. Importantly, no distinct short-term increase in IMD incidence was observed during the Osaka Expo 2025 period. IMD case counts and inbound international travel exhibited parallel temporal patterns over the study period (Spearman’s ρ = 1.000).

## Conclusions
IMD incidence in Japan decreased during the COVID-19 pandemic and increased again following the relaxation of public health measures. The post-pandemic rebound occurred alongside recovery of social contact and population mobility, while most cases were domestically acquired, and no event-specific surge was detected during the Osaka Expo 2025 period. These findings suggest that multiple contextual factors, including societal reopening and travel-related dynamics, may have contributed to the observed trends; however, causal relationships cannot be determined from this ecological analysis.

## Background
Invasive meningococcal disease (IMD), caused by Neisseria meningitidis, is a rapidly progressive infection associated with high mortality. It typically presents as meningitis or sepsis and can result in severe sequelae or death, even with prompt treatment. Owing to its significant public health impact, IMD is a notifiable disease in many countries, including Japan.
Globally, the epidemiology of IMD exhibits substantial regional and temporal variations, particularly in the distribution of meningococcal serogroups (A, B, C, W, and Y). In recent years, the emergence and spread of serogroup W strains in Europe and South America, which have often been linked to atypical clinical presentations and high case-fatality rates, have become an increasing concern for global health authorities [ – ].
IMD is known to spread efficiently in settings characterized by close interpersonal contact and the mixing of populations from different regions. Historical examples, such as outbreaks reported during religious pilgrimages, illustrate how population mobility can facilitate the cross-border dissemination of N. meningitidis [ ]. In Japan, an outbreak related to the World Scout Jamboree in 2015 provided a documented example of IMD occurrence during an international mass gathering, highlighting the importance of evaluating IMD epidemiological trends during similar large-scale events [ ].
During the Coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic, stringent public health measures and widespread restrictions of population movement led to marked reductions in many respiratory and droplet-transmitted infections. IMD exhibited similar decreases [ ]. A recent nationwide analysis in Japan demonstrated substantial reductions in IMD incidence, mortality, and serogroup distribution through 2023, likely reflecting decreased interpersonal contact during the pandemic period [ ]. However, these studies did not capture epidemiological changes that occurred after the relaxation of COVID-19 preventive measures.
Following the relaxation of these measures, several countries in Europe and South America reported renewed increases in IMD from late 2022 through 2023, coinciding with the recovery of social contact and the resumption of international travel [ , ]. Proposed explanations for this resurgence include increased interpersonal contact, an immunity gap created during the period of low transmission, expansion of specific serogroups or clones, and the return of mass gatherings and cross-border movement [ ]. In contrast, published reports describing post-pandemic IMD patterns in Japan remain limited, and epidemiological trends after the relaxation of COVID-19 measures have not been fully characterized.
After border restrictions were lifted, Japan experienced a rapid increase in the number of foreign visitors, alongside a recovery of domestic social contact and population movement to pre-pandemic levels. In this context, the Osaka Expo 2025, which drew more than 25 million attendees over a 6-month period from April 13 to October 13, 2025, represented a period of exceptionally high population mobility. Such periods of intensified population mixing and mobility warrant careful epidemiological evaluation from a surveillance perspective.
This study analyzed IMD surveillance data from 2017 to 2025 to evaluate epidemiological patterns following the relaxation of COVID-19 measures during a period of recovering population mobility, including the time period surrounding the Osaka Expo 2025. By examining temporal trends, age distributions, and geographic patterns, the aim was to provide an updated and comprehensive characterization of IMD epidemiology in Japan.

## Methods

## Data sources
Data on IMD cases reported from January 2017 to October 2025 were obtained from the National Epidemiological Surveillance of Infectious Diseases system in Japan. Additional case level information was accessed through the Infectious Diseases Weekly Report of the National Institute of Infectious Diseases [ ]. In Japan, IMD is classified as a Category (Class) V infectious disease requiring all-case notification, mandating reporting of every diagnosed case to the national surveillance system. Category V diseases include both all-case and sentinel-reporting conditions. All data used in this study are publicly available from official national surveillance reports; no restricted or individual-level datasets were accessed.
Available demographic variables included date of diagnosis, age group, and prefecture of residence. IMD was defined according to national reporting criteria that required compatible clinical manifestations and laboratory confirmation of N. meningitidis isolated from a normally sterile site, such as blood or cerebrospinal fluid, by either traditional culture or polymerase chain reaction. Suspected or probable clinical cases without laboratory confirmation were excluded. Population denominators for incidence rate calculations were obtained from the Statistics Bureau of Japan using mid-year population estimates [ ]. Annual numbers of foreign visitors to Japan were retrieved from the Japan National Tourism Organization and the Japan Tourism Agency [ ].

## Study phases
The study period was divided into three predefined analytical phases. Phase 1 (2017–2019) represented the pre-pandemic period with relatively stable social activity. Phase 2 (2020–2022) corresponded to the period during which nationwide COVID-19 preventive measures were implemented, including widespread mask use, restrictions on population movement, and border control policies. Phase 3 (2023–October 2025) represented the period after the progressive relaxation of these measures, including the transition to voluntary mask use in March 2023, the removal of border control measures in April 2023, and the reclassification of COVID-19 as a Category Five infectious disease in May 2023. Whereas IMD continued to be subject to all-case notification throughout the study period, COVID-19 surveillance shifted from all-case reporting to a sentinel-based reporting system after this reclassification. These phase definitions were used for comparative analyses of temporal and geographical differences in IMD incidence.

## Statistical analysis
Annual national and prefectural incidence rates per 100,000 population were calculated to assess temporal and spatial variation across phases. Age groups were categorized as 0–9, 10–19, 20–29, 30–39, 40–49, 50–59, 60–69, and ≥ 70 years. Because the number of cases was small within individual age strata, age-related patterns were evaluated using absolute case counts rather than age-specific incidence rates.
To compare IMD occurrence across the three study phases, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was performed using annual national case counts, followed by Tukey’s honestly significant difference (HSD) test for pairwise comparisons when significant differences were observed.
Geographic variation across all 47 prefectures was examined using incidence maps and heatmaps generated from annual prefecture-level incidence rates. Patterns in Osaka and the surrounding Kansai region were evaluated within the nationwide geographic framework.
To quantify regional changes in IMD incidence, fold changes in annual prefectural incidence rates were calculated using 2019 as the reference year. Fold change was defined as the ratio of the incidence rate in 2025 to that in 2019. Because surveillance data for 2025 covered January–October only, these values were treated as descriptive comparisons.
To explore the relationship between IMD occurrence and international travel, annual IMD case counts were compared with inbound visitor statistics. Normality of variables was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Associations were evaluated using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficients because of the limited sample size and potential temporal autocorrelation. These analyses assessed temporal co-variation and were not intended to infer causality.

## Software and tools
All data processing, statistical analyses, and geospatial operations were performed using Python version 3.13. The main packages used included pandas version 2.2.2 for data handling, openpyxl version 3.1.2 for spreadsheet file input, matplotlib version 3.10.3 and seaborn version 0.12.2 for visualization, and geopandas version 0.14.3 together with shapely version 2.0.3 and pyproj version 3.6.1 for geospatial processing and management of coordinate reference systems [ ].

## Results

## Temporal trends in IMD cases
A total of 288 cases were reported during the surveillance period. Cases decreased sharply during Phase 2, decreasing from a peak of 48 in 2019 (Phase 1) to a record low of 1 in 2021. Following Phase 2, cases rebounded in Phase 3, with 66 cases reported in 2024 and 68 cases recorded up to October 2025. Figure  illustrates the temporal trends in IMD cases in Japan from 2017 to 2025. The highest monthly number of IMD cases during the study period was recorded in June 2024 (n = 14). Secondary peaks were observed in January 2025 (n = 13) and May 2025 (n = 12). Following these peaks, monthly case counts decreased, and after the start of the Osaka Expo 2025 (April–October 2025), IMD cases demonstrated an overall downward trend compared with the preceding months. 
One-way analysis of variance followed by Tukey’s honestly significant difference post hoc test showed a significant reduction in cases from Phase 1 to Phase 2 (p = 0.0003) and a significant increase from Phase 2 to Phase 3 (p < 0.0001). A significant difference was also observed between Phase 1 and Phase 3 (p = 0.0369), indicating higher case counts in Phase 3 than in Phase 1, while acknowledging that the 2025 data reflect a partial year (10 months).

## IMD case distribution by age group across three phases
Across the entire study period, IMD cases were predominantly concentrated in older adults (Fig.  a, b). Figure  shows the age distribution of IMD cases from 2017 to 2025. The ≥ 70-year age group had the highest cumulative total (n = 76), followed by the 50–59 (n = 52), 60–69 (n = 39), and 40–49 (n = 32) year age groups. 
Phase-specific patterns (Fig.  b) demonstrated that, in Phase 3 (January 2023–October 2025), the ≥ 70-year age group continued to record the most cases (n = 38), followed by the 50–59 (n = 33) and 60–69 (n = 20) year age groups. IMD cases in the 10–19, 50–59, and ≥ 70-year age groups were 8, 17, and 33, respectively, in Phase 1. These numbers decreased markedly during Phase 2 to 0, 2, and 7 cases, respectively. In Phase 3, however, case counts in these age groups rebounded and exceeded the Phase 1 levels, reaching 18, 33, and 36 cases, respectively.

## Geographic distribution and regional differences of IMD cases
Higher incidence rates were observed in several prefectures across Japan. These included metropolitan areas such as Tokyo, Osaka, and Hyogo, as well as non-metropolitan prefectures such as Wakayama and Nagano, which showed relatively high incidence during specific phases. Figure  depicts the geographic distribution of IMD incidence rates across the 47 prefectures for Phases 1–3. During Phase 2 (2020–2022), the incidence decreased markedly nationwide, especially in previously high-incidence regions. In Phase 3 (2023–October 2025), a rebound and localized increases were observed in several prefectures. 
Figure  quantifies the magnitude of this rebound by showing fold changes in incidence relative to 2019, using values observed in 2025 (partial-year data). The largest increases were seen in Tokyo (6.38-fold), followed by Hyogo (3.58-fold), Fukuoka (3.01-fold), and Chiba (3.00-fold), reflecting the pattern observed in Fig.  . Other metropolitan areas exhibited heterogeneous trends; for example, Kanagawa (0.75-fold) and Aichi (0.51-fold) showed decreases. 

## Association between international travel and IMD cases
Annual IMD case counts and distributions by case source from 2017 to 2025 are shown in Table  . Domestic infections constituted the majority in all study years, although the proportion of imported or unknown-origin cases increased in selected years (16.7% in 2019, 25.0% in 2022, and 20.5% in 2025). 
Figure  illustrates the temporal patterns of annual IMD cases and inbound visitor numbers. Both metrics decreased sharply during Phase 2 (2020–2022) and rebounded during Phase 3 (2023–2024). Correlation analysis was conducted to quantify the association. Given the small sample size (N = 8) and potential temporal autocorrelation, Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient was used, showing a monotonic correlation (ρ = 1.000, p < 0.0001). The right panel of Fig.  illustrates a population-level temporal correlation between international travel volume and IMD case counts. Together with the trends shown in Table  , the data show parallel temporal patterns in international travel and IMD cases. 

## Discussion
This study provides a comprehensive nationwide assessment of invasive meningococcal disease (IMD) epidemiology in Japan from 2017 to 2025 across three phases: Phase 1 (2017–2019, pre-COVID-19), Phase 2 (2020–2022, pandemic period), and Phase 3 (2023–October 2025, post-pandemic period with relaxed preventive measures). Several key findings emerged. First, IMD incidence declined during the pandemic period and increased again during the post-pandemic phase. Second, most reported cases were considered domestically acquired. Third, older adults consistently accounted for the largest number of cases, and substantial geographic heterogeneity was observed across prefectures. Overall, these findings suggest that the post-pandemic increase in IMD in Japan reflected broader nationwide epidemiological changes accompanying societal reopening rather than a transient surge attributable to a single large-scale event.

## Temporal trends
IMD incidence decreased sharply during Phase 2 (2020–2022). This decrease aligns with international reports showing substantial reductions in IMD during the COVID-19 pandemic, largely attributable to non-pharmaceutical interventions such as mask use, reduced mobility, and school closures [ ]. A clear rebound occurred beginning in 2023, with increases continuing through 2024 and 2025. Statistical analyses confirmed significantly fewer cases in Phase 2 and higher counts in Phase 3. These temporal patterns are consistent with the recovery of population mixing and the accumulation of susceptible individuals following the period of suppressed transmission, rather than suggesting a dominant effect of any single large-scale event.
Following the relaxation of COVID-19–related restrictions, several countries reported renewed increases in IMD incidence after the resumption of social contact and international travel [ , ]. Japan experienced similar societal changes during the post-pandemic period, including the recovery of domestic mobility and social interactions. These contextual factors may have created conditions conducive to meningococcal transmission within the domestic population and thus provide a plausible background for the observed rebound, although these findings should be interpreted cautiously given the ecological nature of the analysis.
International travel recovered during the same period and likely represents a marker of broader societal reopening rather than an independent driver of IMD incidence. In addition, the numbers of imported and epidemiologically unclassified cases, together with the implementation and subsequent relaxation of COVID-19 preventive measures, may have been related to both inbound travel volumes and overall IMD case counts, thereby influencing the apparent temporal co-variation between the two. Therefore, the observed co-variation between inbound travel and IMD incidence should be interpreted with caution.
A temporal co-variation between IMD cases and international travel was observed at the national level. This pattern is consistent with previous reports of travel-associated IMD, including those linked to large religious gatherings such as the Hajj [ ]. However, given the ecological study design and the limited number of annual observations, this temporal co-variation should be interpreted cautiously and does not constitute evidence of direct importation or a causal explanation for prefecture-level variation.
During the Osaka Expo 2025 period (April to October 2025), IMD cases remained stable and showed a gradual decrease toward the end of the event. Despite the presence of more than 25 million visitors, no abrupt increase was detected. These observations suggest that the Phase 3 increase had already emerged prior to the Expo and did not appear to intensify during the event period. However, Japan’s past experience with small IMD clusters associated with international gatherings, such as the 2015 World Scout Jamboree outbreak, indicates that mass gatherings can contribute to meningococcal transmission under certain conditions. Therefore, evaluating IMD trends during the Expo period remains epidemiologically relevant, even though no short-term surge was observed in the present study.

## Age-specific patterns
Elderly adults aged 70 years or older consistently showed the highest disease burden, including during the post-pandemic rebound. Similar patterns have been reported in Europe, where the burden of IMD has remained disproportionately high in older populations (ECDC 2022) [ ]. Age-related immune decline (immunosenescence) and the higher prevalence of underlying comorbidities may contribute to this vulnerability.
Adolescents aged 10 to 19 years also experienced a noticeable rebound in Phase 3 following a decrease during Phase 2, consistent with the well-described “second incidence peak” in meningococcal epidemiology [ ]. High-density living environments such as dormitories, boarding schools, and military facilities are recognized high-risk settings for meningococcal transmission [ , ] and could partially account for the age-specific rebound observed in the present study. These findings suggest that the observed age distribution likely reflects a combination of biological susceptibility in older adults and social mixing patterns among adolescents [ , ].

## Geographic patterns
Geographic heterogeneity was observed across prefectures. Tokyo did not have the highest incidence rate; however, it showed the largest fold-change in incidence in 2025 relative to 2019 (Fig.  ). This pattern occurred despite Tokyo’s role as Japan’s principal international gateway and a region with high population turnover. IMD incidence was observed not only in metropolitan areas such as Tokyo and Osaka, but also in non-metropolitan prefectures such as Wakayama and Nagano. Osaka and the broader Kansai region did not show a comparable increase, even though the Expo was held there. Taken together, these findings suggest that the post-pandemic rebound was driven by nationwide epidemiological dynamics rather than localized effects related to the Expo venue. Comparable metropolitan clustering has been reported internationally, where cities with higher population mixing and global travel connections show elevated IMD risk [ , ]. However, such clustering was not consistently observed in the Japanese data, indicating that increased incidence was not confined to major international gateway cities. In Japan, where overall IMD incidence remains low, relatively small fluctuations in case numbers may substantially affect prefecture-level rates, thereby contributing to the observed geographic heterogeneity.

## Implications for public health policy
The present findings highlight the importance of strengthening IMD surveillance in Japan during periods of increased population mobility and social mixing. The observed rebound appears to reflect broader nationwide epidemiological dynamics rather than a short-term surge driven by a single event, underscoring the need for sustained and systematic surveillance.
Although older adults bore the greatest disease burden, the current evidence remains insufficient to support routine adult vaccination. Any reconsideration of vaccination policy would require more detailed data on age-specific disease burden, cost-effectiveness, and circulating serogroups in Japan.
Given the reported global increases in serogroups W and Y, expansion of serogroup-specific surveillance is particularly important. Within Japan’s low-incidence setting, continued risk communication for international travelers and ongoing preparedness for mass gatherings, integrated into routine national surveillance, are likely to be more appropriate than short-term or event-specific interventions.

## Limitations
This study has several limitations. First, the analysis was based on ecological surveillance data; therefore, causal relationships between IMD incidence and societal factors such as population mobility or international travel cannot be determined. Second, temporal patterns between inbound travel and IMD cases may have been influenced by confounding factors, including the numbers of imported and epidemiologically unclassified cases, as well as the implementation and subsequent relaxation of COVID-19 preventive measures, which could jointly affect both population movement and disease transmission patterns.
Third, serogroup information was incomplete and was not analyzed in the present study. Because shifts in circulating serogroups or clonal complexes may affect IMD epidemiology, the absence of standardized serogroup data limited assessment of strain-level dynamics. Fourth, prefecture-level incidence rates may be sensitive to small fluctuations in case counts because IMD remains a rare disease in Japan, which may partly contribute to the observed geographic heterogeneity.
Fifth, age-specific incidence rates were not calculated due to the small number of cases within individual age strata, limiting direct comparison of age-specific risks. Finally, the most recent data for 2024 and 2025 are provisional; however, substantial changes to the overall temporal patterns are unlikely. Future studies incorporating serogroup surveillance, carriage data, and individual-level linkage information would further strengthen the epidemiological understanding of IMD trends in Japan.

## Conclusions
The present study provides a comprehensive nationwide description of IMD epidemiology in Japan from 2017 to 2025. IMD incidence decreased markedly during the COVID-19 pandemic and rebounded following the relaxation of public health measures. This post-pandemic increase was consistent with broader national epidemiological dynamics rather than a discrete surge associated with any single event, with most cases being domestically acquired. Older adults and adolescents emerged as key affected groups in the post-pandemic period, and geographic analyses showed substantial heterogeneity in temporal patterns across prefectures. Importantly, no distinct short-term increase temporally coinciding with a specific international mass-gathering event, including the Osaka Expo 2025, was observed at the national level.
Although overall IMD incidence remains low in Japan, several epidemiological features warrant continued attention, including age-specific vulnerability, geographic heterogeneity, and temporal patterns occurring alongside societal reopening and increased population mobility. Given the ecological nature of the surveillance data, these findings should be interpreted as descriptive and hypothesis-generating rather than indicative of causal relationships. Continued strengthening of national surveillance and sustained preparedness during periods of increased mobility and large-scale gatherings will be essential for ongoing IMD risk assessment in Japan’s low-incidence setting.
