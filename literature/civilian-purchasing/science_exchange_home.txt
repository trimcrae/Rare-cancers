 THE PROBLEM

 Science has advanced. 
 Its infrastructure has not. 

 Life sciences organizations are advancing faster than ever: new modalities, distributed research models, and increasingly specialized partners. But the systems supporting that work remain fragmented.

 Finding qualified research partners takes weeks. Contracts require months of negotiation. Onboarding a new provider means starting a process that, on average, consumes more than 50 hours before any work begins. The problem isn't the people managing it. It's that the infrastructure around R&D was built for a different era. Generic procurement tools, siloed contracting processes, and fragmented payment workflows were not designed for the speed and compliance requirements of life sciences research.

 When programs run behind, the cost is measured in delayed outcomes, missed opportunities, and time that can't be recovered.
