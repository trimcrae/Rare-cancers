We aimed to identify the mutational characterization and potential biomarkers of bone tumors. In this study, a total of 357 bone tumor patients were recruited and the next generation sequencing (NGS)-based YuanSu450 panel, that includes both DNA and RNA sequencing, was performed for genomic alteration identification. The most common mutated genes in bone tumors included <italic toggle="yes">TP53</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">NCOR1</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">VEGFA</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">RB1</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">CCND3</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">CDKN2A</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">GID4, CCNE1</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">TERT</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">MAP2K4</italic>. The amplification of genes such as <italic toggle="yes">NCOR1</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">VEGFA</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">CCND3</italic> mainly occurred in osteosarcoma. Germline mutation analysis reveal a high frequency of HRD related mutations (46.4%, 13/28) in this cohort. With the assistance of RNA sequencing, 16.8% (19/113) gene fusions were independently detected in 20% (16/79) of patients. Nearly 34.2% of patients harbored actionable targeted mutations, of which the most common mutation is <italic toggle="yes">CDKN2A</italic> deletion. The different mutational characterizations between juvenile patients and adult patients indicated the potential effect of age in bone tumor treatment. According to the genomic alterations, the diagnosis of 26 (7.28%) bone tumors were corrected. The most easily misdiagnosed bone tumor included malignant giant cell tumors of bone (2.8%, 10/357) and fibrous dysplasia of bone (1.7%, 6/357). Meanwhile, we found that the mutations of <italic toggle="yes">MUC16</italic> may be a potential biomarker for the diagnosis of mesenchymal chondrosarcomas. Our results indicated that RNA sequencing effectively complements DNA sequencing and increased the detection rate of gene fusions, supporting that NGS technology can effectively assist the diagnosis of bone tumors.</p></abstract><kwd-group><kwd>bone tumor</kwd><kwd>next-generation sequencing</kwd><kwd>diagnosis</kwd><kwd>gene fusion</kwd><kwd>biomarker</kwd></kwd-group><funding-group><award-group><funding-source id="cn001"><institution-wrap><institution>National Natural Science Foundation of China

BY</meta-value></custom-meta></custom-meta-group></article-meta></front><body><sec sec-type="intro" id="s1"><title>Introduction</title><p>Bone tumor often refers to the tumor occurring in bone or its accessory tissues, including primary and metastatic (<xref rid="B1" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>). Compared with other tumors, bone tumors account for about 0.2% of all malignant tumors (<xref rid="B2" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>).The complexity and heterogeneity of bone tumors increase the difficulty of diagnosis and treatment (<xref rid="B3" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>). Osteosarcoma, chondrosarcoma, and Ewing’s sarcoma are common subtypes of bone tumors (<xref rid="B4" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>). Although surgical resection, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy have widely improved patient survival, bone tumors are still considered deadly in a high percentage of cases and seriously affect the quality of patients’ lives (<xref rid="B5" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>). Therefore, a further understanding of the mechanism of bone tumors for overcoming drug resistance and improving the low survival rate is still urgently needed.</p><p>With the development of molecular genetic technology, the molecular characteristics of bone tumors have been studied and some bone tumors can be diagnosed with the help of genomic mutations (<xref rid="B6" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>, <xref rid="B7" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>). For example, Ewing’s sarcoma is characterized by chimeric fusion expression of EWS and ETS family transcription factors (<xref rid="B6" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>), Ewing-like sarcomas are characterized by the rearrangement of <italic toggle="yes">CIC</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">BCOR</italic> (<xref rid="B8" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>), giant cell tumor of bone is characterized by the mutation of <italic toggle="yes">H3F3A</italic> (p.G34W) (<xref rid="B9" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>), chondroblastoma is characterized by the mutation of <italic toggle="yes">H3F3B</italic> (p.K36M) (<xref rid="B9" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>), mesenchymal chondrosarcoma is characterized by the fusion of <italic toggle="yes">HEY1-NCOA2</italic> (<xref rid="B10" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>), chondromyxoid fibroma is characterized by <italic toggle="yes">GRM1</italic> rearrangements (<xref rid="B11" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>), aneurysmal bone cyst is characterized by <italic toggle="yes">USP6</italic> rearrangements (<xref rid="B12" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>), osteoblastoma/osteoid osteoma is characterized by <italic toggle="yes">FOS/FOSB</italic> rearrangements (<xref rid="B13" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>), and synovial chondromatosis is characterized by the fusion of <italic toggle="yes">FN1-ACVR2A</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">ACVR2A-FN1</italic> (<xref rid="B14" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>).</p><p>Osteosarcoma, the most common bone tumor, has begun to be a concern and related molecular characteristics have been studied. Previous studies show that the most frequent driver genes in osteosarcoma include <italic toggle="yes">TP53</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">RB1</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">BRCA2</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">BAP1</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">RET</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">MUTYH</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">ATM</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">PTEN</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">WRN</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">RECQL4</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">ATRX</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">FANCA</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">NUMA1</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">MDC1</italic> (<xref rid="B15" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>, <xref rid="B16" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>). Kovac et al. reported that more than 80% osteosarcomas have genomic mutational characteristic of <italic toggle="yes">BRCA1</italic>/2 deficient tumors (<xref rid="B16" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>). Behjati et al. elucidated mutational characterization and identified the mutations of insulin-like growth factor (IGF) signaling pathway in osteosarcomas by using whole-exome sequencing (WES) and whole-genome sequencing (WGS) based on a large cohort of 112 childhood and adult patients (<xref rid="B17" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>). In addition, aberrant signaling pathways, including PI3K/mTOR and Wnt signaling, were also reported in osteosarcomas (<xref rid="B18" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>). However, due to the low incidence and high heterogeneity, the efficacy of targeted therapy is still limited in osteosarcoma patients (<xref rid="B19" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>).</p><p>To date, the molecular mechanism of bone tumors, including osteosarcoma, chondrosarcoma, and Ewing’s sarcoma is still unclear. Although some bone tumors have been molecular typed, it is important to further elucidate and study the molecular characteristics of bone tumors for more accurate classification and treatment. In this study, we aimed to identify the molecular features of bone tumors and explore potential biomarkers for accurate diagnosis of bone tumor.</p></sec><sec sec-type="materials|methods" id="s2"><title>Materials and Methods</title><sec id="s2_1"><title>Ethics and statements</title><p>This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University (The approval number: [2018] 249). We declare that all methods used in this protocol were carried out in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations. This study was approved by all patients and all participants provided informed consent.</p></sec><sec id="s2_2"><title>Patients and Sample Collection</title><p>A total of 357 bone tumor patients were enrolled in this study from First Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University. Formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tumor tissues and matched blood samples were collected. Genomic DNA was prepared by using QIAamp DNA/RNA FFPE Tissue Kit and QIAamp DNA Blood Midi Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. RNA was isolated by using miRNeasy FFPE Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The concentration of DNA was measured by Qubit and normalized to 20–50 ng/μL for sequencing. Genomic alterations (GAs) were detected at Shanghai OrigiMed Co., Ltd (OrigiMed, Shanghai, China), a laboratory certified by College of American Pathologists (CAP) and Clinical Laboratory Improvement Amendments (CLIA).</p></sec><sec id="s2_3"><title>Identification of GAs and Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB)</title><p>The DNA/RNA samples were analyzed by using the next generation sequencing (NGS)-based YuanSu450 gene panel (OrigiMed, Shanghai, China), which covers all the coding exons of 450 tumor-related genes that are frequently rearranged in solid tumors. The genes were captured and sequenced with a mean depth of 800× by using Illumina NextSeq 500. The procedures followed the steps described by Frampton et al. (<xref rid="B20" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>). GAs identifications followed the previous published methods (<xref rid="B21" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>): Single nucleotide variants (SNVs) were identified by MuTect (v1.7). Insertion-deletions (Indels) were identified by using PINDEL (v0.2.5). The functional impact of GAs was annotated by SnpEff3.0. Copy number variation (CNV) regions were identified by Control-FREEC (v9.7) with the following parameters: window = 50 000 and step = 10 000. Gene fusion/rearrangements were assessed by Integrative Genomics Viewer (IGV). For RNA-Seq data, gene fusions were detected by using STAR-fusion (v1.4) (<xref rid="B22" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>). TMB was estimated by counting the coding somatic mutations, including SNVs and Indels, per megabase of the sequence examined in each patient. The TMB value was further divided into two groups: TMB-H, defined as ≥10 mutations/Mb, and TMB-L, defined as &lt;10 mutations/Mb.</p></sec><sec id="s2_4"><title>Statistical Analysis</title><p>Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 22.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Fisher’s exact test was used for the association analysis of categorical variables. Student’s t−test and Wilcoxon rank sum test were used for the association analysis of normally distributed data and non-normally distributed data, respectively. P &lt; 0.05 was considered statistically significant.</p></sec></sec><sec sec-type="results" id="s3"><title>Results</title><sec id="s3_1"><title>Clinical Characteristics of Bone Tumor Patients</title><p>The 357 bone tumor patients included 266 primary tumors, 50 metastatic tumors, 38 local recurrent tumors, and 3 tumors with unclear origin. There were 214 men and 143 women in this cohort and the median age was 20 years (range 1-86 years). The proportion of male patients is higher than the proportion of female patients in this cohort (60% <italic toggle="yes">vs.</italic> 40%). The proportion of primary tumors, metastatic tumors, and local recurrent tumors in male patients is higher than that in female patients (58% <italic toggle="yes">vs.</italic> 42%, 66% <italic toggle="yes">vs.</italic> 34%, and 61% <italic toggle="yes">vs.</italic>39%, respectively). According to pathology, this cohort includes 227 osteosarcomas, 43 chondrosarcomas, 14 chordomas, 13 giant cell tumors of bone, 12 malignant giant cell tumors of bone, 4 osteoblastomas, 4 undifferentiated sarcomas, 3 undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcomas, 1 low grade malignant fibromyxoid sarcomas, 1 myofibroblastomas, 1 chondromatosises, 1 fibrosarcomas, 1 hemangiomas, 1 angiosarcomas, and 31 unclassified bone tumors (<xref rid="T1" ref-type="table"><bold>Table 1</bold></xref>).</p><table-wrap position="float" id="T1" Bone tumors include many subtypes such as osteosarcoma, chondrosarcoma, chordomas, giant cell tumors of bone, and osteoblastomas (<xref rid="B3" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>, <xref rid="B4" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>). However, due to the rarity of bone tumors and the small number of patients, the molecular mechanism of bone tumors is still unclear. While genome sequencing research of bone tumors are underway, small sample number and limited bone tumor subtypes are the common deficiencies (<xref rid="B17" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>, <xref rid="B24" ref-type="bibr">24</xref>, <xref rid="B25" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>). In this study, we investigated the genomic alterations of 357 bone tumors with 16 subtypes, and osteosarcoma and chondrosarcoma were the main subtypes. Benign bone tumors are easy to cure and have a good prognosis, while malignant bone tumors have a poor prognosis and are easy to relapse. Therefore, the differential diagnosis of benign and malignant tumors is one key step in the diagnosis and treatment. Understanding the molecular characteristics of bone tumors is helpful for differential diagnosis, which is of great significance to guide patients’ treatment and improve patients’ prognosis.</p><p>NGS studies have been widely performed in osteosarcomas in Western countries. Previous studies showed that <italic toggle="yes">TP53</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">RB1</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">CCND3</italic> frequently occurred in osteosarcoma (<xref rid="B15" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>, <xref rid="B26" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>, <xref rid="B27" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>). Notably, we identified the highly frequent <italic toggle="yes">NCOR1</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">VEGFA</italic> amplifications in our cohort, and the significantly higher <italic toggle="yes">NCOR1</italic> copy number in juvenile patients than that in adult patients.</p><p><italic toggle="yes">NCOR</italic>1 is a nuclear receptor co-repressor and mediates transcriptional repression by certain nuclear receptors (<xref rid="B28" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>). The mutations in the <italic toggle="yes">NCOR1</italic> were reported to be associated with the prognosis of hormone receptor negative breast and lung adenocarcinoma (<xref rid="B29" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>). Yan et al. reported that <italic toggle="yes">NCOR1</italic> amplification frequently occurred in high-grade osteosarcoma (<xref rid="B30" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>). Selvarajah et al. showed that NCOR1 is negatively correlated with the prognosis of osteosarcoma in canines (<xref rid="B31" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>). These results suggest that <italic toggle="yes">NCOR1</italic> amplification may be a potential biomarker in osteosarcoma.</p><p>Inhibition of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) signaling may lead to tumor-induced angiogenesis and inhibition of tumor growth (<xref rid="B32" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>, <xref rid="B33" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>). In osteosarcoma, Yang et al. first reported the vascular endothelial growth factor A (<italic toggle="yes">VEGFA</italic>) gene amplification in osteosarcoma patients from Tianjin, China, and pointed that increasing <italic toggle="yes">VEGFA</italic> expression is the biomarker for poor prognosis of Chinese osteosarcoma (<xref rid="B34" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>). Combined with our study, we deduced that there may be different molecular mechanisms between osteosarcoma patients in China and those in western countries. The high proportion of <italic toggle="yes">NCOR1</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">VEGFA</italic> amplification may be a special molecular feature of Chinese osteosarcoma patients. Age is an important factor associated with the incidence of bone tumors. It is reported that the most of osteosarcomas occur in adolescents and chondrosarcoma usually occur in adults patients (<xref rid="B35" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>). Similarly, our results also showed the high incidence of osteosarcoma and chondrosarcoma in juveniles and adults patients, respectively. Also, we found the high incidence of osteoblastoma, but low incidence of chordoma, giant cell tumors of bone, malignant giant cell tumors of bone, and undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcomas in juvenile patients. The achieved results supplement the understanding of the incidence inclination of bone tumor subtypes in different ages.</p><p>Based on the statistical analysis, we identified a series of mutated genes that were correlated with juvenile/adult patients group. Notably, most of juvenile related mutations were also significantly associated with the osteosarcoma. The largest number of osteosarcoma patients in this cohort is a potential reason for this. However, these results also support that osteosarcoma mainly occurred in the juvenile patient group.</p><p>Due to the complexity and heterogeneity, a clear classification of bone tumors is often challenging. Significant progress has been made in the genotyping of bone tumors. For example, <italic toggle="yes">IDH1</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">2</italic> mutations are the molecular feature to distinguish chondrosarcoma from osteosarcoma (<xref rid="B36" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>), the high frequency of <italic toggle="yes">MDM2</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">CDK4</italic> amplifications in low-grade osteosarcoma (<xref rid="B37" ref-type="bibr">37</xref>), the high frequency of <italic toggle="yes">NCOA2</italic> fusion in mesenchymal chondrosarcoma (<xref rid="B38" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>), and the high frequency of <italic toggle="yes">H3F3A</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">H3F3B</italic> mutations in giant cell tumor of bone and chondroblastoma, respectively (<xref rid="B39" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>, <xref rid="B40" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>). In this cohort, we also modified the diagnosis of 20 bone tumors based on the results of NGS detection, including 6 fibrous dysplasia of bone modified from 3 osteosarcomas, 1 malignant giant cell tumor of bone, 1 spindle cell tumor, and 1 unclassified bone tumor. These results supported that NGS-based assisted diagnosis is of great significance to improve the benefit to patients.</p><p>In addition, the rapid development of NGS technology in the past decades has led to the discovery of new tumor mutation features and more accurate differential diagnosis. The 5<sup>th</sup> edition of the WHO classification has named some soft tissue sarcomas or bone tumors by newly recognized molecular and genetic alterations (<xref rid="B41" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>). According to the primary diagnosis in this cohort, we also detected <italic toggle="yes">H3F3A</italic> in giant cell tumor of bone and malignant giant cell tumor of bone, <italic toggle="yes">IDH1/IDH2</italic> mutations in chondrosarcoma, and <italic toggle="yes">NCOA2</italic> fusion in mesenchymal chondrosarcoma. Notably, we also found <italic toggle="yes">MUC16</italic> mutations in these tumors. Muc16 is a transmembrane protein recognized by monoclonal antibody CA125 (<xref rid="B42" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>). Li et al. reported that the <italic toggle="yes">MUC16</italic> mutation may be associated with tumor mutational burden and outcomes in gastric adenocarcinoma patients (<xref rid="B43" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>). Based on a large cohort of 10,195 patients, the <italic toggle="yes">MUC16</italic> mutation was shown to be associated with response to, and improved outcomes for, ICI treatment in solid tumors (<xref rid="B44" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>). Although there are only two cases, considering the rarity of mesenchymal chondrosarcoma, the identification of the <italic toggle="yes">MUC16</italic> mutation indicates the potential opportunity of mesenchymal chondrosarcoma patients to benefit from immunotherapy. Targeted therapy for bone tumors is rarely reported. In this study, it was found that about 34% of Chinese bone tumor patients harbored actionable targeted mutations, including <italic toggle="yes">CDKN2A</italic>
, <italic toggle="yes">PTEN</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">FGFR1/2/3</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">NF1</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">NTRK1/2/3</italic>. Unfortunately, except for targeting a few gene fusions such as <italic toggle="yes">NTRK1/2/3</italic>, targeted therapy for bone tumors has made slow progress. Gene fusions are a common genomic variation in tumors and especially in bone tumors, oncogenic gene fusions commonly occur (<xref rid="B45" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>). Chromoplexy is the main cause of gene fusion generated in bone and soft tissue tumors, including gene fusions within and between chromosomes (<xref rid="B46" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>). In this study, our results showed that gene fusion within chromosomes mainly occurred in bone tumors, and the gene fusion is inclined to occur on chromosome 17 and chromosome 12, including the fusion of <italic toggle="yes">TP53</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">ETV6</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">KMT2D</italic>. Although Ewing’s sarcoma is not considered to be included in osteosarcoma (<xref rid="B41" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>), the identification of fusion in osteosarcoma is still useful for auxiliary diagnosis. However, it is inevitable that gene fusion will missed due to the concentration and abundance of DNA or RNA. In this study, our results confirmed that the combination of DNA and RNA detection can effectively increase the detection rate of gene fusion. This is of great significance in the auxiliary diagnosis and subsequent treatment of osteosarcoma and sarcoma.</p><p>In conclusion, we identified the mutation characteristics of bone tumor patients, pointed out the differences of germline and somatic mutations between juvenile patients and adult patients, and increased the efficiency of fusion detection by the combination of DNA and RNA sequencing. Our results supported that NGS technology can effectively assist in the diagnosis of bone tumors and provided the evidence for the precise treatment of bone tumors.</p></sec><sec sec-type="data-availability" id="s5"><title>Data Availability Statement</title><p>The original contributions presented in the study are included in the article/<xref rid="SM1" ref-type="supplementary-material"><bold>Supplementary Material</bold></xref>.
