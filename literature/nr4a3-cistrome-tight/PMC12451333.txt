## Abstract
Shitou goose (STE) and Wuzong goose (WZE) are both characteristic goose breeds in Guangdong, China. Their growth cycle is similar, but there are huge differences in body size. One of the reasons for the difference in body size is the individual muscle mass, which is determined by myofiber development. The number of myofibers is fixed as early as the embryonic stage. Therefore, in this study, the leg muscles of 15, 23-day-old geese embryos (E15, E23), and the first day of hatching (P1) goslings of both STE and WZE were selected for analysis. Muscle tissue samples were subjected to histological sectioning and staining for morphological analysis. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) across developmental stages were identified through systematic screening. An expression map of the identified DEGs was generated, and a gene interaction network was constructed to elucidate potential regulatory mechanisms. The results showed that the leg muscle development of STE at the embryonic development stage was superior to that of WZE. Through GO and KEGG analyses, it was found that STE was significantly more enriched in processes related to muscle development than WZE. Analysis of the gene expression profiles of STE and WZE revealed different manifestations, and it was observed that STE exhibited significantly more unique genes compared with WZE. We constructed the interaction network of myo-genes, including 77 genes in the network of STE and 17 genes in the network of WZE. We found that the unique gene interaction network in STE is more complex, suggesting that the potential regulatory environment of muscle development in STE may be more complex than that in WZE. The RT-qPCR results of nine identified myogenic genes (MYH7, MYO1F, MYOZ1, MYO5B, MYH11, ITGB8, MSTN, MFN2 and CCNB1) were consistent with the RNA-seq analysis. This study demonstrated that the developmental differences between STE and WZE originated during the embryonic stage. This study is the first to systematically compare the leg muscle development mechanisms of two goose breeds during embryonic stages. The results distinct developmental patterns and molecular regulation patterns of leg muscles between STE and WZE, providing a theoretical foundation for understanding the differences in myofiber development among different breeds.
Keywords: Wuzong goose, Shitou goose, Leg muscle, Embryonic muscle development, Gene regulation

## Introduction
China is a major country producing poultry meat, and the output of poultry meat ranks first in the world, while Guangdong is a famous goose breeding area in China ( ). As an important source of avian protein, the goose meat quality and production have garnered significant attention. Major meat goose breeds in China include the Shitou goose, Magang goose, Wuzong goose, and Wanxi White goose ( ). Notably, the Shitou goose (STE) is one of the largest meat goose breeds both in China and globally, with adult males typically weighing 9-10 kg and females reaching 7.5–9 kg at maturity ( ). The Wuzong goose (WZE), an indigenous small-sized breed, typically weighs around 3 kg at maturity ( ). The two distinctive goose breeds from Guangdong Province exhibit pronounced differences in body size. Focusing on these breeds will help elucidate the fundamental principles of goose growth and development and, more importantly, uncover the micro-level developmental divergence underlying such extreme size disparities. Current research attributes the primary driver of this size variation to differential muscle content; therefore, a comparative analysis of muscle development is crucial for clarifying the physiological mechanisms behind the observed morphological differences.
Leg muscle is an important part of geese. Meat quality and production performance of this muscle are the key factors in determine economic benefits ( ). Anatomically, skeletal muscle comprises a complex organ system of parallel, multinucleated myofiber bundles, where muscle mass results from the combined effects of myofiber hyperplasia (myofiber number) and hypertrophy (myofiber diameter) ( ). Crucially, the prenatal phase establishes the myofiber population through myoblast fusion processes, thereby exerting profound regulatory effects on postnatal muscular development in geese ( ). Avian myofiber development comprises four distinct phases: (1) myoblast differentiation; (2) myoblast fusion; (3) myonuclear migration coupled with myofibril crosslinking; and (4) myofiber maturation ( ). The developmental progression of embryonic skeletal muscle in avian species is governed by sequential activation of gene regulatory networks and signaling pathways ( ). The MYOD family transcription factors orchestrate myogenic determination through initiating muscle-specific gene expression programs, which serves as the molecular basis for the differentiation of myogenic progenitor cells into functional myofibers during embryogenesis ( ). Moreover, Myf5 synergistically occupies core regulatory regions within muscle-specific gene promoters alongside MYOD family members, forming a collaborative transcriptional complex that drives myogenic commitment ( ). MyoG is a muscle-specific transcription factor that mediates TGF-β signaling ( ). This gene is also involved in muscle differentiation, cell cycle exit and muscle atrophy. Together with other key genes, MyoG plays a critical role in embryonic muscle development. The MYH gene family is involved in encoding myosin, with MYH7 being highly expressed in skeletal muscle tissue rich in slow muscle type Ⅰ myofibers ( ).
In our previous study, our group conducted research on the development of the pectoral muscles in the embryonic stages of STE and WZE. It was found that the pectoral myofiber density of STE was significantly higher than that of WZE during E15-P1. We found that the density of pectoral myofibers in STE was significantly higher than that in WZE from E15 to P1, indicating that the between-line difference in myofiber density was greater than the variation observed across these stages ( ). Our previous study also found that differences in embryonic pectoral muscle development patterns may occur before E15.
Many researchers have conducted different studies on avian muscles. A study analyzed the pectoral muscles and leg muscles of Heiwu duck and Peking duck and found that the developmental differences between parts within the same breed were much greater than those between breeds for the same part ( ). In addition, they found significant differences in leg muscle weight between Heiwu duck and Peking duck during the embryonic stage, except for E25, and identified 20 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in the leg muscles of the two breeds of ducks. By studying the differences among individuals of the same breed (Zhedong white geese) with different body weights, they detected 19 DEGs in the pectoral muscles ( ). Among them, the up-regulated genes COL11A2 and COL22A1 in high-weight geese were closely related to the up-regulated gene COL11A1 detected in the STE leg muscles in this study. This study suggests that individual weight differences are highly likely to be related to digestive and metabolic functions. Yang et al. conducted research on Daheng broilers and Tibetan chickens during the embryonic stage ( ). They constructed the expression profiles of six genes (MYF5, PAX7, CDC20, PCNA, MYOG and MYBPH) and found that there were significant differences in the gene expression levels of the two chicken breeds at the same stage. Xiao et al. further explored the regulatory network involved in the proliferation and differentiation of STE myoblasts through their study of the ceRNA network ( ). They found that the mRNA at the proximal end of DNA-encoded libraries (DELs) was associated with pathways related to neural excitation. MiRNAs were involved in the regulation of actin cytoskeleton, Wnt signaling pathway, Ras protein signal transduction and neuronal projection development regulation, and constructed the ceRNA network of important genes. Although molecular-level studies on muscle development have been conducted in poultry, including different goose breeds, the differences in muscle development between STE and WZE geese during the embryonic stage have not been thoroughly studied.
Transcriptomic sequencing (RNA-seq) has emerged as an indispensable tool for deciphering molecular mechanisms underlying animal growth and development ( ). Systematic comparison of cross-breed and multi-stage transcriptomic profiles allows robust identification of candidate genes and signaling pathways associated with target phenotypic traits, thereby advancing mechanistic understanding of morphological diversification ( ). In recent years, RNA-seq technology has provided a powerful tool for studying the molecular mechanisms of animal growth and development.
In this study, the morphological differences in leg muscle development were compared between STE and WZE at E15, E23 and P1 during embryonic stages. Using RNA-seq technology, we analyzed the divergent gene expression profiles underlying leg muscle development in the two goose breeds. Consequently, key genes and signal pathways related to leg muscle development were identified, providing a theoretical foundation for understanding the differences in myofiber development among different goose breeds.

## Materials and methods

## Ethics statement
All experimental procedures strictly complied with the ethical guidelines of the China Council on Animal Care and received approval from the Guangdong Provincial Animal Care and Use Committee (Approval IDs: SCXK [Guangdong] 2011–0029 and SYXK [Guangdong] 2011–0112).

## Experimental materials
The experimental subjects comprised STE and WZE breeds sourced from Guangdong Qingyuan Jinyufeng Goose Co., Ltd. Embryonic specimens were systematically harvested at two developmental stages (embryonic day 15 (E15) and embryonic day 23 (E23), n = 3 per timepoint per breed) ( ; ). Post-hatching specimens were acquired on postnatal day 1 (P1, n = 3 per breed) for subsequent analyses. Studies have demonstrated that during the embryonic stages, sex hormones are secreted in negligible quantities, with myofiber development predominantly regulated by proteins or other hormonal factors ( ; ). Consequently, sex was disregarded as a variable in this experimental design during sample collection.

## H&E staining
Section Preparation: leg muscle tissues from three embryos/goslings per developmental stage (E15, E23, P1) per breed were fixed in 4 % paraformaldehyde (PFA). Specimens underwent graded ethanol dehydration (70–100 %), xylene clearing, and paraffin embedding. Serial 4 μm sections were obtained using a rotary microtome, mounted on glass slides, and dried at 60°C.
H&E Staining: fixed sections underwent sequential processing beginning with xylene-mediated deparaffinization (2 × 10 min), followed by rehydration through a descending ethanol series (100 %, 95 %, 70 %). Nuclei were stained with hematoxylin for 5 min at room temperature (RT), differentiated in 1 % acid ethanol (30 s), and counterstained with eosin Y for cytoplasmic visualization (2 min, RT). Sections were subsequently dehydrated via ascending ethanol gradients (70 %, 95 %, 100 %), cleared in xylene (2 × 5 min), and permanently mounted with neutral balsam under coverslips.

## RNA-sequencing
RNA library construction and high-throughput sequencing were performed by Biomarker Technologies (BMK, commercial service). Total RNA extracted from frozen leg muscle using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Shanghai, China) underwent quality assessment via Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies, USA), with all samples meeting stringent thresholds (RIN ≥ 7.0; 28S/18S ≥ 1.0). Equal RNA quantities pooled from three biological replicates per breed and developmental stage were sequenced on an Illumina HiSeq™ 2000 platform. The HISAT2 (http://ccb.jhu.edu/software/hisat2/) system and StringTie (https://ccb.jhu.edu/software/stringtie/) system were used for data processing. Raw reads were processed by removing adapter/low-quality tags (copy number = 1), followed by alignment to the Anser cygnoides domesticus reference genome (AnsCyg_PRJNA183603_v1.0) allowing one mismatch for polymorphism tolerance. BMK conducted library saturation analysis, with clean tags categorized by copy frequency and distinct tags annotated for expression profiling.

## Differential gene expression
For samples with biological replicates, DESeq2 (http://www.bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/html/DESeq.html) conducts differential expression analysis between sample groups to obtain a set of differentially expressed genes between two biological conditions. In the process of detecting differentially expressed genes, with differentially expressed genes identified through stringent thresholds: |log2 (fold change)| ≥ 1.0 combined with a false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05. Transcript abundance alterations were categorized as significant down-regulation (fold change ≤ 0.5) or up-regulation (fold change ≥ 2.0) based on inter-library read count comparisons.

## Transcriptomic bioinformatics analysis
Cluster 3.0 and TreeView software was used to analyse the systematic clustering of all libraries. Principal component analysis (PCA) and heatmap construction were conducted using tools from the BMK cloud (https://international.biocloud.net/). The myogenic genes were selected based on the GO terms results and muscle development-related articles ( , ; ) . The cellular component (CC), molecular function (MF) and biological process (BP) of the proteins were annotated by the Blast2GO software (http://www.blast2go.org/). GO functional classification and enrichment analyses were performed to identify terms that were significantly enriched in DEGs using DAVID analysis (http://david.abcc.ncifcrf.gov/). Myogenic gene interaction network prediction analysis was conducted by STRING (http:// string-db.org/) and illustrated by Cytoscape 3.1.0 (http://www. cytoscape.org/). Venn diagrams were created using the website (http://bioinformatics.psb.ugent.be/webtools/Venn/).

## Real-time quantitative PCR
Experimental primers were designed using the gene sequence of goose retrieved from the NCBI database ( ). Total RNA was extracted from the same samples used in sequencing analysis by TRIzol Reagent (Invitrogen, Shanghai, China), and then cDNA was synthesised using reverse-transcription Kit (Promega, Beijing, China). Realtime quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) was conducted in triplicate using SYBR Green RT-qPCR Kit (Genestar, Beijing, China) and detected in the LightCycler 480 II system (Roche, Basel, Switzerland). The expression of each gene was normalized to GAPDH transcripts. The correlation coefficient (r) was calculated using Excel (Microsoft Excel 2010). The experimental data were analysed using the 2−∆∆CT method.
 

## Data statistics methods
Three biological replicates were set in each group. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was conducted using Graphpad Prism software (Version 5.0), and difference significance analysis was performed using Tukey’s test. Correlation analysis uses Pearson correlation coefficients for descriptive statistics. Values of P < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

## Results

## Comparison of embryonic leg muscle development between STE and WZE
Quantitative analysis of H&E-stained leg muscle sections revealed dynamic developmental trajectories between STE and WZE. No significant difference was observed in myofiber density and myofiber diameter between STE and WZE at E15 ( A-C). At E23, compared with WZE, the myofiber density of STE showed an advantage ( B). The myofiber diameter of STE was extremely significantly better than that of WZE ( C). There was also no significant difference in myofiber density and myofiber diameter between STE and WZE at P1 ( A-C). It can be observed that in morphology, the largest difference between STE and WZE exists with E23, and STE forms a huge advantage with higher myofiber density and larger myofiber diameter at this stage.
 

## Basic data analysis of RNA-seq for STE and WZE
To elucidate the molecular basis underlying muscular developmental divergence, we conducted comparative transcriptomic profiling of leg muscle tissues from WZE and STE across three critical stages: E15, E23, and P1. RNA-seq data were obtained for all 18 libraries (3 biological replicates × 2 breeds × 3 stages), demonstrating superior alignment rates (> 85 %) to the Anser cygnoides domesticus reference genome (Table S1). Systematic profiling revealed 19,507 expressed genes, including 4,811 unannotated transcripts requiring functional characterization. Hierarchical clustering analysis and principal component analysis (PCA) were employed to evaluate global expression coherence across developmental trajectories ( ).
 The results of cluster analysis indicated that the global gene expression map can be divided into three groups, with significant segregation at each stage, but no significant segregation between breeds ( A). This result suggested that the gene expression differences between embryonic stages are more complex than those between breeds at the same stage. The analysis results of PCA show that, from the perspective of the first principal component, E15 is significantly separated from E23 and P1. In addition, the projection of E15 is mainly concentrated in the positive direction, while the projections of E23 and P1 are both concentrated in the negative direction ( B).

## Identification of myogenic processes of DGEs in leg muscles of STE and WZE embryos
By analyzing the DEGs of different breeds and stages, it was found that the number of DEGs within the same stage of breed development was significantly higher than that between breeds ( , Tables S2-S8). STE and WZE have the highest number of up-regulated DEGs between E15 and E23, with STE at 1,322 DEGs and WZE at 1,107 DEGs. On the contrary, the maximum down-regulation of DEGs occurred between E23 and P1, with a total of 2,454 DEGs for STE and 3,605 DEGs for WZE.
 Comparative GO analysis of DEGs between STE and WZE revealed limited myogenic biological processes (myo-BPs) and associated genes (myo-genes) underlying their divergent leg muscle phenotypes. Despite significant E23-stage morphological differences (P ≤ 0.05), stage-specific comparisons (S-DEGs/S-BPs) exhibited greater myogenic regulation than breed-specific contrasts (B-DEGs/B-BPs). Specifically, intrabreed E15-E23 comparisons identified 5 myo-BPs/11 myo-genes in WZE and 7 myo-BPs/13 myo-genes in STE, whereas interbreed E15 comparisons yielded only 3 myo-BPs/2 myo-genes ( ). Shared regulatory elements included 3 myo-BPs (myoblast fusion, striated muscle contraction regulation, myoblast proliferation) and 7 myo-genes (CACNA1S, CAPN2, CAV3, LOC106029333, MYL3, MET, Anser_cygnoides_newGene_1257). Breed-specific patterns emerged: STE uniquely exhibited 4 myo-BPs (skeletal muscle contraction, striated muscle contraction, myotube differentiation regulation) and 6 myo-genes (TNNI2, TNNC2, NOS1, RBM38, SIK1, LOC106037025), while WZE showed 2 distinct myo-BPs (myoblast differentiation, muscle cell homeostasis) and 4 myo-genes (LOC106039769, LGALS1, Anser_cygnoides_newGene_30771/30773). Notably, no overlap occurred between B-DEGs/B-BPs and S-DEGs/S-BPs, indicating independent regulatory mechanisms governing stage-specific versus breed-specific muscular development.
 At the same time, we also identified the key molecular pathways of development. Analyses revealed breed and stage-specific regulatory patterns: STE exhibited significant enrichment of E15-P1 up-regulated genes in "muscle system process" and "muscle contraction" ( AB), whereas WZE showed analogous but less pronounced enrichment (lower gene counts and statistical significance) during E15-E23 ( A). Down-regulated genes diverged between breeds: STE's E15-E23 down-regulated set was enriched for "muscle tissue development" and "muscle structure development", contrasting with WZE's "muscle contraction" and "sarcomere assembly" associations. During E23-P1, STE down-regulated genes demonstrated enrichment in "skeletal muscle tissue regeneration" ( B). In the E23 stage, the down-regulation of STE and WZE is enriched in "Muscle filament sliding", "Muscle cell fat specification", "Regulation of muscle contract" and "myofibers development" ( C).
 Comparative transcriptomic profiling across the E23-P1 transition revealed no of significant myo-BP enrichment in both intrabreed and interbreed analyses, with regulatory divergence primarily confined to the E15-E23 developmental window. This temporal specificity identifies the E15-E23 transition as a developmental critical window for embryonic leg muscle maturation. Furthermore, myogenic regulatory networks active during this phase may mechanistically underlie the interbreed divergence in leg muscle development characteristics observed between WZE and STE.

## Expression profile and regulatory network of myogenic genes
To validate the potential regulatory roles of myogenic regulatory genes in muscle development between the two goose breeds, we visualized the expression profiles of 89 myogenic genes specific to STE, 27 specific to WZE, and 127 shared between the two breeds. Additionally, we predicted the interaction networks of myogenic genes unique to STE and WZE ( , Tables S9–S11, , Tables S12–S13).
 At embryonic day 15 (E15), the expression levels of TP63, PKP2, BMP2, HEY2, PAK1, TWIST1, FGFR2, KLF5, LGALS1, SDC1, DSP, CXADR, and TP73 were significantly higher in STE compared to WZE. In the STE-specific gene interaction network, PAK1 exhibited strong associations with genes such as SOX9, KCNJ8, MKX, OXTR, and DAG1. Furthermore, PAK1 indirectly interacts with IGF1 and MYL2 through its association with GJA1 ( A). At E23, PPARD, NR4A3, CHRNB4, EDN2, and ASS1 were expressed at higher levels in STE than in WZE ( ). In the unique gene network diagram of WZE, high expression of ASS1 was associated with NOS1 and interacted with ANK2 through SNTA1 ( B). This interaction further linked to MYOC through S100A1, MMP9, and TGFB2, which are components of the TGF-β signaling pathway known to regulate myoblast proliferation, differentiation, and survival, thereby influencing muscle formation and regeneration ( B).
Taken together, these findings highlight the differential expression and complex interaction networks of myogenic genes between the two goose breeds, suggesting that the myogenic process is more pronounced in STE than in WZE.

## RT-qPCR validation of myogenic DEGs and RNA-seq
To validate the RNA-seq findings, RT-qPCR was conducted on nine myogenic genes (MYH7, MYO1F, MYOZ1, MYO5B, MYH11, ITGB8, MSTN, MFN2 and CCNB1) identified within the interaction networks of DEGs or myogenic genes ( ). The RT-qPCR results corroborated the RNA-seq data, with a correlation coefficient of r ≥ 0.91. These nine genes showed a significantly higher expression level in STE than in WZE at least at one developmental stage (E15-P1). This further supports the conclusion that the myogenic process is more pronounced in STE than in WZE ( ).
 

## Discussion

## There were significant differences in the leg muscle development of the two goose breeds from E15 to E23
During the period from embryonic development to birth, geese undergo myofiber differentiation, with the number of myofibers becoming essentially fixed during this stage ( ). In addition to the number of myofibers, the myofiber diameter is also an important factor causing the difference in muscle mass. In the previous research results of the research group on pectoral myofibers, histological analysis revealed that the difference between STE and WZE pectoral myofibers was concentrated in myofiber density ( ). In the study of leg muscle this time, we found that the differences between STE and WZE myofibers were most pronounced in E23 period, particularly in myofiber diameter. This may suggest that the development patterns of pectoral and leg muscles are different. The development of pectoral muscles may focus on the density of myofibers, while the development of leg muscles primarily determined by the myofiber diameter.
In a study on Yili geese ( ), it was found that the cross-sectional area and myofiber diameter increased with age, while the density of myofibers decreased with age. From four weeks old to ten weeks old, it will continue to decline, and the decline is most pronounced from four weeks old to six weeks old. Consistent with avian developmental biology, duck studies also document reduced myofiber hyperplasia during late embryogenesis ( ). This pattern during the postnatal growth phase is consistent with the developmental pattern of leg muscles in STE and WZE embryos. It can be observed that at E23-P1 stage, the myofiber density of both has a downward trend. Similar findings were also found in the study of Zhedong white geese ( ). The myofibers area of large male legs was significantly larger than that of female legs. This suggests that the important reason for the difference in muscle mass between STE and WZE is that the myofiber diameter of STE increases more rapidly during E15-E23. These data suggest that the leg muscle development of STE may be more accelerated, and the development during E15-23 is much superior to that of WZE. To gain a clearer understanding of the mechanisms underlying embryonic muscle development, further research is required targeting other time points during the embryonic stage (e.g., E18, E21, E27, etc.).

## DEGs and myo BPs analysis of STE and WZE
Through PCA, we observed a clear separation between the E15 stage and the subsequent two stages. When comparing DEGs of different breeds at the same developmental stage of E15 and E23, it was found that the number of up-regulated genes in STE was higher than that in WZE. This suggests that gene expression differences between STE and WZE were already present at E15. Following a period of development, these differences became more pronounced by E23. This evidence points to the rapid development of myofibers in STE during the E15 to E23 period as a key factor underlying the distinctions between the two goose breeds. We also observed higher PAX3 expression in STE than in WZE at E15 and E23. PAX3 drives myogenesis and enhances resistance to environmental stress ( ; ). This contributes to faster and more stable leg muscle development in STE embryos.
After GO and KEGG analysis, we found that among these identified myo-BPs, STE has more genes concerning myotube differentiation than WZE. The comparative study between broiler chickens and laying hens also found that broiler chickens can differentiate MHC+myotubes faster in vitro ( ) . Myotubes provide the basic structure of myofibers and acquire contractile capacity upon move and contract after further development and maturation. After myotube differentiation, myotubes fuses with myoblasts to form new myofibers ( ). This process is of great significance for the normal development and regeneration of muscle, and STE has more muscle BP, especially the related content of myotube differentiation, which may imply that it has unique potential and advantages in muscle development, warranting of further in-depth study.
In addition to pathways directly related to muscle development, STE also shows vascular smooth muscle contraction and VEGF signaling pathway. These pathways affect vascular smooth muscle movement and angiogenesis. They indirectly influence muscle repair and blood perfusion. The PPAR signaling pathway also indirectly regulates muscle fiber type switching. Other indirect pathways acting on muscle during the embryonic stage of STE and WZE have not been deeply studied. Future studies should focus on its key epigenetics-related genes and identify potential influencing factors. Additionally, further analyses can be conducted via ATAC-seq or ChIP-seq to explore the upstream mechanisms of regulatory networks, thereby achieving a deeper understanding of the gene regulatory mechanisms underlying muscle development.

## Interaction network of hub myo-genes between STE and WZE
In this study, a total of 243 myogenic genes were identified, whose functions offer insights into the regulatory mechanisms underlying embryonic leg muscle development between WZE and STE. Notably, certain genes exhibited higher expression levels in STE during the E15 and P1 stages, which suggests that STE undergoes a more robust myogenic process during this period compared to WZE.
We have constructed a network diagram of myogenic gene interactions, which facilitates a deeper understanding of the interplay among these genes. In the myogenic gene interaction network of STE, 77 genes are either directly or indirectly interconnected, whereas the WZE network comprises only 17 genes. The gene interaction network of STE is more dense, suggesting that its muscle development involves more complex regulatory pathways.
In STE, 14 genes, including WNT4, MYL2, GJA1, PAK1 and IGF1, exhibit strong interconnections and functional relevance. WNT4 expression is linked to the expression of Pax7 and MYOD1, and it significantly influences muscle mass ( ). WNT4 participates in the Wnt classical pathway, and its high expression level helps promote myofibers formation ( ). MYL2 and GJA1 have a strong interaction, and these two genes are related to muscle contraction and development processes ( ). PAK1 are positioned at key nodes of gene interaction networks, while PAK1 facilitates insulin signaling, induces actin remodeling, and promotes glucose uptake into skeletal muscle cells ( ). IGF1 plays a pivotal role in regulating skeletal muscle growth and development and activates multiple downstream signaling pathways ( , ; ). Another study, similar to the results of this study, found that the expression level of IGF1 in STE was higher than that in WZE, and IGF1 can promote rapid muscle growth and development in the early stages, thus proving that the expression level of IGF1 is a factor causing the difference in body shape between the two goose breeds ( ). WNT4 activates the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, up-regulates MYOD and MYF5, and thereby directs the commitment of myogenic progenitor cells to differentiation ( ) . IGF1 acts via the Akt pathway to inhibit GSK3β, stabilizes β-catenin, and thus cooperates with WNT4 to enhance differentiation ( ) . Akt-mediated signaling also suppresses FoxO-driven protein degradation and, together with MYL2, promotes myofiber hypertrophy and survival ( ) . At the same time, we found that the expression of the five genes listed in the three periods was higher at E15 and lowest at P1. We can speculate that these interconnected genes play a common role in E15, so that the myofibers development of STE has a better performance in embryonic period.
In WZE, there is a strong interaction between SNTA1, ANK2, and KCNJ11, while ASS1 and MYOC exhibit relatively high expression levels. Among the 17 genes analyzed, KCNJ11 is associated with potassium ion channels in skeletal muscle and affects muscle tenderness ( ; ; ). This gene may affect the metabolism of sugar in geese, thereby affecting growth rate and fat deposition. MYOC can indirectly regulate the TGF-β pathway by influencing CAV1 ( ). The TGF-β pathway affects the differentiation of goose embryonic myofibers and also participates in regulating the apoptosis process of muscle cells. MMP9 expression inhibits muscle regeneration ( ). The increased expression of MMP9 in WZE may inhibit the development of its leg muscles, resulting in differences in muscle development compared to STE. MRAS is involved in osteoblast differentiation ( ). CFTR is linked to ATP release during skeletal muscle contraction ( ). RASGRF1 can inhibit the proliferation of alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma and plays a critical role in activating the RAS pathway ( ; ). NOS1 contributes to skeletal muscle fatigue and also participates in skeletal muscle differentiation and homeostasis ( ; ). Among the genes introduced above, the expression peaks of genes with stronger interaction (SNTA1, ANK2, KCNJ11) are not in the same period. Other genes in the network are more concentrated in E23 or P1. WNT4, MYL2, IGF1 and other genes are concentrated in the early and middle stages of STE embryos, and these genes are directly related to muscle development, promoting the rapid growth of STE myofiber diameter and density. The genes with high expression levels in the gene expression profile of WZE involve a wide range of aspects, such as metabolism, cell differentiation, apoptosis, and muscle activity, which make WZE development more balanced at different stages. And the expression period of these genes is partially concentrated in the middle and late stages. Combined with the results of muscle sections, this makes the myofiber diameter and density of WZE reach a comparable level to STE in the later stage.
After analyzing the genes within the myogenic gene network, it demonstrated that the myogenic genes in STE exhibit a more significant and direct association with muscle development. However, future studies should employ multi-techniques to elucidate to determine whether functional genes interact with muscle structural protein encoding genes in the network and mechanistically drive the phenotypic divergence in embryonic leg muscle development between STE and WZE.

## Conclusion
In summary, this study suggests that the E15-E23 period is likely the critical phase for the developmental differences in leg muscles between STE and WZE during the embryonic stage, with STE exhibiting more rapid development during this time. At E15-P1, the leg muscles of STE developed more comprehensively. By analyzing DEGs in STE and WZE, we identified unique genes in each breed and constructed interaction networks for these genes. This study systematically compares the muscle development mechanisms of two goose breeds during the embryonic stage for the first time, which is beneficial for providing molecular markers for selective breeding of STE and WZE in the future.
