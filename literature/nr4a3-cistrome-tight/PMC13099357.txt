## Summary
Diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN) is a prevalent complication characterized by Schwann cell dysfunction. The role of the nuclear receptor subfamily 4 group member 3 (NR4A3) in DPN remains unclear. We found that NR4A3 expression is upregulated in the sciatic nerves of streptozotocin (STZ)-induced DPN mice. Overexpression of NR4A3 via AAV9-Mpz-NR4A3 lumbar intrathecal injection improved motor and sensory nerve function and mitigated sciatic nerve pathology. NR4A3 overexpression also reduced oxidative stress and enhanced mitochondrial function. In the high glucose (HG)-induced Schwann cell injury model, NR4A3 overexpression inhibited apoptosis, mitochondrial dysfunction, and dedifferentiation. Integrated ChIP-seq and mRNA-seq profiling identified glutaminase 2 (GLS2) as a direct transcriptional target of NR4A3. NR4A3 binds to the GLS2 promoter and promotes its transcriptional activation, and suppression of GLS2 abrogates the functional protection conferred by NR4A3 in Schwann cells. Together, NR4A3 represents a promising therapeutic target for DPN.

## Introduction
Diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN) is one of the common causes of neurological damage in clinical practice, and the longer the duration of diabetes, the more pronounced the increase in its prevalence, with patients with DPN accounting for about 40%–60% of the total number of patients with diabetes. , As an independent risk factor for diabetes, DPN is a leading cause of foot ulcers and even lower limb amputations and is associated with premature death. , The pathogenesis of DPN is the result of multifactorial effects. Experimental and clinical evidence suggests that insulin resistance in HG states triggers metabolic dysfunction, which in turn leads to physiological and pathological disorders such as oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and neuronal necrosis, and ultimately advances the disease process. , There is a considerable healthcare-related economic burden associated with DPN that severely reduces patients’ quality of life, and the search for effective therapeutic targets is urgent.
Schwann cells are a type of glial cell that are present in the peripheral nervous system to promote myelin formation, protect axons and actively interact with axons. , In recent years, its role in the development of DPN has attracted increasing attention. In the diabetic setting, the function of Schwann cells is markedly inhibited, and reports have demonstrated that high glucose (HG) inhibits the migratory capacity of Schwann cells and reduces the synthesis of neurotrophic factors. , In addition, Schwann cells act as a key sensor of axonal activity, providing high-energy metabolites to axons, but this process is disrupted in the DPN. Also, when nerve fibers are disrupted, nerve conduction velocity is reduced. , Therefore, maintaining the normal function of Schwann cells may have an important role in improving DPN.
The transcription factor NR4A3, also known as the orphan receptor, is a nuclear receptor subfamily protein that is expressed in a wide variety of cells and is also an important factor in the regulation of normal cellular function. , Loss of NR4A3 expression results in developmental defects of the inner ear in mice. Furthermore, specific knockdown of NR4A3 in hematopoietic stem cells induces atherosclerosis in mice. Drawing our attention to the fact that mitochondrial respiration in β-cells is dependent on NR4A3, the downregulation of NR4A3 inhibits mitochondrial function and hinders ATP production. Inhibition of NR4A3 in myofibroblasts downregulates the expression of genes related to mitochondrial generation. More importantly, NR4A3 has important neuroregulatory functions. Hawk et al. have confirmed that NR4A3 plays a role in memory enhancement through HDAC inhibitors. NR4A3 was found to promote the elongation of neurites and the expression of the nerve cell marker GAP43. Our previous mRNA-seq results showed that NR4A3 expression was up-regulated in sciatic nerve tissue of DPN mice. Therefore, we speculate that NR4A3 may play a certain role in the damage repair of DPN.
In the current study, we aimed to investigate the functional impact of NR4A3 in DPN progression and whether NR4A3 achieves repair of DPN by improving mitochondrial function to maintain the state of myelin differentiation of Schwann cells.

## Results

## NR4A3 is upregulated in the sciatic nerve of mice with streptozotocin (STZ)-induced diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN)
We performed the mRNA-seq detection by collecting sciatic nerve tissues from normal mice and streptozotocin (STZ)-induced diabetic mice. As shown in A, differentially expressed genes (DEGs) screening was performed according to the criterion of p < 0.01 and FC > 1.5 or <2/3. We then performed GO-BP enrichment analysis mainly for the up-regulated DEGs and showed the genes on the TOP10 pathway in the form of a chord diagram ( B). We found that lots of up-regulated DEGs were enriched in GO-BP terms related to neurotransmitter secretion, insulin secretion, and RNA transcriptional regulation. We further analyzed the gene enrichment by GSEA, and the results showed that the gene under this gene set was enriched in biological processes related to RNA transcription regulation, mitochondrial, and energy homeostasis ( C). Among these up-regulated genes, we noted nuclear receptor subfamily 4 group A member 3 (NR4A3), a common transcription factor of the nuclear receptor subfamily, which was closely related to mitochondrial function and energy metabolism. The function of NR4A3 in DPN has never been reported before, so we chose NR4A3 for a follow-up study. Subsequent validation by western blot and qPCR confirmed that NR4A3 protein and mRNA levels were significantly elevated in the sciatic nerves of STZ-induced diabetic mice compared to controls ( D and 1E). Immunohistochemical staining further demonstrated enhanced NR4A3 expression within the sciatic nerve tissue of diabetic animals ( F). These findings established that NR4A3 expression is up-regulated in the sciatic nerve during experimental diabetic neuropathy.
 

## NR4A3 overexpression improves neurological function in DPN mice
Next, we evaluated the effect of NR4A3 on neural function in DPN mice. We performed Schwann cell-specific NR4A3 overexpression using the AAV9-Mpz vector, and STZ-induced diabetic mice were administered either AAV9-Mpz-Vector or AAV9-Mpz-NR4A3 via intrathecal injection. As expected, diabetic mice exhibited significantly lower body weight and sustained hyperglycemia compared to non-diabetic controls, which were improved to some extent by viral treatment ( A). Furthermore, functional assessment revealed that DPN mice displayed markedly reduced motor nerve conduction velocity (MNCV) and sensory nerve action potential (SNAP) amplitude. NR4A3 overexpression significantly improved both MNCV and SNAP parameters, indicating enhanced motor and sensory nerve function ( B). Immunofluorescence analysis of sciatic nerve sections demonstrated decreased expression of S100B in STZ-diabetic mice compared to controls. This loss of S100B was partially restored in mice overexpressing NR4A3. Concurrently, a robust NR4A3 signal was confirmed in the sciatic nerves of the AAV9-Mpz-NR4A3 group ( C). Together, these data demonstrated that the overexpression of NR4A3 can improve functional neurological deficits of DPN mice.
 

## NR4A3 improves the pathological injury of the sciatic nerve in DPN mice
The effect of NR4A3 on the pathological injury of the sciatic nerve in DPN mice was also comprehensively evaluated. The results of H&E staining revealed disrupted nerve fiber morphology and loose arrangement in STZ-induced diabetic mice ( A). Furthermore, we found that myelinated nerve fibers were lost and their density decreased in DPN mice by toluidine blue staining ( B). The severe myelin sheath abnormalities, including lamellar loosening and vacuolation in DPN mice, were observed by transmission electron microscopy. In addition, quantitative assessment showed a significant increase in the percentage of demyelinated fibers and an elevated g-ratio in the STZ group ( C). However, all of these neurological impairments were significantly ameliorated in NR4A3 overexpressing mice. As the study progressed, we further explored the effect of NR4A3 on the differentiation of Schwann cells. We examined the expressions of dedifferentiation markers p-cjun(S63), p75NTR, and differentiation markers MBP and P0 in different groups of mice by immunofluorescence, qPCR, and western blot, respectively ( D–3F; A–S1D). We found that NR4A3 inhibited the expression of STZ-induced p-cjun(S63) and p75NTR associated with Schwann cell dedifferentiation, while promoting the expression of MBP and P0 associated with differentiation. Collectively, these findings indicated that NR4A3 not only ameliorates structural nerve damage but also promotes a pro-myelinating, differentiated state in Schwann cells within the diabetic milieu.
 

## NR4A3 attenuates oxidative stress and restores mitochondrial function in the sciatic nerves of DPN mice
The role of NR4A3 on oxidative stress and mitochondrial function of DPN mice was further explored. The glutathione reduced/oxidized (GSH/GSSG) ratio, a critical indicator of cellular antioxidant capacity, was significantly decreased in STZ-induced diabetic mice. This decline was substantially reversed by NR4A3 overexpression ( A). Concurrently, immunofluorescence staining using dihydroethidium revealed elevated levels of ROS in the sciatic nerves of diabetic mice, which were mitigated by NR4A3 ( B). We next assessed mitochondrial function through measurements of energy metabolites. Diabetic nerves exhibited markedly reduced levels of ATP and flavin adenine dinucleotide (FAD), alongside a decreased NAD+/NADH ratio, collectively indicating impaired mitochondrial energy production. NR4A3 overexpression significantly restored the levels of ATP, FAD, and the NAD+/NADH ratio ( C–4E). Furthermore, quantification of mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) content relative to nuclear DNA (nDNA) showed a decrease in mitochondrial mass in diabetic nerves, which was rescued by NR4A3 ( F). Taken together, these results demonstrated that NR4A3 alleviates oxidative stress and ameliorates multiple facets of mitochondrial dysfunction in the sciatic nerves of DPN mice.
 

## NR4A3 maintains myelin differentiation and attenuates apoptosis in Schwann cells under high-glucose (HG) conditions in vitro
To validate the protective role of NR4A3 in Schwann cells, we infected Schwann cells with lentiviral-mediated NR4A3 overexpression and verified efficient NR4A3 protein expression 48 h post-infection ( A). To distinguish glucose-specific effects from osmotic stress, Schwann cells were exposed either to HG or to an osmotic control medium containing for 48 h. Immunofluorescence staining revealed that HG, but not OC, significantly increased cleaved caspase-3 expression, indicating HG-specific apoptosis induction. This apoptotic response was markedly suppressed by NR4A3 overexpression ( B; A). We further examined the impact of NR4A3 on Schwann cell differentiation state. Immunofluorescence showed that HG treatment elevated the dedifferentiation marker p-cJUN(S63), whereas osmotic control did not elicit a similar change. NR4A3 overexpression effectively reduced p-cJUN(S63) levels in HG-treated cells ( C; B). Consistent with these findings, HG treatment upregulated the dedifferentiation marker p75NTR and downregulated the myelin-specific genes MBP and P0. NR4A3 overexpression significantly reversed these aberrant expression patterns ( D–5F). Collectively, these results demonstrated that NR4A3 protects Schwann cells from HG-induced apoptosis and promotes a differentiated, pro-myelinating phenotype.
 

## NR4A3 ameliorates HG-induced mitochondrial dysfunction in Schwann cells
Next, we further investigated the direct impact of NR4A3 on mitochondrial homeostasis in cultured Schwann cells under HG conditions. We observed a substantial accumulation of mitochondrial ROS in HG-treated cells, a phenotype not induced by osmotic control medium, and this HG-induced ROS accumulation was markedly attenuated by NR4A3 overexpression ( A; C). Concurrently, the NAD+/NADH ratio was significantly decreased by HG exposure but was restored upon NR4A3 overexpression ( B). Consistent with improved bioenergetics, NR4A3 also reversed the HG-induced decline in cellular ATP and FAD levels, notably, ATP levels were unaffected in the OC group ( C and 6D; D). Furthermore, quantification of mtDNA content relative to nDNA revealed that HG treatment reduced mitochondrial mass, a deficit that was rescued by NR4A3 ( E). Finally, the activity of mitochondrial respiratory chain Complex IV, which was specifically suppressed under HG but not OC conditions, was significantly enhanced in NR4A3-overexpressing cells ( F; E). In addition, to further consolidate the protective role of NR4A3, we performed loss-of-function experiments by knockdown NR4A3 in Schwann cells ( A). In contrast to the overexpression results, NR4A3 deficiency exacerbated HG-induced injury, leading to further downregulation of the myelin proteins MBP and P0, and upregulation of the dedifferentiation marker p75NTR ( B–S3D). In addition, NR4A3 knockdown intensified ROS accumulation ( E) and aggravated the loss of ATP content and complex IV activity under HG conditions ( F and S3G). Taken together, these data demonstrated that the observed mitochondrial impairments are specifically attributable to HG rather than hyperosmolarity, and that NR4A3 mitigates this HG-induced mitochondrial oxidative stress and restores cellular energy metabolism in Schwann cells.
 

## Identification of GLS2 as a direct transcriptional target of NR4A3 in Schwann cells
The function of NR4A3 in DPN has been identified, but the exact mechanism is unknown. To gain a deeper understanding of the possible biological substrate of NR4A3, we conducted ChIP-seq experiments. A showed the genomic distribution of NR4A3 peaks and our results demonstrated that the NR4A3 peak bound to the gene promoter. Meanwhile, we collected Schwann cells infected with Lv-Vector or Lv-NR4A3 lentivirus for mRNA-seq assay to screen and analyze genes. As shown in B, the PCA score plot indicated that the samples were distributed in different regions. We further screened DEGs according to the criteria of p < 0.01, FC > 1.5 or FC < 2/3, and these results were reflected in the heatmap and volcano plot ( C and 7D). Subsequently, we subjected the genes up-regulated in the mRNA-seq results and the molecules bound to the promoter in the ChIP-seq to a Venn analysis and found 26 overlapping factors ( E). To prioritize candidates for functional validation, we first ranked the overlapping genes based on fold-change in mRNA-seq and selected the top 10 most upregulated hits. Subsequent literature mining focused on whether their known biological functions aligned with the core NR4A3 phenotypes observed in our study, namely, the improvement of mitochondrial function and attenuation of oxidative stress. Among these factors, glutaminase 2 (GLS2) has attracted our attention, which has been reported to promote ATP production, inhibit ROS, and contribute to the recovery of mitochondrial function. , Therefore, we speculated that NR4A3 may improve DPN by regulating GLS2 expression. With the help of IGV capture tool, we found that NR4A3 may act in the promoter region of GLS2 ( F). These findings position GLS2 as a key downstream effector of NR4A3, providing further insights into the mechanism by which NR4A3 alleviates DPN.
 

## NR4A3 transcriptionally activates GLS2 by binding to its promoter
To validate GLS2 as a functional downstream target of NR4A3, we first examined whether NR4A3 regulates GLS2 expression. As shown in A–8C, lentiviral-mediated overexpression of NR4A3 significantly increased both mRNA and protein levels of GLS2 in Schwann cells, whereas knockdown of NR4A3 reduced GLS2 expression. We then predicted the potential motif sequence of NR4A3 using the JASPER database (https://jaspar.elixir.no/matrix/MA1112.1/) and identified its binding sites (GAAGGTCA) in the GLS2 promoter region ( D). To test whether NR4A3 transactivates GLS2, we performed dual-luciferase assays. Co-transfection of an NR4A3 expression plasmid with a reporter construct containing the wild-type GLS2 promoter markedly enhanced luciferase activity, confirming NR4A3-dependent transcriptional activation ( E). Importantly, mutation of the predicted NR4A3-binding motif within the GLS2 promoter abolished this transactivation effect ( F). These results demonstrated that NR4A3 directly binds to a specific response element in the GLS2 promoter and drives its transcriptional upregulation in Schwann cells.
 

## GLS2 is required for NR4A3-mediated mitochondrial protection in Schwann cells
To determine whether GLS2 is functionally required for the protective effects of NR4A3, we performed loss-of-function studies. Lentiviral-mediated knockdown of GLS2 efficiently reduced its protein expression in Schwann cells ( A). In cells overexpressing NR4A3, co-treatment with shGLS2 abolished the NR4A3-mediated suppression of HG-induced mitochondrial ROS accumulation ( B). Furthermore, the restoration of the NAD+/NADH ratio by NR4A3 overexpression was reversed upon GLS2 knockdown ( C). Similarly, the improvements in cellular ATP and FAD levels conferred by NR4A3 were significantly attenuated when GLS2 expression was silenced ( D and 9E). Collectively, these results demonstrate that GLS2 is an essential downstream effector, and its expression is necessary for NR4A3 to exert its protective effects on mitochondrial redox balance and energy metabolism in Schwann cells under HG stress.
 

## Discussion
Here, our findings demonstrated that NR4A3 could exert anti-neuropathic effects in DPN by attenuating oxidative stress and maintaining mitochondrial function in Schwann cells. We also identified possible downstream biological targets of NR4A3 by ChIP-seq and mRNA-seq assays, and elucidated that the ameliorative effect of NR4A3 on DPN may be related to the modulation of GLS2 expression.
DPN is recognized as a multifactorial disorder involving a complex interplay of metabolic, vascular, inflammatory, and neurotrophic dysregulations. In addition to the oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction emphasized in this study, several other pathogenic mechanisms have been implicated in DPN progression. These include persistent hyperglycemia-induced activation of the polyol and hexosamine pathways, accumulation of advanced glycation end-products, impaired neurotrophic support, chronic low-grade inflammation, and microvascular dysfunction leading to endometrial hypoxia. , , The reason we focus particularly on mitochondrial damage and oxidative stress is that these factors are recognized as key contributors to Schwann cell injury and axonal degeneration, as they directly disrupt cellular bioenergetics, redox balance, and myelin maintenance. , , Furthermore, NR4A3 was reported to prevent diabetes-induced cardiomyopathy by maintaining mitochondrial energy metabolism and reducing oxidative stress. Therefore, we speculate that NR4A3 may also play a role in improving DPN progress. NR4A3 belongs to the NR4A receptor subfamily, and this family of genes is widely expressed in tissues with high metabolic activity, such as the brain, skeletal muscle, and adipose tissue. Since NR4A receptors are closely associated with a variety of cellular processes and vital functions of organs, this has led to the involvement of NR4A3 receptors in a variety of highly prevalent human diseases, including cancer, diabetes, and cardiovascular diseases. , , In our previous mRNA sequencing results for sciatic nerve tissue of STZ-induced diabetic mice model, we noted the NR4A family gene, NR4A3, among the many up-regulated genes. To test this hypothesis, we first constructed a classical DPN mouse model through STZ treatment, consistent with previous reports that indicators such as MNCV and SNAP were inhibited in DPN mice. In addition, the expression of the Schwann cell marker S100B was also downregulated in STZ-induced mice. However, these trends were reversed by NR4A3 overexpression, and these experimental data suggested that NR4A3 had the potential to improve DPN neurological function. Notably, the observed upregulation of NR4A3 in sciatic nerves of STZ-induced DPN mice likely represents a compensatory response to the diabetic pathological milieu, several mechanisms may contribute to this induction: (1) Hyperglycemia-activated signaling pathways: HG can activate the PI3K/Akt and MAPK/ERK pathways, both of which have been shown to enhance NR4A3 transcription via downstream transcription factors such as CREB. , (2) Oxidative stress-responsive induction: ROS accumulation, a hallmark of DPN, can activate NF-κB, which may bind to the NR4A3 promoter and promote its expression. Furthermore, oxidative stress-induced upregulation of EGR1 may also mediate NR4A3 expression. While the upstream regulation of NR4A3 was not the focus of the present study, these potential pathways provide a plausible mechanistic context for its upregulation in DPN and warrant further investigation.
Schwann cells are known to be the main glial cells of the peripheral nervous system and maintain normal nerve function by providing metabolic support for axons through the formation of myelin sheaths. , Peripheral neuropathy usually occurs with axonal degeneration or demyelination. Therefore, maintaining the normal function of Schwann cells is of great significance for improving neuropathy. Indeed, myelin sheath Schwann cells are injured in such a way that they lose contact with axons and undergo a process of dedifferentiation. It involves the down-regulation of pro-myelin-forming genes, including MBP and P0, as well as the up-regulation of markers of immature Schwann cells such as c-Jun and p75NTR. Our experimental data were also consistent with previous studies. Firstly, STZ-induced mice showed obvious pathological changes, including abnormal morphology of sciatic nerve fibers and destruction of myelin structure. In addition, we also found damaged sciatic nerve tissue presented p-cjun(S63)highP75NTRhighMBPlowP0low gene expression patterns. However, NR4A3 overexpression significantly improved sciatic nerve injury and dedifferentiation of Schwann cells in DPN mice.
The process of myelination has high metabolic requirements. Mitochondria, as the key organelles in cell metabolism, have gradually become the key regulatory factors for myelin maintenance in Schwann cells. The mitochondrial protein prohibitin 2 has recently been found to localize at the axon-Schwann cell interface and is required for developmental myelin formation. This has to be a reminder to improve the dedifferentiation of Schwann cells by maintaining mitochondrial function. ATP is required for the growth of nerve protrusions and plays a key role in neuronal synapses. Christopher D Wiley et al. showed that the NAD+/NADH ratio regulates energy production and that cells undergoing mitochondrial dysfunction have a lower NAD+/NADH ratio. FAD is an important cofactor involved in enzymatic redox reactions for cellular metabolism and homeostasis, so damaged mitochondria typically reduce energy-storage FAD levels. Consistent with previous studies, our experimental data demonstrated that in STZ-induced DPN mice, ROS levels were significantly elevated, but NAD+/NADH ratio and ATP, FAD levels were decreased, suggesting that DPN mice exhibit abnormal oxidative stress as well as mitochondrial dysfunction. However, NR4A3 overexpression improved mitochondrial dysfunction in mice, and these conclusions were well replicated in HG-induced Schwann cells in vitro, further highlighting the potential of NR4A3 in improving DPN.
The molecular mechanism of NR4A3 in DPN warrants in-depth excavation, and we adopted a rigorous dual-omics strategy (ChIP-seq combined with mRNA-seq) to systematically identify its downstream transcriptional targets in Schwann cells. Through Venn analysis of promoter-bound molecules from ChIP-seq and upregulated DEGs from mRNA-seq, we identified 26 overlapping candidates, reflecting the specificity of NR4A3-mediated transcriptional regulation. Finally, GLS2 was selected as the downstream factor of NR4A3 for functional study. From the omics perspective, GLS2 ranked among the top 10 most upregulated genes in overlapping candidates (FC > 2.0, p < 0.001), indicating robust transcriptional activation by NR4A3. More importantly, GLS2’s well-documented biological functions align perfectly with the core protective phenotypes of NR4A3 in our study, making it a functionally coherent downstream effector. GLS2 is a mitochondrial phosphate-activated glutaminase that catalyzes the hydrolysis of glutamine to glutamate in cellular mitochondria. Glutamate is involved in the regulation of mitochondrial bioenergy in cells through the tricarboxylic acid cycle for the production of ATP as well as for antioxidant defense via GSH synthesis. Therefore, we made the reasonable speculation that NR4A3 may alleviate DPN by regulating GLS2 expression. We further predicted the canonical binding motif of NR4A3 (AAAGGTCA) predicted by the JASPAR database. This motif is highly conserved across NR4A family members and has been validated as a functional response element in the NR4A3-mediated regulation of metabolic genes in cardiovascular diseases. , Importantly, we identified this binding motif located at positions −305 to −297 of the GLS2 promoter. Subsequent dual-luciferase assays further verified that NR4A3 directly transactivates the GLS2 promoter: co-transfection of NR4A3 with a wild-type GLS2 promoter reporter significantly enhanced luciferase activity, while the mutation of the motif completely abolished this activation. This result excludes indirect regulatory effects and confirms that NR4A3 binds to a specific response element in the GLS2 promoter to drive its transcription, establishing a direct and specific molecular link between NR4A3 and GLS2.
In summary, this study confirms the positive role of NR4A3 in improving DPN progression. NR4A3 can improve the histopathological structure of DPN mice, maintain normal mitochondrial function, protect the myelin formation function of Schwann cells, and these functions may be related to the regulation of GLS2 expression. NR4A3 has the potential to become a promising intervention target for the treatment of DPN.

## Limitations of the study
While our study provides comprehensive evidence supporting the role of NR4A3 in mitochondrial repair, certain technical limitations should be noted: (1) Although in vitro knockdown experiments confirmed that NR4A3 is necessary for protecting Schwann cells against HG-induced injury, future studies using inducible, Schwann cell-specific NR4A3 knockout models are warranted to definitively establish its endogenous protective function in vivo and to validate the effects of its downstream targets on DPN progression. (2) The more direct assessment of mitochondrial respiratory capacity, such as through Seahorse metabolic flux analysis (e.g., oxygen consumption rate, OCR), would provide additional mechanistic depth. (3) This study exclusively utilized male mice. While this choice was made to align with prevalent models in the field and to minimize the potential confounding effects of estrogen on STZ-induced neuropathy, , it limits the generalizability of our findings. DPN develops in both sexes, and future studies are warranted to investigate the role of the NR4A3-GLS2 axis in female animal models. (4) Our ChIP-seq analysis revealed that NR4A3 binds to multiple genomic regions, but our mechanistic investigation primarily centered on promoter-bound targets. The functional roles of NR4A3 binding sites in distal intergenic regions were not explored in depth. This presents a direction for future research to more comprehensively elucidate the transcriptional regulatory network of NR4A3 in Schwann cells.

## Experimental model and study participant details
The 8 weeks of male C57BL/6 mice were used in this project. All experimental procedures were following the Ethics Committee of Tianjin Medical University Chu Hsien-I Memorial Hospital (Approval No. 250310002). After one week of acclimatization, all the animals were randomly divided into 4 groups: Control, STZ, STZ+AAV9-Mpz-Vector, and STZ+AAV9-Mpz-NR4A3. Mice in the STZ group were injected with STZ (150 mg/kg) intraperitoneally after fasting overnight, and blood glucose was measured 1 week later. Mice with fasting blood glucose≥300 mg/dL were considered to have diabetes. The same modeling approach was applied to STZ+AAV9-Mpz-Vector and STZ+AAV9-Mpz-NR4A3 groups of mice, and 20 μL of virus (10ˆ12 vg/mL) was injected intrathecally into the L5-L6 intervertebral space at a maximal injection rate of 5 μL/min after successful diabetes induction. Motor nerve conduction velocity (MNCV) and sensory nerve action potential (SNAP) were measured in animals 7 weeks after virus injection as described previously.

## Production of recombinant adeno-associated virus
The recombinant adeno-associated virus (AAV9) vectors were constructed using the shuttle plasmid RD21583AB#AAV9-Mpz-NC. For overexpression, the target gene (NM_015743.3, NR4A3) was inserted into the SalI site in the same orientation as the promoter. The virus production was carried out by co-transfecting AAV-293 cells (50% confluency, cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS) in 10 cm dishes. Transfection was performed using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, USA) with 43.4 μL reagent and 56 μL P3000 enhancer per dish, along with 7 μg of shuttle plasmid, 14 μg of pHelper (Cat# 6230, Takara, China), and 7 μg of serotype plasmid pRC9 (Cat# BR227, FengHuiBio, China). After 72 h, viruses were harvested by freeze-thaw lysis of cells followed by filtration through a 0.45 μm membrane. The viral titers reached 1 × 1013 vg/mL for both AAV9-Mpz-Vector and AAV9-Mpz-NR4A3.

## Cell culture and lentiviral infection treatment
Schwann cells were provided by Saibaikang company (China) and cultured in a special medium (Saibaikang, China) in a 5% CO2 incubator at 37 °C. Cells with good growth state were selected for lentivirus infection experiment. The lentiviral overexpression vector pLVX-IRES-puro (Cat# BR025, FengHuiBio) was constructed by inserting the NR4A3 gene (NM_015743.3) into the EcoRI/BamHI sites. HEK293T cells (Cyagen; 50% confluency in DMEM) were co-transfected with the shuttle vector, packaging plasmid pSPAX2 (10.5 μg/dish, Cat# BR036, FengHuiBio), and envelope plasmid pMD2.G (3.5 μg/dish, Cat# BR037, FengHuiBio) using Lipofectamine 3000 (43.4 μL/dish) and P3000 (56 μL/dish). The viral supernatants were collected at 48 and 72 h post-transfection, filtered through a 0.45 μm membrane, and titers were determined to be 3.6 × 108 TU/mL for Lv-Vector and 2.8 × 108 TU/mL for Lv-NR4A3. For cell transduction, target cells were seeded at 2 × 105 cells per well in 6-well plates and infected with virus at an MOI of 20. The envelope treatment was performed via low-speed centrifugation to enhance infection efficiency before continuing culture under standard conditions.

## Western blot
Protein extraction was performed using RIPA lysis buffer supplemented with 1 mM PMSF and the purity of protein in the extract was quantified by the BCA method. Protein samples were isolated by SDS-PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes, then blocked with sealing solution for 1 h. Next, membranes were immunoblotted with primary antibody against NR4A3 (1: 300, Santa Cruz, Cat# sc-393902, USA), GLS2 (1: 1000, Affinity, Cat# DF13386, China), p-cjun(S63) (1: 1000, ThermoFisher, Cat# PA5-17890, China), MBP (1: 500, Affinity, Cat# AF4085, China), P0 (1: 500, Abclonal, Cat# A21931, China), p75NTR (1: 500, Affinity, Cat# DF6821, China) overnight, followed by incubating with HRP-labeled goat anti-mouse antibody (1: 3000, Solarbio, Cat# SE131, China) or goat anti-rabbit antibody (1: 3000, Solarbio, Cat# SE134, China) for 1 h. Finally, protein bands were visualized using an ECL substrate and exposed in a darkroom. Band densities were analyzed with Gel-Pro-Analyzer software.

## Immunohistochemistry
In brief, tissue samples were cut into 5-μm sections, deparaffinized in xylene, and hydrated using graded ethanol. Sections were then treated with antigen repair solution for 10 min, incubated with hydrogen peroxide, and blocked with BSA for 15 min. Sections were further incubated with NR4A3 antibody (1: 50, Santa Cruz, Cat# Sc-393902, USA) at 4 °C overnight. On the second day, sections were incubated with HRP-labeled goat anti-mouse antibody (1: 500, ThermoFisher, Cat# 31430, USA) for 1 h. Staining was developed using diaminobenzidine and hematoxylin. Finally, the images were photographed by the microscope.

## HE staining
Briefly, the sciatic nerve tissue of mice was fixed with ethanol and then sectioned after paraffin embedding. Tissue sections were stained with hematoxylin for 5 min, washed with distilled water, and then stained with eosin for 3 min. Finally, sections were observed under the microscope (OLYMPUS, Japan).

## Toluidine blue staining
The 5-μm tissue sections were stained with toluidine blue for 10 min followed by the addition of acetone for differentiation. Subsequently, slides were dehydrated through graded ethanol, cleared in xylene, and mounted with neutral balsam. Stained sections were examined and imaged under an Olympus BX53 microscope.

## qPCR assay
The RNA was extracted by TRIpure reagent and concentration was measured spectrophotometrically by the NanoDrop One (Thermo, USA). The cDNA was synthesized from 1 μg of total RNA using the All-in-One First-Strand SuperMix with dsDNase treatment to eliminate genomic DNA contamination. Then, quantitative PCR was carried out using Pangaea 3 Real-Time PCR System with the following reaction mixture: 1 μL cDNA, 0.5 μL each of forward and reverse primers, 0.5 μL SYBR Green (Solarbio), 10 μL 2×Fast Taq Plus PCR Master Mix, and nuclease-free water to a final volume of 20 μL. The thermal cycling conditions were: 95 °C for 5 min; 40 cycles of 95 °C for 10 s, 60 °C for 10 s, and 72 °C for 15 s. Gene expression was normalized to a reference gene and analyzed using the 2 – ΔΔ CT method with four technical replicates per sample. The primer information is shown as below: NR4A3, forward 5′-GTGTCTCAGTGTCGGGATG-3′ and reverse 5′-CCTGTTGTAGTGGGCTCTT-3′; MBP, forward 5′-GTACCTGGCCACAGCAAGTA-3′ and reverse 5′-CCCCTGTCACCGCTAAAGAA-3′; P0, forward 5′-TTACACGGACAGGGAAAT-3′ and reverse 5′-AGTGGAAGATCGAAATGG-3′; p75NTR, forward 5′-AGACCTCATAGCCAGCACA-3′ and reverse 5′-CCAAGATGGAGCAATAGACA-3′.

## Analysis of mtDNA
For mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) detection, mtDNA was extracted from cell pellets using a Mitochondrial DNA Isolation Kit. Briefly, cells were washed with cold PBS, homogenized in cytoplasmic extraction buffer, and centrifuged (600g, 4 °C, 5 min) to remove nuclei. The supernatant was further centrifuged (10,000g, 4 °C, 30 min) to pellet mitochondria, which were lysed in mitochondrial lysis buffer and treated with Enzyme B (50 °C, 60 min). The DNA was ethanol-precipitated, washed with 70% ethanol, and resuspended in TE buffer, then quantified by qPCR assay. The 2−ΔΔCT method was used for analysis. In the mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) copy number analysis, qPCR was performed using specific primers targeting the mitochondrial genome (forward: 5′-GCCCATGACCAACATAACTG-3′; reverse: 5′-CCTTGACGGCTATGTTGATG-3′), with normalization to the nuclear-encoded reference gene B2M (forward: 5′-AAACCCCTCAAATTCAAGTA-3′; reverse: 5′-GCAGGCGTATGTATCAGTCT-3′).

## Immunofluorescence
For tissue, briefly, 5-μm paraffin sections of tissue were deparaffinized in xylene and hydrated with graded ethanol. Sections were treated sequentially with boiled antigen repair solution for 10 min and BSA for 15 min. Sections were incubated with S100B antibody (1:50, Proteintech, Cat# 66616-1-Ig, China), p-cJUN(S63) antibody (1:100, Thermo, Cat# PA5-17890, China), NR4A3 antibody (1:50, Affinity, Cat# AF0389, China) overnight. Sections were then incubated with FITC-labeled goat anti-mouse antibody (1: 200, Abcam, Cat# ab6785, UK) and CY3-labeled goat anti-rabbit antibody (1: 200, Abcam, Cat# ab6939, UK) for 90 min. After washing with PBS, Sections were stained using DAPI.
Cell immunofluorescence was performed using p-cjun(S63) antibody (1:100, Thermo, Cat# PA5-17890, USA), cleaved caspase3 antibody (1:100, Affinity, Cat# AF7022, China). In brief, cells were fixed using formaldehyde for 15 min, treated with Triton X-100 for 30 min, and incubated with BSA for 15 min. The primary antibodies were then added to the cells at 4 °C. Next day, the secondary antibody CY3-labeled goat anti-rabbit antibody (1: 200, Abcam, Cat# ab6939, UK) was added. Finally, staining was developed using DAPI.

## Quantitative analysis of ATP, FAD, NAD+/NADH, GSH/GSSG
For ATP measurement, tissues (20 mg) or cells were homogenized in lysis buffer (200 μL), centrifuged (12,000g, 4 °C, 5 min), and analyzed using an ATP detection kit. Protein concentration was determined via BCA assay, and ATP levels were calculated from a standard curve using a SynergyH1 plate reader. For FAD measurement, samples (10 mg tissue or cell pellets) were homogenized in cold buffer, centrifuged (10,000g, 4 °C, 5 min), and analyzed with an FAD assay kit. The standard curve was generated, and absorbance (570 nm) was measured (ELX-800) after 15-min incubation with OxiRed Probe. For NAD+/NADH measurement, tissues (0.01 g) or cells were extracted with acidic/alkaline buffers, boiled, neutralized, and centrifuged (10,000g, 4 °C). Levels were quantified (UV752N, 570 nm) using a Coenzyme I NAD (H) Content Test Kit and normalized to sample weight or cell count. For GSH/GSSG measurement, tissues were homogenized (1:9 weight/volume in Reagent 3), centrifuged (10,000g, 4 °C, 10 min), and analyzed using the T-GSH/GSSG Colorimetric Test Kit. Total glutathione (T-GSH) was measured by mixing supernatants (10 μL) with reaction working solution (Reagents 4 + 5+1), incubating (25 min, RT), and reading absorbance at 412 nm (SynergyH1). GSSG was quantified after GSH masking (Reagents 7 + 8, 1 h, 25 °C), and GSH was calculated as [T-GSH] − 2×[GSSG].

## ROS detection
The cells were incubated with 20 nM MitoTracker Green at room temperature and dark for 30 min. Cells were then washed with serum-free cell culture solution three times and were added with 5 μM MitoSOX red. The cells were further incubated for 30 min and photographed under the microscope.

## ChIP-seq and mRNA-seq analysis
To systematically investigate the molecular mechanisms underlying NR4A3-mediated transcriptional regulation in Schwann cells (SCs), we employed a dual-omics strategy combining chromatin immunoprecipitation sequencing (ChIP-seq) and mRNA sequencing (mRNA-seq) technologies. For ChIP-seq analysis, briefly, cells were cross-linked with formaldehyde, and chromatin was sheared into fragments of 200–500 bp by sonication. Immunoprecipitation was conducted using an anti-NR4A3 antibody, with input DNA serving as the control. Each group contained one sample. The enriched DNA fragments were then purified, and libraries were constructed via end repair, A-tailing, adapter ligation, and PCR amplification. High-throughput sequencing was performed on an Illumina platform, generating paired-end reads. Raw data were processed using fastp to remove low-quality reads and adapter sequences, and clean reads were aligned to the mouse genome (mm10) with Bowtie2. Visualization of NR4A3-bound regions and motif enrichment was generated using Integrative Genomics Viewer (IGV).
Total RNA was extracted from Schwann cells that had been infected with NR4A3 overexpression or with an empty vector (n = 5) using the TRIzol reagent, and ribosomal RNA was depleted with the NEBNext rRNA Depletion Kit. Libraries were constructed via cDNA synthesis, end repair, adapter ligation, and PCR amplification (NEBNext Ultra II RNA Library Prep Kit). Paired-end sequencing (150 bp) was performed on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform, yielding ∼40 million clean reads per sample (Q30 > 94%). Raw data were processed with fastp to remove adapters and low-quality reads, followed by alignment to the mm10 genome and quantification (HTSeq v0.13.5). Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between NR4A3 overexpression and control groups were analyzed using p < 0.01 and |FC| ≥ 1 as significance thresholds.

## Dual-luciferase assay
HEK293T cells were cultured in the DMEM medium with 10% FBS in a 5% CO2 incubator at 37 °C. Cells were seeded in 12-well plates at a cell density of approximately 60%. The GLS2 promoter region was connected to the dual luciferase vector pGL3-Basic, and the cells were co-transfected with pcDNA3.1 or PCDNA3.1-NR4A3 overexpression vectors by Lipofectamine 3000, respectively. Briefly, 2.5 μg of plasmid DNA was diluted in 125 μL of Opti-MEM along with 5 μL of P3000 reagent, mixed with another 125 μL of Opti-MEM containing 5 μL Lipofectamine 3000, incubated for 15 min at room temperature, and added dropwise to the cells. After 48 h, the medium was aspirated, cells were washed with PBS, and the Luciferase activity was measured using a dual-luciferase reporter assay kit. The relative luciferase activity was expressed as the ratio of Firefly to Renilla luminescence.
The dual luciferase mutation experiment is based on the previous publication by Hiroyuki et al. The potential motif sequences recognized by NR4A3 was predicted using the JASPAR database (https://jaspar.elixir.no/matrix/MA1112.1/). The domain mutant was generated by General Biosynthesis Company (China) from the pGL3-Basic−567/+67 plasmids. Mutant promotor was changed from 5′-GAAGGTCA-3′ (−305 to −297) to 5′-GTCAGAAG-3′. The transfection study was performed as described earlier.

## Quantification and statistical analysis
All results are presented as the mean ± SD and statistical analyses were used with GraphPad Prism 9.5 software. A two-tailed Student’s t test was used to evaluate the significance of differences between the two experimental groups. Multiple groups were compared by a one-way or two-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s multiple comparisons tests. p < 0.05 was considered to indicate a statistically significant difference.
