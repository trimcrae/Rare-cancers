## Abstract
Rupture of vulnerable carotid atherosclerotic plaque is one of the leading causes of ischemic stroke. However, the mechanisms driving the transition from stable to vulnerable plaques have not yet been elucidated. NR4A1 is an orphan nuclear receptor that functions in various inflammatory diseases. To explore the role of NR4A1 in vulnerable plaque formation, we generated a vulnerable plaque mouse model by combining partial ligation of the left common carotid artery and left renal artery in ApoE−/− and ApoE−/−;NR4A1−/− mice. Our research revealed that NR4A1 deficiency significantly worsened the pathology of vulnerable plaque, increasing intraplaque hemorrhage, rupture with thrombus, and the occurrence of multilayer with discontinuity. Moreover, NR4A1 deficiency exacerbated macrophage infiltration, inflammation, and oxidative stress. Mechanistically, we identified Bcat1 as the target of NR4A1. NR4A1 modulated the integrated stress response (ISR) in macrophages by transcriptionally inhibiting Bcat1, thus influencing the progression of vulnerable plaque. ISR inhibitor GSK2606414 or Bcat1 inhibitor ERG240 significantly ameliorated atherosclerotic plaque formation and increased plaque stability. Notably, supplementation with Celastrol, an herbal extract, stabilized atherosclerotic plaques in mice. These findings suggest that NR4A1 deficiency exacerbates vulnerable plaque by activating ISR via targeting Bcat1. The NR4A1/Bcat1/ISR axis is therefore an important therapeutic target for stabilizing atherosclerotic plaque.

## Introduction
Rupture of vulnerable atherosclerotic plaque is the main trigger of acute cardiovascular events and is the leading cause of mortality worldwide [ ]. Vulnerable plaques, characterized by thin fibrous caps, large lipid cores, decreased extracellular matrix content, and a high inflammatory cell content, are prone to rupture, leading to thrombotic occlusion of the vessel and, consequently, tissue necrosis [ ]. Although the mechanisms driving the transition from stable to vulnerable plaques are not yet fully elucidated, recent studies have highlighted the roles of various factors, including inflammation and macrophage, in this process [ ]. For example, RORα stabilizes rupture-prone plaques by modulating macrophage polarization, which in turn diminishes intraplaque inflammation and the incidence of plaque rupture [ ]. Likewise, the absence of NR1D1 has been linked to heightened plaque vulnerability, primarily through mechanisms involving macrophage pyroptosis and inflammation [ ]. Despite these efforts, the clinical application of such technologies has not yet translated into improved risk prediction for acute coronary events, indicating a need for a broader focus on the overall atherosclerotic disease burden rather than focusing exclusively on individual plaques. A more detailed understanding of the mechanisms underlying plaque destabilization is of great clinical significance.
The integrated stress response (ISR) constitutes an adaptive signaling pathway that responds to both intrinsic and extrinsic stressors, including viral infections, heme deprivation, hypoxia, nutrient deprivation, and acidosis [ ]. The reprogramming of gene expression mediated by the ISR can facilitate either cell survival or cell death, contingent upon the nature, intensity, and duration of the stimulus [ ]. Although primarily serving as an adaptive homeostatic mechanism, ISR signaling may exacerbate pathological conditions if activated excessively or inappropriately [ ]. In the cardiovascular setting, it is noteworthy that a range of atherogenic lipids have been demonstrated to induce ISR signaling in cultured cell models [ , ]. Moreover, ISR signaling is markedly hyperactivated in atherosclerotic plaques observed in both experimental animal models and human subjects [ ]. Current evidence further suggests that ISR signaling serves as a critical intermediary connecting dyslipidemia with the activation of both local and systemic inflammatory responses in the pathogenesis of cardiovascular diseases [ ]. Therefore, elucidating the mechanisms underlying macrophage ISR in vulnerable plaques could offer a novel therapeutic target for addressing rupture-prone plaques and subsequent cardiovascular events.
Nuclear receptor NR4A1, an orphan member of the NR4A superfamily that also comprises Nurr-1 (NR4A2) and NOR-1 (NR4A3), is known to sense microenvironmental changes and control both physiological and pathological processes such as cell proliferation, immune response, metabolic regulation and apoptosis through genomic and non-genomic actions [ ]. The expression of NR4A1 increases following immune cell stimulation and is differentially expressed in chronically inflamed organs in both human and experimental models [ ]. Furthermore, in a variety of animal models dedicated to study inflammatory diseases, changes in NR4A1 expression alter disease outcome [ ]. We previously found that NR4A1 protected against oxidative stress by maintaining mitochondrial homeostasis via regulating mitochondrial fission and mitophagy in smooth muscle cell [ ]. Furthermore, NR4A1 protected against abdominal aortic aneurysm by ameliorating inflammation via suppressing LOX-115. However, the role of NR4A1 in atherosclerotic plaque vulnerability remains unclear. NR4A1 serves as a critical regulator of cellular functions, influencing not only immune responses but also metabolic pathways and cell fate decisions, highlighting its importance in both normal physiology and disease states.
In this study, we aim to investigate the role of NR4A1 in carotid plaque formation and vulnerability and to explore the underlying molecular mechanisms. We found that NR4A1 deficiency promotes carotid plaque vulnerability by regulating Bcat1-mediated ISR in macrophages. Moreover, pharmacological activation of NR4A1 by celastrol, a traditional Chinese medicine extract, stabilized atherosclerotic plaques.

## Materials and methods

## Animal models of rupture-prone vulnerable plaques
Animal experiments were carried out according to the National Institutes of Health Guidelines on the Use of Laboratory Animals and were approved by the Ethics Committee of Shanghai Chest Hospital.
We employed CRISPR/Cas9 approach to generate NR4A1 knockout mice. ApoE−/− mice were purchased from the Shanghai Model Organisms Center, Inc. NR4A1−/− mice were crossbred with ApoE−/− mice to produce ApoE−/−;NR4A1−/− mice. Male mice aged 7 weeks were utilized for the experimental procedures. Mice were maintained in controlled conditions of 24 ± 2 ℃ and 40 ± 5% humidity, subjected to a 12-hour light/dark cycle, and allowed free access to water and food.
In our study, we employed an established protocol for developing an animal model of rupture-prone atherosclerotic plaques [ – ]. Specifically, male mice underwent a one-week acclimatization period preceding the experimental phase, during which they were maintained on a high-fat diet (1.25% cholesterol and 21% fat; 42% kcal as fat). The male ApoE−/− mice were then subjected to partial ligation of the left common carotid artery (LCCA) and left renal artery (LRA). Plaque rupture was defined by an area of fibrous cap disruption. Intraplaque hemorrhage is defined as the deposition of blood products inside the plaque [ , ]. Mice were euthanized 8 weeks after interventions and the carotid arteries were isolated for further analysis. All procedures were performed under a dissecting microscope.
To further verify the hypothesis that NR4A1 plays its roles in the development of vulnerable atherosclerotic plaque via the regulation of Bcat1-mediated ISR, ISR inhibitor GSK2606414 (5 mg/kg/day, Cat# A154425, AmBeed) [ ] and Bcat1 inhibitor ERG240 (5 mg/kg/day, Cat# T67842, Targetmol) [ ] were administered to ApoE−/− and ApoE−/−;NR4A1−/− mice by intraperitoneal injection for 4 weeks, respectively. In addition, Celastrol, a bioactive compound primarily derived from the Chinese medicinal herb Tripterygium wilfordii Hook.f., has been identified as a NR4A1 agonist, which interacts with NR4A1 to mitigate inflammatory processes [ ]. To investigate the effect of celastrol on carotid plaque vulnerability, celastrol (1 mg/kg/d, Cat# HY-13067, MedChemExpress) [ ] was administered by intraperitoneal injection every other day for 4 weeks.

## Tissue collection and analysis
Following anesthesia, blood samples were obtained from the mice via orbital extraction to procure serum, after which the mice were euthanized through cervical dislocation. Tissues designated for pathological diagnosis or immunofluorescence analysis were preserved using the optimal cutting temperature (OCT; Sakura Finetechnic) compound and subsequently processed into frozen sections. The OCT-embedded sections were prepared at a thickness of 8 μm. Samples intended for PCR analysis were rapidly frozen in liquid nitrogen.

## Immunofluorescence staining
For immunofluorescence staining, frozen sections were fixed in cold methanol at -20℃ for 10 min. After blocking with 5% normal goat serum for 1 h, the sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against NR4A1 (1:100; Cat# ab13851; abcam, UK), CD68 (1:50; Cat# ab53444; abcam, UK), Bcat1 (1:200; Cat# 13640-1-AP; proteintech, China), P-PERK (1:200; Cat# 3179; Invitrogen), P-eIF2α (1:200; Cat# 144-28G; Invitrogen). After three washes with PBS, Alexa Fluor 488/555 conjugated secondary antibodies (1:200; Invitrogen, USA) were applied for 1 h at 37℃ in the dark. Finally, the sections were mounted with 4’,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI) and viewed using fluorescence microscopy.

## Protein extraction and Western blot
Total proteins were extracted from cultured cells using RIPA lysis buffer, which was supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitor cocktails and phenylmethylsulfonyl fluoride (PMSF). Protein concentrations were quantified using a standard bicinchoninic acid (BCA) assay. Proteins were heat-denatured, separated via sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE), and subsequently transferred onto polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membranes. Membranes were blocked with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA) in Tris-buffered saline with TBST for 1 h at 37 °C, followed by an overnight incubation with primary antibodies at 4 °C. Afterward, the membranes were incubated with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h at 37 °C. Following three washes with TBST, the membranes were visualized with enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL; Millipore). The following antibodies were used: NR4A1 (1:1000; Cat# ab13851; abcam, UK), Bcat1 (1:1000; Cat# 13640-1-AP; proteintech, China), P-PERK (1:1000; Cat# 3179; CST), PERK (1:1000; Cat# 3192; CST), P-eIF2α (1:1000; Cat# 144-28G; Invitrogen), eIF2α (1:1000; Cat# AHO0802; Invitrogen), GAPDH (1:1000; Cat# ab371668; abcam).

## RNA isolation and real-time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR)
Isolation of total RNA from murine tissues or cultured cells was performed using TRIzol Reagent in accordance with the manufacturer’s protocol (Invitrogen, Cat# a5596-018). Total RNA (1 µg) was used in a 20 µL reaction to synthesise cDNA using PrimeScript™ RT Master Mix (Takara, Cat# RR036A). RT-qPCR amplification was performed employing the SYBR Premix Ex Taq™ (Takara, Cat# DRR081A) on a LightCycler® 480 Real-Time PCR System (Roche). Expression levels of target genes were expressed as fold change normalised to Gapdh levels. The primer sequences utilized are detailed in Table .

## Histological analysis
Frozen carotid artery specimens were serially sectioned and stained using hematoxylin and eosin (H&E), Masson’s trichrome, and Oil Red O, following established protocols, to evaluate morphological alterations associated with various pathological conditions. To assess multiple plaque parameters, such as plaque burden, macrophage infiltration, collagen deposition, and lipid accumulation, cryosections were obtained at 100 μm intervals along the common carotid artery. For morphometric data, 8 sections were analyzed per mouse. As previously described, the positive areas were calculated using ImagePro Plus software and normalized to the plaque area [ ].

## Isolation, culture, and treatment of primary cells
Bone marrow-derived macrophages (BMDMs) were harvested from the femur and tibia of 6-8-week-old mice. Bone marrow was flushed out with PBS, centrifuged at 1000 rpm for 5 min at 4℃, and the supernatant was discarded. Erythrocyte lysate was added, incubated for 10 min, then the reaction was stopped with medium and centrifuged again for 5 min. The resulting cell pellet was resuspended in complete media (RPMI 1640 medium with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 100 U/ mL penicillin, and 100 mg/mL streptomycin) containing 10 ng/mL M-CSF (Cat# 416-ML-050; R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN, USA) and inoculated into petri dishes. The medium was refreshed on day 3, and mature BMDMs were ready by day 7. BMDMs were then treated with specified oxLDL concentrations for designated durations.

## RNA interference
The small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) targeting mouse Bcat1 was purchased from OBiO Technology Corp.,Ltd. (Shanghai, China). A list of siRNA constructs is described in Supplementary Table . The siRNA transfection was performed using Lipofectamine RNAiMAX Transfection Reagent (Invitrogen, USA), in accordance with the manufacturer’s protocol. The knockdown efficiency was confirmed by western blot.

## Transwell assay
Transwell assays were conducted following established protocols as previously described [ ]. For the Transwell invasion assays, 8.0 μm Transwell permeable supports (Corning, USA) were utilized. BMDMs were placed in 100 µL of serum-free medium in the upper chamber at 1 × 10^5 cells, while the lower chamber contained 600 µL of medium with 10% FBS. After incubation, non-invasive cells were removed with a cotton swab. Invading cells on the membrane’s underside were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 10 min, stained with 0.4% crystal violet, and then imaged with a digital microscope.

## RNA-sequencing
Cells were collected for RNA extraction with Trizol, using 1 µg RNA per sample for preparation. Sequencing libraries were created with the Hieff NGS Ultima Kit for Illumina and sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq6000 for 150 bp paired-end reads. Raw reads were processed using the BMK Cloud online platform. Differential expression analysis between two groups was conducted with DESeq2, which uses a negative binomial model for statistical assessment. P-values were adjusted for false discovery rate using the Benjamini-Hochberg method. Genes with an adjusted P-value < 0.01 and a Fold Change ≥ 2 were considered differentially expressed.

## Dual-luciferase assay
To assess Bcat1 activity, 293T cells were initially seeded into 24-well plates. After a 12-hour incubation period, the cells underwent transient transfection with NR4A1 and dual-luciferase reporter plasmids, utilizing Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen), in accordance with the manufacturer’s guidelines. Following a subsequent 24-hour incubation, cell lysates were generated by incubating the cells in a 1× lysis buffer (Luciferase Assay System, Promega, Madison, WI, USA) for 10 min. Finally, the activities of firefly and Renilla luciferases were quantified following the dual-luciferase assay protocol provided by the manufacturer.

## CHIP assay
Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) assays were conducted utilizing the SimpleChIP® Enzymatic Chromatin IP Kit (Cat# 9003, Cell Signaling Technology) in accordance with the manufacturer’s protocol. Bone marrow-derived macrophages (BMDMs) were exposed to either phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) or oxidized low-density lipoprotein (ox-LDL) at a concentration of 100 µg/ml for a duration of 24 h. Subsequently, the cells were crosslinked with 1% formaldehyde for 20 min at ambient temperature. The crosslinking process was halted by the addition of glycine. The crosslinked chromatin was then subjected to digestion with Micrococcal Nuclease, followed by shearing. The sheared chromatin was immunoprecipitated using either anti-NR4A1 or anti-IgG antibodies. The immunoprecipitated chromatin was decrosslinked at 65 °C for 4 h and subsequently purified using spin column purification. ChIP PCR was performed with primers encompassing the loci of the Bcat1 promoter in mice: forward: GCTATCTTCTGCTCCTGCGT and reverse: CCGAAAGGTGTGTCACTGGA. Then, Bcat1 promoter-specific PCR products were subjected to agarose gel electrophoresis analysis.

## Statistical analysis
All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 6 or SPSS version 20.0. Continuous variables were shown as mean ± SEM, and categorical variables as numbers or percentages. The Shapiro-Wilk test assessed data normality. For group comparisons, the student’s t-test was used for equal variances, and the unequal variance t-test for unequal variances, if data were normal. The Mann-Whitney U test was applied for non-normal data. For comparisons with more than two groups, the Brown-Forsythe test checked variance homogeneity. If variances were equal, a one-way ANOVA with Bonferroni post hoc analysis was used. If not, a Welch ANOVA with Tamhane T2 post hoc analysis was conducted. For comparisons involving more than two groups and variables, a two-way ANOVA with Bonferroni post hoc analysis was applied. Bivariate comparisons of categorical variables were performed with the χ2 test. A value of P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results

## NR4A1 expression is upregulated during vulnerable plaque formation
The vulnerable plaque mouse model was generated as shown in Fig.  A. Dual immunofluorescence staining for CD68 and NR4A1 revealed a heightened presence of infiltrating macrophages in vulnerable plaques, with high expression of NR4A1 within these macrophages (Fig.  B). Moreover, we further demonstrated that the mRNA expression of NR4A1 was significantly upregulated in vulnerable carotid plaques, compared with arterial tissues from the sham group (Fig.  C).
 To further elucidate the role of NR4A1 in macrophages in vulnerable plaques, BMDMs were isolated and treated with oxLDL in vitro. OxLDL markedly increased NR4A1 level in a time- and dose-dependent manner, as shown by RT-qPCR and western blot (Fig.  D-G). This phenomenon was further corroborated by immunofluorescence staining, which confirmed the elevated NR4A1 levels in BMDMs following oxLDL stimulation (Fig.  H). Altogether, these results indicate that NR4A1 is upregulated in macrophages during vulnerable plaque formation, suggesting a potential role of NR4A1 in the regulation of plaque vulnerability.

## NR4A1 deficiency promotes carotid plaque vulnerability and rupture
We then examined the effect of NR4A1 genotype on the development of vulnerable plaques in ApoE−/− and ApoE−/−;NR4A1−/− mice (Fig.  A; Figure ). The gross morphology facilitates the observation of intramural or occlusive thrombi with ruptured fibrous caps. Our findings indicated that ApoE−/−;NR4A1−/− mice exhibited a significantly greater carotid artery plaque burden compared to ApoE−/− mice (Fig.  B). Moreover, ApoE−/−;NR4A1−/− mice demonstrated a significantly higher incidence of intraplaque hemorrhage (70% of ApoE−/−;NR4A1−/− mice compared with 20% of ApoE−/− mice; P < 0.05) and spontaneous plaque rupture accompanied by thrombus formation (50% of ApoE−/−;NR4A1−/− mice compared with 15% of ApoE−/− mice; P < 0.05) (Fig.  C and D). Additionally, there was an observed increase in the trend of multiple layers with discontinuity in ApoE−/−;NR4A1−/− mice (65% versus 25%; P < 0.05), which is indicative of heightened plaque instability (Fig.  E).
 To comprehensively assess the overall plaque burden and detailed plaque composition, carotid frozen sections were stained with H&E, Masson’s trichrome, and oil red O. The carotid plaques of ApoE−/−;NR4A1−/− mice exhibited a significantly larger atherosclerotic area and increased lipid content compared to those of ApoE−/− mice (Fig.  F and G). However, there was no statistically significant difference for the collagen levels between these two groups (Fig.  F and G). Furthermore, the collagen-to-lipid ratio, a reliable indicator of plaque stability [ ], was markedly decreased in ApoE−/−;NR4A1−/− mice (Fig.  F and G). Overall, the findings indicate that NR4A1 deficiency promotes carotid plaque formation and vulnerability.

## NR4A1 deficiency aggravates inflammation and oxidative stress in atherosclerotic plaques
To investigate the role of NR4A1 in the regulation of macrophages in inflammatory vascular diseases, we performed bulk RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) of BMDMs isolated from NR4A1−/− mice and wild-type (WT) littermates and stimulated with oxLDL (100 µg/ml) for 3 h. Significant differences in the expression of genes associated with immunity and inflammation between the two groups were identified (Fig.  A). We then assessed the phenotypic variations in the inflammatory response of vulnerable plaque between the ApoE−/− and ApoE−/−;NR4A1−/− groups. Immunofluorescence staining for CD68 revealed a significant increase in macrophage infiltration in vulnerable plaques from ApoE−/−;NR4A1−/− mice compared to those from ApoE−/− mice (Fig.  B). Consistent with increased macrophage infiltration, the results of RT-qPCR further indicated higher levels of proinflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, MCP-1, IL-6, and IL-1β) in atherosclerotic plaques in ApoE−/−;NR4A1−/− mice (Fig.  C). In addition, the expression levels of NLRP3 and IL-1β were significantly up-regulated in vascular lesions in ApoE−/−;NR4A1−/− mice, suggesting that NR4A1 deficiency promotes inflammatory response (Fig.  D). Furthermore, we found that NR4A1-deficient BMDMs exhibited significantly increased oxLDL-induced cell migration compared to control BMDMs (Fig.  E), which was consistent with the results in vivo.
 We further conducted an in-depth analysis of the role of NR4A1 in modulating oxidative stress within vulnerable plaques. Dihydroethidium (DHE) fluorescence staining and ROS flow cytometry demonstrated that NR4A1 knockout significantly enhanced cellular oxidative stress levels upon stimulation with oxLDL (Fig.  F and G). Consistent with in vitro data, we found that the absence of NR4A1 markedly elevated ROS production in carotid atherosclerotic plaques in mice (Fig.  H).
Altogether, these results indicate that NR4A1 deficiency significantly enhances inflammation and oxidative stress in atherosclerotic plaques.

## NR4A1 deficiency promotes carotid plaque vulnerability via its regulation of ISR
The ISR is an adaptive response of cells to environmental stimuli [ ]. However, under conditions of prolonged or severe stress, the ISR can shift towards a cell death program [ ]. Numerous studies have demonstrated that persistent activation of the ISR, indicated by the phosphorylation of eIF2α and PERK, is strongly implicated in the pathogenesis of atherosclerosis [ , , ]. To define the possible mechanism by which NR4A1 exerts its effect on carotid plaque vulnerability, we examined the activation of ISR in atherosclerotic plaques of ApoE−/− and ApoE−/−;NR4A1−/− mice. Double immunofluorescence assays targeting phosphorylated eIF2α (P-eIF2α) or phosphorylated PERK (P-PERK) in conjunction with CD68 revealed an increased ISR activation in vascular lesions from ApoE−/−;NR4A1−/− mice (Fig.  A and B). Consistent with in vivo data, we found that NR4A1-deficient BMDMs exhibited increased P-eIF2α and P-PERK after oxLDL stimulation (Fig.  C-E), suggesting that NR4A1 deficiency exacerbates the activation of the ISR.
 To further elucidate the role of ISR signaling in the function of NR4A1 in the vulnerable plaque, ApoE−/− and ApoE−/−;NR4A1−/− mice were treated with GSK2606414, an ISR inhibitor, at a dosage of 5 mg/kg/day over a period of 4 weeks (Fig.  A). We found that GSK2606414 significantly suppressed the activation of PERK and eIF2α in BMDMs in vitro (Figure S2). As shown by the gross morphology, GSK2606414 treatment led to a reduction in carotid artery plaque burden in ApoE−/− mice. It is noteworthy that, although the carotid plaque burden in ApoE−/−;NR4A1−/− mice was greater than that observed in the ApoE−/− group, GSK2606414 treatment significantly ameliorated the carotid plaque burden in the NR4A1 deficiency group, and the improvement rendered the plaque burden comparable to that of the ApoE−/− mice treated with GSK2606414 (Fig.  B). Moreover, GSK2606414 significantly decreased the incidence of intraplaque hemorrhage, rupture with thrombus, and multilayer with discontinuity in both ApoE−/− and ApoE−/−;NR4A1−/− mice, with particularly pronounced effects observed in the ApoE−/−;NR4A1−/− cohort (Fig.  C-E). To thoroughly evaluate the total plaque burden and specific plaque composition, carotid frozen sections were stained using H&E, Masson’s trichrome, and oil red O. In comparison to mice treated with the vehicle, those treated with GSK2606414 exhibited a significantly reduced carotid atherosclerotic plaque area and a markedly lower lipid content in both ApoE−/− and ApoE−/−;NR4A1−/− genetic backgrounds. Notably, the overall plaque burden in the GSK2606414 treatment groups from ApoE−/− and ApoE−/−;NR4A1−/− mice remained largely consistent. Nonetheless, the collagen deposition did not differ statistically between the groups (Fig.  F and G). These results suggest that the increased carotid artery plaque burden and instability in ApoE−/−;NR4A1−/− mice were partly reversed by GSK2606414 treatment. Furthermore, the deficiency of NR4A1 appears to enhance the vulnerability of carotid plaques, potentially through its regulation of the ISR.
 

## NR4A1 regulates ISR in vulnerable plaques by transcriptionally repressing Bcat1
To identify the molecular mechanism underlying the regulation of NR4A1 in ISR in macrophages, we reviewed the bulk RNA-seq data on BMDMs isolated from WT and NR4A1−/− mice and stimulated with oxLDL. As shown in Figs.  A and B and 350 genes were upregulated, and 49 genes were downregulated in NR4A1−/− BMDMs compared to WT BMDMs. KEGG analysis revealed significant changes in the cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction and JAK-STAT signaling pathways (Fig.  A-C). To identify genes directly regulated by NR4A1, we analyzed the Chromatin immunoprecipitation-sequencing (ChIP-seq) dataset from GEO database (GSE102393) and linked it with RNA-seq data. This analysis revealed that among the differentially expressed genes, there were 71 genes directly upregulated and 16 genes directly downregulated by NR4A1 (Fig.  D and E). Notably, among the top five genes with significant expression changes, Branched-chain amino acid transaminase 1 (Bcat1), an enzyme that initiates the catabolism of branched-chain amino acids, was found to significantly promote atherosclerosis [ ]. Consequently, we hypothesize that NR4A1 could be implicated in the formation and progression of vulnerable plaques via the transcriptional regulation of Bcat1.
 In an in vitro macrophage model, our findings indicated that oxLDL significantly elevated the protein expression levels of Bcat1. Furthermore, knockout of NR4A1 resulted in an additional increase in Bcat1 levels (Fig.  F), implying a regulatory role of NR4A1 on Bcat1 expression. This alteration was corroborated by double immunofluorescence analysis (Fig.  G). Consistent with the in vitro results, both immunofluorescence and RT-qPCR analyses of vulnerable carotid plaques mirrored the observed cellular experimental outcomes (Fig.  H and I).
To elucidate the mechanism through which NR4A1 silencing leads to the upregulation of Bcat1 protein, we investigated the potential direct interaction between NR4A1 and the Bcat1 gene promoter, as well as its role in transcriptional repression. ChIP assay results derived from BMDMs indicated that NR4A1 was capable of binding to the promoter region of Bcat1, with evidence suggesting that this interaction may be augmented following ox-LDL stimulation (Fig.  J and K). We cloned the luciferase reporter gene under the control of the Bcat1 gene promoter fragment and conducted promoter reporter gene assays in 293T cells. The luciferase assay results demonstrated that NR4A1 significantly diminished Bcat1 luciferase activity, suggesting that NR4A1 transcriptionally inhibits the Bcat1 promoter (Fig.  L and M). These findings critically suggest that NR4A1 exerts transcriptional repression on Bcat1 in macrophages through direct binding to the promoter region of Bcat1.
To explore the potential regulatory role of NR4A1 on the ISR via transcriptional modulation of Bcat1, we conducted an analysis of pivotal proteins involved in ISR signaling pathways. Utilizing small interfering RNA (siRNA) specific to Bcat1, we effectively suppressed Bcat1 expression (Figure S3). We found that the knockdown of Bcat1 markedly mitigated the influence of NR4A1 on ISR-related proteins, such as P-PERK and P-eIF2a (Fig.  N). Taken together, these findings indicate that the adverse effect of NR4A1 deficiency in ISR may partly depends on Bcat1 signaling.

## Bcat1 inhibitor counteracts the adverse effects of NR4A1 deficiency on vulnerable plaque
To verify the hypothesis that NR4A1 plays its roles in the development of vulnerable atherosclerotic plaque via its regulation of Bcat1-mediated ISR, we administered ERG240, a leucine analogue that inhibits Bcat1 activity, to ApoE−/− and ApoE−/−;NR4A1−/− mice. The intervention was conducted at a dosage of 5 mg/kg/day for a duration of 4 weeks (Fig.  A). Our study demonstrated that ERG240 effectively reduced carotid plaque burden in both ApoE−/− and ApoE−/−;NR4A1−/− mice. Furthermore, ERG240 mitigated the additional adverse effects associated with NR4A1 deficiency (Fig.  B). In both ApoE−/− and ApoE−/−;NR4A1−/− group, ERG240 significantly decreased the incidence of intraplaque hemorrhage, rupture with thrombus and multilayer with discontinuity compared to the vehicle group (Fig.  C-E). To evaluate the overall plaque burden and the specific composition of plaques, frozen sections of the carotid artery were subjected to staining with H&E, Oil Red O, and Masson’s trichrome. NR4A1 deficiency resulted in an increase in both the atherosclerotic area and lipid content within carotid plaques in mice. In contrast, the presence of ERG240 markedly ameliorated these phenotypic alterations and mitigated the detrimental effects associated with the NR4A1 gene knockout. Nonetheless, the collagen deposition did not differ statistically between the groups (Fig.  F and G). Altogether, the findings indicated that the elevated carotid plaque burden and instability observed in ApoE−/−;NR4A1−/− mice could be ameliorated by the ERG240. Furthermore, NR4A1 appeared to play a role in modulating the progression of vulnerable plaques through the regulation of Bcat1.
 

## NR4A1 agonist celastrol alleviates vulnerable plaque formation
Celastrol is a predominantly active natural product extracted from the Chinese herb Tripterygium wilfordii Hook.f. It has been reported that celastrol is an NR4A1 agonist that binds NR4A1 to inhibit inflammation [ , – ]. We found that celastrol induced a time-dependent increase of NR4A1 expression in BMDMs in vitro (Figure S4). To dissect the in vivo effects of celastrol treatment in vulnerable plaque formation, ApoE−/− mice that were fed a high-fat diet and subjected to partial ligation of the LCCA and LRA were intraperitoneally injected with celastrol (1 mg/kg/d) or vehicle every other day for 4 weeks (Fig.  A). Notably, celastrol treatment led to a reduction in carotid plaque burden in ApoE−/− mice (Fig.  B). Celastrol treatment significantly prevented vulnerable plaque rupture, as indicated by a lower incidence of intraplaque hemorrhage (10% versus 30%; p < 0.05), lower incidence of spontaneous plaque rupture (5% versus 20%; p < 0.05), and lower incidence of multilayer lesions (5% versus 35%; p < 0.05) (Fig.  C and E). Moreover, celastrol treatment reduced lesion size and lipid deposition in carotid arteries of ApoE−/− mice underwent partial ligation of the LCCA and LRA (Fig.  F and G). There was no significant difference in collagen deposition between the two groups (Fig.  F and G). However, in celastrol-treated ApoE−/− mice, there was a significant increase in the collagen-to-lipid ratio (Fig.  F and G), which is a reliable marker of plaque stability. These findings indicate that celastrol treatment ameliorates atherosclerotic plaque formation and enhances plaque stability.
 

## Discussions
The rupture of vulnerable atherosclerotic plaque frequently precipitates acute cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events. Therefore, a more detailed understanding of the mechanisms underlying plaque vulnerability may help us to improve treatment for cardiovascular diseases. Although the roles of NR4A1 in oncology have been well-documented [ ], whether there exists any crosstalk between NR4A1 and the ISR, and how this crosstalk mediates plaque vulnerability remain largely unknown. In this study, we observed an upregulation of NR4A1 in macrophages within vulnerable plaques. NR4A1 deficiency augments plaque vulnerability and rupture. Mechanistically, NR4A1 modulates the ISR by transcriptionally repressing Bcat1, thereby exerting a protective effect on vulnerable plaques. ISR inhibitor GSK2606414 or Bcat1 inhibitor ERG240 significantly ameliorated vulnerable plaque pathological process. Notably, supplementation with Celastrol, an active natural product extracted from the Chinese herb Tripterygium wilfordii Hook.f. ameliorates atherosclerotic plaque formation and enhances plaque stability in mice (Fig.  ). Overall, the results of the present study provide the first evidence for a novel regulatory NR4A1/Bcat1/ISR signaling axis in vulnerable plaques progression.
 The orphan nuclear receptor NR4A1 is a member of the NR4A subfamily of nuclear receptors that is strongly implicated in the pathogenesis of cancer [ ] and cardiovascular diseases, including atherosclerosis, vascular remodeling, cardiac hypertrophy, and cardiac ischemia/reperfusion injury [ – ]. Our group have previously demonstrated the important roles of NR4A1 in vascular remodeling and the formation of abdominal aortic aneurysms [ , , , ]. NR4A1 is highly expressed in human atherosclerotic lesions [ , ], and its expression can be induced by multiple stimuli related to the development of atherosclerosis including cytokines, oxLDL, and vascular injury [ , , ]. NR4A1 has been demonstrated to be crucial in the regulation of inflammatory response, macrophage polarization and migration, monocyte subset differentiation, and lipid and glucose metabolism [ , – ], indicating the potential of NR4A1 as a therapeutic target for atherosclerosis. Two previous studies using NR4A1 knockout mice have showed that NR4A1 has a protective, anti-inflammatory function in atherosclerosis [ , ]. Another research indicated that NR4A1 deletion does not affect the formation of atherosclerotic lesions in mice [ ]. In this present study, we utilized a carotid vulnerable plaque mouse model which was generated by our group [ – ] and tried to elucidate the involvement of NR4A1 in plaque vulnerability and rupture. We demonstrated that NR4A1 deficiency leads to increased plaque vulnerability and rupture, rather than merely promoting atherosclerotic plaque formation. Our findings provide compelling evidence supporting NR4A1 as a crucial regulator for atherosclerotic plaque stability.
The ISR represents a cellular adaptive mechanism capable of facilitating either cell survival or cell death, dependent upon the nature, intensity, and duration of the stimulus [ ]. For instance, exposure to mild stress can activate protective pathways that bolster cell survival, whereas severe or prolonged stress may initiate apoptotic pathways, culminating in cell death. This duality is similarly evident in other stress-related contexts, such as oxidative stress, where the equilibrium between survival and death is intricately modulated by the cellular context and the specific stressor involved. Moreover, the ISR does not function as an isolated pathway; rather, it interacts with a multitude of signaling networks, such as autophagy and apoptosis, to modulate cellular fate decisions. A comprehensive understanding of the ISR and its influence on cell survival or apoptosis is essential for the development of therapeutic strategies targeting stress-related pathologies, including cancer and neurodegenerative disorders. Numerous studies have documented that the ISR is excessively activated in the context of atherosclerosis, resulting in cellular apoptosis. Moreover, the initiation of the ISR has been implicated in the advancement and progression of atherosclerotic disease. Our research identified that in vulnerable carotid plaques, the ISR was activated, and the knockout of NR4A1 further intensified the ISR, thereby enhancing cellular inflammation and oxidative stress. Notably, pharmacological inhibition of the ISR markedly attenuated the progression of vulnerable carotid plaques.
Bcat1 is the major isoform of BCAT, catalyzing the initial step in the catabolic pathway of branched-chain amino acids (BCAAs) [ ]. Bcat1 is critically implicated in a range of pathological conditions, notably cancer and cardiovascular diseases [ ]. In the context of glioblastoma, Bcat1 has been demonstrated to sustain the undifferentiated state of tumor cells, a condition linked to heightened oxidative stress and an immunosuppressive microenvironment [ ]. Moreover, Bcat1 contributes to the pathogenesis of atherosclerosis by modulating lipid accumulation and foam cell formation, and enhancing the inflammatory response in macrophages [ ]. In this study, we present novel findings on the role of Bcat1 in the pathogenesis of vulnerable carotid plaques. Our research demonstrates that Bcat1 is upregulated in these plaques and functions as a mediator between NR4A1 and the ISR, thereby exacerbating plaque vulnerability. Most importantly, Bcat1 inhibition significantly ameliorates carotid plaque burden and vulnerability. Elevated BCAAs have been implicated in atherosclerotic progression [ , ]. In a recent study, NR4A1 has been shown to play a role in amino acid homeostasis through the MEF2D-NR4A1-FAM134B2-mediated reticulophagy pathway [ ]. Future research is needed to investigate whether BCAAs are involved in NR4A1-mediated plaque stability.
Celastrol, a bioactive compound derived from the plant Tripterygium wilfordii, is renowned for its significant anti-inflammatory properties [ ]. Recent research has underscored its modulatory effects on a range of inflammatory diseases [ ]. Specifically, celastrol has been demonstrated to mitigate the inflammatory response in macrophages by inhibiting the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome and decreasing the secretion of proinflammatory cytokines, including IL-6, TNF-α, and IL-1β[ ]. Furthermore, celastrol modulates inflammatory and immune responses through the regulation of the NF-κB signaling pathway, indicating its potential therapeutic role in the treatment of atherosclerosis and its associated complications [ ]. Beyond its direct impact on inflammatory pathways, celastrol has been investigated for its capacity to enhance autophagy and restore cellular homeostasis across various inflammatory contexts. This dual action of inhibiting inflammation while promoting cellular repair mechanisms positions celastrol as a promising candidate for the treatment of chronic inflammatory diseases [ ]. Significantly, prior research has identified NR4A1 as a direct intracellular target of celastrol, which binds to NR4A1 and subsequently inhibits inflammation [ ]. Our investigation revealed that Celastrol can effectively ameliorate the progression of vulnerable plaques in the carotid artery.
Several study limitations should be considered. Firstly, it is important to acknowledge that NR4A1 exhibits diverse roles across various cell types. Therefore, employing macrophage-specific NR4A1 knockout mice remains essential to elucidate the role of macrophage NR4A1 in carotid plaque vulnerability. Secondly, although the role of NR4A1 in vulnerable carotid plaques has been validated in a mouse model, these animal models have inherent limitations in resembling human vulnerable plaque formation. Thirdly, while our findings suggest that the ISR inhibitor GSK2606414, the Bcat1 inhibitor ERG240, and the NR4A1 agonist Celastrol hold promise as therapeutic approaches for vulnerable carotid plaques, this conclusion requires further substantiation through extensive clinical studies.

## Conclusion
In conclusion, the findings of this study underscore the critical role of NR4A1 in the development of vulnerable plaques in murine models. NR4A1 represents a novel and promising target for therapeutic strategies aimed at stabilizing vulnerable plaques and reducing the risk of cardiovascular events associated with atherosclerosis.
