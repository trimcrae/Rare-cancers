## Background:
The utilization of immunotherapeutic approaches in the operable settings has demonstrated promising efficacy and safety in preventing postoperative recurrence and improving patients’ survival across various cancer types. To delineate the developmental trajectory and future directions of this specialized field, a comprehensive bibliometric analysis is necessary.

## Materials and methods:
A total of 541 high-quality publications focusing on the convergence of immunotherapy and surgery were manually extracted from 6973 papers published between 2015 and 2024 in the Web of Science Core Collection. Traditional bibliometric analysis was conducted utilizing VOSviewer, CiteSpace, and the Bibliometrix R package to identify contributions at national and institutional levels, journal impact, and pivotal keywords. The integration of large language model (LLM)-driven text embeddings alongside BERTopic-based semantic modeling enabled a comprehensive synthesis of themes and illuminated prospective directions within this field. Furthermore, advanced dimensionality reduction techniques such as Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection and t-distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding were employed to visualize clustering outcomes and interpret the semantic distances among these academic publications.

## Results:
The distribution of publications over time indicated a growing interest in the integration of immunotherapy and surgery. China and the United States were leaders in publication volume and academic influence, while the “Journal for Immunotherapy of Cancer” stands out as a key resource. Clustering based on LLM-driven embedding vectors identified five categories of papers, the thematic classification of which was presented in a hierarchical structure. Further in-depth analysis revealed the optimal utilization patterns of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) vary across different cancer types. Neoadjuvant ICIs are effective for triple negative breast cancer, non-small cell lung cancer, melanoma, and deficient mismatch repair colon cancer. Adjuvant mRNA vaccines are viable for pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma and melanoma, while oncolytic virus demonstrate enhanced efficacy alongside manageable toxicity in perioperative settings.

## Conclusion:
This bibliometric analysis, which integrates LLM-driven modeling with manual thematic synthesis, has elucidated the developmental trajectory and future directions of the field concerning the combination of immunotherapy and surgery.

## Introduction
Immunotherapy has instigated a paradigm shift in oncology by leveraging the immune system’s inherent capacity to combat malignancies[ – ]. Advanced modalities such as immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs)[ – ], adoptive cell transfer[ – ], and therapeutic vaccines[ – ] have demonstrated promising clinical efficacy through strategic evasion of tumor-induced immune suppression mechanisms. Cumulative clinical evidence substantiates their transformative impact across multiple tumors[ – ]. However, significant heterogeneity remains in therapeutic responses among different tumor types and individual patients[ ], a phenomenon attributable to the complex spatial–temporal dynamics within tumor microenvironments[ , ]. Recent investigations underscore the importance of multi-modal therapeutic integration to optimize treatment efficacy and durability.
While surgical resection continues to be regarded as the gold standard for localized tumors, the risk of postoperative recurrence poses a significant concern in oncological treatment. Additionally, emerging research highlights surgery’s role not only in mechanical tumor debulking but also in tumor regionalization[ , ], thereby indicating its pro-metastatic influence. This challenge has spurred groundbreaking innovations in systemic therapy during the perioperative period[ ]. Neoadjuvant immunotherapy aims to reduce tumor burden to facilitate complete resection and eliminate micrometastases that could otherwise serve as a nidus for postoperative relapse[ , ], while adjuvant immunotherapy capitalizes on surgery-induced antigen exposure to eradicate residual disease[ ]. The groundbreaking perioperative paradigm, which synergistically combines neoadjuvant and adjuvant settings, has achieved unprecedented survival milestones in melanoma[ , ] and non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC)[ – ]. Nevertheless, significant knowledge gaps remain regarding the systematic delineation of optimal sequences of immunotherapy as well as the identification of patient populations that would benefit from combined immunotherapy and surgical strategies.
As an analytical framework for scientific literature, bibliometrics utilizes quantitative techniques that range from citation network analysis to co-authorship mapping, thereby facilitating the visualization of patterns in knowledge production and dissemination[ ]. Building upon Pritchard’s (1969) foundational concept of “mathematical analysis of textual information to reveal disciplinary evolution,” this methodology identifies research frontiers through keyword clustering, maps global collaboration networks, and evaluates institutional impact[ ]. Driven by the rapid advancement of large language models (LLMs) equipped with semantic analysis capabilities[ ], the integration of LLMs into traditional bibliometrics enables the identification of emergent themes directly through extensive publication databases with enhanced accuracy. This methodology provides policymakers and researchers with evidence-based navigation tools that assist in prioritizing resource allocation, accelerating cross-disciplinary innovation, and deciphering complex scientific landscapes.
This bibliometric investigation systematically delineates the intellectual landscape of research concerning the integration of surgery and immunotherapy over the past decade, which has been reported in line with the TITAN criteria[ ]. Through a thorough analysis, we elucidate evolutionary trajectories and emerging trends in the application of immunotherapy during the perioperative period, thereby providing a conceptual framework for future academic inquiries and facilitating clinical translation.

## Materials and methods

## Data source and literature search strategy
A comprehensive literature search was conducted utilizing the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database on 3 March 2025. This particular database was selected due to its extensive coverage of over 12 000 academic journals and robust citation indexing capabilities. It facilitates in-depth bibliometric analysis and precise tracking of scholarly influence across diverse disciplines. The search query was designed to identify publications focused on immunotherapy during the perioperative period, with only the Science Citation Index Expanded being chosen for review. All authors reached a consensus on the search strategy following consultation with two senior literature search experts, employing the following keywords and criteria:
TS = (immunotherapy OR immunotherapies OR immunotherapeutic OR immunotherapeutics) AND TS = (perioperative OR adjuvant OR neoadjuvant OR preoperative OR presurgical OR post-operative OR post-surgical)
The document types were limited to “article.”
The language was limited to English.
The selection of publication years has been deliberately confined to the period from 2015 to 2024, thereby ensuring the relevance and timeliness of the research findings.

Neoadjuvant immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have demonstrated effectiveness across a spectrum of malignancies, especially triple negative breast cancer, non-small-cell lung cancer, melanoma, and colon cancer characterized by deficient mismatch repair.
Adjuvant mRNA vaccines are viable for pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma and melanoma, while oncolytic virus demonstrates enhanced efficacy alongside manageable toxicity in perioperative settings.

This search strategy yielded a total of 6973 publications. Duplicates were removed utilizing the reference management software EndNote 20[ ]. Two reviewers independently screened all records based on their titles and abstracts. Any disagreements were resolved through discussion or consultation with a senior investigator. The blind screening process was carried out according to the predefined exclusion criteria outlined below:
(1) Irrelevant to the theme (n = 2846, 40.9%).
(2) Citation less than 10 in total (n = 3584, 51.4%)
(3) Duplication (n = 2, 0.03%)
The selection process yielded a total of 541 qualified publications, reflecting a retention rate of 7.8%. The articles included in the analysis underwent a qualitative assessment to evaluate their study design, risks of bias, and clarity of reporting. Subsequently, the final dataset was employed for bibliometric and thematic analyses. Any discrepancies in coding or classification were thoroughly discussed among the research team until a consensus was achieved, with oversight provided by the principal investigator. Furthermore, additional research was conducted utilizing complete records and cited references from relevant publications.

## Enhanced literature classification through LLM-driven semantic analysis
LLMs have been employed to capture the intricate nuances of research on the integration of immunotherapy and surgery, leveraging their capabilities in semantic analysis. The abstracts of relevant publications were extracted from the WoSCC, after which rows containing missing values were excluded. Each publication was transformed into a vectorized representation using OpenAI’s novel “text-embedding-ada-003” model[ ]. Publications clustering was subsequently performed utilizing “K-means” analysis[ ], with the optimal number of clusters determined by the Silhouette Score. Furthermore, BERTopic, an advanced unsupervised machine learning algorithm[ ], was applied to identify and label key thematic words from the literature. This step is crucial as it relies on the intrinsic structure of the data rather than predefined categories, thus facilitating the discovery of emergent themes that accurately represent the research landscape[ ]. By integrating computational outputs from BERTopic with manual scholarly insights, we synthesized these themes to ensure that each one is not only semantically grounded but also practically meaningful and accessible to both researchers and practitioners.
To visually interpret the semantic space within papers on the integration of immunotherapy and surgery, we employed dimensionality reduction techniques such as Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) and t-distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (t-SNE) to analyze embeddings generated by LLMs. Subsequently, the resultant two-dimensional scatter plots were color-coded according to the categories discerned through K-means clustering. This visualization not only enhances our understanding of thematic distribution but also assists researchers in navigating the intricate landscape surrounding the integration of immunotherapy and surgery.

## Software for traditional bibliometric analysis
In this study, several software tools such as R[ ] (version 4.2.2), VOSviewer[ , ] (version 1.6.20), and CiteSpace[ , ] (version 6.4.R1) were utilized for conducting a traditional bibliometric analysis. The Bibliometrix R package[ ] (version 4.1.4) was employed to calculate the frequency of collaborations among countries. To analyze the volume of publications, citations, and keyword frequencies, VOSviewer was used as the primary tool. By leveraging the integrated clustering algorithm within this software, we constructed and visualized co-occurrence networks of keywords derived from scientific literature. Furthermore, both VOSviewer and the Bibliometrix R package were instrumental in exploring collaborative efforts among countries, institutions, and authors[ ]. This study also employed CiteSpace to identify keywords that garnered significant citations within a designated timeframe. The online bibliometrics platform facilitated the visualization of international collaborations (https://bibliometric.com/). Additionally, Microsoft Excel was utilized to examine the rapid increase in publication numbers within this field.

## Discussion
This in-depth bibliometric analysis incorporated LLMs to analyze a selection of high-impact articles focusing on the combination of surgery and immunotherapy from 2015 to 2024. Since the first phase 3 clinical trial in 2015 revealed that the adjuvant ICIs significantly prolong the recurrence-free survival (RFS) in melanoma[ ], the annual publication of high-impact articles on immunotherapy-surgery integration has increased steadily, with numbers remaining below 40 per year. A significant upswing was observed in 2020, with the publication of 66 high-impact articles, while this figure further escalated to 129 publications in 2022. This acceleration may be attributed to the emergence of neoadjuvant immunotherapy[ , ], which is verified to satisfy the increasing demand for better survival outcomes and high-quality of life. The primary objective of this study was to delineate the research focal points within the domain of immunotherapy-surgery integration, thereby providing guidance for future scholarly endeavors.

## Innovation in immunotherapeutic approaches
Diverse strategies targeting different pathways have been developed to enhance the immune system’s ability to recognize and eliminate tumors. In operable settings, several immunotherapeutic approaches, including adoptive T-cell therapy, cancer therapeutic vaccines, ICIs, and oncolytic viruses (OVs), have shown promising efficacy and substantial clinical potential. Since 2015, adoptive immunotherapy has been employed as an adjuvant treatment to prevent tumor recurrence, particularly in cancers of the digestive system[ , – ]. This approach involves reengineering patients’ autologous immune cells in vitro and subsequently transferring them back into the patient to effectively target and eliminate tumor cells. The CIK cell therapy is a prominent example of adoptive T-cell immunotherapy, combining the advantages of both natural killer cells and T cells. Postoperative administration of CIK cells has been shown to significantly extend RFS in patients with HCC[ , ]. Furthermore, incorporating CIK cells into conventional adjuvant chemotherapy or radiotherapy markedly reduced recurrence rates and improved disease-free survival (DFS) in gastric cancer[ – ]. However, due to its complexity, time demands, and high costs, the clinical application of adoptive immunotherapy remains limited within perioperative settings where alternative therapeutic modalities with greater feasibility are available[ ].
Cancer therapeutic vaccines have become essential components of oncological immunotherapy, with the key advantage of inducing long-term anti-tumor immune response. In the adjuvant settings, personalized vaccines targeting the unique, highly specific neoantigens derived from tumor samples are a future direction to reduce the recurrence risk. Restricted by antigen non-specificity and intra-tumoral heterogeneity, the efficacy of therapeutic vaccines targeted tumor associated antigens is often suggested to be limited. For instance, two clinical trials concluded that MAGE-A3 vaccines did not diminish the recurrence risk in NSCLC and melanoma patients[ , ], leading to a global cessation of related research. Own to the significant reduction in tumor sequencing costs, mRNA-based vaccines have made personalized neoantigen-targeting strategy feasible. In perioperative settings, the integration of mRNA cancer vaccines with ICIs or chemotherapy have exhibited both feasibility and clinical efficacy in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) and melanoma. Adjuvant autogene cevumeran, an individualized neoantigen vaccine formulated with uridine mRNA-lipoplex nanoparticles, elicited robust T-cell activity and prevented recurrence when combined with atezolizumab and mFOLFIRINOX in PDAC[ ]. Additionally, adjuvant mRNA-4157 in conjunction with pembrolizumab also significantly improved patients’ survival compared to pembrolizumab monotherapy in melanoma[ , ].
OVs are a promising immunotherapeutic strategy that employs genetically engineered or naturally occurring viruses to selectively replicate within tumor cells. This replication process induces lysis of the tumor cells and triggers immunogenic cell death while sparing normal tissues. However, the systemic delivery of OVs is challenged by managing antiviral responses and innate immune responses, which complicates the achievement of clinical efficacy through OVs monotherapy. Combining OVs with other therapeutic modalities, such as ICIs and chemotherapy, has shown enhanced efficacy along with manageable toxicity profiles. Moreover, OVs are particularly suitable for neoadjuvant therapy through inducing “in situ” vaccination effects. This characteristic facilitates the generation of tumor-specific immune responses and may promote T cell infiltration into immunosuppressive tumor microenvironments. To summarize the clinical practice of OVs therapy in perioperative settings, clinical trials in the past 5 years were listed in Table [ – ]. This field is still in its early stages and has primarily been implemented in triple negative breast cancer (TNBC), melanoma, and soft-tissue sarcomas. Neoadjuvant T-VEC has shown improved survival outcomes compared to surgery alone in melanoma (2-year RFS rate: 29.5% vs 16.5%; 2-year overall survival (OS) rate: 88.9% vs 77.4%)[ ] . Notably, a phase Ib clinical trial further demonstrated that combining OVs orienx010 with neoadjuvant toripalimab (a PD-1 inhibitor) exhibited superior antitumor efficacy in melanoma patients [2-year RFS rate: 85.2%; 2-year event-free survival (EFS) rate: 73%][ ]. Meanwhile, neoadjuvant OVs T-VEC plus chemotherapy also achieved great response in TNBC[ , ], indicating substantial potential for OVs in neoadjuvant applications. Large-scale clinical trials are warranted to further confirm both the efficacy and safety of this innovative strategy while exploring additional combination approaches across a broader spectrum of cancer types.

By targeting inhibitory molecules such as CTLA-4 and PD-1/PD-L1, ICIs effectively obstruct the suppressive signals that impede T-cell activation, thereby undermining tumors’ capacity to evade immune responses. Currently, ICIs have exhibited remarkable efficacy in perioperative settings for resectable tumors, resulting in a surge of related clinical trials.

## Sequencing and timing of immunotherapy in operable settings
Adjuvant and neoadjuvant therapies represent two major strategies for integrating immunotherapy with surgical intervention. The concept of adjuvant immunotherapy serves as a preventive measure aimed at reducing the risk of postoperative recurrence in patients with high-risk tumors, a strategy that has been substantiated by numerous clinical trials and subsequently approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for certain types of cancer[ ]. However, emerging scientific evidence suggests that neoadjuvant immunotherapy may be more advantageous when the primary tumor remains intact[ ]. Mechanistically, preoperative administration promotes the reactivation of antigen-experienced PD-1+ T cells already infiltrating the tumor, while also facilitating the priming of antigen-naïve T cells within tumor-draining lymph nodes[ ]. This process enables tumor-specific T cells to enter systemic circulation. Consequently, unlike traditional neoadjuvant chemotherapy, which primarily aims to reduce tumor size to facilitate surgical excision, neoadjuvant immunotherapy may additionally target and eliminate micrometastases that could otherwise serve as a nidus for postoperative relapse[ ]. This hypothesis is bolstered by findings from clinical trials demonstrating an increased frequency of circulating tumor-specific T cells following neoadjuvant ICIs[ , ]. Moreover, two phase III clinical trials involving resectable stage III/IV melanoma have demonstrated that neoadjuvant ICIs exhibit superior efficacy compared to adjuvant setting, resulting in prolonged EFS[ , ].
However, due to variations in tumor burden, metabolic volume, and anatomic location, not all tumors respond effectively to neoadjuvant ICIs. For renal cell carcinoma, ICIs are effective and routinely employed in both adjuvant and advanced unresectable disease settings. However, thus far, ICIs have not demonstrated effectiveness in the neoadjuvant setting[ ]. Furthermore, several clinical trials have explored the efficacy of neoadjuvant immunotherapy for resectable HCC with the aim of enhancing tumor resectability and reducing postoperative recurrence; nevertheless, no evidence has yet confirmed any benefit regarding RFS or OS[ ]. An investigation involving 21 patients with HCC revealed that neoadjuvant cemiplimab (a PD-1 inhibitor) did not achieve a pathological complete response (pCR), suggesting that further research into the potential of neoadjuvant ICIs is warranted[ ]. Additionally, considering the severe adverse events associated with neoadjuvant immunotherapy, which may lead to surgical delays and disease progression, the optimal therapeutic strategy across different cancer types still requires investigation through large-scale clinical trials. To explore the clinical application and compare the therapeutic efficacy and safety of neoadjuvant ICIs across various cancer types, we have summarized relevant clinical trials in Table [ , , , , , – ]. Overall, neoadjuvant ICIs have demonstrated effectiveness in a range of malignancies, with particularly notable success observed in TNBC, NSCLC, melanoma, and colon cancer characterized by dMMR. Furthermore, current clinical investigations into head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, muscle-invasive bladder carcinoma, and ESCC have yielded promising results. 

The emerging “sandwich-style” strategy, which combines neoadjuvant and adjuvant ICIs, demonstrates significant potential in further reducing the risk of recurrence, particularly in melanoma, TNBC, and NSCLC. Since the response to neoadjuvant ICIs is directly correlated with patient prognosis, additional adjuvant systemic therapy has been shown to significantly decrease the risk of both recurrence and mortality in patients who do not achieve pCR. Hence, this perioperative strategy has garnered considerable attention across various cancer types, as detailed in Table [ – , , , , , , – ] and Supplemental Digital Content Table , available at: http://links.lww.com/JS9/F84. Our findings also indicated that promising advancements have been made in bladder cancer[ ] and nasopharyngeal carcinoma[ ]. Importantly, to alleviate financial burdens and reduce the side effects, some scientific investigations have suggested that omitting adjuvant ICIs may be feasible for patients achieving pCR following neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy without adversely affecting their survival outcomes[ ]. 

## Stratification of patients and identification of biomarkers
Furthermore, the emergence of specific keywords in recent years, such as “biomarkers,” “PD-L1,” and “microsatellite instability,” indicates a significant shift toward the investigation of “efficacy prediction and patient selection” as a novel research avenue. The expression of PD-L1 on the surface of tumors[ , ], along with characteristics inherent to the tumor microenvironment[ , ], has been recognized as crucial predictors for the effectiveness of neoadjuvant immunotherapy. Additionally, intrinsic factors related to tumor mutational burden[ , , ], such as dMMR status and microsatellite instability[ , ], have proven essential in identifying suitable candidates for immunotherapy, particularly among patients who demonstrate resistance to conventional chemotherapy. Advances in liquid biopsy technologies, exemplified by the detection of circulating tumor DNA[ , , ], have further enabled a more comprehensive exploration of biomarkers.

## Trends of combined treatment strategy for perioperative immunotherapy
Along with the development of oncological systemic treatment, multimodal therapeutic strategies demonstrate superior efficacy but receivable toxicity in the operable settings. Through mediating different functional pathway, immunotherapy and other approaches may exhibit synthetic cytotoxic roles. Since neoadjuvant chemotherapy triggers tumor cell lysis and releases a surge of tumor-associated antigens[ – ], ICIs will increase the vulnerability of residual tumor cells through activating host’s immune system. For NSCLC and TNBC, neoadjuvant chemotherapy has been confirmed as effective approaches to improve patients’ survival outcomes. Therefore, incorporating ICIs to the perioperative chemotherapy during this antigen-exposed phase may further increase the survival benefit of patients. Several landmark clinical trials have confirmed the potential of this combination strategy[ , , ]. For instance, the KEYNOTE-522 trial confirmed that preoperative chemoimmunotherapy plus adjuvant pembrolizumab significantly increased pCR rate and improved 3-year EFS of patients with stage II–III TNBC[ ]. In the resectable NSCLC, perioperative chemoimmunotherapy also performed superior efficacy and comparable adverse effect, regardless of the types of ICIs[ , , , ]. The CheckMate-816 trial demonstrated neoadjuvant nivolumab plus chemotherapy exhibited superior efficacy compared with neoadjuvant chemotherapy alone in NSCLC (pCR rate: 24% vs 2.2%; median EFS: 54.8 vs 20.9 months)[ , ].
Furthermore, the addition of radiotherapy also provides a promising strategy to enhance the efficacy of perioperative immunotherapy. The phase II INCREASE trials demonstrated the safety and feasibility of neoadjuvant ipilimumab plus nivolumab with chemoradiotherapy in patients with resectable and borderline resectable NSCLC, with major pathological response (MPR) rate of 73% and pCR rate of 58% and no postponement of surgical schedule[ ]. Meanwhile, for tumors with specific mutations, combination between neoadjuvant immunotherapy and targeted therapy is a rational strategy to improve efficacy. Although the addition of atezolizumab to the neoadjuvant anti-human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) therapy and chemotherapy did not elevate pCR rates[ ] in HER2-positive breast cancer, this strategy seemed to be effective in HER2-positive locally advanced resectable gastric cancer and no new safety concerns were raised (pCR rate: 38% vs 14%)[ ]. To conclude, neoadjuvant triple therapy represents an emerging yet promising approach that requires further validation through large-scale studies.

## Clinical applications of immunotherapy in operable cancers
Given the differences in anatomical location and molecular pathology, the clinical application of perioperative immunotherapy has exhibited distinct development trajectories and application patterns among various cancer types. To enhance the clinical relevance, we systematically reviewed more established cancer types within this field based on authoritative guidelines and reviews, integrating key clinical trials to facilitate its translation into practice.

## Melanoma
For patients with resectable advanced melanoma, preoperative immunotherapy remarkedly improved EFS without negative impact on surgical morbidity[ ]. Although the optimal regimen and duration for neoadjuvant immunotherapy is not well established, several prospective clinical trials provided reasonable options as recommended by National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) 2025. In the NADINA study, 423 patients with stage III melanoma were randomly assigned to preoperative ipilimumab plus nivolumab plus response driven adjuvant therapy or surgery plus adjuvant nivolumab[ ]. The neoadjuvant arm was associated with improved 12-month EFS (83.7% vs 57.2%); MPR was 59%, and pCR rate was 48%[ ]. In the SWOG1801 trial, 313 patients with resectable stage III–IV melanoma were randomized to neoadjuvant pembrolizumab plus adjuvant 1-year pembrolizumab or surgery plus adjuvant 1-year pembrolizumab[ ]. The neoadjuvant arm was associated with improved 2-year EFS (72% vs 49%); MPR rate was 53%, and pCR rate was 38%[ ]. In the OpACIN-neo trial, 86 patients with macroscopic stage III melanoma were treated with neoadjuvant ipilimumab plus nivolumab (3-year RFS: 82%; OS: 92%; MPR rate: 64%)[ ]. Notably, another study demonstrated unprecedented efficacy of neoadjuvant nivolumab plus relatlimab (a novel ICI targeting LAG-3) in resectable stage III–IV melanoma (pCR rate: 57%; 2-year RFS in pathologic response group: 92%)[ ]. This combination strategy needs further validation by large randomized studies.
As recommended by NCCN guideline, for patients with MPR after preoperative nivolumab plus ipilimumab, who exhibit low subsequent relapse rates (<10%) even in the absence of additional systemic therapy, adjuvant nivolumab or observation alone may be reasonable options. Conversely, for patients without MPR should consider continued systemic treatment with either anti-PD-1 or target therapy according to its B-Raf proto-oncogene, serine/threonine kinase (BRAF) mutation status.

## Non-small cell lung cancer
NSCLC is one of the most frequent cancer types and accounts for a significant proportion of cancer-related fatalities worldwide. Immunotherapy has demonstrated remarkable efficacy in the perioperative period of resectable NSCLC. Three utilization strategies, namely adjuvant and neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, as well as perioperative immunotherapy in conjunction with neoadjuvant chemotherapy, have achieved significant success and received approval from various regulatory authorities[ ].
First, for completely resected NSCLC with recurrence-related risk factors, adjuvant ICIs plus chemotherapy is proved to prolong patients’ survival time, as recommended by the FDA and EMA. In the Impower-010 trial, 1-year adjuvant atezolizumab following chemotherapy significantly improved DFS compared with best supportive care (HR 0.66, 95% CI 0.50–0.88) in patients with completely resected stage II–IIIA NSCLC and tumor PD-L1 expression >1%[ ]. Notably, the benefit was mainly driven by tumors with high PD-L1 expression[ ], supporting the restriction of the EMA approval to tumors with PD-L1 ≥ 50%. Although the KEYNOTE-091 trial confirmed the DFS benefit of adjuvant pembrolizumab compared with placebo in patients with completely resected stage IB-IIIA NSCLC, subgroup analysis demonstrated that this survival benefit disappeared in patients who did not receive previous adjuvant chemotherapy[ ]. Therefore, adjuvant pembrolizumab was approved by the EMA in patients with completely resected stage IB-IIIA NSCLC, which have received adjuvant chemotherapy, regardless of the PD-L1 expression.
Second, incorporating ICIs to neoadjuvant chemotherapy also demonstrated an increase of pCR rate in early-stage NSCLC. The CheckMate-816 trial confirmed that preoperative nivolumab plus chemotherapy significantly improve the pCR rate compared with neoadjuvant chemotherapy alone (24% vs 2.2%)[ ]. Neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy also prolongs median EFS (54.8 vs 20.9 months)[ ], with higher benefit in patients with tumor PD-L1 expression ≥1%[ ]. A meta-analysis incorporating nine trials also confirmed the superior efficacy of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy than chemotherapy alone in resectable NSCLC[ ].
Meanwhile, several studies focusing on perioperative ICIs plus neoadjuvant chemotherapy also demonstrated superior efficacy compared with neoadjuvant chemotherapy alone. The phase III KEYNOTE-671 trial confirmed the feasibility of perioperative pembrolizumab plus neoadjuvant chemotherapy[ ], leading to both the FDA and EMA approval. The Phase III AEGEAN and NADIM-II trials further supported the utilization of perioperative durvalumab or nivolumab alongside neoadjuvant chemotherapy, respectively[ , ]. However, the necessity of adjuvant ICIs for 1 year following neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy presents a significant question, as it is essential to mitigate unnecessary treatment-related toxicity and financial burden[ ]. Although no trial exists to address this question, a patient-level meta-analysis included high-quality clinical trials indicated that the adjuvant ICIs did not improve EFS compared with neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy alone, irrespective of the pCR status[ ]. However, considering the discrepancy of cross-trial comparison, further investigations will provide valid evidence for the systemic treatment of operable NSCLC.

## Breast cancer
TNBC is a subtype of breast cancer with the highest rates of recurrence and mortality[ , ]. The limited expression of targetable proteins, such as the estrogen receptor, along with the absence of HER2 amplification, has necessitated reliance on cytotoxic chemotherapy for several decades[ ]. In the operable setting, efforts to improve outcomes have focused on escalation of systemic therapy and a shift toward preoperative delivery followed by a response adapted approach to postoperative systemic therapy. Notably, the phase III KEYNOTE-522 trial confirmed that the addition of pembrolizumab to a preoperative chemotherapy backbone plus adjuvant pembrolizumab significantly increased pCR rate and improved 3-year EFS[ ]. Based on this result, the FDA approved for the neoadjuvant ICIs plus chemotherapy for TNBC in 2021[ ], while the KEYNOTE-522 regimen was recommended for patients with stage II–III TNBC by both NCCN 2025 and a rapid recommendation update issued by ASCO in 2022[ ]. However, the residual cancer burden (RCB) after neoadjuvant therapy directly influences patients’ prognosis, while additional adjuvant systemic therapy significantly decreases the risk of recurrence and death[ ]. Hence, for patients with RCB3, who still exhibited poor survival outcomes in this trial[ ], incorporating additional adjuvant agents into KEYNOTE-522 regimen is promising. Conversely, for those achieving pCR after neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, the ignorance of adjuvant pembrolizumab reduces the financial burden and decreases the possibility of treatment-related adverse events[ ]. The ongoing OptimICE-pCR clinical trial will provide further evidence on the value of adjuvant pembrolizumab in the pCR patients with preoperative chemoimmunotherapy.
However, for HER2-positive early breast cancer, anti-HER2 therapy plus chemotherapy remains the standard of care in the neoadjuvant setting. Although the phase II Neo-PATH trial demonstrated an acceptable pCR rate (61%) of neoadjuvant pertuzumab, atezolizumab, docetaxel, and trastuzumab regimen in patients with HER2-positive stage II–III breast cancer[ ], further comparable study confirmed that the addition of atezolizumab to the neoadjuvant anti-HER2 therapy and chemotherapy did not elevate pCR rates[ ].

## Hepatocellular carcinoma
The high recurrence risk is one of the main questions of early resectable HCC. However, the efficacy of perioperative immunotherapy in HCC was too preliminary to direct the clinical recommendation. The efficacy of adjuvant immunotherapy in HCC preventing postoperative recurrence was limited. Although the Korean phase III trial demonstrated the promising efficacy of adjuvant CIK cells, it owned some key limitations, including insufficient power, unbalanced prognostic characteristics, and lack of external confirmation[ ]. The interim analysis of IMbrave 050 study indicated the potential efficacy of adjuvant atezolizumab plus bevacizumab on prolonging RFS of high-risk HCC patients[ ]. However, along with the maturation of final result, survival benefit reported previous did not exist (ESMO, 2024). However, in an open-label phase II trial, the adjuvant sintilimab (a PD-1 inhibitor) exhibited promising efficacy for resected HCC patients with microscopic vascular invasion compared with active surveillance (median RFS 27.7 vs 15.5 months; HR 0.534, 95% CI 0.36–0.79)[ ]. Due to small sample size and limited population resources, the generalizability of its result needs further validation in larger, multicenter studies.

## Colon cancer
For resectable colon patients with dMMR and high microsatellite instability, neoadjuvant ICIs demonstrated high pCR rate as evidenced by the NICHE and NICHE-2 trials[ , ]. The NICHE-2 study enrolled 115 patients with nonmetastatic dMMR colon cancer treated with neoadjuvant ipilimumab plus nivolumab, with MPR rate of 98% and pCR rate of 68%[ ]. Moreover, at a median follow-up of 26 months, no cases of disease recurrence were reported. Furthermore, for colon cancer with dMMR, the neoadjuvant anti-PD-1 in combination with anti-LAG-3 also yielded a high MPR rate of 92% and pCR rate of 68%[ ], while further investigation of this treatment in larger studies was warranted.

## Limitations
Some limitations merit careful consideration. We focused on English-language articles from the WoSCC. The reliance on citation counts as a screening criterion might result in the ignorance of relevant literature. Additionally, the temporal citation biases inherent in bibliometric analyses pose a challenge, particularly in the delayed acknowledgment of recent publications. While this study utilized only the abstracts for text embedding instead of the full texts, potentially leading to some information loss, the abstracts in academic literature are highly condensed structured texts that can yield effective results within a limited token count. Additionally, given that embedding models are sensitive to high-frequency local features, technical detail noise present in full texts may obscure the global semantic structure, consequently resulting in clustering outcomes biased toward “method similarity.” Focusing on experimental investigations, the discrepancies between randomized trials and clinical applications may impact the accuracy of our conclusions, which has been effectively addressed by incorporating established clinical guidelines and expert recommendations.

## Conclusion
In conclusion, this bibliometric analysis integrates LLM-driven modeling with manual thematic synthesis to delineate the developmental trajectory and future directions of perioperative immunotherapy as a specialized field. Regarding the modalities of immunotherapy, postoperative mRNA vaccines facilitated personalized neoantigen-targeting strategies with promising efficacy and safety in PDAC and melanoma. The combination of OVs with other therapeutic approaches in the operable settings has also illustrated enhanced efficacy alongside manageable toxicity profiles. Although ICIs represent one of the most crucial strategies in immunotherapy, the optimal utilization patterns of ICIs in conjunction with surgical interventions and the development of additional combination strategies vary across different cancer types. Neoadjuvant ICIs have demonstrated effectiveness across a spectrum of malignancies, with particularly notable success observed in TNBC, NSCLC, melanoma, and colon cancer characterized by dMMR. The emerging “sandwich-style” strategy, combining neoadjuvant and adjuvant ICIs, exhibits significant potential for further reducing recurrence risk, especially in melanoma, TNBC, and NSCLC. The response to neoadjuvant ICIs may be critical in determining whether subsequent adjuvant ICIs or alternative systemic therapies are warranted. Furthermore, predicting therapeutic responses to perioperative ICIs, coupled with the subsequent stratification of patients for improved precision medicine, represents a crucial avenue for future research.
