## Abstract
Head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) remains a challenging malignancy, with limited survival improvements despite recent advances with immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs). Within this context, personalized neoantigen vaccines and HPV-targeted therapeutic vaccines are emerging as innovative strategies aimed at enhancing antitumor immunity. Personalized vaccines exploit tumor-specific mutational landscape to generate highly individualized T cell responses, demonstrating favorable safety profiles and durable immunogenicity in early-phase trials. Compounds such as TG4050, mRNA-4157, and PGV001 have shown early, hypothesis-generating signals of activity (from small early-phase studies), particularly in the adjuvant setting. HPV-targeted vaccines, including PDS0101, BNT113, and CUE-101, are being explored in HPV-positive HNSCC, mainly in combination with ICIs. While some early clinical activity has been observed, these signals should be interpreted cautiously given limited sample sizes, heterogeneous populations, and exploratory endpoints in many studies, and randomized trials such as ISA101b have failed to show survival benefits over ICIs alone. Overall, therapeutic vaccination appears safe and immunologically active, yet its efficacy is constrained by the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment. The greatest clinical potential of therapeutic vaccination may lie in earlier disease stages, where the tumor burden is lower and immune modulation more feasible. Further clinical and translational research is needed to define the optimal integration of vaccine-based therapies into multimodal treatment paradigms for HNSCC.
Keywords: Personalized neoantigen vaccines, HPV therapeutic vaccines, Head and neck cancer, Immunotherapy, tumor microenvironment

## Introduction
Head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) comprise a heterogeneous group of tumors characterized by diverse genetic and molecular alterations, as well as a variety of etiologic factors including tobacco, alcohol exposure and viral infections. Among these, high-risk human papillomavirus (HPV), particularly HPV type 16, has emerged as a major driver of oropharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma, accounting for an increasing proportion of cases worldwide [ ]. Despite advances in multimodal therapy and the adoption of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), the global survival rate for HNSCC has remained essentially unchanged over the past 30 years, stabilizing at approximately 50 % at 5 years [ ].
Recently, the KEYNOTE-689 and NIVOPOST-OP trials have represented a significant step forward by investigating the potential application of ICIs in resectable locally advanced disease, demonstrating improved long-term outcomes in selected patients [ , ]. Nevertheless, in the recurrent/metastatic (R/M) setting where ICIs are currently being utilized, only a minority of patients derive clinical benefit [ , ], underscoring the need for refined patient selection and innovative therapeutic strategies capable of overcoming intrinsic and acquired resistance. The tumor microenvironment represents a major challenge to immunotherapy efficacy, as it is inherently immunosuppressive [ , ]. Tumor-related factors, such as infiltration by regulatory T cells (Tregs), myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs), and cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), as well as hypoxia, chronic inflammation, viral infection, tobacco exposure, and treatment-induced immune dysfunction, all contribute to the maintenance of a “cold” tumor milieu that impairs cytotoxic T cell activity [ ].
Within this challenging context, therapeutic cancer vaccines have emerged as a promising strategy to elicit potent, antigen-specific T cell responses that could complement checkpoint inhibition and improve tumor control [ ]. Among the different vaccine modalities under development, two principal approaches have gained increasing attention: personalized neoantigen vaccines, which exploit the unique mutational landscape of each patient’s tumor to generate highly specific immune responses [ ] and therapeutic HPV vaccines, which target viral oncoproteins expressed in HPV-positive malignancies [ ]. A comparative summary of the main features distinguishing therapeutic HPV vaccines from personalized neoantigen vaccines is provided in .
 Here, we outline the key concepts and rationale of personalized cancer vaccines and therapeutic HPV vaccines, highlighting their immunologic principles and potential advantages. Furthermore, we present a review of recent clinical trials evaluating these vaccine strategies in HNSCC.

## Personalized cancer vaccines: concept and rationale
Personalized cancer vaccines represent a promising approach in immuno-oncology, designed to harness the unique mutational landscape of a patient’s tumor to elicit potent and specific antitumor immune responses. Unlike conventional vaccines targeting shared tumor-associated antigens, personalized vaccines are based on the identification of patient-specific neoantigens, novel peptides arising from somatic mutations including single nucleotide variants, insertions and deletions, structural rearrangements and aberrant splicing events. Neoantigen-specific T cells are not subject to central tolerance mechanisms, highlighting their potential to generate a diversified T cell repertoire capable of recognizing and eliminating malignant cells with high specificity [ , ].
The workflow for personalized vaccine development typically involves next-generation sequencing (NGS) of tumor and matched normal DNA, combined with RNA sequencing to identify expressed mutations. Bioinformatic pipelines then prioritize candidate neoantigens based on predicted MHC class I and II binding affinity, clonality, and expression levels [ ]. Selected peptides can be synthesized as long peptides, encoded in RNA or DNA platforms, or pulsed onto autologous dendritic cells to facilitate in vivo presentation and T cell priming [ , ] ( ). shows the pipeline of personalized cancer vaccine.
 Despite advances, several technical and translational challenges remain. These include the time-intensive manufacturing process, variable immunogenicity of neoepitopes, and the need to overcome the typical immunosuppressive mechanisms in the tumor microenvironment, mainly in HNSCC [ ]. However, recent evidence from early-phase trials indicates that personalized neoantigen vaccines can elicit durable CD4+ and CD8+ T cell responses, supporting further clinical development and combinatorial strategies with ICIs and other immunomodulators [ ]. Importantly, most clinical trials in HNSCC derive from early-phase and are primarily designed to establish feasibility, safety, and immunogenicity; therefore, reported clinical activity signals should generally be considered hypothesis-generating until confirmed in adequately powered randomized studies.

## Clinical development of personalized cancer vaccines in HNSCC
 provides a detailed overview of trials evaluating personalized vaccines in HNSCC that are described below.
 

## Adjuvant setting

-
TG4050

TG4050 is a highly innovative personalized cancer vaccine developed by Transgene using the myvac® platform. It employs a Modified Vaccinia Virus Ankara (MVA) vector to deliver up to 30 neoantigen epitopes that are specifically selected for each patient. Administered subcutaneously over an initial schedule of six weekly injections followed by maintenance doses, TG4050 was evaluated in a randomized phase I clinical trial in patients with HPV-negative, resected, locally advanced HNSCC. Notably, TG4050 improved 2-years disease-free survival (DFS) with no recurrences in the vaccinated arm (100 % DFS compared to 81 % in the control arm after a median follow-up of 30 months), without experiencing any grade (G) 3 or higher treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs). Given the phase I design, limited sample size, and the exploratory nature of efficacy endpoints, these DFS findings should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than definitive evidence of benefit. Immune monitoring revealed that the vaccine induced persistent polyclonal CD8 T cell responses targeting multiple epitopes, which remained detectable over 1 year after the end of the treatment. This strong safety and efficacy profile support the ongoing randomized phase II trial [ ].
-YE-NEO-001
YE-NEO-001 is a personalized peptide-based cancer vaccine designed to stimulate T cell immunity by presenting a small number of patient-specific neoantigens [ – ]. Although detailed clinical results have not yet been published, YE-NEO-001 is being investigated as an adjuvant treatment, after curative treatment, across multiple solid tumors including HNSCC, in a phase I trial.
-PGV001
PGV001 is a multi-peptide personalized vaccine that incorporates 10 synthetic neoantigen peptides, using a formulation with polyvinylpyrrolidone as a carrier and poly-ICLC, an immune-activating adjuvant. PGV001 was evaluated in a phase I trial in combination with atezolizumab in patients with various solid tumors, including HNSCC, in the adjuvant setting, and demonstrated a median relapse-free survival (RFS)/overall survival (OS) of 49/51 months, respectively. No G3 or higher TRAEs were revealed. Because this was an early-phase study with mixed tumor types and limited HNSCC-specific numbers, these survival estimates should be considered descriptive and hypothesis-generating, and cannot establish comparative efficacy. These preliminary findings suggest that PGV001 can effectively support long-term disease control when administered in the adjuvant setting, with atezolizumab [ , ].

## Adjuvant and R/M setting

-
mRNA-4157

The vaccine mRNA-4157, developed by Moderna, represents a next-generation approach to personalized cancer vaccination using lipid nanoparticle-encapsulated messenger RNA. Each vaccine dose encodes up to 34 neoantigens. In a currently recruiting phase I trial for patients with various solid malignancies, including HPV negative HNSCC, mRNA-4157 showed preliminary efficacy both as monotherapy (adjuvant setting) and in combination with pembrolizumab (R/M disease). In the adjuvant setting, the DFS rate reached 92 % at eight months of follow-up. Given the short follow-up and early-phase context, the adjuvant DFS signal should be interpreted cautiously. In the R/M disease, data only on HNSCC cohort are available: among 22 patients with ICI-naïve HNSCC, the ORR was 27.3 %, and the DCR reached 63.6 %. The median PFS was 4 months, while the median OS was 26 months, with a favorable safety profile (14 % experiencing G3 TRAEs). These results could justify further evaluation of this vaccine combined with pembrolizumab in a randomized trial [ , ].

## R/M setting

-
MVX-ONCO-1

MVX-ONCO-1 takes a unique approach by using irradiated autologous tumor cells combined with granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF), encapsulated in biocompatible microcapsules designed for slow release. This strategy aims to create a local pro-inflammatory environment that recruits dendritic cells to process and present tumor antigens, thereby initiating a robust immune response. It was evaluated in a phase I trial in metastatic cancer patients (2 patients with HNSCC), showing an ORR of 6.3 %, a DCR exceeding 50 % and a mean OS of approximately 3.2 years in the HNSCC subgroup. Given the extremely small HNSCC sample size and tumor-type heterogeneity, these efficacy signals should be interpreted with substantial caution and considered exploratory. Although one patient experienced a severe TRAE, the therapy was generally tolerable [ ].
-Allostim / AlloVax™
Allostim, or AlloVax™, is a cancer vaccine based on irradiated allogeneic tumor cells, administered intradermally, together with immunostimulatory adjuvants. A phase II trial in 12 patients with R/M HNSCC has been completed, although no reported data have yet been released regarding the vaccine’s clinical benefit or safety outcomes.
-GEN-009
GEN-009 is a personalized multi-peptide vaccine that includes between 5 and 18 synthetic neoantigens, formulated with the potent adjuvant Montanide ISA 51 VG to enhance immune activation. The vaccine has been evaluated in a phase I/II trial in both curative and metastatic settings; in 16 patients with R/M solid tumors, in combination with a PD-1 inhibitor (15 patients) or as single agent (1 patient), Clinically, among nine ICI-responsive patients, eight (89 %) remained progression-free for 3 to 10 months following the first vaccine dose, with three demonstrating further tumor reduction suggestive of a potential vaccine effect. In the ICI-refractory cohort, four of seven patients (57 %) experienced prolonged stable disease of up to 8 months. No G3 or higher TRAEs were revealed. Notably, the majority of patients were not experiencing progressive disease at the time of vaccination initiation, as many had achieved disease stabilization or partial response during prior PD-1 inhibitor therapy. This aspect should be carefully considered, as the predominance of patients with controlled disease at inclusion may confound clinical outcomes solely for the vaccine. Nonetheless, these results highlight the potential of GEN-009 to elicit robust, multi-epitope T cell responses and support further investigation [ ].
-PANDA-VAC
PANDA-VAC is a neoantigen-based personalized vaccine currently in development for use in combination with pembrolizumab. As the phase I trial has not yet started recruitment, there are no clinical data available, but the approach reflects growing interest in combining personalized immunotherapies with ICIs.
-PNeoVCA
PNeoVCA is another experimental personalized vaccine under investigation for advanced solid tumors, evaluated in a phase I trial in combination with pembrolizumab. The phase I study is ongoing, and no preliminary safety or efficacy results have been released so far.

## HPV therapeutic vaccines in hnscc
Therapeutic vaccines targeting HPV oncoproteins have shown promise in HPV-associated HNSCCs, particularly oropharyngeal carcinoma. These vaccines are designed to stimulate robust T cell responses directed against the E6 and E7 oncoproteins, which are constitutively expressed and essential for malignant transformation. Multiple vaccine platforms are under clinical investigation, including long-peptide vaccines, nucleic acid-based (DNA and mRNA) vaccines, viral vector-based approaches, and dendritic cell vaccines ( ). Early-phase trials combining therapeutic HPV vaccines with ICIs have demonstrated encouraging immunogenicity and early signals of efficacy, suggesting that integration of these approaches could potentiate antitumor immunity. However, clinical activity signals across studies should be interpreted in light of early-phase designs, small samples, and heterogeneity in treatment lines and subsets.
 summarizes clinical trials evaluating HPV vaccines combined with ICIs in HPV-positive HNSCC, in the R/M setting.
‐ISA101b
 ISA101b is a synthetic long peptide vaccine specifically targeting the HPV16 E6 and E7 oncoproteins. This vaccine, administered subcutaneously, has been evaluated in combination with cemiplimab, in a randomized phase II trial enrolling 198 patients with HPV16-positive oropharyngeal carcinoma, anti-PD-1–naïve in both first or second-line of treatment. This combination showed an ORR of 25% Vs 23 % in the placebo arm (in the full analysis set); The median OS was shorter with ISA101b (15.8 months) compared to the placebo arm (27 months). In the CPS ≥20 subgroup, median OS reached 35 months versus 29 months with placebo. The difference in survival rates should be interpreted cautiously, particularly when subgroup analyses are underpowered for OS, and may reflect baseline imbalances, differences in post-progression therapies, limited follow-up/event numbers; PD-L1-defined subgroup findings are exploratory and not powered for definitive OS conclusions. The combination therapy was generally well tolerated, with G3–4 TRAEs occurring in 4 % of patients. Despite its ability to induce HPV16-specific immune responses, the combination of ISA101b and cemiplimab did not demonstrate a clinically meaningful benefit over cemiplimab alone in this randomized phase II trial. Although some improvement was observed in patients with higher CPS, the overall results did not support further development [ ].
‐PDS0101
PDS0101 (Versamune® HPV) is an investigational immunotherapy that leverages a lipid-based nanoparticle delivery system to present HPV16 E6 and E7 antigens. In the VERSATILE-002 phase II trial, PDS0101 plus pembrolizumab demonstrated in 53 enrolled patients an ORR of 34 % in patients with R/M ICIs naïve and ICIs pre-treated HPV16-positive HNSCC. Median progression-free survival (PFS) was 6.3 months, and median OS was 30 months. The safety profile was acceptable. The combination represents a promising strategy to broaden and deepen antitumor immune responses in HPV-driven HNSCC and could support the advancement of PDS0101 into phase III development [ ].
‐MEDI0457
MEDI0457 is a DNA vaccine that encodes HPV16 and HPV18 E6/E7 antigens, delivered with the IL-12 plasmid as an adjuvant to enhance T cell activation. The vaccine is administered intramuscularly with electroporation, a method shown to improve DNA uptake and immunogenicity. In a phase I/IIa trial combining MEDI0457 with durvalumab, 35 patients with R/M HNSCC were treated. The ORR was 28 %, and median PFS/OS were 3.5 and 29 months, respectively. A 14 % of patients developed G3 or higher TRAEs. The strategy aims to synergize viral antigen–specific priming with PD-L1 inhibition to reinvigorate exhausted T cells [ ].
‐CUE-101
CUE-101 is a fusion protein consisting of an HPV16 E7-derived peptide presented by an HLA complex linked to attenuated IL-2, designed to selectively activate and expand E7-specific CD8+ T cells. This sophisticated construct delivers both antigen and cytokine signals to enhance T cell proliferation and cytotoxicity while minimizing systemic IL-2 exposure. In a phase I study evaluating both monotherapy and combination with pembrolizumab, CUE-101 demonstrated an ORR of 5% as monotherapy (treatment refractory) and 47% when combined with pembrolizumab (as first-line treatment). In the combination therapy, median PFS was 5.8 months, whereas median OS was not reached. The treatment was well tolerated, with 8% of patients experiencing G3–4 TRAES. These results highlight the promise of engineered immuno-cytokine fusions in HPV-associated malignancies [ ].
‐BNT113
BNT113 is an uridine-modified mRNA-lipoplex vaccine encoding HPV16 E6 and E7 proteins, developed by BioNTech. Upon intravenous administration, the RNA-LPX platform selectively delivers the mRNA to dendritic cells, leading to strong type I interferon–driven maturation and potent antigen-specific T cell responses. The AHEAD-MERIT phase II trial evaluated BNT113 in combination with pembrolizumab as first-line treatment in PD-L1-positive, HPV16-positive R/M HNSCC. Among 15 patients, the confirmed ORR was 40 % by independent review. Median PFS and OS were 3.9 and 22.6 months, respectively. Immunological analyses revealed induction of de novo HPV-specific CD8+ T cell responses with an effector memory phenotype. G3–4 TRAES were revealed in 13 % of patients. These results could support continued clinical development [ ].

## Key challenges and future directions
Personalized and HPV-targeted therapeutic vaccines represent one of the most innovative immunotherapeutic approaches currently under clinical development for HNSCC. Early-phase studies have consistently demonstrated that these vaccines are safe, well tolerated and capable of inducing measurable antigen-specific T cell responses, particularly when administered in combination with ICIs. Nevertheless, the degree of clinical benefit observed to date has generally been more modest in the R/M setting compared to other emerging immunotherapeutic strategies, such as bispecific antibodies, which have shown rapid and deep antitumor activity even in heavily pretreated patients [ , ]. This observation suggests that, while therapeutic vaccination possesses a clear immunologic rationale and strong potential, its efficacy may be constrained by the profoundly immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment of HNSCC [ ].
Accordingly, the most promising future applications of both personalized neoantigen vaccines and HPV-targeted vaccines could be more likely in earlier disease settings, specifically as adjuvant or consolidation therapy following definitive treatment, when the tumor burden is minimal and the microenvironment could be less hostile to effective immune infiltration and activation. In HNSCC, this concept can be translated into three clinically implementable strategies: (a) perioperative vaccination (neoadjuvant priming with adjuvant boosting) to expand tumor-reactive T cells before and after surgery; (b) purely adjuvant vaccination in high-risk resected disease (e.g., HPV-negative LA-HNSCC or HPV16+ oropharyngeal cancer with adverse features) using relapse-prevention endpoints; and (c) post-(chemo)radiotherapy consolidation (administering vaccination after completion of definitive treatment in patients without evident radiological disease) to eradicate minimal residual disease. In these settings, trial designs may incorporate biomarker-driven enrichment and longitudinal monitoring, including circulating tumor DNA/ HPV DNA to potentially detect minimal residual disease assessment, PD-L1 CPS and immune-inflamed signatures for stratification, and standardized on-treatment immunomonitoring in blood or tissue to link vaccine-induced T cells with intratumoral trafficking and function.
Here, the therapeutic vaccines, mostly personalized vaccines, may play a critical role in eliminating residual microscopic disease and preventing relapse. Importantly, the rationale for implementing therapeutic vaccines in earlier disease stages is supported by evidence from other malignancies. The randomized phase IIb trial of mRNA-4157 (V940) in resected high-risk melanoma demonstrated that combining a personalized neoantigen vaccine with pembrolizumab significantly prolonged RFS compared with pembrolizumab alone, with 18-month RFS rates of 79 % Vs 62 %. These results provided the first robust randomized evidence that personalized vaccination can meaningfully enhance outcomes in the adjuvant setting [ ].
Similarly, in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, a disease with notoriously low immunogenicity, two recent phase I studies have evaluated autogene cevumeran, an individualized mRNA–lipoplex vaccine encoding up to 20 patient-specific neoantigens identified from resected tumor tissue. Administered sequentially after surgical resection and a priming dose of the anti–PD-L1 antibody atezolizumab, followed by mFOLFIRINOX chemotherapy, the vaccine induced robust de novo CD8+ T cell responses in approximately 50 % of treated patients, with early signals suggesting improved outcomes among immune responders [ , ].
In renal cell carcinoma, a recent phase I trial of a personalized neoantigen peptide vaccine administered in the adjuvant setting after complete resection demonstrated that all patients showed robust T cell responses against multiple neoantigen targets, including key driver mutations such as VHL, PBRM1, and BAP1. Notably, after a median follow-up exceeding three years, none of the vaccinated patients experienced disease recurrence, underscoring the feasibility of generating effective antitumor immunity even in tumors with low mutational burden [ ].
These insights underscore both the promise and the complexity of developing therapeutic cancer vaccines for HNSCC. While emerging evidence across multiple tumor types supports their potential to generate durable antitumor immunity and improve long-term outcomes, several challenges remain, including optimizing delivery platforms, overcoming tumor-induced immunosuppression, and identifying reliable biomarkers, such as vaccine-targeted antigens and robust T cell responses, to select patients most likely to benefit.
Trial design also represents a central challenge and results must be interpreted with caution. The majority of available data for both personalized and HPV vaccines in HNSCC derive from phase I or early phase II studies, often with small patient cohorts, heterogeneous populations, and limited follow-up. These designs are primarily intended to demonstrate feasibility, safety, and immunogenicity rather than to provide definitive evidence of clinical benefit. As such, reported response rates or survival signals should be considered exploratory.
Incorporating more rigorous design elements, such as randomized comparisons, biomarker-driven stratification, and standardized immunomonitoring, will be critical to strengthen the translational value of these studies and pave the way for conclusive evidence of benefit.
Notably, the randomized trial evaluating TG4050.02 has reported promising immunogenicity and preliminary clinical activity. Confirmation of these findings will depend on results from the randomized phase II study, which has recently completed accrual. Continued progress will depend on rigorous clinical investigation and translational research to refine these strategies and integrate them effectively into multimodal treatment paradigms.
