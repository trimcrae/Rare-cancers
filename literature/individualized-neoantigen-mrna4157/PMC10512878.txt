## ABSTRACT
The investigation of therapeutic cancer vaccines has been ongoing for the past century. Herein, we used VOSviewer and CiteSpace to perform the first global bibliometric analysis of the literature on therapeutic cancer vaccines from 2013 to 2022 aiming to explore the current status and potential research trends. The findings revealed a consistent upward trend in both publication counts and citations. The United States emerged as the leading contributor with the highest number of published papers. Additionally, the analysis of references and keywords indicated that therapeutic cancer vaccines have long been popular topics, whereas neoantigen vaccines, mRNA vaccines, combination strategies, and vaccine delivery systems are emerging research hotspots. This bibliometric study provides a comprehensive and important overview of the current knowledge and potential developments in therapeutic cancer vaccines from 2013 to 2022, which may serve as a valuable reference for scholars interested in further exploring this promising field.

## Introduction
Cancer poses a significant global health threat, with high rates of both incidence and mortality. , In recent years, cancer immunotherapy has emerged as a game-changer in cancer treatment, following the approval of immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) modulators by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA). Unlike traditional methods like surgery, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy, which directly target tumor cells, immunotherapy focuses on activating the body’s natural anti-tumor immune response. It also works to modify the tumor microenvironment, suppress tumor growth, and eliminate cancer cells. , Ultimately, this approach aims to reduce tumor size and improve overall patient survival.
Despite the encouraging outcomes observed in patients treated with immune checkpoint inhibitors, oncologists and clinicians still face significant challenges due to the limited patient response rate and serious adverse events. As a result, therapeutic cancer vaccines have regained substantial attention in the past decade as a promising form of immunotherapy. These vaccines have a long history dating back to the early 1890s. These vaccines can be classified into various types based on different tumor antigen platforms, which include cell-based vaccines, nucleic acid-based vaccines, peptide-based vaccines, and others. A specific type of therapeutic cancer vaccine, known as in situ anti-tumor vaccines, is designed to generate endogenous antigens directly from tumors by inducing tumor cell apoptosis. Historically, shared antigens like cancer testis antigens and overexpressed antigens have been the main targets for cancer vaccines. However, the effectiveness of these vaccines has been disappointing, likely due to the unique nature of antigens in each individual’s cancer. As a result, there has been a recent focus on developing personalized vaccines that target patient-specific neoantigens. Advancements in next-generation sequencing technology and whole-genome mapping have facilitated the development of neoantigen vaccines, which selectively deliver highly immunogenic epitopes, thereby enhancing immune priming. An ideal therapeutic cancer vaccine should elicit specific immune responses against tumor antigens, establish long-lasting anti-tumor memory, and minimize adverse events. However, despite extensive efforts in the field, the successful translation of therapeutic cancer vaccines into effective clinical applications has remained challenging for several decades. To overcome this challenge, it is important to address tumor immunosuppression and enhance the immunogenicity of tumor antigens. These approaches are crucial for improving the clinical efficacy of therapeutic cancer vaccines. In order to enhance the successful clinical translation rate, several scientists have been exploring different strategies to overcome the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME) and combat tumor immune escape.
Over the past decade, technological advancements have positioned therapeutic cancer vaccines as promising candidates for immunotherapy, reigniting substantial interest from researchers in this area. However, the performance of many therapeutic cancer vaccines remains suboptimal, resulting in inconsistent findings and conclusions. , There is a lack of quantitative analyses in the existing literature of this research field. Although meta-analyses have been conducted to evaluate the effectiveness of specific types of therapeutic cancer vaccines, such as peptide vaccines, and their efficacy against certain cancer types, the scope of these discussions is relatively narrow. Reviews on therapeutic cancer vaccines have been published, but they often lack necessary completeness, objectivity, and macroscopicity as they rely on personal experience and a comprehensive reading of the literature. To address this limitation, bibliometrics, a comprehensive and effective analysis method for scientific information, can provide researchers with qualitative and quantitative insights into research publications. By examining various indicators such as countries, journals, institutions, keywords, and references, bibliometric analysis offers valuable insights into current research trends and frontiers in a specific field. The concept of bibliometrics has been applied in various scientific research fields, such as autophagy and immunotherapy. , However, no bibliometric studies specifically focusing on therapeutic cancer vaccines have been conducted to date. Therefore, it is crucial to investigate the current state of research in this area.
This study conducted a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of scientific outputs on therapeutic cancer vaccines from 2013 to 2022 to provide valuable insights into the current status and emerging trends in this research field. The bibliometric analysis focused on four main aspects: I. Quantifying the global development trends in therapeutic cancer vaccines over the past decade. II. Identifying the most productive and influential countries/regions, institutions, and authors in this field. III. Determining the most productive and preferred journals for publishing documents in this field. IV. Uncovering the main research directions and hotspots through co-cited reference burst and keyword analysis.

## Methods
Bibliometric data were obtained from the Web of Science Core Collection (WOSCC) database, which is widely used for bibliometric analyses and offers comprehensive bibliometric information. , A search was conducted in the WOSCC database on March 30, 2023, covering the period from 2013 to 2022. The search strategy outlined in was used. The initial search resulted in a total of 4286 publications. After excluding meeting abstracts (282), proceedings (35), early access (21), editorial materials (94), book chapters (18), corrections (6), news items (6) and letters (20), a total of 3804 articles and reviews remained. To narrow down the search, we focused on English-language publications, resulting in a total of 3,762 publications. We carefully reviewed the titles and abstracts of these publications and excluded 1,021 that were not relevant to our research topic. As a result, we included a total of 2,741 publications published between 2013 and 2022 for our bibliometric analysis. The filtering process is visually represented in .

 The bibliometric information, including author names, journal titles, affiliations, countries/regions, publication year, citation counts, reference records and keywords, was exported as a ‘full record and cited references’ and analyzed using VOSviewer (version 1.6.18) and CiteSpace (V.6.2.R2). These software tools are widely used for knowledge mapping and visual analysis to identify current research hotspots and trends. , Additionally, an online bibliometric analysis tool (https://bibliometric.com) was used to analyze the co-authorship between countries/regions and generate a visualization cooperation map.

## Results

## Annual tendencies and annual citations
A total of 2,741 eligible publications were identified based on the retrieval strategy outlined earlier. Among these, 2,112 were English articles and 629 were English reviews. The data analysis revealed a gradual increase in annual publication counts and citation counts for therapeutic cancer vaccine research over the past decade. The highest number of publications, 377, was observed in 2022, with a corresponding peak of 16,131 citations ( ). From 2013 to 2022, the cumulative citation count for the included studies was 66,546, averaging 26.64 citations per document. These findings demonstrate a significant research interest in therapeutic cancer vaccines over the past 10 years.

 

## Global contribution of countries/regions, institutions to the field
The final collected papers were produced in 89 countries/regions. Table S1 lists the top 10 most productive countries/regions in terms of publication output from 2013 to 2022. The United States (US) contributed the highest number of published papers (996, 36.3%), followed by China (648, 23.6%) and Japan (136, 9.6%). When it comes to citation frequency, the US also ranked first with 36,711 citations, followed by China (11,520 times) and Germany (7,459 times). These numbers highlight significantly higher citation counts compared to other countries. Additionally, the US had the highest H-index (85), which is a comprehensive indicator evaluating the research performance in terms of quantity and quality for authors, countries/regions, journals, or institutions. These findings emphasize the prominent role of the United States in the field of therapeutic cancer vaccines during the last ten years. In terms of geographical distribution, a majority of the publications originated from North America and East Asia ( ). The international cooperation visualization map ( ) reveals that the US collaborated with multiple countries, with China having the closest international collaboration with the US. Furthermore, the analysis revealed that collaborations among other countries occurred less frequently. This finding implies the possibility of future collaborations between various nations and regions.
In terms of the leading research organizations, the majority of the top-10 institutions are based in the US (7/10), while two are from Europe (2/10), and one is from China (1/10) (Table S2). The network map of institutions was visualized using Citespace. illustrates the map, where each node represents an institution. The size of each node is proportional to the number of publications. The lines connecting the nodes indicate the collaborative relationships. The National Institutes of Health (NIH) in the US emerged as the most productive institution in the field of therapeutic cancer vaccine research. They had 116 publications that were cited 3838 times. The National Cancer Institute (NCI) also made a significant contribution with 100 publications that received a total of 3119 citations. Other prominent institutions in this field include the University of California System, Harvard University, University of Texas System, and Johns Hopkins University. Each of these institutions produced over 60 publications and received more than 2500 citations. These findings highlight the dominant and influential position of US institutions in the field of therapeutic cancer vaccine research.

## Periodicals and co-cited periodicals
From 2013 to 2022, a total of 715 publications on therapeutic cancer vaccines were published by the top 10 productive academic journals (Table S3 and ). Oncoimmunology published the highest number of articles (113), followed by Cancer Immunology Immunotherapy (107), Frontiers in Immunology (106), and Journal for Immunotherapy of Cancer (73). The ranking based on publication counts did not align with the ranking based on total citation counts. Nature, despite having only 8 publications, received the highest total citation count of 4,788, indicating its significant influence in this research domain. Following Nature, Clinical Cancer Research received 3,470 citations, Oncoimmunology received 3,090 citations, and Cancer Immunology Immunotherapy received 2,496 citations. Additionally, Nature also had the highest H-index of 598.5, further highlighting its impact and contributions to this field. A density visualization map of the journal co-citation network was generated using VOSviewer. In the map ( ), the color scheme was used to represent the density of co-citations for each journal. Red color indicated high density, suggesting a large number of co-citations, while blue color indicated lower density, representing less frequently cited journals. By analyzing the density visualization map ( ), it was observed that Clinical Cancer Research, Nature, Cancer Research, Journal of Immunotherapy of Cancer, and Journal of Clinical Oncology emerged as the most influential publication sources in the field of therapeutic cancer vaccines during the analyzed period.

 

## The most-productive authors
The network visualization map of authors, generated using Citespace, consisted of 455 nodes ( ). Each node represents an author, and the size of the node corresponds to the number of publications by that author. The lines between nodes indicate collaborative relationships. The top 10 productive authors in the field of therapeutic cancer vaccines are listed in Table S4. Each of these authors has published 17 or more papers, with a minimum of 280 citations per scholar from 2013 to 2022. Among them, Jeffrey Schlom has contributed the largest number of documents (31 publications) with a total of 827 citations, followed by Kyogo Itoh (29 publications) with a total of 541 citations.

## Co-cited references and co-cited reference bursts
Citation analysis, a widely used bibliometric tool, was applied to conduct co-citation analysis of references. In this analysis, the co-citation relationship is established when two or more articles are simultaneously cited as references in another literature. VOSviewer was utilized to conduct co-citation analysis of references, resulting in the creation of a network visualization map and a density visualization map. In the network visualization map ( ), publications are represented as nodes, and the connections between nodes represent co-citation relationships. The size of each node reflects the number of co-citations it has received. The density visualization map ( ) uses varying shades of red to indicate the frequency of co-citations for references, with darker shades representing higher frequencies. Table S5 presents the top 20 most co-cited references, ranked by their total link strength (TLS). The majority of highly co-cited references in this study were found to be published in journals with high impact factors (IF), with 19 publications having an IF greater than 60. It is worth noting that among the top 20 co-cited references, 16 were articles, while the remaining four were reviews. Among these, the most co-cited reference was an article published by Patrick Alexander and colleagues in Nature in 2017. This article demonstrated the feasibility, safety, and immunogenicity of a neoantigen vaccine, thereby providing a strong rationale for further development of this cancer therapeutic vaccine either alone or in combination with checkpoint blockade or other immunotherapies. According to the second most co-cited reference by Sahin et al., it was suggested that individual mutations could be utilized for therapeutic cancer vaccines. This discovery opened up possibilities for personalized immunotherapy for cancer patients. In addition, the third co-cited reference showed that metastatic melanoma patients who received a gp100 peptide vaccine along with Ipilimumab experienced improved overall survival. These findings highlight the significance of therapeutic cancer vaccines as a research topic that has gained popularity over the past decade, either as standalone treatments or in combination with other immunotherapeutic approaches.

 Citation burst, as defined by a significant increase in the frequency of citations for a specific reference within a certain time period, indicates rapid recognition and dissemination of the reference within the research field. In order to gain a deeper insight into the developmental process over the past decade, our team utilized CiteSpace to analyze citation bursts in publications focusing on therapeutic cancer vaccines. We considered burst durations of ≥2 years and identified 111 references with strong bursts. presents the top 25 references with the most substantial citation bursts.

 In the visualization, each publication is represented by a red line indicating the burst interval, which signifies the start and end year of the citation explosion and the duration of the burst. The blue timeline represents the publication timeline, with light and dark blue segments indicating the absence and presence of citations, respectively. The references with strong bursts primarily pertain to clinical studies of therapeutic cancer vaccines, either alone or in combination with other therapeutic strategies. Among these references, the article published by Kantoff et al. had the highest citation burst strength (49.9) suggesting its significant impact in the research field. Moreover, the studies conducted by Sahin, Ott, Keskin, Hilf and Tanyi have consistently shown a sustained impact in the field of cancer vaccines, as indicated in . This suggests that their influence remains significant and continuous.

## Analysis of keyword co-occurrence
Keywords play a crucial role in summarizing the main ideas and core content of a literature. By visualizing the co-occurrence of keywords, researchers can gain a concise overview of research trends and potential future directions, which can offer valuable insights. In this study, a total of 8,915 keywords were identified using VOSviewer software. From these, we extracted and analyzed 158 keywords that appeared more than 30 times. illustrates the density visualization of keyword co-occurrence, with deeper colors indicating higher frequencies of co-occurrence. The most frequently occurring keywords, as shown in , include ‘dendritic cell vaccine’, ‘cancer vaccine’, ‘T-cells’, ‘immune response’, and ‘cancer vaccines’. presents the overlay visualization of keywords, where the colors represent their average publication year (APY), showcasing the evolution of keywords over time. In the overlay visualization, blue-colored keywords indicate earlier appearances, while green denotes continuous occurrence. Notably, yellow-colored keywords indicate newly emerging terms in recent years, highlighting research hotspots and trends in the field. Accordingly, the research focus in therapeutic cancer vaccines has shifted from peptide vaccines and dendritic cell vaccines to mRNA vaccines and neoantigens. Moreover, there has been significant attention given to nanoparticle-based drug delivery systems for therapeutic cancer vaccines in preclinical models.

 Keyword clustering is a technique used to group keywords that share similar topics within a specific research field. In , CiteSpace has divided the 15 relevant clusters as follows: cancer immunotherapy, regulatory T cells, antigen presentation, pancreatic cancer, immune checkpoint inhibitor, combination therapy, colorectal cancer, delivery, lung cancer, breast cancer, therapeutic vaccine, tumor microenvironment, response, mRNA vaccine, and immunogenic cell death. To gain further insights into the research on therapeutic cancer vaccines, the intellectual base can be summarized into three main classes. First, research in this area focuses on understanding the mechanisms of therapeutic cancer vaccines and developing strategies to overcome immune resistance and escape. This is represented by clusters labeled as ‘regulatory T cells’, ‘antigen presentation’, ‘tumor microenvironment’, and ‘immunogenic cell death’. Second, another important class of research involves the application of therapeutic cancer vaccines in different types of cancers. This is evident from clusters labeled as ‘breast cancer’, ‘pancreatic cancer’, ‘lung cancer’, and ‘colorectal cancer’. Furthermore, an important area of research is the exploration of combination therapeutic strategies that combine cancer vaccines with other treatment modalities, including ‘immune checkpoint inhibitors’. Another significant focus is the development of vaccine delivery systems utilizing diverse biomaterials.

## Discussion

## General information
In the past decade, there has been a resurgence of therapeutic cancer vaccines, thanks to advancements in biotechnology. Particularly, the identification of immunogenic tumor antigens has played a significant role in this resurgence. These vaccines have the potential to reshape the body’s immune system, transforming previously ‘cold’ tumors into ‘hot’ tumors. When combined with other therapy methods, they can greatly improve treatment outcomes. These vaccines, when used in conjunction with other therapeutic methods, have been demonstrated to effectively enhance treatment outcomes, , , indicating a promising strategy for cancer therapy. By conducting a bibliometric analysis on therapeutic cancer vaccines, researchers can identify the knowledge structure, key areas of focus, and emerging trends in this field. This study presents the first comprehensive bibliometric and visualization analysis of research on therapeutic cancer vaccines published between 2013 and 2022, offering valuable insights into the development and current trends in this area of research.
VOSviewer and CiteSpace are widely used tools for conducting bibliometric analysis to construct, analyze, and visualize the network and trends of scientific publications within a specific research area. , In this study, we employed VOSviewer and CiteSpace software to analyze and visualize publications extracted from the WOSCC database. Our analysis revealed a total of 2,741 English-written publications, comprising of 2,112 articles and 629 reviews. The escalating annual publication counts and citation frequencies observed in the last decade signify the heightened interest in therapeutic cancer vaccine research.
This research field has seen contributions from thousands of authors across 89 countries/regions. The United States (US) has emerged as the most productive country, leading in terms of publication output, citation frequency, and H-index, which signifies its prominent academic reputation in this field. China, Japan, and Germany have also demonstrated productive in this area. In the realm of international cooperation, the USA and China have exhibited the closest collaboration, while other countries/regions have shown less extensive interaction and cooperation. This highlights the significant potential for further collaborative research. In terms of productivity, the National Institutes of Health (NIH) of the US secured the top spot, with the National Cancer Institute (NCI) and the University of California System closely following. These institutions made significant contributions, with each having more than 90 publications and cumulative citation counts surpassing 3,000. Among the research journals, Nature emerged as the most influential, with the highest citation frequencies and H-index. Jeffrey Schlom stood out as the most productive author.
Co-citation analysis of references revealed that several promising therapeutic approaches for anti-tumor immunotherapy have been identified. These include cancer vaccines based on neoantigens, peptides, tumor cells, and mRNA. One notable study, conducted by Patrick et al., stands out with the highest total link strength (TLS). In their study, they designed a personalized neoantigen vaccine that showed the ability to generate highly specific immune responses. This finding highlights the significant influence of their work on therapeutic cancer vaccine research. Gubin et al. developed a therapeutic synthetic long-peptide vaccine by employing genomics and bioinformatics techniques to identify tumor-specific mutant proteins. Their study demonstrated a comparable level of tumor rejection to that achieved by checkpoint blockade immunotherapy. Another highly co-cited study investigated the development of a synthetic poly-neo-epitope mRNA vaccine that specifically targeted the individual tumor-specific mutations of patients. This vaccine demonstrated remarkable tumor control and successfully eliminated established aggressively growing tumors in mice. Sipuleucel-T, a dendritic cell vaccine, and gp100, a peptide vaccine, were observed to have strong bursts of interest, highlighting their significant contributions to the field of therapeutic cancer vaccine research.
Keyword analysis has revealed a shift in research trends from peptide vaccines and dendritic cell vaccines to neoantigens and mRNA vaccines in recent years. Cluster analysis of keywords has identified the main aspects of research as the mechanism, application, and delivery of therapeutic cancer vaccines. Historically, research on therapeutic cancer vaccines has primarily focused on shared antigens to induce anti-tumor immune responses. In recent years, there has been significant focus on the development of cancer vaccines that can stimulate specific immune responses. These vaccines aim to target tumor-associated antigens (TAAs) and tumor-specific antigens (TSAs) to enhance the immune system’s ability to recognize and attack cancer cells. Various types of therapeutic cancer vaccines have been created using different antigen delivery methods, such as peptide vaccines, dendritic cell vaccines, nucleic acid vaccines, and viral-based vaccines. Dendritic cells play a crucial role in regulating both innate and adaptive immune responses by presenting antigens to naïve CD4+ and CD8+ T cells. Dendritic cell vaccines, such as Sipuleucel-T, have been extensively studied in the past decade. Sipuleucel-T was the first therapeutic cancer vaccine approved by the FDA in 2010 for metastatic castrate-resistant prostate cancer. Peptide vaccines, which consist of highly immunogenic antigenic amino acid sequences, have been developed. Specifically, synthetic long peptides have been designed to efficiently deliver antigens to antigen-presenting cells (APCs) and induce CD4+ and CD8+ T cell responses. However, the success observed in animal models with synthetic peptide vaccines has seldom been replicated in human clinical trials. , In recent years, significant technological advancements have made mRNA a more viable candidate for vaccines. Moreover, the outbreak of COVID-19 has accelerated the development of vaccine technology. mRNA vaccines have taken the lead in global COVID-19 vaccine research due to their shorter research cycle, lower cost, and ability for large-scale production. These factors have also instilled hope among researchers for the potential use of mRNA vaccines in tumor treatment.
In addition, nanoparticles have been extensively utilized as vaccine carriers for delivering antigens and adjuvants to tumor sites or lymph nodes. This approach aims to stimulate strong anti-tumor immune responses and inhibit tumor progression. Various nanomaterials have been shown to elicit danger signals to dendritic cells (DCs) during tumor antigen processing. For example, Wang et al. developed an acidic/photo-sensitive dendritic cell-based neoantigen nano-vaccine for treating hepatocellular carcinoma. This nano-vaccine specifically targeted H22 tumors, induced immunological cell death to enhance the release of tumor-associated antigens through photodynamic therapy, and homed to lymph nodes. As a result, it activated and proliferated neoantigen-specific T cells, thereby acting as an in situ tumor vaccination. The nano-vaccine effectively strengthened the antitumor T-cell response against primary H22 tumor growth and significantly prolonged survival. Nanotechnology offers a promising avenue for the development of innovative therapeutic cancer vaccines. While numerous cancer vaccines have been formulated and studied in both pre-clinical and clinical settings, only a handful have shown clinical effectiveness. As a result, there is growing interest in exploring combination strategies that integrate therapeutic cancer vaccines with other treatment modalities such as chemotherapy, radiotherapy, surgical treatment, and immune checkpoint blockade. In a phase 3 prospective externally controlled cohort trial, it was shown that the addition of DCVax-L, an autologous tumor lysate-loaded dendritic cell vaccine, to standard care resulted in a significant improvement in median overall survival for patients with newly diagnosed and recurrent glioblastoma. This improvement was both clinically meaningful and statistically significant. The combination of therapeutic cancer vaccines with immune checkpoint inhibitors shows great promise, as indicated by the growing number of clinical trials studying these treatment strategies. 

## Hotspots and development trends
In the last ten years, researchers have made considerable efforts to improve the tumor specificity of cancer vaccines. This study presents the first bibliometric analysis of publications on therapeutic cancer vaccines, providing valuable insights into the progress and development of this research field from 2013 to 2022. The most productive regions were found to be North America and East Asia. Neoantigen and mRNA vaccines have received growing attention compared to cell-based and peptide vaccines. Additionally, the combination of therapeutic cancer vaccines with other treatment strategies such as chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and immunotherapy has demonstrated promising results. The integration of nanotechnology in the fabrication of therapeutic cancer vaccines is a promising research trend. This is mainly due to advancements in biomaterial design and synthesis.
With an enhanced understanding of tumor immune response mechanisms, immune evasion, and the rapid advancement of tumor immunotherapy, therapeutic cancer vaccines have gained considerable attention from clinicians and the pharmaceutical industry. For instance, Parkhurst et al. conducted a study where they successfully identified neoantigen-reactive tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TIL) in 83% of patients (62 out of 75) with gastrointestinal cancers. This was achieved through high-throughput immunologic screening of mutant gene products that were identified via whole-exome sequencing. The study demonstrated that cultured TILs recognized specific neoantigens produced by genetic mutations in the patient’s own tumor cells. This finding lays the groundwork for the development of personalized cancer vaccines. mRNA-4157, a cancer vaccine that uses messenger RNA encapsulated in lipid nanoparticles, has shown potential in encoding neoantigens that are unique to each patient’s tumor. Zhong et al. assessed the effectiveness of the bioinformatics algorithm used in mRNA-4157 to identify neoantigens in a group of colorectal cancer patients. The results demonstrated that the bioinformatics algorithm for mRNA-4157 successfully predicted and selected neoantigens with preexisting TIL reactivities through human ex vivo assays. This accuracy makes it a promising tool for designing vaccines. The latest clinical research progress on mRNA-4157 was announced at the AACR meeting in April 2023. The study showed that when used in combination with Pembrolizumab as adjuvant therapy, mRNA-4157 exhibited encouraging outcomes. This randomized controlled trial provided the first evidence supporting the potential benefits of personalized mRNA vaccines for cancer patients.
Despite numerous therapeutic cancer vaccines having been tested for their anticancer therapeutic efficacy and safety, only a handful of them have been approved for clinical application with proven clinical efficacy. Considerable research efforts are still required to accelerate and broaden their application in clinical practice. The clinical translation of therapeutic cancer vaccines remains challenging, and future research and development should consider several aspects. The design of the therapeutic cancer vaccine plays a crucial role in its successful clinical application. Researchers need to consider various mechanisms that govern cancer immune ignorance, exclusion, suppression, and escape when designing a vaccine. Understanding the multifaceted and complex mechanisms of tumor resistance to immunotherapy is essential for the development of effective therapeutic cancer vaccines. Additionally, the selection of immunogenic vaccine platforms and antigens is a key area of focus in cancer vaccine research. Technological advancements will provide valuable insights in choosing the most suitable target antigens for each vaccine platform. Encouraging studies have shown that therapeutic cancer vaccines are most effective in patients who have a low disease burden and limited or reduced systemic and local immunosuppression. Therefore, patient selection for clinical trials of therapeutic cancer vaccines is of utmost importance. Furthermore, due to the complex mechanisms of tumor resistance to vaccines, combination strategies that integrate multiple treatment modalities have been proposed to overcome a suppressive tumor microenvironment (TME) and improve treatment outcomes. It is crucial to determine the appropriate initial clinical dose and administration schedule when conducting clinical studies involving combination therapies. Ensuring the effective delivery of vaccine components, including antigens and adjuvants, to the appropriate destination (such as the tumor site, proximal secondary lymphoid organ, or the tumor microenvironment) is a challenge that needs to be addressed. Further investigation is required to explore the ideal release kinetics and tumor retention of the carrying components in different cancer types, as the delivery platform of antigen-based vaccines plays a major role in determining the vaccine’s success. This bibliometric analysis shows that combination therapy, which integrates therapeutic cancer vaccines with other treatment modalities like immunotherapy, as well as nanotechnology-based vaccine delivery systems, has received significant research attention over the past decade.

## Limitations
This study offers a comprehensive visualization analysis of therapeutic cancer vaccines in a clear and objective manner. However, there are some limitations to consider. Firstly, the analysis solely relied on the WOSCC database, neglecting other databases such as PubMed, Embase, and Ovid, which could potentially lead to a limited extraction of publications. Nevertheless, the WOSCC database includes influential and high-quality journals, providing extensive citation index records, making it suitable for scientific and bibliometric analysis. Secondly, the inclusion of only English articles and reviews may introduce a linguistic bias. The analysis does not include the latest publications from 2023, potentially overlooking recently published high-quality articles. However, the results and findings remain robust as the study extensively includes authoritative and influential academic journals.

## Conclusions
In summary, the field of therapeutic cancer vaccines has undergone significant development and growth in the last decade. The potential of this treatment approach, especially when used in combination with other therapies, is increasingly recognized in numerous studies. In this study, we conducted a comprehensive analysis of scientific publications on therapeutic cancer vaccines, examining various aspects such as annual research trends, key contributors, and prominent academic journals. This study presents the first bibliometric and visualization analysis of therapeutic cancer vaccine research, summarizing the main research directions, current research hotspots, and potential research frontiers and trends in the future. It serves as a valuable guide and reference for researchers aiming to enhance their knowledge in this promising research field.
