## Introduction
The aim of this study was to assess the applicability of an external control arm (ECA) approach in the clinical development of the oral factor XIa inhibitor asundexian for stroke prevention in patients with atrial fibrillation (AF), using prospectively collected data from the phase 2 PACIFIC-AF trial (NCT04218266) and real-world individual-level data from patients with AF treated with apixaban in the Optum® de-identified Electronic Health Record data set (Optum® EHR) 2013–2019.

## Methods
To build ECAs, real-world patients meeting trial eligibility criteria were matched to patients enrolled in PACIFIC-AF. The primary outcome was the composite of International Society on Thrombosis and Haemostasis–defined major bleeding or clinically relevant non-major bleeding. Event rates were compared between PACIFIC-AF and ECAs at 85 days of trial duration and projected up to 2 years.

## Results
Overall, 160,153 real-world patients met PACIFIC-AF eligibility criteria and were matched to patients from the PACIFIC-AF apixaban arm on 101 variables, with matching ratios of 1:10, 1:5, and 1:1. At day 85, the number of events for the primary outcome was 92 (3.68%) in the 1:10 ECA (2500 patients) and 6 (2.40%) in the PACIFIC-AF apixaban arm (250 patients), with incidence rates of 16.67 (90% confidence interval [CI] 13.92–19.63) and 11.10 (90% CI 4.83–19.45) per 100 person-years, respectively.

## Conclusions
ECAs matching the PACIFIC-AF apixaban arm could be built from EHRs with concordant event rates for key trial endpoints. The ECA approach enabled the determination of event rates for treatment duration up to 2 years, thereby informing the asundexian pivotal phase 3 trial in AF.

## Key Summary Points

 

## Introduction
Direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) and vitamin K antagonists are the recommended therapies for prevention of stroke in patients with atrial fibrillation (AF) [ , ]. An unmet need exists for safer methods of anticoagulation than the current standard of care [ ].
Inhibition of factor (F) XI/XIa, an enzyme of the intrinsic coagulation pathway upstream of FXa, has gained interest as an alternative anticoagulation target [ – ]. Asundexian (BAY 2433334) is an oral small-molecule FXIa inhibitor that was compared with the DOAC apixaban in patients with AF in PACIFIC-AF (Program of AntiCoagulation via Inhibition of FXIa by the oral Compound BAY 2433334 – Atrial Fibrillation; NCT04218266), a phase 2, multicenter, international, randomized, double-blind, double-dummy trial. During the 12-week (85-day) study period, patients receiving once-daily asundexian 20 mg or 50 mg experienced a lower rate of the composite primary endpoint of International Society on Thrombosis and Haemostasis (ISTH)-defined major bleeding and clinically relevant non-major bleeding (CRNMB) than those in the apixaban arm [ ]. Given the short-term, dose-finding design of PACIFIC-AF, the comparative efficacy of asundexian versus apixaban was not formally assessed [ ]. A real-world study was initiated in parallel to PACIFIC-AF as a pilot project aiming to assess applicability and to demonstrate benefits of an external control arm (ECA) approach in the asundexian clinical development program using data from patients with AF collected in routine clinical practice.
“External controls” is an umbrella term that describes a sample of individuals external to a given study that is comparable to the study treatment arm population. A control arm built from individual-level external data is an ECA. An ECA approach comprises a collection of statistical methods to evaluate the comparative effects of a treatment using external control data [ ]. ECAs help to contextualize and to improve the interpretation of single-arm trials or long-term uncontrolled extensions to randomized controlled trials (RCTs) commonly conducted in rare or serious health conditions [ – ]. External controls may be used to augment randomized control arms as part of a hybrid approach [ ]. If an RCT has unequal randomization ratios or there is a need to evaluate the effects of a new treatment in specific patient subgroups, adding external controls to augment RCT data may result in improved statistical power of comparative analyses [ ]. The ECA approach is viable to generate evidence of the effect of an investigational therapy compared with several existing therapies besides the one chosen in the RCT. Contemporary real-world data (RWD) and the ECA approach provide a viable alternative to indirect treatment comparisons with historical RCTs used in health technology assessments [ ]. Furthermore, the ECA approach is instrumental for informing and optimizing clinical development programs through hypothesis generation, clinical study design adaptations, and data-driven decision-making [ , ].
The objectives of this study were: to build an ECA for PACIFIC-AF using real-world electronic health record (EHR) data from patients with AF treated with apixaban; to visually demonstrate concordance between event rates for key trial endpoints in the ECAs and the internal control arm; and to inform phase 3 study design with regard to event rate estimates for long-term treatment duration exceeding 85-day estimates obtained in PACIFIC-AF. The information used to design a pivotal RCT often comes from the published literature or early phase clinical studies with short duration, limited sample size, altered patient population, and different endpoints. Real-world evidence (RWE) programs may generate contemporary evidence to inform the pivotal study design with regard to event rates in the target patient population for the anticipated endpoints with high precision due to increased sample size. For that purpose, the ECA approach and the target trial emulation framework are instrumental. Ultimately, the aim of our study was to demonstrate the validity of, and to report the limitations of, the ECA approach to inform future applications in clinical research of cardiovascular (CV) diseases and beyond.

## Methods
This was a hybrid observational study that used prospectively collected data from PACIFIC-AF and longitudinal real-world patient-level data from the Optum® de-identified Electronic Health Record data set (Optum® EHR) from January 1, 2013, to June 30, 2019. The start date was defined based on the date of apixaban approval for medical use in the USA. The data cut from Optum® EHR was based on the data available at the point when the feasibility of using an ECA approach was originally determined. No updates of the data set were arranged, and it was not anticipated that the results of the study would be influenced by the choice of a newer data cut, given no substantial changes in US routine care for AF over the time period in question.
Details of PACIFIC-AF have been published previously [ ]. Briefly, patients with AF who qualified for anticoagulant therapy were randomly assigned 1:1:1 to once-daily asundexian 20 mg (N = 251), once-daily asundexian 50 mg (N = 254), or apixaban in accordance with the approved label (N = 250) for 85 days of blinded treatment.
The Optum® EHR is a longitudinal EHR repository derived from dozens of healthcare provider organizations in the USA. Administrative medical data is obtained from both inpatient and ambulatory EHRs, practice management systems, and other internal systems. The Optum® EHR data set contains records from over 100 million patients and provides a representative sample of the US population. Optum® EHR data elements include demographics, medications prescribed and administered, immunizations, allergies, lab results, vital signs and other observable measurements, clinical and inpatient stay administrative data, and coded diagnoses and procedures. Data are recorded using International Classification of Diseases, Ninth/Tenth Revision (ICD-9/10), Clinical Modification diagnosis codes, Current Procedural Terminology 4 (CPT-4), Healthcare Common Procedure Coding System (HCPCS), ICD-9/10-Procedure Coding System procedure codes, and National Drug Codes (NDC).
Measures were taken to ensure that conceptual and operational definitions of the study variables were of high quality. Assignment of Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA) terms and Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical (ATC) classification system codes used to collect data in PACIFIC-AF to real-world data (RWD) coding systems, and translation of the adjudication algorithm for identification of bleeding events from PACIFIC-AF to the RWD environment were performed by subject-matter experts.
Secondary use of the PACIFIC-AF data followed the established framework for clinical data sharing and was covered by the informed consent form of the trial. Data included in the Optum® EHR are de-identified and follow the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) of 1996 to preserve participant anonymity and confidentiality, and as such, this study followed the principles of the declaration of Helsinki without the requirement for review from a formal ethics review committee. The use of the provided Optum® EHR data was determined by the New England Institutional Review Board not to constitute research involving human participants and was therefore exempt from board oversight.

## Generation of an ECA-Eligible Patient Pool
Patients in the Optum® EHR data set were included in the ECA-eligible patient pool if they had newly initiated apixaban between January 1, 2014 and June 30, 2019 per written prescription in accordance with the approved label [ ], had ≥ 1 year of EHR activity before apixaban initiation, and met the PACIFIC-AF inclusion and exclusion criteria as mapped to the RWD environment. Due to the anticipated completeness of baseline data collection in PACIFIC-AF, patients with missing baseline values for numeric and categoric variables (Supplementary Material Table ) in the Optum® EHR data set were excluded. Comorbid conditions and co-medication use at baseline were defined as binary variables. Given the nature of RWD, the absence of data for comorbidities or co-medications was assumed to indicate the absence of the condition or medication use (no patient exclusion or missing data imputation were performed). Some PACIFIC-AF exclusion criteria (e.g., patients who had planned major surgery or intervention during the study period and patients with an anticipated need for chronic therapy with nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs) could not be mapped to the real-world environment owing to inherent differences in data collection between the two sources.
The full list of PACIFIC-AF patient selection criteria mapped to the real-world environment are presented in Supplementary Material Table ; corresponding operational definitions are provided in the supplementary file “pacific_af_eca_phenotypes.xlsx.” All required medical codes are provided in the supplementary file “pacific_af_eca_codelists.xlsx.” In PACIFIC-AF, 45% of patients had previously been treated with a DOAC [ ]. To ensure that a similar proportion of apixaban-treatment-experienced patients were included in the ECA, an algorithm was used to assign an index date after the apixaban initiation date to mimic the fraction of “treatment-experienced” patients. This algorithm was designed to simulate a trial enrollment process and, thus, never took any future data (e.g., duration of coverage in the database) into account. The algorithm worked by stepping sequentially through a set of discrete dates and sampling patients for enrollment at each date. If a sampled patient satisfied all inclusion and exclusion criteria on the given date, the patient was assigned this date as the index date. Patients who did not satisfy the criteria or who were not sampled on the given date were returned to the sampling pool and considered for the next possible index date.

## Matching
To build ECAs, patients from the ECA-eligible patient pool were required to match patients enrolled in PACIFIC-AF on demographic and observation parameters, comorbidities, and co-medications as collected at baseline in PACIFIC-AF, resulting in 101 variables (Supplementary Material Table ). Medical codes required for matching variables are provided in the supplementary file “pacific_af_eca_codelists.xlsx.” A predefined matching quality threshold of absolute standardized mean difference (SMD) < 0.1 was set. For categorical variables, the threshold had to be met for a mean of absolute SMDs across categories. Three matching techniques were used to build ECAs: propensity score (PS); evolutionary algorithm (EA); and linear programming (LP) with arbitrarily chosen matching ratios of 1:1, 1:5, 1:10, and 1:20. Details of the matching procedure have been published elsewhere [ ].

## Outcomes
Study outcomes corresponded to the best possible approximation of the key PACIFIC-AF trial endpoints. The primary safety outcome was the composite of ISTH major bleeding or CRNMB [ , ]. The secondary safety outcomes were ISTH major bleeding and ISTH CRNMB evaluated individually. Identification of the safety outcomes in the Optum® EHR data set was performed using a novel algorithm [ ]. Namely, the ISTH definition for major bleeding was divided into three subclauses: fatal bleeds, critical organ bleeds, and symptomatic bleeds associated with hemoglobin reductions. Data elements from EHRs required to identify patients fulfilling these subclauses were defined by the ICD codes that describe key bleeding events. Data providing context to bleeding severity included “interaction type” (inpatient or outpatient), “position” (primary/discharge or secondary), laboratory tests, blood transfusion procedures, and mortality data. Exploratory outcomes included a composite of ischemic stroke (IS), systemic embolism (SE), myocardial infarction (MI), or CV death, as well as those events individually, a composite of IS or SE, and all-cause mortality. CV death was identified using the month of death and presence of CV death medical codes in patient EHRs. Conceptual and operational definitions of study outcomes are provided in the supplementary file “pacific_af_eca_phenotypes.xlsx.”

## Data Analysis
Baseline characteristics of patients in the ECA-eligible patient pool, the ECA, and the PACIFIC-AF apixaban arm were analyzed using descriptive statistics. Patients in the ECA were followed up from the index date until occurrence of the outcome, the end of EHR activity, or the end of data availability, whichever came first. The number of events, the incidence rate (per 100 person-years [PY]) with corresponding 90% confidence intervals (CIs) based on Poisson distribution, and the cumulative incidence (with 95% CIs) of primary, secondary, and exploratory outcomes were estimated for the ECA population at 85 days and for up to 2 years of the follow-up period through an intent-to-treat approach and time-to-first-event analysis using the Aalen–Johansen estimator. The competing event was non-CV death for the outcomes that included CV death. There was no competing event for the all-cause mortality outcome. All-cause mortality was the competing event for all other outcomes. The number of events, the incidence rate, and the cumulative incidence at 85 days observed in the ECA were descriptively compared with the corresponding measures in the PACIFIC-AF apixaban arm. Aalen–Johansen curves were plotted for the PACIFIC-AF asundexian and apixaban arms, and the ECA if at least three events were observed during the 85-day treatment period.

## Results
In total, 160,153 patients were included in the ECA-eligible patient pool from the Optum® EHR data set (Fig.  ). Three matching techniques used to build ECAs demonstrated various levels of performance (Supplementary Material Figure ). The EA method outperformed the PS and LP methods in the ability to provide the highest number of ECAs with the assessed matching ratios of 1:1, 1:5, 1:10, and 1:20, with all 101 matching variables reaching the predefined matching threshold [ ]. None of the methods investigated were able to build an ECA with a 1:20 matching ratio; the EA method resulted in three ECAs, whereas the LP and PS methods resulted in two ECAs each (Supplementary Material Figure ). The categorical variable “time since AF diagnosis” was missing for four participants enrolled in PACIFIC-AF but was available for all those included in the ECA-eligible patient pool. Mean absolute SMD across all categories of the variable “time since AF diagnosis” was below the matching threshold. In the 1:10 ECA, for the categorical variable “race/ethnicity,” the EA method matched patients on all categories besides “African American or Black,” but the threshold of mean absolute SMD < 0.1 across all categories was met. The results of the EA-based analysis are reported here for three ECAs with matching ratios of 1:10 (main analysis), 1:5, and 1:1. 
The baseline characteristics of patients in the PACIFIC-AF apixaban arm and the ECA-eligible patient pool were broadly similar before matching but improved after applying matching, as shown in Table  for the 1:10 ECA and in Supplementary Material Table for the 1:1 and 1:5 ECAs. The median follow-up period was 568 days (interquartile range [IQR] 292–965 days) for the 1:10 ECA, and 590 days (IQR 261–1046 days), and 545 days (IQR 275–965 days) for the 1:1 and 1:5 ECAs, respectively. 
At 85 days, the number of events (proportion of patients with an event) for the primary composite outcome was 6 (2.40%) in the PACIFIC-AF apixaban arm (250 patients) and 92 (3.68%) in the 1:10 ECA (2500 patients) [ ]. The corresponding incidence rates were 11.10 (90% CI 4.83–19.45) per 100 PY in the PACIFIC-AF apixaban arm and 16.67 (90% CI 13.92–19.63) per 100 PY in the 1:10 ECA at 85 days (Table  ) [ ]. 
For the secondary outcomes at day 85, none of the ISTH major bleeding events were observed in the PACFIC-AF apixaban arm, and there were 17 such events (0.68%) in the 1:10 ECA with an incidence rate of 3.02 (90% CI 1.93–4.32) per 100 PY. There were six ISTH CRNMB events (2.40%) in the PACFIC-AF apixaban arm, with 78 such events (3.12%) observed in the 1:10 ECA. The corresponding incidence rates were 11.10 (90% CI 4.83–19.45) per 100 PY in the PACIFIC-AF control arm and 14.10 (90% CI 11.58–16.82) per 100 PY in the 1:10 ECA [ ]. For the exploratory composite outcome of IS, SE, MI, and CV death at day 85, three events (1.20%) occurred in the PACIFIC-AF control arm, and there were 56 such events (2.24%) in the 1:10 ECA. There was no IS or SE observed in the PACIFIC-AF apixaban arm at day 85, and there were 19 such events (0.76%) in the 1:10 ECA. All-cause mortality at day 85 in the PACIFIC-AF apixaban arm was slightly higher than in the 1:10 ECA, with four (1.60%) versus 21 (0.84%) deaths. ## Discussion
The broadly accepted standard for demonstrating a favorable benefit–risk profile of a given therapy is an RCT. Although RCTs undoubtedly form the basis of drug development and regulatory approval, they are associated with various limitations, such as ethical concerns regarding the use of placebo as control therapy, resource intensity, recruitment challenges, lack of general population representativeness, and limited generalizability to a real-world setting [ ]. Consequently, there is a need to supplement RCTs with evidence generated from other types of studies, such as single-arm, observational, or hybrid studies that analyze data collected in various settings. RCTs are conducted under controlled conditions, ensuring that the only difference between groups is the intervention being studied. Externally controlled studies often rely on RWD, which can introduce variability and confounding factors that are difficult to control.
Increased availability of high-quality RWD has led to a growing acceptance of RWE by regulators of medical products [ – ]. Best practices to promote standards for the use of the ECA approach are being developed by the scientific community, investigators, regulators, payers, and sponsors [ , – ]. When applying the ECA approach using RWD, general principles of regulatory-grade RWE generation should be followed [ , ]. Key elements include fit-for-purpose data source identification, accurate definition and completeness of study variables, assignment of index date, adherence to data curation and data processing standards, adequate choice of analytical methods for addressing confounding in non-randomized studies, and adherence to the Guidelines for Good Pharmacoepidemiology Practice.
Although the ECA framework is a rapidly evolving field, its use by healthcare decision-makers is yet to be fully accepted. Adoption of the ECA approach depends on continued dialog among relevant parties [ – ]. Multiple proof-of-concept studies in individuals with cancer have demonstrated the feasibility of the ECA approach using EHR databases to complement single-arm clinical trials [ , ]; however, a limited number of regulatory submissions containing real-world external comparators have been successful [ , , ]. Some health authorities have indicated a preference for a hybrid design with a mix of randomized and external controls over complete replacement of an RCT control arm [ , ]. A hybrid design allows regulators to evaluate the comparability of the randomized control arm and ECAs, thereby minimizing the risk of misinterpreting the results. Performing a health technology assessment of a new therapy often requires external comparisons. Indirect treatment comparisons (ITCs) represent a classical technique and utilize historical data from RCTs that may have been conducted several decades ago. Contemporary RWD and the ECA approach provide a viable alternative to ITCs [ ].
The present study contributes to the body of evidence on the applicability of the ECA approach and RWD collected in routine clinical practice to drug development.
The study was initiated in parallel to the PACIFIC-AF phase 2 trial as a pilot project with the objectives of building an ECA using real-world EHR data from patients with AF treated with apixaban, demonstrating concordance of event rates for key trial endpoints in the internal control arm and the ECAs, and informing a related phase 3 study design (OCEANIC-AF, NCT05643573) [ ]. A large cohort of 160,153 patients in the US Optum® EHR data set satisfied the selection criteria of PACIFIC-AF and constituted a substantially larger population than the PACIFIC-AF apixaban control arm. Various matching techniques were used to select patients from this cohort who matched the patients in the PACIFIC-AF apixaban arm on 101 variables [ ]. ECAs with ratios of up to 1:10 were built without sacrificing matching quality, allowing high accuracy in treatment effect estimates for key clinical endpoints over a follow-up duration of up to 2 years.
The event rates for the primary, secondary, and exploratory outcomes were broadly similar in the PACIFIC-AF control arm and in the ECAs at 85 days. In the PACIFIC-AF apixaban arm and the 1:10 ECA at 85 days, 2.40% (0 and 2.40%) and 3.68% (0.68% and 3.12%) of patients reported ISTH major bleeding or ISTH CRNMB events (ISTH major bleeding and ISTH CRNMB separately), respectively. An incidence risk of the primary endpoint was expected to be 4% at 85 days in the apixaban control group of PACIFIC-AF during the study planning [ ]. IS/SE events were not observed in the PACIFIC-AF control arm, and there were 19 such events (0.76% of patients) in the 1:10 ECA at day 85. Concordance between event rates for the key trial endpoints in the ECAs and the internal control arm was assessed visually with no statistical method applied, which represents a limitation of the study. However, visual assessment was deemed adequate due to the low number of events in the internal control arm of PACIFIC-AF.
The ECA approach enabled the projection of event rates beyond PACIFIC-AF treatment duration of 85 days up to 2 years. For the primary composite outcome of ISTH major bleeding or CRNMB at day 360, the cumulative incidence was estimated at 11.55% (95% CI 10.26–12.93%) in the 1:10 ECA. The cumulative incidence of ISTH major bleeding was estimated at 2.12% (95% CI 1.58–2.78%) in the 1:10 ECA at 360 days and was consistent with the annual cumulative incidence of ISTH major bleeding previously observed for apixaban treatment (2.13%) in the phase 3 ARISTOTLE trial [ ]. However, this comparison should be interpreted with caution owing to differences in investigated patient populations. In the present study, for ISTH CRNMB and the composite outcome of IS/SE at day 360, the cumulative incidences were 9.90% (95% CI 8.69–11.19%) and 2.60% (95% CI 1.99–3.32%) in the 1:10 ECA, respectively.
OCEANIC-AF was a pivotal phase 3 RCT with the aim of comparing asundexian and apixaban for preventing stroke or SE in patients with AF [ ]. The trial was terminated in November 2023 owing to the efficacy of asundexian being inferior to that of the control treatment. In total, 7395 patients were randomly assigned to receive apixaban. The mean ages of patients in the control arms of both PACIFIC-AF and OCEANIC-AF were identical at 74 years (standard deviation [SD]: 8 years); the mean CHA2DS2-VASc scores were 4.1 (SD: 1.5) and 4.3 (SD: 1.3), respectively. However, there were several critical differences in characteristics such as sex, comorbidities, and previous oral anticoagulant use due to variations in the eligibility criteria of the two trials, and such characteristics are known risk factors for safety and efficacy outcomes in patients with AF. Consequently, differences in the event rates of clinical outcomes observed in the ECAs of PACIFIC-AF and the control arm of OCEANIC-AF are anticipated. Nevertheless, a contextual comparison is warranted.
The median follow-up of OCEANIC-AF was 155 days [ ]. The most comparable follow-up period reported in the present ECA study was 180 days. In the apixaban control arm of OCEANIC-AF, the incidence rate of the primary safety endpoint of ISTH major bleeding was 1.93 (95% CI 1.45–2.48) per 100 PY [ ] and in good concordance with the incidence rate of 2.39 (95% CI 1.58–3.38) per 100 PY in the 1:10 ECA at 180 days. For the primary efficacy endpoint of stroke/SE, the incidence rate was 1.02 (95% CI 0.66–1.44) per 100 PY in the apixaban control arm of OCEANIC-AF [ ] compared with 3.38 (95% CI 2.39–4.53) per 100 PY in the 1:10 ECA at 180 days. It was noted that the incidence rate of IS in the control arm of OCEANIC-AF was notably lower than predicted by the CHA2DS2-VASc score and lower than that observed in the ARISTOTLE trial [ ]. Further research is needed to elucidate the impact of coexisting conditions and treatments of patients with AF on the risk of clinical outcomes over time. The ECA approach might play a crucial role in this endeavor.
Owing to differences in the mechanisms by which data are collected in RWD and RCTs, close attention was given in our study to the transfer of clinical definitions used in the RCT to the real-world setting. A rigorous process of applying high-quality conceptual and operational definitions was implemented by subject-matter experts, including de novo development of a RWD-applicable algorithm to identify ISTH bleeding events [ ]. Owing to mapping between coding systems (MedDRA and ICD/ATC and NDC) and harmonization of study variables, some deviation in reporting baseline characteristics of patients in PACIFIC-AF in this study and in the published article is inevitable [ ].
Individual patient-level data collected in the Optum® EHR data set were sufficiently detailed to replicate the main elements of the PACIFIC-AF trial; however, some limitations associated with use of EHR data apply to this study. As with most secondary data sources, the Optum® EHR data set is vulnerable to misclassification of data, sampling bias, and potential limited follow-up time. To minimize the possibility of missing data in this study, only encounters in the healthcare system were used. The Optum® EHR data set lacks information on dispensation of drug prescriptions and on the exact date and cause of death, with the latter limiting the ability to assess mortality-related endpoints. Secure, de-identified linkage of EHRs with other data sources could potentially address some of these issues but was not implemented in this study.
The Optum® EHR data set covers the USA only, whereas patients in PACIFIC-AF were enrolled from 12 European countries, Canada, and Japan. Matching patients enrolled in PACIFIC-AF to those from the ECA-eligible patient pool on 101 variables was anticipated to eliminate differences in healthcare systems and population structure. Use of multiple RWD sources from various geographical regions should be considered for future cases to address population heterogeneity and the generalizability of the results; however, this might increase operational complexity.
It is also noted that a further limitation of this study is the use of EHR data from 2013 to 2019, with the PACIFIC-AF data being from as late as 2021. To assess the impact of the initial period of DOAC use, a sensitivity analysis was conducted using the EHR data starting from 2015; it demonstrated consistent results with the analysis of the EHR data starting from 2013.

## Conclusion
This study demonstrated the validity of the ECA approach to mimic results of the phase 2 PACIFIC-AF trial using patient-level data from an EHR data set. The ECA approach enabled the determination of event rates for long-term treatment duration up to 2 years in a PACIFIC-AF-like population, thereby informing a pivotal phase 3 trial of asundexian in patients with AF. The ECA approach is well positioned to support modern drug development.
