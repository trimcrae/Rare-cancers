http://orcid.org/0009-0000-3489-0660WeiTing 52049976@qq.comZhouHong 278803770@qq.comWuShuyi 970494842@qq.comHeFei i.fei.he@fjmu.edu.cnYiHuan yihuangsrk@126.comhttp://orcid.org/0000-0002-5629-0348ZhangJinhua There is an association between the type of cancer and the incidence of VTE. Ovarian cancer and endometrial cancer are cancer types with a high incidence of VTE. A meta-analysis of data from 6324 patients with ovarian cancer determined that the incidence of VTE was 12.2%. The overall incidence of VTE after major surgery for gynaecological cancer is 5–25%. American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO) clinical practice guidelines recommend that hospitalised patients with active malignancies should be given medication to prevent thrombosis in the absence of bleeding or other contraindications. The CLOT (Randomized Comparison of Low-Molecular-Weight Heparin versus Oral Anticoagulant Therapy for the Prevention of Recurrent Venous Thromboembolism in Patients with Cancer) study, published in 2003, showed that treatment with low-molecular weight heparin (LMWH) was more effective than the oral vitamin K antagonist (VKA) warfarin in the case of acute VTE associated with malignancy, without increasing the risk of bleeding. National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines recommend LMWH as a first-line medication for preventing thromboembolic events in patients with cancer, and enoxaparin is a member of the LMWH class of drugs. For patients who require long-term anticoagulation therapy, the pain, bruising and high drug costs associated with subcutaneous injection of LMWH are unavoidable issues.
Factor Xa, which is a component of the coagulation cascade and is produced through both intrinsic and extrinsic pathways, is also a rate-limiting step in the propagation of thrombin production and is, therefore, a particularly attractive anticoagulant target. Rivaroxaban is an oral factor Xa inhibitor that can directly and reversibly bind to factor Xa and competitively inhibit factor Xa. It has high selectivity for factor Xa and does not require cofactors to exert its anticoagulant effect. It is approved for the treatment and secondary prevention of VTE and may be an attractive oral treatment option for patients with cancer. Compared with VKA, rivaroxaban has fewer food/drug interactions, predictable pharmacokinetics, rapid onset, and dose-dependent anticoagulant effects and generally does not require monitoring. Its use avoids the heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT) that may occur with LMWH injection. In contrast, the patient-friendly application of rivaroxaban provides a more convenient option for patients with cancer to prevent and treat VTE, and it is more likely to improve medication compliance.
Peripherally inserted central catheters (PICCs) are commonly used in chemotherapy patients and often lead to upper extremity venous thrombosis (UEVT), which should be prevented. A study has shown that rivaroxaban and LMWH significantly reduce the occurrence of PICC-related UEVT in patients with cancer receiving chemotherapy, and the safety of both thromboprophylaxis is equivalent. A study has comparatively analysed the efficacy of rivaroxaban and enoxaparin in the treatment of cancer-related VTE, and there is no significant difference in the efficacy and safety between the two. A retrospective study showed that the incidence of VTE and bleeding events with rivaroxaban was similar to that of LMWH in thromboprophylaxis in hospitalised patients with cancer. One study that fell short of the expected sample size showed that thrombosis prophylaxis with 10 mg of rivaroxaban per day for 30 days in patients undergoing major gynaecological cancer surgery had a similar incidence of thrombosis and bleeding events as subcutaneous injection of 40 mg of enoxaparin per day; the findings support this hypothesis that direct oral anticoagulant (DOAC) may be an attractive alternative to LMWH to prevent VTE in this high-risk population. 
Given the lack of high-level evidence supporting the safety and effectiveness of rivaroxaban in preventing VTE in patients after surgery for gynaecological malignancies, we decided to conduct this G-alfalfa (G stands for gynaecological tumour; Alfalfa is clover, symbolising luck and health) trial to compare and analyse the safety and effectiveness of rivaroxaban and LMWH in preventing VTE in patients after surgery for gynaecological malignancies. We hope to obtain effective evidence to support the use of rivaroxaban, in order to provide a reference in clinical practice for the prevention of VTE in patients after surgery for gynaecological malignancies.

## Methods

## Study design
This is a single-centre, randomised, controlled, open-label assessor-blind clinical trial that will be conducted at the Fujian Provincial Maternal and Child Health Hospital from June 2024 to December 2026. Researchers include pharmacists, physicians, nurses and statisticians. The allocation ratio between the two intervention groups is 1:1. G-alfalfa aims to compare the efficacy and safety of oral rivaroxaban and subcutaneous injection enoxaparin in preventing VTE in patients after surgery for gynaecological malignancies. The study process includes initial screening, treatment period and follow-up period, with two follow-ups on the 7th and 30th day after surgery, and the follow-up on the 30th day after surgery to confirm the participants’ medication compliance ( ). If the protocol is not modified, the study data and results of trials advanced under this protocol will be published in such a way that the identity of the subject will not be disclosed and cannot be confirmed.
 The Clinical Events Committee (CEC) is composed of clinicians who are not involved in the recruitment and follow-up of subjects and are unaware of the patients’ treatment allocation. The CEC will use the criteria outlined in the protocol to evaluate major bleeding and clinically relevant non-major bleeding (CRNMB) during study treatment and follow-up, which are verified by clinical and laboratory data, thereby reducing misclassification and optimising the research effect of safety outcomes.
Subjects who withdraw from the study will not be replaced, and every effort should be made to follow-up by the end of treatment and to perform a VTE evaluation. All subject files (including withdrawal researcher from participation to withdrawal time) will be retained and used for data analysis. If the subject is lost to follow-up, three attempts to contact the subject for end-of-treatment follow-up should be recorded. The subject can only be registered as lost to follow-up after three failed attempts.

## Study population
Participants will be recruited from the gynaecologic oncology ward of Fujian Maternal and Child Health Hospital who are scheduled to undergo gynaecological tumour surgery during hospitalisation. Participants will continue to receive intervention/follow-up in the community after discharge and receive outpatient or inpatient follow-up on the 30th day after surgery.

## Inclusion criteria
Women who are aged≥18 years and able to provide written informed consent.
All patients who are suspected or diagnosed with gynaecological malignancies (including ovarian cancer, uterine cancer, endometrial cancer, cervical cancer, vulvar cancer, vaginal cancer or other rare gynaecological malignancies confirmed by pathological examination).
Patients who plan to undergo surgery for gynaecological malignant tumours during hospitalisation.
Patients who are able to receive 30-day follow-up.

## Exclusion criteria
The doctor decides that participation in the trial is not in the best interests of the patient.
Patients with thrombotic diseases.
Long-term use of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) (combination with anticoagulants may increase bleeding risk).
Concurrent use of strong inhibitors of cytochrome P450 (CYP3A4) and/or glycoprotein P (P-gp) (azole antifungal medication (such as ketoconazole, itraconazole, voriconazole and posaconazole) or HIV protease inhibitors (such as ritonavir, cobicistat, saquinavir, indinavir and nelfinavir)); concurrent use of strong inducers of P-gp and/or CYP3A4 (such as rifampicin, rifabutin, rifapentine, phenytoin, phenobarbital, carbamazepine, rifamycin, fosphenytoin, mitotane, primidone, dabrafenib, enzalutamide and St. John’s wort) (increases/decreases rivaroxaban exposure and may increase bleeding/blood clot risk).
Total bilirubin exceeds two times the upper limit of normal, and aspartate aminotransferase/alanine aminotransferase/alkaline phosphatase exceeds three times the upper limit of normal (use of anticoagulants increases the risk of bleeding).
Endogenous creatinine clearance≤30 mL/min (expected to increase rivaroxaban exposure and enhance pharmacodynamics effects).
Patients with contraindications to anticoagulation (active bleeding, liver failure, blood disease or risk of contraindications to bleeding and contraindications to rivaroxaban or enoxaparin).
Patients diagnosed with benign gynaecological tumours during surgery (the subjects of this study are patients with gynaecological malignant tumours).
Pregnancy or breastfeeding (this is a contraindication to rivaroxaban as rivaroxaban has potential reproductive toxicity and endogenous bleeding risk).
Existence of one of the following uncontrolled or unstable cardiovascular diseases: stroke, electrocardiogram-confirmed acute ischaemia or myocardial infarction and/or clinically significant arrhythmia (high risk of bleeding).
Uncontrolled severe hypertension (systolic blood pressure>200 mm Hg or diastolic blood pressure>120 mm Hg) (high risk of bleeding).
Use of the artificial heart valve (data to support applications in this area are lacking).

## Objectives
The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the effectiveness of oral rivaroxaban versus subcutaneous injection enoxaparin in preventing VTE in patients after surgery for gynaecological malignancies.
Secondary objectives are as follows: (1) to compare the safety of the two treatments; (2) compare compliance to the two medications; (3) determine the steady-state peak and trough plasma concentrations of rivaroxaban at standard doses to explore the relationship between efficacy/adverse reactions and plasma concentrations; (4) determine the genetic polymorphisms of rivaroxaban metabolic enzymes/transporters ABCB1, CYP and ABCG2 and to investigate the effects of gene sequence polymorphisms on the pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics of rivaroxaban; (5) to construct a population pharmacokinetics (PPK) model of rivaroxaban in postoperative patients with gynaecological malignancies.

## Primary outcome
The primary (efficacy-related) outcome is the first objectively documented VTE (DVT or PE) during the treatment and follow-up period after randomisation. VTE is defined as any symptomatic or incidental DVT of the lower or upper extremities; any non-fatal, symptomatic or incidental PE; and death related to PE. Patients who develop DVT during the follow-up period will undergo colour Doppler ultrasonography of both lower limb veins, and patients who develop PE will undergo CT pulmonary angiography (CTPA) to confirm the primary outcome.

## Secondary outcomes
Secondary (safety-related) outcomes: major bleeding, CRNMB, and minor bleeding during the treatment and follow-up period after randomisation. The International Society on Thrombosis and Haemostasis (ISTH) defines major bleeding from the following three cases: (1) fatal bleeding and/or (2) symptomatic bleeding in a critical area or organ, such as intracranial, intraspinal, intraocular, retroperitoneal, intra-articular or pericardial or intramuscular with compartment syndrome and/or (3) bleeding causing a decrease in haemoglobin level of 20 g/L or more or leading to transfusion of two or more units of whole blood or red cells. CRNMB is defined as overt bleeding not meeting the criteria for major bleeding but associated with medical intervention, unscheduled contact with a physician, temporary cessation of study treatment or discomfort for the subject such as pain or impairment of activities of daily life; minor bleeding is defined as obvious bleeding that does not meet the criteria for major bleeding or CRNMB. 

## Outcomes related to medication compliance
We aimed to investigate the equivalence of the two treatment groups in terms of study medication compliance. Compliance will be measured by the number of days the subject is on medication, and compliance rates between treatment groups will be compared.

## Outcomes related to pharmacokinetics
On the third day after surgery, the steady-state trough plasma concentration of rivaroxaban is measured 30 min before medication; the steady-state peak plasma concentration is measured 3 hours after medication. The effects of physiological indicators, tumours, liver and kidney function, concomitant medication, concomitant diseases and genetic polymorphisms of related metabolic enzymes/transporters on the pharmacokinetics of rivaroxaban are quantitatively investigated.

## Outcomes related to pharmacogenomics
During the study, the peripheral blood of the subjects is collected, DNA sequences containing single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) sites are amplified by polymerase chain reaction (PCR) using MassARRAY technology and the PCR products are amplified by specific single-base extension primers. Finally, the genetic polymorphisms of ABCB1, CYP and ABCG2 genes are determined.

## Sample size calculation and power
With rivaroxaban to prevent thrombosis in postoperative patients with malignancies, the incidence of VTE is assumed to be 2% (based on 3.5% in the VALERIA (Venous thromboembolism prophylAxis after gynecoLogical pElvic cancer surgery with RIvaroxaban vs enoxAparin) trial and 1.0% in Cecilia Becattini’s study). With enoxaparin for thrombus prevention, the incidence of VTE is assumed to be 8% (based on 4.4% in the VALERIA trial and 12.2% in our previous results). The sample size will be calculated using PASS 11, and the relative risk of VTE is reduced by approximately 6%, with a two-sided alpha of 0.05, a power of 80%, a sample size of 1:1 for the two intervention groups and an assumed loss to follow-up rate of 10%. A total of 458 patients are required for each group, with a sample size of 229 patients.

## Treatment of subjects

## Intervention
The subjects start taking the medication 12–24 hours after surgery and continue for 28 days. The oral rivaroxaban group receives 10 mg once a day, and the subcutaneous enoxaparin group receives 4000 AXaIU (Anti-Factor Xa International Units) once a day. For patients after surgery with high risk in VTE evaluation, the intermittent pneumatic compression (IPC) device is used to prevent thrombosis after VTE is excluded by colour Doppler ultrasonography of both lower limb veins. The patients continue medication after discharge through self-management.

## Assessments during study treatment

## Related to efficacy
The subjects will be followed up on the 7th and 30th day after surgery and will be required to undergo colour Doppler ultrasonography of both lower limb veins to confirm whether there is any DVT; subjects with suspected PE symptoms will undergo CTPA examination.

## Related to the medication compliance rate
We enhance participant medication adherence through several initiatives. These include educating patients about medication usage, storage, and record-keeping; standardising the acquisition of study drugs; conducting on-site teaching for self-injections; and distributing instructional videos for self-injection.
On the 30th day post-surgery, we will request the subjects to submit their self-reported medication record cards along with the research medication prescriptions to the researchers. By cross-verifying these documents, we aim to assess the patient’s medication compliance rate.

## Procedures

## Recruitment and consent
Patients who meet the inclusion criteria but do not meet the exclusion criteria are defined as potential participants. Potential participants will be informed in detail of the study procedures, treatment options, risks and benefits. Each researcher involved in the trial has been trained to provide standard oral and written trial information and to facilitate communication between patients and doctors during the recruitment process. Patients and their families are advised to consider or discuss for at least 24 hours before deciding whether to participate in this trial. Written informed consent is obtained from all eligible participants and kept separately by them and the researchers.

## Randomisation, allocation concealment, study medication and patient education
Fei He, a statistician at the School of Public Health of Fujian Medical University, will use SAS software to generate randomised sequences, adopting a block randomisation scheme, with block sizes accessible only to statisticians who performed randomisation and generated assigned sequences.
Opaque-sealed envelopes will be distributed to the researchers to conceal the allocation order. Once the patient is enrolled in the study, the two researchers open the patient’s corresponding randomisation envelope at the same time and randomly assign the patient 1:1 to the rivaroxaban group or the enoxaparin group. The two researchers will sign the envelopes and write the opening time and the next patient study number.
The study medications are all prescribed from the pharmacy of Fujian Maternal and Child Health Hospital: rivaroxaban tablets (specification: 10 mg/tablet) and enoxaparin injection (specification: 0.4 mL: 4000 AXaIU).
All participants will receive standard postoperative nursing treatment, as well as education on medication, storage, and record-keeping. Patients in the enoxaparin group will learn and master the correct method of self-injection.

## Withdrawal of subjects
Participants are allowed to withdraw from the study at any time and for any reason without affecting their medical treatment and rights. In addition, researchers can withdraw subjects from the study for urgent medical reasons.

## Adverse events
Any adverse medical events that are judged to be related to the trial procedures are defined as adverse events (AEs), regardless of whether they are related to the study intervention. Throughout the trial, all potential AEs will be monitored under the supervision of the institutional ethics committee (IEC) from the time randomisation was performed on the participants to the 30-day follow-up after surgery. Data from all study participants will be included in the safety analysis.
A serious adverse event (SAE) is any untoward medical occurrence that results in death, is life-threatening, requires hospitalisation or prolongs existing hospitalisation (exceptions are noted below*), causes persistent or severe disability/incapacity or is a congenital anomaly/birth defect.
Note*: The following hospitalisations are not considered SAEs: (1) receiving anticancer treatment in the absence of any other SAEs. (2) Visits to the emergency room or other hospital departments for less than 24 hours that do not result in hospitalisation (unless considered a ‘PE or major bleeding’ or life-threatening event). (3) Elective surgery planned before signing the informed consent. (4) Hospitalisation according to the arranged medical/surgical plan. (5) Health assessments that require admission for baseline/health status trend analysis, such as colonoscopy. (6) Medical/surgical admissions planned before the study, such as removal of the catheter after cytoreductive surgery, radiotherapy and chemotherapy. (7) Hospitalisation due to another life situation that is not related to health status and does not require medical/surgical intervention, such as lack of housing, caregiver rest and family situation.
If an SAE occurs, researchers will report it to the principal investigator (PI) and the IEC within 24 hours after the event is confirmed, actively carry out treatment or medical treatment and fill out and sign the SAE report form.
During the study, researchers will submit annual safety reports to the IEC.

## Statistical analysis
SPSS V.27.0 software (IBM) will be used for data analysis based on the study endpoints. The analysis is based on the modified intention-to-treat principle, and all subjects who receive correctly assigned study medication at least once are included in the analysis. In the two intervention groups, continuous data are expressed as mean±SD, and categorical data are expressed as percentages. All tests are two-sided, and a p value of <0.05 is considered statistically significant. Paired t-tests will be used to compare continuous variables, and chi-square tests are used to compare categorical variables. The hazard ratio of VTE is estimated using the Cox proportional hazards model. The time to the first event is estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method. We will use a multivariate logistic regression model to calculate the odds ratio of VTE with rivaroxaban compared with enoxaparin, and regression analysis will be performed for the outcomes of DVT, PE and major bleeding.
The concentration of rivaroxaban in plasma is determined using high-performance liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (HPLC-MS/MS). The MassARRAY SNP genotyping technology is used to determine the genetic polymorphisms of the enzymes/transporters involved in the metabolism of rivaroxaban. TYPER4.0 software is used to obtain the genotyping map, χ2 test is used to confirm the genotype distribution of the subject population and gene loci conforming to the Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium are included in PPK analysis. The non-linear mixed-effect model (NONMEM) method is used to quantitatively assess the impact of physiological indicators, tumours, liver and kidney functions, comorbidities, concomitant medications and genetic factors on the pharmacokinetics of rivaroxaban. The PPK model for rivaroxaban is established using NONMEM (V.7.4, ICON Development Solutions, USA). The structural model describes the pharmacokinetic characteristics of rivaroxaban using a one-compartment model for oral administration. The random-effects model uses an exponential model to describe the inter-individual variability in pharmacokinetic parameters and a proportional additive model to describe the intra-individual variability. A covariate model is constructed to identify covariates affecting the structural model parameters. Continuous covariates are represented by exponential models, while categorical variables are modelled using piece-wise functions. The covariates with significant influence factors are screened by the fixed-effect model, and the final model is established. The goodness of fit of the constructed model is evaluated through diagnostic plots, and the internal stability and reliability of the model are assessed using the non-parametric bootstrap method.

## Data handling and study monitoring
The records in electronic Case Report Form (eCRF) will be completed for each patient in the study, and the data anonymised by the unique study code for each patient. The key file of each patient’s name linked to the study code will be stored at the PI’s Institute location and encrypted for the period specified by the legislation.
A Data Safety Monitoring Committee will be established for this study to monitor the safety and efficacy of the trial and report to the Medical Research Registration and Filing Information System (www.medicalresearch.org.cn) and the institution ethics committee as required. Investigators and clinical research coordinators will discuss the treatment outcomes of each subject at regularly scheduled disease-oriented working group meetings for internal review. Data on the number of subjects, clinical events and treatment responses will be discussed and recorded in the meeting minutes. All SAEs, unexpected problems and reportable AEs will be reviewed continuously.
Regular monitoring visits will be conducted during the trial by independent monitors who are not involved in subject recruitment and follow-up to ensure compliance with the study protocol and good clinical practice. The monitors will be able to review source documents to confirm that the data collected in the eCRF are accurate.

## Discussion
Currently, there is only one RCT study literature available on the effectiveness and safety of rivaroxaban versus LMWH in preventing VTE after surgery in patients with gynaecological malignancies, the VALERIA trial, which did not reach the expected sample size. The present study is based in the gynaecological oncology ward of Fujian Maternal and Child Health Hospital, which has an average annual surgical volume of 700. After completion of the trial, it is expected to provide effective evidence for the clinical use of rivaroxaban for the prevention of anticoagulation after surgery in patients with gynaecological malignancies.
The diagnosis of gynaecological malignancies is based on the final pathological diagnosis. Patients diagnosed with benign tumours during surgery are excluded, and patients with suspected gynaecological malignancies whose pathological diagnosis is not confirmed within 24 hours after surgery are included. Discontinuation of anticoagulants, including rivaroxaban, due to active bleeding, elective surgery or invasive surgery increases the risk of thrombosis in patients. If rivaroxaban anticoagulant prophylaxis must be suspended for any reason, prophylactic medication should be restarted as soon as possible to avoid treatment errors. In this trial protocol, good medication compliance was defined as no more than 2 days without medication; if the treatment needs to be suspended, the medication can be delayed for up to 2 days; if subjects miss more than 2 days of medication, we will re-contact them by phone or clinically for further follow-up on the reason for their non-compliance, which is often after the 30-day follow-up period. Participants who stop taking the medication for more than 2 days were withdrawn from the study.
The choice to start therapy 12–24 hours postsurgery in the current study is based on the following considerations: (1) The study refers to the NCCN guidelines, which recommend the first injection of 40 mg of enoxaparin 6–12 hours after surgery for surgical oncology inpatients and the administration of 2.5 mg bid of apixaban on the first day postsurgery for gynecologic cancer inpatients. This study considers the consistency of medication timing between the rivaroxaban and enoxaparin intervention groups, hence setting the administration time to 12–24 hours postsurgery. (2) For patients with a high risk of developing VTE following gynaecologic cancer surgery, the use of intermittent pneumatic compression devices to prevent thrombosis is indicated after ruling out VTE with colour Doppler ultrasonography of both lower limb veins; anticoagulant drugs are only administered after ensuring that adequate haemostasis has been achieved postsurgery. Thus, the choice of the 12–24-hour window for anticoagulation is also a result of a balanced consideration between thrombosis prevention and haemostasis. (3) An RCT comparing rivaroxaban and nadroparin for thrombosis prevention following thoracic surgery for lung cancer started anticoagulation treatment 12–24 hours postsurgery. 
One study has shown that rivaroxaban has significant clinical differences between individuals. Excessive anticoagulation may lead to adverse reactions of bleeding, and insufficient anticoagulation may not achieve the anticoagulation effect. Therefore, it is necessary to investigate the factors affecting the pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics of rivaroxaban. The most recommended pharmacodynamic indicator for evaluating the efficacy and safety of rivaroxaban in current guidelines is anti-Xa activity. HPLC-MS/MS is the gold standard for detecting rivaroxaban. According to Tingting Wu’s research, the plasma concentration of rivaroxaban was strongly correlated with the determination of chromogenic anti-Xa assay. Compared with HPLC-MS/MS, chromogenic anti-Xa assay is rapid, simple and inexpensive. Chromogenic anti-Xa assay can be used for rapid detection of rivaroxaban and clinically, with the established PPK model in the present protocol, it is able to achieve individualised dosing of rivaroxaban, further improving the safety and effectiveness of rivaroxaban anticoagulation.
The limitations of being a single-centre, open-label study increase the risk of trial bias; the open-label approach is adopted due to the different dosing methods in the two intervention groups, which makes it impossible to blind the investigators and subjects. Our study still expects a plan to collect patient-reported outcomes, which we will consider improving in future studies.

## Ethics and dissemination
This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Fujian Maternal and Child Health Hospital on 2 April 2024, with approval number 2023KY174-03. This study was registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (trial registration number ChiCTR2300078535; name of trial registry:Efficacy and Safety of Rivaroxaban in Preventing Venous Thromboembolism in Postoperative Patients with Gynecologic Malignancies), and was filed in the Medical Research Registration and Filing Information System (www.medicalresearch.org.cn).
Eligible clinical patients can only enter this study after signing the informed consent form approved by the IEC. If the subject is unable to sign, her legal guardian shall sign the informed consent form on her behalf. The researcher should explain the situation of this study to the subjects in this group or their guardians. The study will be conducted in accordance with the protocol, the Measures for the Administration of Investigator-Initiated Clinical Research in Chinese Medical and Health Institutions (Trial), and the Declaration of Helsinki and with the informed consent of the patients. Except for emergencies requiring specific treatment measures, the researchers guarantee that they will comply with the protocol in all aspects, especially in obtaining informed consent and reporting and monitoring SAEs.
Any formal publication or release of the data from this study will protect the privacy of clinical patients. Laboratory tests and other examination results will be published only for scientific purposes, and the identity of the subjects will not be disclosed.
Researchers not listed as authors will be listed as non-author contributors.
