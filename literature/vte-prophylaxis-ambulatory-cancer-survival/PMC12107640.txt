Al-SallamiHesham 1College of Pharmacy, QU Health, Qatar University, Doha, Qatar2University of Otago, Dunedin, New ZealandCorresponding author email: h.al-sallami@qu.edu.qaBackground: Insulin dose requirements vary between patients with type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1DM). Bolus doses depend on the patient's insulin to carbohydrate ratio (ICR), for meal-time dosing, and on the correction factor (CF), for elevated blood glucose correction. In clinical practice, it usually takes several days of heuristic insulin dose adjustments, guided by blood glucose concentration measurements and carbohydrate counting before a patient's ICR and CF values can be accurately estimated.
Aims: (1) To review commonly used ICR and CF estimation models, and (2) to develop and evaluate new models for ICR and CF using retrospective patient data.
Methods: 1) A scoping literature review of online databases and search engines was conducted to identify commonly used ICR and CF formulae. 2) Linear regression analyses of retrospectively collected data was undertaken using clinical characteristic, laboratory measurements, and insulin dosing data from 70 children (≤ 18 years old) admitted to Dunedin hospitals (New Zealand) due to new onset T1DM. Regression models for ICR and CF values were developed and evaluated for bias and precision using mean error and root mean squared error, respectively.
Results: 1) According to the literature, covariates such as age and weight are commonly used to estimate ICR and CF. 2) Based on the final ICR and CF prediction models, for patients < 6 years old, total daily dose of insulin administered on first day (TDD_first) was the only significant predictor for ICR and CF (p < 0.05). For patients ≥ 6 years old, patient's total daily dose of insulin administered on first day, and age were found to be significant predictors for both ICR and CF (p < 0.05).
Conclusion: Accounting for a patient's age and insulin dose on first day allows for better estimation of true (i.e. asymptotic) ICR and CF values. This can potentially provide better glycaemic control sooner during hospital stay.

7AbstractEfficacy and safety of adding ribavirin to direct-acting antivirals (DAAs) in re-treating non-genotype1 hepatitis C- a systematic review and meta-analysis
HamranShahd JasimKawthar Al-TheyabMajed A. ElahtamMohamed Al-HailMooza Al-FahaidiWadha KhamisYaman DweidriYara ElzoukiAbdel-Naser ChiveseTawanda 1College of Medicine, QU Health, Qatar University, Doha, Qatar2College of Dental Medicine, QU Health, Qatar University, Doha, Qatar3Hamad Medical Corporation, Doha, QatarCorresponding author email: sh2004556@qu.edu.qaBackground: Despite the advent of highly efficacious direct-acting antivirals (DAAs), a small percentage of individuals require re-treatment for hepatitis C.
Aim: This study aims to compare the efficacy and safety of adding ribavirin to DAAs compared to DAAs alone in treating non-genotype 1 HCV in individuals who have been previously treated.
Methods: A systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) was performed. Cochrane, PubMed, SCOPUS, and preprint databases were searched for trials that compared DAAs with ribavirin to DAAs alone in previously treated individuals with non-genotype 1 HCV infection. The primary outcome of interest was sustained virological response (SVR) at 12 weeks after treatment completion (SVR12). The quality of studies was evaluated using the Methodologic Al STandard for Epidemiological Research (MASTER) scale, heterogeneity using I2, and publication bias using Doi and funnel plots. Bias adjusted inverse heterogeneity models were used to compute pooled odds ratios and their 95% confidence intervals. The Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development, and Evaluations (GRADE) framework was used to evaluate the quality of evidence for the primary outcome.
Results: Ten RCTs with a total of 1000 participants were included. Overall, the addition of ribavirin to DAAs, compared to DAAs alone, did not show benefit in achieving SVR12 (OR 1.01, 95% CI 0.31–3.29, I2 = 69.8%), with moderate certainty GRADE evidence, and the additional ribavirin showed increased odds of adverse events (OR 2.37, 95%CI 1.88–2.98, I2 = 28.9%).
Conclusion: The moderate certainty evidence suggests that adding ribavirin to DAAs may not confer benefit in achieving SVR in previously treated individuals with non-genotype 1 HCV.

AtwehStephanie AbushanabDina Al MasalmehOla AlkhatibHala 1College of Pharmacy, QU Health, Qatar University, Doha, Qatar2Hamad Medical Corporation, Doha, QatarCorresponding author email: s.atweh@qu.edu.qaBackground: Phosphoinositide 3-kinase inhibitors (PI3Kis) represent a targeted therapeutic class with growing use in oncology due to their role in regulating tumor cell growth, survival, and metabolism. Five PI3K inhibitors – Idelalisib, Copanlisib, Duvelisib, Alpelisib, and Umbralisib – have received FDA approval for use in various cancers. Despite their approval, there remains variability in their clinical efficacy and safety profiles across different cancer types.
Aim: This systematic review aims to synthesize the existing evidence about the efficacy and safety of the five Phosphoinositide 3-kinases inhibitors (PI3Kis) (Idelalisib, Copanlisib, Duvelisib, Alpelisib, Umbralisib) that have received Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approval for treatment in cancer patients.
Methods: Several databases, such as PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane library, were searched using targeted keywords. Only meta-analyses (MAs) that evaluated the PI3Kis as monotherapy or adjunctive therapy in adult patients with any type of cancer were included. Data extraction focused on efficacy and safety outcomes reported in eligible studies. The Risk of Bias In Systematic Reviews (ROBIS) tool and the Assessment of Multiple Systematic Reviews-2 (AMSTAR-2) were used to evaluate the risk of bias and quality of methods of the included MAs.
Results: MAs involving 12,535 patients were included. Two MAs focused on Alpelisib in breast cancer BC patients, demonstrating its superiority in improving progression free survival (PFS) among patients with PI3KCA-mutated BC. Two MAs evaluated Idelalisib combined with standard immunotherapy in relapsed/refractory chronic lymphocytic leukemia (R/R CLL), showing promising efficacy but it demonstrated some severe hematological adverse events. Two MAs were conducted in relapsed/refractory B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma (R/R B-NHL) patients; one of them studied Copanlisib monotherapy or in combination with other agents, while the other studied Duvelisib as monotherapy. Lastly, three MAs addressed only safety outcomes.
Conclusion: Alpelisib showed favorable efficacy outcomes in breast cancer treatment regardless of the patient's mutation status while maintaining a tolerable adverse event profile. It was challenging to find a meaningful difference between the remaining PI3Kis that are included in this review. It has been clarified, that Idelalisib and Duvelisib have a role in the treatment of R/R CLL and R/R NHL whether alone or in combination with immunotherapy.
Keywords: Pi3k inhibitors, PIK3CA mutation, Breast cancer, Chronic lymphocytic leukemia, Lymphoma
277AbstractCharacteristics of psychotropic medications adverse drug reactions: A retrospective descriptive analysis
EltorkiYassin AbdallaOraib ZolezziMonica ElaminWaad AlKudsiZeana AbushamehNoor MustafaMariam MahramIslam RiazSadaf Al-KhuzaeiNoriya 1Hamad Medical Corporation, Doha, Qatar2College of Pharmacy, QU Health, Qatar University, Doha, QatarBackground: Spontaneous reporting systems for Adverse Drug Reactions (ADRs) play a key role in detecting new, rare, and severe ADRs in clinical practice. However, there is limited research on ADRs associated with psychotropic medications in Qatar.
Aim: To investigate psychotropic-related ADR characteristics and risk factors.
Methods: A retrospective chart review analyzed ADR reports linked to psychotropic medications submitted to Hamad Medical Corporation's (HMC) Spontaneous Reporting System database from 2018 to 2022. Data on clinical descriptions, suspected agents, severity, seriousness, preventability, and causality were assessed. Associations between patient demographics, comorbidities, and ADR types were also explored.
Results: In 908 ADR reports, the majority involved male patients (543, 59.83%) and were reported in 2020 (256, 28.2%). As per the reporters’ assessment, approximately two-thirds of the ADRs (67.7%) were moderate in severity, and 64.5% of patients recovered or were in the process of recovery at the time of reporting. Notably, 65.6% of the ADRs were non-preventable. Regarding causality, 56.8% were probable, while 3.9% were definite. Extrapyramidal symptoms were the most frequently reported ADRs (46.5%), followed by gastrointestinal (13.3%), hyperprolactinemia (13.2%), and cardiac (3.7%) side effects. The most common offending agents were haloperidol (17.2%), olanzapine (13%), risperidone (12.6%), and clozapine (9.6%). Pharmacists reported 87% (790) of reports.
Multiple associations were discovered between patient characteristics and certain ADR types. Gender and weight were associated with the highest number of ADR types.
Conclusion: Although the recovery rate from ADRs associated with psychotropic medications is relatively high, the notable prevalence of non-preventable ADRs highlights the necessity of adopting a comprehensive approach to enhance the safety and efficacy of these medications. This approach should encompass advancements in regulatory policies, the adoption of holistic treatment strategies, and the implementation of personalized medicine.
Keywords: Adverse drug reactions, Psychotropic medications, Mental health
