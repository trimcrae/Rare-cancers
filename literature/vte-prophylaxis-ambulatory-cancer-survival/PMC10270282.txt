## Background:
COVID-19 (coronavirus disease 2019) is associated with heightened risks of venous and arterial thrombosis and hospitalization due to respiratory failure. To assess whether prophylactic anticoagulation can safely reduce the frequency of venous and arterial thrombosis, hospitalization, and death in nonhospitalized patients with symptomatic COVID-19 and at least one thrombosis risk factor, we conducted the PREVENT-HD double-blind, placebo-controlled randomized trial (A Study of Rivaroxaban to Reduce the Risk of Major Venous and Arterial Thrombotic Events, Hospitalization and Death in Medically Ill Outpatients With Acute, Symptomatic COVID-19] Infection).

## Methods:
PREVENT-HD was conducted between August 2020 and April 2022 at 14 US integrated health care delivery networks. A virtual trial design used remote informed consent and clinical monitoring and facilitated data collection through electronic health record integration with a cloud-based research platform. Nonhospitalized patients with symptomatic COVID-19 and at least one thrombosis risk factor were enrolled and randomly assigned to either 10 mg of oral rivaroxaban or placebo daily for 35 days. The primary efficacy outcome was time to first occurrence of a composite of symptomatic venous thromboembolism, myocardial infarction, ischemic stroke, acute limb ischemia, non–central nervous system systemic arterial embolism, hospitalization, or death through day 35. The principal safety end point was International Society on Thrombosis and Hemostasis critical-site or fatal bleeding. The last study visit was on day 49.

## Results:
The study was terminated prematurely because of enrollment challenges and a lower-than-expected blinded pooled event rate. A total of 1284 patients underwent randomization with complete accrual of primary events through May 2022. No patients were lost to follow-up. The primary efficacy outcome occurred in 22 of 641 in the rivaroxaban group and 19 of 643 in the placebo group (3.4% versus 3.0%; hazard ratio, 1.16 [95% CI, 0.63–2.15]; P=0.63). No patient in either group experienced critical-site or fatal bleeding. One patient receiving rivaroxaban had a major bleed.

## Conclusions:
The study was terminated prematurely after enrollment of 32% of planned accrual because of recruitment challenges and lower-than-expected event rate. Rivaroxaban prescribed for 35 days in nonhospitalized patients with symptomatic COVID-19 at risk for thrombosis did not appear to reduce a composite end point of venous and arterial thrombotic events, hospitalization, and death.

## Clinical Perspective

## What Is New?
The PREVENT-HD randomized controlled trial (A Study of Rivaroxaban to Reduce the Risk of Major Venous and Arterial Thrombotic Events, Hospitalization and Death in Medically Ill Outpatients With Acute, Symptomatic Coronavirus Disease 2019 [COVID-19] Infection) assessed the efficacy and safety of 10 mg of rivaroxaban daily for the prevention of venous and arterial thrombosis, hospitalization, and death in 1284 high-risk outpatients with symptomatic COVID-19.
A virtual trial design used patient engagement and remote informed consent and clinical monitoring and facilitated data collection through electronic health record integration with a cloud-based research platform enabling robust data ascertainment; no patients were lost to follow-up.
With the caveats that enrollment was terminated early and the trial was underpowered to a provide definitive conclusion, prophylactic rivaroxaban was not found to reduce a composite end point of symptomatic venous thromboembolism, myocardial infarction, stroke, acute limb ischemia, systemic arterial embolism, hospitalization, or death.

## What Are the Clinical Implications?
Like other trials of thromboprophylaxis in outpatients with symptomatic COVID-19, PREVENT-HD observed a lower-than-expected event rate, supporting a low overall risk of symptomatic venous thromboembolism, myocardial infarction, stroke, acute limb ischemia, systemic arterial embolism, hospitalization, and death.
In aggregate, data from PREVENT-HD and other previous clinical trials do not seem to support routine antithrombotic prophylaxis in nonhospitalized patients with symptomatic COVID-19.

Although the incidence of venous and arterial thrombosis in hospitalized patients with COVID-19 exceeds that of those not requiring hospitalization (16% versus 1.4%), – a growing majority of patients infected with SARS-CoV-2 (severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus-2) do not require hospital admission. Increased viral infectivity, shifting demographics of infected patients toward younger ages, widespread vaccine availability, and increasing therapies for mitigating disease severity favor a shift in the burden of COVID-19 to the outpatient setting. Accordingly, the large denominator of patients with COVID-19 not requiring initial hospitalization comprises a substantial population potentially vulnerable to thrombotic complications, including venous thromboembolism (VTE), myocardial infarction, and ischemic stroke.
In addition to potentially life-threatening macrovascular complications, microvascular thrombosis has been hypothesized to contribute to clinical deterioration, hospitalization, and death in patients with COVID-19, including outpatients. – In a cohort of 4297 patients admitted with COVID-19, prophylactic anticoagulation within 24 hours of hospitalization was associated with a 4.4% absolute risk reduction in 30-day mortality. To date, randomized controlled trials of prehospitalization prophylactic anticoagulation have terminated early without showing benefit for prevention of thrombosis or clinical deterioration. – 
The PREVENT-HD trial (A Study of Rivaroxaban to Reduce the Risk of Major Venous and Arterial Thrombotic Events, Hospitalization and Death in Medically Ill Outpatients With Acute, Symptomatic Coronavirus Disease 2019 [COVID-19] Infection) was designed to assess the efficacy and safety of prophylactic-dose (10 mg) rivaroxaban daily for the prevention of venous and arterial thrombosis, hospitalization, and death in high-risk outpatients with symptomatic COVID-19.

## Study Design and Oversight
PREVENT-HD was a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial assessing the efficacy and safety of prophylactic rivaroxaban for prevention of venous and arterial thrombosis, hospitalization, and death in outpatients with symptomatic COVID-19 (NCT04508023). Because of the challenges of the pandemic, PREVENT-HD was designed as a pragmatic trial with a virtual consent process and virtual monitoring of study subjects. The study was performed in accordance with all local laws and regulations and the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and International Council on Harmonization Good Clinical Practice Guideline. The study protocol (Supplemental Material) and informed consent form were approved by central (Advarra, Inc, Columbia, MD) and local institutional review boards for all sites. An independent data monitoring committee met at prespecified intervals to monitor the trial (Supplemental Material).
Colorado Prevention Center Clinical Research (Aurora, CO), a nonprofit academic research organization affiliated with the University of Colorado Anschutz Medical Campus, provided trial oversight. ICON Plc (Dublin, Ireland) coordinated trial functions and supported the virtual informed consent and data management procedures. PREVENT-HD used a central electronic data capture tool (Medidata Rave, Medidata Solutions, New York, NY).

## Study Population
Patients were required to be at least 18 years of age and to have polymerase chain reaction– or antigen-confirmed SARS-CoV-2 infection, symptomatic COVID-19, an initial treatment plan not including hospitalization, and at least one thrombosis risk factor: increased D-dimer; any documented thrombophilia; previous VTE; history of cancer, coronary artery disease, peripheral artery disease, cerebrovascular disease, or ischemic stroke; or a predictor of adverse COVID-19 outcomes (Supplemental Material). Key exclusion criteria included positive SARS-CoV-2 antibody test at least 2 weeks after the acute infection, bleeding risk factors, and therapy with prohibited medications.
Potentially eligible patients were identified through electronic health record–specific decision support tools. After initial record review, study staff confirmed eligibility and assessed interest by telephone or direct messaging. Informed consent was obtained from all participants through smartphone, tablet, computer, or, for those unable to do so electronically, paper consent.

## Randomization and Study Intervention
Patients meeting all inclusion and no exclusion criteria were enrolled from the date of positive test to 14 days later and randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive 10 mg of oral rivaroxaban or matching placebo daily for 35 days by a central computer program that concealed treatment assignment. Treatment allocation was balanced within study sites by block randomization and was stratified by time interval from positive test to randomization: early (days 1–5) and later (days 6–14). The rationale for the rivaroxaban dose and duration was based on the US Food and Drug Administration–approved regimen for prophylaxis of VTE in acutely ill medical patients at risk for thromboembolic complications but not at high risk of bleeding and 2 previously published randomized controlled trials validating this strategy. , 
A centralized pharmacy (Marken, Durham, NC) shipped study medication directly to the patients’ homes by next-day delivery with confirmation of receipt by telephone on day 3. The first dose of study medication was specified to be taken within 2 days of randomization, if possible.

## Follow-Up
To reduce exposure of health care providers to SARS-CoV-2, all trial activities were conducted remotely. Patients underwent virtual study visits on days 1, 3, 14, 35, and 49. Medication adherence was defined as the number of doses taken divided by the number of doses prescribed and was assessed during virtual study visits. To aid in follow-up, PREVENT-HD leveraged the REDCap Cloud platform (REDCap Cloud, Encinitas, CA) to integrate with the local electronic health record at each site.

## Outcome Measures
The primary efficacy end point was the composite of symptomatic VTE, myocardial infarction, ischemic stroke, acute limb ischemia, non–central nervous system (CNS) systemic arterial embolism, all-cause hospitalization, and all-cause mortality up to day 35. The principal safety end point was International Society on Thrombosis and Haemostasis fatal and critical-site bleeding. Secondary and exploratory end points included emergency department visits and major and clinically relevant nonmajor bleeding (Supplemental Material). A post hoc exploratory composite outcome included symptomatic VTE and myocardial infarction, ischemic stroke, acute limb ischemia, and non-CNS systemic arterial embolism.
All major efficacy and bleeding end points were adjudicated with the use of standardized definitions (Supplemental Material) by blinded, centrally trained physician specialists at each individual study site through a decentralized adjudication process with central quality control. 

## Statistical Analysis
The primary efficacy analysis was based on time from randomization to first occurrence of any component of the primary end point up to and including day 35 in the intention-to-treat population, which included all randomized patients (Supplemental Material). Sensitivity analyses were conducted in the safety population, defined as patients taking a least one dose of study medication. Safety analyses were conducted in the safety population on treatment plus 2 days. The primary statistical hypothesis was tested with a stratified log-rank test by the time from a positive COVID-19 test to randomization with treatment as the only variable and was tested at a 2-sided significance level of 5%. Hazard ratios (HRs) and 2-sided 95% CIs were provided and based on the stratified Cox proportional hazards model. Proportional hazards assumptions were evaluated by fitting an interaction term of treatment by log-transformed time in the primary Cox model. Assuming success on the primary end point, secondary end points were to be analyzed hierarchically on the basis of similar time-to-event procedures, except for the end point of prevalence of all-cause mortality or all-cause hospitalization at day 35, which was based on the stratified Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel test to estimate a weighted relative risk and 95% CI. All subgroup analyses were conducted with the unstratified Cox proportional hazard model. Absolute risk differences and corresponding exact 95% CIs were provided for select exploratory end points. No imputation was conducted for missing outcome data due to the short duration of the study and expected high rate of study completion.
Designed as an event-driven trial, PREVENT-HD required 333 patients with a confirmed first primary end point to detect an anticipated HR of 0.70 between the 2 study arms, with 90% power at a 2-sided significance level of 5%. With an estimated primary outcome event rate in the placebo group based on data suggesting a 10% hospitalization rate at the time of study design, the trial required ≈4000 patients. The estimate for primary outcome event rate was derived from unpublished data from the Northwell Health network (A.C.S., personal communication, May 7, 2020) available at the time of study design, suggesting a 10% hospitalization rate in unselected patients and Centers for Disease Control and Prevention data, suggesting an 8% hospitalization rate nationally in patients ≥18 years of age. It was anticipated that most but not all thromboembolic events and deaths might occur after hospitalization.
Analyses were conducted with SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Inc, Cary, NC).

## Results

## Study Population
From August 2020 to April 2022, 11 200 patients were prescreened and contacted to discuss the study. This interval included periods of the pandemic dominated by the original SARS-CoV-2 strain and the alpha, delta, and omicron variants. Of these, 1298 responded, provided informed consent, and were screened at 14 US integrated health care delivery networks (Supplemental Material; Figure ). Of these, 1284 patients met eligibility criteria, underwent randomization, and were included in the intention-to-treat analysis. Follow-up at day 49 after randomization was obtained in 100% of patients. On April 30, 2022, the executive committee, using feedback from the independent data monitoring committee, terminated the study prematurely because of enrollment challenges and a lower-than-expected blinded pooled event rate. Although the independent data monitoring committee had access to unblinded safety data, the decision to terminate the study was based on a blinded pooled event rate. The executive committee and independent data monitoring committee also considered the impact of widespread use of highly effective vaccines and the approval of monoclonal antibodies and antiviral medications on the already lower-than-anticipated event rate for the primary efficacy outcome.
 Of 1284 patients, 1197 (93.2%) received at least one dose of study medication and were included in the safety population. In the intention-to-treat analysis set, 1281 patients (99.8%) completed the trial, and 3 (0.2%) prematurely discontinued the study by withdrawing consent. In the safety analysis set, 1081 patients (90.3%) completed treatment, and 116 (9.7%) stopped study medication due to an adverse event (1.9%), bleeding (0.6%), death (0.2%), efficacy outcome (0.8%), initiation of a prohibited medication (0.2%), physician decision (0.6%), patient refusal (2.2%), or other individually reported reasons (3.3%). The rivaroxaban group had a numerically lower frequency of premature discontinuation of study medication (8.3%) than the placebo group (11%).
Among the 1284 patients who underwent randomization, the median age was 60 years, 61% were female, and 27.6% were non-White (Table ). Median time from diagnosis of COVID-19 to randomization was 5 days (interquartile range, 4–8 days). Median time from randomization to first dose of study medication was 2 days (interquartile range, 1–3 days). The time between randomization and first dose of study medication was ≤3 days in 75.6% of study patients. Demographic and baseline clinical characteristics and individual and total number of thrombosis risk factors were generally well balanced between the study groups (Table ). The frequency of a single risk factor, 2 risk factors, and ≥3 risk factors was 64.3%, 27.9%, and 7.7%, respectively. Patients were evenly distributed between the early (days 1–5) and later (days 6–14) cohorts (643 versus 641, respectively). Mean medication adherence was 98.1% and 97.7% in the rivaroxaban and placebo groups, respectively.
 

## Efficacy Outcomes
In the intention-to-treat population, the primary efficacy outcome, evaluated at day 35, occurred in 22 of 641 patients (3.4%) in the rivaroxaban group and 19 of 643 patients (3.0%) in the placebo group (HR, 1.16 [95% CI, 0.63–2.15]; P=0.63; Table ; Figure ). The assumption of proportional hazards was met in the primary Cox model (P for log-transformed time=0.48). A secondary composite end point of symptomatic VTE, myocardial infarction, ischemic stroke, acute limb ischemia, non-CNS systemic arterial embolism, and all-cause mortality occurred in 0.3% in the rivaroxaban group and 1.1% in the placebo group (HR, 0.29 [95% CI, 0.06–1.37]; P=0.10). All-cause mortality (2 of 641 [0.3%] versus 2 of 643 [0.3%]; HR, 1.00 [95% CI, 0.14–7.10]; P=1.0) and all-cause hospitalization (21 of 641 [3.3%] versus 17 of 643 [2.6%]; HR, 1.24 [95% CI, 0.66–2.36]; P=0.5) were not significantly different between groups. Of patients never receiving study medication, the primary efficacy end point, mostly due to all-cause hospitalization, was imbalanced, with a greater proportion observed in the rivaroxaban group than the placebo group (10 of 42 [23.8%] versus 3 of 45 [6.7%]).
 In the safety population, which excluded patients who never received study medication, the primary efficacy outcome, evaluated in a prespecified sensitivity analysis at day 35, occurred in 2.0% in the rivaroxaban group and 2.7% in the placebo group (HR, 0.75 [95% CI, 0.35–1.58]; P=0.44; Figure ). All-cause mortality (1 patient assigned to rivaroxaban and 1 assigned to placebo) and all-cause hospitalization events (1.8% in the rivaroxaban group versus 2.5% in the placebo group) were not significantly different.
With respect to the primary efficacy outcome, there were no significant interactions between subgroups and trial regimen (Figure S1). Specifically, there was no significant interaction (Pinteraction=0.51) between randomization strata days 1 to 5 (HR, 1.37 [95% CI, 0.63–2.98]) and days 6 to 14 (HR, 0.89 [95% CI, 0.32–2.44]) for the primary outcome of a composite of symptomatic VTE, myocardial infarction, ischemic stroke, acute limb ischemia, non-CNS systemic arterial embolism, all-cause hospitalization, and all-cause mortality up to day 35.

## Safety Outcomes
No critical-site or fatal bleeding events were observed in either study group (Table ). One major bleed requiring hospitalization occurred in a patient receiving rivaroxaban who had a hemoglobin decrease due to a gastric polyp. Another occurred in a patient assigned to the rivaroxaban group but who never took study medication. No major bleeds were observed in the placebo group. Clinically relevant nonmajor bleeding was more frequent with rivaroxaban than placebo (1.5% versus 0.2%; HR, 8.97 [95% CI, 1.14–70.82]; P=0.01).
 At least one treatment-related adverse event was observed in 53 patients (8.8%) in the rivaroxaban group and in 47 patients (7.9%) in the placebo group. A serious adverse event occurred in one patient (0.2%) taking rivaroxaban, related to drug hypersensitivity (rash). Adverse events resulting in permanent study medication discontinuation were balanced between the rivaroxaban and placebo groups (1.8% versus 2.0%).

## Exploratory Outcome
In the intention-to-treat population, a post hoc exploratory composite end point of symptomatic VTE, myocardial infarction, ischemic stroke, acute limb ischemia, and non-CNS systemic arterial embolism was infrequent. The post hoc exploratory composite end point occurred in none of the patients in the rivaroxaban group and 5 in the placebo group (absolute risk difference, −0.8% [95% CI, −1.8% to −0.1%; Figure S2).

## DISCUSSION
PREVENT-HD demonstrated that it is feasible to conduct a randomized controlled trial using computerized decision support–based identification of study subjects, remote informed consent and clinical monitoring, and comprehensive data collection through electronic health record integration with a cloud-based research platform. In patients with symptomatic COVID-19 and at least one additional thrombosis risk factor who were not initially hospitalized, 10 mg of rivaroxaban daily prescribed for 35 days was not found to reduce the frequency of symptomatic VTE, myocardial infarction, ischemic stroke, acute limb ischemia, non-CNS systemic arterial embolism, all-cause hospitalization, and all-cause mortality. However, because the enrollment was terminated early, the trial was underpowered to provide a definitive conclusion. All-cause hospitalization was imbalanced in the intention-to-treat analysis due to a greater proportion of patients in the rivaroxaban group being hospitalized before receiving study medication. No critical-site or fatal bleeds and no significant increase in major bleeding were observed with rivaroxaban. Symptomatic VTE, myocardial infarction, ischemic stroke, acute limb ischemia, and non-CNS systemic arterial embolism were infrequent and observed with marginally lower frequency in the rivaroxaban group in a post hoc exploratory analysis.
PREVENT-HD used an innovative virtual trial design that enhanced patient engagement, enabled remote informed consent and clinical monitoring, and facilitated comprehensive data collection through electronic health record integration with a cloud-based research platform. The virtual consent platform promoted enrollment of a more ethnically and racially inclusive study population, with nearly 30% of patients being non-White. The trial also demonstrated the feasibility of completely remote monitoring for efficacy and safety outcomes, including the use of telemedicine to eliminate the need for in-person visits and laboratory testing and direct delivery of study drugs to patients by a central pharmacy. This trial design enabled follow-up in 100% of patients through day 49 in the midst of myriad pandemic logistical challenges.
Similar to most published trials of thromboprophylaxis in outpatients with symptomatic COVID-19, – PREVENT-HD observed a lower-than-expected event rate and did not demonstrate a benefit for prevention of symptomatic VTE and arterial thrombosis, all-cause hospitalization, and death. In a study of 243 outpatients with COVID-19, sulodexide, an oral mixture of glycosaminoglycans (low-molecular-weight heparin and dermatan sulfate), decreased the need for hospitalization. Although thromboinflammation was suggested as a final common pathway for clinical deterioration early in the pandemic, , subsequent data support non–thrombosis-based cardiovascular diseases , such as heart failure and sepsis as greater contributors to morbidity and mortality. , A reduction in thrombosis, clinical deterioration, and death in patients with COVID-19 has also been explained by more widespread implementation of corticosteroids, monoclonal antibodies, and antiviral agents, as well as nearly 70% of the population being fully vaccinated. – Underenrollment, as encountered in other similar trials, also likely contributed to the inability to detect a benefit of anticoagulation. – Finally, although half of the trial population was enrolled within the first 5 days after COVID-19 diagnosis, a significant benefit may have been restricted to the interval immediately after symptom onset and therefore may have been missed in PREVENT-HD and other studies. 
We observed a discordance in the point estimate favoring the placebo in the intention-to-treat analysis and rivaroxaban in the safety analysis. This difference was driven largely by an excess of hospitalizations in the rivaroxaban group compared with placebo (21 versus 17) in the intention-to-treat analysis, whereas hospitalization was less frequent in the 2 groups in the safety analysis population (11 versus 15, respectively). We postulate that this observation was likely play of chance in the context of a lower-than-expected event rate and a disproportionate number of patients assigned to rivaroxaban not receiving or taking study medication before experiencing clinical deterioration requiring hospitalization compared with patients assigned to placebo (10 versus 2). This imbalance highlights the challenges and limitations of conducting a randomized controlled trial remotely during the pandemic with an associated impact on medication supply chains and delivery services.
Similar to other trials, – PREVENT-HD observed a low frequency of symptomatic VTE, myocardial infarction, ischemic stroke, acute limb ischemia, and non-CNS systemic arterial embolism. Although events were marginally less frequent in the rivaroxaban group, the nominal number of events and the post hoc exploratory nature of this descriptive analysis preclude interpretation of a treatment benefit of rivaroxaban, and the number of events is insufficient to recommend thromboprophylaxis to nonhospitalized patients with symptomatic COVID-19. Furthermore, in the context of such a low rate of thrombosis, a difference in the frequency of such events with rivaroxaban is unlikely to be clinically meaningful. Of note, all symptomatic VTE and arterial events occurred from the start of the trial in August 2020 to the summer of 2021, an interval dominated by the original strain and the alpha variant. Further investigation in select high-risk outpatients with COVID-19 may be warranted on the basis of rivaroxaban being well tolerated in this trial and others and the prevention of clinical thrombosis being recognized as a patient safety priority. , 
PREVENT-HD should be interpreted within the context of its limitations. Although enrollment continued through April 2022, a substantial proportion of patients were enrolled early in the pandemic before widespread vaccination and availability of disease severity–modifying therapies. Accordingly, the study findings may not be generalizable to current and future phases of the pandemic, especially to those who are up-to-date with COVID-19 vaccination. However, the large unvaccinated US population continues to comprise a population at higher risk for SARs-CoV-2 infection and death due to COVID-19. Similarly, SARS-CoV-2 variants and demographic shifts in the infected population (such as younger, healthier populations) may demonstrate variable risk of thrombotic events such that the early-pandemic, older, and higher–body mass index population seen in our trial may not be representative. , The enrollment of PREVENT-HD included periods dominated by the original strain as well as alpha, delta, and omicron variants. Because of concern for COVID-19 transmission to study staff with site-conducted pill counts of returned study medication, medication adherence was monitored remotely through patient self-report of missed doses. Although medication adherence may have been overestimated due to self-reporting bias, other techniques such as pill count also have limited accuracy. Finally, because PREVENT-HD was terminated early and the event rates were lower than expected, our analyses were underpowered and may have missed small but significant differences in safety or efficacy. Alternatively, because of the very small number of events in the post hoc exploratory end point of symptomatic VTE and any arterial thrombosis, we cannot exclude a chance imbalance in outcomes favoring rivaroxaban.

## Conclusions
Rivaroxaban prescribed for 35 days after the diagnosis of symptomatic COVID-19 in nonhospitalized patients with at least one risk factor for thrombosis did not seem to reduce a composite end point of venous and arterial thrombotic events, hospitalization, and death. With the caveat that the trial was underpowered to provide a definitive conclusion, these data do not appear to support routine antithrombotic prophylaxis in nonhospitalized patients with symptomatic COVID-19.
