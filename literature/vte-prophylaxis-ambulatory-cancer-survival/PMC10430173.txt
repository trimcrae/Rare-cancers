Thrombosis and vascular biology - Biology & Translational Research
Background: Venous thromboembolism (VTE) is a frequent complication of cancer and cancer treatment. Lymphoma is a highly thrombogenic cancer according to the Khorana score (KS) in outpatient setting. Lymphoma cells promote thrombin generation by several mechanisms and can also cause lymphadenopathy-related venous stasis. Systemic anticancer therapy can disrupt endothelium and enhance the procoagulant effect of neoplastic cells by several mechanisms, and therefore, yields an additional risk factor for VTE. Cancer‐associated thrombosis is the second leading cause of death in cancer patients. Recently, PATP in solid cancer patients were performed in trials to decrease VTE rates with the aim to improve survival.
Aims: We performed a systematic review and meta‐analysis of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) to determine the benefit of PATP in lymphoma patients who were on chemotherapy.
Methods: We conducted a literature search using MEDLINE and EMBASE databases through January 31, 2023 using the following keywords; ‘low‐molecular weight heparin’ OR ‘direct oral anticoagulant’ AND ‘lymphoma. The RCTs with reduction in VTE as a primary or secondary end‐point were incorporated in the analysis. The primary meta‐analytic approach was a random effects model using the Mantel–Haenszel (MH) method. It was used to calculate the estimated pooled risk ratio (RR), and risk difference (RD) with 95% confidence interval (CI). Heterogeneity was assessed with I2 and Cochran’s Q‐ statistic. A ‘P value’ of <0·05 was considered significant and I2 > 50% was considered substantially heterogeneous.
Results: 202 patients from subsets of two RCTs (AVERT and CASSINI) were included in our meta‐analysis. Both AVERT and CASSINI trials enrolled patients with lymphoma, KS ≥ 2. Apixaban 2.5 mg twice daily and rivaroxaban 10 mg once daily were prescribed for 6 months in AVERT and CASSINI trials, respectively. The randomization ratio was 1 to 1 in both studies. The I2 statistic showed moderate heterogeneity among RCTs. The VTE incidence was similar (4·9%) in both groups. The pooled RR was not significant at 1.02 (95% CI: 0·24–4.42, P = 0·28). The absolute RD was −0·01 (95% CI: −0·09 to 0·07, P = 0·19).
Summary/Conclusion: Our analysis showed that there was no reduction in VTE rates by providing PATP to ambulatory lymphoma patients who were on chemotherapy. Hence, routine PATP should not be recommended in ambulatory patients with lymphoma. Further RCTs are needed to determine the high‐risk subset of lymphoma patients who may benefit from PATP.
